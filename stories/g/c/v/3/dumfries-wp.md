# Dumfries

A former royal burgh on the Nith where Robert the Bruce committed murder, Robert Burns died, and Norwegian soldiers waited out a war.

**Location:** 55.0700, -3.6000

**Categories:** Scotland, Royal Burgh, Robert Burns, Robert the Bruce, Dumfries and Galloway

## Introduction

Before he was a king, Robert the Bruce was a murderer. In 1306, inside the precincts of Greyfriars Kirk in Dumfries, he killed his rival John Comyn III of Badenoch in a quarrel that would set Scotland's wars of independence on their final, bloody course. The act was sacrilege and treason rolled together, committed within yards of where shoppers now browse the High Street. Dumfries has been making history of this consequential, awkward kind for nine centuries, granted royal burgh status by William the Lion in 1186 and rarely quiet for long since.

## The River and the Red Sandstone

The Nith splits the town on its way south to the Solway Firth, the seventh longest river in Scotland once you count the 13 extra kilometres the shallow Solway sands give it at low tide. Several bridges span the river in town, and between Devorgilla Bridge and the suspension bridge lies a weir locals call 'The Caul'. In wet months the Nith swallows surrounding streets; the Whitesands has flooded on average once a year since 1827. The town's distinctive warm-red buildings come from the Locharbriggs quarry just north, a Permian New Red Sandstone dimension stone that has been worked continuously since 1890. Locharbriggs stone left Dumfriesshire to face the Manchester Central Convention Complex, the Queen Alexandra Bridge in Sunderland, and the base of the Statue of Liberty in New York.

## Norwegian Soldiers in Exile

During the Second World War the bulk of the Free Norwegian Forces in Britain encamped in and around Dumfries. The reception camp opened in June 1940, taking in around 500 men and women, many of them Norwegians who had volunteered abroad while the Nazis occupied their homeland. Through that summer the numbers grew to about 1,500 under General Carl Gustav Fleischer. RAF Dumfries opened nearby in June 1940 as well, becoming No. 18 Maintenance Unit. The base saw one moment of war when a German Dornier Do 217 strafed the runway beacon on 25 March 1943 before crashing nearby. Oberleutnant Martin Piscke, the pilot, was buried in Troqueer Cemetery with full military honours, an enemy treated with dignity in death.

## Burns Among the Pews

Robert Burns moved to Dumfriesshire in 1788 and into the town itself in 1791. He died here on 21 July 1796 and was first buried in St Michael's churchyard. In 1815, his remains were moved to a grand mausoleum in the south-east corner of the same churchyard, where his wife Jean Armour and several family members were also eventually laid. The Greyfriars Church overlooks a statue of the poet, sculpted in Carrara marble in 1882 by Amelia Robertson Hill and unveiled by Archibald Primrose, the future Prime Minister. That statue features on Bank of Scotland five-pound notes from 2007. Burns the man drank at the Globe Inn; Burns the poet now stands eternally in stone, a few hundred yards from where Bruce's dagger flashed.

## Steamboats and the Doonhamers

On 14 October 1788, inventor William Symington tested an engine-powered boat on Dalswinton Loch near Dumfries while working with Patrick Miller of Dalswinton. The experiment proved a steam engine could run on water without setting the hull alight, and Symington went on to build the first practical steamboat. Burns himself is said to have watched the trial. Burns was also a regular at Ellisland Farm just up the Nith, and the Symington test happened within a few miles of his door. The townspeople are still called Doonhamers, from the way Dumfries natives, when working away, called the place doon hame. The Guid Nychburris festival each June keeps the burgh tradition alive, with up to 250 horses gathering at dawn to Ride the Marches.

## A Town That Rebuilds

In 1999 Scotland's first multi-institutional university campus opened on the 85-acre Crichton estate two miles south of town, sharing buildings between the University of the West of Scotland, Dumfries and Galloway College, and the University of Glasgow. Calvin Harris grew up here. So did Allan McNish, winner of Le Mans three times, and BBC presenter Kirsty Wark, and Frank Williams of Formula One, and J.M. Barrie author of Peter Pan, all educated locally. The bypass built in the 1980s sucked traffic, and some business, away from the town centre; refurbishment has been the slow answer ever since. Two Boxing Day earthquakes shook the town in 1979 and 2006, neither doing serious harm. The Nith floods, the rugby club plays on, and the Theatre Royal, claimed as the oldest working theatre in Scotland, has been staging shows since 1792.

## Flight Context

Dumfries sits at 55.07 degrees north, 3.62 degrees west, in southern Scotland's Southern Uplands region. The town lies 25 miles north of the English border, with the Solway Firth's shallow sands visible to the south. Cruise at 3,000 to 4,500 feet to take in the Nith's meandering course, the red sandstone town centre, and the surrounding patchwork of Locharbriggs and farmland. Dumfries Aerodrome (EGCO) sits just north of town and handles light aircraft. Carlisle Lake District Airport (EGNC) lies 25 miles southeast; Glasgow Prestwick (EGPK) is 50 miles northwest. Watch for the Locharbriggs quarry north of town and the broad Solway estuary opening south.
