# Blackpool

A working-class holiday capital whose ballrooms launched ballroom dancing as we know it, whose grime scene became a Channel 4 controversy, and whose football team won the 1953 FA Cup with Stanley Matthews on the wing.

**Location:** 53.8167, -3.0500

**Categories:** city, blackpool, lancashire, ballroom-dancing, music, sport, lgbt-history

## Introduction

The first British ballroom dancing championship was held in this town. So was the inaugural British Amateur Latin American Tournament. The Empress Ballroom in the Winter Gardens, built in 1896 with a 3,000-person capacity on a former roller-rink site, hosts around fifty countries every year at the Blackpool Dance Festival - widely considered the world's first and foremost festival of dancing. Strictly Come Dancing comes here every November for its Blackpool week and broadcasts from the Tower Ballroom Wurlitzer. The dance hall city of England is also a tram town, a coaster town, an LGBT-friendly town, and, lately, the unlikely cradle of a British grime scene. None of those things sit easily together. All of them are Blackpool.

## A Town That Dances

Ballrooms opened across Blackpool from the 1890s. The original Tower Ballroom from 1894 was a modest pavilion; the Winter Gardens responded in 1896 with the much grander Empress Ballroom, designed by Mangnall and Littlewood, on the site of a roller rink. The Blackpool Dance Festival began as a tournament to find three new sequence dances - waltz, two-step and foxtrot - over three days, with a winner chosen on the fourth. In 1931 it hosted the inaugural British Professional and Amateur Ballroom Championships. By 1953 the lineup included the British and North of England championships plus formation dancing. The British Amateur and Professional Latin American tournaments arrived in 1961 and 1962, upgraded to championship status in 1964. Across town the Tower Ballroom kept its Wurlitzer organ rising from below the stage, played by Reginald Dixon for forty years and Phil Kelsall since 1977. The BBC's Come Dancing aired from here from 1950 to 1998; its successor Strictly broadcasts from the Tower every Blackpool week.

## The Golden Age

At its 1930s peak Blackpool's theatres and cinemas seated more than 60,000 people across multiple venues. The Opera House, third on its site, opened in 1939 as the largest theatre in the country with 3,000 seats. Frank Matcham's Grand Theatre from 1894 still operates and received Arts Council England National Portfolio status in 2022, worth more than £1.5 million over three years. The post-war period made Blackpool the centre of live entertainment outside London; three or four variety shows ran per night each tourist season. The Shadows, Tom Jones, Engelbert Humperdinck and Frank Sinatra all played here. The Central Pier producer was offered The Beatles for the 1963 summer season and turned them down - having never heard of them. They instead played multiple Sunday nights at the ABC Theatre in the summer of 1963 and returned for the Opera House in August 1964.

## Grime, Section 25, and the Membranes

Section 25 formed in Blackpool in 1977 and signed to Factory Records, scoring a US crossover hit with Looking From a Hilltop and recording the album From the Hip. The post-punk scene around the Membranes, the Fits, and the Ceramic Hobbs grew in parallel. In the 2010s a wholly different sound emerged - children from Blackpool's deprived neighbourhoods making aggressive grime under the BGMedia label. Afghan Dan, Little T, Millie B and Sophie Aspin became the subject of a 2016 Vice documentary, Noisey Blackpool: The Controversial Rise of Blackpool Grime, followed by a one-year update in 2017. In October 2019 Channel 4 aired It's Grime Up North, criticised for what reviewers called sneering derision of children as young as twelve. Whatever you think of the music, the artists were real people growing up in some of the most deprived wards in England, articulating what they saw.

## Stanley Matthews and the Cod Army Within Reach

Blackpool F.C. won the 1953 FA Cup Final, with Stanley Matthews on the wing in the match remembered as the Matthews Final. The club spent thirty-one seasons in the top division. Sir Stanley played here, Jimmy Armfield captained from here, Roy Gratrix patrolled the back. The town's other big sport story is the wrestling booth at Pleasure Beach's Horseshoe Show Bar, run by Bobby Barron until his death in 1994 - reputedly the last working wrestling booth of its kind in the world. Members of the public could challenge professional wrestlers for cash, with shooters skilled in catch wrestling defending the prize. Future WWE star William Regal worked the booth as a young wrestler. The Blackpool Combat Club, a heel faction in All Elite Wrestling led by Regal, is named for it. Tyler Bate won the first WWE United Kingdom Champion tournament held in the town. Live Like Ralph, a charity celebrating local skater Ralph Roberts who died suddenly of sarcoidosis in 2021, has built skateparks and provided boards to local kids.

## The Town That Tries

Blackpool has had a gay pride since 2006; at the 2021 census 3.26% of residents aged 16+ identified as gay or lesbian, the twelfth-highest proportion of 331 local authorities in England and Wales. The We're Still Here oral history project, supported by the National Lottery Heritage Fund, has built the town's first permanent LGBTQIA+ collection. The deprivation is real - three of the town's five wards still fall in the most deprived 10% in England - and the Stay Safe section in any honest Blackpool guide is candid about it. But the town also has Showtown, an interactive showbiz museum that opened in 2024; an architectural prize-nominated Library and Laundry Room on the Mereside estate; and a stubborn refusal to be only one thing. The Tower stands. The Wurlitzer rises. The trams roll. The Cod Army sings.

## Flight Context

Blackpool's town centre sits at 53.82 N, 3.05 W on the Fylde Coast. EGNH Blackpool airport lies 1.5 nm east of the central promenade. The Blackpool Tower (518 ft) is a designated Visual Reporting Point known to general aviation as 'the tall tower', visible 30+ nm in clear weather and instantly recognisable from any direction. The Pleasure Beach lattice marks the south end of the strip; Bispham cliff marks the north end. Population about 145,000. Manchester (EGCC) 35 nm southeast, Liverpool John Lennon (EGGP) 28 nm south. From altitude during the autumn Illuminations, six continuous miles of coloured light run between Starr Gate and Bispham - one of the most distinctive night-time aerial signatures on the British west coast.
