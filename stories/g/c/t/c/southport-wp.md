# Southport

A planned Victorian resort on the Lancashire coast, knit together by railways, dunes, golf, and Red Rum, whose exercise on the beach made the National's three-time winner.

**Location:** 53.6469, -3.0081

**Categories:** seaside resort, Merseyside, Lancashire, Victorian planned town, golf

## Introduction

Red Rum, the three-time winner of the Grand National, did most of his training on Southport beach. The sand is wide and flat and goes a long way out at low tide; the horse galloped through the shallows because the saltwater was thought to be good for his hooves. That is one way to start a story about this town: with the racehorse who was famous because he survived a disease called pedal osteitis on the wet sand of an unlikely seaside resort.

## Railways and Origins

Southport's modern existence is owed to the railway. The Liverpool, Crosby and Southport Railway built the first line into the town in 1848, terminating temporarily at one station and extending to another by 1851. On 9 April 1855, the Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway and the East Lancashire Railway, who had jointly taken over the Manchester and Southport Railway, opened the line from the east. All operations were consolidated at Chapel Street in 1857. Three other lines once served the town. The West Lancashire Railway ran from Southport Derby Road to Preston Fishergate Hill from 1882 until 1964. The Cheshire Lines Committee's Southport and Cheshire Lines Extension Railway ran from Liverpool Central to Southport Lord Street from 1884 until 1952. The Liverpool, Southport and Preston Junction Railway provided a connecting branch. The Lakeside Miniature Railway, which still runs along the western side of the marine lake under the pier, claims to be the oldest continuously running miniature railway in the world.

## Two Ryder Cups in the Dunes

Royal Birkdale Golf Club, in the dunes south of the town, is on the rotation for The Open Championship and has hosted two Ryder Cups. Nearby Southport and Ainsdale Golf Club has also hosted two Ryder Cups. Hillside Golf Club and Hesketh Golf Club are Open final qualifying courses in their own right. Smaller links courses surround the town. Royal Birkdale will host The Open from 12 to 19 July 2026. The dune system that makes the golf possible is itself a habitat of national importance, a designated Site of Special Scientific Interest, and the same wind that shapes the courses is what makes Ainsdale Beach south of the town a popular spot for kite surfing.

## The Beach Speed Record

On 16 March 1926, Henry Segrave set a world land speed record of 152.33 miles per hour on the flat sand at Ainsdale Beach in a four-litre Sunbeam Tiger nicknamed Ladybird. A public house on Lord Street still bears his name. The beach has hosted a few other unusual moments: Marc Almond of Soft Cell, Lee Mack, golfer Tommy Fleetwood, celebrity chef Marcus Wareing, and singer Låpsley all came from this town. The Stooges' Ron Asheton decided on a music career after visiting the Cavern Club during a stay in Southport. The boys' comic Eagle, in which Dan Dare first flew his rocket, was started here. The indie rock band Gomez originated here. Southport rugby football club, founded on 29 November 1872, is one of the oldest in the world.

## Football, Tennis, and Endurance

Southport Football Club have played at Haig Avenue since 1905. They entered the Football League in 1921, were voted out in 1978 after three consecutive twenty-third-place finishes, and now play in the National League North, the sixth tier of English football. They were the last club to leave the Football League through the re-election process. The town hosted the North of England Hard Court Championships on clay from 1956 to 1988; the women's titles were won, among others, five times by Shirley Bloomer and twice by Angela Buxton, and Evonne Goolagong also took one. The annual Southport 24 Hour Race, an endurance sailing event on Marine Lake organised by the West Lancashire Yacht Club, has been running since 1966 and averages sixty to eighty boats.

## July 2024

On 29 July 2024, three girls were killed at a dance class in this town. Bebe King was six. Elsie Dot Stancombe was seven. Alice da Silva Aguiar was nine. Two adults and several other children were seriously wounded. The country looked at Southport in horror. In the days that followed, riots broke out across England, hijacking the families' grief and seeking targets that had nothing to do with the children. The local MP and the bereaved parents asked for the violence to stop. Thousands attended a vigil. A ten-million-pound revamp of nearby gardens, announced in June 2025, was inspired by the three girls. They were children at a Taylor Swift dance workshop on a summer morning, attended by twenty-six children aged six to thirteen. That is who they were, and that is how this town will keep remembering them.

## Flight Context

Southport sits at 53.647 N, 3.008 W on the Lancashire/Merseyside coast in the borough of Sefton. The nearest commercial airport is Liverpool John Lennon (EGGP, LPL), about 18 nm to the south; Blackpool International (EGNH, BLK) is about 14 nm to the north. From altitude, the most distinctive features are Southport Pier reaching west into Liverpool Bay, Marine Lake just south of the pier, and the dune complex containing Royal Birkdale Golf Club on the south side of the town. Lord Street's covered Victorian canopy runs parallel to the seafront a few hundred metres inland. The Ribble Estuary opens northward into Liverpool Bay; Blackpool Tower is sometimes visible up the coast in clear weather, and the Welsh hills are visible to the southwest across the bay.
