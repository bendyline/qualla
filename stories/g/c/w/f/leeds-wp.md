# Leeds

The metropolitan district of Leeds - a city built on cloth and coal that today is the UK's largest financial centre outside London.

**Location:** 53.7975, -1.5436

**Categories:** cities, Yorkshire, finance, Victorian, universities, industrial heritage

## Introduction

Eight hundred eleven thousand, nine hundred and fifty-three. That was the population of the Leeds metropolitan district at the 2021 Census - an eight per cent rise over the previous decade, and a number that hides the more interesting story underneath. The urban subdivision of Leeds itself houses about four hundred and seventy-five thousand people; the rest of the borough's population spreads across thirty-one civil parishes, the suburbs and former mill towns that the city has slowly absorbed but never quite digested. Leeds is the third-largest city in Britain by some measures, the largest in West Yorkshire by any, and home to the most diverse economy of any UK employment centre - the only place in the country where banking, public administration, and retail each contribute roughly a quarter of total jobs.

## What the Money Built

Modern Leeds is the largest legal and financial centre in the UK outside London. There are more than thirty national and international banks operating in the city, including an office of the Bank of England; First Direct and Yorkshire Bank are headquartered here, and Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds, NatWest, and Santander all run major operations. Around 150 law firms employ over 6,700 people. The 2.5 billion pounds of property development that swept through central Leeds between 1999 and 2008 reshaped the south bank of the Aire - the skyline grew. Bridgewater Place, known locally as the Dalek, rose to 112 metres in 2007. Altus House in Arena Quarter took the tallest-building title in 2021. Meanwhile Channel 4 opened its national headquarters at the Majestic in 2020 - the first time a major British broadcaster relocated significant central operations outside London.

## Le Prince's Garden

In October 1888, the French inventor Louis Le Prince filmed two seconds of his son and in-laws walking in circles in a Roundhay garden, three miles north of Leeds city centre. The Roundhay Garden Scene is the oldest surviving motion picture in the world - filmed years before Edison's kinetoscope, years before the Lumière brothers' first Cinématographe show. Le Prince filmed Traffic Crossing Leeds Bridge in the same period. He was preparing to demonstrate his single-lens camera publicly when he boarded a train at Dijon in September 1890 and vanished without trace. Wordsworth Donisthorpe, another Leeds inventor, had filmed what is now the second-oldest surviving film; his camera patent predated Le Prince's by twelve years. They both belonged to the Leeds Philosophical and Literary Society. Whether they ever met is unknown. The Cottage Road Cinema in Headingley has been showing films continuously since 1912, the Hyde Park Picture House since 1914 - both among the oldest still-running cinemas in the United Kingdom.

## Stone and Stage

The civic and cultural infrastructure of Leeds was built mostly between 1858 and 1933, then steadily augmented. The trio of Cuthbert Brodrick's buildings - the Town Hall (1858), the Corn Exchange (1864), and the Mechanics' Institute that became the City Museum - anchor the centre. The Civic Hall opened in 1933, its twin spires topped with golden owls visible across the skyline. Kirkstall Abbey, the twelfth-century Cistercian ruin on the Aire, sits in its own parkland west of the centre; Armley Mills, once the world's largest woollen mill, shows the industry that paid for everything. Leeds is also home to Opera North, the only national opera company outside London, based at the refurbished Grand Theatre. The Leeds Playhouse - formerly the West Yorkshire Playhouse - contains two auditoriums. The City Varieties Music Hall once hosted Charlie Chaplin and Harry Houdini, and gave its stage to the BBC television show The Good Old Days for thirty years.

## Live at Leeds

On Valentine's Day 1970, The Who climbed onto the stage at the University of Leeds Refectory and recorded what music critics have repeatedly called the greatest live rock album ever made. Pink Floyd wrote See Emily Play after a 1967 gig at what was then Kitson College. The bands that came out of Leeds since - Soft Cell, the Sisters of Mercy, the Wedding Present, Gang of Four, Kaiser Chiefs, Corinne Bailey Rae, Alt-J, Melanie B of the Spice Girls, Yard Act - read like a parallel history of British post-punk and indie. The F Club ran from 1977 to 1982 and shaped the goth scene; Le Phonographique in the Merrion Centre was the first gothic nightclub in the world. Roundhay Park has hosted Michael Jackson, Madonna, Bruce Springsteen, the Rolling Stones, and Robbie Williams. First Direct Arena, opened 2013, holds 13,500 and was voted best new venue in the world at the 2014 Stadium Business Awards.

## Carnival and Pride

Leeds West Indian Carnival has run through the streets of Chapeltown and Harehills since 1967, the oldest in Western Europe and the UK's third-largest. Around 100,000 people attend each summer. Leeds Pride, founded in 2006, drew 40,000 to its 2018 march with over a hundred floats and benefits the city economy by an estimated 3.8 million pounds. Leeds Civic Trust runs a scheme of fifteen Rainbow Plaques commemorating places of LGBTQ+ significance across the city. Five universities, Britain's fourth-largest student population, a Premier League football club restored after sixteen years away, and Yorkshire County Cricket Club playing at Headingley - the city has more identities than any single visit can hold.

## Flight Context

Central Leeds is at 53.798°N, 1.544°W. The metropolitan district extends across 552 km², from the Pennine fringe in the west to the agricultural land near Selby in the east. Leeds Bradford Airport (EGNM) lies 7 nm to the north-northwest with runway 14/32. From altitude the urban core forms a distinctive grid wedged between the M621 motorway to the south and the Inner Ring Road to the north. Visual landmarks: the Town Hall clock tower in the centre, Altus House (380 m above ground level) as the tallest structure, Elland Road stadium south-west, Headingley cricket ground 2 nm north, Roundhay Park as a large green patch north-east, and the white Parkinson building of Leeds University on rising ground. Best viewing altitude 2,500-4,500 ft AGL. Mid-summer evenings often give the clearest light.
