# Caldey Abbey

A Trappist monastery on an island off the Pembrokeshire coast where monks have made perfume and chocolate since the early twentieth century, built on a site where Celtic monks first settled in the sixth century.

**Location:** 51.6373, -4.6843

**Categories:** Benedictine monasteries in Wales, Cistercian monasteries in Wales, Trappist monasteries in the United Kingdom

## Introduction

Caldey Island sits two and a half miles off the coast of Tenby in Pembrokeshire, a twenty-minute boat ride that feels like a journey between centuries. Monks have lived here since the sixth century -- first Celtic hermits, then Benedictines from 1136 until the Dissolution in 1536, then a gap of nearly four hundred years before an Anglican Benedictine community arrived in 1906 and built the abbey that stands today. The current residents are Trappists, members of the strict Cistercian order who divide their days between prayer, silence, and the manufacture of lavender perfume, shortbread, and chocolate. It is a combination that sounds whimsical until you see it in practice: the discipline is monastic, the business is real, and the island has been self-sustaining through commerce and faith for longer than most corporations have existed.

## Fifteen Centuries of Solitude

A Celtic monastery was established on Caldey in the sixth century. Saint Pyr served as the first abbot, and Saint Samson of Dol was among the early leaders. A Benedictine foundation took root in 1136 and endured four centuries until Henry VIII's Dissolution shuttered it in 1536. The island lay dormant as a monastic site until 1900, when William Done Bushell offered it to Dom Aelred Carlyle. An Anglican Benedictine community led by Carlyle arrived in 1906 and constructed the current abbey between 1906 and 1910 in an Italian style. The architect John Cyril Hawes designed a master plan intended to evoke the medieval abbeys of Cluny, Vezelay, and Durham, while John Coates Carter executed the structure in the Arts and Crafts style -- his largest project. In 1913, the monks were received into the Roman Catholic Church. A small Anglican remnant departed for Pershore Abbey in Worcestershire and eventually settled at Nashdom Abbey. The Catholic Benedictines moved to Prinknash Abbey in Gloucestershire by 1928, and the Trappists have occupied Caldey since 1929.

## The Scent of Gorse and Lavender

Monastic life on Caldey has always required practical enterprise. The profuse growth of wild lavender on the island inspired the monks to develop fragrances, which they branded and marketed as Caldey Abbey Perfumes. As demand grew beyond what the island's flowers could supply, they began importing scent oil, and production now continues year-round using both lavender and the island's native gorse. Alongside perfume, the monks produce shortbread and chocolate sold under the 'Abbot's Kitchen' brand. A dairy once operated on the island, selling iced confectionery and cake, though it has since closed. The abbey's commercial operations are modest by any secular standard, but they sustain a way of life that has persisted on this island for fifteen centuries. The Grade II-listed abbey buildings, the post office, and the shop all serve both the monastic community and the visitors who arrive by boat from Tenby during the summer months.

## A Darker Chapter

The island's isolation, which has long nurtured contemplation, also concealed abuse. In 2016, three women launched legal action against the abbey over sexual abuse they had suffered as children at the hands of Father Thaddeus Kotik, a monk who lived in the abbey from 1947 until his death in 1992. At least six girls from families who holidayed on the island were abused between 1972 and 1987. The abbey reached a financial settlement with six claimants in March 2017 and apologized for failing to report the abuse when they learned of it in 1990. Following media coverage, five more women came forward. Separately, it emerged that Paul Ashton, a fugitive child sex offender, had fled to the abbey in 2004 using a pseudonym and remained until 2011, during which time he managed the island's internet and telephone systems and worked in the mail room. A visitor recognized him from the Crimestoppers 'Most Wanted' list, and he was arrested and pleaded guilty to possessing thousands of indecent images. In 2024, the abbey commissioned an independent review led by Jan Pickles, a former assistant police and crime commissioner. The review represents an attempt to reckon honestly with failures that the abbey's remoteness made possible and its institutional culture perpetuated.

## Faith and the Sea

Caldey Island belongs to a particular tradition of island monasticism found along the Atlantic coasts of Britain and Ireland -- places where the boundary between land and sea mirrors the boundary between the earthly and the divine. Skellig Michael off Ireland's coast, Iona in the Scottish Hebrides, Lindisfarne in Northumbria -- all share with Caldey this sense of deliberate separation, of seeking God at the edge of the known world. The Trappist rule demands silence, manual labor, and communal prayer. The monks rise before dawn. Their days follow a rhythm set not by commerce or convenience but by the liturgical hours. That this rhythm has persisted through Celtic, Benedictine, Anglican, and Trappist iterations -- surviving dissolution, conversion, scandal, and the relentless economics of island life -- suggests that what draws people to Caldey is not any particular theology but the island itself: its remoteness, its beauty, and the particular quality of attention that isolation demands.
