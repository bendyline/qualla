# Creeslough Explosion

A gas explosion at a petrol station in this quiet Donegal village killed ten people in October 2022, uniting Ireland in grief and revealing the fierce bonds of a tight-knit rural community.

**Location:** 55.1192, -7.9057

**Categories:** disasters, ireland, community

## Introduction

Creeslough is the kind of place where everyone knows everyone. A village of a few hundred people tucked into the hills of north County Donegal, it sits where the road bends between Letterkenny and the wild Atlantic coast, the sort of place visitors pass through on the way to somewhere else. On the afternoon of 7 October 2022, an explosion at the Applegreen petrol station complex shattered that quiet in an instant. The building -- apartments stacked above a convenience store, post office, and hair salon -- collapsed onto the forecourt. Up to thirty people had been inside, including schoolchildren stopping in after class. By the following morning, ten were confirmed dead, their ages ranging from five to fifty-nine. It was the deadliest single incident in County Donegal in nearly eighty years.

## A Friday Afternoon

The Applegreen complex sat along the N56, serving as a hub for a community that had few gathering places. People came not just for petrol but for the deli counter, the post office, a haircut, a chat with neighbors. When the explosion occurred on that Friday afternoon, its force blew out windows on an upper floor and brought the structure down on those below. Emergency services rushed in from across the region. Helicopters from the Irish Coast Guard and Air Ambulance Northern Ireland airlifted the critically injured -- one young man was flown all the way to St. James's Hospital in Dublin. Eight people were hospitalized. But for ten others, the damage was beyond rescue. Among the dead were four men, three women, a teenage boy and girl, and a five-year-old girl -- people whose absence would be felt in every corner of the village.

## A Tsunami of Grief

The parish priest, Fr. John Joe Duffy, found words that the nation borrowed: Creeslough had been hit by a "tsunami of grief." A special mass the morning after drew hundreds. By Sunday, thousands attended vigils across Donegal, the first held in nearby Milford. Books of condolence opened in city halls from Cork to Belfast. The Taoiseach came, and the Tanaiste, and political leaders from every party. Pope Francis sent a letter. King Charles expressed condolences. Irish flags at embassies worldwide flew at half mast. The scale of response reflected something particular about this tragedy -- it was not a disaster in an anonymous city block, but in a place small enough that every one of the ten lives lost had a face, a story, a family known by name throughout the community.

## Carrying On

In the weeks that followed, Creeslough did what small communities do: it carried on, visibly wounded. The destroyed post office reopened temporarily elsewhere in the village, with residents driving to Kilmacrennan in the meantime. The Irish Red Cross established a support fund that surpassed one million euros within a week. Sporting events had been postponed across Donegal the weekend of the explosion -- some members of St. Michael's GAA club in Creeslough were among those killed. In a moment that pierced the grief, footballer Amber Barrett, from nearby Milford, scored the winning goal in Ireland's Women's World Cup qualifier against Scotland that October evening and dedicated it to Creeslough.

## Questions Without Answers

The investigation into the explosion moved slowly and carefully. Garda forensic teams spent more than a month examining the site, securing CCTV footage that showed the moment of collapse. The Irish Aviation Authority imposed a drone exclusion zone around the village. Experts from the Garda Technical Bureau, the Commission for Regulation of Utilities, and independent consultants joined the inquiry. Initial assessments pointed to a tragic accident rather than deliberate action. Between 2024 and late 2024, several arrests were made under the Non-Fatal Offences Against the Person Act, though no charges followed. The investigation continues, and the families of the ten people who died still await definitive answers about what destroyed the heart of their village on an ordinary Friday afternoon.

## A Place Changed Forever

In January 2023, a "Together for Creeslough" concert was held at the Aura Centre in Letterkenny, hosted by Donegal's own Moya Brennan and Mairead Ni Mhaonaigh, to thank the first responders who had worked through the night of the explosion. Local school choirs performed alongside national artists. It was a gesture of resilience and gratitude, but also of the kind of grief that has no endpoint. TD Pearse Doherty said what everyone in Creeslough already knew: the village would be "forever changed." The site of the explosion remains a wound in the landscape, a gap where the daily life of a community once gathered. What happened here is remembered not as a statistic but as ten names, ten lives cut short in a place where life is measured by the people you know and the bonds between them.
