# Strandhill

A village known to the Irish as Larass grew into one of Ireland's surfing capitals on a peninsula already shaped by 5,000 years of human habitation and the relentless work of shifting sand.

**Location:** 54.2719, -8.5933

**Categories:** coast, village, surfing, ireland, sligo

## Introduction

The Irish know Strandhill by an older name: Larass. It sits on the Coolera Peninsula in County Sligo, the village pressed between the western face of Knocknarea mountain and the Atlantic that gives it its English name. The 2022 census put the population at 1,982 - up 40% from 2006, a striking growth rate for a small Irish coastal village. The growth came from surfing, from remote workers, from people who wanted to live somewhere the wave-faces stack neatly into rideable lines. Five thousand years before any of that, Neolithic farmers were also picking this exact landscape, raising tombs that still survive on the hills above the dunes.

## Five Thousand Years of Settlement

The Coolera Peninsula was an important social and ritual centre during the Neolithic, between roughly 4000 and 2500 BC. The Carrowmore complex began rising around 3600 BC - at least sixty megalithic tombs scattered across the small peninsula, making it one of the densest concentrations of such monuments anywhere in Europe. There is a Neolithic court tomb beside the Airport road. Queen Maeve's cairn stands on top of Knocknarea, the great mound built around 3000 BC. The people who built these things lived here for centuries, then mostly vanished from the historical record. Their stones remained. Long before there was a Strandhill, a Larass, an Irish-speaking parish or an English-speaking resort, this peninsula was already old.

## The Buenos Ayres Speculation

The modern village owes itself to a developer named Benjamin Murrow, who in 1895 bought undeveloped land between the upper road and the sea for £1,760. He spent another £1,000 building a road down to the strand and offered plots either side, marketing it with the imperial flourish of calling his new street Buenos Ayres Drive. In 1912 he built a bathhouse to attract Victorian visitors interested in sea-bathing for their health. The development worked, in stages. Buenos Ayres Drive passed into public ownership in 1928, the shore strip in 1936. Strandhill grew up around Murrow's speculation, eventually engulfing the old farm settlement of Larass that had given the area its Irish name. The village now extends through three townlands - Strandhill, Killaspugbrone, and Carrowbunnaun.

## Why It's Famous Now

Strandhill is a vast beach break that can hold huge waves when the conditions line up - Atlantic groundswell from the west, offshore wind, the right tide. Surfing is best when the tide is pushing in from mid to high. The beach is not safe for swimming; the rip currents are real and the council posts warning signs. Paddlers head instead to nearby Culleenamore beach, sheltered behind the golf course on Ballysadare Bay, where seals haul out on the sand and there's sometimes pony racing in summer. The Culleenamore Races have been running since the 1800s. Marram-grass dunes line the coast, slowly eroding under Atlantic storms despite repeated council efforts to hold them in place.

## Sport, Music, and the Warriors' Run

The village runs Strand Celtic for soccer and shares the Coolera/Strandhill GAA club with the neighbouring parish of Ransboro - both Gaelic football and hurling are played. Sligo Rugby Football Club is based at nearby Hamilton Park. The 18-hole Strandhill Golf Club has been described as having "undulating fairways and unforgiving short cuts." The Warriors' Run, held annually since 1985, takes runners on a 15-kilometre course from the beach up the steep slopes of Knocknarea to the cairn at the summit and back down again, while the village fills with music. The Strandhill Guitar Festival arrives every September. The Strandhill People's Market - long held in a hangar at Sligo Airport on Sundays - was suspended in 2025 when the airport needed the space back. Locally produced food, textiles, and crafts will reappear somewhere soon.

## Dolly's Cottage and Familiar Faces

Dolly's Cottage on the seafront is a thatched cottage roughly 200 years old, open to the public in summer - a small surviving fragment of the Larass farm settlement that predated the resort. Up the road, St Asicus National School and the two parish churches - St Patrick's Catholic and St Anne's Church of Ireland - quietly mark the village's daily life. Westlife's Kian Egan and his wife, the actress and model Jodi Albert, live here with their children, a fact that draws fan attention from time to time but has not noticeably changed the village's flow. The Klaus Gundchen sculpture that once stood at the entrance to the beach was removed in February 2016, the council citing condition concerns. The sand stays. The waves arrive. Strandhill keeps growing.

## Flight Context

Strandhill sits at 54.272°N, 8.593°W on the south shore of Sligo Bay at the western base of Knocknarea, 9 km west of Sligo town. The village runs along the west-facing Atlantic coast with a long sand beach and dunes. Sligo Airport (EISG) lies within the village. From the air, Knocknarea's flat-topped summit with Queen Maeve's cairn (3 km east), Coney Island offshore, and the long surf-line on the beach are all visible. Donegal Airport (EIDL) is 60 km north. Best viewed at 1,500-3,000 ft AGL; expect significant turbulence and lift near Knocknarea in westerly conditions.
