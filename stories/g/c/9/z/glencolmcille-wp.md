# Glencolmcille

A Gaeltacht parish on Donegal's Atlantic coast where Christian saints layered themselves onto sites already sacred for three thousand years.

**Location:** 54.7103, -8.7203

**Categories:** gaeltacht, civil-parish, saint-columba, megalithic, national-monument

## Introduction

The earliest names for this place were just Glend, the Glen, and Senglenn, the Old Glen. It was old then. Court cairns dating to around 3000 BC stand where pilgrims still circle them three times sunwise on June 9th, praying, before turning their backs to the stone and renouncing the World, the Flesh, and the Devil. The Christian name, Gleann Cholm Cille, the Valley of Colm Cille, was not formally recorded until 1532, when Manus O'Donnell wrote it down in his Life of Colm Cille. Saint Columba (521-597) had been dead for nearly a thousand years. The pilgrimage was older than the saint it was named for.

## Four National Monuments

Four sites in the parish together make up Ireland's National Monument number 139. The first is the Glencolumbkille Cashel, the penitential station also called the Turas, where pilgrims walk 15 stations beginning at the Neolithic court cairn. The second is the Glencolumbkille Church of Ireland church, with a holy well in the townland of Beefan. The third is at Malin Beg, where the Church of St Kevin sits inside an ancient ringfort. The fourth is the portal tomb at Malin More, dated to roughly 2000 BC. Three of the four sites predate Christianity in Ireland entirely. The Christian layer came later and accommodated itself to what was already there, which is the pattern across the Irish landscape but rarely so visible in a single small parish.

## A Living Gaeltacht

Glencolmcille is a civil parish in the historic barony of Banagh. It sits inside the Donegal Gaeltacht, one of the largest Irish-speaking areas in the country. The 2002 figures showed only 34% of residents using Irish daily, and the trend has continued downward, but the language is still here, woven into ordinary life. Since 1984, Oideas Gael has run Irish language and culture courses out of the village, drawing students from across Ireland and the world. The parish council, Comhairle Paroiste Ghleann Cholm Cille, has been running since the 1930s, elected every four years by the residents of the Glencolmcille church area. Practical things, language, place names, who speaks to whom, are still local decisions here.

## Arnold Bax in the Glen

The English composer Sir Arnold Bax spent extended visits in Glencolmcille between 1904 and the early 1930s. He wrote much of his music and many of his poems here, and described the district at length in his autobiography Farewell My Youth. The Dublin-born marine artist Kenneth King made the parish his home, painting naval and merchant shipping, lighthouses, and the cliffs that define this coast. The 1992 film The Railway Station Man, starring Julie Christie and Donald Sutherland, was shot on location here. The parish has produced novelists, including Patrick 'Sonny' McGinley, born in 1937, and Gaelic footballers, including John Joe Doherty and Noel Hegarty. James McDyer, the priest born nearby in Glenties in 1910, became known across Ireland for his decades of work organizing rural cooperatives in this part of Donegal.

## The Cliffs and the Strand

The natural sites around Glencolmcille are why most visitors come at all. Slieve League, Sliabh Liag in Irish, rises sheer from the Atlantic in cliffs that, depending on how you measure, are among the highest sea cliffs in Europe. The Silver Strand, An Traigh Bhan, sits at Malin Beg, a horseshoe cove of pale sand reached by a long flight of steps down through the dunes and cliffs. Glen Head, Cionn Ghlinne itself, marks the northern boundary of the glen. The Battle of Doire Leathan was fought near Teelin in September 1590, and the eldest son of Sir Hugh McManus O'Donnell, Sir Domhnall O Domhnaill, was killed there. Charles Inglis, born in 1734 as the son of the local Church of Ireland rector, would emigrate and eventually become the first Bishop of Nova Scotia. The glen sends people out as well as taking them in.

## The Folk Village

At the edge of Cashel sits the Folk Village Museum, a small cluster of thatched cottages set up to show what daily life looked like in this parish across three different centuries. Each cottage represents a different period, furnished with the objects ordinary people would have used. The petrol station, grocer, post office, woollen mill, hill-walking centre, restaurant, village cafe, and two pubs make up the working life of modern Glencolmcille. Fiddle music is common in the pubs in the evenings. The parish is small and scattered, the kind of place that depends on its own inhabitants for almost everything. The Turas walk on June 9th, the date of Saint Colm Cille's death in 597, remains the annual rhythm. Some pilgrims still walk it barefoot. Most do not.

## Flight Context

Located at 54.71°N, 8.72°W on the Atlantic coast of southwest County Donegal, in the historic barony of Banagh. Best viewed at 2,000-4,000 feet to catch the cliffs of Slieve League rising to the east and Glen Head to the north. Nearest airport is Donegal (EIDL), 55 km northeast; Sligo (EISG) 75 km southeast. The parish stretches from coast to mountain; weather here changes fast.
