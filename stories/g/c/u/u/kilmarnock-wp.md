# Kilmarnock

The Ayrshire town that printed the first collection of Robert Burns's poetry in 1786, then spent two centuries inventing, building and losing the industries that defined it.

**Location:** 55.6117, -4.4944

**Categories:** town, scotland, ayrshire, industrial history, burns

## Introduction

In 1786, a printer named John Wilson on King Street in Kilmarnock pressed 612 copies of a little book called Poems, Chiefly in the Scottish Dialect. The poet was an unknown Ayrshire farmer named Robert Burns. The slim volume sold out within weeks, and a town that nobody in the wider literary world had heard of suddenly held a place in Scottish memory it would never lose. They call it the Kilmarnock Edition. Most of the 612 copies are gone now, scattered or perished. A handful survive, and they are among the most valuable books printed in Britain. Kilmarnock has done much else since, but this is the moment that put it on the map.

## Marnock's Church

The town's name comes from the Gaelic cill, meaning cell or church, joined to the name of Saint Marnock, the Irish missionary who tradition says founded a chapel here in the seventh century. The earliest mention of the town in surviving records comes from the fourteenth century, when the Boyd family came to prominence and built the keep of Dean Castle. By 1592 King James VI granted a charter to Thomas, Lord Boyd, raising Kilmarnock to a burgh of barony. At the beginning of the sixteenth century the town was described as a large village of great repair, with nearby Kilmaurs the larger of the two. Within a hundred years Kilmarnock had overtaken its neighbour and was challenging the royal burghs of Ayr and Irvine for supremacy in Ayrshire.

## The Industrial Century

Victorian Kilmarnock turned cottage crafts into national industries. Leather makers expanded into shoes, and by 1837 local cobblers were producing more than 2,400 pairs annually. George Clark's firm began exporting to Brazil in 1840, often using cargo space on ships already carrying Kilmarnock whisky, and the two products became staples in Brazilian markets. Clark's Saxone Shoes had forty premises in Brazil by 1900. Charlotte Maria Gardiner, half-aunt of the 4th Earl of Kilmarnock, brought carpet makers north from Dalkeith, and BMK Carpets, founded in 1908, became Kilmarnock's main employer until it closed in 2005. Glenfield and Kennedy grew into the largest hydraulic engineering firm in the British Empire. And the Johnnie Walker name, born in 1820 from a teetotal grocer's whisky-blending sideline, became the best-selling Scotch in the world.

## Two Nobel Laureates

Kilmarnock Academy is one of a small number of UK schools, and was the first in Scotland, to have educated two Nobel laureates. Sir Alexander Fleming, born in 1881 in nearby Darvel, attended the Academy before going on to discover lysozyme and then penicillin, sharing the 1945 Nobel Prize in Medicine with Florey and Chain. John Boyd Orr, born in 1880, made his name in protein metabolism and nutrition before turning to national and global food policy, winning the 1949 Nobel Peace Prize for his work as first Director-General of the UN Food and Agriculture Organization. Other Kilmarnock connections include James Shaw, Lord Mayor of London in 1805, and the band Biffy Clyro, formed by classmates who grew up here.

## The Bottling Plant Closes

Johnnie Walker had bottled in Kilmarnock for 190 years. In September 2009, Diageo confirmed that the Hill Street plant, opened in 1956 as the largest bottling plant in the world, would close despite local protests. First Minister Alex Salmond, local MPs and MSPs joined the campaign, but the closure went ahead. Production ceased in March 2012 after 192 years. The buildings were demolished and the site now houses the Kilmarnock campus of Ayrshire College and the HALO Urban Regeneration park. Bottling moved to a new Diageo plant at Leven in Fife. Kilmarnock had lost Saxone Shoes, BMK Carpets, the Massey-Harris combine harvester factory that closed in 1980, and now its whisky. Brodie Engineering still builds and maintains locomotives in town, the lineal successor to Andrew Barclay Sons and Co. The lights have not all gone out.

## Music, Theatre and Football

Scottish duo The Proclaimers titled a track "The Joyful Kilmarnock Blues" on their 1987 debut album. The Sweet's "The Ballroom Blitz," a global hit, was inspired by an incident in 1973 at the Grand Hall when the band were driven from the stage by a barrage of thrown bottles. The song reached the top ten on both the UK Singles Chart and the Billboard Hot 100. Kilmarnock F.C., formed before the Scottish Football Association even existed, is one of the SFA's eight founding members from 1873 and the oldest professional football club in Scotland. Their 2-0 defeat against Renton on 18 October 1873 in the inaugural Scottish Cup is thought to be the first match ever played in the competition. In 2015 Kilmarnock was named Scotland's Most Improved Town at the Scottish Urban Regeneration Forum awards after a £43 million investment in the town centre.

## Flight Context

Kilmarnock sits at 55.61°N, 4.49°W in East Ayrshire. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000-4,500 feet. The Kilmarnock railway viaduct and the M77 corridor are the clearest visual markers in approach. Nearest airports: Glasgow Prestwick (EGPK) about 10 nm southwest and Glasgow (EGPF) about 22 nm north. The Irvine Valley extends east, with Loudoun Hill rising 1,037 feet near the A71 as a navigational feature.
