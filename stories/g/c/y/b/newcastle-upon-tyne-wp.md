# Newcastle upon Tyne

The Tyneside city whose international ties reach from Atlanta to Bergen to Haifa, and whose sister-city ledger stretches back to a 1948 agreement with Gelsenkirchen.

**Location:** 54.9778, -1.6133

**Categories:** City, International, Diplomatic, Cultural, Historical

## Introduction

Three years after the end of the Second World War, in 1948, Newcastle signed a twinning agreement with the German city of Gelsenkirchen. Both places had bombed each other's coal-and-steel hinterlands flat. Both were rebuilding from the same rubble of a continent. The gesture was small but pointed: the first formal civic friendship Newcastle ever made was with a former enemy, before the Federal Republic of Germany even officially existed. Seventy-eight years later that twin sits beside a network that stretches from Atlanta to Bergen to Nancy to Haifa.

## A Geography of Friendship

Newcastle's twin towns map the city's particular sense of place. Newcastle, Australia, the coal port north of Sydney, shares both name and industrial DNA. Atlanta, Georgia became a twin in 1977, an unusual transatlantic pairing for a British provincial city. Bergen in Norway followed in 1968, linked by the North Sea ferry route that ran from the Port of Tyne until DFDS discontinued the service in 2008. Nancy in France signed in 1954. Groningen in the Netherlands joined the list later. Haifa in Israel rounds out the formal twins. The agreement with Taiyuan, China, signed in 1985, was unilaterally terminated by Newcastle in 2022, a rare ending to a sister-city relationship that reflected wider deterioration in Sino-British relations.

## Cities Named Newcastle

In 1998 the city hosted a summit of every place in the world called Newcastle. The list reads like a quirky atlas. Neuburg an der Donau in Germany, Neuchatel in Switzerland, Neufchateau in the Vosges of France, three American New Castles in Delaware, Indiana and Pennsylvania, Newcastle-under-Lyme in Staffordshire, Newcastle in KwaZulu-Natal in South Africa, and Shinshiro in Japan, whose name translates to New Castle. Friendship agreements followed with all of them. The city also keeps a special cooperation agreement with Malmo in Sweden, signed in 2003, and a friendship pact with Little Rock, Arkansas. The list is a reminder that Newcastle's outward gaze is not a recent thing. A Tyneside town that once shipped coal across the North Sea sees nothing strange about adopting cousins on every continent.

## The Consular Quarter

Beyond the twinning ledger, Newcastle hosts consular representation for ten countries: Denmark, Finland, Romania, Belgium, France, Germany, Iceland, Italy, Norway and Sweden. The list looks like a North Sea map drawn from the Tyne outward. Most of those flags reflect old maritime trade routes that connected Tyneside to the Baltic, the Low Countries and Scandinavia. Several reflect the diaspora of post-war engineering recruitment, particularly the Italians who came to work in the heavy industries in the 1950s and 1960s. The cluster is far larger than a city of three hundred thousand people would typically warrant, and it reflects the way Tyneside has always thought of itself as a port that happens to have a city behind it, rather than the other way around.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 54.98 N, 1.61 W on the north bank of the Tyne. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000 to 6,000 feet for a sense of the conurbation. From altitude the Tyne corridor between Newcastle and Gateshead is unmistakable, with seven bridges spanning the river in less than two kilometres. The city centre sits north of the river around Grey Street and Grainger Town. The Port of Tyne, where the DFDS ferries to IJmuiden dock, lies east toward the river mouth at Tynemouth. Nearest ICAO airport Newcastle International (EGNT) is 4 nautical miles north-west. Durham Tees Valley (EGNV) is 26 nautical miles south.
