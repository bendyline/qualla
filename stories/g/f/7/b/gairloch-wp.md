# Gairloch

The Wester Ross village whose lairds refused to evict a single tenant during the Highland Clearances.

**Location:** 57.7280, -5.6910

**Categories:** villages, scotland, wester-ross, highland-history, gaelic-culture

## Introduction

Sir Hector Mackenzie of Gairloch let his estate run at a loss rather than evict a single tenant during the Highland Clearances. So did his sons, Sir Francis and Dr John Mackenzie. Through the worst of the nineteenth century, as landlords across the Highlands were turning crofters off ancestral ground to make room for sheep, the Mackenzies of Gairloch held the line. Word travelled. Evicted Highlanders from other estates came here to start again, and the parish thickened with refugees of a kind the history books rarely name. That is why Gairloch, with a population of 950 today, still has a parish at all.

## Three Settlements, One Name

Gairloch is less a village than a loose cluster of three, scattered along the shores of Loch Gairloch where the A832 from Kinlochewe meets the coast. The Harbour and Charlestown sit at the south end, with the small commercial pier and the golf course on the headland. Achtercairn, roughly a mile north at the road junction with the B8021, contains most of the civic infrastructure: the police station, the high school, the leisure centre, the museum, and the small supermarket. Strath, further west along the coastal road, holds the third cluster of houses and shops. The parish extends much further, taking in the villages of Poolewe, Kinlochewe, and Aultbea, but Gairloch proper is these three knots of buildings strung along the loch.

## The Mackenzies Who Stayed

The Mackenzies of Gairloch acquired these lands in the late fifteenth century when Hector Roy Mackenzie, who died in 1528, made them his family seat. The clan house at Flowerdale, in a sheltered glen behind the village, sits in a microclimate warm enough that Christina Byam Shaw recalled in her memoir Pigeonholes of Memory that her father grew fruiting peaches outdoors. During the nineteenth-century Clearances, Sir Hector Mackenzie and his sons Sir Francis and Dr John refused to evict any of their tenants, even as their estate ran into the red. Evicted Highlanders from neighbouring estates moved here for that reason. Osgood Mackenzie, of the same family, created the Inverewe Garden at Poolewe out of the bare rock of the coast, building one of the most celebrated horticultural projects in Britain on land most people would have called impossible. Today the estate has begun developing micro-hydroelectric schemes; in 2024, however, it obstructed walker access to the popular Cherry Tree Hill path, a reminder that the politics of land here are still very much alive.

## The Gairloch Bard

Uilleam Ros, William Ross, was the parish schoolmaster at Gairloch until his early death in 1791 at the age of twenty-nine. He is one of the most loved Scottish Gaelic poets of his generation, known simply as the Gairloch bard. His 1783 poem Moladh Gheàrrloch, 'In Praise of Gairloch,' includes a description of a New Year's Day shinty match played on Big Sand beach at low tide, which the Gaelic scholar Ronald Black has called 'as succinct a description as we have of the great festive shinty matches of the past.' Ross died young of tuberculosis, complicated by a broken heart over an unrequited love whose name the tradition still preserves. He is buried at Gairloch, and his poetry is still read aloud at Gaelic ceilidhs across the West Highlands.

## Where Stardust Was Filmed

The peculiar combination of coastline, mountain, and changing light has drawn filmmakers to Gairloch for decades. The 1955 educational film Crofter Boy was shot here. The 1990s Channel 4 documentary Last Postbus to Gairloch followed the minivan that doubled as mail and passenger service for the outlying communities. In recent years the area has been a location for the 2007 fantasy film Stardust, the 2014 comedy What We Did on Our Holiday, and the 2022 prehistoric horror Out of Darkness. Footballer Harold Davis, the Rangers and Scotland international who survived being seriously wounded in the Korean War, lived out his retirement years here running a hotel. Australian science-fiction writer George McIver was born in Gairloch and emigrated young. The Pictish stone with its salmon carving, found in 1880 and now displayed in the Gairloch Museum, is the westernmost known example of its kind, a reminder that people have been finding reasons to come to this particular stretch of coast for at least fifteen hundred years.

## Flight Context

Gairloch lies at 57.73 N, 5.69 W at the head of Loch Gairloch in Wester Ross, north-west Scottish Highlands. The three settlement clusters (Harbour/Charlestown, Achtercairn, Strath) thread along the A832 and B8021 near the loch shore. Visible from the air as a sheltered inlet between steep brown moorland; Beinn Eighe and the Torridon range stand 10 nm south-east. Nearest airfields: Plockton private grass strip 30 nm south, Inverness (EGPE) 60 nm east-southeast, Stornoway (EGPO) 50 nm northwest. Weather is typical maritime Highland: fast-moving fronts, frequent low cloud on the high ground.
