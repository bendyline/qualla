# North Coast 500

A 516-mile scenic route around the north Highlands of Scotland, launched in 2015 and quickly reshaping the economies and traffic patterns of every village it touches.

**Location:** 57.9700, -4.6000

**Categories:** scenic-route, scottish-highlands, road-trip, coastal, tourism, endurance

## Introduction

In August 2015 the cyclist Mark Beaumont rode the North Coast 500 in 37 hours and 58 minutes. Seven years later he reclaimed the record he had lost in the meantime, finishing the same loop in 28 hours and 35 minutes. For most people that 516-mile circuit takes about a week. The fact that anyone treats it as a stopwatch event tells you something about how thoroughly Scotland's northern coastline has been reinvented as a destination in the decade since the North Coast 500 was launched.

## How a Road Became a Brand

The North Coast 500 was launched in March 2015 by the Tourism Project Board of the North Highland Initiative, supported by Visit Scotland and Highlands and Islands Enterprise. The route itself was not new. Inverness has been the natural hub for a loop tour of Caithness, Sutherland, and Ross-shire for as long as the roads have existed. What was new was the unified marketing: a single brand, a single number, a single 516-mile story that could be pitched to international travellers who had never heard of Lochinver or Durness but would now consider a holiday wish-list item called Scotland's Route 66. Within months of launch, Now Travel Magazine named the route fifth in its top five coastal drives in the world.

## Clockwise from Inverness Castle

The official starting line is the baronial revival pile that is Inverness Castle, a nineteenth-century structure that looks more hotel than fortress. From there the clockwise route runs up to Muir of Ord and out to the west coast through Applecross, climbing the single-track Bealach na Ba pass with its 2,054-foot summit. Then come Gairloch, the bustling little port of Ullapool, and the empty stretches above Scourie and Durness. The road turns east along Scotland's north coast past the Castle of Mey, the late Queen Mother's summer home, before reaching Thurso, John o' Groats, and Wick. The eastern leg drops south past Dunrobin Castle - the seat of the Duke of Sutherland - and Dingwall, then closes the loop at Muir of Ord and Inverness. The route follows portions of the North Highland Way, a long-distance walking trail.

## The Endurance Cult

Almost from the moment the brand existed, the NC500 became a target for endurance athletes who treat 516 miles of winding two-lane road as a challenge worthy of the stopwatch. Beaumont set the original cycling record in August 2015 in 37 hours and 58 minutes; James McCallum and others trimmed the time over subsequent years; Beaumont reclaimed the cycling record in September 2022 at 28 hours and 35 minutes. The running record was set by Luke Ivory in July 2021 at 5 days, 23 hours, 4 minutes, and 17 seconds. William Sichel had completed the first successful run of the route in April 2019, taking 8 days, 19 hours, 7 minutes, and 7 seconds. None of these times include sleep in any conventional sense.

## The Costs of Success

The marketing worked, perhaps too well. Visitor numbers rose; many small communities saw real economic benefit, though much of the gain accrued to the larger towns rather than the tiniest villages. On the single-track stretches of the western coast, drivers unfamiliar with passing-place etiquette caused convoys, blockages, and damaged verges. Speeding and excessively slow driving both became hazards. Local journey times tripled in peak season. In 2016, the first full year of NC500 operation, deaths and serious accidents on the main roads of the route rose 45 per cent compared with 2014. On four separate occasions in 2018, tacks were scattered across the road, although the motive was never established. By 2021, reports of dirty camping and strained infrastructure were prompting talk of the route as a victim of its own success. In December 2025, NC500 Ltd, the company that promotes the route, commissioned a full economic and environmental impact analysis to take stock of what the brand had done to the country it celebrated.

## Imitators and Heirs

The NC500's success inspired imitators. The Heart 200, a shorter route through the Central Highlands and Perthshire, launched a few years later. The South West Coastal 300 loops around Dumfries and Galloway and southern Ayrshire. Whether any of them will reshape their regions as thoroughly as the NC500 reshaped the Highlands is an open question. What is no longer in question is that a road which had quietly served the north of Scotland for a century turned out, when given a name and a hashtag, to be one of the most powerful tourism brands the United Kingdom has produced this century. The road was always beautiful. The challenge now is keeping it that way.

## Flight Context

The NC500 loops Scotland's northernmost mainland, centred on Inverness Airport (EGPE) at 57.54 N, 4.05 W where the route begins and ends. Wick John o' Groats Airport (EGPC) sits near the northeast corner at 58.46 N, 3.09 W; Stornoway (EGPO) on Lewis at 58.21 N, 6.33 W. Recommended viewing 3,000-6,000 ft AGL to keep both the coastline and the inland ranges - Torridon, Assynt, the Flow Country - in frame. Expect rapid coastal weather changes and orographic cloud over the western mountains. The Bealach na Ba pass at 2,054 ft is a recognizable terrain feature on the Applecross peninsula.
