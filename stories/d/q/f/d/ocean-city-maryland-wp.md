# Ocean City, Maryland

The Ladies' Resort to the Ocean became Maryland's biggest summer city - and the White Marlin Capital of the World - after a hurricane cut its harbor in 1933.

**Location:** 38.3914, -75.0694

**Categories:** Ocean City, Maryland, Populated places established in 1875, Cities in Maryland, Cities in Worcester County, Maryland, Beaches of Maryland, Resorts in Maryland

## Introduction

Before 1870, the place did not yet have a name. People called it 'The Ladies' Resort to the Ocean' - a label that captured both the social class of the early visitors and the nineteenth-century assumption that men did not need a beach in particular. Isaac Coffin built the first paying cottage in 1869. Five years later the developers carved 250 lots from the spit and a corporation sold 4,000 shares at $25 apiece to underwrite the new resort. The Atlantic Hotel opened on July 4, 1875, with 400 rooms, billiards, dancing, and the boast of being the northernmost attraction in town - because there was no town further north. The boast lasted exactly long enough for the next hotel to open.

## Railroad and Inlet, 1878-1933

The Wicomico and Pocomoke Railroad reached the Sinepuxent Bay shore opposite Ocean City in 1878. By 1881 the Baltimore, Chesapeake and Atlantic Railroad had completed a bridge across the bay and ran trains directly into a downtown station, hauling vacationers in from Baltimore and Philadelphia and hauling back fresh fish for the urban markets. The Beach Patrol was formed in 1930. Then came the hurricane of August 23, 1933 - a Category 1 storm that sat over the lower Eastern Shore for two days, building up bay water until it cut a 50-foot-wide channel from Sinepuxent Bay through to the Atlantic at the south end of town. The same storm destroyed the train bridge across the bay. A second hurricane in September widened the channel to over 500 feet. The Army Corps of Engineers, recognizing the harbor that businessmen had been petitioning to dig for decades, stabilized the inlet permanently with jetties. The railroad never came back. The inlet became Ocean City's defining feature. The town traded its train station for a deepwater harbor and never really looked back.

## The Chesapeake Bay Bridge and the Boom

Two infrastructure projects on the western shore reshaped Ocean City. The Chesapeake Bay Bridge opened in 1952, replacing the ferry crossing from Annapolis with a four-lane span that put Baltimore and Washington within three hours of the beach. In 1964 the Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel opened the southern Delmarva to Norfolk, drawing Virginia traffic into the same circuit. The two bridges turned Ocean City from a Lower Shore summer destination into one of the largest beach resorts on the East Coast. By the 1970s, developers had built more than 15,000 condominium units along Coastal Highway. Then the beach started shrinking. By the late 1980s the shoreline had migrated west far enough that the Ash Wednesday Storm of 1962, Hurricane Gloria in 1985, and a chain of nor'easters had eaten the front line of cottages. The first major beach replenishment program began after Gloria; another major project followed in 2002. The current beach is partly imported sand pumped in from offshore dredge sites. Maintaining the look of the Ocean City that the 1970s tourists remember is now a multimillion-dollar engineering exercise.

## The White Marlin Capital

Ocean City calls itself the White Marlin Capital of the World. The claim is grounded: the Atlantic canyons just offshore - the Washington, Norfolk, and Baltimore canyons in particular - hold one of the most reliable concentrations of pelagic billfish on the East Coast. Every August, the White Marlin Open draws hundreds of boats and thousands of contestants to a five-day fishing tournament where the largest qualifying billfish can win the boat captain several million dollars in prize money. The tournament has grown into the largest billfish tournament in the world by purse. In 2016, a controversial white marlin disqualification triggered a federal lawsuit when the tournament ruled the winning boat had begun fishing before the official start time, violating tournament rules. The court upheld the disqualification, and the disputed $2.8 million purse was redistributed among the remaining eligible finishers. The fishing fleet runs out of the inlet that the 1933 hurricane carved, on boats whose insurance premiums alone exceed the cost of a year of college tuition.

## The Beach Patrol and the Boardwalk's Storms

The Ocean City Boardwalk runs about three miles from the Inlet at South 2nd Street north to 27th Street. The current length is the post-1962 reconstruction; the original 1902 boardwalk was extended and rebuilt several times. Hurricane Gloria in 1985 hit the boardwalk with 89-mph winds and forced both repairs and the addition of a concrete sea wall. Hurricane Sandy in 2012 destroyed about half the boardwalk; rebuilding took eighteen months. The Beach Patrol, founded in 1930 in partnership with the Coast Guard, now numbers over a hundred lifeguards in the summer season - the busiest blocks have a guard every 50 yards. Trimper's Rides, founded in 1893 as the Windsor Resort, holds the 1912 Herschell-Spillman carousel that the National Carousel Association named the historic carousel of 1996. The Ocean Bowl Skatepark, opened in June 1976, was the first East Coast skatepark and is the longest continuously-running municipal skatepark in the country. Each of these features survives because someone keeps rebuilding it after the next storm.

## The Numbers That Don't Match

Ocean City had 6,844 year-round residents in the 2020 census. The 2010 numbers showed 30,119 housing units, with about 26,000 of those vacant for most of the year. The median age of the year-round population is 54.2. The percentage of households containing children: 11.1. These are not the numbers of a beach town in the summer brochure. They describe a year-round community that is older, smaller, and quieter than the boardwalk crowds suggest - retirees, the people who work the hotels, the police and firefighters, the year-round restaurants, the convention center staff. On a peak summer weekend, the city becomes the second-most-populated municipality in Maryland, behind only Baltimore. On a February Tuesday, it is the size of a small Eastern Shore town. The Ocean City Fire Department has 200 volunteer members and 100 career members. The Police Department has 105 full-time officers and 100 to 110 seasonal officers. The summer staff almost doubles the city's police force. The off-season city is the real city. The summer city is the show.

## Flight Context

Ocean City stretches along the Atlantic from 38.32 degrees north (the inlet) to 38.46 degrees north (the Delaware border). The barrier island is easy to identify from altitude - high-rise development pressed against a narrow east-west axis, with Sinepuxent and Assawoman Bays clearly visible to the west. Ocean City Municipal (KOXB) sits 4 nautical miles southwest of downtown on the mainland. Salisbury Regional (KSBY) is 24 west. Watch for active Restricted Areas near Wallops Flight Facility 35 nautical miles south during launches. The Inlet at the south end of the island is unmistakable - the developed shore ends and wild Assateague Island begins immediately south. Pattern altitude of 1,500 feet AGL gives a clean view. Sport-fishing fleet movement is dense during white marlin season in August.
