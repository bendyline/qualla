# Greenville, North Carolina

A tobacco town on the Tar River that grew into eastern North Carolina's medical, university, and increasingly industrial hub.

**Location:** 35.6017, -77.3725

**Categories:** city, north-carolina, tobacco-history, university-town, medical-hub, coastal-plain

## Introduction

Locals call it G-Vegas, mostly in affection and partly in irony - a college-town nickname for a place that is decidedly not Las Vegas but does have, by eastern North Carolina standards, an unusual amount going on. Pitt Memorial Hospital became ECU Health Medical Center, which became the only Level I trauma center east of Raleigh and one of five academic medical centers in the state. The Brody School of Medicine moved its students through clinical rotations there. The university grew. The tobacco warehouses around Dickinson Avenue emptied out, then partially refilled with breweries and small businesses. And in the past few years the world's largest hammock manufacturer and a YouTuber named MrBeast both decided that the same flat coastal-plain city was the right place to build.

## Tobacco Roots

Greenville was founded in 1774 on the south bank of the Tar River, deep in Pitt County, in territory that nineteenth-century farmers turned into one of the most productive bright-leaf tobacco regions in the South. The Greenville Tobacco Warehouse Historic District still preserves the long brick buildings where the auction trade once happened, and Dickinson Avenue carries the bones of that industrial past. The Voice of America Greenville Transmitting Station, built outside town in the 1960s, sent shortwave broadcasts across the Atlantic toward Latin America, the Caribbean, and Africa - three antenna farms forming a 19-mile equilateral triangle. At the height of the Cold War, it was the largest international shortwave broadcasting facility in the United States. Most of it has now been decommissioned, but the cleared antenna fields are still visible in satellite imagery.

## The Medical District

Drive west from downtown along Stantonsburg Road and the city changes. Apartment complexes give way to brick medical office buildings, parking decks, and the sprawl of ECU Health Medical Center - 974 licensed beds, one of North Carolina's largest academic medical centers, and the teaching hospital for the Brody School of Medicine. Over 1,700 licensed providers work there. The campus serves more than 1.2 million people across eastern North Carolina, a region that historically struggled to recruit physicians at all. The East Carolina Heart Institute opened with a six-floor cardiac facility. The Eddie and Jo Allison Smith Tower, dedicated to oncology, opened in 2018 with a 418,000-square-foot cancer center. For thousands of patients across the rural east, the drive to Greenville is the drive to the only doctor who can help them.

## MrBeast and the New Economy

Greenville's economy has diversified well beyond tobacco. Thermo Fisher Scientific, Catalent, Avient, and Hyster-Yale all have operations here. The HammockSource - the world's largest hammock manufacturer - is headquartered nearby. In 2024, two international companies picked Greenville for their first U.S. manufacturing locations: Vietnam's Boviet Solar committed $294 million and 908 jobs to a solar panel plant, and Japan's Nipro Medical pledged $397 million and 232 jobs for a new healthcare manufacturing campus and U.S. headquarters. Jimmy Donaldson, who creates videos under the name MrBeast and is one of the most-watched people on the planet, runs his operation from Greenville. The same city that once shipped tobacco shipped to global media markets now ships to YouTube, and the people in the warehouses on the edge of town are building solar panels and medical devices.

## Tar River Mornings

On a clear morning in spring, kayakers push off into the slow brown current of the Tar River and paddle past the Town Common toward the railroad bridge. The South Tar River Greenway runs along the bank. ECU's Marching Pirates rehearse on the practice field. Little Leaguers play at Stallings Stadium at Elm Street Park, the home of a baseball program that has sent multiple teams to the Little League World Series - in 2017, pitchers from Greenville threw a combined perfect game followed by a no-hitter, the first U.S. team to throw back-to-back no-hitters in tournament history. The Greenville Museum of Art rotates exhibitions in a quiet building near downtown. And every Friday in summer, Uptown Greenville closes streets for Freeboot Friday - free concerts named for the Pirate mascot - and a small eastern North Carolina city briefly fills up.

## Flight Context

Located at 35.60°N, 77.37°W on the Tar River in Pitt County, about 75 miles east of Raleigh and 25 miles southeast of Tarboro. From cruise altitude the city reads as a dense grid south of the river, with ECU's campus and Dowdy-Ficklen Stadium prominent on the south side. Pitt-Greenville Airport (KPGV) sits 3 miles southwest of downtown with daily flights to Charlotte via PSA. Rocky Mount-Wilson Regional (KRWI) is 40 miles to the northwest, Kinston Regional Jetport (KISO) about 35 miles south, and Raleigh-Durham (KRDU) 75 miles west.
