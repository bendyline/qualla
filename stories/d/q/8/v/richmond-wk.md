# Richmond: Capital of the Confederacy, Rising from the Ashes

Virginia's capital where Thomas Jefferson designed the State Capitol, where the Confederacy made its stand, and where a city that burned as the Civil War ended reinvented itself as a craft beer capital with James River rapids running through downtown.

**Location:** 37.5421, -77.4334

**Categories:** virginia, richmond, city, capital, civil-war

## Introduction

On April 2, 1865, Confederate forces evacuated Richmond. Before leaving, they set fire to tobacco warehouses and munitions stores rather than let them fall to Union hands. The fires spread. When Union soldiers entered the next morning, much of Virginia's capital lay in ashes. Abraham Lincoln walked these streets days later, touring the fallen Confederate White House. Richmond has been burning and rebuilding ever since - literally and figuratively. The Confederate monuments that dominated Monument Avenue for 130 years came down between 2020 and 2021. The city that served as the Confederacy's capital now reckons with that history while reinventing itself around craft breweries in Scott's Addition, whitewater kayaking on the James River, and one of the most vibrant arts scenes in the South.

## Capital of the Confederacy

Richmond became the Confederate capital in May 1861, chosen for Virginia's industrial capacity and symbolic importance. The Virginia State Capitol - designed by Thomas Jefferson and modeled on a Roman temple in Nîmes - served as the Confederate Capitol. The Confederate White House, where Jefferson Davis lived, still stands a block away. Hollywood Cemetery holds 18,000 Confederate soldiers and Jefferson Davis himself. The 60-foot Robert E. Lee statue that dominated Monument Avenue from 1890 until its removal in 2021 was the last Confederate monument to fall, its pedestal now covered in protest graffiti that some consider public art. Richmond doesn't forget its Confederate history; it's learning how to remember it differently.

## Jefferson's Capitol

Thomas Jefferson designed the Virginia State Capitol while serving as ambassador to France, modeling it on the Maison Carrée in Nîmes - a Roman temple he considered the most perfect example of cubic architecture. Completed in 1788, it was the first neoclassical public building in America, influencing capitol design nationwide. The building served as both Virginia's and the Confederacy's capitol; it's where Robert E. Lee accepted command of Virginia's forces. A life-sized Houdon sculpture of George Washington stands in the rotunda - the only statue Washington posed for in life. The building remains the working seat of Virginia's government, the oldest legislative body in the Americas (the House of Burgesses dates to 1619).

## The James River Through Town

The James River cuts through Richmond with Class III and IV rapids - the only urban whitewater on the East Coast. Where other cities built their waterfronts for commerce or condos, Richmond kept its rocky riverbed wild. Belle Isle, a former Civil War prison camp in the middle of the river, is now a park accessible by footbridge. Kayakers and tubers run the rapids downtown while office workers watch from their windows. The James River Park System provides 550 acres of urban wilderness along both banks. The Texas Beach section offers swimming holes; the Pipeline Trail runs along a pipeline above the rapids. Richmond turned its industrial river into an outdoor recreation hub.

## Craft Beer Capital

Scott's Addition was a decaying industrial neighborhood until breweries discovered its warehouses. Now the district holds a dozen alcohol producers within walking distance: Ardent Craft Ales, The Veil Brewing Company, Vasen Brewing, cideries, meaderies, and a distillery. The concentration rivals any craft beverage district in America. Beyond Scott's Addition, breweries proliferate throughout the city - The Answer, Triple Crossing, Hardywood. Richmond has become one of the top craft beer destinations in the country, its industrial spaces converted to tap rooms where the art of fermentation replaced manufacturing. The First Friday gallery crawl connects breweries and art spaces across multiple neighborhoods.

## Old Dominion Capital

Richmond International Airport (RIC) serves the city with regional connections; Washington's airports are 100 miles north. I-95 runs through town, connecting Richmond to the Northeast Corridor. Amtrak serves two stations with frequent service to Washington, New York, and points south. The city spreads from the James River north and west, historic neighborhoods like the Fan District and Church Hill surrounding a revitalized downtown. From altitude, Richmond appears as a city at the fall line of the James River - the white dome of the State Capitol visible, the river rapids cutting through downtown, Monument Avenue's grassy median extending west. What appears from the air as Virginia's capital was the Confederacy's capital, burned in 1865, and still reinventing itself.
