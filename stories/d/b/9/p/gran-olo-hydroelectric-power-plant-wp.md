# Gran Olo Hydroelectric Power Plant

A tiny hydroelectric plant on a Suriname rapid that took a dozen years and three transformers to start delivering a handful of kilowatts to two jungle villages.

**Location:** 4.1258, -54.6353

**Categories:** Energy Infrastructure, Suriname, Hydroelectric, Amazon Interior

## Introduction

The announcement came in October 2005, and in most places an announcement like that would mean the lights would come on inside two years. The Gran Olo rapids sit deep in Suriname's interior, on the Tapanahony River, and deep interior Suriname does not move on most places' schedules. The mini-hydro plant announced that month would take almost twelve years to produce its first watt for the villages of Puketi and Futupasi. When it finally did, in February 2017, it came online at 35 kilowatts - roughly enough to run a small restaurant back in Paramaribo.

## The River That Draws the Line

The Tapanahony River carves through one of the least-traveled corners of the Amazon. Near the village of Puketi, the water breaks into a set of rapids the Saramaccan locals call Gran Olo - the great fall. No road reaches this place. Supplies arrive by pirogue or by small plane to a grass strip. From 1981 to 1987, a 50-kilowatt micro-hydro plant in the village hummed along quietly, then fell silent. The machinery rusted. Nobody made it back in to fix it until 1995, and even then the effort fizzled. Electricity, in this part of the country, has a way of arriving, leaving, and not returning for a while.

## A Professor's Proposal

In 2003 and 2004, a delegation from the Anton de Kom University of Suriname, led by professor Sieuwnath Naipal, made the long trip upriver. Rather than revive the old facility, they proposed something more ambitious: a new plant at the Gran Olo rapids themselves, designed for 300 kilowatts across three turbines. The Ministry of Regional Development approved the plan. A foundation called Stichting Fonds Ontwikkeling Binnenland put up 325,000 euros. The design called for a modest hydraulic head of three meters, with flow rates between 15 and 50 cubic meters per second - nothing that would make a commercial engineer break a sweat. In theory, this was straightforward civil works.

## What the Interior Does to Schedules

Construction started in late 2005. The 2006 floods arrived almost immediately, inundating the interior and drowning the site. Work resumed in 2007, but the contractor had never built a hydro plant before, and the site was accessible only by river. The first turbine, ordered from Germany, arrived in 2011. The powerhouse was finished and tested in 2012. And then nothing happened, because nobody had strung the overhead line from the plant to the villages. When the transformer finally showed up in 2014, it was sized for all three turbines that had been promised but never installed. A smaller transformer had to be ordered. The villagers of Puketi and Futupasi had now waited nine years.

## Thirty-Five Kilowatts

February 2017 brought the switch-on. The single operating turbine produces about 35 kilowatts, a fraction of what was planned and a fraction of what the region needs. For households in Puketi and Futupasi, though, it meant the difference between generator fumes and a lamp that turns on when you flip a switch. The high-capacity transformer sits in storage, waiting for the day two more turbines arrive and the grid finally stretches to the other villages along the Tapanahony. In a country where 90 percent of electricity already comes from the massive Afobaka dam to the north, Gran Olo is a footnote. For the few hundred people it serves, it is the whole point.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.126°N, 54.635°W, on the Tapanahony River in southeastern Suriname. The site sits deep in rainforest at the Gran Olo rapids, roughly 300 km south of Paramaribo. Recommended viewing altitude 4,000-6,000 ft for river detail; the site is unmarked from the air. Nearest airport of significance: Paramaribo-Zanderij (SMJP / PBM) far to the north. No controlled airspace in the immediate area; VFR over unbroken canopy.
