# Hôtel de Ville, Matoury

A Creole-style timber town hall from 1870 and a steel-and-glass replacement across the street tell the story of Matoury - a French Guianan airport suburb that grew up around the Port of Larivot.

**Location:** 4.8505, -52.3314

**Categories:** City Halls, Matoury, French Guiana, Creole Architecture, Civic Buildings

## Introduction

Stand on Rue Victor Ceide in the center of Matoury and you can see two town halls at once. The old one, on the south side of the street, went up in 1870 in the Creole style - timber-framed, four bays wide, covered verandas on both floors to keep the tropical rain at bay. The new one, facing it from the north side, is steel and glass. It was finished around 1980, when Matoury's population had outgrown anything wooden could hold. Nobody tore the old mairie down. It just changed jobs, becoming a bank, then a post office, then the police station, and finally the tourist office. In most French towns the old town hall and the new town hall are the same building. In Matoury they stare at each other across the street.

## The Village Called Tour-de-L'Isle

In the late 1860s, Matoury did not yet exist as a commune. The settlement was called Tour-de-L'Isle, a scattering of houses on the flat land just south of Cayenne where the Mahury River curls inland. Civic leaders decided the village needed three things: a church, a school, and a town hall. They picked a site on what is now Rue Victor Ceide - named for the same gold merchant whose house became the first mairie of Cayenne. The building they raised in 1870 was built in what the French colonies called the Creole style: a timber frame, a symmetrical frontage of four bays, and covered verandas on both floors. Sloping roofs projected out above the walls, designed to shed the enormous amounts of water a wet-season storm can dump in an afternoon. The outer bays on the upper floor were shuttered against the sun.

## The Commune and its Restoration

The commune of Matoury was officially established on 25 February 1891. The first town council, led by mayor Jules Eglantin, decided to restore the aging timber hall at a cost of 15,000 French francs. The work wrapped up around 1907. For another seventy years the Creole mairie handled municipal business - marriages, deeds, the slow paperwork of colonial and then departmental administration. When the building was finally retired from civic duties, it was passed down through a series of tenants that read like a short history of what a small French town needs: first a bank, then a post office, then the local police station, and finally the tourist office, which still occupies it.

## The Port and the Growth

The thing that changed Matoury was the Port of Larivot. The fishing and commercial port on the Mahury River, just west of the town, drew workers and their families into what had been a sleepy agricultural commune. Population pressure pushed the council to build something bigger, and the mayor who got it done was Raoul Roumillac. The site they chose was on the north side of Rue Victor Ceide - directly across from the old town hall. The new building, completed around 1980, is what the Wikipedia article calls the modern style: steel and glass, a frontage of seven bays, a glass doorway under a canopy in the center, plate-glass windows surmounted by an entablature on the first floor, and metal grills over the ground-floor openings for security. The bays are separated by steel columns supporting a projecting sloped roof - the same rain-management trick the 1870 building used, just rendered in steel instead of timber.

## A Soldier and a Statue

On 11 November 2023, Armistice Day, mayor Serge Smock unveiled a new monument in front of the town hall: a statue of Louis-Gustave Fayde, a local soldier who died in February 1917 serving with the 4th Colonial Infantry Regiment at Vaucluse during the First World War. Colonial regiments drew heavily from France's overseas territories, and Fayde is one of thousands of Guianans who traveled to the trenches of mainland Europe to fight in a war that was not, in any direct sense, about them. The statue faces the new mairie across the street from the old one - three moments in Matoury's history arranged in a line: a wooden colonial past, a steel present, and a bronze memorial to one man in between.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.850°N, 52.331°W, just south of Cayenne – Félix Eboué International Airport (SOCA / CAY) - the airport itself is inside the commune of Matoury, with its runway less than 7 km from the town hall. The town is essentially the airport's suburb, visible from the approach to runway 08/26. Recommended viewing altitude 2,000-3,000 ft. Mahury River estuary wraps around the east and south of the commune.
