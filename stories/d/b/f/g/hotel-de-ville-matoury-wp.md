# Hôtel de Ville, Matoury

A Creole-style timber town hall from 1870 and a steel-and-glass replacement across the street tell the story of Matoury - a French Guianan airport commune that grew with the Port of Larivot.

**Location:** 4.8505, -52.3314

**Categories:** City Halls, Matoury, French Guiana, Creole Architecture, Civic Buildings

## Introduction

Stand on Rue Victor Ceide in the center of Matoury and you can see two town halls at once. The old one, on the south side of the street, went up in 1870 in the Creole style - timber-framed, four bays wide, covered verandas on both floors to keep the tropical rain at bay. The new one, facing it from the north side, is steel and glass. It was finished around 1980, when Matoury's population had outgrown anything wooden could hold. Nobody tore the old mairie down. It just changed jobs, serving as a bank and a post office, then the police station, and then the tourist office. In Matoury the old and new town halls stare at each other across the street.

## The Village Called Tour-de-L'Isle

In the late 1860s, Matoury did not yet exist as a commune. The settlement was called Tour-de-L'Isle, a scattering of houses on the flat land just south of Cayenne. Civic leaders decided the village needed three things: a church, a school, and a town hall. They picked a site on the south side of what is now Rue Victor Ceide. The building they raised in 1870 was built in what the French colonies called the Creole style: a timber frame, a symmetrical frontage of four bays, and covered verandas on both floors. Sloping roofs projected out above the walls, designed to shed the enormous amounts of water a wet-season storm can dump in an afternoon. The outer bays on the upper floor had shuttered casement windows.

## The Commune and its Restoration

The commune of Matoury was officially established on 25 February 1891. The first town council, led by mayor Jules Eglantin, decided to restore the aging timber hall at a cost of 15,000 French francs. The work wrapped up around 1907. For another seventy years the Creole mairie handled municipal business - marriages, deeds, the slow paperwork of colonial and then departmental administration. When the building was finally retired from civic duties, it was passed down through a series of tenants that read like a short history of what a small French town needs: first a bank, then a post office, then the local police station, and then the tourist office.

## The Port and the Growth

The thing that changed Matoury was the Port of Larivot. The fishing port on the Cayenne River estuary, just west of the town, drew workers and their families into what had been a sleepy agricultural commune. Population pressure pushed the council to build something bigger, and the mayor who got it done was Raoul Roumillac. The site they chose was on the north side of Rue Victor Ceide - directly across from the old town hall. The new building, completed around 1980, is in the modern style: steel and glass, a frontage of seven bays, a glass doorway under a canopy in the center, plate-glass windows surmounted by an entablature on the first floor, and metal grills over the ground-floor openings for security. The bays are separated by steel columns supporting a projecting sloped roof - the same rain-management trick the 1870 building used, just rendered in steel instead of timber.

## A Soldier and a Statue

On 11 November 2022, Armistice Day, mayor Serge Smock unveiled a new monument on the forecourt of the town hall: a statue of Louis-Gustave Fayde, a soldier born in Matoury in 1891 who served with the 4th Colonial Infantry Regiment and died on 2 February 1917 in a temporary military hospital in the Vaucluse, in southern France. Colonial regiments drew heavily from France's overseas territories, and Fayde is one of many Guianans who crossed the Atlantic to serve in a war that was not, in any direct sense, about them. The statue stands at the entrance to the new mairie, across the street from the old one - three moments in Matoury's history: a wooden colonial past, a steel present, and a memorial to one man.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.850°N, 52.331°W, about 4.5 km northeast of Cayenne – Félix Eboué International Airport (SOCA / CAY) - the airport itself is inside the commune of Matoury. The town is essentially the airport's suburb, visible from the approach to runway 08/26. Recommended viewing altitude 2,000-3,000 ft. The Mahury River runs along the commune's eastern edge and the Cayenne River estuary along its west.
