# Hôtel de Ville, Cayenne

Cayenne's neoclassical city hall, completed in 1924 on Rue de Rémire, grew out of a gold merchant's house, a quartermaster's office, and a mayor's decision to build in brick.

**Location:** 4.9382, -52.3350

**Categories:** City Halls, Cayenne, French Guiana, Neoclassical Architecture, Civic Buildings

## Introduction

For more than a century the town hall of Cayenne moved around the block. It started in an eighteenth-century house that had been the home of a wealthy gold merchant named Victor Ceïde. It moved to an old quartermaster's office. It spent a spell in a building called Maison Vitalo on the corner of Rue Arago and Rue Barrat while the bricklayers worked. Only in 1924, under mayor Eugène Gober and the assistant engineer of public works Paul Amusant, did the neoclassical mairie you see today finally open its doors on Rue de Rémire - a clock above the center bay and iron railings on the balcony.

## The Gold Merchant's House

Maison Ceïde, the first town hall, stood on what is now Avenue du Général de Gaulle. It was timber-framed in the eighteenth-century Creole manner, and by 1842 it had evolved into a symmetrical composition of a main block with two square pavilions flanking a courtyard. A round-headed opening in the center allowed carts to pass through to the yard inside. The first floor's covered walkway jettied out over the pavement - a bit of local climate engineering that kept the rain off pedestrians without giving up space upstairs. After it stopped serving as the town hall, the house became a small shopping arcade, the Galerie des Trois-Fontaines, named after the fountains that in 1867 first brought fresh water to local people from a reservoir on Mount Cépérou. One of the fountains stood in the courtyard.

## The Quartermaster's Office

By the late nineteenth century, the Ceïde house was too small. The council moved a short distance southwest to the old quartermaster's office on Rue de Rémire. This was a handsome military building: an eleven-bay frontage with the central three bays recessed around a doorway, a mix of square-headed and round-headed windows, shuttered on the ground floor, with four dormers poking out of the attic. It served the town through the end of the 1800s and into the 1900s, though it was timber-framed, which in the climate of French Guiana is not a recipe for longevity.

## Mayor Gober's Brick

Population pressure at the start of the twentieth century pushed the council toward something more permanent. Mayor Eugène Gober wanted brick. The timber-framed town hall on Rue de Rémire came down. The council decamped to Maison Vitalo while construction proceeded. Paul Amusant, assistant engineer of public works, supervised the work - a neoclassical brick building finished with cement render and completed in 1924. The frontage runs seven bays wide. A segmental-headed doorway with stone surround and keystone marks the center, framed by casement windows on both floors. The central five bays are fronted by a balcony with iron railings. Above the doorway, a clock. At the roofline, two dormers. It is a confident building in a modest idiom, exactly the kind of public architecture France exported to its colonies in the 1920s, adapted to the heat and rain of Cayenne.

## Bureaux and Salle

Inside, the arrangement is as straightforward as the outside. The mayor's office - the Bureaux de Maire - occupies the ground floor. The council chamber, the Salle du Conseil, sits upstairs. This is where Cayenne's elected representatives debate the affairs of a city of about 63,000 people, the capital of France's only South American department. Since 2002 the building has carried a listing on the Inventaire général des monuments maintained by the French Ministry of Culture - not a historic-monument classification in the strict sense, but official recognition that this unassuming brick building is part of the architectural heritage France cares enough about to document.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.938°N, 52.335°W, on Rue de Rémire in central Cayenne. The city is identifiable from altitude by the fort-capped hill of Mount Cépérou and the dark estuary of the Mahury River to the east. Cayenne – Félix Eboué International Airport (SOCA / CAY) lies about 13 km south-southwest. The city hall stands in the old street grid just east of the fort hill.
