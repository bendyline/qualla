# Kourou

The French town where Europe's rockets launch from the same coast where thousands of would-be colonists died chasing the myth of an Amazonian El Dorado.

**Location:** 5.1583, -52.6428

**Categories:** town, french-guiana, historical, spaceport, commune

## Introduction

In 1763, France lost Canada. Searching for a consolation empire, the government advertised French Guiana as an El Dorado waiting on the far side of the Atlantic, and between ten and twelve thousand people believed them. Most of these settlers came ashore at Kourou, a small village ringed by marsh and rainforest. Within a year or two, thousands of the newcomers were dead - estimates run from about six thousand to nine thousand or more - killed by fevers and other diseases. The survivors fled to three islands offshore they renamed the Îles du Salut - Salvation Islands - because the ocean breeze kept the mosquitoes away. Two centuries later, Europe launches spacecraft from this same stretch of coast.

## Ringed by Hills, Cut by Rivers

Kourou sits some 60 kilometers by road northwest of Cayenne, where the Kourou River empties into the Atlantic. Four hills hem the town in: Carapa, Pariacabo, Café, and Lombard, with the Singes and Condamine mountains visible inland. The name Condamine comes from the 1744 visit of mathematician Charles Marie de La Condamine, on his way back from measuring a degree of the meridian near the equator in Peru. Three lakes sit within the city limits. Lake Bois Chaudat, the largest, draws canoers and kayakers on weekends. Lake Marie-Claire is small and still. Beyond the white-sand beaches, mangrove and dense rainforest absorb everything. Kourou had 24,824 residents in 2023. At the 2015 census about half had been born in French Guiana - Creoles, Kalina (Galibi) and Maroon communities among them - about a sixth in metropolitan France, and nearly a third abroad, mostly in Suriname, Haiti and Brazil.

## The Disaster of 1763

After the Treaty of Paris handed New France to Britain, the Duke of Choiseul launched an expedition to build a new colonial foundation in Guiana. The pitch promised land, gold, and reinvention. Ten to twelve thousand would-be settlers sailed south. Kourou, a small settlement surrounded by wetlands, had nothing close to the housing, sanitation, or food the newcomers required. Thousands - at least six thousand, by some estimates far more - died within a year or two from fevers the Europeans had no resistance to. The survivors crowded onto three small islands offshore, where constant winds blew the mosquitoes away. They renamed them the Îles du Salut. Previously they had been called the Îles du Diable, Devil's Islands, and one of the three - Île du Diable - still carries the old name. The Expedition's failure cemented French Guiana's reputation as Enfer Vert - Green Hell - a name the territory has never fully shaken.

## The Penal Colony

A century after the Expedition, the islands returned to darker purposes. In May 1852 the first convoy of convicts landed on the Îles du Salut. The Kourou Prison on the mainland, also part of the network, specialized in forced agricultural labor. The islands held political prisoners alongside France's toughest criminals. Captain Alfred Dreyfus - falsely convicted of treason in the antisemitic scandal that divided late nineteenth-century France - was held on Île du Diable from 1895 to 1899. The transport of new prisoners to Guiana was banned in 1938; Kourou's prison closed in 1946, and the last prisoners were released elsewhere in the colony by 1953. Most of the prison buildings were torn down to make way for the Hôtel des Roches. What remains includes the Dreyfus Tower, which once used semaphore signals to communicate with the islands, and the old prison bakery, still visible from a cul-de-sac.

## Europe's Spaceport

In 1965 the French space agency CNES founded the Guiana Space Centre just behind the town. The European Space Agency began using the facility in 1975. The location isn't accidental - at 5 degrees north, Kourou sits close enough to the equator that rockets launching eastward get a free speed boost from Earth's rotation. Since 1973 the 3rd Foreign Infantry Regiment of the French Foreign Legion has been based in the Forget neighbourhood, their mission to protect the launch complex. On launch days, portions of the town and the islands close to the public. In 2010 a large solar power plant opened on the Aubanèle Savannah outside town, at the time described as the largest in Overseas France. The rockets, the legionnaires, and the satellites have grafted a twenty-first-century economy onto a town that began as a Jesuit mission, collapsed as a failed colony, and served for nearly a century as part of France's penal colony.

## Daily Life at the Edge

Modern Kourou carries the weight of its geography. In the mid-2000s the town averaged two armed robberies a day, and shopkeepers of Chinese descent were particular targets. Residents marched against insecurity on 27 October 2006. A fire that same year in the Saramaka village by the river left 55 people homeless. The Legion clashed with Creole residents in 1985 and again in 2006. For those who live here, Kourou is not exotic. It's a working town where space launches and Sunday canoe trips upriver happen on the same calendar, where the equator is ordinary weather, and where the past - colonial, carceral, cosmic - is always close to the surface.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 5.158°N, 52.643°W. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000-6,000 feet for coastal approach, higher to see both town and Space Centre complex. Landmarks: Kourou River mouth, the four town hills (Carapa, Pariacabo, Café, Lombard), the Îles du Salut roughly 15 km offshore, and the Guiana Space Centre facilities extending west along the coast. Nearest airports: Kourou's own small airfield (SOOK) 2 km west of town; Cayenne - Félix Eboué (SOCA), about 49 km southeast. Weather: tropical, high humidity year-round, trade winds from the northeast; major rainy season December-July. Temperature range 24-31°C nearly constant.
