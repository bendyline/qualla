# Kourou

A small French town on the Atlantic coast of South America where Europe launches rockets from the same beaches that once held its most notorious prisoners.

**Location:** 5.1583, -52.6428

**Categories:** town, french-guiana, historical, coastal

## Introduction

The catamaran leaves the dock at Kourou and within an hour deposits you on an island where, a century ago, no one was supposed to leave. The Îles du Salut sit about 15 kilometers off this small French town on the South American coast, and the smallest of them, Devil's Island, once held France's political prisoners, Alfred Dreyfus among them. Today visitors hang hammocks on Île Royale, swim in water that convicts stared at but were forbidden to cross, and watch macaws startle from trees that grew up around the ruins. Back on the mainland, rockets climb into the equatorial sky. Kourou is where France keeps its longest reckoning with distance.

## Town Between Rivers and Rockets

Kourou itself is modest. The town is only about 2 kilometers across, most of it walkable on foot, with no real public transit system. The road from Cayenne runs through it, carrying taxis, rental cars, and an interurban bus that makes the trip from the capital in a little over an hour; shared minivans also run from Saint-Laurent on the Surinamese border. The place feels provisional at the edges, dense with palm trees and mangrove, but its center holds an old quarter with bars and a fish hall worth visiting even if you don't buy anything. Somewhere behind the town, hidden in the coastal forest, the launchpads wait.

## Markets and the Old Quarter

On Tuesday and Friday mornings the Place de la République fills with food stalls until around one in the afternoon. Fish is sold every morning except Sunday at the old fish hall. Saturday mornings bring a separate vegetable market along Avenue du Général De Gaulle, near the fish hall. The old part of town has bars for a drink when the sun goes down, some of them long popular with off-duty French Foreign Legion servicemen from the regiment based in town.

## Beaches and Carnival

The beaches here are genuinely good, and on summer evenings in July and August the town sets up a free open-air cinema on the sand. Carnival season runs from January into February or early March, the whole thing noisier and looser than you might expect from an overseas French commune. Street parades roll through on Sundays, and the nightlife thickens for the season.

## The Salvation Islands

The name is paradoxical: Îles du Salut, Salvation's Islands, the ones where salvation was impossible for those sent there. Between 1852 and 1953, France used this archipelago as a prison for political dissidents and hardened criminals. Captain Alfred Dreyfus spent four years in the 1890s on Île du Diable - Devil's Island - falsely convicted in one of France's most infamous miscarriages of justice. Catamarans and shuttles run from Kourou's harbor most days, weather and rocket launches permitting, and on weekends you should book ahead. Île Royale is where the boat lands; it has a hotel-restaurant where you can also hang a hammock, and a basic campsite. Île Saint-Joseph sits a short boat ride away. Île du Diable itself remains closed to visitors, which feels appropriate - some places haven't earned their visitors back.

## Wildlife at the Ruins

On Île Royale the forest has grown over and around the prison buildings, and monkeys chase each other through trees where guards once paced. Iguanas sun themselves on stone steps worn by generations of feet that were never allowed to leave. Sea turtles surface in the coves. Macaws flash color through the canopy. The contrast is strange and not quite resolvable - you can spend an afternoon swimming and napping in a place whose whole purpose was confinement and punishment. Humans abandoned their cruelty here. The wildlife moved in almost immediately.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 5.158°N, 52.643°W. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000-6,000 feet for the coastal approach showing both Kourou and the Îles du Salut offshore. Landmarks: the Kourou River mouth, the three islands of the Salut group roughly 15 km offshore, and to the west the Guiana Space Centre complex stretching along the coast. Nearest airports: Kourou's small airfield (SOOK) 2 km west of town; Cayenne - Félix Eboué (SOCA), about 49 km southeast; Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni (SOOM). Weather: tropical, humid, frequent afternoon rain during the December-to-July wet season; trade winds offshore keep the islands clear of mosquitoes.
