# Kourou

A small French town on the Atlantic coast of South America where Europe launches rockets from the same beaches that once held its most notorious prisoners.

**Location:** 5.1583, -52.6428

**Categories:** town, french-guiana, historical, coastal

## Introduction

The catamaran leaves the dock at Kourou and within an hour deposits you on an island where, a century ago, no one was supposed to leave. The Îles du Salut sit just off this small French town on the South American coast, and three of their rocky hectares once held some of France's most stubborn political prisoners. Today visitors hang hammocks where cells once stood, swim in water that convicts stared at but were forbidden to cross, and watch macaws startle from trees that grew up around the ruins. Back on the mainland, rockets climb into the equatorial sky. Kourou is where France keeps its longest reckoning with distance.

## Town Between Rivers and Rockets

Kourou itself is modest. The town is only about 2 kilometers across, most of it walkable on foot, with no real public transit system. The road from Cayenne runs through it, carrying taxis, rental cars, and TIG bus line 5 on the hour-long trip from the capital for ten euros. A shared minivan down from Saint-Laurent on the Surinamese border runs around 25 euros. The place feels provisional at the edges, dense with palm trees and mangrove, but its center holds an old quarter with bars and a fish hall worth visiting even if you don't buy anything. The air smells of salt and river silt. Somewhere behind the town, hidden in the coastal forest, the launchpads wait.

## Markets and the Old Quarter

Three mornings a week the Place de la République fills with food stalls until around one in the afternoon, vendors packing up as the heat becomes unbearable. Fish arrives daily except Sunday at the old fish hall, spread on ice in a building that smells exactly the way you'd expect. Saturdays bring a separate vegetable market along Avenue du Général De Gaulle, where piles of green cayenne peppers and tropical fruit you may never have seen catch the morning light. The old part of town holds l'Yguana café and Vieux Montmartre, places for a drink when the sun goes down. Bar des Sports, opposite the post house, draws French Foreign Legion servicemen off-duty. Bar le 13 has a big terrace and Belgian beers on tap.

## Beaches and Carnival

The beaches here are genuinely good, and on summer evenings in July and August the town sets up a free open-air cinema on the sand. February brings carnival, the whole thing noisier and looser than you might expect from an overseas French commune. Street parades roll through every Sunday, and the nightlife thickens for the season. For residents of Kourou, the mediatheque by the lake offers free internet for an hour at a time, a small civic perk in a town where many things cost more than they should.

## The Salvation Islands

The name is paradoxical: Îles du Salut, Salvation's Islands, the ones where salvation was impossible for those sent there. Between 1852 and 1953, France used this archipelago as a prison for political dissidents and hardened criminals. Captain Alfred Dreyfus spent four years in the 1890s on Île du Diable - Devil's Island - falsely convicted in one of France's most infamous miscarriages of justice. The catamaran from Kourou costs around 21 euros one-way, and on weekends you should book ahead. Île Royale is where the boat lands; there you can hang a hammock at the hotel restaurant for 10 euros or camp free near the shore. Île Saint-Joseph sits a short boat ride away for five euros more. Île du Diable itself remains closed to visitors, which feels appropriate - some places haven't earned their visitors back.

## Wildlife at the Ruins

On Île Royale the forest has grown over and around the prison buildings, and monkeys chase each other through trees where guards once paced. Iguanas sun themselves on stone steps worn by generations of feet that were never allowed to leave. Sea turtles surface in the coves. Macaws flash color through the canopy. The contrast is strange and not quite resolvable - you can spend an afternoon swimming and napping in a place whose whole purpose was confinement and punishment. Humans abandoned their cruelty here. The wildlife moved in almost immediately.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 5.158°N, 52.643°W. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000-6,000 feet for the coastal approach showing both Kourou and the Îles du Salut offshore. Landmarks: the Kourou River mouth, the three islands of the Salut group roughly 15 km offshore, and to the west the Guiana Space Centre complex stretching along the coast. Nearest airports: Cayenne - Félix Eboué (SOCA), Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni (SOOM). Weather: tropical, humid, frequent afternoon rain during the December-to-July wet season; trade winds offshore keep the islands clear of mosquitoes.
