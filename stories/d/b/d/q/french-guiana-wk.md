# French Guiana

A corner of the European Union tucked into the Amazon, where you pay for a cafe in euros, your passport gets checked on arrival, and the rainforest starts where the road ends.

**Location:** 4.0000, -53.0000

**Categories:** Regions, Travel guides, South America, France

## Introduction

Ask a traveller to point to the European Union on a map of South America and you will usually get a blank look. The answer is a rectangle of rainforest between Suriname and Brazil - 84,000 square kilometers of it, most covered in primary forest, cut by a single paved coastal road. Here euros buy baguettes at the market in Cayenne, Ariane rockets roar off a pad at Kourou and arc toward geostationary orbit, and you may pay €85 for a one-way taxi from the capital to the space centre. French Guiana is France, but not mainland France. It is Amazon, but not independent Amazon. It is, maybe more than anywhere else, a contradiction with customs booths.

## Arriving

Most travellers arrive by air, at Félix Eboué Airport just outside Cayenne, with daily Air France and Air Caraibes flights from Paris Orly. Others come from Martinique or Guadeloupe on connecting hops, or from neighbouring Suriname and Brazil on regional flights. From the United States expect transfers; there are no direct flights from North America. Two things trip people up. First, French Guiana is *not* part of the Schengen Area, so your passport is checked on arrival even if you flew in from metropolitan France - and a Schengen visa for Paris does not admit you to Cayenne. Second, a yellow fever vaccination certificate is mandatory for everyone aged one and over, regardless of citizenship or where you have been. Bring the little yellow card. Show it at the border.

## The Capital and the Coast

Cayenne is a small, relaxed capital with colonial architecture, a few museums, and one of the best markets in the Guianas at Place Victor Schoelcher. Tropical fruits stack beside fragrant spices; Vietnamese noodle soup simmers next to West African stews; the smells of the Caribbean, West Africa, Indochina and Brazil braid together into something that is recognizably its own. From Cayenne you can drive in either direction along the coast - west toward Kourou and the Guiana Space Centre, or east toward Régina and eventually the Brazilian border. Everything that is not the coast is forest, and the coast itself is a narrow ribbon of towns pinched between the mangroves and the rainforest. Remire-Montjoly has beaches. Ilet la Mère, a 30-minute boat ride off Dégrad des Cannes, is home to very social squirrel monkeys who will expect a careful welcome rather than bananas.

## Rockets and Ruins

Kourou, about 60 kilometers west of Cayenne, is where the European Space Agency launches from the Guiana Space Centre - one of the major spaceports closest to the equator, which gives it a physics advantage no site in Europe can match. Free tours run twice a day, and there is a museum for a few euros. Check the launch schedule before you go: watching an Ariane ignite at dusk, the sea beyond the gantry, is on most short lists of the world's finest travel experiences. A short boat ride offshore lies the other, stranger, face of French Guiana's history - the Îles du Salut, the Salvation Islands, better known collectively as Devil's Island. The penal colony that held men here from 1852 until the bagne closed in 1946 was notorious for disease, brutality and death. Today two of the three islands are open to visitors; some of the old prison buildings are museums, and the birds and the light and the sea are very beautiful. Devil's Island proper is closed. Visit the others with the men who were sent here in mind.

## Rainforest Country

The Guiana Amazonian Park covers 41 percent of French Guiana's territory and is the largest national park in the European Union. It is also, in practical terms, close to inaccessible - a place reached by small plane, dugout canoe, or patience. The more realistic first encounter with the forest is the drive from Cayenne to the Brazilian border at Saint-Georges-de-l'Oyapock, a road that mostly passes through uninhabited jungle. Or walk the Montabo Trail on Cayenne's outskirts, a short loop through a small patch of preserved rainforest and mangrove. Further afield the Trésor Nature Reserve, a two-hour drive from the capital, opens onto the marshes of the Kaw ecosystem - black caimans, scarlet ibis, and a heavier silence than the city ever manages. At Awala-Yalimapo in the northwest, Plage les Hattes has long been counted among the world's most important nesting beaches for giant leatherback turtles, though their numbers there have fallen sharply. Stand there at night during nesting season and you will understand scale in a new way.

## What to Eat, How to Stay, What to Know

The local kitchen is Creole and fierce. The capital shares its name with cayenne pepper, and the restaurants take the hint; ask for less heat and the chef will usually oblige. Try *bouillon d'awara* from the local palm fruit, *colombo* (a Creole curry) with pork or chicken, *pimentade* (fish in peppered lemon-tomato sauce), or *blaf de poisson* in court-bouillon. Drink ti-punch or the local rum; try wassaï (açaí) juice cold. Think twice about caiman and turtle if you see them on the menu - some of the species served are threatened. Hotels are expensive everywhere; the most adventurous - and cheapest - lodging is a hammock in a *carbet*, an open-walled shelter in the forest. Respect runs both ways: French Guianese are proud of being French, and also proud of their Creole culture; say "metropolitan France" rather than "France" when you mean Paris. Slavery and the penal colonies are sensitive subjects, handled best by listening. You came to a department of the European Union that is also part of the Amazon. Let it be both.

## Flight Context

French Guiana sits on the Atlantic coast of South America between roughly 2° and 6° N and 51° and 55° W. Main airport: Félix Eboué (SOCA / CAY) just outside Cayenne. Regional fields include Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni (SOOM), Saint-Georges-de-l'Oyapock (SOOG), and Maripasoula (SOOA). The Guiana Space Centre at Kourou creates temporary airspace restrictions during Ariane launches. From cruising altitude, the coast is a thin green and mangrove strip backed by a vast dark canopy of rainforest; the Maroni (west) and Oyapock (east) rivers mark the borders with Suriname and Brazil.
