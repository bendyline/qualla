# Rio Iratapuru Sustainable Development Reserve

Eight hundred thousand hectares of terra firme Amazon where jaguars still hunt, Brazil-nut gatherers still climb, and a perfume maker's bioprospecting project centres on a tree resin called breu branco.

**Location:** 0.3182, -52.7247

**Categories:** conservation, amazon, brazil, biodiversity, sustainable-development

## Introduction

Somewhere in a perfume bottle there may be a scent that started on a specific tree in a specific patch of Amazonian rainforest in southern Amapá. The tree is the Protium heptaphyllum, the scent is a white aromatic resin called breu branco, and the patch is the Rio Iratapuru Sustainable Development Reserve — 806,184 hectares of well-preserved terra firme forest where jaguars, giant otters, and giant anteaters still move through the understory. The reserve is one of the more successful experiments in what Brazil calls sustainable development reserves: places where the conservation of the ecosystem and the livelihood of the people living within it are meant to be the same project. Most of the time, more or less, it works.

## An Old Shield, a Young Law

The rocks under the reserve are more than two billion years old — part of the Guiana Shield, one of the most ancient exposed surfaces on the planet. The land is rugged, cut by streams into hills and plateaus, and covered by a forest that has had a very long time to develop its layers. The legal protection on top of this ancient geology is much newer. On 11 December 1997, Amapá state law 392 created the reserve, driven by Governor João Capiberibe and his Amapá Sustainable Development Program. The reserve was designed to support the Comaru Brazil nut cooperative and the five surrounding communities who asked to be part of it. The framework preceded Brazil's national policy on traditional peoples by nine years. It was an experiment in local law leading national law, and it held. The reserve is now part of the Amazon Region Protected Areas Program, often described as the world's largest tropical-forest conservation program.

## Where the Reserve Sits

The reserve sprawls across three municipalities: Laranjal do Jari holds 69 percent, Mazagão 19 percent, and Pedra Branca do Amapari 12 percent. The Jari River forms the western edge. The Iratapuru, a tributary of the Jari, runs from north to south through the center, fed by many smaller streams. The reserve does not stand alone. The Waiãpi Indigenous Territory borders it to the north. The Jari Ecological Station lies to the south. The Amapá State Forest adjoins to the east. The Tumucumaque Mountains National Park — Brazil's largest national park — lies just to the north. The Rio Cajari Extractive Reserve lies to the southeast. Strung together, these units form the Amapá Corridor, a conservation geography large enough to matter at the scale of continents.

## What the Forest Holds

The numbers come from quick surveys that have only scratched the surface. Botanists have logged 398 plant species in 78 families, with emergent trees reaching roughly 43 meters and some trunks more than two meters across. There are 41 mammal species from 36 genera, including 8 species of primates. Bats, 41 species; birds, 376 species in 61 families; fish, at least 164 species with many more suspected; amphibians, 54 species; reptiles, 67. Two amphibians and one lizard were previously unknown to science. The threatened harlequin toad Atelopus spumarius has been recorded. At the top of the food web, giant anteaters sweep termite mounds, giant otters patrol the streams, and jaguars move through the undergrowth. Across the entire Jari River Basin, which includes the reserve, just 0.48 percent of the land has been deforested.

## Brazil Nuts, Copaiba Oil, and a Perfume

About 175 families live in nine communities around the reserve's edges — Vila Cupixi, São Raimundo, and São Miguel do Cupixi along the Cupixi River in the north, and Retiro, Padaria, São José, Santo Antônio da Cachoeira, São Francisco do Iratapuru, and Comunidade do S along the Jari in the south. They are permitted to hunt, fish, collect Brazil nuts, farm for subsistence, and host ecotourism. Just five percent of the reserve is used for extractive activity. The Cooperative of Rio Iratapuru Producers and Extracters has certified 32,000 hectares under the SmartWood label for sustainable harvest of Brazil nuts, copaiba oil, and breu branco. On 16 February 2012, the company Natura Inovação e Tecnologia de Produtos was authorized — under protocols for accessing traditional knowledge — to develop products under the "Perfume do Brasil" project. A forest resin used locally for its medicinal properties became a subject of perfume research under Brazil's rules for sharing the benefits of traditional knowledge. The model is not perfect. It is rare.

## The Pressures and the Work

A technical mission by the Guiana Shield Facility in April 2008 identified the usual threats: logging, mining, road-building, human settlement, and agricultural expansion. The most damaging is illegal garimpo mining, concentrated in the reserve's northeast near the BR-210 highway, polluting the waterways with mercury. Bird populations in the north of the reserve seem to have been affected by the mining. Brazil nut harvests have declined since 2013. The hydroelectric plant at Santo Antônio do Cachoeira, completed in 2014, may bring its own pressures. And the reserve's 24-member management board was not appointed until 19 May 2013, more than fifteen years after the reserve was created. The reserve is a success at the scale of a continent and a negotiation at the scale of every village within it. Both things are true at once.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 0.318°N, 52.725°W, in the southern interior of Amapá state, Brazil. Recommended viewing altitude 4,000–6,000 feet to capture the terra firme canopy of the Iratapuru basin as it drains south to join the Jari River. The nearest paved airport is Monte Dourado (SBMD), across the Jari from Laranjal do Jari; Macapá International (SBMQ) is about 190 km to the east. Equatorial rainforest climate; expect cumulus buildups by mid-afternoon and heavy rain December–May.
