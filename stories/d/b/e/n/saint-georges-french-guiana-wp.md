# Saint-Georges, French Guiana

On the Oyapock River, a tiny French town faces Brazil across the only land border France shares with another South American country.

**Location:** 3.8894, -51.8011

**Categories:** border-towns, french-guiana, amazon, penal-colony-history

## Introduction

Stand on the riverbank at Saint-Georges and look across the Oyapock. The town on the other side is Oiapoque, Brazil. The river is the border - and until 2017, it was also the end of the road. There was no bridge. For centuries, this was where France, improbably, met the Amazon by water or not at all.

## A Coin in the Dirt

The town's founding has the feel of an omen. On 23 April 1853, French authorities established a penal colony on the banks of the Oyapock and named it after a Dutch coin of Saint George someone had turned up at the site. A religious medal, dropped by who knows whom, and suddenly a settlement has its patron saint. The penal camp did not last long - it closed in 1863 - but the name stuck. What revived the place was not piety but gold. The 1885 strike on the nearby Camopi River brought prospectors, and the hamlet that had almost dissolved back into the forest became a town officially in 1947. Today the commune is home to about 500 Palikur Amerindians, whose settlements include the village of Trois-Paletuviers - their own communities older than any French coin.

## The Bridge That Took Twelve Years

For most of Saint-Georges's history, there was no way to drive to Cayenne, the regional capital. The asphalted road through the jungle was only completed in 2004, ending the town's isolation at last. Then came the bridge. France and Brazil signed the treaty in 2005, finished construction in 2011, and then - stubbornly, bureaucratically - waited six more years before opening the span for use in March 2017. When it finally opened, something genuinely new happened: for the first time, France shared a working land crossing with a neighboring country in South America. You can now drive uninterrupted from Cayenne all the way to Macapa, on the Amazon itself. A journey that once required a ferry and some patience now takes a tank of fuel.

## Where the Legion Patrols

For a town of a few thousand at the edge of the Amazon, Saint-Georges carries surprising weight. In October 2022 it became the seat of a new French administrative region, the Arrondissement of Saint-Georges, joining Cayenne and Saint-Laurent-du-Maroni as one of the three sub-prefectures of French Guiana. A detachment of the French Foreign Legion is stationed here, because this border is not only a border - it is also France's deepest projection into the Amazon, and illegal gold mining and smuggling make the jungle a place where patrols matter. The town has its mayor's office, a few hotels with names like Le Tamarin and Chez Modestine, and the particular hum of a place that is simultaneously a European administrative capital and a river crossing in the middle of the rainforest.

## Equatorial Weather

Saint-Georges sits just under four degrees north of the equator, and the climate makes no secret of it. The year divides into rainy and slightly less rainy, with May the soggiest of all. October is the hottest month; January cools to an average around 26 degrees Celsius. On 22 October 2015, the town set its all-time high. The oldest coldest record dates to 1958, which gives some sense of how long the monitoring has gone on. The rain on the Oyapock is not the gray drizzle of European France. It comes in sudden tropical cloudbursts, drums on metal roofs, turns the red dirt streets to slurry, and moves on. Within minutes the sun returns and steam rises from the asphalt.

## Four Centuries of Trying

The Oyapock has seen colonial hopes come and go. In 1604, Charles Leigh founded a British colony here called Oyapoc. In February 1677, a Dutchman named John Apricius tried his hand and built something he called Fort Orange, only to be chased out by the French four months later. The 1853 penal camp was France's own failed experiment. What has endured, beneath all that coming and going, is the river itself and the Palikur settlements along it. The border between France and Brazil may be a modern political line, but the people who have lived on these banks the longest measure their time in different units entirely.

## Flight Context

Saint-Georges lies at 3.89 degrees N, 51.80 degrees W, on the west bank of the Oyapock River forming the Brazil-French Guiana border. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000-6,000 feet for river and bridge observation. The Oyapock River Bridge is a distinctive landmark connecting Saint-Georges to Oiapoque, Brazil. Saint-Georges-de-l'Oyapock Airport (ICAO: SOOG) serves the commune. Nearest major airport is Cayenne-Felix Eboue (SOCA), roughly 190 km northwest.
