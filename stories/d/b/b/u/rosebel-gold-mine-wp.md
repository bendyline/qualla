# Rosebel gold mine

A 170-square-kilometer open-pit operation in the Surinamese rainforest had produced 4.4 million ounces of gold by 2016 — a concession that has changed hands again and again since 1879 and now belongs, mostly, to a Chinese mining giant.

**Location:** 5.0769, -55.1558

**Categories:** mining, suriname, industry, maroon-communities, gold

## Introduction

In 1879, a small group of miners with pans and hand tools worked the creeks of the Rosebel concession, eighty-five kilometers south of the Surinamese capital at Paramaribo. They were chasing gold that had eroded out of the ancient Precambrian rocks of the Guiana Shield, the same geological province that holds the region's other gold fields and Guyana's diamonds. The miners did not get rich. For most of the next century, the Rosebel concession would bounce between prospectors, aluminum companies, and exploration juniors, never quite delivering the payday its ore assays promised. Then, in February 2004, a Canadian company called Cambior started up a modern open-pit operation, and the earth under Brokopondo District began moving in earnest. By the end of 2016, Rosebel had produced 4.4 million ounces of gold. In recent years it has produced roughly 270,000 to 310,000 ounces a year, and it now belongs to the Chinese Zijin group.

## A Hundred Years of False Starts

The ownership chain reads like an accelerating relay. After the small-scale prospectors of 1879 came Placer Development of Vancouver, which held the ground from 1974 to 1977. Then the Grasshopper Aluminum Company (Grassalco), Suriname's state-owned mining company, from 1979 to 1985. Then Golden Star Resources picked it up in 1992. Cambior, a Montreal-headquartered mining house, bought Golden Star's interest in October 2001. Cambior built the mine. Commercial production started in February 2004. Iamgold, another Canadian major, acquired Cambior in late 2006 and with it Rosebel. By 2008, 1,199 people worked at the mine. The concession is 170 square kilometers. It sits 85 kilometers south of Paramaribo, reachable by 110 kilometers of paved and gravel road or by the one-kilometer Gross Rosebel Airstrip on the property. Power comes from the Afobaka Dam on the Brokopondo Reservoir and a backup diesel plant; a fourteen-million-dollar solar installation, billed as the largest solar project in the country, began construction in May 2014.

## The Ownership Question

In June 2013, Iamgold and the Surinamese government signed a lease extension that runs until 2042. The government holds a five-percent free-carried interest; the operator holds the rest. On 18 October 2022, the ownership shifted again: Zijin Mining, a major Chinese producer, announced the purchase of Iamgold's 95-percent interest for $360 million. The deal closed on 1 February 2023. Rosebel is now operated by Rosebel Gold Mines N.V., part of Zijin's gold arm, Zijin Gold International, with the government of Suriname holding the continuing five-percent stake. As of 2020, proved and probable reserves stood at 2.9 million ounces, with further upside in the neighboring Saramacca expansion property and potential for zinc extraction. Iamgold once projected closure around 2028; Zijin is now expanding the operation, with a new pit at Saramacca and a larger processing plant.

## The People Who Live Next Door

Rosebel sits near the town of Brownsweg, on land historically occupied by Saramaka Maroon communities whose ancestors escaped Dutch plantation slavery centuries ago and built self-governing villages in the interior. The relationship between a foreign-owned, industrial open-pit mine and its neighbors has not been smooth. Many local residents run their own small-scale mining operations — porknokkers, in Surinamese usage — panning and digging on and near the concession. In July 2019, disturbances at the mine reached national news. Police moved against local people who were prospecting on their own, and in the confrontation a man, Furgill Aloeboetoe, died. Local parliamentarian Diana Pokie urged the mine's owners to find a solution and to compensate his family. The death crystallized a tension that exists around many large mines: the metal is worth a lot of money to people far away, and the ground it comes out of is home to the people who live on it.

## What 4.4 Million Ounces Looks Like

By the end of 2016, Rosebel had produced 4.4 million ounces of gold. At recent prices that is well over ten billion dollars. Moved through the global gold market, it has ended up in bank vaults, wedding bands, electronic components, and central-bank reserves on six continents. The pit itself, from the air, is a series of stepped terraces the color of red-brown laterite cut into what used to be rainforest, with a processing plant, tailings ponds, waste-rock dumps, haul roads, and the airstrip about two kilometers from the mill. Restoration of open-pit gold operations is possible but slow and expensive. Rosebel's lease runs to 2042. What the landscape looks like after that will depend on what Zijin and the Surinamese state — and the Maroon communities around Brownsweg — decide is owed.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 5.077°N, 55.156°W, in Brokopondo District, north-central Suriname, about 85 km south of Paramaribo and near the town of Brownsweg. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000–5,000 feet to see the open-pit terraces against the surrounding rainforest and the Brokopondo Reservoir (Afobaka Lake) to the southeast. The Gross Rosebel Airstrip (1 km) sits on the concession. Nearest international airport is Paramaribo–Zanderij (SMJP), about 40 km north. Tropical climate; visibility best in the drier months of August–November.
