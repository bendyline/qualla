# Pereira, Colombia

In the geometric center of Colombia's Golden Triangle, Pereira grew from the ruins of a forgotten conquistador city into the coffee capital of an UNESCO World Heritage landscape.

**Location:** 4.8143, -75.6946

**Categories:** cities, coffee, unesco, colombia, risaralda, coffee-axis

## Introduction

On July 26, 1890, a 19-year-old named David Lopez faced a firing squad in Pereira and told the soldiers to aim at his heart. He had been convicted of the murder of Ricardo Torres nearly two years earlier, on November 30, 1888, and every appeal had failed. What Lopez could not have known is that his execution was the last in Colombian history - after him, the country stopped killing its convicted. That piece of history almost gets buried in the softer stories Pereira tells about itself: that it is the pearl of Otun, the center of a UNESCO coffee landscape, the birthplace of a president. But the city, planted on the ruins of a forgotten conquistador town in 1863, has always held the hard stories alongside the sweet ones.

## A City on Top of a City

The land under Pereira was already a city in 1540, when Marshal Jorge Robledo founded Cartago here. By 1691 the Spanish had given up - sieges by the Pijao people and the economics of the Cauca Valley pushed Cartago north to the La Vieja river, where it still stands. The original site returned to jungle. Nearly two centuries later, on August 24, 1863, the priest Remigio Antonio Canarte and Jesus Maria Ormaza Nino led a small group back to the ruins and built huts that were blessed six days later. For six years they called the settlement Cartago too. In 1869, they renamed it after Francisco Pereira Martinez, the Patriot doctor who had hidden here in 1816 after the Battle of Cachiri during the Spanish reconquest. The city remembered the man who had once chosen it as a refuge.

## The Coffee Landscape

In 2011, UNESCO designated the Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia a World Heritage Site, and Pereira - as the most populous city in the Coffee Axis - is its unofficial capital. The settlers who came from Antioquia in the 1920s planted their first high-quality beans in the volcanic soil of the Andean foothills, and the pattern of steep smallholder farms, hand-picked harvests, and seasonal labor that grew from those plantings is what the UNESCO listing preserves. The Harvest Festival celebrates the founding of the city every August, coinciding with the coffee harvest. Colombians still use the phrase 'hicieron su Agosto' - 'they made their August' - to mean someone made good money, remembering the wages coffee pickers earned in that month. Pereira's annual average rainfall is 2,425 mm; its average temperature 21.5 C. These numbers grow coffee.

## The Viaduct and the Naked Bolivar

On November 15, 1997, the Cesar Gaviria Trujillo Viaduct opened across the Otun River, linking Pereira with its twin city Dosquebradas. Its center span of 211 meters is one of the longest cable-stayed bridges in South America, and its namesake - Cesar Gaviria, the 40th president of Colombia - was born in the city. But the signature monument is stranger. In the Plaza de Bolivar, sculptor Rodrigo Arenas Betancur's Naked Bolivar sits on horseback, depicted without clothes. The same sculptor also made the Monument to the Founders on Carrera 13, the Chained Prometheus at the Technological University, and Christ Without the Cross at Our Lady of Fatima church. Pereira gave Arenas Betancur an open canvas, and he filled it with symbols of struggle.

## The Bioparque and the Boulders

On June 30, 2015, the Ukumari Biopark opened in the Galicia sector 14 kilometers from the city, taking the animals from the recently closed Matecana Zoo into something the city calls the largest theme park in Latin America - 820,000 square meters divided into bioregions for the Andes, the Amazon, Africa, and elsewhere. The project cost roughly 80 billion pesos, split among the national government, the tourism ministry, and the municipal INFIPEREIRA. Its stated ambition was to escape the Victorian zoo tradition of concrete cages. Along the Circunvalar Avenue, the Cesar Gaviria Viaduct, and the Cocora Valley just 45 minutes away with its giant wax palms, Pereira built a tourism economy that no longer depended only on coffee.

## The Active City

In 2015, Pereira earned the title of Colombia's most active city. Every Sunday, the major avenues close for the Ciclovia - the weekly cycle way - and every neighborhood has an open-air gym anyone can use. The Olympic Village holds the renovated Hernan Ramirez Villegas Stadium, a velodrome, Olympic swimming pools, and Colombia's largest skatepark. Pereira's main football club, Deportivo Pereira, plays in the top flight. The city gave Colombia the tennis player Santiago Giraldo and the striker Cucho Hernandez. The water coming out of the tap is ranked among the top 20 in the world for quality - tourists are routinely surprised to be told they can drink it straight from the faucet. It is that kind of city: quietly, competently proud of what it does well.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.81 N, 75.69 W in the Central Cordillera foothills at roughly 1,411 m elevation, in the heart of the Coffee Axis. Matecana International Airport (SKPE) sits just west of the city; connects to Bogota, Medellin, and Miami. Best viewed from 10,000 ft for the Cesar Gaviria Viaduct spanning the Otun valley and the coffee plantations terracing up the mountainsides. Watch for the snowcapped Nevado del Ruiz to the east.
