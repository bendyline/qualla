# Pereira

A traveler's guide to the capital of Colombia's Coffee Axis - a 482,000-person university town with excellent tap water, a 211-meter viaduct, and great hot springs nearby.

**Location:** 4.8143, -75.6946

**Categories:** travel, cities, coffee, colombia, risaralda, hot-springs

## Introduction

There is a practical fact about Pereira you won't find in many travel guides: you can drink the tap water. The city's supply is ranked among the world's top 20 for quality, which means no plastic-bottle accumulation in your hostel room, no calculus about ice in your drink, just a glass from the faucet and a shrug. That small, unremarkable freedom is the texture of travel in Pereira - a 482,000-person university city perched between Bogota, Medellin, and Cali, where the motto is 'Pereira con Vida' and the hardest decision most days is whether to go to Cocora Valley or the hot springs first.

## How You Get There

Matecana International Airport, just outside the city, is the largest in the region. Avianca, LATAM, Wingo, JetSMART, and ClicAir fly domestic routes to Bogota, Medellin, Cartagena, and San Andres; international flights run to Panama City via Copa and Miami via American. A taxi from the airport to downtown takes about 20 minutes and runs around 26,000 COP (2024). If you're coming overland, the bus terminal is clean and well-served. Buses from Cali take 4 hours for 29,000 COP; from Bogota 9 hours for 50,000; from Medellin 6 hours for 33,000; from Manizales just 1 hour 15 minutes for 16,000 (with minibuses leaving every 15 minutes in October 2023). Salento, the gateway to Valle de Cocora, is COP 10,500 hourly.

## Getting Around

Pereira invented its own mass transit. The Megabus is modeled on the bus rapid transit systems of Curitiba in Brazil and Ottawa in Canada, with dedicated lanes and enclosed stations - green buses that run from 4:30 AM to 11:35 PM for 2,950 COP a trip. You'll need a Megatarjeta card, available at stations for 6,000 COP and good for three trips. For neighborhood transit and outlying areas, ordinary city buses and Willys jeeps run everywhere for around 2,000 COP, with destinations scrawled on cardboard in the windshield. Taxis start at 6,000 COP with a 1,600 pickup charge; the airport surcharge is 3,000 COP going out and 4,500 coming back.

## Looking Out from La Popa

There are two Pereira vantage points everyone ends up at. La Popa, a hill on the edge of town, gives a sweeping view of the city. The mirador at the Cesar Gaviria Trujillo Viaduct - a 211-meter cable-stayed span opened in November 1997 - is the other. In between, the main avenue of Simon Bolivar holds most of the best discos and restaurants, with a secondary nightlife scene in the upscale residential neighborhood to the south, where students and the younger crowd gather from Thursday onward. Pereira also loves its football. The Deportivo Pereira stadium seats 30,000 fans, and the Once fans fill it every match day. 'Once' is shorthand for Once Caldas, the club from nearby Manizales - regional rivalries run hot.

## What to Eat and Where

The food here comes from Antioquia, the region to the northwest that built much of modern Pereira with coffee money. A typical lunch is plantain soup followed by rice with fried pork, beef, or chicken, with a small vinegar cabbage salad and a natural fruit juice - mango, or one of the unpronounceable native flavors locals swear by. Finish with coffee, which is served astonishingly sweet unless you ask specifically for it 'hecho con agua' (made with water only). If you're headed to Los Nevados National Park, order aguapanela con queso on the way - hot sugar cane water served with a slab of cheese dropped in to melt. Standout restaurants include Kilaba for Arabic food on Circunvalar, Tradiciones Peruanas at C.C. Santa Monica Plaza for ceviche and lomo saltado, and Mama Flor with its quiet balcony and Sopa de Guineo.

## The Hot Springs Across the Valley

The real reason many travelers come to Pereira is to leave it - not because the city disappoints but because its surroundings are spectacular. The Termales de Santa Rosa and Termales del Nevado sit within easy drives, each offering lower and upper pools, the upper ones warmer because they sit nearer the volcanic source. Admission runs around 30,000 COP per day and the pools stay open until midnight. The four-star Termales de Santa Rosa Hotel is pricier - 150,000 COP per person per night - but includes all meals and access to private wading pools, plus waterfalls lit with colored lights after dark. Further afield, Salento, the little colonial town famous for coffee farms, is the gateway to Valle de Cocora with its sixty-meter wax palms and Los Nevados National Park. Pereira is a city you use as a hinge.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.81 N, 75.69 W in Colombia's Coffee Axis, at the center of the Bogota-Medellin-Cali triangle. Matecana International Airport (SKPE) is the main regional hub with flights to Bogota, Medellin, Miami, and Panama City. Surrounding snowcapped volcanoes (Ruiz, Quindio, Santa Isabel) are the Los Nevados National Park. Altitude averages 1,411 m; warm tropical highland climate year-round with significant afternoon showers.
