# Otavalo

Two hours north of Quito, Otavalo is the Andean town travelers plan a weekend around - Saturday market, Peguche Waterfall, Cuicocha Lake, and a skyline of volcanoes.

**Location:** 0.2333, -78.2667

**Categories:** Travel guides, Cities in Ecuador, Imbabura Province, Andean tourism, Markets

## Introduction

Pick any Saturday, take the two-hour bus ride north from Quito's Terminal Carcelén, and the first thing you'll notice as you step out in Otavalo is that the air is a few degrees cooler, the light is sharper, and the smell of fresh bread is fighting with the smell of wool dye. The town is small - about 50,000 inhabitants - but on market day it doubles. Indigenous vendors from Otavalo and the surrounding villages spread blankets, stack ponchos, and hang strings of gold beads under the concrete canopies of the Plaza de los Ponchos. Travelers come for that market. Most end up staying longer than they meant to.

## Getting There (and What It Costs)

From Quito, the easy route is a bus from Terminal Carcelén in the city's north end. The fare as of 2023 runs about $3.10. Some buses roll through en route to Ibarra and only stop on the town's outskirts - fine if you know where you're going, inconvenient if you don't. To reach Carcelén from the Centro Histórico or La Mariscal, take a taxi or ride the Ecovía line to its last stop at Río Coca and transfer. A taxi directly from Quito's Mariscal Sucre International Airport runs $50-$60 and saves the backtrack into Quito. Coming south from the Colombian border, buses from Tulcán drop you at the Pan American Highway with a ten-minute walk into town. Outbound buses to Quito and Ibarra (the latter a charming $0.65 ride) leave every fifteen minutes from the main terminal.

## The Market, Day by Day

Saturday is the main event - the Mercado Artesanal between Calles Sucre and Jaramillo, where the Plaza de los Ponchos fills with textile stalls, wood carvings, jewelry, and "hand-made" goods in varying degrees of actual handmaking. Experienced shoppers learn the tells: machine-cut edges versus hand-finished ones, acrylic fibers marketed as alpaca, the particular heft of real handwoven wool. Other days, the plaza is quieter and the pressure lighter. For groceries, hit the Mercado 24 de Mayo, which runs all week. North of town, the Mercado de Animales operates on livestock auction days - an authentic scene but not one for animal welfare fans. Bargaining is standard in the artisan market, but prices are already modest. Better prices and better selection can often be found at the source, in surrounding villages.

## Water, Rock, and Lake

Within a short taxi or bus ride, Otavalo opens onto some of the most photographable landscape in Ecuador. Peguche Waterfall tumbles eighteen meters over a basalt face three kilometers from town ($1 entry as of 2023). Indigenous Kichwa communities treat it as a sacred site; ritual baths and offerings still happen here. San Pablo Lake sits 3.5 kilometers southeast at the foot of Imbabura volcano, occupying a tectonic basin about four kilometers long. Cuicocha Lake, fourteen kilometers northwest at the base of Cotacachi, fills a deep volcanic crater with two domed vegetation-covered islands at its center, separated by a channel locals call the "channel of dreams." The name Cuicocha comes from Quechua Tsui Cocha - "lake of the gods." A twelve-kilometer trail circles it in about five hours.

## Villages with Specialties

The towns around Otavalo each do something particular. Cotacachi, twenty kilometers north - a town of about 20,000 - is Ecuador's leather capital; expect wallets, jackets, and bags at better prices than Otavalo sells them. San Antonio specializes in wood carving, its main street lined with carved picture frames, statues, and furniture. Ilumán, fifteen minutes away by bus, is home to over 100 traditional healers in an officially recognized shaman's association - a quieter place than Otavalo with its own walking trails and a slower pace. Chachimbiro offers volcanic hot springs at 45-55 °C rich in minerals - iron, magnesium, copper, sulfur - with the kind of cardiovascular and inflammatory benefits volcanic waters have always promised. Ibarra, the provincial capital, is known for helados de paila - a fresh-fruit sorbet made in large copper pans, something no visit to the region should skip.

## Where to Eat, Where to Sleep

Otavalo has more hotels than a town this size should reasonably need. Prices compete hard - negotiating at the desk is expected, even for mid-range places. The only times to book a week in advance are Inti Raymi around June 21 and the Yamor festival in early September. For food, a set lunch with soup and a main course runs $1.50-$3. A nicer dinner might hit $7-$9. Food stalls around the handicraft market, at Mercado 24 de Mayo, and at Feria Imbabio serve roast pork and fried tilapia, plus cuy asado (roast guinea pig) for the adventurous. The Pie Shop at the southern corner of the Plaza de Ponchos and the third-floor café inside Cinema Sisa both do sweet fixes. Nightlife clusters along the northern end of Calle 31 de Octubre, with peñas (folk-music clubs) and bars. Most visitors end up extending their stay by at least one day.

## Flight Context

Otavalo sits at 0.233°N, 78.267°W in the inter-Andean valley of northern Ecuador. Elevation about 2,550 m (8,366 ft). The nearest airport is Quito's Mariscal Sucre (SEQM), 65 km south. Three volcanoes form the horizon: Imbabura (4,630 m), Cotacachi (4,939 m), and Mojanda to the south. Approach flights from the east on clear mornings will show the inter-Andean corridor clearly; afternoons typically build cloud over the peaks.
