# Yotoco

A small Valle del Cauca town whose name means 'blowgun' in a forgotten indigenous language, and whose 600-year-old ceiba tree still shades the courtyard of a colonial hacienda.

**Location:** 3.8603, -76.3833

**Categories:** Colombia, Valle del Cauca, Colonial architecture, Wetlands, Andes

## Introduction

The tree is older than the town. At the Hacienda Hato Viejo, just outside Yotoco, a single ceiba rises out of the courtyard with a trunk so broad it takes three adults to span with outstretched arms. It has been standing there for roughly 600 years - which means it was already a substantial tree when Captain Diego Rengifo Salazar arrived with his men in 1622 and founded a village on the banks of the river. The town took its name from the chief who had ruled the valley before them. In the indigenous language, Yotat meant blowgun, because the people of this place hunted with them. The tree has outlived everyone - chief, captain, colony, republic - and still drops its pale cotton seeds across the hacienda's tile roof every dry season.

## King of the Winds

Yotoco calls itself El Rey de los Vientos, the King of the Winds. The name comes from the way afternoon breezes channel down the valley between the Cordillera Occidental and the Cauca River, strong enough that kites rise easily and banners snap at the plaza. The town celebrates this identity through music. The municipal music school, one of only three of its kind in Colombia, is called the Rey de los Vientos school, and it trains children from the surrounding countryside in brass, woodwinds, and strings. Directed by Juan Pablo Parra Noreña, the school is sponsored by the municipal government and forms the backbone of a surprisingly strong local musical culture for a town of fewer than 20,000 people.

## Hato Viejo's Three Centuries

The hacienda that gives Yotoco its architectural fame was founded in the 18th century and still stands largely as it was built. The main house and its chapel both date back roughly 350 years, built of adobe and tile in the plain colonial style that Valle del Cauca favored over the ornate Popayán model. In the 1700s Hato Viejo supplied food and sugar-derived products to the mines of the Pacific slope - a critical supply node in the colonial economy and a base from which new settlements spread through what would become Valle del Cauca. Colombia declared the estate a national monument in 1996. It remains privately owned but can be visited along the Vía Panorama road between Yotoco and Vijes, where the 600-year-old ceiba still shades the inner patio.

## Laguna de Sonso

East of town, between Yotoco and Buga, 2,045 hectares of wetland stretch along the Cauca River. Laguna de Sonso - sometimes called Laguna del Chircal - became a nature reserve in 1987 and is now the 14th largest wetland in the upper Cauca system and one of the most important bird-watching sites in Colombia. Swamp vultures, ospreys, and cattle herons wheel over the water. Common possums, vampire bats, and in recent years capybaras hold the mammal list. The fish - bocachico, mojarra, and the odd catfish called corroncho - have fed local families for generations and still support artisanal fishermen. The lagoon also functions as a natural flood regulator for the Cauca, absorbing overflow when the river rises during the rainy season.

## A Town at the Crossroads

Yotoco sits 75 kilometers north of Cali, 115 kilometers from the Pacific port of Buenaventura, and just 12 kilometers from the religious pilgrimage town of Buga. Drivers crossing Colombia east-to-west on the way to the coast pass through, and the town has adapted. Restaurants line the highways selling sausages and fried specialties - the Puente Tierra truck stop on the Buenaventura road, at kilometer 20, has a national reputation for its chorizo. The economy otherwise runs on agriculture: coffee on the higher slopes, sugarcane on the valley floor, pineapple and avocado on the middle terraces, along with poultry and pig farming and some quarrying for rock and sand.

## Stories the Town Tells Itself

Beyond the music school, Yotoco keeps its culture alive through books. The Alejandro Arce Library, inaugurated in the 2000s, serves as a community hub. The local cultural house publishes Cuenta cuentos que yo me invento - 'Tales I Invent Myself' - a collection of stories written by schoolchildren and teachers at Alfonso Zawadzky, Gabriela Mistral, and San Juan Bosco schools. Another book, Cruce de Miradas (Crossing Glances), collects poems, narrations, and photographs of memorable locals and landscapes. Both projects treat a small town's ordinary life as worth writing down. The Chiquique wetland just outside town, long part of the fishing tradition, is the kind of place those stories come from - a quiet corner where the bocachico still bite at dawn and the kids still learn to clean them on the riverbank.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 3.86°N, 76.38°W. Yotoco sits in Valle del Cauca on the western side of the Cauca River valley at roughly 1,000 m elevation, at the foot of the Cordillera Occidental. From the air, the broad flat Cauca Valley floor (sugarcane fields) is unmistakable, with the Cordillera Occidental rising immediately to the west. Laguna de Sonso east of town appears as a significant water feature between Yotoco and Buga. Alfonso Bonilla Aragón International (SKCL) at Cali is 75 km south and serves as the primary airfield reference. The Pan-American Highway passes through nearby.
