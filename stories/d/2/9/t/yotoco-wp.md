# Yotoco

A small Valle del Cauca town whose name, legend says, comes from an indigenous word for 'blowgun', and whose ceiba tree, said to be 600 years old, still shades the courtyard of a colonial hacienda.

**Location:** 3.8603, -76.3833

**Categories:** Colombia, Valle del Cauca, Colonial architecture, Wetlands, Andes

## Introduction

The tree is older than the town. At the Hacienda Hato Viejo, just outside Yotoco, a single monumental ceiba rises out of the courtyard. It is said to be about 600 years old - which would mean it was already a substantial tree when Captain Diego Rengifo Salazar arrived with his men in 1622 and founded a village on the banks of the river. According to legend, the town took its name from a chief called Yotoco who had ruled the site before them, and the word comes from Yotat, said to mean blowgun. The tree has outlived everyone - chief, captain, colony, republic.

## King of the Winds

Yotoco is known as El Rey de los Vientos, the King of the Winds, and the town carries the name into music. The municipal music school is called the Rey de los Vientos school, and it trains local children. Directed by Juan Pablo Parra Noreña and sponsored by the municipal government, the school forms the backbone of a surprisingly strong local musical culture for a town of fewer than 20,000 people.

## Hato Viejo's Three Centuries

The hacienda that gives Yotoco its architectural fame still stands largely as it was built. The main house and its chapel are said to be about 350 years old, built of adobe and tile in the plain colonial style of the Cauca Valley. In colonial times haciendas like Hato Viejo supplied food and cane products to the gold mines of the Pacific slope. Colombia declared the estate a national monument in 1996. It stands along the Vía Panorama road toward Vijes, where the old ceiba still shades the inner patio.

## Laguna de Sonso

East of town, between Yotoco and Buga, a 2,045-hectare reserve of wetland stretches along the Cauca River. Laguna de Sonso - sometimes called Laguna del Chircal - became a nature reserve in 1987, is often described as the largest wetland in Valle del Cauca, and has been part of a Ramsar wetland site since 2017; some 186 bird species have been recorded there. Swamp vultures, ospreys, and cattle herons wheel over the water. Common opossums, vampire bats, and in recent years capybaras are among its mammals. The fish - bocachico, mojarra, and the odd catfish called corroncho - have fed local families for generations and still support artisanal fishermen. The lagoon also functions as a natural flood regulator for the Cauca, absorbing overflow when the river rises during the rainy season.

## A Town at the Crossroads

Yotoco sits about 75 kilometers by road north-northeast of Cali, 115 kilometers from the Pacific port of Buenaventura, and just 12 kilometers from the religious pilgrimage town of Buga. Drivers crossing Colombia east-to-west on the way to the coast pass through, and the town has adapted. Restaurants line the highways selling sausages and fried specialties - the Puente Tierra truck stop on the Buenaventura road, at kilometer 20, has a national reputation for its chorizo. The economy otherwise runs on agriculture - coffee, sugarcane, pineapple, avocado, plantain and beans - along with cattle, poultry and pig farming and some quarrying for rock and sand.

## Stories the Town Tells Itself

Beyond the music school, Yotoco keeps its culture alive through books. The Alejandro Arce Library serves as a community hub. The local cultural house publishes Cuenta cuentos que yo me invento - 'Tales I Invent Myself' - a collection of stories written by schoolchildren and teachers at Alfonso Zawadzky, Gabriela Mistral, and San Juan Bosco schools. Another book, Cruce de Miradas (Crossing Glances), collects poems, narrations, and photographs of memorable locals and landscapes. Both projects treat a small town's ordinary life as worth writing down. The Chiquique wetland just outside town, long part of the fishing tradition with its bocachico, corroncho and mojarra, is the kind of place those stories come from.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 3.86°N, 76.38°W. Yotoco sits in Valle del Cauca on the western side of the Cauca River valley at roughly 1,000 m elevation, at the foot of the Cordillera Occidental. From the air, the broad flat Cauca Valley floor (sugarcane fields) is unmistakable, with the Cordillera Occidental rising immediately to the west. Laguna de Sonso east of town appears as a significant water feature between Yotoco and Buga. Alfonso Bonilla Aragón International (SKCL), serving Cali, is about 35 km due south and serves as the primary airfield reference. The Pan-American Highway runs through Buga, about 12 km east.
