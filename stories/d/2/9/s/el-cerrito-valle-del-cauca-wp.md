# El Cerrito, Valle del Cauca

The Cauca Valley town whose hacienda El Paraíso became the setting for Jorge Isaacs's Maria - one of the most famous novels ever written in Spanish.

**Location:** 3.6839, -76.3111

**Categories:** Municipalities of Valle del Cauca Department, Populated places established in 1797, Literary settings, Colonial Colombia

## Introduction

In 1867 a young Colombian writer named Jorge Isaacs published a novel called María, set on a hacienda called El Paraíso on the western slope of the Cordillera Central. The book became one of the most widely read Spanish-language novels of the 19th century - read in Mexico and Argentina and Spain, translated into many languages, studied by generations of schoolchildren. El Paraíso is a real place. It still stands, 16 kilometers from the center of the town of El Cerrito, in the Cauca Valley department of Valle del Cauca, 47 kilometers by road northeast of Cali. You can walk its verandas, look out at the same view the novel describes, and understand why a book about lost love has kept a small Colombian municipality on the literary map for 150 years.

## The Small Hill

El Cerrito - "the small hill" in Spanish - is named for a small hill in the town, which sits on the valley floor at nearly 1,000 meters above sea level, with an average temperature of 24 degrees Celsius. Long before Spaniards named it, this country was Pijao territory; the Pijao cacique Calarcá led resistance against the Spanish until his death around 1607. The colonial town proper came later. Captain Gregorio de Astigarreta built the hacienda San Jerónimo de los Ingenios, under the jurisdiction of neighboring Guacarí, and a village grew there from 1797. The first Mass on the present site was said by Father Manuel José Guzmán on August 30, 1825, giving the settlement its original name of San Jerónimo. In 1854 it was renamed Guzmán in the priest's honor, and in 1864 it was elevated to municipal status under the name El Cerrito. The town sits on a floodplain where the Amaime, Cauca, Cerrito, and Sabaletas rivers cross the valley floor.

## Jorge Isaacs and María

Jorge Isaacs wrote María between 1864 and 1867, after fighting in Colombia's civil wars of 1854 and 1860-1862, failing as a merchant, and taking a job supervising work on the mule road between Cali and Buenaventura. The novel tells the story of Efraín, who returns from studies in Bogotá to his family's hacienda in the Cauca Valley and falls in love with his cousin María. She falls ill; he is sent to study medicine in London; he returns too late. It is a story of landscape as much as love. Isaacs describes the palms, the birds, the mist at dawn, the storms rolling off the Cordillera - the Cauca Valley as a paradise that cannot hold the people it shelters. The hacienda where Isaacs spent his youth, El Paraíso, became the novel's setting. Today it is a house-museum in the district of Santa Elena. Generations of Colombian readers know its rooms from the book before they ever see them; when they visit, they are checking reality against memory.

## The Haciendas

El Cerrito's territory is unusually dense with colonial haciendas. Beyond El Paraíso, the area contains Piedechinche (now home to the Sugarcane Museum), Hacienda El Hatico, Trapiche de San Fernando, La Concepción, La María, Novillero, San Joaquín, and about a dozen others - most of them with architecture and furnishings dating back to the 18th and 19th centuries. This concentration is no accident. The Cauca Valley's flat alluvial plains were the cradle of Colombian sugar production: cane arrived with the Spanish in the sixteenth century, and trapiches (sugar mills) multiplied on the haciendas over the centuries that followed. The Sugarcane Museum at Piedechinche documents that history with restored milling equipment, colonial furniture, and explanations of how the labor of enslaved people - slavery was legally abolished in Colombia in 1851 - underwrote much of the valley's early wealth. The Hacienda El Paraíso is built in the typical vallecaucana colonial style: wide verandas, thick adobe walls, red tile roofs, and inner courtyards that funnel cool air through the house.

## The Heroes of El Cerrito

The town's connection to Colombian independence runs deep. José María Cabal was born at the Hacienda de la Concepción del Alisal near El Cerrito on May 25, 1769. He was caught up with Antonio Nariño's circle, which printed a Spanish translation of the Rights of Man in 1794, and Spanish authorities sent him to Europe as a prisoner. When he returned, he fought against Spanish reconquest forces in Cauca, won the Battle of Río Palo at Caloto, and defended the city of Popayán. Captured by Royalist troops, he was executed by firing squad in the main square of Popayán on August 19, 1816. El Cerrito also claims a different kind of hero: María Antonia Ruiz, an Afro-Colombian woman born into slavery in Tuluá in 1762 and honored by a 2023 national law as a heroine of independence. Local tradition says she grew up on the Hacienda San Agustín and, when Royalist troops under Simón Muñoz approached, led a small group of soldiers armed with a drum and a battle trumpet, making enough noise to convince Muñoz he was facing a whole Republican army. He withdrew. Other accounts credit her with burning a royalist arms depot near Buga. When Simón Bolívar passed through Tuluá in 1829, he paid her homage.

## Festivals and Flavors

El Cerrito is a farming town in the most literal sense. Its economy rests on cattle, sugar cane, grapes, corn, rice and other crops, along with leatherwork and rural tourism. The town calls itself the main producer of panderitos, small baked goods made from cassava flour. Each year Santa Elena hosts a Grape Festival, Tenerife holds its National Day of the Onion in August, and the Universitas Cultural Center runs the Isaacsian Culture Festival in honor of the novelist. Holy Week processions fill the streets in March or April. Gastronomy carries the marks of the Cauca Valley: arroz atollado (a wet, savory rice dish), sancocho, pandebono (cheese-filled corn bread), chontaduro palm fruit, and sweets from tres leches cake to blancmange. From the high districts of Tenerife you can see wax palms, which can grow to about 60 meters - the national tree of Colombia.

## Flight Context

Located at 3.68°N, 76.31°W in the Valle del Cauca department. The town sits against the Cordillera Central, with the Amaime and Cerrito rivers crossing the flat valley floor. Alfonso Bonilla Aragón International Airport (SKCL), which serves Cali, is about 17 km southwest; Cali itself is 47 km by road. Paramo de las Hermosas rises above 3,500 m in the eastern part of the municipality and forms part of Las Hermosas National Natural Park. The valley climate is consistent year-round at about 24 °C on the floor; mornings are typically clearer than afternoons.
