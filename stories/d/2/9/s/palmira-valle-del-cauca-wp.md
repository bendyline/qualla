# Palmira, Valle del Cauca

Seventeen miles east of Cali, Palmira turns sugarcane into sweetness and scholars, earning two nicknames at once: Colombia's agricultural capital and the University City.

**Location:** 3.5347, -76.2956

**Categories:** cities, agriculture, sugarcane, universities, colombia, valle-del-cauca

## Introduction

The airport that most travelers call Cali's doesn't actually sit in Cali. Alfonso Bonilla Aragon International - the main aviation gateway to southwestern Colombia - lies on the flat green carpet of sugarcane fields just outside Palmira. That little geographical slight of hand is the city in miniature: Palmira does the work, grows the crop, and lets the bigger neighbor take the credit. With more than 354,000 people and the country's most important sugar mills, it is the second largest city in the Valle del Cauca department and, in everything but fame, the engine that keeps the region running.

## Sugar, Coffee, and the Scent of Cut Cane

Palmira calls itself Colombia's agricultural capital, and the claim is not idle. The Manuelita sugar mill - founded in the nineteenth century by industrial pioneer Santiago Eder - still operates from the municipality, joined by the Providencia and Del Cauca mills. Together they turn the Cauca River valley into one of the most productive sugarcane regions on the continent. Coffee, rice, corn, and tobacco grow here too, but it is cane that defines the landscape and, at harvest, the air. The illegal burning of waste cane fouls the sky some nights, a reminder that the economy has costs locals breathe in. Sugar cane is not a romantic crop. But it is a crop that lets Palmira pay for its universities.

## The University City

Palmiranos call their town la ciudad universitaria, and the name is earned. The National University of Colombia's Palmira branch - part of the country's oldest and largest public university, founded by congressional act in 1867 - sits here beside the University of Valle's Palmira campus, whose land was donated by the Manuelita sugar company itself. Univalle has 30,000 students across its four campuses, and when its new three-story building opened on June 11, 2009, it gave the town a hub for chemistry, biology, electronics, and food science labs. Add the Pontifical Bolivarian University, Universidad Antonio Narino, and several private colleges, and you get a cane town where lectures compete with harvest season for students' attention.

## A Climate with Two Dry Seasons

Palmira sits at 1,000 meters of elevation in the tropical savanna belt, which means there are two rainy seasons and two dry ones. June through August is dry. So is December to February. In between, the sky opens. Rain at the airport averages only about 900 millimeters a year - remarkably little for a tropical city - but up in the higher parts of the municipality the total climbs to 1,700. That gradient is why a short drive east or west can feel like a different country. Palmireno weather is generous to the cane, the coffee, and the visitors who pass through in the white horse-drawn carriages that still circle the colonial plazas.

## Motorcycles, Mototaxis, and the TUPAL

Ask Palmireno friends how they get around and the answer is probably the motorcycle. The city has an unusual density of them, and in the last few years owners turned their bikes into informal taxis - mototaxis - that shuttle people around for less than a cab would charge. The taxi drivers were not amused. The city responded with TUPAL, a municipal bus system running natural-gas-powered vehicles. In 2005 Palmira got its first big shopping mall, Centro Comercial Llanogrande Plaza, popularly called La Catorce after the anchor tenant La 14 department store - which closed when the chain liquidated in 2021. A second mall, Unicentro Palmira, opened in 2014 on the northwest side. Modernity arrived on the city's own schedule.

## Estadios and Daughters

Football runs deep in Palmira - four professional men's clubs have represented the city in Colombia's second tier, and Orsomarso and Internacional F.C. de Palmira carry the colors today. But the city's football glory is mostly women's. Generaciones Palmiranas, an amateur local team, regularly sends players to the Colombian women's national squad. Estadio Deportivo Cali, built on Palmira's outskirts and inaugurated in February 2010, was the first stadium owned by a professional club in Colombia, home to ten-time champions Deportivo Cali. And the city has produced more than athletes. Esmeralda Arboleda Cadavid, born here, became Colombia's first female senator - a quiet revolution for a country where women only voted nationally from 1957.

## Flight Context

Located at 3.53 N, 76.30 W in the Cauca River valley of southwestern Colombia, at 1,000 m elevation. The region's main airport, Alfonso Bonilla Aragon International (SKCL), sits inside the municipality and serves Cali. Best viewed from 5,000-8,000 ft for the geometry of cane fields between the Western and Central Andes. Watch for smoke plumes from cane burning during harvest.
