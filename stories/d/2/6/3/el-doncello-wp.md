# El Doncello

A rubber-growing frontier town on the Amazon piedmont, founded when a settler and a Huitoto woman built a house in the jungle and others followed.

**Location:** 1.6800, -75.2850

**Categories:** town, colombia, amazon, rubber, frontier

## Introduction

On the night of March 20, 1951, a group of settlers sat up late in a jungle house at the foot of the Eastern Cordillera and decided to build a town. Jorge Abel Molina offered up two lots - one for a plaza, one for a school - and that was enough. Soon a small kiosk served as school and church, and there were the beginnings of what would become San Juan de El Doncello. The settlement took its first name from John the Baptist and its second from a local tree. The rest came from the forest itself, which Molina had entered earlier with his Huitoto partner Maria, the pair of them raising a home and a *chagra* - a forest garden - in a place that had no name yet at all.

## The Couple in the Chagra

Before there was a plaza, there was Jorge Abel Molina and Maria. He was a settler in search of land. She was Huitoto, from one of the Amazonian peoples whose territory this had been for centuries before the word Caqueta appeared on any map. They built a house together. They cleared a chagra and planted it. And somehow, in a region the Colombian government at the time still considered an empty frontier, they attracted other families who wanted the same thing. Daniel Claros came. Ricardo Espana, Jesus Collazos, Rufino Quichoya, Calixto Morales, Carlos Polanco - the list of founders is remembered locally because in a town this young, the founding is still living memory. El Doncello's origin is not conquest or corporation. It is a mixed-race marriage and a garden in the trees.

## Rubber, Roads, and the Slow Arrival of the State

The road did not reach El Doncello until 1959. The telegraph came in 1960. Electric power did not arrive until 1986 - after a local merchants' association pushed for it - and direct-dial telephone service arrived only in 1992. Before that, the whole town shared two telephone lines. This is a place where the twentieth century showed up late and in pieces. What the town did have was rubber. It is planted in the Maguare and Puerto Manrique inspectorates, El Doncello is counted among Caqueta's largest rubber producers, and a 2024 bill in Congress proposed recognizing it as the cradle of natural rubber in Colombia. Around 1959 the Caja Agraria made Maguare a colonization center, opening more than 25,000 hectares to some 3,000 settlers, then handed the project over to the agrarian reform agency Incora in 1961 because the bank lacked experience running such a scheme.

## The Missionaries of the Forest

The religious history of El Doncello runs through the Consolata Missionaries, an order from Turin that accepted the Caqueta region as a mission field in the twentieth century. Father Juan Demichelis was a pioneer of the church here. Father Juan Bautista Migani kept two promises to the town: he baptized it, on July 14, 1951, and he delivered its first teachers, Eva Cedeno de Orozco and Margarita Arboleda de Hoyos, who opened classrooms in February 1952.

## The Amazon Piedmont Problem

El Doncello sits at 480 meters above sea level, where the Eastern Cordillera drops off its last foothills and slides into the Amazon basin. The air hovers around 26 degrees Celsius year-round. Seven rivers and streams wash the municipality - the Doncello, the Anaya, the Granada, the Anayacito, the Quebradon, the San Jose, the Nemal - and the Rio Negro Lagoon draws visitors from the interior of the country to its eastern edge. But the soils of the Amazon piedmont are acidic and thin, and the economic model that has come to dominate them is extensive cattle ranching. Forest clearing, illicit crops, and the slow erosion of the land itself shadow every conversation about El Doncello's future. The fertility that Molina and Maria walked into has proven harder to hold onto than to find.

## A Municipality at Last

El Doncello climbed through the administrative ranks methodically. Police inspectorate in 1952. Corregimiento in 1956. Finally, Decree 1678 of September 7, 1967 elevated the territory to full municipal status, a change dated locally to October 12, 1967, with Jose Ignacio Rojas as its first mayor. The festivities calendar that keeps the town humming now - the Folkloric Festival of Caqueta, the San Pedrinas in June, the Departmental Rubber Festival, a commercial and cattle fair, an Old Year's parade - reflects both the indigenous and settler lineages that converge here. Holy Week is observed with the seriousness of a frontier Catholic town that remembers exactly which priest brought the church and which teacher opened the school.

## Flight Context

El Doncello sits at 1.68 degrees N, 75.28 degrees W, on the Amazon piedmont of the Eastern Cordillera of Colombia, at 480 meters elevation. The town lies about 36 km east-northeast of Florencia, the departmental capital (69 km by road), whose Gustavo Artunduaga Paredes Airport (ICAO SKFL) is about 33 km west-southwest. Bogota's El Dorado International (SKBO) is the nearest major hub, roughly 360 km north-northeast. The surrounding landscape reads as a sharp transition: Andean foothills rising to the west, a carpet of rainforest and scattered pastures to the east, and rivers braiding out toward the Amazon basin.
