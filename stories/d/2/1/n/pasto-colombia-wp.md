# Pasto, Colombia

At the foot of an active volcano, the capital of Narino mixes Andean crafts, guinea pig dinners, and a January carnival where strangers paint each other's faces for a week.

**Location:** 1.2100, -77.2747

**Categories:** cities, volcanoes, carnival, unesco, colombia, narino, crafts

## Introduction

Every year between January 2 and 7, the streets of Pasto become an agreement. Strangers smear black cosmetic paint on each other's cheeks on the 5th, white on the 6th, then throw colored talcum and party foam until everyone looks like a cake that fell off a truck. This is the Carnaval de Negros y Blancos, and UNESCO listed it as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2009. For the rest of the year, Pasto is an Andean capital of nearly 400,000 people at 2,527 meters above sea level, built literally at the foot of Galeras volcano.

## Living at Galeras's Feet

Galeras is not a postcard volcano. It is an active stratovolcano, one of Colombia's most dangerous, visible across the city that cradles it in the Atriz Valley. The Pasto Volcanological and Seismological Observatory watches its every twitch. Average highs run only 15 to 18 degrees Celsius and lows 9 to 11 - a 'cold' climate by Colombian standards - with humidity around 80 percent. Residents carry sweaters in a country famous for tropical heat. The Pan-American Highway reaches up to Pasto from Ipiales to the south, near Ecuador, and drops back down toward Popayan and eventually Bogota, 798 km and eighteen hours away by road.

## Barniz de Pasto

In a Pasto workshop, an artisan warms a piece of sap in his mouth. The resin comes from mopa-mopa, a tree from the nearby Amazonian slopes, and must be chewed soft before it can be stretched. Then, dyed with mineral pigments, it is pulled by hand into translucent sheets, cut with a knife, and laid across wooden boxes, trays, and cups. This is Barniz de Pasto, a pre-Columbian art technique that has survived more than four centuries since the Spanish arrived. The results glow like stained glass. In 2020, UNESCO added the technique to its Intangible Cultural Heritage list, making Pasto one of the rare cities with two such listings - the carnival and the craft that decorates it.

## Cuy and Chicha

The most distinctive plate on a Pastuso table is a roasted cuy - guinea pig, domesticated for food in the Andes for thousands of years before Europeans arrived. It is served split open on the plate, crisp-skinned, with potatoes and toasted corn. Some find it confronting. On January 7, the nearby village of Catambuco holds a festival devoted entirely to cuy, closing out the carnival. Quimbolitos - steamed corn dumplings in leaves - and empanadas de anejo are the everyday standards. For drinks, try hervidos: fruit juice warmed with aguardiente, a local anise spirit, until the kitchen smells like hot candy. The bucket ice cream, churned in a copper bowl packed with crushed ice, is worth finding.

## Pilgrims and Pastusos

Pasto is an old city, founded in 1537 and settled on its present site in the Atriz Valley in 1539, and it wears its religion in its walls. The eighteenth-century Iglesia de San Juan Bautista, the cathedral, San Felipe, San Sebastian, locally nicknamed 'the Bakery,' the Temple of Christ the King - one of Pasto's old nicknames is the 'Theological City.' The University of Narino shares the city with Universidad Mariana, Universidad Cooperativa, and others. Twenty-seven kilometers outside town, La Cocha lake - also called Lake Guamuez - sits at 2,800 meters with the forested Island of La Corota in its middle, a national forest sanctuary that feels more Patagonian than equatorial.

## Chaza and the Ball That Flies

In a rectangular court on the edge of town, two teams of men in leather-bound wooden paddles called drums slam a heavy ball back and forth. This is chaza, a sport unique to Narino and parts of neighboring Ecuador. It has the geometry of tennis and the ferocity of jai alai. Football came later - in November 1909, when a visiting British merchant named Leslie Osmond Spain organized a match in the Barrio San Andres - but Deportivo Pasto, founded in 1949, finally delivered a national title in June 2006, and the city erupted. Pasto also produced race walker Luis Fernando Lopez, who medaled at the 2011 World Athletics Championships in Daegu, and Carlos Daniel Hidalgo, top scorer at the 2003 FIFA U-17 World Cup.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.21 N, 77.27 W in the Atriz Valley at 2,527 m elevation, at the foot of active Galeras volcano. Antonio Narino Airport (SKPS) is 35 km north in Chachagui; flights from Bogota via Avianca. Best viewed from 12,000 ft where the volcano's crater and the city's rectangular grid are both visible. Check volcanic activity notices before flights - Galeras has erupted multiple times in recent decades.
