# Pasto

A traveler's guide to sleeping at the foot of a volcano, painting strangers' faces in January, and finding the best roasted guinea pig between Quito and Cali.

**Location:** 1.2078, -77.2772

**Categories:** travel, cities, volcanoes, carnival, colombia, narino

## Introduction

Arrive in Pasto between January 2 and 7 and you quickly learn not to wear nice clothes. That is because the city will paint you. Black on the 5th, white on the 6th, talcum powder and party foam in between. Pasto is the capital of Narino, in the cold-climate Andes at 2,527 meters above sea level, and it is one of the oldest cities in Colombia. The Galeras volcano looms from every street corner. The welcome is warmer than the weather.

## Sleeping Next to a Volcano

It is something special to sleep in a city lying at the base of a volcano. Galeras is active, and its ash has settled on rooftops in living memory. Budget hotels cluster near the bus terminal, and the Koala-Inn, set in an old mansion on Calle 18 No 22-37, offered basic rooms with WiFi and breakfast for about 25,000 COP per person as of October 2022. For a bit more comfort, the Metropol on Calle 15 or the Hotel Chambu Plaza on Carrera 20 keep prices reasonable. Nights are cold, so expect heavy blankets.

## Getting In and Getting Around

Antonio Narino Airport, about 35 km north of town in Chachagui, has flights from Bogota and Cali. Most travelers arrive by bus - direct buses run from Bogota, Cali and Popayan (about 50,000 COP from Popayan as of October 2023), and the short hop from Ipiales on the Ecuadorian border is a scenic climb through the Andes. Inside the city, taxis are inexpensive, and Pasto is compact enough that many days you will simply walk.

## Eating Cheap and Eating Weird

Pasto eats cheap, though some dishes cost more. The dish you are supposed to try is cuy - roasted guinea pig - protein-dense, low-fat, and served at specialty restaurants like Asadero de Cuyes Pinzon on Carrera 40. Casa Vasca in the San Ignacio neighborhood is another local standby, and across Narino the empanadas are made with fermented corn dough, which gives them a sharp, distinctive flavor. The local sweet, chilacuan, is made with mountain papaya. Hot drinks called hervidos - fruit juice heated with aguardiente - are the wintertime equivalent of a hug from a stranger. Chicha, fermented from corn, and guarapo, fermented from sugar cane, are the casual drinks; aguardiente de Narino is the formal one.

## Day Trips into the Andes

Laguna Verde, 72 kilometers from Pasto in the Tuquerres area, sits in the crater of the Azufral volcano, above a park entrance at around 3,700 meters. The lake is a startling mineral green. The route runs by bus to Tuquerres, then a six-kilometer hike from the park entrance - but since 2017 the Indigenous Pasto community that protects the lake, also Tuquerres's main water source, has closed it to general tourism, and visits need their authorization. Check before you travel. For a gentler day, take the road to the Chimayoy Environmental Center just four kilometers outside town. The name means 'go to meet with the sun' in the language of the Quillacinga people who lived here long before the Spanish, and the place does feel like a meeting.

## The Week the City Paints Itself

If your trip lands between January 2 and 7, everything else becomes optional. The Carnaval de Negros y Blancos is UNESCO-listed Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and nothing prepares you for it. January 4 brings the Castaneda family arrival parade, recalling the legendary arrival in town of a travelling country family in the late 1920s. On the 5th - Black's Day - people greet each other by painting black cosmetic cream on faces and arms; on the 6th, the reverse in white. The paint is meant to be cosmetic-grade and skin-safe, the floats are hand-built by local artisans over many months, and bands come from as far as Peru and Bolivia. Bring cheap clothes and an unbreakable mood.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.21 N, 77.28 W in the Atriz Valley at 2,527 m elevation in southwest Colombia, at the foot of Galeras volcano. Antonio Narino Airport (SKPS) is 35 km north in Chachagui; flights from Bogota and Cali. Cold climate year-round - average highs 15-18 C, lows around 9-11 C. Best seen from 12,000 ft for both the volcano cone and the city grid below.
