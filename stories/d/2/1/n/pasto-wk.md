# Pasto

A traveler's guide to sleeping at the foot of a volcano, painting strangers' faces in January, and finding the best roasted guinea pig between Quito and Cali.

**Location:** 1.2078, -77.2772

**Categories:** travel, cities, volcanoes, carnival, colombia, narino

## Introduction

You arrive in Pasto and your guidebook warns you not to wear nice clothes between January 2 and 7. That is because the city will paint you. Black on the 5th, white on the 6th, talcum powder and party foam in between, and if you protest the pastusos will laugh and paint you harder. Pasto is the capital of Narino, in the cold-climate Andes at 2,527 meters above sea level, and it is one of the oldest cities in Colombia. The Galeras volcano looms from every street corner. The welcome is warmer than the weather.

## Sleeping Next to a Volcano

It is magic to sleep in a city lying at the base of a volcano - and if you're lucky you'll see ashes drift down, or even spewing lava. Galeras is active, and its ash has settled on rooftops in living memory. Budget travelers cluster near the bus terminal - the Hotel Paola offers singles starting around 12,000 COP - while the Koala-Inn, set in an old mansion on Calle 18 No 22-37, runs about 25,000 COP per person with WiFi and breakfast. The administrator, Luis, is a famously friendly source of Colombia travel lore. For a bit more comfort, the Metropol on Calle 15 or the Hotel Chambu Plaza on Carrera 20 keep prices reasonable. The cold nights are the reason they hand you heavy blankets without asking.

## Getting In and Getting Around

Antonio Narino Airport, 30 minutes outside town in Chachagui, takes daily Avianca and Satena flights from Bogota and Cali. Most travelers arrive by bus - from Popayan takes about six hours and costs 50,000 COP as of October 2023; Cali runs 35,000 COP. The cheapest option is the shared ride from Ipiales on the Ecuadorian border, just 15,000 COP for a scenic climb through the Andes. Inside the city, taxis run on a fixed-price handshake - about 5,000 COP across the center, 6,000 from one end to the other - and drivers will pick you up if you call 7320000 or 7323743. Pasto is compact enough that many days you will simply walk.

## Eating Cheap and Eating Weird

Pasto eats cheap. Eating out at most places costs less than the taxi there. The dish you are supposed to try is cuy - roasted guinea pig - protein-dense, low-fat, and served at specialty restaurants like Asadero de Cuyes Pinzon on Carrera 40. Casa Vasca in the San Ignacio neighborhood does hearty empanadas made with fermented corn dough, a flavor found nowhere else. The local sweet, chilacuan, is made with mountain papaya. Hot drinks called hervidos - fruit juice heated with aguardiente - are the wintertime equivalent of a hug from a stranger. Chicha, fermented from corn, and guarapo, fermented from sugar cane, are the casual drinks; aguardiente de Narino is the formal one.

## Day Trips into the Andes

Laguna Verde, 72 kilometers from Pasto in the Tuquerres area, sits in the crater of the Azufral volcano at around 3,700 meters. The lake is a startling mineral green. You reach it by bus to Tuquerres (6,000 COP) or shared taxi (9,000 COP), then hike the final six kilometers into the park. Note that as of April 2018 the park was closed until further notice - check before you travel. For a gentler day, take the road to the Chimayoy Environmental Center just four kilometers outside town. The name means 'go to meet with the sun' in the language of the Quillacinga people who lived here long before the Spanish, and the place does feel like a meeting.

## The Week the City Paints Itself

If your trip lands between January 2 and 7, everything else becomes optional. The Carnaval de Negros y Blancos is UNESCO-listed Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, and nothing prepares you for it. January 4 brings the Castaneda family arrival parade, a re-enactment of early settlers entering the valley. On the 5th - Black's Day - people greet each other by painting black cosmetic cream on faces and arms; on the 6th, the reverse in white. The painting is consensual and skin-safe, the floats are hand-built by local artisans over many months, and bands come from as far as Peru and Bolivia. Bring cheap clothes and an unbreakable mood.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.21 N, 77.28 W in the Atriz Valley at 2,527 m elevation in southwest Colombia, at the foot of Galeras volcano. Antonio Narino Airport (SKPS) is 35 km north in Chachagui; direct flights from Bogota and Cali via Avianca and Satena. Cold climate year-round - expect 13-20 C. Best seen from 12,000 ft for both the volcano cone and the city grid below.
