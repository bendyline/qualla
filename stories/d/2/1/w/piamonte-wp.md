# Piamonte

Where the Amazon reaches toward the Pacific, a Colombian frontier municipality of oil wells, coca plots, and Inga territorial councils, only 30 years old.

**Location:** 1.1158, -76.3261

**Categories:** cities, indigenous, amazon, oil, colombia, cauca

## Introduction

By one local reckoning, the Pacific Ocean is about 335 kilometers from the municipality of Piamonte in southeastern Cauca. That doesn't sound remarkable until you realize you are already in the Amazon. Piamonte is often described as one of the places where the Amazon rainforest comes closest to the Pacific coast - a corridor where the Central Cordillera, the Eastern Cordillera, the Magdalena Valley, and the Amazon rainforest all meet. The town that sits on this geographic pressure point was founded in 1959 by a man named Aristides Perez at a spot called La Barbasca, and recognized as a municipality only on November 18, 1996. It is one of Cauca's younger municipalities, and the land it sits on has long been contested.

## The Baja Bota Caucana

Look at a map of Colombia's Cauca department and its southeastern corner narrows to a boot-shape: the Baja Bota Caucana, Piamonte's geographic home. It touches three other departments - Huila to the north, Caqueta to the east, Putumayo to the south - with the rest of Cauca to the west, and the Rio Caqueta runs along its western edge. The climate is tropical rainforest in the Koppen sense: hot, humid, rain almost every day, more than 4,000 millimeters a year. The vegetation is dense, the biodiversity is extreme, and the land climbs from about 300 meters to 2,500 meters in the Andean foothills. Its main road is the Carretera Marginal de la Selva, the Peripheral Road of the Jungle.

## Waves of Arrival

Piamonte's population is a record of successive Colombian crises. The Inga indigenous people hold land here, including the Guayuyaco resguardo in the Andean foothills. In the 1930s came the quinine boom - cinchona bark harvested for the anti-malarial drug - which pulled migrants from across the country. Then La Violencia in the 1950s and 1960s sent refugees fleeing political killings. Later came the oil boom. Napoles, the area's oldest settlement, founded by migrants from Narino, had to be moved in the early 1970s after the Caqueta River flooded it. In 2012 the municipality counted 7,241 people (DANE projections have since put it above 9,000), 12.24% of them Inga, with a significant Afro-descendant community and settlers from many parts of Colombia.

## Oil, Coca, and a Broken Promise

Piamonte's economy is an open argument between extraction and subsistence. Under its subsoil sits part of a vast oil deposit that stretches through Ecuador and Peru. From 1993 to 2008, Argosy Energy International, which Calgary-based Gran Tierra Energy bought in 2006, drilled five wells here - Mary 1, 2, 3 and 5 and Miraflor 1 - and in 2002 production was about 2,290 barrels per day. Above ground, families grow bananas, cassava, corn, and chontaduro, plus the Amazonian fruits araza and borojo, while others cultivate coca. Under Law 141 of 1994 the municipality is owed 12.5 percent of the 20 percent oil royalty, money that must go to social investment. Even so, many residents still lack electricity and sanitation. Only Piamonte's town center and Miraflor have recent electrical connections; many villages still do not.

## The Inga Resguardos

The Inga people of Piamonte are organized into nine traditional councils - Bajo Chuspizacha, San Gabriel, Alto Suspizacha, Rumiñawi, San Jose del Inchiyaco, Ambiwasi, Musurrunacuna, Caucapapungo, and Aukawasi - and hold six legally recognized resguardos, or indigenous territorial reservations: Guayuyaco, Inga Wasipanga, La Floresta Espanola, La Leona, Las Brisas, and San Rafael. These are not symbolic designations. A resguardo is a collective land title that Colombian law treats as inalienable, giving Inga authorities a real say over what happens on their territory even when oil and coca economies press in from every side. The mountainous slice of the municipality holds the Serrania de Los Churumbelos Auka-Wasi National Natural Park, a protected stretch of Amazonian jungle where spectacled bears and mountain tapirs have been recorded.

## The Corridor

The Carretera Marginal de la Selva that crosses Piamonte is no ordinary road. It is part of IIRSA, the Initiative for the Integration of Regional Infrastructure in South America - a continental program to link South America's transport networks across borders. From Piamonte the road runs to Villagarzon and Puerto Asis and out toward Ecuador; in the other direction, from Villagarzon toward Pasto and the Pacific port of Tumaco. For a town that spent decades being at the geographic edge of four departments, this corridor is what makes it, perhaps inevitably, a crossroads. Whether that status helps or harms its Inga councils and its settlers remains, like so much else in this municipality, an argument being written in real time.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.12 N, 76.33 W in the Baja Bota region of southeast Cauca, at the transition between the Andes and the Amazon rainforest. Nearest airfield: Villa Garzon (SKVG) in Putumayo, about 35 km west-southwest; Puerto Asis (SKAS) is about 70 km south and Florencia (SKFL) about 100 km northeast. Pasto's Antonio Narino (SKPS) is about 110 km west and Popayan's Guillermo Leon Valencia (SKPP) about 150 km north. Local accounts put the municipality about 335 km from the Pacific. Expect frequent cloud cover from tropical rainforest convection.
