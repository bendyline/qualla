# Llanos Basin

A savanna the size of England stretches from the Andean foothills to the Orinoco, moved by rivers and DC-3s in equal measure.

**Location:** 5.4000, -71.6667

**Categories:** Geology, Sedimentary basins, Oil and gas, Colombia, Savanna ecosystems

## Introduction

In the Llanos Basin, the buses have wings. Douglas DC-3s, seventy-year-old workhorses built before World War II, still fly cargo and passengers between grass airstrips because there are not enough roads, and the roads that exist dissolve in the wet season. Nearly 96,000 square kilometers of wavy, treeless plains stretch from the eastern foothills of the Andes to the Orinoco, spilling over the Venezuelan border. Above the grass, the DC-3s drone. Below, cattle and capybaras graze the same flood-prone flats. And below them, two kilometers and more beneath the topsoil, sits some of the richest petroleum real estate in South America.

## A Sea That Used to Be a Sea

The Llanos looks flat because it is flat. Average elevation varies only gently across the basin's nine departments - Arauca, Casanare, Meta, slivers of Boyacá, Cundinamarca, Guainía, Guaviare, and Norte de Santander. What the eye misses, the seismic survey reveals: a sedimentary pile going back to the Precambrian, stacked with source rocks like the Gachetá, reservoir rocks like the Mirador and Barco, and shale seals that trap the hydrocarbons in place. The Guiana Shield pokes through in a few dramatic spots, at the Cerros de Mavecure inselbergs in Guainía and in the tepuis of the Serranía de Chiribiquete. Those exposed shoulders of Proterozoic crystalline rock are what the rest of the basin rests on, buried beneath kilometers of younger sediment washed down from the rising Andes.

## The Rising of the Cordillera

The basin exists because the Andes rose. When the South American and North Andes plates converged, compressional stress folded rock into thrust sheets that became the Cordillera Oriental, and the weight of those new mountains pressed the adjacent crust downward. That downward flexure created the foreland trough now filled with sediment and oil. The Eastern Frontal Fault System runs 921 kilometers along this boundary, a seam in the continent that still moves. Earthquakes in 1827, 1834, 1917, 1967, 1995, and 2008 have all traced movement along the same system. When paleotemperatures in the basin dropped from 115 degrees Celsius to 70 between the Early and Late Miocene, that cooling was the signature of an uplift event, the mountains pulling the source rocks out of the hydrocarbon generation window and sealing the deposits in place.

## Colombia's Oil Heart

Nine of the twenty top-producing oil fields in Colombia are here. Rubiales, in Puerto Gaitán of Meta department, is the largest, a recent giant with reserves over 4 billion barrels and daily production above 132,000 barrels in 2016. Castilla, Chichimene, Quifa, and Caño Limón round out the majors. Caño Limón in Arauca, at the Venezuelan border, has been a target of guerrilla pipeline bombings for decades, a reminder that this quiet-looking savanna has been contested terrain through much of Colombia's recent conflict. The oil leaves via long pipelines to the Pacific and Caribbean coasts. The cattle leaves on the hoof or in refrigerated trucks that trundle west across the Andean passes toward Bogotá's markets.

## Life on the Plain

The Llanos supports a distinct culture, the llaneros, horsemen who work the cattle and whose ballads, called joropos, mix harp and maracas into a driving rhythm unlike anything else in Colombia. The rivers belong to the Orinoco watershed. The Meta alone collects a dozen tributaries from the Andean foothills - the Casanare, the Cusiana, the Upía, the Guatiquía. Lynch's swamp frog, Pseudopaludicola llanera, lives only in this ecosystem, its Latin name a small tribute to the plains. The whip scorpion Mastigoproctus colombianus, armored and slow, also calls these plains home. During the dry season, the ground hardens and cracks. During the wet season, vast stretches drown, and the capybaras - the world's largest rodent - wade through shallow water alongside jabiru storks that stand as tall as a child.

## Flying the Llanos

From altitude, the Llanos is a patchwork of greens and browns depending on the season, quilted by silver rivers winding toward the Orinoco. The treeless horizon runs forever, broken only by gallery forests along the streams and the occasional isolated sandstone massif rising like a ship out of grass. Villavicencio is the main gateway city at the basin's western edge, nestled where the savanna meets the last foothills. Its airport, Vanguardia (SKVV), sits about 4,200 feet below Bogotá's El Dorado, which means hot, wet, and stormy in the afternoons. To the east, bush strips vanish into grass. The DC-3s find them by landmark.

## Flight Context

Located at roughly 5.4°N, 71.67°W, the Llanos Basin covers 96,000 km² across northeastern Colombia. The main gateway airport is Vanguardia (SKVV) at Villavicencio. Oil-producing areas cluster near Puerto Gaitán and in Arauca department near the Venezuelan border. Cruising altitude offers striking contrasts between green savanna and the sharp rise of the Cordillera Oriental on the western horizon. Afternoon thunderstorms common in wet season (April-November).
