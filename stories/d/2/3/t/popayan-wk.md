# Popayán

Colombia's white city - a 1537 colonial capital whose Semana Santa processions are often ranked second only to Seville's, and whose cuisine earned the first UNESCO City of Gastronomy title.

**Location:** 2.4542, -76.6092

**Categories:** cities, colonial, unesco, gastronomy, semana-santa, colombia, cauca

## Introduction

During Holy Week in Popayan, volunteers shoulder floats carrying religious images through the city's colonial streets. The floats can weigh up to 500 kilograms, and carriers can come away with injured shoulders. Being chosen as a carrier is considered such an honor that people endure the injuries and carry anyway. These are Colombia's most celebrated Semana Santa processions - often said to be second only to Seville's, and listed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. In 2005, the same city became the first anywhere to be designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy. Popayan is nearly 490 years old, its historic center famously painted white, and it carries its history more publicly than almost any other place in the country.

## The White City

Popayan was founded in 1537 by Sebastian de Belalcazar, one of Pizarro's captains detached north from the conquest of Peru. The city survived the conquest and the republic and, on every subsequent whitewashing, kept its coat. This is how it became the Ciudad Blanca - colonial houses whose facades glow in morning light, balconies of carved wood, and central plazas where the cathedral and Torre del Reloj mark time for a city of roughly 350,000 people (DANE's 2025 projection). The architecture of central Popayan is so uniform that photographers can work an entire afternoon without seeing a jarring color.

## Presidents and Poets

For a city of its size, Popayan has produced an uncanny number of Colombia's political class. Guillermo Leon Valencia, president from 1962 to 1966, was born here, as were multiple earlier presidents. Francisco Jose de Caldas, the scientist and patriot executed in 1816 during the Spanish reconquest, called Popayan home, as did Camilo Torres, the Patriot lawyer shot the same year. The Universidad del Cauca, founded in 1827, is one of Colombia's oldest, and its law and medical schools still send generations of policy-makers into Bogota. Politicians who grew up inside these walls carry with them the city's particular mixture: Catholic conservatism, intellectual seriousness, and an expectation that architecture should outlast its occupants.

## Semana Santa

On five nights of Holy Week, Tuesday through Saturday, thousands of people line the sidewalks as volunteers carry religious floats - pasos - along a two-kilometer route through the Centro Historico. Nearby Puelenje has its own devotion: the Amo Jesus Nazareno of Puelenje, an eighteenth-century polychrome wood carving of the Quito school, honored with a feast of fireworks, ceremonies, and chirimia music. UNESCO inscribed the Holy Week processions, held since colonial times, as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009. The floats are carried on human shoulders, a privilege often passed down within families, and the year-long preparations are taught to children.

## City of Gastronomy

UNESCO's Creative Cities initiative tapped Popayan in 2005 as its first designated City of Gastronomy. The recognition is deserved. The market near Plaza Bolivar, north of the Puente del Humilladero, serves cheap meals that introduce you to the local canon: carantanta, a corn chip of the Cauca department eaten with soup; pipian, a peanut sauce served with tamales or small potato empanadas; champus, a drink made from crushed maize, panela, lulo, pineapple, and orange leaves; manjar blanco, a milk-rice cream; breva calada, figs cooked with panela served over white cheese, a Christmas specialty; helado de paila, ice cream churned in a heavy copper bowl. The signature drink is aguardiente caucano, an anise spirit and a fixture of local parties.

## Day Trips in the Andes

Coconuco, an hour from Popayan, has the Hirviendos hot springs. Purace National Natural Park, two hours out, is home to Andean spectacled bears and to the Purace volcano, which Colombia's Geological Service put on orange alert in August 2026, warning visitors away from its craters. Silvia, also an hour away, hosts a Tuesday market famous throughout Colombia, held in the land of the Misak (Guambiano) people. San Agustin, about five hours away by road, shelters hundreds of pre-Columbian stone statues, most carved in the first eight centuries CE - UNESCO World Heritage since 1995. A note for the traveler: Cauca has long been one of the more dangerous departments in Colombia - a 2011 car bomb at one of Popayan's entrances wounded seventeen people - and armed-group violence in rural Cauca rose again in the 2020s, so check current advisories.

## Flight Context

Located at 2.45 N, 76.61 W on the Pubenza plateau near the upper Cauca River, at about 1,760 m elevation, in southwestern Colombia. Guillermo Leon Valencia Airport (SKPP), on the city's northern edge, has scheduled flights from Bogota; Cali is about 135 km north by road. The white-walled historic center shows as a pale grid. The Purace volcano rises about 27 km east-southeast, and Nevado del Huila looms about 85 km northeast.
