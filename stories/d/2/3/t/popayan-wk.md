# Popayán

Colombia's white city - a 1537 colonial capital whose Semana Santa processions are second only to Seville's, and whose cuisine earned the first UNESCO City of Gastronomy title.

**Location:** 2.4542, -76.6092

**Categories:** cities, colonial, unesco, gastronomy, semana-santa, colombia, cauca

## Introduction

During Holy Week in Popayan, volunteers shoulder floats carrying religious images through the city's colonial streets. The floats can weigh up to 500 kilograms. Dislocated shoulders are common. Being chosen as a carrier is considered such an honor that people endure the injuries and carry anyway. These are Colombia's most celebrated Semana Santa processions - second only to Seville's in scale, and listed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. In 2005, the same city became the first anywhere to be designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy. Popayan is 487 years old, painted entirely white by municipal ordinance, and carries its history more publicly than almost any other place in the country.

## The White City

Popayan was founded in 1537 by Sebastian de Belalcazar, one of Pizarro's captains detached north from the conquest of Peru. The city survived the conquest and the republic and, on every subsequent whitewashing, kept its coat. This is how it became the Ciudad Blanca - colonial houses whose facades glow in morning light, balconies of carved wood, and central plazas where the cathedral and Torre del Reloj mark time for a population of around 220,000. The architecture of central Popayan is so uniform that photographers can work an entire afternoon without seeing a jarring color. When the city appears in Colombian film or television, it is always itself - a vision of the country as it looked when Simon Bolivar passed through.

## Presidents and Poets

For a city of its size, Popayan has produced an uncanny number of Colombia's political class. Guillermo Leon Valencia, president from 1962 to 1966, was born here, as were multiple earlier presidents. Francisco Jose de Caldas, the scientist and patriot executed in 1816 during the Spanish reconquest, called Popayan home, as did Camilo Torres, the Patriot lawyer shot the same year. The Universidad del Cauca, founded in 1827, is one of Colombia's oldest, and its law and medical schools still send generations of policy-makers into Bogota. Politicians who grew up inside these walls carry with them the city's particular mixture: Catholic conservatism, intellectual seriousness, and an expectation that architecture should outlast its occupants.

## Semana Santa

Every night of Holy Week, thousands of people line the sidewalks as volunteers carry religious floats - pasos - through the Centro Historico. The Amo Jesus Nazareno of Puelenje, a polychrome wood carving made in the Quito school in the eighteenth century, processes on Holy Wednesday accompanied by fireworks, ceremonies, and chirimia music. UNESCO inscribed these processions as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009, citing more than 450 continuous years of unbroken practice. The floats are carried on human shoulders, in rotations organized by families who pass the privilege down across generations. Dropping a paso is considered a failure that takes years to live down. The crowd does not cheer. It watches, in a kind of breath-held silence that anyone who has ever attended Semana Santa in Seville will recognize instantly.

## City of Gastronomy

UNESCO's Creative Cities initiative tapped Popayan in 2005 as its first designated City of Gastronomy. The recognition is deserved. The market near Plaza Bolivar, north of the Puente del Humilladero, serves meals from 5,000 COP that introduce you to the local canon: carantanta, a corn chip of the Cauca department eaten with soup; pipian, a peanut sauce served with tamales or small potato empanadas; champus, a drink made from crushed maize, panela, lulo, pineapple, and orange leaves; manjar blanco, a milk-rice cream; breva calada, figs cooked with panela served over white cheese, a Christmas specialty; helado de paila, ice cream churned in a heavy copper bowl. The signature drink is aguardiente caucano, an anise spirit stronger than wine and, by local custom, the only proper fuel for an informal party.

## Day Trips in the Andes

Coconuco, an hour from Popayan, has the Hirviendos hot springs; HostelTrail runs a popular downhill cycling trip there, leaving at 10 PM and returning by late afternoon. Purace National Natural Park, two hours out, lets you trek the Purace volcano or look for Andean spectacled bears; the bus leaves the main station at 5 AM. Silvia, also an hour away, hosts a Tuesday market famous throughout Colombia, held in the land of the Guambiano people. San Agustin, five hours south, shelters dozens of pre-Columbian stone statues carved between 100 BCE and 800 CE - UNESCO World Heritage since 1995. A note for the traveler: Cauca has in past decades been one of the more dangerous departments in Colombia, with car bombs rocking central Popayan as recently as 2011. Security has improved substantially, but check current advisories.

## Flight Context

Located at 2.45 N, 76.61 W in the Cauca River valley at 1,738 m elevation, in southwestern Colombia. Guillermo Leon Valencia Airport (SKPP) handles four daily flights from Bogota (no flights to Cali, which is 100 minutes by road). The city's famous white-walled historic center is visible from 8,000-10,000 ft as a gridded pale square on the valley floor. The Purace volcano rises 35 km east - a frequent feature in the view - and Nevado del Huila looms further northeast.
