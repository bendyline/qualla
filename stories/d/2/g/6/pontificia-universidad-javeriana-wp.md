# Pontificia Universidad Javeriana

Founded in 1623 by Jesuits, closed for 163 years, and reopened in 1937, Bogota's Javeriana has educated Colombian presidents, Academy Award nominees, and a Miss Universe.

**Location:** 4.6289, -74.0649

**Categories:** universities, jesuit, education, colombia, bogota, history

## Introduction

In 1767, the king of Spain signed an order expelling the Jesuits from every colony of the empire. In Santa Fe de Bogota, this meant the closing of the University and Academy of Saint Francis Xavier, founded only 144 years earlier by the Society of Jesus. Its doors stayed shut for 163 years. When the restoration finally came and the School of Economics and Legal Sciences opened in 1937, the Pontificia Universidad Javeriana resumed a story it had started in 1623 - and has since educated more Colombian presidents than any other university in the country, along with a Miss Universe, an Academy Award nominee, a Tony-nominated Broadway producer, and the neuroscientist who ran NASA's Neurolab.

## From Colegio to University

The Society of Jesus first arrived in Bogota in 1604 and founded a college connected to the San Bartolome School and Cloister. In 1623, the royal Audience and the Archbishop formally recognized the academic degrees the college was conferring - and that act of recognition is the birth certificate of the Javeriana. Early graduates included Pedro Claver, the Spanish priest who would spend four decades in Cartagena ministering to enslaved Africans arriving from the Middle Passage and who was canonized as Saint Peter Claver. From those first classes came a Colombian educational tradition older than Colombia itself. When the Jesuits were expelled, the university vanished. When the order was restored, so was it.

## The Chapinero Campus

The university's main campus sits in the Chapinero neighborhood of eastern Bogota, a 180-hectare - 445-acre - property that has functioned as an open campus since 1940. Pedestrians can wander through it. Parque Nacional Enrique Olaya Herrera borders it to the south; the emblematic Carrera Septima bounds its west side; and the Cerros Orientales - the eastern hills that define Bogota's skyline - rise behind it. The first modern building, the Edificio Emilio Arango S.J., was completed in 1951. The Hospital Universitario San Ignacio followed in 1955. By 2020, the university had 45 buildings, 18 cafeterias, 130 laboratories, a 15-story Engineering Laboratory Building (the tallest on campus), and the nicknamed 'The Arc' - the Jorge Hoyos S.J. Building, its curved form opened in 2016.

## The Cali Branch

In October 1970, a group of working accountants in Cali who wanted to complete university degrees petitioned the Society of Jesus. The result was a Public Accounting program that grew into the Pontificia Universidad Javeriana Cali, formally named a 'branch' on November 20, 1978. Today the Cali campus sits in Pance, at the foot of the Farallones de Cali mountains, a rural setting where students sometimes spot sloths, armadillos, and brightly colored birds during class breaks. It offers 19 undergraduate programs, with 35 research groups, a radio station (Javeriana Stereo FM 107.5), and academic cooperation agreements with universities in 87 countries. The law school is the most prestigious in southwestern Colombia, and the moot court facility is among the most complete on the continent.

## An Alumni List That Reads Like a Country

Open the Javeriana alumni roster and Colombia's modern history pours out. Ernesto Samper, president from 1994 to 1998. Misael Pastrana, president from 1970 to 1974. Gustavo Petro, Colombia's first leftist president, elected in 2022. Vice presidents, foreign ministers, defense ministers. Among non-politicians: Rodolfo Llinas, the neuroscientist who directed NASA's Neurolab and won the Ralph W. Gerard Prize for lifetime achievement in neuroscience; Catalina Sandino Moreno, nominated for an Academy Award in 2005 for Maria Full of Grace; Henry Krieger, the Tony-nominated and Grammy-winning Broadway producer of Dreamgirls; Paulina Vega, Miss Universe 2014; and Fonseca, the Latin Grammy-winning singer. The list of Olympic medallists, architects, and writers runs for pages - a university that has, over four centuries, quietly trained the people who run a country.

## Libraries and the Muisca Question

The Javeriana has four libraries. The largest is the Alfonso Borrero Cabal S.J. General Library, named for a former rector. The Mario Valenzuela S.J. Library, specializing in philosophy and theology, is rated the best of its kind in Latin America. The Alfonso Llano Escobar S.J. Bio-ethics Library and the CIRE Ignatius library complete the set, holding 418,008 titles among them. In research, the Javeriana is among the leading centers for the study of the Muisca people - the indigenous civilization that inhabited the high plain of Bogota before the Spanish arrived, whose gold work inspired the El Dorado legend and whose language and territorial rights Javeriana scholars have worked to document. In the QS World University Rankings for 2023, the university was placed #382 globally, #18 in Latin America, and #3 in Colombia. Not bad for an institution that spent 163 years as a ruin.

## Flight Context

Located at 4.63 N, 74.06 W in the Chapinero neighborhood of eastern Bogota, Colombia, at 2,640 m elevation. El Dorado International Airport (SKBO) is about 10 km west. The Bogota campus nestles against the Cerros Orientales - the eastern foothills of the Andes that rise abruptly behind the city - making it recognizable from altitude by the Monserrate sanctuary and the abrupt green hills to the campus's immediate east. A second campus in Cali (SKCL) lies 300 km southwest.
