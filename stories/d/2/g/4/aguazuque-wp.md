# Aguazuque

An archaeological site on the Bogotá savanna where five thousand years of human life — hunter-gatherers becoming farmers — are preserved under two metres of Andean earth.

**Location:** 4.6022, -74.2764

**Categories:** Andean preceramic, Pre-Columbian archaeological sites, Archaeological sites in Colombia, Altiplano Cundiboyacense, Muisca precursors

## Introduction

Five thousand years ago, on a low hill above what is now the Bogotá River, a group of people did something that looks small from a distance and is actually enormous: they stopped moving quite so much. At Aguazuque, on the flat highlands of the Altiplano Cundiboyacense at 2,600 metres, archaeologists have uncovered round houses framed with deer bones, firepits still thick with charcoal, stone tools knapped from local chert, and fifty-nine burials of the people who made them. The site sits quietly today in the western edge of Soacha, hemmed in by modern farms. But under those farms, the long hinge between hunting and agriculture is still pressed into the ground.

## A Hill Above the River

Aguazuque occupies a low, oval rise on the southwestern edge of the Bogotá savanna, cradled by higher hills that climb to 2,850 metres. The Bogotá River loops around the site in a wide bend. Geologically, the hill is an anticline of Guadalupe Group sandstones, with Late Pleistocene and early Holocene sediments — claystones, layers of volcanic ash, the old lake bed of Pleistocene Lake Humboldt — laid down on top. The climate is cool, averaging 13 °C, and drier than most of the savanna. The area archaeologists have investigated is only 76 square metres, excavated to a depth of two metres — a small window onto a long story.

## Before the Pottery

The oldest dated layer at Aguazuque reaches back to about 5,025 years before present — roughly 3000 BCE. Deeper history surrounds it: El Abra, at the northern edge of the Bogotá savanna, preserves stone tools from 12,500 years ago; Tibitó dates to 11,400 BP, Tequendama to 11,000, Checua to 8,500. Aguazuque belongs to the later, pivotal chapter, when people across the Altiplano were moving out of rock shelters and into open-air settlements. Their round houses, two to four metres across, were excavated slightly into the earth and framed with deer bones driven inward at an angle, or with wooden poles. Inside, the packed-earth floors were painted red using pigments from animal bones and shells, or covered with sandstone fragments and volcanic ash. Fires burned against the floor; ash and charcoal still stain the soil.

## A Long Meal

White-tailed deer were the central food. Bones of guinea pigs appear throughout the layers — at some points domesticated, at others not. The diet also included nine-banded armadillo, lowland paca, peccary, spectacled bear, ocelot, puma, coati, opossum, and little red brocket, along with turtles, fish from the savanna's wetlands, and invertebrates. In the uppermost layers, caiman bones appear — an animal of the hot Magdalena River lowlands, far to the west. That one shift in the bone record tells a story by itself: the people of Aguazuque were moving beyond their plateau, trading or travelling into tropical country. The middle layers of the sequence preserve the earliest agricultural evidence at the site: seeds of Cucurbita pepo, the squash family, alongside wild tubers like oca and yam. Only in the uppermost beds — around 2,800 years before present — does pottery appear, marking the transition to the Herrera Period.

## Fifty-nine Graves

Fifty-nine burials have been excavated at Aguazuque — some individual, some double, some collective. One grave held the remains of twenty-three adults and children together, which archaeologists have tentatively interpreted as victims of an epidemic, though no disease traces survive. The dead were laid on their sides or backs, arms crossed over the chest, legs folded against the abdomen — a posture that would continue into later Muisca mummification practice thousands of years afterward. Stone tools, mortars, and scrapers were placed around them. Pieces of rock, painted red or black, accompanied one body. Offerings of white-tailed deer, guinea pig, and peccary meat were included for the journey the survivors believed the dead were taking. Paleopathology has offered a quiet portrait of the people: average height about 160 centimetres, average brain volume around 1,400 cubic centimetres, osteoarthritis in almost three-quarters of the skeletons, dental cavities more common here than at nearby Tequendama or Checua. These were not statistics. They ate, suffered, buried their dead with care, and left enough of themselves behind that we can still recognise the gesture.

## Archaeology on a Living Landscape

Excavation at Aguazuque began in 1986, led by Colombian archaeologist Gonzalo Correal Urrego, who published his findings in 1990 in "Aguazuque — evidencias de cazadores, recolectores y plantadores en la altiplanicie de la Cordillera Oriental." The site sits in the municipality of Soacha, now the Colombian capital's largest satellite city, and the uppermost layer of the dig has been disturbed by modern farming — a reminder that the hill was never really abandoned. In 2014, one of the largest pre-Columbian towns ever found in the area was excavated nearby in Soacha, with the remains of 2,200 people and over 600 ceramic pots. The thread that runs from Aguazuque's hunter-gatherers through Herrera Period potters to the Muisca whom the Spanish conquered in 1537 is unbroken. These are ancestors, not specimens — people whose descendants still live on this plateau today.

## Flight Context

Aguazuque is located at approximately 4.60°N, 74.28°W, in the municipality of Soacha, Cundinamarca Department, on the Bogotá savanna at 2,600 metres elevation. The site lies roughly 25 km southwest of Bogotá's El Dorado International Airport (SKBO), between Mosquera and San Antonio del Tequendama. From altitude, look for the broad flat plain of the savanna ringed by the Eastern Cordillera, the meandering Bogotá River, and the Tequendama Falls to the southwest where the river plunges off the plateau. Weather is cool year-round with common afternoon hail; expect cloud build-ups over the surrounding mountains.
