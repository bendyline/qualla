# Manta, Cundinamarca

A Muisca name meaning 'your farmfields' attaches to a small Andean town that may be the birthplace of Jorge Eliécer Gaitán and has been quietly emptying toward Bogotá for sixty years.

**Location:** 5.0081, -73.5403

**Categories:** Towns, Colombia, Muisca heritage, Cundinamarca

## Introduction

The name means your fields. In the Chibcha language of the Muisca, Manta is a word of address - it tells somebody what is theirs. The town in Colombia's Cundinamarca department, 90 kilometers northeast of Bogotá in the Almeidas Province of the Tenza Valley, has carried that name since the Muisca Confederation farmed these slopes a thousand years ago. About 4,500 people live here year round now, a number that can swell to 16,000 on holiday weekends when Bogotanos return to family fincas. The town sits on a single plateau where the land briefly flattens between 1,500 and 3,400 meters of elevation. Everything above and below the town is steep.

## The Lake That Was a Town

According to local founding myth, the flat center of modern Manta was once a small lake, and in the middle of the lake sat an island on which the first settlers saw the image of San Joaquín. The church that now stands at the center of town takes that name. The myth encodes a real geological fact - the plateau's shape suggests an ancient lake basin - and a real historical fact. The first inhabitants were Muisca, part of a confederation that grew maize and potatoes and traded emeralds with neighboring civilizations. Another village name nearby, Fuchatoque, was the seat of a former cacique with the same name, and the word survives as the name of a vereda on the eastern side of town. The Spanish arrived, conquered, and redistributed the land. The Muisca inhabitants of Manta were displaced to the neighboring municipality of Tibiritá. The Spanish and mestizos stayed to form the town that exists now.

## Founded in 1773, Fought Over Ever Since

Modern Manta was officially founded on July 24, 1773, by José Joaquín Pompeya and Juan José Bernal Ramírez. The jurisdiction was carved out of an area called Manta Grande, with a piece added from the old municipality of Guateque. In the late nineteenth century, the town produced one of the key figures in Colombia's federalist-centralist wars. General Daniel Aldana, born in Manta, served as president of the Federal State of Cundinamarca and led a failed revolutionary coup against the centralist President Rafael Núñez. He was removed from office, and Cundinamarca was reorganized as a Federal District by Decree 769 of November 7, 1885. The early twentieth century brought more fighting - the long, bloody feud between Liberal and Conservative parties that would culminate in La Violencia. Some trenches from that period still scar the hillsides, slowly filling with scrub.

## A Possible Birthplace

Manta may be where Jorge Eliécer Gaitán was born. The 1948 assassination of Gaitán, the populist Liberal leader, triggered the Bogotazo - one of the worst urban riots in twentieth-century Latin America - and opened the decade of La Violencia that followed. His official birthplace is Cucunubá or Bogotá in various records, but Manta is listed among the plausible locations. In a country where populist saints loom large in political memory, being among the contenders for Gaitán's cradle carries weight. Whether he was born here or not, he visited. His oratory echoed in the plazas of small Cundinamarca towns, and the trenches in the hills above Manta are partly his legacy, dug by people convinced after his death that only force could save their politics.

## Lake on the Hill, Creek That Strikes

The geography of Manta is vertical. Above the town rises Cerro de la Petaca at 3,400 meters, carrying at its summit the Laguna del Cerro - the Lake of the Hill - now managed as a municipal nature reserve. Below the plateau, the Aguacia River splits the territory in two, joining the Machetá River at the northern boundary to form the Sunuba, which feeds the Esmeralda Reservoir at Chivor. Waterfalls thread the slopes. The creek of El Golpe, the Strike, is named for the sound its waterfalls make hitting stone in the rainy season. The climate is classified as dry high-Andean forest, pleasant all year between 18 and 23 degrees Celsius, influenced more by winds from the Llanos Orientales than by the Bogotá savanna. In La Niña years, when the rest of Colombia floods, Manta stays comparatively dry.

## Flying Over the Tenza Valley

From altitude, the Tenza Valley is a deeply cut landscape of green ridges, narrow farms, and sudden drops. Manta itself shows as a small grid on a lone plateau, surrounded by much steeper terrain than most travelers expect of central Colombia. El Dorado International (SKBO) in Bogotá is the gateway; the drive out takes about two hours, longer in rain. In clear weather the snow peak of Nevado del Ruiz is visible to the west. Afternoon cloud builds fast over the Cordillera Oriental. The best aerial views come in the early morning, when the valley below town has not yet fogged over and the ridge beyond it is washed in oblique light.
