# Paja Formation

A 450-kilometer stripe of black shale and multicolored claystone in central Colombia preserves the world's most complete record of Early Cretaceous marine reptiles.

**Location:** 5.5000, -73.5000

**Categories:** paleontology, geology, fossils, marine reptiles, colombia, cretaceous

## Introduction

In 2019, turtle expert Edwin Cadena and colleagues described a fossil from the hills outside Villa de Leyva: a sea turtle that had died some 120 million years ago with at least 48 eggs still inside her. She belongs to Desmatochelys padillai, named in 2015 as the oldest definitive sea turtle known. She is not an isolated find but one of many from the Paja Formation, a band of Cretaceous rock that crosses four Colombian departments and holds what the International Union of Geological Sciences has called the planet's most complete record of Early Cretaceous marine reptiles.

## A Shallow Sea, a Hungry Seafloor

About 125 million years ago, a warm, shallow sea covered most of what is now central Colombia. Its shoreline ran roughly west to east, and its bottom waters held little oxygen. Corpses sank into mud where scavengers could not reach them. That poisonous stillness is what turned the sediments into a Lagerstatte - a fossil bed so fine that skin impressions, embryos, and soft tissue can survive. The Paja Formation's three members - Lower Black Shales, the multicolored Mottled Claystones, and the nodule-rich upper member - read like chapters of that anoxic story, from the late Hauterivian through the late Aptian, capturing the Selli ocean crisis on the way.

## The Villa de Leyva Bestiary

Around the colonial town of Villa de Leyva, the Paja outcrops as a folded, faulted stretch of desert called La Candelaria. From these hills palaeontologists have pulled a menagerie that reads like a fever dream. Sachicasaurus vitae, a ten-meter subadult pliosaur, its skull alone longer than an adult man. The ichthyosaur Kyhytysuka sachicarum, named for a Muisca word meaning 'the one who cuts with something sharp.' Plesiosaurs with necks like rigging - Callawayasaurus, Leivanectes, Stenorhynchosaurus. And the improbable outlier, Padillasaurus leivaensis, a long-necked sauropod dinosaur described in 2015 as the first Cretaceous brachiosaurid known outside North America, and a rare land animal among the sea creatures. How it washed out to sea, nobody knows.

## Floors of Ammonites

In the Convento del Santo Ecce Homo, not far from Villa de Leyva, the friars did something most monasteries would never dream of. They built ammonites into the floor and walls. Fossils - spiral-shelled cephalopods - are set directly into the stones, walked over by pilgrims and tourists. These ammonites come from the Paja Formation, where they are so abundant that entire horizons are practically paved with them. The Paja's middle member also preserves fossil wood riddled with the tunnels of Cretaceous pholadoid bivalves - relatives of today's shipworms and piddocks - that bored through logs drifting for long periods across that vanished sea, leaving wooden lattices of their appetite.

## Emeralds and Their Cousins

For decades geologists assumed the Paja Formation hosted Colombia's legendary emeralds. The reality turned out to be more specific. The emerald-bearing rock was split off as a separate unit, the Muzo Formation, and the Paja was left with a consolation prize: gypsum quarries in Santander, sphalerite and malachite near Guavata, pyrite and galena near Otanche, lead and zinc around Paime and Yacopi. Argon dating of muscovite from the emerald districts of Borbur and Muzo shows the mineralization happened long after the sea vanished - in the Late Eocene and Early Oligocene, about 37 to 31 million years ago, when hot fluids moved through the ancient mud. The Paja is also a minor source rock for oil in the Middle Magdalena Valley. It gave up its dead only to be mined again.

## Why It Matters

In October 2022 the International Union of Geological Sciences added the Marine Reptile Lagerstatte of the Ricaurte Alto to its list of the world's 100 geological heritage sites, citing the world's most complete record of Lower Cretaceous marine reptiles and their fauna. Around Villa de Leyva, the fossils already support three institutions: the Centro de Investigaciones Paleontologicas, the Museo El Fosil, and the dinosaur-themed park Gondava. For a place whose tourist trade was once just whitewashed walls and cobblestones, the Paja Formation has added something older - the memory of a sea that is still, quietly, giving up its monsters.

## Flight Context

Centered near 5.50 N, 73.50 W in the hills around Villa de Leyva, Boyaca. Best viewed from 10,000-15,000 ft for a sense of the 450-km northeast-southwest band of folded black shale. Nearest airports: El Dorado International (SKBO) in Bogota about 115 km southwest; Alberto Lleras Camargo (SKSO) at Sogamoso, about 60 km east-northeast, is smaller and closer. Watch for the bare hills of the Desierto de La Candelaria west of Villa de Leyva.
