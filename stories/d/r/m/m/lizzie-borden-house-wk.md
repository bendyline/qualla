# The Lizzie Borden House

The Fall River house where Andrew and Abby Borden were axe-murdered in 1892 - where Lizzie was tried and acquitted, and where guests now pay to sleep in the murder rooms while eating breakfast that includes 'bloody' pancakes.

**Location:** 41.7015, -71.1631

**Categories:** massachusetts, true-crime, haunted, victorian, murder

## Introduction

On August 4, 1892, Andrew Borden and his wife Abby were hacked to death with a hatchet in their Fall River, Massachusetts, home. Andrew was killed on the sitting room sofa; Abby was found in the upstairs guest room, struck 19 times. The prime suspect was Andrew's 32-year-old daughter Lizzie, who was home at the time, claimed to have been in the barn, and gave contradictory statements to police. She was tried and acquitted in 1893 - the jury couldn't believe a well-bred woman could commit such violence. The rhyme children still chant ('Lizzie Borden took an axe...') convicted her in the court of public opinion. The house is now a bed-and-breakfast where guests can sleep in the very rooms where the murders occurred, eat breakfast on the murder date, and tour the crime scene. It's either profoundly disrespectful or the logical endpoint of American true-crime obsession. Probably both.

## The Murders

Andrew Borden was a successful businessman, notoriously frugal despite his wealth. His second wife Abby was despised by his daughters Lizzie and Emma. On August 4, 1892, while Emma was away, someone entered the house and killed Abby Borden with a hatchet - 19 blows to the head. About 90 minutes later, Andrew was killed on the sitting room sofa - 11 blows. The Irish maid, Bridget Sullivan, was in the house but claimed to have been napping. Lizzie claimed she'd been in the barn looking for fishing sinkers. She was arrested after police found inconsistencies in her statements and after she was seen burning a dress days after the murders. The weapon was never found.

## The Trial

The trial of Lizzie Borden was a national sensation. Newspapers covered every detail. The prosecution argued that Lizzie had motive (she hated her stepmother and wanted her father's money), opportunity (she was home), and had behaved suspiciously (burning the dress, inconsistent statements). The defense argued that a woman of Lizzie's standing couldn't commit such brutal violence. The judge excluded Lizzie's contradictory statements to police. The all-male jury deliberated 90 minutes before acquitting. Lizzie inherited her father's estate, moved to a larger house, and lived in Fall River until her death in 1927. She never confessed. Most historians believe she was guilty. No one else was ever charged.

## The House

The Borden house at 230 Second Street is a modest Greek Revival home, unremarkable except for its history. After the murders, the Borden sisters moved away but kept the house in the family until 1948. Various owners used it as apartments and offices. In 1996, it was converted to a bed-and-breakfast and museum. The house has been restored to its 1892 appearance using crime scene photographs. Guests can stay in the room where Abby was murdered, the room where Andrew was murdered, or other rooms with lesser historical significance. The furniture, wallpaper, and layout match the original crime scene. It's preservation through monetization.

## The Experience

Staying at the Lizzie Borden Bed & Breakfast is not for everyone. The rooms are named for the victims. The breakfast includes 'bananas borden' (bananas shaped like a hatchet), 'bloody' cranberry juice, and johnnycakes served where Andrew's blood pooled. Tours explain the case in gruesome detail, pointing out exactly where Abby fell, exactly where Andrew's head struck the sofa, exactly where the blood splattered. Guests report paranormal activity: footsteps, cold spots, voices. The house has appeared on numerous ghost-hunting television shows. Whether you're drawn by true crime, Victorian history, or the paranormal, the Borden house delivers a genuinely unsettling experience.

## Visiting the Lizzie Borden House

The Lizzie Borden Bed & Breakfast Museum is located at 230 Second Street in Fall River, Massachusetts. Day tours are available; overnight stays require advance reservation and cost significantly more than standard hotels. August 4 - the murder anniversary - books months in advance. The museum includes crime scene artifacts, newspaper coverage, and trial exhibits. Fall River has additional Borden-related sites: the Oak Grove Cemetery (where the Bordens are buried) and the Maplecroft mansion (where Lizzie lived after the acquittal). Fall River is 50 miles south of Boston; Providence is 20 miles west. T.F. Green Airport in Providence is the nearest commercial service. The experience is macabre by design. Come prepared.
