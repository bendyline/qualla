# Hartford: The Insurance Capital Where Mark Twain Lived

Connecticut's capital where America's oldest newspaper still publishes, where Mark Twain wrote his greatest works in a Gothic mansion, and where the insurance industry built a skyline of corporate towers along the Connecticut River.

**Location:** 41.7670, -72.6770

**Categories:** connecticut, hartford, city, capital, insurance

## Introduction

Hartford was founded in 1635, making it among the oldest cities in America - predating Boston, predating New York, one of the first English settlements in New England. The Fundamental Orders adopted here in 1639 became one of the first written constitutions in the Western tradition, earning Connecticut its 'Constitution State' nickname. The Hartford Courant has published continuously since 1764, America's oldest newspaper. When Mark Twain moved to Hartford in 1874, he built an eccentric Gothic mansion on Farmington Avenue where he wrote 'The Adventures of Tom Sawyer,' 'Adventures of Huckleberry Finn,' and 'A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur's Court.' The insurance industry arrived in the 1800s and never left - Aetna, The Hartford, Cigna, and others made this city America's insurance capital, their glass and steel towers defining the modern skyline.

## Mark Twain's Hartford

Samuel Clemens moved to Hartford in 1874 to be near his publisher, building a 25-room Victorian Gothic mansion that cost $31,000 - over a million in today's money. The house on Farmington Avenue featured a grand staircase, a billiard room where Twain wrote into the night, and a butler's pantry phone (one of the first residential telephones in America). Twain lived here for 17 years, his most productive period, writing 'Tom Sawyer,' 'Huck Finn,' 'The Prince and the Pauper,' and 'A Connecticut Yankee.' Financial troubles forced the family to leave in 1891. His neighbor Harriet Beecher Stowe, author of 'Uncle Tom's Cabin,' lived next door; the Stowe house remains open for tours alongside Twain's mansion in what's now called Nook Farm.

## The Insurance Capital

Hartford's insurance industry began after fire devastated New York City in 1835 - New York insurers went bankrupt, but Hartford firms survived and expanded. The Hartford Steam Boiler Inspection and Insurance Company pioneered industrial equipment insurance after devastating boiler explosions. Aetna, founded in 1853, became one of the nation's largest health insurers. The Travelers, The Hartford, Phoenix - the industry clustered here, building headquarters that transformed the skyline. By the late 20th century, insurance employed more than 50,000 in the Hartford region. The industry has consolidated and downsized since, but Hartford remains synonymous with insurance in American business.

## America's Oldest Firsts

Hartford collects 'oldest' and 'first' superlatives like a trophy case. The Wadsworth Atheneum, founded in 1842, is America's oldest public art museum. Bushnell Park, completed in 1868, was the first publicly funded park in the nation. Hartford Public High School, established in 1638, is the second-oldest secondary school in America. The Hartford Courant began publishing in 1764 - older than the Constitution, older than the Declaration of Independence, still publishing daily. The Ancient Burying Ground, behind Center Church downtown, contains graves dating to 1640. The old State House, completed in 1796 and designed by Charles Bulfinch, now houses a museum of Connecticut history.

## Connecticut's Capital

The Connecticut State Capitol rises on Bushnell Park, a gold-domed Gothic Revival structure completed in 1878. The building houses the Connecticut General Assembly and the governor's offices. Inside, the Hall of Flags contains battle standards from Connecticut regiments dating to the Civil War. Hartford shares capital duties with New Haven until 1875, when Hartford won permanent designation. The city has struggled economically since deindustrialization and insurance industry consolidation, but remains the seat of state government and the heart of the Hartford-Springfield metropolitan area - the Knowledge Corridor, with more than two million people and 29 colleges and universities.

## Gateway to New England

Bradley International Airport (BDL) serves the Hartford-Springfield region from Windsor Locks, 12 miles north. Amtrak's Northeast Regional and Hartford Line connect Hartford to New Haven, Springfield, and points beyond. I-84 and I-91 intersect downtown. The Connecticut River flows south through the city toward Long Island Sound. From altitude, Hartford appears as a medium-sized city at the river's bend - the insurance towers defining the skyline, the gold dome of the State Capitol visible in Bushnell Park, the historic neighborhoods spreading west toward Mark Twain's Gothic mansion. What appears from the air as Connecticut's modest capital is where American insurance was invented and where America's greatest humorist wrote his masterpieces.
