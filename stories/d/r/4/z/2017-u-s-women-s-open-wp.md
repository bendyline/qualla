# 2017 U.S. Women's Open

Park Sung-hyun's stunning comeback victory at the 72nd U.S. Women's Open at Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster, New Jersey, turned a seven-stroke deficit into a historic first major title.

## Introduction

Seven strokes back after 36 holes, Park Sung-hyun stood on the wrong side of a seemingly insurmountable gap. The 72nd U.S. Women's Open, played in July 2017 over the rolling fairways of Trump National Golf Club in Bedminster, New Jersey, had been a test of patience and precision all week. Park had won ten times on the Korean tour but never on the LPGA stage. What happened over the weekend rewrote her career and reminded the golf world why the U.S. Women's Open remains the most unpredictable event in the sport.

## The Oldest Major's Biggest Purse

The U.S. Women's Open is the oldest of the five current women's major championships, and in 2017 it carried the largest purse in women's golf: $5 million, a figure that reflected the growing stature of the professional women's game. Held at Trump National Golf Club's Old Course in Bedminster, the event drew a truly international field. Players qualified through twenty 36-hole tournaments held across the United States and at international sites in China, England, Japan, and South Korea. Any female professional or amateur with a USGA handicap index not exceeding 2.4 could attempt to qualify, ensuring that the championship remained open to the broadest possible talent pool. Fox Sports televised the proceedings to a national audience.

## A Weekend Charge for the Ages

Park Sung-hyun entered the weekend as an afterthought. Her first two rounds had left her seven strokes behind the leaders, a margin that most players would consider a polite exit cue rather than a launching pad. But Park played the weekend rounds with the kind of controlled aggression that transforms tournaments. Consecutive low rounds across Saturday and Sunday brought her roaring through the field. By Sunday afternoon, the leaderboard had been rewritten. She finished two strokes ahead of amateur Choi Hye-jin, who had qualified through sectional qualifying in South Korea and delivered a performance that announced her own future on the world stage. For Park, it was vindication after years of dominance on the Korean circuit. Her ten victories on the LPGA of Korea Tour had established her credentials, but a major title on American soil was something else entirely.

## Bedminster's Challenge

Trump National Golf Club's Old Course offered a demanding test of positioning and nerve. The course winds through the Somerset Hills of central New Jersey, where the terrain rises and falls across manicured fairways bordered by thick rough and strategically placed bunkers. In the heat of a mid-July week, the greens firmed up and the scoring conditions tightened. This was not a course that rewarded recklessness. The players who thrived were those who combined length off the tee with surgical precision on approach shots. The field included past major champions like Inbee Park, Cristie Kerr, and Lydia Ko, along with rising talents such as Brooke Henderson, Danielle Kang, and Lexi Thompson. Amateurs competed alongside seasoned professionals, a hallmark of the U.S. Women's Open's democratic qualifying structure.

## An International Stage

The 2017 championship underscored the global nature of elite women's golf. The exempt list alone read like a United Nations roll call: South Korean stars dominated the upper rankings, while players from Australia, Sweden, England, Thailand, Japan, and across the Americas filled out a field that represented the worldwide reach of the women's game. Choi Hye-jin, just a teenager competing as an amateur, finished as runner-up and demonstrated that the pipeline of Korean talent showed no signs of slowing. Future stars including Nelly Korda, who entered as an alternate, were gaining their first experience at the highest level. The U.S. Women's Open has always been a proving ground, and 2017 was no exception.

## Legacy on the Fairway

Park Sung-hyun's victory launched her into the upper echelon of women's golf. She would go on to reach world number one and capture additional major titles, but it was this breakthrough in Bedminster that set everything in motion. The tournament also marked the continued evolution of the U.S. Women's Open as a global sporting event, with prize money, media coverage, and competitive depth all reaching new heights. For the Somerset Hills region of New Jersey, it brought four days of world-class competition to a landscape better known for its horse farms and hedge-fund estates. The echoes of that July weekend -- the comeback, the international field, the amateur who nearly won it all -- remain part of the championship's lore.
