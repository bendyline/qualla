# Perrysville, Indiana

Named for the hero of Lake Erie and built on a Wabash River bluff, Perrysville shipped flatboats to New Orleans and was reputed to be the largest town between Chicago and Terre Haute, until the railroads passed it by.

**Location:** 40.0522, -87.4353

**Categories:** Towns in Vermillion County, Indiana, Towns in Indiana, Populated places established in 1825, Terre Haute metropolitan area

## Introduction

Before the railroads came, Perrysville was reputed to be the largest town between Chicago and Terre Haute. Flatboats carried local products from here to New Orleans, steamboats brought in heavy equipment and manufactured goods, and stagecoaches came and went often. Then the tracks were laid somewhere else. At the 2020 census, roughly 455 people lived in this town on a bluff above the Wabash River, and its story is really a story about which way the traffic flowed.

## A Name From Lake Erie

James Blair platted and surveyed the town in 1825 on a bluff on the west side of the Wabash River, and it took its name from Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry. Twelve years earlier, on September 10, 1813, Perry had won the Battle of Lake Erie. When his flagship, the Lawrence, was battered, he rowed about half a mile under heavy fire to the Niagara, resumed command there and turned the fight. His note to General William Henry Harrison afterward began, "We have met the enemy and they are ours." The victory secured American control of Lake Erie for the rest of the War of 1812. In 1827, two years after Blair laid out the town, Perrysville got a post office, and it has been in operation ever since.

## Downriver to New Orleans

The river made Perrysville. The town became a local center for shipping products to New Orleans on flatboats, which traveled down the Wabash to the Ohio and then down the Mississippi. Traffic moved the other way too. Steamboats could reach the town, and they let it receive heavy equipment and manufactured items. In the decades before the railroad, that position on the river made for a thriving community. Its reputation at the height of its success, as the largest town between Chicago and Terre Haute, is a striking one for a place that today covers about a quarter of a square mile, and a measure of how much the river once mattered.

## Locks, Planks and Stagecoaches

The arrival of the Wabash and Erie Canal raised the town's importance even further. At more than 460 miles, it was the longest canal ever built in North America, linking Toledo, Ohio, on Lake Erie with Evansville, Indiana, on the Ohio River. Perrysville was tied into it by a sidecut with locks that allowed boats to be towed across the river to the town. There is a pleasing symmetry in that: a town named for the victor of Lake Erie now had a water connection to a canal whose far end lay on the same lake. Overland routes converged here as well. Various roads intersected at Perrysville, among them a plank toll road from Danville, Illinois, to the west, and stagecoach traffic became frequent.

## The Tracks That Went Elsewhere

Railroads reached the region in the second half of the nineteenth century, and they did not pass through Perrysville. That spelled the eventual end of the vital river traffic, and with it the town's importance. The canal did not last either; the stretch south of Terre Haute had closed by 1860, and the canal trustees sold the rest at auction in 1876. What remains is a small town in Highland Township, in the northeast part of Vermillion County, on the western bank of the Wabash, with Indiana State Road 32 meeting State Road 63 nearby. In 2010 its 456 residents lived in 184 households, and the median age was 40.2. Local students attend schools of the North Vermillion Community School Corporation, which operates North Vermillion High School.

## A Daughter of the Bluff

One of Perrysville's own went on to a remarkable public life. Luella Frances Smith was born here on October 1, 1859, the only child of the Rev. Hezekiah Smith and Susan Smith. In 1878 she married Felix T. McWhirter, whom she had met in Athens, Tennessee, while he was mayor there; the couple later settled in Indianapolis, where he became a banker, and as Luella F. McWhirter she became a philanthropist, clubwoman and temperance leader. She was president of the state Woman's Christian Temperance Union from 1896 to 1900 and, from 1897 until at least 1928, edited its state organ, The Message. In the early 1910s she led the Indiana Federation of Women's Clubs. She also served as a director of the People's State Bank of Indianapolis from its organization, the first woman chosen for such an office in that city. She died in Indianapolis on December 10, 1952, at the age of 93.

## Flight Context

Perrysville sits at about 40.0522°N, 87.4353°W on the west bank of the Wabash River in northeastern Vermillion County, Indiana, with Indiana State Road 32 meeting State Road 63 nearby. Follow the river at about 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL and look for the small town grid on the bluff along its western bank. Vermilion Regional Airport (KDNV), just northeast of Danville, Illinois, is about 12 nautical miles to the northwest, and Terre Haute Regional Airport (KHUF) is about 37 nautical miles to the south. The Wabash valley is the dominant landmark, so on clear days the river itself is the best guide to the town.
