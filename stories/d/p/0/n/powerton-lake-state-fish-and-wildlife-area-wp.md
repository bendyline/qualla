# Powerton Lake State Fish and Wildlife Area

Built in 1971 to cool a coal-fired power plant near Pekin, the 1,426-acre Powerton Lake stays warm through the winter, grows fast fish, shelters wintering ducks and bald eagles, and now faces the plant's scheduled retirement.

**Location:** 40.5490, -89.7040

**Categories:** Illinois River, Protected areas of Tazewell County, Illinois, State parks of Illinois, Cooling ponds, NRG Energy

## Introduction

Even in winter, the water at Powerton Lake can run anywhere from 40 to 60 degrees Fahrenheit, and the lake generally stays open all season. Diving ducks and bald eagles find it when much of central Illinois is locked in cold. The warmth is borrowed. Powerton Lake is a 1,426-acre cooling pond beside the Illinois River, southwest of Pekin in Tazewell County, and the heat in its water comes from Powerton Station, a 1,538-megawatt coal-fired power plant on its southeast shore. The plant's owner, NRG Energy, owns the lake as well, and the Illinois Department of Natural Resources runs it as a state fish and wildlife area.

## Water With a Job

The lake was built to do work. Commonwealth Edison constructed and filled it in 1971, next to a river whose low-lying wetland bed is only partly suitable for farming at best, and where long strips of riverbank have been put to other purposes. The power station, whose first unit began operating in 1972 and second in 1975, uses large quantities of water in its turbine operations, and water quality laws meant it needed a way to cool its heat exchanger other than drawing on the Illinois River. The pond was the answer. Water leaving the plant is steered through a series of baffles, and at maximum cooling capacity the whole lake turns over approximately every 48 hours. It is shallow, with a maximum depth of 18 feet and an average of about 8. Three causeways reach out from the mainland and split the water into four segments, so wind and storms cannot build waves big enough to erode the shoreline, part of an engineering design meant to protect the plant and the lake's banks alike.

## Fast Fish in Warm Water

Warm water changes the rules for fish. At Powerton they grow quickly, but the heat takes a toll on reproduction. Fishing here centers on blue catfish, first stocked in 1999 and again in 2001 from Arkansas stock and stocked regularly since. Smallmouth bass are stocked here too, and the lake has been called one of the top sites in Illinois for smallmouth fishing. The species list runs long: hybrid striped bass, smallmouth, largemouth and white bass, walleyes and crappies, bluegill, redear and green sunfish, freshwater drum, pirate perch and shortnose gar, gizzard and threadfin shad, bigmouth and smallmouth buffalo, and carp, among them silver and bighead carp. One newer arrival carries a larger story. Alligator gar had vanished from Illinois by the 1960s, and the state began stocking them in 2010 as part of a reintroduction program. Powerton received its first alligator gar in 2011, and fish collected there since have measured up to 60 inches long, a living echo of a species the state had lost.

## A Refuge on the Flyway

In 1984 the state's Department of Natural Resources opened the lake under a cooperative agreement with the utility to lease the site for fishing and waterfowl hunting. Today it is managed as a disjunct site of Spring Lake State Fish and Wildlife Area, a separate hunting preserve near Manito, and the water is divided by purpose. About 60 percent of the cooling pond is open to duck and goose hunting during legal seasons, while the remaining 40 percent is kept as a waterfowl refuge all year. Diving ducks, mallards, mergansers, buffleheads, goldeneyes and bald eagles are among the most common birds. The Peoria Audubon Society describes diving ducks and bald eagles wintering here, and notes that unusual birds such as western grebes sometimes turn up. Facilities are basic: a double boat ramp and parking for about 90 cars, with no camping, picnicking, swimming or boat rentals. There is no horsepower restriction, but boats are allowed on the lake only for fishing and waterfowl hunting.

## Heat Wave and Deadline

A lake that doubles as machinery can be pushed too far. In the summer of 2012, as extreme heat baked the Midwest, so many fish died that their bodies were drawn into an intake screen near Powerton Lake, lowering water levels. Workers shut down one of the plant's two generators for several hours because of the heat and the low water. Fish and turbines, it turned out, depend on the same water. Now the plant has a scheduled end date. NRG first announced it would retire Powerton Station by the end of 2028 to comply with federal wastewater rules. At the request of the regional grid operator, PJM, and in an agreement with the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency, the closing moved to December 31, 2029. The warmth behind the lake's fast-growing fish and its open winter water comes from that plant.

## Flight Context

Powerton Lake lies at 40.549 N, 89.704 W on the east bank of the Illinois River in Tazewell County, southwest of Pekin. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL it is easy to identify: a broad sheet of water split into four segments by three causeways, with the Powerton Station power plant on its southeast shore and Pekin's grid to the east-northeast. Mapleton's industrial plants line U.S. Route 24 about 2 nautical miles to the northwest across the river. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 7 nautical miles to the north; it is Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Pekin Municipal Airport (FAA identifier C15, no ICAO code) is about 4 nautical miles to the south-southeast. Expect concentrations of waterfowl and bald eagles over the lake, especially in winter, and avoid low passes over the generating station.
