# Kingston Mines, Illinois

A small Illinois River village that once shipped coal from the mines around it, Kingston Mines lost an Indigenous village site to a gravel company and lent its name to the Chicago theatre where Grease was first staged.

**Location:** 40.5581, -89.7719

**Categories:** Villages in Peoria County, Illinois, Peoria metropolitan area, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

In 1880, a history of Peoria County summed up this place briefly. Kingston, formerly Palmyra, stood on the Illinois River and had been laid out by James Monroe, and its chief business was coal mining. The name on today's maps adds a second word, and that word explains a great deal. Kingston Mines was a shipping port on the river, loading coal from the mines around it, and the largest of those mines was called Kingston too. The coal port has passed into the village's history, but the name holds on to it, and that name has traveled farther than anyone in 1880 could have guessed.

## Coal at the Water's Edge

The coal was noticed early. Traveling upriver in 1673, Father Jacques Marquette and Louis Joliet recorded coal outcroppings near Peoria, and from the earliest days of mining in the region the Illinois River offered an avenue for shipping what came out of the ground. Kingston Mines, just downriver from Peoria, became one of the places where that coal met the water. As a port, it loaded coal from the nearby mines, among them the one called Kingston. The mines produced the fuel, and the river carried it away. The village belongs to a family of local place names, Orchard Mines near Pekin among them, that still point back to the work that shaped this part of the Illinois valley. Nothing in the village's present-day description calls it a port any longer. The second word in its name does the remembering instead.

## What the Gravel Took

Before the coal trade, people made their homes on the north shore of Kingston Lake, beside the Illinois River. Their settlement survived as an archaeological site, the remains of an Indigenous village. In the early 20th century, the Kingston Lake Gravel Company destroyed it. The Peoria Academy of Science carried out some fieldwork there in the 1930s, and the site drew years of study. Anson M. Simpson wrote of a "Kingston (Illinois) Focus of the Mississippi Culture" and of "Various Types of Kingston Site Burials," and other studies carried titles such as "Kingston Lake Site Burials" (1944) and "The Kingston Village Site" (1951). The word that keeps returning in those titles is burials. The people who lived on this shore laid their dead to rest here, and what their community left behind was destroyed for gravel. That loss belongs to Kingston Mines' history as surely as the coal does.

## A Name That Traveled North

The village's name found a second life in Chicago. In 1969, producer and director June Pyskacek founded the Kingston Mines Theatre Company in a former trolley barn at 2356 N. Lincoln Avenue. The company was named after this river village, where the father of one of its actors, Jack Wallace, worked. Wallace himself was born in Pekin, just across the Illinois River, and became a fixture of Chicago's off-Loop theater in those years. In February 1971 the Kingston Mines Theatre staged the first version of Grease, Jim Jacobs and Warren Casey's musical about high school life in the 1950s, drawn from Jacobs's memories of Chicago's Taft High School. It ran to packed houses for months. A revised version opened off-Broadway in New York in February 1972 and moved to Broadway that June, where it became a hit. Today the name lives on in Chicago's Kingston Mines blues club, which took it from the theatre company.

## A Small Village Today

Back home, Kingston Mines remains a small village in the southwestern corner of Timber Township, part of the Peoria metropolitan area. It covers about one and a half square miles, and nearly a tenth of that area is water. The 2000 census counted 259 people in 98 households, with a median age of 35 and almost a quarter of residents under 18; by 2020 the population stood at 266. The village's post office and village hall date to about 1880 and were rebuilt in 1953. Children attend schools in Illini Bluffs Community Unit School District 327. It is a modest place to have lent its name to so much: a coal landing, an archaeological site that drew years of study, and the stage where Grease first found its audience.

## Flight Context

Kingston Mines lies at 40.5581 N, 89.7719 W on the west bank of the Illinois River in southwestern Timber Township, Peoria County, downriver from Peoria. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, follow the river south-southwest from Peoria and look for a small village grid at the water's edge; Pekin's larger grid sits about 6 nautical miles east across the river, and the warm, causeway-divided Powerton Lake cooling pond lies about 3 nautical miles to the east on the far bank. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 7 nautical miles to the north-northeast, and its Class C airspace means you should check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Pekin Municipal Airport (C15) is about 6 nautical miles to the southeast. River valley fog can form on cool, still mornings, and spring and summer bring convective storms across central Illinois.
