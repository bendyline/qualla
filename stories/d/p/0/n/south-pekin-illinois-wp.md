# South Pekin, Illinois

Born as a Chicago and North Western rail yard on land once called McFadden Flats, South Pekin became a railroad crew town and has rebuilt itself after some of Central Illinois's worst tornadoes.

**Location:** 40.4928, -89.6519

**Categories:** Villages in Tazewell County, Illinois, Peoria metropolitan area, Illinois, Populated places established in 1914, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Al Casper and his family arrived on Christmas Day, 1912, and Casper became the first permanent resident of what would become South Pekin. The land still went by a humbler name, McFadden Flats. What brought people there was the railroad. The Chicago and North Western was pushing a new line through Tazewell County, and within a few years a village of railroad families would grow up beside its tracks, five miles south of Pekin.

## Coal Pulls a Railroad South

White settlers had arrived in the South Pekin area in the 1820s, long before the town existed, and Cincinnati Township was formed in 1850. The spark for the village came from much farther south. In 1904 the Chicago and North Western Railroad discovered a large coalfield near Staunton, in Macoupin County, and a few years later it decided to build a line to reach the coal and carry freight between Chicago and St. Louis. South Pekin began as a station on that line, in Sections 27 and 34 of Cincinnati Township. The Caspers came in 1912, and the village was incorporated on April 12, 1917.

## Life Around the Kick Yard

Pekin's business leaders wanted the new switch yard, and the well-paid "railroad people" who worked it, closer to their own town. In the end most employees chose to settle near the yard, which was already in South Pekin. The C&NW brought blue-collar jobs and management positions, a three-story masonry office building and a bunkhouse where crews could stop for the night. Trains were made up in a "Kick" yard in the middle of town. Crews then took them south to East St. Louis or north to Nelson, where they were relieved and allowed to sleep before the next run; by the 1970s they continued through Nelson to Proviso, outside Chicago, or to Clinton, Iowa. At times as many as 20 trains a day were made up in South Pekin or passed through it. The office building was demolished around the start of the 21st century.

## Storm Country

South Pekin has lived through more than its share of disasters, beginning with flooding in the Great Mississippi Flood of 1927. The worst came on March 30, 1938, when an F3 tornado destroyed much of the village and killed at least eight of its residents; the toll is commonly given as nine. It is often described as the deadliest tornado in Central Illinois. National Weather Service research suggests the storm was a supercell that spawned as many as five tornadoes. Storms kept coming. On May 9, 1995, an F1 tornado moved northeast across town, destroying several garages and a railroad barracks, injuring two people and causing around a million dollars in damage; that same month, on May 13, an F3 tornado traveled 25 miles before lifting in Tremont.

## The Night of May 10, 2003

At around 9:45 p.m. on May 10, 2003, a tornado touched down southwest of South Pekin. It crossed Highway 29 into the village at F3 intensity and passed through town at 9:50 p.m., roughly a quarter mile wide. At least 23 people in South Pekin were injured, but no one was killed. The village spent the next ten years rebuilding, and it put up a new tornado warning system. Not every loss here came from the sky. A recording studio, The Golden Voice Recording, owned and operated by Jerry L. and Mary Anne Milam, worked here from 1966 until 1978, when a fire believed to be arson destroyed it.

## A Young Village

South Pekin covers half a square mile, all of it land, and counted 996 residents in 2020, down from 1,146 in 2010. Its population has skewed young. At the 2010 count nearly a third of residents were under 18 and the median age was 31, with fewer than one in ten aged 65 or older. Children attend South Pekin School District 137 for elementary grades and Pekin Community High School afterward. In 2015 the village's unemployment rate of 6.2 percent was higher than the national rate but lower than the state average and well below other communities in the metro area.

## Flight Context

South Pekin lies at 40.4928 N, 89.6519 W in Cincinnati Township, Tazewell County, Illinois, about 4.5 nautical miles south of Pekin and part of the Peoria metropolitan area. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a small village grid on flat land along the rail corridor, with Highway 29 passing nearby and Pekin's larger grid and the Illinois River to the north. Pekin Municipal Airport (FAA identifier C15, no ICAO code) sits about 1 nautical mile west of the village; General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 10 nautical miles to the north, and its Class C airspace means you should check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Spring brings severe-weather season, so check convective forecasts from March through June.
