# Mapleton, Illinois

Laid out in 1868 on the Toledo, Peoria and Western line, the onetime coal village of Mapleton still has only about 260 residents, yet four industrial plants line U.S. Route 24, among them a Caterpillar foundry that casts engine blocks.

**Location:** 40.5725, -89.7281

**Categories:** Villages in Peoria County, Illinois, Peoria metropolitan area, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Mapleton covers less than a square mile, and the 2020 census found 261 people living there. Yet four industrial plants line U.S. Route 24 through the village: an Evonik chemical plant, an Arxada plant that once belonged to the Lonza Group, a Caterpillar foundry and an Ingredion facility. That is a great deal of industry for so few residents, and the pattern is an old one. In 1880, when Mapleton had about a hundred inhabitants, a county history already listed three coal mines there.

## Mr. Maple's Village

William T. Maple laid out the village in 1868, and it carries his name. A history of Peoria County published in 1880 spells him "Maples" and notes that he was by then "now of Missouri," so the man who gave Mapleton its name had moved on within a dozen years. The same history placed the village on the T., P. & W. R. R., the Toledo, Peoria and Western Railroad, twelve miles southwest of Peoria, and counted about 100 inhabitants. A founder who left, a name that stayed, a rail line linking a small settlement to the city up the road: the outline of Mapleton was drawn early. The village still sits a short distance west of the Illinois River, and today it belongs to the Peoria metropolitan area. Its children attend schools in Illini Bluffs Community Unit School District 327, the same district that serves neighboring Kingston Mines.

## Three Mines and Two Stores

The 1880 history also recorded what kept the village busy. It listed three coal mines. One, owned and operated by Linsley & Walker, employed forty men, and the Mapleton mine, owned by Mansfield, Gilfoy and J. T. Linsley, employed twenty-five. Two general stores served the community. Thomas Linsley ran one of them, so the Linsley name turns up again and again in the record of the young village. The other store belonged to Frank Newsam, who went into business in 1874 and, two years later, put up what the history called "a large, commodious store building." It is a small snapshot, but a telling one: a village on the railroad with its founder already gone, a pair of general merchants, and mines that gave dozens of men their work.

## Iron at 2,500 Degrees

Some of the heaviest work in modern Mapleton happens at the Caterpillar foundry, where the first iron at the current factory was poured in 1978. In 2004, Caterpillar announced a $40 million update to equip the plant for large engine castings. When the Peoria business magazine iBi described a day there in 2015, up to 500 tons of sand were being trucked in each day, and the foundry could melt up to 500 tons of iron a day. A single sand core for a 3500-series engine block takes 1,200 pounds of sand, and each block mold needs ten of them. Ladles holding 48,000 pounds of molten iron at about 2,500 degrees fill the molds. Once the metal solidifies, the blocks are shot-blasted and ground, then heated to relieve stress. At that time it poured all the cylinder blocks for Caterpillar's 3500 and C175 engines and also cast heads and liners. About 650 people worked there in 2015, at what the magazine called "the only foundry of its size in all of Caterpillar."

## Neighbors Along Route 24

The foundry has company along the highway. Evonik runs a chemical plant here, and Ingredion operates a facility. Arxada's plant belonged to the Lonza Group until 2021, when Lonza sold its specialty-ingredients business to the private equity firms Bain Capital and Cinven, and the new company took the name Arxada. For all that industry, the village itself has stayed small. The 2000 census counted 227 people in 100 households, with 111 housing units. More than a third of those households were people living alone, the median age was 41, and men outnumbered women, at 122.5 males for every 100 females. The median household income was $45,357, and while 6.6 percent of residents lived below the poverty line, none of them were children or residents aged 65 and older. Twenty years later the count stood at 261, still a village of a few hundred people living beside its industrial neighbors.

## Flight Context

Mapleton lies at 40.5725 N, 89.7281 W in Peoria County, a short distance west of the Illinois River and about 10 nautical miles southwest of downtown Peoria. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, look for the industrial plants strung along U.S. Route 24 beside a small residential grid; Kingston Mines sits about 2 nautical miles to the west-southwest, and the causeway-divided Powerton Lake cooling pond lies about 2 nautical miles to the southeast across the river, with Pekin about 4 nautical miles east. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 6 nautical miles to the north-northeast; it is Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Pekin Municipal Airport (FAA identifier C15, no ICAO code) is about 6 nautical miles to the south-southeast. Expect river valley fog on cool, calm mornings and convective storms in spring and summer.
