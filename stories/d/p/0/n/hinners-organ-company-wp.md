# Hinners Organ Company

From a workshop behind a Pekin music store, John Hinners sold standard-model pipe and reed organs by catalog, putting real pipe organs within reach of small rural churches across the Midwest.

**Location:** 40.5715, -89.6514

**Categories:** Musical instrument manufacturing companies of the United States, American pipe organ builders, Pipe organ building companies, Companies established in 1879, 1879 establishments in Illinois, Pekin, Illinois

## Introduction

A country congregation that wanted to save a little more money had an option that says a great deal about the company that built its organ: its members could hitch up their own wagons, drive to the factory in Pekin, Illinois, and haul the organ home themselves. That was the Hinners way. Instead of crafting every pipe organ as a custom commission, John Hinners built standard models, sold them by catalog and letter while rarely sending out salesmen, and put the grand sound of a pipe organ within reach of small rural churches across the Midwest.

## Behind the Music Store

John L. Hinners was born in 1846, the son of German Methodist Episcopals, and began as a carpenter. He learned to build pump organs as an apprentice at Mason & Hamlin in Chicago. In 1879 he set up his own shop, the Perfection Organ Manufactury, at the back of Schæfer's music store in Pekin. When Schæfer sold the store, Hinners went looking for investors, and the business took on a series of partners and names: Hinners & Fink when J. J. Fink joined in 1881, Hinners & Albertsen after Ubbo J. Albertsen bought out the earlier partner in the mid-1880s, and finally the incorporated Hinners Organ Company in 1902, when Albertsen retired. Hinners himself died in 1906.

## Organs by Catalog

Hinners was one of the first American mail-order catalogue companies, and its method was built for the countryside. Standard models, mass production and direct marketing kept prices low. In the 1890s, a one-manual Hinners instrument cost about $100 per rank, shipping and installation included. The cases, by contrast, came in real variety, some of them quite elaborate, and that mattered: in an otherwise spartan country church, the organ was often the most prominent architectural feature. The instruments were also built to be serviced by local craftsmen, an important quality in an era before reliable roads and electricity. By making these complex instruments affordable to ordinary people across rural America, Hinners has been compared to both Henry Ford and Aaron Montgomery Ward.

## Hymns in Two Languages

Hinners began building pipe organs in 1890, and his primary customers were the German-speaking immigrant congregations then flourishing across the Midwest. Company literature was printed in both English and German, and German was the language spoken on the factory floor. The earliest known Hinners pipe organ went to the German Evangelical Church in Edwardsville, Illinois, in 1890. Because Hinners did not consistently date and number his nameplates, the first pipe organ in the company's own records is a later one, installed in 1892 at the German Evangelical Church in Huntingburg, Indiana. Another early instrument, installed in 1891 at the original St. Bartholomew Catholic Church in Columbus, Indiana, bore a nameplate reading "Established 1879 Hinners Organ Co. Pekin, Illinois U.S.A." That organ remains, although it no longer works.

## Three Organs a Week

The company's peak came in 1912, when 97 employees were shipping three pipe organs a week out of Pekin. Over its history Hinners built more than 10,000 reed organs, and its pipe organ opus numbers reached #3097, a count that includes rebuilds and enlargements. Most of its pipe organs were two-manual stock models with mechanical action, with electro-pneumatic instruments added in the 1910s. There were grander projects too, including several three- and four-manual installations. Hinners built an organ for the 1915 Panama–Pacific International Exposition in San Francisco that, according to a pipe-organ database, went on to St. Mary's Cathedral in Colorado Springs, where a Hinners organ was installed in 1916. The firm supplied what is described as Chicago's first theatre organ in 1908 but never capitalized on that boom, building at least 49 theatre organs in all.

## When the Music Faded

In the 1920s Hinners set up a subsidiary, the Illinois Organ Supply Company, to mass-produce parts for its own instruments and for other firms. Then the 1930s turned against it. The Great Depression, changing technology and growing competition all cut into the business, and in 1936 Hinners became a service company. It closed in 1942. Over more than half a century, Hinners was said to have "filled the plains with music." Its notable instruments include a 1905 organ at Rock Rapids United Methodist Church in Iowa, rebuilt by Dobson in 1977, and two in Nebraska: a 1916 organ at St. Leonard Catholic Church in Madison and a 1917 organ at Our Redeemer Lutheran Church in Staplehurst. In Norman, Oklahoma, Trinity Lutheran Church's 1909 Hinners, built for a German Methodist church in Oklahoma City and restored and installed in Norman in 2017, is described by the congregation as the oldest pipe organ original to the state.
