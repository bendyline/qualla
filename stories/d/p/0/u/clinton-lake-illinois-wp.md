# The Lake With a Day Job

Built in the 1970s to cool a nuclear reactor, 4,900-acre Clinton Lake also anchors a 9,300-acre state recreation area where rowers race six lanes, sailors hold a Glow In The Dark Regatta and anglers chase walleye and striped bass.

**Location:** 40.1389, -88.8765

**Categories:** Protected areas of DeWitt County, Illinois, Reservoirs in Illinois, Bodies of water of DeWitt County, Illinois, 1978 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Clinton Lake was made for a machine. The 4,900-acre reservoir near Clinton, Illinois, was built in the 1970s as a cooling source for the Clinton nuclear power station. The dam closed in 1977, the lake filled to its normal level in 1978, and it opened to fishing in 1979. It still does that job. Yet the water and the land around it also make up the 9,300-acre Clinton Lake State Recreation Area, where the Illinois Department of Natural Resources manages recreation on the lake and its surrounding lands. A reservoir engineered to carry away a reactor's heat spends its summers hosting anglers, campers, swimmers, sailors and a university rowing team, and its winters, when the snow cooperates, hosting skiers and snowmobilers.

## Two Forks of Salt Creek

Two streams feed the lake, Salt Creek and the North Fork of Salt Creek, and Salt Creek carries the water away again. The reservoir has an average depth of 19.5 feet and a maximum depth of 48 feet, with a surface about 690 feet above sea level. Its rules divide the water by temperament. Boating power is unrestricted on the main body of the lake, while the upper arm is reserved as a no-wake area and some limited areas are set aside for electric trolling motors. Six boat launches and a canoe launch put craft on the water, including accessible launches at the Mascoutin and West Side Access Areas. Water skiing is among the lake's pastimes, and with the upper arm kept to no-wake speeds, the open main body is where the engines get to run.

## Walleye to Stripers

Fishing is the headline. The state calls the 4,900-acre lake and its outstanding fishing the site's primary draws, and Clinton Lake is a good lake for catfish and other game fish. Its sport fish community is the one common to central Illinois reservoirs: largemouth bass, bluegill, white and black crappie, channel and flathead catfish, bullheads, walleye, striped bass and hybrid striped bass. Anglers without a boat are not left out, with a fishing pier at the Spillway Access Area and bank fishing at Valley Mill. Fishing and other water sports have been the predominant attraction at Clinton Lake, but the recreation area provides a good deal more.

## Six Lanes and a Glow

The lake draws crews as well as anglers. The University of Illinois rowing teams practice and host races here, and the Clinton Lake Rowing Center, at the north-eastern tip of the lake near Farmer City on a site the Illinois Rowing Association leases from the state, has been their home since spring 2017. Each spring a six-lane race course is set up on the water. Sailors gather at the marina, on the south shoreline of the lake's Salt Creek area between the towns of Lane and Weldon, reached from Illinois Route 10 or DeWitt County Highway 14. The Clinton Lake Sailing Association runs sailing instruction and events from its facility near the marina, and its best-known event takes its name from the lake's day job. Inspired by the fact that Clinton Lake was created to cool a nuclear power station, the association hosts the Glow In The Dark Regatta, a Midwestern race it has staged with Flying Scot Fleet 135. It attracts sailboat racers from as far as Florida, Pennsylvania and Maryland. In 2018, 34 teams registered from eight states.

## Sand, Trails and Snow

Away from the boats, a 1,000-foot sand beach opens from Memorial Day to Labor Day, its swimming area buoyed off from the rest of the lake. The campgrounds offer 17 Class AA, 277 Class A and 14 Class B/S campsites, and hiking trails, easy to moderate in difficulty, run through the site and along the shoreline. Hunting is by permit only, on more than 3,600 acres open for rabbit, pheasant, squirrel and dove as well as archery and firearm deer hunting. Horse owners can ride an equestrian trail, and there are facilities for picnicking. Winter does not close the place: cross-country ski facilities are available, and snowmobiling is allowed when conditions are favorable. Reaching the lake is simple. It is less than a four-hour drive from both Chicago and St. Louis, and about 30 miles from each of Champaign, Bloomington, Decatur and Lincoln, sitting roughly in the middle of all four. Interstates 55 and 57 serve it from the north and south, Interstates 72 and 74 from the east and west, U.S. 51 passes within 10 miles, and Illinois Routes 10, 54 and 48 lead directly to the lake.

## Flight Context

Clinton Lake is centered near 40.14°N, 88.88°W in DeWitt County, Illinois, with a surface about 690 feet above sea level. From 2,000 to 3,500 feet above ground the reservoir's arms are easy to trace, and the Clinton Power Station, recognizable by its sky-blue reactor building, sits beside the lake a little under 3 nautical miles northeast of the lake's center point. The University of Illinois rowing center is at the lake's north-eastern tip near Farmer City. Decatur Airport (KDEC) is about 18 nautical miles to the south, Central Illinois Regional Airport at Bloomington-Normal (KBMI) about 20 nautical miles to the north, and University of Illinois/Willard Airport (KCMI) about 28 nautical miles to the east. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common over central Illinois.
