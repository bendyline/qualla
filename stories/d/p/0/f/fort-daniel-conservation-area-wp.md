# The Ghost Town Among the Oaks

A Macon County preserve of roughly 370 acres, a little over 600 with its newest additions, protects the site of Whistleville, a pioneer village that grew up among oaks and hickories on a stagecoach route and, by one account, dwindled into ghost-town status after the Civil War.

**Location:** 39.7778, -88.8256

**Categories:** Ghost towns in Illinois, Protected areas of Macon County, Illinois, Tourist attractions in Macon County, Illinois

## Introduction

Fort Daniel sounds like a frontier stockade, but the Macon County Conservation District says the name comes from the legacy of a man, Reverend Daniel Traughber, who between 1838 and 1861 became one of the most influential and beloved men in Macon County. What the preserve protects is something else: the site of Whistleville, a pioneer village often described as a ghost town. The district's own page counts a 370-acre site in the Big Creek watershed, just east of Mount Zion, Illinois, and a September 2026 local report puts its combined holdings with the connected Woodbine Farm at a little over 600 acres. Today the land is given over to hiking and picnicking southeast of Decatur.

## Oaks, Hickories and a Stagecoach Road

In the 1820s, the upper Big Creek watershed was one of the first places Euro-Americans settled in what became Macon County. The surrounding country was unforested prairie, and the newcomers were looking for wooded land and firewood. Here they found mature oaks and hickories. The conservation district says some of the first families came out of the forested mountains of Kentucky, and that they named their pioneer village Whistleville.

Then came the road. Soon a stagecoach route made Whistleville a port of call between Indiana and Central Illinois. Early settlers were mostly from the American South, and during the Civil War the settlement was identified as a location of Southern sympathizers. By one account, Whistleville dwindled after the war and disappeared into ghost-town status; the district instead describes a self-sufficient community that stayed "locked in time" for more than a century.

## The Reverend’s Name

The preserve carries the name of Reverend Daniel Traughber, and the district’s history of the area records his reputation as a beloved one. It also passes along a darker local belief: some believe he led a group of local Southern sympathizers during the Civil War, who rode at night to terrorize local Union farmers.

That is a belief, not settled history, and the district offers it with that caution. Even so, it points to how the divisions of the Civil War reached into this corner of Macon County.

## Seven Trails Over Big Creek

Today the creek organizes a hiker’s day. The district lists six marked trails totaling about 4.5 miles for hiking and cross-country skiing, and they follow and cross Big Creek. A large pavilion and a playground serve picnickers. Tree watchers are encouraged to find and identify the sugar maples, chinquapin oaks, pawpaw, wafer ash and Kentucky coffeetrees growing here; oaks, which the first settlers found standing on this ground, still make the list.

The Macon County Conservation District maintains the area. The nearest major highway is Illinois Route 121, southeast of Decatur.

## A Preserve That Keeps Growing

The preserve has grown by grant. In 2019, the Illinois Clean Energy Community Foundation awarded the district $1 million to enable a 173-acre land acquisition adjacent to the conservation area, and in 2022 it awarded a $2.9 million grant for a 236-acre acquisition alongside it. In June 2023 the district acquired a five-acre parcel holding the Eli Ulery House, also known as Woodbine Farm, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The house, an Italianate building with bracketed eaves and a cupola, dates to around 1860 and was built for Eli Ulery, an early settler of Macon County who came in the 1830s and started his own cattle farm in 1851.

The newest project goes back to the land itself. In 2024 the district was awarded a $600,000 Open Space Land Acquisition and Development grant from the Illinois Department of Natural Resources to convert old farmland it acquired in 2023 into a pothole prairie, a habitat of many small, shallow wetlands surrounded by prairie. The district planned to add new trails, an access point and educational signs there. By September 2026 it reported about 220 acres of former farmland restored as seasonal and pothole prairie, with roughly 16 acres of wetlands, and held a grand opening for the first phase of Woodbine Farm, the connected 242-acre site, though its trail was not yet open. Settlers once came to this valley looking for firewood. As the trails open, visitors will be walking out to wetlands.

## Flight Context

Fort Daniel Conservation Area lies at about 39.778 N, 88.826 W in Macon County, Illinois, about 2 nautical miles east of Mount Zion and southeast of Decatur. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the wooded corridor of Big Creek running through the preserve, with Illinois Route 121 southeast of Decatur as a nearby road reference. Decatur Airport (KDEC), which has a control tower, lies about 4 nautical miles to the north-northwest, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield is about 39 nautical miles to the west. Central Illinois summers bring haze and afternoon thunderstorms; winter can bring low ceilings.
