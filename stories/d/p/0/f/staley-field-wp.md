# Staley Field

The company athletic field of Decatur's A. E. Staley Manufacturing Company, Staley Field was the first home of the team that became the Chicago Bears, until 1,500 seats and discounted tickets sent young George Halas looking for a bigger stage.

## Introduction

The Chicago Bears' first home field had a listed capacity of about 1,500 people, though the 1920 season's biggest Staley Field crowd, against Hammond, was recorded at 3,000. It was not a stadium in any modern sense but a company athletic field in Decatur, Illinois, belonging to the A. E. Staley Manufacturing Company, whose employees got in at half price. In 1920 the young George Halas coached, managed and played for the Decatur Staleys, who played several of their home games here, in the first season of the American Professional Football Association, the league that later became the NFL. His team went 10–1–2. Within a year the Staleys were gone to Chicago, and within a decade Staley Field had vanished from the city directory.

## The Company Field

The Staley company owned the football team, and Staley Field was the company's athletic ground. It was busy in every season. During the summer months the company's baseball team played here, and Halas, a two-sport athlete, took the field for that team too. The grounds also hosted Decatur's entry in the Three-I League, the Commodores, in the 1920s; the club's own history lists Staley Field as its home from 1922 to 1926. Picture the scene in the fall of 1920: railroad shops beyond the tracks to the north, the Staley plant close by, and a young coach who also lined up with his players. The Staleys were popular with Decatur fans that year. Popularity alone, though, could not fill a ledger when the stands were listed at only 1,500.

## The Arithmetic of Leaving

Staley Field's small capacity was only part of the problem. Tickets cost $1, and company employees received a 50 percent discount. For a professional team, that was not economically feasible, and the Staleys struggled financially despite their success on the field. Halas began moving games to Cubs Park in Chicago, the ballpark now known as Wrigley Field. The Staley company then turned the team over to Halas and his partners; accounts differ on whether it was a sale, but the Pro Football Hall of Fame says Staley gave Halas the team, $5,000 and permission to move it to Chicago if he kept the Staleys name for a year. The team played at Cubs Park in 1921, still as the Staleys, and that season the Staleys won the league championship. For 1922 the team took a new name to associate itself with its landlords, the Cubs. They became the Chicago Bears, and the name has stuck for more than a century.

## Finding a Vanished Field

Pinning down exactly where Staley Field stood has taken some detective work. Michael Benson's Ballparks of North America places it at Eldorado and 22nd Streets, and the Staley company's own address was 2200 East Eldorado Street. City directories tell a more precise story. The 1921 directory lists "Staley Athletic Field" at "North 7th corner Wabash," and the 1922 edition gives 521 North 7th. By 1927 it is listed at Eldorado corner 7th, and by 1929, with the streets renumbered, the field no longer appears at all. Pieced together with an aerial photograph, the evidence puts the field just east of what was then 7th Street, a short distance north of Eldorado. Home plate sat in the southeast corner, the third base line ran parallel to Eldorado, left field bordered 7th Street, and right field ended a couple of blocks south of the Wabash Railroad tracks.

## Flight Context

Staley Field's site is at approximately 39.8467 N, 88.9264 W on the east side of Decatur, Illinois, a short distance north of East Eldorado Street near the site of the Staley company's plant; the field dropped out of the city directories by 1929 and is no longer a sports venue. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, orient on downtown Decatur about 1.4 nautical miles west-southwest and on Lake Decatur about 2 nautical miles south. Decatur Airport (KDEC), which has a control tower, is only about 3 nautical miles east-southeast, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield is about 35 nautical miles west and University of Illinois Willard Airport (KCMI) about 32 nautical miles east-northeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are the main visibility concerns.
