# Decatur, Illinois

Once the "Soybean Capital of the World," the Macon County seat on the Sangamon River was Abraham Lincoln's first Illinois home, the birthplace of the Chicago Bears, and a city now working to reinvent itself after decades of industrial change.

**Location:** 39.8517, -88.9442

## Introduction

By a frequently repeated local claim, more than a third of all the soybeans grown in the world were once processed in a single central Illinois city. For much of the 20th century Decatur was known as "The Soybean Capital of the World," because it was home to the headquarters of the A. E. Staley Manufacturing Company, a major grain processor that popularized soybeans as food for people in the form of oil, meal and flour. In 1955 a group of Decatur businessmen founded the Soy Capital Bank to trade on the nickname. Founded in 1829 on the Sangamon River and named for the naval hero Stephen Decatur, the Macon County seat has been an industrial town, a railroad junction and a political stage, and its story runs straight through the life of Abraham Lincoln.

## Where Lincoln Began

Decatur was Abraham Lincoln's first home in Illinois. His family settled just west of town in 1830, and a historical marker in Decatur claims that the 21-year-old gave his first political speech here, defending the state's Whig leaders. Later, riding the 8th Judicial Circuit as a lawyer, Lincoln argued five cases in the log courthouse at the corner of Main and Main; that courthouse now stands on the grounds of the Macon County Historical Museum. On May 9 and 10, 1860, the Illinois Republican State Convention met in Decatur and gave Lincoln his first endorsement for president, as "The Railsplitter Candidate." Decatur's early years hold darker memories too. In 1838 the Potawatomi Trail of Death passed through the city: the forced march of more than 850 Potawatomi people from their ancestral lands in northern Indiana to a reservation in present-day Kansas, a journey that claimed more than 40 lives, most of them children. After the Civil War, on April 6, 1866, Union veterans founded Post No. 1 of the Grand Army of the Republic here.

## Beans, Corn and a Fly Swatter

Decatur's economy has long rested on processing crops and building machines. Caterpillar and Archer Daniels Midland both have production facilities here, and in 2013 the city created the Midwest Inland Port, tying ADM's intermodal container ramp to two Class I railroads, five major roadways and Decatur Airport. The city's inventive streak runs to smaller things as well. In 1900, Decatur entrepreneur Robert R. Montgomery received Patent No. 640,790 for the "Fly-Killer," a "cheap device of unusual elasticity and durability" made of wire netting on a handle, a design often credited as the first modern fly swatter. Industry brought scandal as well as prosperity. In 1992, ADM executive Mark Whitacre told an FBI agent that he and other executives were involved in a scheme to fix the price of lysine, a food additive, and went on to work with the FBI as a cooperating witness; ADM ultimately settled federal charges for more than $100 million. Hollywood later filmed much of The Informant!, starring Matt Damon, in Decatur.

## An Octagon on the Square

The city's symbol is the Transfer House, an 1896 octagonal shelter built in the original town square, now Lincoln Square, where Decatur's streetcars and interurban trains met. Chicago architect William W. Boyington, who also designed the Chicago Water Tower, gave it an open-air gazebo on the second story, a stage for public speeches and for concerts by the Goodman Band. That band traces its roots to September 19, 1857, making the present Decatur Municipal Band one of the oldest nonmilitary bands in continuous service in the United States and Canada. As automobile traffic on US 51 grew, cars had to circle the Transfer House, and in 1962 it was trucked to nearby Central Park, where it still stands. Decatur's architecture holds other treasures, including the Edward P. Irving House at 2 Millikin Place, designed by Frank Lloyd Wright and built in 1911.

## Park City, USA

Decatur once had more parks per person than any other city in the country, earning the nickname "Park City USA," and it was also called "Playtown USA" for its early leadership in providing recreational space; a 1944 short film by that name showcased the effort. Today the Decatur Park District manages about 2,000 acres of parkland, golf courses, Scovill Zoo and even the city's airport. Sports history runs deep here. The team that became the Chicago Bears began in Decatur as the Decatur Staleys in 1919 and 1920, before moving north in 1921. The minor-league Commodores played baseball in Decatur, with interruptions, from 1900 to 1974, and spent their last decades at Fans Field, which opened in the 1920s. In 1960, Decatur was one of eleven cities to receive the All-America City Award.

## Hard Years and New Bets

On July 19, 1974, a tanker car of isobutane collided with a boxcar in the Norfolk & Western rail yard on the city's East End. The explosion killed seven people, injured 349 and caused $18 million in damage. In April 1996, back-to-back tornadoes struck the southeast and northwest sides. The deeper challenge has been slow decline. The Chicago Tribune has put the city's 1980 peak at 94,000 residents; the 2020 census counted 70,522. ADM moved its upper management to a new world headquarters in Chicago in 2014, Firestone closed its Decatur plant, and the Census Bureau ranked Decatur third among the nation's fifteen fastest-declining large cities for 2010 to 2019. The city has kept placing new bets, from a 2015 branding campaign called Limitless Decatur to a brass foundry that Mueller announced in 2019 and a large insect-protein plant for animal feed that ADM and InnovaFeed announced in 2020 beside ADM's corn complex.

## Flight Context

Decatur is at 39.8517 N, 88.9442 W in Macon County, Illinois, about 32 nautical miles east of Springfield, 135 nautical miles south-southwest of Chicago and 94 nautical miles northeast of St. Louis. From 3,000-5,000 feet AGL, the best landmark is Lake Decatur, the long reservoir on the Sangamon River along the city's east and south sides; the large grain-processing complexes and rail yards are also easy to spot. Interstate 72 and U.S. Routes 51 and 36 make good line features. Decatur Airport (KDEC), which has a control tower, is about 4 nautical miles east-southeast of the city center; check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Other nearby airports: University of Illinois Willard Airport (KCMI), about 33 nautical miles east-northeast; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield, about 34 nautical miles west. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
