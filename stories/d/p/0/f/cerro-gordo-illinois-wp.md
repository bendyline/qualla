# Cerro Gordo, Illinois

A small Piatt County railroad village named for a Mexican War battle, Cerro Gordo gave the world the mechanic who built the Wright brothers' first engine and gave Richard Peck the unnamed small town of his award-winning Grandma Dowdel novels.

**Location:** 39.8911, -88.7333

**Categories:** Villages in Piatt County, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

When Orville and Wilbur Wright's Flyer lifted off in 1903, making the first sustained, controlled flight of a powered airplane, the engine that powered it had been built by a mechanic from a small village in central Illinois. Charlie Taylor was born in Cerro Gordo, a Piatt County town nicknamed Fat Hill, after the English meaning of its Spanish name. It is an unlikely birthplace for a piece of aviation history: a village of 1,316 people on less than a square mile of land, founded to follow a railroad and named for a battle fought far away in Mexico.

## Moving the Town to the Tracks

Before there was a Cerro Gordo, there was Griswold, a town on the stagecoach line along the river. Around 1855, by the usual account, its people made a practical decision: they picked up and moved the town to the path of the Great Western Railroad. Sheridan Wait and Henry Durfee are said to have platted the new town. For a name, the town looked to the recent Mexican–American War, though accounts differ on who chose it. An 1883 county history says a post office kept in George Peck's house on the old stage route had been named Cerro Gordo in memory of the 1847 battle, and that the town took the name when the post office moved to the railroad. It also records a second version crediting Colonel Williams, a large landholder nicknamed "Cerro Gordo" Williams, though its author found the post office story the more plausible. The relocated town would be Cerro Gordo, a Mexican battlefield remembered in the middle of Illinois farm country.

## The Man Called Cerro Gordo

John S. Williams served in the Mexican–American War, first as a captain and later as colonel of the 4th Kentucky Volunteers, and his gallantry at the Battle of Cerro Gordo earned him a lasting nickname: "Cerro Gordo" Williams. He became one of the heavy landholders in the township, and Wikipedia says he settled in nearby Bement. His story took a sharp turn when the Civil War broke out. Williams went back to Kentucky, became a colonel and then, in late 1862, a brigadier general in the Confederate army. Wikipedia's Cerro Gordo entry says his residence was destroyed by fire on May 31, 1864, with arson suspected. After the war he returned to farming in Kentucky, served again in the Kentucky legislature, and sat in the U.S. Senate from 1879 to 1885. The Illinois town kept the name long after he left.

## The Engine Man and Other Natives

Charlie Taylor, born here in 1868, became an inventor and mechanic, and his greatest work was the engine that powered the Wright Flyer, the first aircraft engine the Wright brothers used. He lived until 1956, more than half a century after that first flight. Cerro Gordo's other notable people ran toward the arts. Fern Coppedge, born here in 1883, became an impressionist painter. David W. Tucker, who grew up here, went on to direct the University of California Jazz Ensembles until 1985. For a village this size, it is a remarkable spread: a mechanic, a painter and a jazz educator, all tied to one small railroad village.

## Grandma's Town

Readers who have never heard of Cerro Gordo may already know it. Author Richard Peck set three of his books in an unnamed small Illinois town that he later confirmed was Cerro Gordo, where his own Grandma Peck lived and where he visited as a boy from his home in Decatur. A Long Way from Chicago was named a 1999 Newbery Honor Book and a National Book Award finalist. Its sequel, A Year Down Yonder, won the 2001 Newbery Medal, sending fifteen-year-old Mary Alice from Chicago to spend a year with the formidable Grandma Dowdel. A Season of Gifts is the third book set in the same town. The books never print the village's name, but for those who know the story, the quiet streets of Fat Hill carry a little of Grandma Dowdel's mischief.

## Flight Context

Cerro Gordo is at 39.8911 N, 88.7333 W in Piatt County, Illinois, along the railroad line that the town moved to in the 1850s. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a compact village of less than a square mile surrounded by open farmland. Decatur Airport (KDEC), which has a control tower, is only about 7 nautical miles west-southwest, making Cerro Gordo an easy sightseeing target from Decatur; check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. University of Illinois Willard Airport (KCMI) is about 23 nautical miles east-northeast and Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield about 44 nautical miles west. Bement, where "Cerro Gordo" Williams is said to have settled, lies about 8 nautical miles east-northeast. Watch for summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
