# Williamsville, Illinois

Laid out in 1853 as Benton and renamed when its founders learned the name was taken, Williamsville honors Springfield merchant-banker John Williams, a friend of the Lincolns who was named a pallbearer at the president's funeral.

**Location:** 39.9508, -89.5508

**Categories:** Villages in Sangamon County, Illinois, Springfield metropolitan area, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

The man this village is named for was among those who bore Abraham Lincoln to his rest. John Williams, a Springfield merchant and banker and a friend of the Lincoln family, was named a pallbearer at the president's funeral in Springfield, and was named a pallbearer again, in some accounts an honorary one, when Mary Lincoln died. His name was not the village's first choice, though. When it was laid out in 1853, this farm town north of Springfield was called Benton, and it took a bureaucratic surprise to make it Williamsville.

## Benton, Briefly

Settlers of European descent were living in the area by the early 1820s, but the village itself waited for the railroad. The village was laid out in 1853 as Benton, named for Missouri's Thomas H. Benton, with lots owned by Abraham V. Flagg sold that September, and that fall Jacob Flagg built the community's first house. Then the organizers realized there was a problem: Illinois already had a town called Benton. A new name was needed, and they chose to honor Col. John Williams. Growth came quickly after that. By the end of 1855, Williamsville had its post office, several stores, a doctor and a one-room schoolhouse.

## The Merchant Behind the Name

Williams was an early resident of central Illinois whose career tracked the region's rise. He started out as a clerk in frontier stores and saved enough to open his own business. In time he ran retail and wholesale stores, acted as a local banker, organized a national bank in Springfield, promoted railroads and worked as an agent for farm machinery. His career illustrates the important role merchant bankers played in central Illinois's economic development before the Civil War. It is fitting that a railroad village surrounded by farmland carries the name of a man who promoted railroads and sold farm machinery.

## Depot, Road and Storm

Traces of the town's transportation history remain. The former Williamsville Depot, standing between the railroad tracks and old Route 66, has served as a senior center and community gathering place, and a travel account describes a small historical museum built from two railroad boxcars next door; the village now lists a Public Library & Museum on North Elm Street. About a quarter mile of historic Route 66 runs along the village's border. The Price-Prather House at Main and Elkhart streets is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and electricity innovator Albert Ide grew up nearby. Not every chapter has been gentle. On August 19, 2009, a tornado destroyed more than two dozen homes and businesses along with the Williamsville Christian Church. The village, covering about 1.26 square miles at the 2010 census, counted 1,425 residents in 2020 and today forms part of the Springfield metropolitan area.

## Flight Context

Williamsville lies at 39.9508N, 89.5508W in northern Sangamon County, Illinois, about 10 nautical miles north-northeast of downtown Springfield. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a compact village grid set among open farmland, with the railroad passing through and the old Route 66 corridor along its edge. Elkhart and its distinctive hill lie about 5 nautical miles to the northeast. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is about 9 nautical miles to the southwest and its Class C airspace may extend over this area, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. The flat prairie typically offers long visibility outside summer haze and winter overcast.
