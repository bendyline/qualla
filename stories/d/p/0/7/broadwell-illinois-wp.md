# Broadwell, Illinois

A railroad village of 136 people on old Route 66, Broadwell owes its name to a founder who refused the honor, its legends to a frontier inn called Tantivy, and its fame to Ernie Edwards and the Pig Hip Restaurant.

**Location:** 40.0675, -89.4428

**Categories:** Villages in Logan County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1856, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Jacob Eisiminger turned down the chance to put his own name on the map. When the village he helped lay out in 1856 was offered his name, he declined, reasoning that such an unusual name would handicap the new town. So it became Broadwell, after his partner William Broadwell of Springfield, who owned the original land, had little further connection with the place and eventually moved to Kansas. The Eisimingers stayed. For generations they were Broadwell's merchants, postmasters and schoolteachers, until Leola Eisiminger, the last of the family to carry the name, reportedly died on August 16, 2005.

## Tantivy

Before there was a village, there was an inn. About three-quarters of a mile east of today's Broadwell stood a lodge called "Tantivy," a name that apparently comes from an old English hunting cry. It stood from about 1840 into the early 1950s, and it may have been moved about an eighth of a mile at least once. Tradition holds that Abraham Lincoln stopped there while riding the judicial circuit as a lawyer, on the road from Springfield to the Logan County courthouse at Postville, now part of the city of Lincoln. A second story is also told. Around 1840, John Dean Gillett, who would become a central Illinois cattle and land baron, is said to have proposed marriage to Lemira Parke in the Tantivy cabin. Whether or not Lincoln ever stopped there, the inn is said to have witnessed at least one life-changing moment.

## Rails, Hogs and Deep Soil

Broadwell was platted alongside construction of the railroad linking Chicago and Springfield, and it became a shipping point for hogs, corn and wheat, and later soybeans. Its fortunes have been remarkably even: the village and township have never known any great boom or bust. What lies underfoot is remarkable, though. Broadwell Township is reputed to have some of the finest productive agricultural soils in North America. The village is said to have sent two of its native sons to the Illinois General Assembly in the late twentieth century, John R. "Jack" Lauer, a state representative from 1973 to 1978, and Robert F. "Bob" Olson, who served from 1986 to 1994. Both men farmed locally for many years. The 2020 census counted 136 residents.

## The Original Guy at the Pig Hip

From 1926 to 1978, U.S. Route 66 ran directly through Broadwell, and in 1937 Ernest L. "Ernie" Edwards and his family opened the Pig Hip Restaurant beside it. They ran it until 1991. Afterward the building became a museum of Route 66 memorabilia, with Ernie himself, born August 5, 1917, as host, and he was honored for "the BEST U.S. Route 66 attraction where the ORIGINAL GUY is still there." On June 9, 1990, he became one of the inaugural inductees of the Route 66 Hall of Fame of Illinois. Then, on March 5, 2007, fire destroyed the restaurant-museum and most of its contents. Five months later, on his 90th birthday, family, friends and neighbors presented him with a large boulder and bronze marker honoring the Pig Hip and Route 66, which Ernie unveiled himself. He kept a small museum going from his home next door until late 2010, and died in nearby Lincoln on April 11, 2012, a fount of Route 66 stories with his memory sharp to the end.

## Where the Old Road Still Runs

Interstate 55 replaced the old highway and now skirts the western edge of the village, with a full exit, number 119, serving Broadwell and its township. The past has not been paved over entirely. The former northbound lane of Route 66 remains intact and still serves the village as a major local road, and it remains a magnet for tourists. Lincoln, the county seat, lies seven miles up the interstate to the northeast, and Springfield is 25 miles to the southwest. In between sits a village of well under a square mile, where the story of the road is marked in bronze and stone.

## Flight Context

Broadwell sits at 40.0675N, 89.4428W in southwestern Logan County, Illinois. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for the small village grid tucked against the east side of Interstate 55 at Exit 119; the surviving lane of old Route 66 runs through town. The city of Lincoln lies about 6 nautical miles northeast along I-55, and Logan County Airport (KAAA), about 7 nautical miles northeast, serves it; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield is about 17 nautical miles southwest and has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control as you get close. The surrounding farmland is flat and open, so visibility is usually excellent outside summer haze and winter overcast.
