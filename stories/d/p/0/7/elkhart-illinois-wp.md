# Elkhart, Illinois

Named for a grove shaped like an elk's heart, the Logan County village of Elkhart grew from a frontier horse mill and circuit-riding stopover into a railroad town, and its rare hill holds a governor's tomb and a chapel built in a cattle baron's memory.

**Location:** 40.0206, -89.4836

**Categories:** Villages in Logan County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1855, 1855 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Central Illinois is flat enough that a small rise feels like an event, which makes Elkhart Hill all the more startling. Its summit is listed at 771 to 777 feet depending on the source, roughly 180 feet above the village center and the surrounding flatlands. At its foot lies Elkhart, a village of 450 people whose name comes from a grove of woodland said to be shaped like the heart of an elk. Frontier travelers, circuit-riding lawyers and a governor of Illinois all found their way here, and some of them never left.

## Heart of an Elk

Elkhart Grove, sometimes written "Elk Heart," was one of the patches of woodland along the Edwards Trace, alongside another grove called Buffalo Hart. Long before settlers named it, Kickapoo people lived in this country, and one account places a Kickapoo village at the grove itself, until the forced expulsion of Indigenous peoples from Illinois drove them from the land. The first non-Indigenous settlers, James Latham and his family, arrived in the spring of 1819. James's house, and later his son Richard's place on the brow of the hill farther south, became stops on the early stage routes between Springfield and Bloomington. Travelers crossing the prairie had found a landmark, and a place to rest.

## Waiting Their Turn at the Mill

Around 1822 James and Richard Latham built a horse mill at the foot of Elkhart Hill. Until then, settlers had to haul their grain all the way to Edwardsville to have it ground. Once the Latham mill was running, people came a great distance to use it and frequently camped overnight while they waited their turn. Later, the grove drew a different kind of traveler. Abraham Lincoln, David Davis, John T. Stuart and other lawyers riding the 8th Judicial Circuit frequently spent the night at Elkhart Grove on their rounds from courthouse to courthouse.

## Elkhart City

Early settler Aquilla Davis had laid out a town site at the grove in the 1820s, but nothing much came of it until the Alton & Sangamon Railroad arrived in 1853. Once the tracks were down, William Mozee brought an old horse mill owned by Seneca Woods from Springfield and converted it into a warehouse, the first building in the new town. In 1855 John Shockey of Franklin County, Pennsylvania, laid out the town, and County Surveyor Conaway Pence surveyed it on April 11. It was called Elkhart City, to set it apart from nearby Elkhart Hill and Elkhart Grove. Shockey put up a large frame hotel that same year, J. R. Saunders opened a brick store with the town's first general stock of goods, and quite a number of houses rose that summer. A post office followed in 1856, and Methodist, Catholic and Christian churches went up in 1863, 1864 and 1867. In 1883 the post office dropped "City" from its name.

## Governor, Cattle Baron, Sharpshooter

Richard J. Oglesby, a friend of Lincoln, a Civil War major general, a U.S. senator and a governor of Illinois, lived and died in Elkhart. After his death on April 24, 1899, he was laid to rest in a mausoleum in Elkhart Cemetery, and Robert Lincoln, Abraham's son, is said to have walked in his funeral procession to the cemetery. The Gillett name runs deep here. In 1890 Lemira Parke Gillett built an Episcopal chapel in memory of her husband, the cattle and land baron John Dean Gillett, who is said to have proposed to her around 1840 in a cabin inn near what is now Broadwell. The cemetery also holds Captain Adam Bogardus, a world champion trap shooter billed as the "Wingshot Champion of the World" in Buffalo Bill Cody's Wild West show. Baseball's Jake Stahl, a first baseman who played for three major-league clubs and managed two of them, Washington and the Boston Red Sox, was born here. And each early spring, bluebells grow wild around the village, drawing walkers out to see them.

## Flight Context

Elkhart sits at 40.0206N, 89.4836W in southern Logan County, Illinois. Elkhart Hill, rising to roughly 770 feet at its summit on the southeast side of the village, stands about 180 feet above the surrounding plain, a distinctive rise in otherwise flat farmland and a useful visual reference from 2,000-3,000 feet AGL. The village of Broadwell lies about 3 nautical miles to the northeast, and the city of Lincoln farther northeast. Logan County Airport (KAAA), about 11 nautical miles northeast, serves Lincoln, and Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield lies about 14 nautical miles to the southwest and has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control as you get close. Visibility is generally good across the open prairie outside summer haze and winter overcast.
