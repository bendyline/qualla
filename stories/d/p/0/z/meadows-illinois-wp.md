# Meadows, Illinois

A railroad hamlet between Chenoa and Gridley, Meadows once had a bank, a school and the roots of a washing machine company, and its Mennonite congregation lasted 117 years before closing in 2007.

**Location:** 40.7444, -88.8047

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Illinois, Unincorporated communities in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1903, 1903 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

On Sunday, August 19, 2007, the Meadows Mennonite Church closed, ending 117 years in this small McLean County community. Its congregation had once numbered 300, at its peak in the 1940s, and its roots here reached back to the 1870s. Meadows, an unincorporated community on U.S. Route 24 midway between Chenoa and Gridley, once had a bank, a general store, an auto garage, a school and even the beginnings of a washing machine company. Little remains now: a nursing home, a grain elevator and a church building, strung along the single rail line that runs through the center of town.

## A Station Before a Town

The railroad came first. A station called Meadows, with a small cluster of buildings around it, had stood here since at least 1874, on the line of the Toledo, Peoria and Western. In 1877 Charles Parker platted and recorded the land he owned in the area, and a post office opened, with Charles Klein as postmaster by 1879. There was a store and a grainhouse, and the station was a flag stop where trains halted on signal. The town itself was formally laid out on May 18, 1903, by Samuel W. Baker. His Original Town was modest: ten blocks on either side of the railroad, with additional lots just north of the tracks. Meadows was also where Meadows Manufacturing began: John Rocke founded the company here in 1903 to build grain elevators, and it went on to make washing machines, an unexpected bit of industry in a farm hamlet. Its washer plant, though, was in Bloomington, where it opened in 1920.

## A Congregation's Long Road

The Meadows Mennonite Church began as an Alsace Amish congregation in 1874 and was officially organized in 1890. In 1908 it became a charter member of the Central Conference. Where to build a church was a serious question in an age when most people traveled by horse and carriage, and Meadows had an advantage few small towns could claim: a passenger railway stop. The church was built and dedicated in 1909, then added to and updated over the decades until a new building replaced it in 1967. The congregation peaked at 300 in the 1940s and declined from there, until the day-to-day expenses became too much for the church to shoulder.

## Bypassed

Then came a highway that passed Meadows by. When Interstate 55 was built about three miles to the east, near Chenoa, Meadows came to be seen as out of the way. The bank closed, and so, in time, did the general store, the auto garage and the school, losses blamed on the interstate. Two institutions have endured. The Meadows Mennonite Home, which traces its roots to a retirement community said to date from 1919, is still listed in senior-care directories as providing nursing care. And one of the Prairie Central Co-Op grain elevators stands by the Toledo, Peoria and Western tracks; an 1879 county history already noted that a large quantity of grain was shipped from Meadows.

## Flight Context

Meadows lies at 40.7444°N, 88.8047°W on U.S. Route 24, midway between Chenoa to the east and Gridley to the west, with the Toledo, Peoria and Western Railway running through the center of the hamlet. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, the grain elevator beside the tracks is the best marker, with the retirement community's buildings nearby; follow US-24 and the parallel rail line to find it. Interstate 55 passes a few miles to the east near Chenoa. Pontiac Municipal Airport (KPNT) is about 14 nautical miles to the northeast, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal is about 17 nautical miles to the south-southwest. The surrounding land is flat, open farmland with long sightlines on clear days.
