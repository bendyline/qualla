# Matthew T. Scott House

In Chenoa's founding family home, a future vice president married his bride in 1866, two sisters who would each lead the Daughters of the American Revolution made their mark, and a great-niece bought and restored it more than a century later.

**Location:** 40.7431, -88.7200

**Categories:** Houses in McLean County, Illinois, National Register of Historic Places in McLean County, Illinois, Museums in McLean County, Illinois, Historic house museums in Illinois, Houses on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois

## Introduction

On December 20, 1866, Letitia Green married a circuit lawyer from Metamora inside her sister's house on the prairie at Chenoa. The groom, Adlai E. Stevenson, would one day be vice president of the United States. The bride would twice serve as President General of the Daughters of the American Revolution, and her sister Julia, whose home this was, would later hold the same office. Two years after the wedding the couple's first child was born under this roof. More than a century later, that child's daughter bought the house and restored it.

## A Banker's Son on the Grand Prairie

Matthew Thompson Scott Jr. was born in Lexington, Kentucky, on February 24, 1828, the son of the president of the Northern Bank of Kentucky. He made his fortune on the grand prairie of Illinois, developing thousands of acres of farmland. According to the historical marker in front of the house, he bought and sold more than 45,000 acres of Illinois farmland, though his real interest lay in the 5,000 acres he kept for himself in Livingston and McLean counties. To raise the yields of that land, he had it drained with pipe tile and 250 miles of ditching. Scott founded Chenoa as a center for his business, and on May 12, 1859, he married Julia Green. The couple were among the town's first residents as well as its founders, and this house on 1st Avenue was their home.

## Eight Years Between Two Halves

The house was not built all at once. Its first section went up in 1855 in the form of a true I-house, and the City of Chenoa's own description gives that older part a folksier name, a "Kentucky Cat Slide," which suits the Kentucky-born man who built it. That section is now the caretaker's living quarters. Eight years later, in 1863, Scott added the front section, built in the form known as Georgian, and gave the house a more formal face. Walking from the back of the house to the front means stepping across those eight years in the life of a young town. Together, the two halves make the house significant as an example of a home from its period.

## The Lawyer Who Kept Coming Back

Among the house's frequent visitors were Adlai Ewing Stevenson and his cousin James Stevenson Ewing, who would later serve as U.S. minister to Belgium. Here Stevenson courted Letitia Green, Julia's sister and Matthew Scott's sister-in-law, and here the two were married. On August 15, 1868, their first child, Lewis Green Stevenson, was born under the same roof. Stevenson's career would carry him far beyond McLean County. He became a congressman, served as First Assistant Postmaster General in President Grover Cleveland's first administration from 1885 to 1889, and was the 23rd vice president of the United States during Cleveland's second, from 1893 to 1897. Business bound the two households together as well: in 1867 Scott and Stevenson founded the McLean County Coal Company.

## Two Sisters, One Society

The Green sisters made their own names. Letitia served as President General of the National Society Daughters of the American Revolution from 1893 to 1895 and again from 1896 to 1898, years that overlapped with her husband's term as vice president. Julia outlived Matthew, who died in 1891, and became a landowner in her own right, taking charge of 9,000 acres of farmland in Illinois and Indiana. She was elected Vice-President General of the DAR in 1901 and served for four years. In 1909 she became President General herself, and she held the post until 1913. It is fitting, then, that the City of Chenoa lists a DAR room among the rooms in the house.

## A Great-Niece Steps In

The house was eventually bought and restored by Elizabeth Stevenson Ives, a great-niece of Matthew T. Scott. Known as Buffie, she was born in Bloomington in 1897, the daughter of Lewis Green Stevenson, the baby born in this house in 1868. She was also the sister of Adlai E. Stevenson II, and she campaigned alongside him in his race for Illinois governor and his two runs for president. Active in historic preservation, she was a key driver behind the purchase and restoration, which the marker out front dates to 1983. On February 10 of that year the house was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The City of Chenoa describes the house as holding three period rooms, a DAR room and a Chenoa room.

## Flight Context

The Matthew T. Scott House stands on 1st Avenue in Chenoa, at about 40.7431°N, 88.7200°W in northeastern McLean County, Illinois. A single house is a small target, so look first for the town, which sits where Interstate 55, Historic Route 66 and U.S. Route 24 meet; from about 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL the highway junction and the town's street grid are easy to separate from the surrounding farmland. Pontiac Municipal Airport (KPNT) is about 12 nautical miles to the north-northeast, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal is about 18 nautical miles to the south-southwest. The land all around is open, flat farmland, so on clear days the town stands out from a long way off.
