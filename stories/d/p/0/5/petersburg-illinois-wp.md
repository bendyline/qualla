# Petersburg, Illinois

The Menard County seat on the Sangamon bluffs outlasted Lincoln's New Salem, kept its Victorian homes and cobblestones, and holds the graves of Ann Rutledge and the poet who carved her legend in verse.

**Location:** 40.0114, -89.8514

## Introduction

"Out of me unworthy and unknown / The vibrations of deathless music." Since 1921 those words, from Edgar Lee Masters's poem about Ann Rutledge, have been engraved on her monument in Oakland Cemetery in Petersburg, Illinois. Rutledge is remembered, rightly or not, as Abraham Lincoln's first love. Masters had already placed her in the fictional cemetery of his Spoon River Anthology in 1915. Now the poet rests a short walk away from her, in the same cemetery, in a town where legend, literature and Lincoln have grown so tangled that it is hard to say where one ends and the next begins.

## The Town That Won the County Seat

Petersburg sits on the bluffs, and partly on the floodplain, overlooking the Sangamon River in central Menard County, and it dates to 1833. It began as a trade center for the region's farms and a shipping point, where a railhead met a spot on the Sangamon that was both navigable and crossable. The town's rise came partly at the expense of a neighbor. When Menard County's seat went to Petersburg, the village of New Salem, where the young Lincoln lived, began to shrink, and by 1840 it was nearly abandoned. That year Henry Onstot even moved his cooper shop from New Salem to Petersburg. The building eventually went back: in 1922 it was returned to New Salem, where it stands today as the only original structure in the reconstructed village.

## Victorian Bluffs and Cobblestones

The prosperity of those early years is still written on the hillsides. Many of the lush Victorian-era homes that early wealthy residents built on Petersburg's bluffs still stand, and the town takes great pride in them. It is also said to have preserved some of its original cobblestone streets to set off the architecture. Several of the Victorian houses have become bed and breakfast inns, serving a tourist trade that is steady, if small. Visitors come mostly for Lincoln. Petersburg is the gateway to New Salem, about two miles to the south, and it keeps some relics of Lincoln's early life in Illinois. The town is small, covering just 1.56 square miles and counting 2,258 residents at the 2020 census.

## Two Graves in Oakland Cemetery

Ann Rutledge is the most famous name connected with Petersburg, though her place in Lincoln's story is uncertain: she is remembered as the young woman he allegedly loved first. Edgar Lee Masters, a lawyer and author, did much to keep that memory alive. His Spoon River Anthology gave Rutledge a voice among the dead of its fictional Spoon River cemetery in 1915, and in 1921 the poem was engraved on a new monument at her real grave in Oakland Cemetery. When Masters himself died, he was buried here too, a short walk from the woman whose legend he had helped make. Walking between the two stones, you stand at the place where a frontier romance and an American book meet, and the line between them is hard to find.

## Where the River Went Shallow

The Sangamon that once made Petersburg a shipping point has grown too shallow for navigation in recent decades, reportedly choked by silt from local farming and by the diversion of natural runoff into reservoirs such as Lake Petersburg and Lake Springfield. The economy leaned back on the land. Agriculture, especially corn, still drives the area, and Petersburg has also become a bedroom community for Springfield, roughly 20 miles to the southeast along Illinois Route 97. The town has produced a surprising range of people. Admiral Harris Laning of the U.S. Navy was born here, as were newspaper editor William Taylor Davidson and ballplayer Bill Krieg, a utility man for several teams. U.S. Senator William B. McKinley is also counted among its notable names. Local news comes from two weeklies based in town, The Petersburg Observer and the Menard County Review, alongside Springfield's State Journal-Register.

## Flight Context

Petersburg lies at 40.0114°N, 89.8514°W on the bluffs above the Sangamon River in central Menard County, Illinois. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the town's grid climbing from the river floodplain onto the bluffs, with Illinois Route 97 running through on Sixth Street, northwest toward Havana and southeast toward Springfield. Lincoln's New Salem State Historic Site lies about two nautical miles to the south on its wooded ridge above the river, an easy follow-on target. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) in Springfield is about 13 nautical miles to the southeast, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with approach control. The river corridor stands out clearly on clear days, and river fog can linger in the valley on calm mornings.
