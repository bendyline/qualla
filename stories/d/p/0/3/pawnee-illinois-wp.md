# Pawnee, Illinois

A Sangamon County village south of Springfield that borrowed its name from a Great Plains nation, lost miners in a 1902 mine explosion, and in 2023 saw a 25-mile tornado touch down a few miles to its north.

**Location:** 39.5919, -89.5819

**Categories:** Villages in Sangamon County, Illinois, Springfield metropolitan area, Illinois, Populated places established in 1854, 1854 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

The Pawnee built their earth-lodge villages along the Loup, Republican and South Platte rivers, in what is now Nebraska and Kansas. Their name, though, ended up on a village in central Illinois, far to the east of any of those rivers. Pawnee, Illinois, established in 1854 in southern Sangamon County, is named for the Pawnee Tribe, and in its long life it has known sudden violence both underground and overhead.

## A Name From the Plains

The village did not start out as Pawnee. It began as the Horse Creek Settlement, founded in 1854, and accounts say that when residents asked for a post office that year, postal authorities objected to the name and "Pawnee" was suggested instead, in keeping with the fashion of the time for naming places after Native nations. The new name has outlasted the old one. The Pawnee themselves did not stay on the rivers where they once built their lodges. Today they are the federally recognized Pawnee Nation of Oklahoma, headquartered in another town that shares their name: Pawnee, Oklahoma. So the name now marks two places far from the Pawnee homeland, one of them the nation's own capital and the other a village in Sangamon County, Illinois, that has carried it since the 1850s. The village never set out to explain itself; it simply kept the name, and more than a century and a half later it still answers to it.

## Below Ground and Overhead

On October 13, 1902, the Victor Mine at Pawnee exploded, and six workers died. Newspaper dispatches the next day reported three men killed outright and three more fatally injured, and said the blast came from too much powder in a shot. Six people who went into the mine to work that day never came home. More than a century later, danger arrived from the sky instead. On August 6, 2023, a tornado touched down about three miles north of the village, first downing trees and doing minor roof damage to a home before crossing into Christian County. It strengthened over Sangchris Lake and passed between Kincaid and Sharpsburg, snapping and uprooting large trees, tearing roofing and siding from homes and damaging barns, farm buildings and grain bins. Its worst damage came near Willeys, past the north side of Taylorville, where about two-thirds of a one-story house was destroyed. Rated EF2 by the National Weather Service, with peak winds of 125 mph, it was a long-tracked storm, up to a quarter mile wide, and it traveled about 25 miles before it finally dissipated.

## A Village of Young Families

Pawnee has grown into a compact community of about 1.26 square miles, part of the Springfield metropolitan area. It counted 2,647 people in 2000, 2,739 at the 2010 census, and 2,678 at the 2020 census. The census portrait is that of a family town. In 2000, four in ten of its 1,028 households had children under 18 living in them, and 59 percent were married couples. More than a quarter of residents were under 18, and the median age was 35. The village sits close enough to Springfield to be counted part of its metro area, yet it keeps its own name, its own identity and its own history, from a mine disaster more than a century old to a storm that touched down a few miles to its north.

## Flight Context

Pawnee sits at 39.5919 N, 89.5819 W in southern Sangamon County, Illinois, about 13 nautical miles south-southeast of downtown Springfield, at roughly 600 feet above sea level. From 2,500-4,000 feet AGL, look for a compact village grid in open farmland; Sangchris Lake lies to the east, near the start of the corridor the August 2023 tornado followed toward Kincaid and Taylorville. Nearest airports: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 16 nautical miles to the north-northwest at Springfield, and Taylorville Municipal Airport (KTAZ), about 12 nautical miles to the east-southeast. Central Illinois summers bring haze and fast-building thunderstorms.
