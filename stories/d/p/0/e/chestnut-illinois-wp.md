# Chestnut, Illinois

A Logan County railroad town of about 220 people that proudly marks itself as the geographic center of Illinois with a small monument, even though the true center lies about seven miles to the southwest.

**Location:** 40.0533, -89.1850

**Categories:** Census-designated places in Logan County, Illinois, Census-designated places in Illinois

## Introduction

In a small park in Chestnut, Illinois, a modest monument declares this Logan County community the geographic center of the state. It is a grand claim for a place of 220 people, and it was made for the sake of town promotion. There is just one problem. The exact center of Illinois lies about seven miles southwest of Chestnut, on the far side of the town of Mount Pulaski, at 40°0.8' north and 89°18.4' west by one commonly cited reckoning. The monument stands all the same, a small flag of civic pride planted by a community that has been making the most of its spot on the map since the railroad created it in 1872.

## Close Enough to the Middle

The monument sits in a park near the southwest corner of Melvin Street and East Olive Street, in what surveyors would call section 34 of Township 19 North, Range 1 West. That legal description is exact; the claim on the marker is not. Measured precisely, the center of Illinois falls roughly seven miles to the southwest, past Mount Pulaski. Chestnut itself lines up along Illinois Route 54 beside the railroad tracks that gave it life. It is a census-designated place in Aetna Township rather than an incorporated village, and it is small: 246 residents in 2010 and 220 in 2020. For a community that size, a claim to the center of the state is roadside boosterism at its most cheerful, confident and not quite true.

## A Town Replaces a Town

Chestnut was not the first town on this ground. In 1870, William M. Allen and S. Linn Beidler laid out, platted and surveyed a community on the same section of land, and a post office opened there under the name Allenville, in Allen's honor. Then the railroad came. In 1872 David Clark, working with the directors of the newly completed Gilman, Clinton and Springfield railroad, laid out a new town, and county surveyor Thomas G. Gardner surveyed the land on April 24 of that year. The line later became the Springfield branch of the Illinois Central. The new town took its name from one of the railroad's directors, not from any tree, and once Chestnut was laid out, Allenville was abandoned.

## Storefronts and Flames

The first businesses arrived quickly. The first store arrived in a building moved from "Yankeetown," a few miles away; one county history credits Lakin and Britton, while other accounts name the Britton Brothers. Dement and Clark put up the second store, William H. Daniels started a blacksmith shop, and George Lakin opened a drug store and served as postmaster. In 1873 or 1874 I. J. Michener built an elevator and worked as the railroad agent, and a Methodist congregation that had begun in Yankeetown built a frame church in the new town. Fire proved a recurring enemy. In 1876 flames consumed the Britton Brothers' store and stock, a $3,000 loss. The very next year Dement and Clark lost their store and stock to fire, at a cost of $5,000. Decades later, on January 24, 1905, fire destroyed the general store of Gobleman & Bapst, a $10,000 loss. In between, in 1898, a special election to incorporate Chestnut as a village went down to defeat, and the community has remained unincorporated ever since.

## Flight Context

Chestnut is at 40.0533 N, 89.1850 W in Logan County, Illinois, along Illinois Route 54 and the railroad line that created the town. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL it appears as a small cluster of homes beside the tracks amid open farmland. For a fun comparison, fly about 6 nm west-southwest past Mount Pulaski to the commonly cited geographic center of Illinois near 40°00.8' N, 89°18.4' W. Nearby airports: Logan County Airport (KAAA) at Lincoln, about 9 nm northwest; Decatur Airport (KDEC), about 20 nm southeast; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield, about 26 nm west-southwest, whose Class C airspace is the one to check on the sectional chart. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
