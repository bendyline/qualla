# The Marble and the Mob

A history of the Moultrie County Courthouse, where architectural grandeur collided with rural volatility.

**Location:** 39.5983, -88.6100

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Moultrie County, Illinois, County courthouses in Illinois, Courthouses on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois, Neoclassical architecture in Illinois, National Register of Historic Places in Moultrie County, Illinois

## Introduction

In Sullivan, the county seat, the legal authority of the courthouse once stood in contrast to the roaming livestock of the village. The first brick courthouse was completed on March 6, 1848, at a cost of $2,800. By 1849, a fence was placed around the courthouse grounds to keep out "the pigs, cows and horses that roamed the village."

## Lost to Fire

On November 25, 1864, a fire destroyed the building and most of the records from 1843 to 1864. Plans for a new courthouse built with local materials were made in 1865, and by September 1866, a replacement had been built. This second building featured a fireproof vault.

## Law and Lawlessness

Even with a fireproof vault, the building could not always contain the town's anger. In 1896, an angry mob stormed the courthouse and demanded that the sheriff release Grant Atteberry, who had been imprisoned under accusations that he assaulted a local woman. The sheriff agreed to the mob's demands, and Atteberry was taken from custody. He was lynched, hanged from a tree on the northwest corner of the courthouse lawn.

## The Present Courthouse

As the county outgrew its facilities by 1904, plans were approved for a grander replacement. Architects Deal & Ginzel and contractor H. B. Walters of Danville, Illinois constructed a new courthouse that was completed on August 6, 1906, for $76,952. Facing west, the three-story building combines dark red colored brick and buff colored sandstone with concrete; it is crowned by a large green octagonal dome featuring a clock and rounded roof. Porticos on each side feature arched stone entrances on the first story and two tall Doric columns extending from the second story to the top of the third.

Inside, Tennessee marble wainscoting lines the corridors and the rotunda, whose walls are adorned with pastoral and harvest scenes. From about 1907 to 1930, this setting hosted the Moultrie County Farmers' Institute, a popular three-day educational meeting co-sponsored by the University of Illinois College of Agriculture. The marble corridors also saw legal turmoil in 1921, when the building hosted the embezzlement trial of Jimmy Steel and Z. B. Whitfield of the Merchants & Farmers State Bank, the most noteworthy trial to take place there.

## The County's Sole Landmark

The building continues to house functions of county government and is home to the County Circuit Court of the 6th Judicial Circuit. On April 20, 1995, the National Park Service listed the building on the National Register of Historic Places. It remains the only site in Moultrie County, Illinois to hold this designation. Today, its large Circuit Courtroom remains a popular location for community functions.
