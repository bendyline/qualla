# Interstate 55 in Illinois

Illinois's St. Louis-to-Chicago interstate grew out of the 1915 Pontiac Trail and Route 66, and it now carries the names of a governor, a humorist and a president.

**Location:** 40.3564, -89.1105

## Introduction

One highway, three honorees. In Cook County, Interstate 55 is the Stevenson Expressway, named for a governor. In DuPage County it is also listed as the Joliet Freeway, or the Will Rogers Freeway, for the humorist. From the south suburbs to the area near Pontiac it carries a newer name, the Barack Obama Presidential Expressway, after the president who launched his political career from Illinois. Beneath all those titles lies one of the state's hardest-working roads: 294.38 miles of pavement that enter Illinois from Missouri near East St. Louis and run through Springfield, Bloomington and Joliet to end at Lake Shore Drive near Downtown Chicago.

## From Pontiac Trail to Route 66

The corridor is far older than the interstate. It began in 1915 as the Pontiac Trail, which was largely improved and paved as Illinois Route 4 by 1924. In 1926, IL 4 was designated as the route of the new US 66, and by 1930 a new section of US 66 had been built to bypass slower stretches of IL 4 south of Springfield. The famous road never stopped changing. Through the 1950s it was continually widened, straightened and improved to handle its growing traffic, until by 1957 the whole Illinois route was four lanes wide. Even as those last lanes were being finished, a far larger idea was already taking shape, one that would eventually replace Route 66 altogether.

## An Idea Brought Home From Germany

The roots of I-55 lie in the need for a national highway system, a need President Dwight D. Eisenhower saw clearly. He had seen Germany's autobahn during World War II and come away impressed, and he wanted a national network of highways that would help with the mobilization of the army. In 1956 he signed the Federal Aid Highway Act. Illinois did not have to start from scratch. The earliest stretch of I-55 was a portion of US 66 already built as a freeway between Gardner and I-294 in Indian Head Park, and it joined the Interstate System in 1960 simply by getting new signs. Where US 66 was already four lanes wide, a common tactic was to build new southbound lanes west of the original road. The 1960s brought the section between East St. Louis and Hamel and the bypasses of Springfield and Bloomington–Normal, and the rest was completed in the 1970s. By then I-55 had replaced US 66 as the fourth St. Louis–Chicago highway, serving most of the same communities. Many signs for Historic US 66 still stand, especially where the old road wanders away from the new one.

## The Long Diagonal

North of Springfield, I-55 settles into the northeast–southwest line it follows all the way to Chicago. It meets IL 123 in Williamsville, then reaches Lincoln, where a business loop begins and a spur, I-155, heads off toward I-74 just east of Peoria. Next come an interchange with a local road leading to Atlanta, US 136 at McLean, and another local road near Shirley. Then the highway enters Bloomington–Normal, where a complex interchange ties it to a business loop running north and to I-74 and US 51 running east. Beyond the twin cities, I-55 keeps up its diagonal trek toward Joliet, skirting the western edges of town after town. Pontiac marks the southern end of the Obama expressway: in 2017 the Illinois General Assembly voted to rename roughly 75 miles of I-55, from the Tri-State Tollway down to Pontiac, in the president's honor.

## The Stevenson

At the Chicago end, the road becomes an expressway with a biography of its own. The Stevenson opened on October 24, 1964, under the plainer name of the Southwest Expressway. On September 1, 1965, a month and a half after the death of former Illinois governor Adlai Stevenson II, it was renamed for him. Its original ends were US 66 in DuPage County and the Dan Ryan Expressway. In 1999 and 2000 it was completely rebuilt from Central Avenue north to Lake Shore Drive, ramps to the Dan Ryan included, and the Illinois Department of Transportation drew criticism for not adding a fourth lane in each direction. The city stretch has also served as a testing ground. In November 2011, IDOT began a two-year pilot letting Pace bus routes 755 and 855 ride on dedicated shoulder lanes. In 2014, Governor Pat Quinn signed a bill making the bus-on-shoulder service permanent and allowing Pace to expand it.

## A Road That Is Never Finished

More than 20,000 vehicles a day travel most of I-55's length in Illinois, and long six-lane stretches fill with commuters. Keeping up costs IDOT millions of dollars a year in maintenance, added lanes and replaced bridges. Near Joliet, a project that widened the road from two to three lanes in each direction between I-80 and Weber Road was completed on October 29, 2008. At the southern gateway, where I-55 crosses the Mississippi River into Illinois on the Poplar Street Bridge, the nearly $700 million Stan Musial Veterans Memorial Bridge project was completed in 2014, taking I-70 off the Poplar Street Bridge to relieve the congestion. More recently, between mid-2021 and October 2024, the IL 59 interchange was rebuilt as a diverging diamond, and a road extension south of it, Gateway Boulevard, now connects to the Hollywood Casino in Joliet.

## Flight Context

This story is pinned at 40.3564°N, 89.1105°W, where I-55 crosses McLean County southwest of Bloomington–Normal, between the US 136 interchange at McLean and the local-road interchange near Shirley. From 3,000 to 4,500 feet AGL, the divided interstate is easy to follow as a long, straight northeast–southwest diagonal across flat central Illinois. Follow it northeast to the Bloomington–Normal urban area and the interchange with I-74 and US 51, or southwest toward Lincoln, where I-155 branches off toward Peoria. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 11 nautical miles to the northeast, General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 32 nautical miles to the northwest, and Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield is about 40 nautical miles to the southwest; KPIA and KSPI are Class C airports, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control if you route toward them. On clear days the highway reads like a ruled line across the landscape.
