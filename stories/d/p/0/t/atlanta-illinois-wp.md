# Atlanta, Illinois

A Logan County railroad town that settled on its name only after two others were taken, Atlanta now greets Route 66 travelers with a smiling water tower, an eight-sided library and a giant holding a hot dog.

**Location:** 40.2628, -89.2331

**Categories:** Cities in Illinois, Cities in Logan County, Illinois, 1853 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Atlanta, Illinois, was nobody's first choice of name. When the town applied for a post office in the 1850s, it wanted to be Xenia, but a Xenia already existed in Clay County. The settlers tried Hamilton next, after Colonel L. D. Hamilton, only to find a Hamilton in Hancock County. So they borrowed the name of Atlanta, Georgia, and because no other Illinois town had claimed it, the name was approved. More than a century and a half later, the little Logan County city on Route 66 greets travelers with a smile, literally painted on its water tower.

## New Castle Gives Way

The first town here was New Castle, platted by surveyors in December 1836 on the site of present-day Atlanta; its post office opened on November 26, 1847. Then the railroad chose a different spot. In May 1853 surveyors platted Xenia, where the newly built main line of the Chicago & Alton Railroad crossed the town. Bypassed by the tracks, New Castle's residents voted to join Xenia, and after the naming troubles were sorted out, the Illinois General Assembly incorporated the community of Atlanta on February 14, 1855. A second railroad followed. The Peoria, Atlanta and Decatur Railroad was surveyed in 1869, construction began the next year, and the line was completed by November 1874. Today Atlanta covers about 1.26 square miles and counted 1,669 residents at the 2020 census.

## Eight Sides and a Seth Thomas

Atlanta's most distinctive building has eight walls. The Atlanta Public Library, built of limestone in 1908, is octagonal, and it has operated continuously as a library ever since; in December 1979 it was added to the National Register of Historic Places. Out front stands a 36-foot clock tower housing a Seth Thomas clock that the city dates to 1907, though some guides say 1909. The tower was originally part of the Atlanta High School building and was later moved to its place in front of the library. The clock is still wound by hand: a group of local volunteers known as the "Keepers of the Clock" winds it up to three times a week. Atlanta's students today attend Olympia High School, part of Olympia Community Unit School District 16.

## A Giant and His Hot Dog

Atlanta's giant came from the Chicago suburbs. For more than 40 years, Hamlet A. Stephens ran a hot dog stand called Bunyon's in Cicero, outside Chicago, and in the 1960s he ordered one of the giant "Muffler Man" statues for it, fitted with a custom oversized hot dog. Bunyon's closed in October 2002, and the statue went up for sale. A year later it found a new home in Atlanta, which arranged a free, long-term loan with the owners and put the giant on display on Arch Street. The figure that once advertised a Chicago-area hot dog stand now presides over a Route 66 town of fewer than 1,700 people.

## Welcoming the World with a Smile

Atlanta's motto is "Welcoming the world with a smile," and since 2003 its water tower has carried a smiley face, painted at the request of alderwoman Billie Cheek. The town's newspaper ran on its own until 1964, when it merged with The Armington Helper from nearby Armington in Tazewell County; the combined Atlanta Argus and The Armington Helper ran until 1983. Country music station WLCN (96.3 FM) is licensed to Atlanta. Atlanta's list of notable people includes U.S. Congressman Jeremiah D. Botkin, organist and conductor Paul Callaway, sculptor Ellen Rankin Copp, sportswriter Dave Kindred, and composer and music educator Eleanor Sophia Smith.

## Flight Context

Atlanta sits at 40.2628 N, 89.2331 W in Logan County, Illinois, along historic Route 66. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a compact small-city grid in flat farmland and a water tower painted with a smiley face. Logan County Airport (KAAA) at Lincoln lies about 8 nautical miles to the southwest, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal about 19 nautical miles to the northeast. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the prairie, and low ceilings in winter.
