# McLean, Illinois

A railroad village laid out in 1855 by a Bloomington mayor who never lived there, McLean keeps its original diagonal town plan and a Route 66 stop that began as a sandwich stand in 1928.

**Location:** 40.3150, -89.1706

**Categories:** Villages in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1855, 1855 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Franklin Price laid out McLean on June 22, 1855, and never lived in it. He was a Bloomington man by then, born in Chester Valley, Pennsylvania, who had come to Bloomington in 1849 and worked as a clerk and a newspaper writer. He was the brother-in-law of the Bloomington real estate developer Kersey Fell, and in 1855 and again in 1856 he was elected the city's mayor. The town he founded on the new railroad line has kept its shape remarkably well. McLean, a village of 743 people in southwestern McLean County, Illinois, still follows its original town plan almost unaltered.

## The Colony That Came First

Someone had tried before. In 1836 the Providence Farmers and Mechanics Emigrating Society of Rhode Island established a town called Mt. Hope two miles east of the present village. The colony was not a success, and only three settlers actually made it to McLean County. When McLean was founded, Mt. Hope's church building was moved into the new town. McLean itself grew out of the Alton and Springfield Railroad, soon to become the Alton Railroad, which was being built through the county at the time; Atlanta, Normal and Towanda were laid out along the same line in the same period. The first residents were two railroad employees, the brothers G.L. and F.A. Wheelock, who moved into the new station house to conduct business. A blacksmith shop was the first business, and H.H. Dillon built the first warehouse. McLean incorporated as a village on May 29, 1866, and by 1900 it had grown to 532 people.

## A Square Cut on the Diagonal

Look at a map of old McLean and the plan jumps out. The original town was basically a square, its streets running north–south and east–west, sliced diagonally by the railroad with a line of lots paralleling each side of the tracks. Normal, Towanda, Odell and Dwight were laid out along similar lines. A widened strip beside the tracks was labeled "Depot Grounds," though in McLean it appeared only on the southeast side. The triangle on the northwest side was set aside as public property and is still used as a park, while the matching triangle across the tracks went unlabeled, its intended purpose now unclear. McLean had one quirk of its own: no streets ran between the diagonal lots along the tracks. Perhaps because of that, much of the business district grew up along Morgan Street, just north of the park, and Hamilton Street, just west of it. The hotel and the town jail stood on the lots southeast of the railroad.

## Where Dixie Road Meets the Interstate

Modern McLean sits at a crossroads. Interstate 55 passes along the east side of the village, reached from Exit 145 at U.S. Route 136, and leads 15 miles northeast to Bloomington, the county seat, and 16 miles southwest to Lincoln. US 136, known locally as Dixie Road, runs along the south edge of the village center, heading 10 miles east to Heyworth and 23 miles west to San Jose. The village is compact, just 1.18 square miles, all of it land. Its population has slipped in recent years, from 830 in 2010 to 743 in 2020, and local students attend the Olympia Community Unit School District 16. For travelers, though, McLean's name is tied to one address more than any other.

## A Sandwich Stand on Route 66

In 1928, J.P. Walters and John Geske opened a small sandwich stand in a truck mechanic's garage on old US Route 66 in McLean. Demand grew so quickly that by 1930 they had expanded into a full-size restaurant, and after a fire in 1965 it was rebuilt. When Interstate 55 was built, the stretch of Route 66 where the business stood became part of the new highway. Once known as the Dixie Truckers Home and now as the Dixie Travel Plaza, it was honored along with its founders by the Route 66 Association of Illinois, which inducted them into its hall of fame on June 9, 1990. The Route 66 Hall of Fame itself was housed at the Dixie when it opened that year, until it moved to Pontiac in 2004. In 2003 the owners sold the business to a Rhode Island company, an unexpected echo of the Rhode Island colonists at Mt. Hope. Ben Gulley of LeRoy bought it in 2009, and Road Ranger of Rockford followed in 2012.

## A Quarter a Play

McLean has a newer draw, too. Arcadia: America's Playable Arcade Museum grew out of John Yates's childhood passion for games. It started almost by accident, when he began renovating a building, and it took off after he shared it on Facebook. The collection has since spread across multiple buildings, mixing classics like Pac-Man and Donkey Kong with modern titles for different generations of players. Yates keeps the older machines going partly because they are easier to repair, and most games still take real quarters. It is a family place, where parents and children bond over the same joysticks, and it has brought an economic lift to McLean by drawing visitors from neighboring states. A village that began with a station house and a blacksmith shop now counts an arcade among its reasons to stop.

## Flight Context

McLean lies at 40.3150°N, 89.1706°W in southwestern McLean County, Illinois. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for Interstate 55 running northeast–southwest along the east side of the village and U.S. Route 136 meeting it at Exit 145. Inside the village, the original plan is still readable: a square street grid cut diagonally by the railroad corridor, with the triangular park on its northwest side. Logan County Airport (KAAA) at Lincoln is about 12 nautical miles to the southwest, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 15 nautical miles to the northeast. Flat terrain makes the interstate an easy guide on clear days.
