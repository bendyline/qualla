# Dixie Travel Plaza

Born in 1928 as a sandwich stand in a McLean, Illinois, truck garage, the Dixie Trucker's Home grew into a Route 66 landmark said to have closed for just one day in its first 75 years, when fire struck in 1965.

**Location:** 40.3104, -89.1690

## Introduction

In 1928, J.P. Walters and John Geske opened a small sandwich stand in a rented part of a truck mechanic's garage at McLean, Illinois. It soon grew to offer coffee, donuts and a lunch counter with six stools for hungry drivers. The road was U.S. Route 66, and the business they founded would become the Dixie Trucker's Home, a place so dependable that, by one account, it closed for only a single day between 1928 and 2003.

## From Garage to Landmark

Demand grew quickly. By 1930 the Dixie needed a full-size restaurant, and it kept expanding in the years that followed. The McLean County Museum of History calls it possibly the first truck stop on Route 66, and it became a fixture of travel books and histories of the old road. The Geske family and its descendants owned and managed it for 75 years, from 1928 until 2003. In 1990 the Route 66 Association of Illinois inducted Walters, Geske and the Dixie Trucker's Home into its hall of fame, recognizing their contributions to the character of Route 66. The Dixie was also home to the Route 66 Hall of Fame museum when it opened in 1990, and kept it until 2004, when the museum moved to a larger location in Pontiac, Illinois.

## One Day Dark

The single day the Dixie is said to have closed came in 1965, when fire struck. It was rebuilt, this time along the newer alignment of Route 66 near Interstate 55. The highway itself was changing around it. When the Interstate system arrived and Route 66 was decommissioned, I-55 took over the corridor, and the truck stop that had been built for the Mother Road found itself serving the interstate that replaced it. Its old role carried over, and the Dixie came to be described as "one of the most impressive" late-night stops along I-55.

## New Owners, Same Crossroads

In 2003 the founding family's descendants sold the business to a company from Rhode Island, which renamed it the Dixie Travel Plaza. The sale also covered the family's other two Dixie truck stops, in Tuscola and Effingham, Illinois, which were branded Dixie Travel Plazas as well. Ownership kept changing. In January 2009 the McLean plaza was sold to Ben Gulley of LeRoy, Illinois, who gave it a major renovation, and in June 2012 it passed to Road Ranger of Rockford, Illinois, which remodeled it completely. Today it operates under the Road Ranger brand, but the diner at its heart still carries the character of a place that has been feeding travelers on this route since 1928.

## Flight Context

The Dixie Travel Plaza sits at 40.3104 N, 89.1690 W in the village of McLean, McLean County, Illinois, on Interstate 55. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, follow I-55 southwest from Bloomington-Normal and look for a large truck stop with wide truck parking lots at the McLean interchange, with the small village grid nearby and Atlanta about 4 nautical miles to the southwest. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal is about 15 nautical miles to the northeast, and Logan County Airport (KAAA) at Lincoln about 12 nautical miles to the southwest. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the prairie, and low ceilings in winter.
