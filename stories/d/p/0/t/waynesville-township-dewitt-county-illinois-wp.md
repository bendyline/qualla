# Waynesville Township, DeWitt County, Illinois

A quiet DeWitt County township whose village is one of central Illinois's oldest settlements, passed over by the county seat, the Illinois Central and the highways, and marked by six cemeteries and the memory of the 1855 cholera outbreak.

**Location:** 40.2558, -89.0886

**Categories:** Townships in DeWitt County, Illinois, 1858 establishments in Illinois, Populated places established in 1858, Townships in Illinois

## Introduction

Waynesville has a long history of being passed over. The village inside this rural township is one of the oldest settlements in the region, first settled around 1825 and platted in 1832, and yet the big prizes of the 19th century kept going somewhere else. The county seat went elsewhere. So did the Illinois Central Railroad, and later so did the state highways. What remains is Waynesville Township, 24.39 square miles of DeWitt County, all of it land, where 640 people lived at the 2020 census.

## Named for a Revolutionary Hero

The first settlers were Prettyman Marvel and his wife, Rebecca (Barr) Marvel, who arrived around 1825. A settlement grew up about a mile south of Kickapoo Creek, near where the village stands today, and when it was platted in 1832 it took the name Waynesville, after General Anthony Wayne, the Revolutionary War hero. For a few years it looked like a place with a future. Then the map of Illinois began to be redrawn around it.

## Bypassed Three Times

The first blow came in 1839, when DeWitt County was established and Waynesville found itself at the far northwest corner of the new county, out of the running for county seat. The second came with the railroad. Influential politicians in Bloomington and Clinton managed to have the Illinois Central routed through their own cities, and the line bypassed Waynesville. A smaller line, the Illinois Midland, was serving the village by 1882, but the main route was gone. The third blow came much later, when the state highway system was laid out and the village was passed over once again. Each decision sent growth toward other towns. By the 2020 census, the village of Waynesville counted 381 residents, and the township around it remained deeply rural, with just over 26 people per square mile.

## The Cholera Year

The hardest chapter in Waynesville's history came in 1855, when cholera swept through the village. By the McLean County Museum of History's account it killed some 50 residents out of a population of perhaps 300 to 350, one of the worst outbreaks of the disease in central Illinois, though an 1882 county history names only a dozen village victims. In a village that small, that meant roughly one resident in six or seven, a loss measured not in statistics but in neighbors, parents and children. The township today holds six cemeteries, named Big Grove, Evergreen, Fairview, Halsey, Rock Creek and Union. They are small and quiet, and they are among the oldest things in the landscape.

## A Township by the Numbers

Modern Waynesville Township is a place of 254 households and 303 housing units. Its residents skew older, with a median age of 42.6 and nearly a quarter of the population, 24.9 percent, aged 65 or older. Children here may attend school in any of three directions, since the township is served by the Clinton, Heyworth and Olympia community unit school districts. Geographic records list a small landing strip, the Holt Landing Strip, within its borders, though it is not clear that it is still active. None of this adds up to a boomtown, and it never has. But the village that lost the county seat, the Illinois Central and the highways has held on for two centuries, and that persistence is its own kind of story.

## Flight Context

Waynesville Township is centered near 40.2558°N, 89.0886°W in northwestern DeWitt County, Illinois. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the small grid of Waynesville village about a mile south of Kickapoo Creek, set in open rural country. Interstate 55 runs northeast–southwest several miles to the west, and U.S. Route 51 lies about five miles to the east near Heyworth. Logan County Airport (KAAA) at Lincoln is about 13 nautical miles to the west-southwest, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 15 nautical miles to the north-northeast. The flat terrain gives long views in clear weather.
