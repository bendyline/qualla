# Heyworth, Illinois

A McLean County village built on one landowner's gifts of land, and named for an English railroad backer after its first name, Elmwood, turned out to be taken.

**Location:** 40.3147, -88.9778

**Categories:** Villages in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1859, 1858 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

When the Illinois Central Railroad opened a station here in 1856, it was called Elmwood. The name did not last. It was soon discovered that a locality in Peoria County already had it, so the railroad's president proposed a replacement honoring Lawrence Heyworth, an English investor in the line whom the village's own history calls a director. A village on the prairie of southern McLean County, Illinois, has carried the Englishman's surname ever since. The man who actually made the place happen, a landowner named Campbell Wakefield, never lent his name to the town at all. At the 2020 census Heyworth had 2,791 people, and it sits twelve miles south of Bloomington at the meeting of two U.S. highways.

## Timber, Turkeys and Gray Wolves

Before settlement, this ground was thick with timber and underbrush. The Kickapoo lived here, and the woods supported a great number of deer, wild turkeys and packs of large gray wolves. As settlers moved west, families began taking up land nearby as early as 1824: the Rutledges, Funks, Passwaters, Bishops, Nobles, Wakefields and Martens. The village's early history was later written up by Charles A. Marker in A History of Heyworth, published by The Heyworth Star in 1926. Among those early families, one man kept buying. Campbell Wakefield added to his original tract again and again until he held about 1,500 acres in a single plot, a spread that took in the ground where the village stands today. He is remembered as Heyworth's primary founder, and nearly everything that came next ran through his land.

## The Man Who Gave Away the Depot

As the region's population grew and industries began to spring up, railroads were becoming a great advantage. In 1852 and 1853 the Illinois Central was built through the Wakefield land, and when the line began operating in 1855, residents hoped for a station of their own. Many property owners vied for it. Wakefield acted at once, donating a large tract for the station, and beside it he set aside land for a new town, a Presbyterian church and a district school. In other words, he laid out a community's civic bones before it even had a lasting name. The station opened in 1856 along with the first post office. Wakefield kept giving, making several more donations to encourage trade and persuade businessmen to set up shop, and he became the proprietor of the new town. By the village's own history, Heyworth was incorporated on March 31, 1869, though a 2005 Illinois House resolution gives May 10, 1901.

## Where Two Highways Cross

Heyworth sits in southern McLean County, served by two U.S. highways. U.S. Route 51 passes along the west side of the village as a four-lane divided highway, leading 12 miles north to Bloomington, the county seat, and the same distance south to Clinton. U.S. Route 136 runs right through the village center, heading 43 miles east to Rantoul and 10 miles west to McLean. The village covers 3.39 square miles, all but 0.06 of them land. Railroads brought Heyworth its growth in the 19th century. Now the pull comes from the north, where recession-resistant business expansion in Bloomington–Normal drives the village's growth, and the road that connects them has taken over the job the depot once did.

## Hey Days and a Clinic Back in Town

Village life keeps a small-town rhythm. Heyworth has its own police department and several churches of various denominations, while fire and ambulance service come from the Randolph Township Fire Protection District, whose voters approved a tax increase in March 2026 to build a $4 million combined fire and ambulance station on Route 136. Every May the Hey Days celebration brings a carnival, food and live entertainment, and at Christmas the "Holidays Heyworth Style" parade and craft show takes over. One milestone came in January 2010, when OSF Primary Care opened a clinic in the village, run by the OSF Healthcare System under OSF St. Joseph's Medical Center in Bloomington. It gave residents healthcare access in town for the first time since 1989. Children can stay in Heyworth from their first classroom to graduation. Heyworth Elementary School on South Joselyn Street serves pre-K through sixth grade, and Heyworth Jr/Sr High School on West Cleveland Street takes grades seven through twelve, with teams ranging from football and golf to soccer and wrestling, plus a marching band, a speech program and FFA.

## Flight Context

Heyworth lies at 40.3147°N, 88.9778°W in southern McLean County, Illinois. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the four-lane divided U.S. Route 51 running north–south along the village's west side and U.S. Route 136 crossing east–west through the village center. Bloomington–Normal spreads across the horizon about 10 nautical miles to the north along Route 51, and Clinton lies about the same distance to the south. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 10 nautical miles to the north-northeast and has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower if you route near it; Decatur Airport (KDEC) is roughly 29 nautical miles to the south. The terrain is flat, so the village grid and the highway junction stand out clearly on a clear day.
