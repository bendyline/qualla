# The Bibliophile's Homestead

A Victorian mansion expanded to house a massive private library was rescued from decades of disrepair by a county museum association, whose Apple 'N Pork festival helped fund its restoration.

**Location:** 40.1522, -88.9592

**Categories:** Houses on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois, Houses in DeWitt County, Illinois, Historic house museums in Illinois, Open-air museums in Illinois, Museums in DeWitt County, Illinois, National Register of Historic Places in DeWitt County, Illinois

## Introduction

It is a study in contrasts: a man once surrounded himself with several thousand volumes of literature, yet today thousands gather at his home for a celebration of agricultural history.

In the 1870s, John Bishop and his wife Minerva began construction on a mansion in Clinton, Illinois. The couple occupied the home together. Then, following Minerva's death in 1880, Mr. Bishop sold the house to his brother-in-law, Clifton H. Moore. A wealthy land speculator and Clinton's premiere attorney, Moore took ownership of the property that had belonged to his sister.

## The Bibliophile's Wing

At one point in time, it was reported that Moore had the largest private library in Illinois, excluding Chicago. He added a west wing to the mansion in 1887 specifically to house his vast collection of books. This two-story room featured a vaulted ceiling, stenciled walls, and an iron railing around a suspended upper gallery, with four-season windows lining the upper level. Accounts of the library's final size differ: one puts it at about 5,000 volumes when he died, another at more than 6,000.

## The Empty Shelves

Moore's era as a private collector ended in 1901. Upon his death, Moore left his library collection to the city of Clinton to form the basis of the Vespasian Warner Public Library, where that collection is archived today. After the books were gone, the house sat in disrepair for several decades.

## A Community Rescue

That decline met its match in 1967. That year, the newly formed DeWitt County Museum Association restored the home to preserve and promote the history and heritage of DeWitt County. To fund the maintenance and restoration of the mansion, the Association established a quirky event: the Apple 'N Pork festival. First held in 1967, this celebration helped raise funds for the mansion's restoration and maintenance. The museum receives no tax money for maintenance and operations, funding itself instead through memberships, donations, admissions, and events.

## The Modern Homestead

Today, the C.H. Moore Homestead and DeWitt County Museum occupies ten acres of rolling ground on the north edge of Clinton. It is no longer a private residence, but a public site that was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1979.

Visitors can walk through the restored Victorian Mansion House and the Kent Collection or explore an original 1860s carriage barn. The grounds include a replica Indiana-style covered bridge and period herb and flower gardens, alongside an operable blacksmith shop. Three barns house a working telephone display, railroad items, autos, sleighs, and antique farm equipment.

The cycle of the year brings the crowd back to the grounds on the last full weekend of September. Thousands of people gather for the Apple 'N Pork festival, helping to keep the quiet sanctuary once home to a bibliophile open to the public.
