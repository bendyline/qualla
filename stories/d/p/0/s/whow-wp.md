# WHOW

Clinton, Illinois's daytime-only farm station has broadcast from a building made to look like a big red barn, launched a young John Hartford, gone silent amid money troubles and an FCC fine, and lost its tower to an ice storm.

**Location:** 40.0953, -88.9642

**Categories:** Radio stations in Illinois, News and talk radio stations in the United States, Radio stations established in 1947, DeWitt County, Illinois, 1947 establishments in Illinois, Daytime-only radio stations in Illinois

## Introduction

Every evening, WHOW falls silent. The station in Clinton, Illinois, broadcasts on 1520 AM, a clear-channel frequency, and its license requires it to sign off at night to protect two Class A stations on the same frequency, WWKB in Buffalo, New York, and KOKC in Oklahoma City. Since 1947, that daylight-only voice has served Central Illinois, and today it speaks above all to farm country, in market reports, agriculture talk and a tradio show called "RFD Trading Post."

## Sunup to Sundown

WHOW signed on in August 1947 as a 1,000-watt daytime-only station, owned by Dr. Keith Rhea, H.E. Rhea, Rex K. Rhea, Tom Dinsmore and Frank Moots, doing business as the Cornbelt Broadcasting Company. In September 1950, James R. "Ray" Livesay acquired Cornbelt, and his family would hold the station for more than five decades. In 1962 WHOW raised its daytime power to 5,000 watts. Livesay's influence reached well beyond Clinton. In 1955 he founded the Daytime Broadcasters Association and led it as president until 1982, and in 1989 the National Association of Broadcasters gave him its National Radio Award, citing his "lifelong contributions to the industry." After Livesay died in 1995, his son, James R. "Jim" Livesay II, took over WHOW and its FM sister station.

## The Big Red Barn

On April 19, 1972, WHOW moved its studios from Clinton's downtown square to an office building at its tower site four miles south of town, built to resemble a "big red barn." For a station built around agriculture, the look suited the programming. WHOW places special emphasis on serving Central Illinois farmers, with a weekday morning farm show, live and local agriculture talk shows, a syndicated noon farm show, "The Horse Show" and frequent market reports, alongside local news and public affairs. That focus sharpened after a 2004 change of ownership, when the station added more live, local programming on farm and agricultural issues. Both WHOW and its FM sister have long operated from the barn. A 2025 trade report said owner Miller Media Group had agreed to sell its stations, WHOW among them, to Hart Media Group.

## From Clinton to Nashville

WHOW's former staff includes at least two people whose careers traveled far from DeWitt County. In the early 1960s, the station employed a disc jockey then known by his birth name, John Harford. He later moved to Nashville to pursue country music and, as John Hartford, wrote "Gentle on My Mind," the song Glen Campbell made famous. He went on to record more than 30 albums of newgrass and traditional bluegrass. Brian Leonard started at WHOW in 1979 and stayed until 1980, then moved to California, where he co-hosted a live comedy and music show on KSTS-TV in San Jose before a long stand-up career on television, in films and as a writer and performer for BBC Radio.

## Silence, Sale and Ice

The early 2000s nearly ended the station. In June 2002 the Federal Communications Commission cited Cornbelt Broadcasting for failing to keep an Emergency Alert System decoder working, post its antenna registration number and fence its AM tower. The company did not respond, and on October 31 the FCC ordered it to pay $17,000. The next day, November 1, 2002, WHOW and WHOW-FM went off the air. The licensee told the FCC the cause was vandalism and destroyed equipment at the hands of a former employee, and it argued it could not afford the fine, which the FCC cut to $1,000 in April 2003. The financially struggling stations had only eight to ten part-time employees. In January 2003 they resumed at least intermittent broadcasting, and normal operation returned in early November 2003 with borrowed staff and recorded programs. A group led by state senator Bill Brady bought both for a reported $300,000, closing in February 2004. Then, on December 1, 2006, the shared tower collapsed under ice from a storm the day before. WHOW limped back on a long-wire antenna, the owners sold to Randy Miller's Miller Media Group, and both stations regained full power on February 25, 2008.

## Flight Context

WHOW's transmitter and barn-style studio site is marked at 40.0953 N, 88.9642 W, about 3.5 nautical miles south of Clinton in DeWitt County, Illinois. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a red, barn-shaped building beside the station's antenna site in open farmland south of the Clinton city grid; treat any broadcast tower in the area as an obstruction and keep well clear at low altitude. Decatur Airport (KDEC) lies about 16 nautical miles to the south-southeast, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal about 23 nautical miles to the north; both have control towers, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower if you route near either. Central Illinois winters can bring icing; this site's own tower came down under ice in December 2006.
