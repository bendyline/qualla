# The Luck of the Draw

A village defined by a random drawing and professional golf, and hit by a powerful tornado.

**Location:** 39.8922, -89.6047

**Categories:** Villages in Sangamon County, Illinois, Springfield metropolitan area, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

In 1858, four men—Virgil Hickox, Cornelius Flagg, Joseph Ledlie and David Sherman—placed their names in a hat to determine the name of the land they had bought, surveyed and platted in Sangamon County. David Sherman’s name was drawn, establishing the town's name through a simple luck of the draw.

## Italian Settlers and the Mines

Early settlers in Sherman were predominately Italian and involved in the coal mining industry. The village did not officially incorporate until 1959, entering the record with a population of only 209 people. During these early years of incorporation, John Jerome served as the first village president until his death in 1978. He was succeeded by Sherwood Zimmerman, who was appointed to fill the term and held office until 1987, followed by Frank Meredith, who was elected in April of that year.

## The Green and the Highway

Growth began in the 1970s, driven by proximity to Springfield and Interstate 55. This expansion started primarily with residential development and later brought a surge of diverse service businesses.

The village is also home to the Rail Golf Course, a championship 18-hole course. From 1976 to 2006, it served as the home of the LPGA State Farm Classic. The village's own history says the course hosted the annual Labor Day Rail Charity Golf Classic until 2007, an event that drew thousands of annual visitors and generated nearly two million dollars for local charities. This tournament stands as the second longest running golf tournament in LPGA history.

The village now reflects a shift from its coal-mining origins, with a median household income of $148,906 and a poverty rate of 2.21%. This modern landscape includes the Route 66 Crossing commercial and retail district on the north side and the Sherman Village Park on the south side. The 10-acre park features an amphitheatre, pavilions, a pond, and a one-mile walking and cycling trail. For those traveling between towns, the 4.4-mile Sherman-Williamsville rail trail links the village to its neighbor, with whom it also shares a consolidated school system. Since May 2007, Trevor J. Clatfelter has served as the village president.

## The Rain-Wrapped Storm

On March 31, 2023, a high-end EF-2 tornado moved through Sherman between 6:12 and 6:20 pm. The storm produced estimated peak winds of 130 mph, carving a path 5.5 miles long and up to 400 yards wide. At 6:15 pm, the tornado was on the ground near Country Market; several storm chasers noted that the funnel was "rain wrapped," making it difficult to see as it approached.

The storm's path left a trail of wreckage across part of the village. By 6:28 pm, heavy damage was recorded on structures by the fire station and old Business 55. In a subdivision immediately east of I-55, numerous houses sustained severe roof and siding damage alongside several severely damaged detached garages and outbuildings. As the tornado continued northeast into rural areas, it struck a horse farm, damaging two large barns. Farther northeast, minor damage was noted, with sheet metal from outbuildings strewn into open fields.
