# WCIX

Springfield's channel 49 began in 1967 as a quiet relay for Champaign's WCIA, born of a 1950s dispute over where a transmitter tower could stand, and spent decades as an unacknowledged echo before finding its own identity.

**Location:** 39.7908, -89.5147

## Introduction

For years, the station on channel 49 barely admitted it existed. It aired every program, every newscast and even every commercial from Champaign's WCIA, and viewers in Springfield could watch it without ever learning its call letters. Only in WCIA's legal station identifications did its name appear at all. Today it is WCIX, programmed mainly as an independent station with a secondary MyNetworkTV affiliation, and its transmitter is in Clear Lake Township east of Springfield, and its story begins not with a broadcast but with an argument over where to put a tower.

## A Tower in the Wrong Place

Before WCIA signed on in 1953, it planned to build its transmitter tower at White Heath, halfway between Champaign and Decatur, on some of the highest ground in Central Illinois. Construction had already begun when WTVP, then the ABC affiliate on channel 17 and today's WAND, filed an objection. Its owner, Prairie Television, claimed WCIA was trying to encroach on its territory, even though it was obvious that Champaign–Urbana and Springfield–Decatur would be treated as a single market. To avoid delays, WCIA moved its transmitter to Seymour. The new site covered Decatur well, but in Springfield the signal was barely viewable until cable television arrived in the early 1960s. WCIA became the dominant station in the region anyway, and its owners went looking for a way to reach the capital properly.

## An Echo on Channel 49

The fix arrived in 1967. Then-owner Midwest Television launched a low-powered satellite relay of WCIA on UHF channel 49, under the call sign W49AA, to give Springfield and its surroundings better coverage. On February 4, 1985, the relay was upgraded to a full-power station with new calls, WCFN. Nothing about the programming changed. WCFN remained a straight simulcast of WCIA, commercials and all, a second transmitter wearing someone else's face. It would stay that way for nearly two more decades. Even the number 49 would eventually become more idea than frequency: when full-power stations switched to digital under federal mandate on June 12, 2009, WCFN shut down its analog signal on UHF channel 49 and moved its digital signal to VHF channel 13 (the FCC now lists channel 11), keeping 49 as its virtual channel.

## Finding Its Own Voice

In 2002 WCFN finally broke away from the simulcast to become the market's first full-time UPN affiliate. Few viewers lost access to WCIA in the process, because cable and satellite reached an extremely high share of homes in central Illinois, and the new digital transmitters soon let each station carry the other's schedule on a subchannel. When UPN and The WB merged, WCFN joined MyNetworkTV in September 2006. The station also became a home for sports. From 2007, an agreement between Nexstar and the Illinois High School Association brought IHSA tournaments to channel 49, giving Central Illinois an over-the-air flagship for those events it had previously lacked. From the 2010–11 NBA season through the 2019 baseball season, it carried Chicago Bulls, Cubs and White Sox games, and from 2011 it aired Indianapolis Colts preseason football. For a time it even picked up CBS's Labor Day US Open tennis coverage, because WCIA was committed to the Jerry Lewis MDA Labor Day Telethon.

## News at Nine and a Borrowed Name

On April 7, 2005, WCIA began airing the market's first prime-time newscast on the station. Produced in Champaign but aimed squarely at a Springfield audience, Primetime News at 9 had the slot to itself until rival stations launched their own 9 p.m. newscast in September 2006. The newscast later moved to 7 p.m., then returned to 9 p.m. in September 2017. On August 29, 2011, the station took a new identity: WCIX, a call sign that had belonged from 1967 to 1995 to the Miami station now known as WFOR-TV. Nexstar's 2019 acquisition of Tribune Media gave WCIX and WCIA new sister stations in Chicago and St. Louis. The station now shares studios with WCIA in downtown Champaign and keeps a news bureau on West Edwards Street near the Illinois State Capitol, finally present, in its own name, in the city it was built to reach.

## Flight Context

WCIX's transmitter site lies at 39.7908N, 89.5147W in Clear Lake Township, east of Springfield, Illinois. Broadcast towers are the main hazard here rather than a sight: stay well above charted obstruction heights and consult the sectional for the tower's published elevation before descending in the area. From 3,000 feet AGL, Springfield's city grid spreads to the west, with flat farmland in every other direction. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) lies northwest of downtown Springfield, about 8 nautical miles west-northwest of the transmitter site, and has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Visibility over central Illinois is usually good, though summer haze and low winter overcast can hide towers until you are close.
