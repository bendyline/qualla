# Murder of Sonya Massey

In July 2024 Sonya Massey called 911 about a possible prowler at her home near Springfield, Illinois, and was shot dead in her kitchen by a responding sheriff's deputy, a killing that led to a second-degree murder conviction, a $10 million settlement and a state law on police hiring checks.

## Introduction

Sonya Massey picked up the phone because she was afraid. In the early hours of July 6, 2024, she called 911 to report a possible prowler at her house in Woodside Township, just outside Springfield, Illinois. She was 36, born on February 12, 1988, the mother of a son and a daughter, and not long before she had voluntarily entered an inpatient treatment program, while her family worried about her mental health. Two Sangamon County sheriff's deputies answered her call that night. Before it was over, she had been shot in her own kitchen by one of them, and the footage of what happened would be seen across the country.

## Nothing Outside

Deputies Sean Grayson and Dawson Farley searched her backyard and the surrounding area and found no sign of anyone. Grayson knocked on the front door several times, and about three minutes later Massey opened it. The deputies told her they had found no one while she looked at her cell phone. As they were preparing to leave, Grayson asked about a black SUV in her driveway. It was not hers, she said, and Farley walked around the house to report its license plate. Then all three went inside. The deputies asked for her ID, and Massey began looking for it. It had been, until then, a routine call that turned up nothing.

## The Pot on the Stove

A pot of water was boiling on her stove, and Grayson asked her to remove it. Massey lifted the pot and carried it toward the sink. As the deputies stepped back, she asked where they were going. "Away from your hot steaming water," Grayson replied, chuckling. Massey then said, twice, "I rebuke you in the name of Jesus." Grayson answered with a profane threat to shoot her in the face and drew his pistol; Farley drew his as well. Massey said, "OK, I'm sorry," and ducked behind the kitchen counter. The deputies advanced with their weapons trained on her, shouting at her to drop the pot. Grayson fired three times, and one shot struck her in the head. He switched on his body camera only after the shooting. While Farley pressed dish towels to her wound, Grayson threw his medical kit to the floor, saying he would not "waste" it. Sonya Massey was pronounced dead at 1:47 a.m. at St. John's Hospital in Springfield.

## A Record Comes to Light

The Illinois State Police investigated the shooting. On July 17 Grayson was fired and charged with three counts of first-degree murder, along with aggravated battery with a firearm and official misconduct; those last two charges were later dropped. The body camera footage was released on July 22, and Grayson's past drew scrutiny as well. His record included two misdemeanor DUI convictions, the first in 2015 while he was in the Army, which gave him a general discharge for misconduct, and a 2022 high-speed chase as a Logan County deputy in which he ignored orders to end the pursuit. Massey's father criticized the sheriff's office for hiring him despite "serious blemishes on his record," and her family said police had initially blamed her death on suicide and on a neighbor. President Joe Biden said she "should be alive today," and Governor JB Pritzker said he was "enraged."

## What Changed

The shock carried consequences. Sheriff Jack Campbell, whom Pritzker had called on to resign, announced he would retire by August 31, 2024, and was succeeded by Paula Crouch. Massey's family, represented by civil rights attorney Ben Crump, reached a $10 million settlement with Sangamon County in February 2025. Separately, in January 2025 the county and its sheriff's office reached an agreement with the U.S. Department of Justice, without admitting liability, that called for mobile crisis team services, crisis-response and de-escalation training, and a review of citizen complaints. On August 12, 2025, Governor Pritzker signed Senate Bill 1953, known as "Sonya Massey's Bill," which requires police agencies to review an applicant's detailed employment history, including records from past employers, before making a job offer. Her name now travels with an effort to change who is trusted to carry a badge in the state where she died.

## The Verdict

Grayson's trial opened on October 20, 2025, in Peoria County. He claimed self-defense, and testified that he had not drawn his Taser because he was not sure it would work given what Massey was wearing. Farley, his partner that night, testified for the prosecution: "She never did anything that made me think she was a threat." After more than eleven hours of deliberation over two days, the jury found Grayson guilty of second-degree murder on October 29. On January 29, 2026, he received the maximum sentence of 20 years in prison, followed by two years of supervised release. At sentencing he said he had made "terrible decisions" that night and apologized. Massey's daughter Summer told reporters, "Twenty years is not enough." Grayson died in prison on August 23, 2026, his attorney said; he had stage 4 colon cancer. Lawyers for the Massey family said his death in custody "does not bring Sonya back."
