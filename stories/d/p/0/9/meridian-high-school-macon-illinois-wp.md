# Meridian High School (Macon, Illinois)

Born in 1994 from the merger of the Macon and Blue Mound school districts, Meridian High School carries the memory of the 1971 Macon Ironmen, the tiny-school baseball team whose run to the state final became a book and produced a World Series-winning manager.

**Location:** 39.7028, -88.9968

**Categories:** Public high schools in Illinois, Educational institutions established in 1994, Schools in Macon County, Illinois, 1994 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

On June 4, 1971, a baseball team from Macon, Illinois, played for a state championship. The Macon Ironmen wore hand-me-down uniforms and peace signs on their hats, and they had come out of a field of 370 teams to get there. They finished second. Four decades later, Sports Illustrated writer Chris Ballard told their story in a book, and one of those players, Brian Snitker, went on to manage the Atlanta Braves to the 2021 World Series title. Macon High School no longer exists under that name. Its successor, Meridian High School, stands in Macon about nine miles south of Decatur, a public four-year school that has been adding its own chapters since 1994.

## Two Districts, One Name

Meridian was born of a merger. In the fall of 1994, the Macon School District, which dated to 1927, joined the Blue Mound School District, founded in 1917, and the combined school opened its doors for its first year. Both old districts ended that year, after lifetimes of 67 and 77 years respectively. What replaced them is Meridian Community Unit School District #15, which stretches across a scatter of small central Illinois communities: Macon, Blue Mound, Boody and Elwin, along with some areas at the southern tip of Decatur. The district has divided its grades between two towns. Meridian Middle School, for grades six through eight, and Meridian High School, for grades nine through twelve, have both been in Macon, while Meridian Elementary School, serving pre-kindergarten through fifth grade, is in Blue Mound. The arrangement lets each town keep a school of its own, and it binds families across the farm country into a single set of Hawks.

## The Summer of the Mod Squad

The 1971 Ironmen were, by their publisher's account, the smallest school in Illinois history to reach the state final, a distinction the publisher's description of One Shot at Forever: A Small Town, an Unlikely Coach, and a Magical Baseball Season says still stands. The unlikely coach was Lynn Sweet, who had arrived in Macon in 1966. His publisher describes him as a hippie, a dreamer and an intellectual who reluctantly took over a ragtag team. Once he did, he set out to teach the boys as much about life as about baseball. The team that came together under him is remembered as the Mod Squad, a name a Herald & Review sports reporter gave it for its long hair, peace symbols and mismatched uniforms, and among its members was Brian Snitker, who graduated from Macon High School in 1973. Half a century after that June day, Snitker stood at the top of the sport as the manager of a World Series champion.

## Hawks on the Podium

The baseball run was not Macon's only brush with a state title. In November 1986, the Macon Ironmen football team took second place in the IHSA Class 1A playoffs. After the merger, the Meridian Hawks kept knocking on the door, finishing second in the IHSA 2A football championship in November 1999. On May 27, 2006, Rodney Oyler won an individual IHSA boys state championship in the open 800-meter run. Then, on March 14, 2009, the Hawks finally claimed a team crown, winning the IHSA Boys Class 1A basketball championship. Individual titles have kept coming since. On May 20, 2023, Kaylin Moreland won the 100-meter dash at the IHSA 1A girls state track and field championships, a blur of speed across a single straightaway.

## Rebuilding the Campuses

In the early 2010s, Meridian reshaped its schools. Macon County voters approved a one percent sales tax for school facilities in 2010, and together with a state school construction grant it paid for a project of about $45 million that the Herald & Review described when ground was broken in August 2014. At the old Meridian Middle School in Blue Mound, the classroom wing was to be torn down once a new elementary school next to the existing gym was finished. Middle school students were to move into the former high school building in Macon, and high school students were to have a new building, then already under way. Federal school data now list Meridian Elementary School (pre-kindergarten through fifth grade) in Blue Mound, and Meridian Middle School (grades six through eight) and Meridian High School (grades nine through twelve) in Macon. In June 2026 a severe storm and tornado badly damaged the elementary school in Blue Mound, and the district said it would not be ready when school started in August.

## From Macon to the World

The halls of Macon High School sent graduates in surprising directions. Toby Towson, class of 1965, became an NCAA gymnastics champion and later worked as a coach and a dancer. Dale Connelly, who graduated in 1973, went into radio and eventually succeeded Garrison Keillor as co-host of Minnesota Public Radio's Morning Show. Snitker, from the same class, built his life around the diamond. The Meridian era has produced its own international athlete: Lauren Doyle represented the United States in rugby sevens at the 2016 Summer Olympics. Put side by side, it is a varied roll call for a district built from a handful of small towns: a gymnast who danced, a voice on the morning radio, a big-league manager and an Olympian.

## Flight Context

Meridian High School sits at 39.7028 N, 88.9968 W in Macon, Illinois, about eight nautical miles south of Decatur in flat Macon County farmland. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, the small town of Macon stands out amid the surrounding cropland, with the city of Decatur and the waters of Lake Decatur visible to the north on a clear day. Blue Mound, home of the district's elementary school, lies about 5 nautical miles to the west. Decatur Airport (KDEC), four miles east of Decatur, is about 10 nautical miles to the northeast; it has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control nearby. Taylorville Municipal (KTAZ) is about 18 nautical miles southwest and Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield about 33 nautical miles west-northwest. Central Illinois is usually easy VFR country, but expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
