# The Ghost of Motion Raceway

Once a high-octane haven for nitro-burning legends, Motion Raceway transitioned from a professional drag strip to a makeshift grain depot before fading into a rural ruin.

**Location:** 39.5642, -89.0506

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Christian County, Illinois, Defunct drag racing venues, Motorsport venues in Illinois, Defunct motorsport venues in the United States, Sports venues completed in 1970

## Introduction

For years, the wide racing surface of Motion Raceway hosted nitro-burning fuel dragsters; later, the Moweaqua Farmer's Co-Op used that same asphalt to store grain, laying it directly on the pavement and covering it with tarps until it could be moved into the elevator.

John Jones, an Assumption native and former street racer recently discharged from the armed forces, initially planned to lay a strip of asphalt on his land so locals could race safely and legally. However, after visiting other area facilities, he surveyed the land and drew up blueprints himself to build a full-fledged drag racing facility.

## The Luxury of the Rollers

Completed in November 1970 and officially opened for competition in 1971, the track began as a quarter-mile strip. Because its total length of 3,000 feet became insufficient, it was later shortened to a 1,000-foot configuration to allow for more shutdown room. To cater to his racers, Jones installed rollers for cars requiring push-starting—a feature most racers considered a luxury. These rollers allowed funny cars and fuel dragsters to run, attracting big crowds.

The site hosted "Big Daddy" Don Garlits and Shirley Muldowney, along with Bill “Grumpy” Jenkins, Della Woods, and Steve Carbone. The Chi-Town Hustler AA/FC also competed there, and Ronnie Sox occasionally rented the track for weekday testing. Other legends, including Bill “Maverick” Golden, raced on the wide surface.

## The Sudden Silence

The noise stopped after the 1983 season. When the track shut down, the facility was dismantled; the stands and fuel island were removed, and the Hemi-powered in-ground starter for cars was filled in. The timing tower was moved north to Macon Speedway in Macon, Illinois. In the wake of the closure, a popular rumor emerged that the owner had been robbed of purse money at gunpoint.

## Grain and Ghost Racing

The Moweaqua Farmer's Co-Op used the track for grain storage until 2008. That year, a brief window of hope returned when John Jones gave Greg Clayton of New Covenant Performance the opportunity to hold muffled street car competition in an eighth-mile format. Racing was scheduled for May 3, 2008, though no timing system would have been used because there was no electricity to the property.

The plans did not materialize. Greg Clayton later posted a statement on his website that the plans had fallen through, and property owners rejected two insurance policy submissions. It was also stated that people entered the property at night and caused damage.

## The Field

A massive strip of cracked pavement now sits quietly in the grass of a field in Assumption. For some time, the strip remained in relatively good condition compared to many other shuttered counterparts. The rest of the facility is gone.
