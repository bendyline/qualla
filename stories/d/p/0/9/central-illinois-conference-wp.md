# Six Votes to One

A central Illinois high school conference formed in 2014-15 by schools leaving the Okaw Valley Conference, now down to six members after a 6-1 vote and a departure.

**Location:** 39.6253, -89.0178

**Categories:** Illinois high school sports conferences

## Introduction

The vote on December 13, 2021, came out six to one, and one school sat it out. Sullivan abstained from the decision to remove St. Teresa from the Central Illinois Conference because Sullivan was already on its way out, having accepted an invitation to join the Lincoln Prairie Conference beginning in the 2023-24 school year. A Herald & Review headline recorded it plainly: members voted to remove St. Teresa following the 2022-23 season. For a league whose first season was 2014-15, it was a major reshuffling of the lineup. When both departures were complete, a conference that had started with eight schools would be left with six.

## Leaving the Okaw Valley

The conference began with the 2014-15 school year. Its founding roster had nine names, all of them schools from the Okaw Valley Conference: Argenta-Oreana, Clinton, St. Teresa, Meridian, Central A&M, Sullivan, Shelbyville, Warrensburg-Latham and Tuscola. Before the new conference formally took shape, though, Argenta-Oreana decided to join the Little Okaw Valley Conference instead, which left the Central Illinois Conference, or CIC, with eight schools. One news account of the founding carried the headline "Okaw defectors to form 'Central Illinois Conference.'" Its members belong to the Illinois High School Association, the IHSA, and compete in many sports and other activities.

## The Six That Remain

Six schools make up the conference today. Central A&M, whose name joins the towns of Assumption and Moweaqua, plays in Moweaqua as the Raiders, and the map point for this article falls there. Clinton fields the Maroons. Meridian, in Macon, plays as the Hawks, Shelbyville as the Rams, and Tuscola as the Warriors. Warrensburg-Latham, from Warrensburg, plays as the Cardinals. The sports lists show how much the arrangements vary from school to school, down to co-op teams such as the one in which Warrensburg-Latham joins Maroa-Forsyth in cross-country and soccer.

## What They Play

Every school in the conference fields football, basketball, baseball, golf, and track and field for boys, and volleyball, basketball, softball and track and field for girls. Football is the exception to the shared schedule, though: IHSA's 2024 standings place Central A&M, Meridian, Tuscola and Warrensburg-Latham in the Heart of Central Illinois (Small) football conference, not in the CIC. After that, the menu depends on the school. Boys' wrestling is offered at Clinton, Shelbyville and Warrensburg-Latham. On the girls' side, golf is played at every school except Warrensburg-Latham, and competitive cheering at every school except Tuscola and Warrensburg-Latham. Tennis is played by Meridian and Shelbyville on the boys' side and by Central A&M, Shelbyville and Warrensburg-Latham on the girls'. Cross-country is offered at every school except Meridian. Soccer is played at Meridian, and Warrensburg-Latham fields both its cross-country and its soccer teams as a co-op with Maroa-Forsyth.

## State Finals and Scholastic Bowl

The conference article keeps a ledger of IHSA state-level finishes, school by school, and many of the entries predate the conference itself. Start with football. Central A&M finished first in 1997 and second in 1992, 1995, 1996, 2001 and 2019, while Tuscola took first place in 2006-07 and 2009-10, with runner-up finishes in 2007-08, 2010-11 and 2011-12. Basketball has its moments too: Meridian's boys finished first in 2008-09, Shelbyville's in 1996, and in 2014-15, the conference's opening school year, Central A&M's girls took first place. Central A&M's girls' track team did the same in both 2011-12 and 2012-13, and Shelbyville's competitive cheering squad added a first in 2005-06. Clinton's wrestlers placed in the state's top four five times between 1989-90 and 2000-01. Further down the ledger, Warrensburg-Latham's boys' basketball team placed third in 2002-03 and 2007-08 and its softball team was second in 2003-04, while its scholastic bowl team finished first in 2000-01, a reminder that these results reach beyond the playing field.

## Traces of the Old Lineup

The lists have not fully caught up with the membership. St. Teresa still appears in the soccer, tennis, and swimming and diving entries, and Sullivan in swimming and diving, and both keep their lines in the ledger of state results. St. Teresa's girls' cross country team won state in 2010 and its volleyball team in 2019. Sullivan's girls' basketball team finished first in 1990-91 and second the following year. The conference, in other words, is still being written: six schools share today's schedule in most sports, and two names that left remain part of its record.

## Flight Context

The article's mapped point is 39.6253 N, 89.0178 W at Moweaqua, Illinois, home of Central A&M High School. The conference has no single site; its member towns, among them Macon, Clinton, Shelbyville, Tuscola and Warrensburg, are spread across central Illinois, so there is no one viewing area. From 3,000-4,000 feet AGL over Moweaqua you are within easy reach of several airports: Decatur Airport (KDEC), about 14 nm north-northeast; Taylorville Municipal (KTAZ), about 15 nm west-southwest; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 33 nm west-northwest; Coles County Memorial (KMTO), about 35 nm east-southeast. Summer haze and convective storms are common over central Illinois.
