# Macon Speedway

Built in 1946 on the site of a brick factory, the tiny high-banked dirt oval nicknamed "The Other Brickyard" has survived headlight demolition derbies, shifting sanctioning bodies, a near-collapse in 2006 and a rescue by NASCAR stars.

**Location:** 39.7121, -89.0069

## Introduction

Every so often, the track gives back a brick. Macon Speedway was built in 1946 on the site of an old brick factory on the west side of Macon, Illinois, and from time to time buried bricks work their way up through the dirt to the racing surface. That quirk earned the place a nickname with a wink: "The Other Brickyard," a nod to the Indianapolis Motor Speedway's famous moniker. Macon's version is a short, high-banked dirt oval, and for many years it has been touted as "The World's fastest 1/5 mile dirt track."

## A Fifth of a Mile, Give or Take

Wayne Webb built the track, and it has run continuously ever since. The grandstands and facilities have changed many times, but the oval itself is the same size it was in 1946, even if no one has quite agreed on what that size is: over the years its length has been given as both a quarter mile and a fifth of a mile. What has never been in doubt is its standing. It has been described as one of Illinois's premier dirt ovals and one of the longest continuously operating racetracks in the state, and the track's own site says it has been in continuous operation since 1946. Many of the best-known drivers on the dirt-track circuit have raced here at one time or another. The track's listed one-lap record for Super Late Models belongs to Billy Moyer Sr. of Batesville, Arkansas, who circled the track in 10.228 seconds on April 30, 2015.

## Racing in the Dark

For many years the track belonged to the Webb family. Wayne and his brother Lloyd ran it as Webb Enterprises, which also staged demolition derbies at county fairs around the region, and they filled Macon's schedule with the most varied programs in its history. Almost every kind of motor-vehicle race turned up, from USAC Midgets and Sprints to unsanctioned Outlaw Late Models. Figure-eight races were held here too, which explains the track's unique triangle-shaped judges' stand. Some events were gloriously strange. In the "Roundy-round Demo Derby," drivers had to complete a set number of laps while also making contact with another car between the fourth corner and the finish line. In the "Headlight Demo Derby," the track lights went out, and entrants battled by the glow of their own headlights; to win, a driver had to eliminate every other car while keeping working headlights. Endurance racing had its place too, with 75-lap features and three-hour or 300-lap "Enduro" races.

## Big Purses and Hard Times

One event stood out. The Herald & Review 100 for UMP Late Models became one of the premier races in the Midwest, and its $10,000 purse drew top Late Model drivers from across the country. In 1985 local promoter Bob Sargent leased the track and aligned it with the newly formed United Midwestern Promoters, and big-purse races were won there by drivers including Gary Webb, Scott Bloomquist, Ken Schrader, Billy Moyer and Jim Leka. In 1994 Macon moved its weekly racing to the NASCAR Winston Racing Series. Ownership changed again in 1998, and a year later the track dropped its top class, Late Models, sending many of the area's best drivers looking for somewhere else to race. In 2001 the Webb family bought the track back in the name of Rich Webb, the founder's son and a former Macon driver. Then came 2006, when high expenses outran box-office and advertising revenue and the season closed early. Even so, on the last night of racing, 100 race cars showed up.

## Stars on a Short Track

Rescue arrived in 2007. Bob Sargent returned, teaming up with NASCAR stars Ken Schrader, Kenny Wallace and Tony Stewart as co-owners. They added new catch-fencing, repaired the grandstands, put up billboards along the backstretch and turned 12 acres of adjacent farmland into a larger pit area. Three nights of racing featuring NASCAR drivers drew sell-out crowds that season, and Speed TV brought its "Night of NASCAR Stars" to the track. In 2008 fans could even ride along in a two-seat Late Model race car. The track has produced champions of its own, including Jeff Leka, the 1999 NASCAR Winston Racing Series national champion, and Brandon Sheppard, the 2017 World of Outlaws Late Model Series champion, who once raced here weekly. In November 2022 the speedway changed hands once more, bought by Wild West Shootout promoter Chris Kearns, and in May 2024 it changed hands again, when Eric Turner and Kenny Brown and their families announced that their purchase was final. The track's own news pages show weekly racing in divisions from Modifieds to Hornets running through the 2026 season, which keeps the old brickyard busy.

## Flight Context

Macon Speedway sits at 39.7121N, 89.0069W on the west side of Macon, Illinois, about eight nautical miles south of Decatur along U.S. Highway 51. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, look for a small, tight dirt oval with grandstands on the edge of town, its enlarged pit area alongside. On race nights the lit track stands out clearly against the surrounding darkness. Decatur Airport (KDEC) lies about 10 nautical miles to the northeast and is the nearest major reference; it has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control nearby. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield is about 32 nautical miles to the west-northwest. Flat terrain gives good visibility, though summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
