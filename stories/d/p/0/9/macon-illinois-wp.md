# Macon, Illinois

A railroad town of about 1,200 south of Decatur, Macon is best known for its 1971 high school baseball team, which reached the state final under an English teacher with no coaching experience and produced future Atlanta Braves manager Brian Snitker.

**Location:** 39.7089, -89.0003

**Categories:** Cities in Illinois, Cities in Macon County, Illinois

## Introduction

They played in hand-me-down uniforms with peace signs on their caps. In the spring of 1971, the baseball team from Macon High School, in a small town in rural Illinois just south of Decatur, did something no one expected. Coached by an English teacher who had never coached before, the Macon Ironmen came out of a field of 370 teams and reached the state final. They finished second, on June 4, 1971, losing the championship game to Waukegan, but the run became a legend, and four decades later it became a book.

## Named for a Statesman, Built by a Railroad

Macon takes its name from Nathaniel Macon, an American politician who lived from 1757 to 1837. The town was platted in 1856 on land owned by the Illinois Central Railroad and was incorporated as a city on April 19, 1869. It remains compact, covering less than a square and a half mile in southern Macon County, with U.S. Highway 51 running north nine miles to Decatur, the county seat, and south 25 miles to Pana. The 2020 census counted 1,177 residents. Nelson G. Kraschel, who served as governor of Iowa from 1937 to 1939, was born on a farm near Macon on October 27, 1889.

## The Ironmen of 1971

The coach was Lynn Sweet, who arrived in Macon in 1966. The book's publisher describes him vividly as "a hippie, dreamer, and intellectual" bringing progressive ideas to a town "stuck in the Eisenhower era," intent on teaching his players as much about life as about baseball. Under him, the Ironmen made an unlikely run through the Illinois High School Association tournament and finished second in the state championship. According to the publisher, Macon became the smallest school in Illinois history to reach the state final. Journalist Chris Ballard told the story in his 2012 book, One Shot at Forever: A Small Town, an Unlikely Coach, and a Magical Baseball Season. The title captures what the season meant to a town this size: a single chance, taken all the way to the last game.

## From Macon to Atlanta

One of the boys on that 1971 team made baseball his life's work. Brian Snitker, who graduated from Macon High School in 1973, joined the Atlanta Braves organization as a player in 1977. He coached third base for the Braves, and in 2013 he was named manager of their Triple-A affiliate, the Gwinnett Braves. In 2016 the Atlanta Braves made him interim manager and then, that October, full-time manager, carrying a piece of the Ironmen story to the major leagues. The Braves won the 2021 World Series under him, and after the 2025 season he stepped down as manager and moved into an advisory role with the club. Macon has sent other names into the world as well. Toby Towson, a 1965 graduate, became an NCAA gymnastics champion, and Dale Connelly, of the class of 1973, went on to co-host Minnesota Public Radio's morning show. Art Wilson, born in Macon in 1885, caught in the major leagues.

## Hawks and Dirt

The town's sporting streak did not end in 1971. On May 27, 2006, Meridian High School's Rodney Oyler won a state championship in the 800-meter run at O'Brien Stadium in Charleston, and in 2009 the Meridian Hawks won the IHSA Class 1A boys basketball state title. Macon also has a louder tradition. Macon Speedway, a dirt oval of about one-fifth of a mile that has been touted as the world's fastest of its size, has run since 1946. When crowds dwindled in 2006 and the track's future looked uncertain, NASCAR drivers Tony Stewart, Ken Schrader and Kenny Wallace joined a former owner, Bob Sargent, as co-owners in 2007. On race nights, the roar of engines carries across the fields around a small town with an outsized sporting streak.

## Flight Context

Macon sits at 39.7089N, 89.0003W in southern Macon County, Illinois. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, follow U.S. Highway 51 south from Decatur for about nine miles; Macon's small grid sits along the corridor amid flat farmland. Decatur's larger urban area lies to the north, and Moweaqua is about five nautical miles to the south. Decatur Airport (KDEC) is roughly 10 nautical miles to the northeast, making it the natural reference airport; it has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control nearby. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield lies about 32 nautical miles to the west-northwest. Visibility over the open prairie is usually good, though summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
