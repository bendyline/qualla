# Lincoln College (Illinois)

Chartered in 1865 as the only college named for Abraham Lincoln in his lifetime, Lincoln College survived the Depression, grew a Lincoln museum and national-champion teams, and closed in 2022 after 157 years.

**Location:** 40.1570, -89.3607

## Introduction

On February 12, 1865, Abraham Lincoln turned 56, and in a small Illinois city that already bore his name, people broke ground for a college named after him too. The charter had come through just six days earlier. Lincoln University, as it was first called, became the only institution of higher learning named for Lincoln while he was still alive. It would outlast him by more than a century and a half, teaching students through depression, war and reinvention, until a spring morning in 2022 when the campus in Lincoln, Illinois, closed for good.

## A Name Given in His Lifetime

The Cumberland Presbyterian Church founded the school, and Lincoln was not the only town considered for it. Several sites were weighed before the city of Lincoln was chosen in December 1864. On February 6, 1865, the Illinois General Assembly granted the charter. Money soon became the question, as it would be again. James Millikin, a wealthy livestock breeder from Decatur, offered $50,000 for a new building on the Lincoln campus if the school would turn over its charter, on one condition: the citizens of Lincoln had to raise $25,000 toward the project themselves. They did, and the grant followed. From its first days, the college depended on a town willing to invest in it.

## Shrinking to Survive

For more than sixty years the school granted four-year degrees. In 1929 it became a two-year junior college, part of a wave of such schools created in the 1920s and 1930s, and the change helped carry it through the financial strain of the Great Depression and World War II. After the war the campus grew substantially, eventually counting seven dormitories, numerous classroom buildings, a library and the Lincoln Center, which held a gymnasium, modern classrooms and a museum. The college took a radio license for WLNX in 1974 and opened an extension in Normal in 1979, offering working adults an accelerated bachelor's program one night a week. Since 2010, the U.S. Department of Education had recognized Lincoln as a Predominantly Black Institution. In 2015, under its 22nd president, David Gerlach, the trustees voted to return the school to its roots as a bachelor's-degree college.

## Relics of a President

The collection began in 1941 as the Lincoln Room, founded on a large donation of Abraham Lincoln artifacts from an alumnus, Judge Lawrence Stringer. It kept growing, notably with a gift from Robert Todd Lincoln Beckwith, the last descendant of the Lincoln family. The room became a museum inside the McKinstry Library, outgrew that space too, and by 2014 had become the Lincoln Heritage Museum in the new Lincoln Center. Five miles north of town, on the college's 104-acre Creekside Outdoor Center for environmental education, a Lincoln College student named Judd McCullum made a discovery of a different age entirely: what is described as Illinois' largest mammoth fossil.

## Home of the Lynx

Lincoln's teams were the Lynx, and for a small school they won a great deal. As a junior college, its wrestlers produced numerous national junior-college All-Americans and took national championships in 1989 and 1991. The basketball program won back-to-back national titles in 2010 and 2011. After the move to four-year status, the Lynx joined the NAIA in 2018 and later competed in the Chicagoland Collegiate Athletic Conference. The alumni roll runs in unexpected directions: animation director Brenda Chapman, longtime Atlanta Braves manager Brian Snitker, who won the 2021 World Series, mixed martial artist Matt Hughes, and Congressman Edward Madigan all passed through Lincoln.

## The Last Semester

On March 30, 2022, the college announced that falling enrollment had left it unable to continue past the semester. Unless a "transformational donation or partnership" appeared, it would close on May 13. The school also blamed COVID-19 and a cyberattack. No rescue came. After 157 years, Lincoln College closed on the announced date, voluntarily resigning its accreditation, and the Illinois Board of Higher Education took custody of student transcripts and records. The Lincoln Heritage Museum stayed open. One odd coda followed: in 2025, three years after the doors shut, the Illinois state budget still listed a $500,000 earmark for Lincoln College, an item added in a 2018 capital bill, never funded, that keeps reappearing in the budget.

## Flight Context

Lincoln College's former campus sits at 40.1570 N, 89.3607 W in the city of Lincoln in central Illinois. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, look for the college grounds within the city grid, with Interstate 55 skirting the city and open farmland in every direction. Logan County Airport (KAAA) serves Lincoln directly; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) in Springfield lies about 24 nautical miles southwest, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal about 28 nautical miles northeast. Springfield is Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control near KSPI. Expect summer haze and convective storms over the prairie, and low winter ceilings.
