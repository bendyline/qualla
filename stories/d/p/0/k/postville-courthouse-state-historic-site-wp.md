# The Traveling Courtroom

A simple 1840s courthouse was dismantled and moved to Michigan by Henry Ford, only to return to Illinois decades later as a replica.

**Location:** 40.1472, -89.3803

**Categories:** Government buildings completed in 1840, Government buildings completed in 1953, Abraham Lincoln National Heritage Area, County courthouses in Illinois, History of Illinois, Illinois State Historic Sites, Lincoln, Illinois, Replica buildings, Law museums in Illinois, Museums in Logan County, Illinois

## Introduction

While waiting for their cases to come up on the docket, Abraham Lincoln and other young lawyers reportedly played town ball—a 19th-century version of baseball—in a vacant lot across from the Logan County courthouse. Raised in 1840, this simple frame building was designed in an austere Federal style that resembled a private home more than a seat of government. For eight years, it served as the original seat of county government, housing a circuit courtroom alongside offices for the sheriff, coroner, county clerk, recorder of deeds, and county surveyor.

## The Eighth Judicial Circuit

The courthouse was a destination on the old Eighth Judicial Circuit, located about 25 miles north of Lincoln's home in Springfield. When he first attended court here, Lincoln was a junior partner of John Todd Stuart. Between 1840 and 1848, he visited the site twice a year for his circuit law practice. He traveled with Samuel Treat, a colleague and friend who served as the presiding judge at Postville.

## The Great Dismantling

The building eventually left its original soil. In 1929, inventor Henry Ford purchased the old structure for $8,000. His contractors dismantled the frame structure and dug up the foundation, moving everything to Greenfield Village and the Henry Ford Museum in Dearborn, Michigan. Once there, a reconstructed version of the Postville Courthouse was built using those original materials, where it still stands today.

## The Centennial Return

The void in Lincoln, Illinois, remained for decades until 1953. For the centennial celebration of the city of Lincoln, the state of Illinois built a replica courthouse on the original site. The state used the dimensions and data of the original building to create a close reproduction. Furnished as an 1840s courtroom, the building stands on the original site not as a working seat of government, but as the Postville Courthouse State Historic Site.

## The Site Today

The replica now stands as a two-story frame building near the center of a landscaped city block within a 1.33-acre historical park. Managed by the Illinois State Historic Preservation Office, the terrain consists of trees and open grass space where picnicking is an activity.

The first floor contains a reception and orientation room and an exhibit gallery. These displays detail the legal system in mid-nineteenth century Illinois and the experiences of judicial officials and attorneys, including Lincoln. On the second floor, the space opens into rooms representing a county office and an 1840s courtroom. The site remains active through seasonal events, such as a craft fair on the last Sunday in August and an Abraham Lincoln Birthday Party open house held the Saturday before his birthday. During summer months, the park also highlights Mary Todd's Medicinal Garden.

The building stands as a reproduction of the place where Lincoln once practiced law—a structure that traveled from the Illinois prairie to Michigan, only to return as a replica of itself.
