# Three Silver Footballs

A 170-pound silver trophy has passed among Army, Navy and Air Force since 1972, presented by presidents, kept in glass cases, and, by one unconfirmed account, once carried off to Annapolis in a prank.

**Location:** 40.1900, -89.4000

**Categories:** College football rivalry trophies in the United States, Army Black Knights football, Navy Midshipmen football, Air Force Falcons football, Army–Navy Game, Military competitions in American football

## Introduction

In late November 2019, the Brigade of Midshipmen sat down to its noon meal at the Naval Academy and found a 170-pound silver trophy on display, one that had no business being in Annapolis. West Point, the previous year’s winner, was supposed to be holding it. How it got there remains unexplained, and the story of that prank, which rests on a single unconfirmed account, comes later. First comes the object itself: the Commander-in-Chief’s Trophy, awarded to each season’s winner of the football series among Army, Navy and Air Force, and passed among the three academies since 1972.

## The Third Academy’s Idea

Air Force’s football team first played Army in 1959 and Navy in 1960. Before 1972 it met Army in odd years and Navy in even years, and neither in 1961. The trophy was the brainchild of Air Force General George B. Simler, a former athletic director at the Air Force Academy, who envisioned it as a way to create an annual series of games against the Military Academy and the Naval Academy. President Richard Nixon first awarded it in 1972, and the alumni associations of the three academies jointly sponsor it.

Since that first year, Air Force has played both Army and Navy every season. Because the Falcons played Army but not Navy in 1971, the Army–Air Force game is described as the longest uninterrupted intersectional rivalry in college football, by one game over Air Force–Navy.

## Three Saturdays

The series keeps a fixed calendar. Navy and Air Force normally meet on the first Saturday in October, Army and Air Force on the first Saturday in November, and Army and Navy on the second Saturday in December. Air Force hosts Navy in even-numbered years and Army in odd-numbered years, while Army–Navy is played at a neutral site, usually in a major eastern city and most often in Philadelphia. When that game does land on a home field, circumstance is usually the reason: the 1942 and 1943 editions moved to Annapolis and West Point because of World War II, and the 2020 game was played on Army’s campus because of COVID-19.

Through 2025, Air Force has won the trophy 21 times, Navy 18 and Army 10, and it has been shared on five occasions, most recently in 2021. When it is shared, the previous winner keeps physical possession. In 2025 Navy took it, beating Air Force 34–31 and Army 17–16, while Army beat Air Force 20–17. It is a rare kind of prize, too: together with the Michigan MAC Trophy, the Commander-in-Chief’s Trophy is one of only two NCAA Division I FBS triangular rivalry trophies still awarded annually.

## Handed Over by the President

The trophy is named for the president, who is Commander-in-Chief of all U.S. military services under the Constitution, and presidents have presented it personally on a number of occasions. During the 1980s, Ronald Reagan did it in an annual White House ceremony. In 1996, Bill Clinton gave it to the Army team at Veterans Stadium after the Army–Navy Game. From 2003 to 2008, George W. Bush presented it to Navy teams at the White House, and the White House presentation continued under Barack Obama, Donald Trump and Joe Biden.

The prize once came with a bowl invitation as well. In the late 1980s and early 1990s, the winner, if bowl eligible, was invited to the Liberty Bowl in Memphis, Tennessee.

## Silver Footballs, Glass Cases

The trophy stands 2.5 feet high and weighs 170 pounds. Three silver footballs form a pyramid-like arrangement on a circular base, and three arc-shaped sections are cut out of that base, one for each academy. In each cut-out stands a silver figurine of that academy’s mascot, in front of small engraved plates marking the years the academy has won. Beneath each football is the crest of one of the three schools.

Where it sits depends on who holds it. Air Force keeps it in a glass case in the Cadet Fieldhouse, the indoor sports complex at the Air Force Academy. Navy displays it in a glass case in Bancroft Hall, the Midshipmen’s dormitory. Army houses it in a glass case outside the football offices in the Army Sports Hall of Fame, part of the Kimsey Athletic Center in Michie Stadium. After the 2020 season the original was replaced with an identical trophy, and the 1972–2020 trophy is reported to be housed at the College Football Hall of Fame in Atlanta.

## The Spirit Mission

By one unconfirmed account, the trophy has been the subject of at least one “spirit mission,” or prank, leading up to a service academy rivalry game. In late November 2019, that account says, a group of Naval Academy midshipmen took it from West Point and transported it to Annapolis. The secret came out when the trophy was placed on display at the noon meal in front of the Brigade of Midshipmen, and the event drew significant attention on social media.

When West Point’s leadership learned what had happened, the trophy was promptly returned. It is not known how it was stolen or transported, and neither academy offered an official comment. Navy’s season ended better than the caper did: it beat Army 31–7 on December 14, 2019, and went on to win the Commander-in-Chief’s Trophy.

## Flight Context

The map marker for this story, about 40.19 N, 89.40 W a few miles northwest of Lincoln in Logan County, Illinois, is only an anchor point: the trophy has no home there. It sits with whichever academy holds it, at the Air Force Academy, the Naval Academy in Annapolis or West Point.
