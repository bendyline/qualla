# The Horseshoe of Salt Creek

A conservation area in Logan County, on land once managed by the Department of Mental Health, encircles two state prisons.

**Location:** 40.1167, -89.3917

**Categories:** State parks of Illinois, Protected areas of Logan County, Illinois, Protected areas established in 1971, 1971 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Nature preserves usually exist in isolation, but the Edward R. Madigan State Fish and Wildlife Area is shaped like a horseshoe that surrounds the Lincoln and Logan Correctional Centers. This curve of land in Logan County, listed at 974 acres by the Illinois Department of Natural Resources, wraps around the institutional complex, with woods and water along Salt Creek.

## From Institutions to Railsplitters

The land was not always a public sanctuary. In 1970, the State of Illinois acquired the acreage from the Department of Mental Health, and development began in 1971. That same year, the site was founded as Railsplitter State Park. For more than two decades, this area south of Lincoln served as a public space for outdoor activities. It remained under the name Railsplitter until 1995, when the state sought to honor a local figure's career in government.

## The Secretary's Acreage

The park was renamed in 1995 in memory of Edward R. Madigan (1936-1994) to honor his lifelong dedication to state and national public service. He served as a member of the U.S. House of Representatives from the town of Lincoln and held the position of U.S. Secretary of Agriculture.

## The Wild Interior

A 2.5-mile section of Salt Creek—a major tributary of the Sangamon River—runs through the area. In 2006, the DNR stated that these waters contained bluegill, sunfish, crappie, drum, channel catfish, bullhead, carp, and both smallmouth and largemouth bass. Along the creek's bottomlands grows one of the largest American sycamore trees in Illinois, and the park's forest canopy also includes ash, hackberry, hickory, oak, and black walnut.

Beyond the creek bed, several groves of hickory and white oak are present, and a herd of 100 to 150 deer graze the trees and brush. Upland birds like quail, doves, and pheasants are hunted here, and the site serves as a principal pheasant hatchery for the IDNR, producing 80,000 to 100,000 hatchlings every year. The terrain varies from forest and hills to prairie and open grass space.

For those visiting the site, trails totaling 7.75 miles wind through wooded areas, and a three-quarter-mile jogging trail serves fitness enthusiasts. The park also features a 140-acre lake for fishing, though only electric motors are permitted on boats. Visitors can utilize a boat ramp for paddling and fishing, or spend time at the playground, picnic areas with outdoor stoves, and horseshoe pits.

## The Road Through

A historic route cuts through this mix of prison walls and wilderness. Interstate Business 55, formerly Old U.S. Highway 66, passes through the Madigan State Fish and Wildlife Area.

**Visitor Information** *   **Location:** South of Lincoln, Logan County. *   **Access:** Nearest limited-access exit is Interstate 55 exit 123. *   **Admission:** Free (fees apply for rentals). *   **Hours:** Open seasonally, generally from sunrise to sunset.
