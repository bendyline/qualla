# Farmington Central High School (Illinois)

Home of the Farmers, Farmington Central High School sits on a 2004 K-12 campus just east of Farmington, the latest chapter in a school story that began with a log cabin in 1833.

**Location:** 40.6936, -89.9842

## Introduction

The school building that Farmington, Illinois, put up in 1893 is said to have won an award at the World's Fair, not for its architecture or its teachers but for its ventilation system. It was the replacement for a school that had burned three years earlier, and it was a long way from the log cabin where Farmington's schooling began. That thread of rebuilding, merging and starting over runs all the way to Farmington Central High School, which opened on a new campus in 2004 just across the Peoria County line.

## Log Cabin to Chapman School

Farmington's first school was a log cabin, built on the outskirts of town in 1833. Those crude early buildings did not last long and were constantly replaced, and over time the one-room schoolhouses grew crowded enough to demand something better. The first of the new generation was the Chapman School, built in 1866 on East Fort Street on land donated by Phineas Chapman, whose name it carried. It stood three stories tall, with the lower grades on the bottom two floors and the high school on top. Late in 1890 fire destroyed it. The replacement, finished in 1893, was state of the art for its day, and it was that building whose ventilation system, by local account, won the World's Fair award.

## Room for Everyone

Enrollment kept outrunning the buildings. The Ward School, later renamed the Harris School, went up in 1908, and Chapman School gained another wing, but even that was not enough. In 1926 Farmington built a separate high school, dividing its students among an elementary school, a junior high and a high school. Consolidation came next. In 1968 Farmington East Unit District #324 was formed from Smithville, Hanna City, Farmington, Trivoli and Middle Grove. Nearby Yates City had kept its own high school since 1856, with a final building dating to 1928, but after failed attempts to consolidate with Elmwood it joined Farmington instead. The two districts combined in 1986 to form today's Farmington Central District #265. Yates City High School graduated its last class in May 1987, and the high school merger took effect that July 1.

## A New Campus East of Town

In 2001, voters passed a referendum, and the district began planning a new kindergarten-through-12th-grade campus just east of Farmington. The district partnered with the surrounding towns and handed the old school buildings over to them. Some became community centers; others now house city offices. In August 2004 the new complex opened at 310 North Lightfoot Road, technically in Peoria County, across the line from Farmington, which lies in Fulton County. Along with the high school, the district runs Farmington Central Junior High and Farmington Central Elementary. The campus is about 20 miles west of Peoria and serves a mix of town and rural families. The past did not entirely leave quietly: in May 2014, part of the old school building caught fire and burned down.

## Home of the Farmers

The school's mascot is the Farmer. FCHS competes in the Illinois High School Association and has two team state championships on record, both in girls' softball, in 1988-89 and 1990-91. The classroom record has had its bright spots too. In 2008 the school received an Illinois Honor Roll Academic Improvement Award, which recognizes schools with at least three years of rising test scores and sharp recent gains, and in 2009 it made Adequate Yearly Progress on the Prairie State Achievement Examination. Its graduation rate averaged 89 percent between 2000 and 2009. In a 2009 report, the district's faculty numbered 85 teachers averaging 14.6 years of experience, and 29 percent of them held advanced degrees.

## Flight Context

Farmington Central's campus sits at 40.6936 N, 89.9842 W, just east of Farmington on the Peoria County side of the Fulton-Peoria county line in central Illinois. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a large school complex set in farmland at the eastern edge of Farmington's small-city grid. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 13 nautical miles to the east; it is Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. The surrounding terrain is open cropland; expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms, and low ceilings in winter.
