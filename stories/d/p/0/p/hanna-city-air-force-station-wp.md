# The Sentinel of Hanna City

A Cold War radar station designed to monitor the skies was repurposed into a correctional facility where its towers watched the ground.

**Location:** 40.6989, -89.8258

**Categories:** Installations of the United States Air Force in Illinois, 1951 establishments in Illinois, Semi-Automatic Ground Environment sites, Joint Surveillance System radar stations, Aerospace Defense Command military installations, Buildings and structures in Peoria County, Illinois, Military installations established in 1951, Military installations closed in 1968

## Introduction

Four-legged steel temperate radar towers, which once scanned for unidentified intruders, later appeared to be used as observation towers. The station lies 1.6 miles west-northwest of Hanna City, Illinois, at a site defined by shifting vigilance. Established during the Korean War and operated from 1952 to 1968, the Hanna City Air Force Station (ADC ID: P-63) was one of twenty-eight stations built as part of the second segment of the Air Defense Command permanent radar network. In Peoria County, this installation served as a silent sentinel designed to watch for threats arriving from the horizon.

## Watching the Skies

On May 10, 1951, the 791st Aircraft Control and Warning Squadron was activated at the station. Initially functioning as a warning station and Ground-Control Intercept (GCI) using AN/FPS-3 and AN/FPS-4 radars, the squadron’s role was to guide interceptor aircraft toward unidentified intruders picked up on its radar scopes. The technical intensity of the mission grew over time; by 1959, the station joined the Semi Automatic Ground Environment (SAGE) system and fed data to DC-07 at Truax Field, Wisconsin. The hardware required massive support to function. Each FST-2 system used over 6,900 vacuum tubes and required 43.5 kva of prime power, alongside 21 air-conditioned cabinets and 40 tons of air conditioning.

## The Budget Cut

It did not last. Due to budget reductions, the 791st Radar Squadron was inactivated on June 18, 1968, and the entire site was closed on June 30 of that year. The facility sat in a state of transition until January 28, 1969, when it was declared excess to the General Services Administration. On August 8, 1969, the remainder of the site was conveyed to the Department of Health, Education and Welfare. The mission shifted from national defense to state corrections as the site began operating as the Hanna City Work Camp, a minimum security prison for the Illinois Department of Corrections.

## Watching the Ground

The facility opened in 1969 as a juvenile institution - in the early 1970s it was the Hanna City Boys' School - before being converted in 1983 into an Adult Work Camp for adult male felons, serving as a satellite of the Logan Correctional Center. Old USAF buildings remained in use until 2002, but the surveillance did not vanish; it simply changed direction. The old four-legged steel temperate radar towers, which had once scanned for unidentified intruders, appeared to be used as observation towers.

## The Final Transition

Then the prison era ended. The Hanna City Work Camp closed in October 2002, and by 2009, the state of Illinois turned the site over to Peoria County. For a time, as of August 2009, the facility was occasionally used for Special Weapons and Tactics (SWAT) training by the Peoria County Sheriff's Office. However, not all of the military footprint disappeared. Four buildings and the radar towers were transferred to the FAA, where the site continues to be an active JSS radar site, its old radars since replaced by a modern long-range set under a radome. As of 2012, while the FAA radar remained operational, Peoria County was considering using the rest of the property for a community organic farm or an agribusiness incubator. In 2018 a new state law gave the county full control of the 40-acre site and allowed it to be sold for private development, though who would pay to clean it up remained unsettled.
