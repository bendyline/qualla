# New Berlin, Illinois

A Sangamon County village that hosts the county fair every June, sits on the route of the 1838 Potawatomi Trail of Death, and once threw a wedding for Mr. Burger and Ms. King.

**Location:** 39.7253, -89.9119

**Categories:** Villages in Sangamon County, Illinois, Springfield metropolitan area, Illinois, Populated places established in 1865, German-American history, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Every June, a village of about 1,400 people west of Springfield turns its attention to the fairgrounds. New Berlin is home to the Sangamon County Fairgrounds and the annual Sangamon County Fair, which usually opens on the Wednesday before Father's Day weekend. For the rest of the year New Berlin is a quiet farm village strung along old Illinois Route 54, a place platted in 1838 and incorporated in 1865. But the ground here holds older and harder memories than any fair week, and at least one very modern piece of small-town fame.

## The Road Before the Town

The Potawatomi Trail of Death passed through this ground in the same autumn that New Berlin was platted. In the fall of 1838, about 859 members of the Potawatomi nation were forced from Twin Lakes, Indiana, and marched roughly 660 miles over 61 days to Osawatomie, Kansas, under militia escort. Soldiers had burned their crops and destroyed their village of about 100 structures so they would not try to return. More than 40 people died on the way, most of them children. Near New Berlin, a marker at Old Jacksonville Road and New Salem Church Road identifies the McCoy's Mill encampment. Another, near the Island Grove Methodist Church and Woodwreath Cemetery, marks the Island Grove camp. Markers now stand along the whole route, placed roughly every 15 to 20 miles where the Potawatomi camped between one day's walk and the next.

## Eighteen Sixty-Five

New Berlin was platted in 1838 and incorporated as a village in August 1865. Only a very few buildings from that original village still stand along the town's main thoroughfare, old Illinois Route 54, so the founding generation is easier to find in the records than in the architecture. The village has stayed compact, covering about 1.1 square miles. It counted 1,030 residents in 2000, 1,346 at the 2010 census and 1,381 at the 2020 census, and today it is part of the Springfield metropolitan area. New Berlin is also home to its own school district, CUSD #16, and New Berlin High School, which gives a village this size a center of gravity it might otherwise lack.

## Fair Week and a Memorial

The Sangamon County Fair is New Berlin's signature event, held each year at the county fairgrounds in the village and typically starting on the Wednesday before Father's Day weekend in June. The village's other public landmark is quieter. The New Berlin Area Veterans Memorial was dedicated on July 6, 2013. Between the fairgrounds and the memorial, the village carries two different kinds of civic life: the loud, communal gathering of summer, and the steady work of remembering.

## Mr. Burger and Ms. King

Joel Burger and Ashley King met in kindergarten in New Berlin. When they got engaged, they ran their announcement in the local paper, the New Berlin Bee, with a photo of the two of them posing behind a Burger King sign as a joke. The State Journal-Register picked up the story, and Burger King noticed. The company offered to sponsor the wedding, and on July 17, 2015, the couple married in what is believed to be the first wedding the fast-food chain ever sponsored. The wedding party wore paper Burger King crowns, and the guests' gift bags held personalized yo-yos, Mason jars and more crowns. It is hard to imagine a better return on a small-town newspaper announcement.

## Flight Context

New Berlin lies at 39.7253 N, 89.9119 W, about 13 nm west of downtown Springfield, Illinois, at roughly 650 feet above sea level. From 2,500-4,000 feet AGL, look for a compact village grid laid along old Illinois Route 54 in flat, heavily farmed country; the Sangamon County Fairgrounds sits within the village. Springfield's skyline lies to the east in clear weather. Nearest major airport: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) on Springfield's north side, about 13 nm to the east-northeast, with a control tower and Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over central Illinois in June, the same month the county fair fills the grounds.
