# Chatham, Illinois

Just south of Springfield on the original line of Route 66, the fast-growing village of Chatham hosts the Illinois Championship Cow Chip Throw, anchors a rail-trail to the capital and sits a few miles from a covered bridge whose age the sources dispute.

**Location:** 39.6728, -89.6983

**Categories:** Villages in Sangamon County, Illinois, Springfield metropolitan area, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Every July, the Illinois Championship Cow Chip Throw is decided in Chatham. The contest shares the calendar with the village's Sweet Corn Festival, both sponsored by the Chatham Jaycees, and it tells you a good deal about the place: a little irreverent, rooted in farm country, and proud enough of its traditions to crown a state champion in flinging dried dung. Chatham sits just south of Springfield, the Illinois capital, on the original alignment of historic Route 66, and it has grown into a community of more than 14,000 people.

## On the Original Mother Road

Route 66 came through here first. Chatham lies along the original alignment of the historic highway, the road now designated Illinois Route 4, a reminder of the highway's earliest years. Today Chatham is part of the Springfield metropolitan area, close enough to the capital that the two feel like neighbors. By the 2020 census the village covered about 7.4 square miles, nearly all of it land. Around it spreads the open, level farm country of Sangamon County, and the town square serves as the stage for festivals that punctuate the summer and early fall. Several of those events are organized by the Chatham Jaycees.

## A Village of Young Families

Chatham's defining story in recent decades is growth. In 2000 the census counted 8,583 residents. By 2010 the population had reached 11,500, and by 2020 it stood at 14,377, roughly two-thirds more than two decades earlier. The 2000 census painted a portrait of a young community: 47.4 percent of households had children under 18 living with them, nearly a third of residents were under 18, and the median age was just 35. Chatham is part of the Ball-Chatham School District, which includes Glenwood High School, three elementary schools, an intermediate school and a middle school. The village's list of notable people leans athletic. Olympic diver Kelci Bryant and Olympic figure skater Gracie Gold are on it, as are baseball players Reid Detmers, Phil Maton, Nick Maton and Jayson Werth.

## Rails, Trails and a Covered Bridge

The railroad left its mark here. The Chatham Railroad Museum (1902) recalls the Alton Railroad, and the Interurban Trail runs about 8 miles on an abandoned Illinois Terminal Railroad interurban corridor from the south side of Springfield to the center of Chatham, alongside a mainline now operated by Union Pacific. The Caldwell Farmstead is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Five miles southeast of the village stands the Sugar Creek Covered Bridge, a 110-foot Burr truss span built by Thomas Black. Sources disagree on when, placing its construction in either 1827 or 1880. The State of Illinois acquired it in 1963 and renovated it two years later, it joined the National Register on January 9, 1978, and it closed to traffic in 1984. Today it stands in a small park with a picnic area.

## A Calendar of Festivals

Chatham keeps a busy calendar. Every June the Chatham American Legion Homecoming Festival takes over the town square. July brings the Sweet Corn Festival and, alongside it, that famous cow chip competition, both sponsored by the Chatham Jaycees. The same group hosts Oktoberfest in downtown Chatham each year, and in a small twist of village logic, it takes place in September. For a village that has added thousands of residents since 2000, these homegrown events do real work, giving newcomers and longtime residents the same reasons to turn out. There is something reassuring about a community that grows this quickly and still gathers every summer to see who can throw a cow chip the farthest. A legion homecoming, sweet corn and an early Oktoberfest: together they set the rhythm of the Chatham year.

## Flight Context

Chatham lies at about 39.673°N, 89.698°W in Sangamon County, Illinois, just south of Springfield. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, look for the village's grid of newer subdivisions spreading across flat farmland, with the rail line and the Interurban Trail corridor running north toward Springfield and the Illinois State Capitol area on the northern horizon. Lake Springfield lies a few miles to the northeast. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is about 10 nm to the north, with a control tower and Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control near the city. Expect wide visibility over the prairie on clear days, with summer haze and thunderstorms.
