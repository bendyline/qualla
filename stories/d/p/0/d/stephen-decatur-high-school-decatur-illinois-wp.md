# Stephen Decatur High School (Decatur, Illinois)

For 138 years Decatur's original high school moved from church rooms to brick halls to a modern campus, won four state basketball titles in the single-class era, and lost its legendary coach on a rival's court.

**Location:** 39.8452, -88.9523

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Decatur, Illinois, Defunct high schools in Illinois, Schools in Macon County, Illinois

## Introduction

The Running Reds led by eight points at halftime. It was February 15, 1960, the last game of the regular season, and Stephen Decatur High School's basketball team was on the home court of its crosstown rival, the MacArthur Generals. Many observers thought it was coach Gay Kintner's best team since his 1945 state champions. Kintner came out of the locker room with his players to start the second half, collapsed from a heart attack, and died on the court. He was 64 and had coached the Reds for 32 years. It is one of the defining moments in the long life of a school that lasted from the Civil War to the year 2000.

## Classes in a Church

Decatur's first secondary school opened in 1862, at the height of the Civil War and thirty-three years after the city began as little more than a log cabin settlement on the banks of the Sangamon River. The first students met for a six-month school year in a room of the Church Street School. From 1863 to 1869, classes moved into the Baptist church at East William and North Water Streets, and it was there, in 1867, that Decatur High School graduated its first class: four girls who had completed a three-year course. In the fall of 1869 the school finally got a building of its own at East North and North Broadway, the street known today as Martin Luther King Drive. Eight students graduated in 1870, but nobody graduated in 1871, because the course had been stretched to four years. By 1900 the school counted 731 students and 19 faculty members, and a new building was clearly needed.

## Red Brick, Sandstone Pillars

The replacement opened in the fall of 1911 one block west, at 400 North Franklin Street. Built of red brick, with tall pillars of reddish Jacobsville sandstone framing its main entrance, it gained a three-story classroom wing on the north end in 1932. For more than sixty years this was the building generations of Decatur students knew as their high school. Like the city, the school carried the name of Stephen Decatur Jr., a naval hero who battled Barbary pirates twice and fought in the War of 1812, made captain at 25, and died in a duel with a fellow officer. It was simply Decatur High School until 1957, when the city's only high school was joined by Lakeview High School, a small rural district absorbed through consolidation, and by the newly built MacArthur and Eisenhower High Schools, constructed for the baby boom's surge of students. Its fight song, "Go You Decatur," was an adaptation of Northwestern University's "Go You Northwestern."

## The House That Kintner Filled

Gay Kintner's teams won state championships in 1931, 1936 and 1945, and after that last title the Reds became the "Running Reds," a name that stuck. In the late 1940s, with crowds packing in to watch him chase a fourth title that no Illinois school had ever won, the school added a 4,200-seat gymnasium on its east side. Some called it the finest gym between Chicago and St. Louis. Kintner never got that fourth title. Over 948 games he won 649 and lost 299, and the gym was renamed Kintner Gymnasium after his death. His final team went 22-2 in the regular season and reached the state semifinals of 1960, losing to eventual champion Chicago Marshall. Two years later, under successor John Schneiter, Decatur beat a Chicago Carver team featuring future NBA star Cazzie Russell, and Ken Barnes sank the deciding free throw with six seconds left. It was the fourth title, won in the single-class era when every school in Illinois competed in one tournament.

## Fire, Blast and a New Campus

In March 1970 an arson fire destroyed classrooms and offices in its southwest corner, and although the school reopened weeks later, a study recommended replacing rather than repairing the building. Its last year, 1974–75, brought another emergency. On July 19, 1974, a railroad car filled with isobutane exploded in a nearby rail yard and badly damaged Lakeview High School, so for that year Stephen Decatur students used the old building in the mornings and Lakeview students took over in the afternoons. In the fall of 1975, a new campus opened on the far north side of the city, all on one level. It was the first Decatur high school accessible to students with disabilities and the first with air-conditioned classrooms. The center jump circle from Kintner Gym's old wooden floor came along and was hung on the wall of the new gym, since the rubberized new floor could not take it.

## The Last Edition

Falling enrollment in District 61 forced Decatur to consolidate from three high schools to two, and Stephen Decatur closed as a high school in 2000. Its newspaper, The Observer, went out as one of the oldest continuously published school newspapers in Illinois, with 106 years of issues. The building reopened in 2001 as Stephen Decatur Middle School, which serves grades 7 and 8, and it served as a high school once more while Eisenhower and MacArthur were renovated from 2012 to 2015. Its graduates include cool-jazz singer June Christy, born Shirley Luster, class of 1943; Metropolitan Opera soprano Annamary Dickey; James Benton Parsons, who became a U.S. District Judge and, in World War II, directed the Navy's B-1 Fleet Band, made up of the first African Americans in the modern Navy to hold a rank above messman; and Jackie Spinner, once the Washington Post's Baghdad bureau chief.

## Flight Context

The coordinates, 39.8452 N, 88.9523 W, fall just north of downtown Decatur, Illinois, close to the historic site of the 1911 building at 400 North Franklin Street; the 1975 campus that later became Stephen Decatur Middle School is on the city's far north side. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, orient on downtown Decatur and on Lake Decatur, the reservoir to the east and south of downtown. Decatur Airport (KDEC) is only about 4 nm east and has a control tower (part-time, per AirNav), so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield lies about 33 nm west and University of Illinois Willard Airport (KCMI) about 33 nm east-northeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are the main visibility concerns in central Illinois.
