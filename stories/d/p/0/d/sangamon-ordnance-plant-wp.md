# The Industrial Flash-Bang

The rapid rise and mostly complete erasure of a 20,000-acre facility served by IRT Second Avenue Elevated coaches, with many of its buildings dismantled for schools, colleges and the Springfield airport.

**Location:** 39.8527, -89.2924

**Categories:** Industrial buildings completed in 1942, 1942 establishments in Illinois, Buildings and structures demolished in 1948, 1948 disestablishments in Illinois, Buildings and structures in Sangamon County, Illinois, United States Army arsenals during World War II, Remington Rand, Johnson & Johnson

## Introduction

To connect the Illiopolis Plant with Springfield, the Illinois Terminal used IRT Second Avenue Elevated coaches, rigged with trolley poles to provide power for car lighting and heating. These cars served a military complex west of Illiopolis in Sangamon County, Illinois, where around 15,000 people were employed. In April 1942, the government broke ground on the site after acquiring local farms through eminent domain. By September 1942, construction of the 20,000-acre facility was largely completed at a cost of $35 million.

## The Steel Veins of Sangamon

The facility began as two separate plants, the Sangamon and the Oak Ordnance Plants—operated by Remington Rand and Johnson & Johnson respectively—separated by Illinois Route 36. Before the war ended, they were consolidated into the Sangamon Ordnance Plant under Remington Rand. Thousands of people worked here, including many Woman Ordnance Workers (WOW). They produced 3 inch (76 mm) armor-piercing and high-explosive artillery shells, as well as 20, 57, 75, and 90 millimeter shells. The plant also manufactured bomb fuses and the cores of fire bombs known as "bursters."

## The Price of Speed

In October 1942, a courier traveling from the Post Office to the Farmers State Bank of Illiopolis was robbed of $20,000 in cash intended for the payroll of the two ordnance plants. Danger also existed on the production line. In August 1943, an artillery shell exploded at the Oak ordnance plant, killing Maurice Pryor, age 22 of Springfield, and injuring ten others. Two fatal production line accidents occurred in total, both resulting from mishandled explosives.

## The Great Redistribution

It did not last. Following World War II, the facility closed and was mostly dismantled. The Federal Works Agency received 359 buildings, many of which were removed for use at schools and colleges. Meanwhile, six warehouses from the site were dismantled and reassembled at the Springfield, Illinois airport. Surplus lumber from numerous dismantled wood-frame buildings was used for the veterans emergency housing program. By April 15, 1948, the last surplus property had been sold; earlier sales included eight buildings and 553 acres sold to the DeKalb Hybrid Seed Company for $98,000 for a poultry breeding operation.

## Erasure from the Map

The land eventually returned mostly to its former owners, and by June 1949, 90% of that farmland had returned to production. Some parts still remain—including bunkers that stood after the plant closed—and the ruins of the Sangamon Ordnance Plant lie west of Illiopolis. The financial cost of the reversal was stark: the government sold the land for $2,126,490, mostly to former owners, after acquiring it for $3,186,922.
