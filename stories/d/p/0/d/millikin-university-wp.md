# Millikin University

Dedicated by Theodore Roosevelt in 1903, Decatur's Millikin University began as half of a two-campus experiment and grew into a small private university whose popular majors include nursing and musical theatre and whose graduates range from the voice of Disney's Ariel to the credited chief designer of Cinderella Castle.

## Introduction

On June 4, 1903, the President of the United States came to Decatur, Illinois, to dedicate a college. Theodore Roosevelt delivered the dedicatory address as the new Decatur campus of James Millikin University was formally dedicated, and it opened for classes that September 15, the culmination of a plan set in motion two years earlier with Decatur businessman James Millikin as its chief champion. Millikin lent the school his name, and more than a century later Millikin University is still here: a private university historically affiliated with the Presbyterian Church (USA), with small classes and a stage tradition whose graduates include the original voice of Disney's Ariel.

## A University in Two Places

Millikin's birth was an act of institutional creativity. On April 30, 1901, Decatur's college was established through a partnership with Lincoln University, an existing school in Lincoln, Illinois, that, like the new venture, was tied to the Cumberland Presbyterian Church. Lincoln University's charter, in force since February 1865, was modified to create an overarching institution called the James Millikin University. Under that umbrella sat two units: Lincoln College, the renamed campus in Lincoln, and the Decatur College and Industrial School, which was still to be built. The arrangement let the Decatur project borrow the resources of an established college while it got on its feet, and the combined school took the name of its chief advocate. The two campuses shared that identity for half a century. In 1952 they separated into fully independent institutions, Lincoln College keeping its name and the Decatur campus becoming Millikin University, whose independent charter the state approved on July 23, 1953.

## Curtain Calls and Castle Towers

Millikin's graduation numbers reveal a campus with a strong theatrical streak. In 2024-25, according to federal data, Millikin awarded 16 bachelor's degrees in musical theatre and eight in drama and theatre arts, alongside 19 in nursing. The alumni list reads like a playbill. Jodi Benson, the original voice of Ariel in Disney's Academy Award-winning The Little Mermaid, arrived at Millikin at seventeen in 1979, left during her sophomore year to work professionally, and came back to finish her degree in 1993. Actress and singer Sierra Boggess, the late actress Annie Wersching, who died in 2023, actor Joel Kim Booster and musician Matthew West are alumni too. Disney runs through the story twice: Herbert D. Ryman, a Disney artist and imagineer, is credited as the chief designer of Cinderella Castle.

## Ink and Airwaves

Student voices have been part of campus since the very beginning. The Decaturian, the student newspaper, published its first issue in 1903, the same year the Decatur campus opened, and it is still student-run; its early issues are archived online. Today WJMU 89.5, known as The Quad, is the university's college radio station, with student staff who handle music programming, news and Millikin football broadcasts. Millikin's broadcasting story reaches back much further. In 1922 a license was issued for a new AM station on a wavelength of 360 meters, and it drew the call letters WBAO from a sequential roster of available signs. The station kept a limited schedule, and in 1928 federal regulators folded it into WJBL; WBAO was formally deleted on October 1 of that year, its programs moving to the other station.

## Big Blue

Millikin fielded its first athletic teams in the 1903–04 academic year, while the Decatur campus was still brand new. For decades the school competed mostly as an independent, until it joined the College Conference of Illinois and Wisconsin in the 1946–47 season. The Big Blue now compete in NCAA Division III, with men's and women's programs ranging from football, wrestling and baseball to softball, swimming and diving, and triathlon. The athletic alumni include professional football players such as George Musso and Jeff Query, college football coaches including Jeff Monken and Chuck Martin, baseball players, and Olympic swimmer Marcia Morey.

## Beyond the Quad

The university's reach extends well past the stage and the playing field. James Benton Parsons, a Millikin graduate, is described as the first African American to serve as a U.S. federal district judge, appointed in 1961 to the Northern District of Illinois in Chicago. Rickey Williams Jr. became the first African American mayor of Danville, Illinois. Douglas R. Oberhelman rose to chairman and chief executive of Caterpillar Inc., and philosopher and logician Alice Ambrose built a career in scholarship. The school remains intimate in scale, with a student-to-faculty ratio of 10 to 1 in federal data, and U.S. News & World Report's 2025 profile reported that nearly three-quarters of its classes enrolled fewer than twenty students. U.S. News ranked Millikin 14th among Best Regional Colleges in the Midwest in its 2025 edition and 15th in 2026, and its 2027 edition, released in September 2026, places Millikin fourth among Illinois colleges in that category.

## Flight Context

Millikin University's campus is at 39.8425 N, 88.9750 W on the west side of Decatur, Illinois. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for the cluster of campus buildings and athletic fields set within the city's residential grid; Lake Decatur, the reservoir along the city's south and east sides, is the easiest regional reference. Decatur Airport (KDEC) lies about 5 nautical miles east of campus and has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield is about 32 nautical miles west and University of Illinois Willard Airport (KCMI) near Champaign about 34 nautical miles east-northeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are the main visibility concerns.
