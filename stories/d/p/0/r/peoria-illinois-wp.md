# Peoria, Illinois

One of Illinois's oldest European settlements grew into a leading center of American whiskey, Caterpillar's longtime home and America's shorthand for mainstream taste, and gave the world the moldy cantaloupe that helped put penicillin into mass production.

## Introduction

In the early 1940s, a moldy cantaloupe turned up in a Peoria grocery store. The mold on it, a Penicillium strain known as NRRL 1951 (long labeled Penicillium chrysogenum, now classed as Penicillium rubens), became the parent of the strains used to produce penicillin commercially. Peoria's federal agricultural laboratory, now the National Center for Agricultural Utilization Research, was one of the places where mass production of the drug was developed. It is a fitting story for a city so often cast as the archetype of middle America: behind the stereotype, Peoria keeps turning out surprises.

## Among the Oldest in Illinois

French explorers came up the Illinois River from the Mississippi, and in 1680 René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, and Henri de Tonti built Fort Crevecoeur on lands that would become Peoria. Tonti established the settlement itself in 1691, making Peoria one of the earliest European settlements in Illinois; Cahokia, founded in 1699, is usually called the oldest permanent one. Originally known as Fort Clark, it took its current name when Peoria County was organized in 1825, honoring the Peoria people of the Illinois Confederation. What the word first meant is uncertain; one 21st-century proposal traces it to a Proto-Algonquian word meaning "to dream with the help of a manitou." Peoria became a village in 1835 and a city in 1845. Nine years later, on October 16, 1854, Abraham Lincoln stood in Peoria to deliver a speech against the Kansas-Nebraska Act.

## Whiskey Capital

Industry began modestly, with a flour mill built on Kickapoo Creek in 1830 by John Hamlin and John Sharp. Then came distilling. Before Prohibition, Peoria was a major center of the American whiskey industry, and by the end of the 19th century more than a dozen distilleries operated here. By local accounts, at the peak Peoria's tax collection district supplied nearly half of the federal government's revenue, almost all of it from the tax on spirits, and the money fed a building boom of fine private homes, schools, parks, churches and municipal buildings. Prohibition brought a different trade: Peoria became one of the major bootlegging areas of the era and home to the Shelton brothers, the famed mobsters. For much of the 20th century the city also hosted a red-light district of brothels and bars known as the Merry-Go-Round.

## Will It Play in Peoria?

In 1925 the Caterpillar Tractor Co. was formed from two California companies, and for generations Peoria was its home; the company kept its global headquarters here until the late 2010s, when it moved to Deerfield, Illinois, before relocating again to Irving, Texas, in 2022. Earthmoving machinery made Peoria a world leader in the field, and Robert G. LeTourneau's earthmoving company, which began producing new scrapers and dozers in 1935, evolved into what is now Komatsu's off-highway truck operation. Health care has since grown into a pillar of the economy, and OSF HealthCare, headquartered in Peoria since its founding in 1877, became the city's top employer in 2021. Through it all Peoria became shorthand for middle America. The phrase "Will it play in Peoria?" may date to the vaudeville era and is often spuriously attributed to Groucho Marx. The city is even the setting for the first level of the arcade game Rampage.

## Legends Under Scrutiny

Peoria enjoys a good story, and not all of them survive a close look. Charles Lindbergh's first air mail route, Contract Air Mail route #2, began carrying mail from Chicago to Peoria, Springfield and St. Louis on April 15, 1926. Nothing supports the local legend that he offered Peoria the chance to sponsor his transatlantic flight in a plane called the "Spirit of Peoria," but he did say he first pondered the journey after taking off from the Peoria air mail field. Theodore Roosevelt supposedly called Grandview Drive, on the bluffs above the river, "the world's most beautiful drive" in 1910, yet no account of the remark appeared for more than two decades. Other stories are simply true. Cyclist Marshall "Major" Taylor, who became world champion, raced at Lake View Park and called Peoria the "Mecca" of his sport, and Richard Pryor got his start performing on North Washington Street in the early 1960s.

## A City Rewriting Its Script

Peoria counted 113,150 residents in 2020, the eighth-most populous city in Illinois, anchoring a metro area of 402,391. It has faced hard numbers. Its poverty rate stood at 19.7 percent in 2021, and in 2018 a 24/7 Wall St. ranking placed it fifth-worst among U.S. cities for Black Americans, based on gaps in income, education and employment. In 2021, Rita Ali became Peoria's first female and first African American mayor. That same year it had been named one of the fastest-shrinking cities, but later in the 2020s it drew attention for a rarer trend in midsized Midwestern cities, gentrification. One TikTok user, Angie Ostazewski, was profiled in the New York Times after being credited with persuading upwards of 300 people to move to Peoria for its dining, arts and affordable historic homes. Some traditions endure: the Peoria Santa Claus Parade, begun in 1888, is billed as the oldest running holiday parade in the United States.

## Flight Context

Downtown Peoria lies near 40.6925 N, 89.5900 W on the west bank of the Illinois River in Peoria County, Illinois. From 2,500-4,000 feet AGL, the river and its bridges make the city easy to read: Interstate 74 crosses on the Murray Baker Bridge, U.S. 150 on the McClugage Bridge's twin steel trusses, Illinois 40 on the Bob Michel Bridge and I-474 on the Shade-Lohmann Bridge to the south. Grandview Drive traces the bluffs above the river, and East Peoria's Levee District faces downtown across the water. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA, Class C) is about 5 nautical miles to the west-southwest, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control, and Mount Hawley Auxiliary Airport serves general aviation on the city's north end. Expect river valley fog on cool mornings and summer thunderstorms.
