# The Pumpkin Crown

The title "Pumpkin Capital of the World" was once claimed by nearby Eureka; today it belongs to Morton, a Tazewell County village built on drainage tile, tractors and a Libby's cannery that throws itself a four-day pumpkin festival every September.

**Location:** 40.6117, -89.4633

**Categories:** Villages in Tazewell County, Illinois, 1830s establishments in Illinois, Populated places established in the 1830s, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Long before Morton wore the crown, the nearby town of Eureka laid claim to the title of "Pumpkin Capital of the World," on the strength of a canning factory founded there in 1895. Then, by the end of the 1950s, the Libby Corporation closed its Eureka plant and consolidated its operations in Morton, where they have remained ever since. In 1978, Illinois Governor James Thompson made the change official and declared this Tazewell County village the Pumpkin Capital of the World. Morton, home to 17,117 people at the 2020 census, had already been celebrating for more than a decade. Every September since 1967 it has given the pumpkin four days of its undivided attention.

## Corn, Peas and Pumpkin

The cannery is older than its fame. The Dickinson Canning Company built it in 1925, and the plant produced canned sweet corn and peas as well as pumpkin. In the 1970s Nestlé bought it and kept the Libby's brand name. A 2007 profile put the crop at approximately 5,000 acres of Dickinson Select pumpkins planted each year exclusively for the Libby's brand. How much of the world's canned pumpkin passes through Morton depends on who is counting. The village's own tourism page says its Libby's plant supplies over 85 percent of the world's canned pumpkin market, while a bolder local claim holds that "99 percent of the world's canned pumpkin is produced in Morton." An Associated Press report put the plant at an estimated 80 to 85 percent of the canned pumpkin eaten in the United States. By any of those counts, most of the canned pumpkin on American tables passes through one village of about 13 square miles.

## Four Days in September

The Morton Chamber of Commerce organized the first Morton Pumpkin Festival in 1967, as a fundraiser and a celebration of the beginning of the pumpkin harvest and canning season at the local Libby's plant. The festival now welcomes an estimated 75,000 visitors, and more than 1,500 volunteers host and organize its 30-plus special events and venues. Libby's became the official sponsor in 2012. Each year brings a theme, voted on by the public in the annual Pumpkin Festival Survey and settled by the Pumpkin Festival Oversight Committee and the chamber's board, and the parades, pageants and opening ceremony dress up to match. Pumpkins have gone Prehistoric (1998), Under the Sea (2004) and Superhero (2008). In 2015 they became Pumpkins of the Caribbean, and in 2016 the festival marked its half-century as the Golden Pumpkin: A Celebration of 50 Years. When the COVID-19 pandemic scrapped many in-person gatherings in 2020, Morton held its festival virtually.

## Tile, Pottery and Tractors

According to the village, Morton's first settlers came from England, Ohio and New York in the mid-1800s, with Swiss and German settlers arriving later in the century. One of the most important early companies began in 1877 as the Rapp Brick & Tiles Works, where the family made bricks and drainage tiles. The fertile land those tiles revealed, the village says, was a valuable discovery that still benefits Morton today. To meet the demand for hard-to-find mixing bowls and utensils came Rapp Brothers Pottery Works, later called Morton Pottery Works, whose signature pieces are now collectors' items at auction. Caterpillar arrived in the 1950s and helped draw Interstate 74 to its current location. Today Morton is home to a Caterpillar distribution facility and the corporate headquarters of Morton Buildings, Inc. Its economy has been shaped by four nearby interstates (I-74, I-474, I-155 and I-55), a central location between Chicago, St. Louis and Indianapolis, Caterpillar's manufacturing plants in nearby Peoria and the surrounding farmland. So many major employers have gathered here that Morton is a net importer of workers.

## Pie, Politics and Disc Golf

For its size, Morton keeps busy. The village has four disc golf courses. Morton Community Unit School District 709 runs one high school, a junior high for grades seven and eight and four primary schools for kindergarten through sixth grade, and Blessed Sacrament and Bethel Lutheran teach preschool through eighth grade. Its list of notable people mixes pumpkins and power. Jay Ackerman was an Illinois state representative and a pumpkin farmer. David Burritt became CEO of U.S. Steel, Dave Kindred wrote about sports, and Derek Grimm and J. R. Koch played college basketball for Missouri and Iowa. David E. Lilienthal led both the Tennessee Valley Authority and the Atomic Energy Commission. Pumpkins have even reached the statute books. State Representative Keith Sommer of Morton co-sponsored a bill to make pumpkin pie the official State Pie of Illinois, and in 2015 a law naming pumpkin pie the State Pie passed the legislature and was signed by the governor. The state says the designation honors a pumpkin industry that is prevalent in the Morton area.

## Flight Context

Morton lies at about 40.61°N, 89.46°W in Tazewell County, at an elevation of roughly 712 feet. From 2,000 to 3,500 feet above ground, Interstate 74, which the village says Caterpillar helped draw to its present route, is the easiest line to follow toward the village, with farmland on every side. General Downing - Peoria International Airport (KPIA, Class C) is about 11 nautical miles to the west-northwest, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control, and Central Illinois Regional Airport at Bloomington-Normal (KBMI) is about 26 nautical miles to the east-southeast. Expect haze and thunderstorms in central Illinois summers; the Pumpkin Festival falls in mid-September, at the start of the pumpkin harvest and canning season.
