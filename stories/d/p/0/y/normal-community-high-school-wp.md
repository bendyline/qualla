# Normal Community High School

Founded in 1905 and nicknamed the Ironmen after a 1934 football game, Normal Community High School grew from a 14-classroom building into a 2,000-student campus on the northeast edge of Normal.

**Location:** 40.5333, -88.9145

**Categories:** Public high schools in Illinois, Schools in Bloomington–Normal, Educational institutions established in 1905, 1905 establishments in Illinois, Normal, Illinois

## Introduction

In 1934, Normal's high school football team took the field against Bloomington High School, and when it was over, a local reporter called the Normal players a team of ironmen. The name stuck. Nearly a century later, Normal Community High School's teams still play as the Ironmen, and the school behind them has grown from a fourteen-classroom building into a campus of more than 2,000 students in grades nine through twelve, with 150 faculty and staff serving parts of Normal, Bloomington and Towanda.

## Fourteen Classrooms and a Growing Town

Normal Community High School was founded in 1905. An early school building went up in 1927 with fourteen classrooms, a gymnasium, a library and administrative offices, and its average enrollment was about 350 students. By 1954 the school had outgrown it, and two additions followed. The north end gained a cafeteria and classrooms for music and speech. The south end got a new gymnasium and a row of hands-on workshops: physics, agriculture, metals, woodworking and electronics labs. Wing by wing, that old site would serve Normal Community for more than three-quarters of a century, until the school moved to a new campus in 2003.

## Growing Pains

In 1967 the community's growth forced yet another expansion. This one brought another new gymnasium, named Neuman, along with a new cafeteria and library, biology and chemistry labs, an automotive lab, more classrooms and office space for the Unit 5 superintendent. After the addition, average enrollment topped 1,350, and in 1974 it peaked at 1,956 students. Relief came when Parkside Junior High School opened and ninth graders moved to Chiddix and Parkside. The pressure returned in the 1980s and 1990s, when the Unit 5 district grew explosively. In 1995 a second high school, Normal Community West, opened, and some students who would have attended NCHS were sent there instead. The two schools have been rivals, on the field and off, ever since.

## A New Home on Raab Road

On March 21, 2000, voters passed a Unit 5 referendum approving a spending budget of more than $73 million, with the State of Illinois covering nearly $18 million of the new construction and renovation. The main result was an entirely new campus for Normal Community on Raab Road, northeast of Normal, which opened in August 2003 for the new school year. The old building did not go to waste. Part of it was remodeled into Kingsley Junior High School; the original 1927 section was torn down, and the 1954 and 1967 wings were completely updated. The new campus had a frightening day on September 7, 2012, when a student fired gunshots into a classroom ceiling. A teacher tackled the student, and nobody was injured.

## Ironmen, On and Off the Field

Today the Ironmen compete in the Big Twelve Conference, which they joined in 1983, alongside crosstown rivals Normal West and Bloomington High School. They left the Corn Belt Conference in 1971. In the classroom, several departments offer Advanced Placement courses, and honors sections cover subjects from biology and chemistry to geometry and English. According to the Illinois State Board of Education, 60 percent of the class of 2012 was ready for college coursework, against a 46 percent state average. The school's alumni include football players Omar Clayton, Marcellus Johnson and Mike Wells, and, by the school's Wikipedia alumni list, journalist Tom Ashbrook, actor John D. LeMay and Mudvayne bassist Ryan Martinie.

## Flight Context

Normal Community High School's Raab Road campus is at 40.5333°N, 88.9145°W, on the northeast side of Normal. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for a large school complex with its athletic fields at the northeastern edge of the Bloomington-Normal urban area; the small village of Towanda lies about 2 nautical miles to the north-northeast. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) is only about 3 nautical miles to the south and has a control tower (staffed 0600 to 2200 local), so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control when sightseeing here. The terrain is flat, and visibility is generally good outside summer haze and winter overcast.
