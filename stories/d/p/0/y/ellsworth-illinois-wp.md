# Ellsworth, Illinois

Ellsworth was sold to the public in 1871 with a free train ride, a free lunch and free beer, and the village that grew up on either side of the railroad soon drew the merchants out of a neighboring settlement.

**Location:** 40.4494, -88.7161

**Categories:** Villages in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1871, 1871 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Free train. Free lunch. Free beer. In November 1871 an advertisement in the Bloomington Pantagraph invited anyone interested to gather at the Illinois Central depot in Bloomington at nine in the morning on November 8, where a train would carry them, at no charge, to a town that barely existed yet. Waiting at the site were 150 lots up for auction, a free meal, and beer supplied by the Bloomington brewers Meyer and Wochner. Some two hundred people made the trip. They went through 200 loaves of bread and 150 pounds of boiled ham, along with cheese and a barrel of crackers, and by the end of the day Ellsworth had its first property owners.

## Selling the Prairie

The advertisement pitched Ellsworth with care. It praised the fertility of the surrounding countryside and stressed that the local farms were of moderate size. The new town, readers were told, would be the logical place to ship grain, and grain shipped from Ellsworth would travel by way of Toledo, Ohio, "avoiding the grain scalpers of Chicago." It was a pitch aimed squarely at the farmers who would be hauling that grain, and it worked. A. J. Wampler bought the first lot, and most lots sold for between forty and sixty-five dollars. Wampler would go on to become the town's first postmaster.

## Railroad Men

Ellsworth was laid out by Jonathan H. Cheney, Almon Brigham Ives and Oliver Ellsworth, whose name the town took. Cheney was a son of the early McLean County settler Jonathan Cheney and, by the 1879 county history's account, a leading promoter of the Lafayette, Bloomington and Mississippi Railroad. A relative, state senator W. H. Cheney, is said to have helped arrange the railroad's charter. Ives was a Bloomington lawyer who specialized in land law. Both Cheney and Ives sat on the new railroad's board of directors, so town and railroad were bound together from the start. Construction on the line had begun in 1869. In 1876 it was leased to the Lake Erie and Western Railroad, and in 1879 it became part of that road.

## A Town Cut in Two

The townsite took in 40 acres on each side of the tracks, and its plan was almost identical to that of its neighbor, Arrowsmith. A 200-foot swath of railroad land cut Ellsworth into two nearly equal halves. Most of the early commercial buildings went up along Main Street on the south side of the tracks, where the depot also stood, while the first grain elevator rose on the north side. Regular freight service began in 1872, and grain was soon being bought and shipped. The founders donated "a good lot" for a church, and many new houses went up.

## The Town That Swallowed Stumptown

Ellsworth drew merchants from all around. John Campbell moved his store in from Stumptown, about four miles to the south, and Stumptown was soon abandoned entirely. Edwards and Wampler brought their stock over from a store in rural Blue Mound Township and were soon reported to be doing a thriving trade. Henry Vandervoort and Joseph Pierce opened a hardware store in 1873, and August Flint ran a wagon shop. After a brief boom the population passed 200, but growth soon slowed. Ellsworth remains a small place, a village in Dawson Township covering about a fifth of a square mile, with 271 residents in 2000 and 184 at the 2020 census.

## Flight Context

Ellsworth sits at 40.4494°N, 88.7161°W in Dawson Township, eastern McLean County, about 13 nautical miles east of Bloomington and 6 nautical miles north-northeast of Le Roy. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the small village grid, laid out on either side of its railroad corridor, set among flat farm fields; Arrowsmith lies about 4 nautical miles due east. The Twin Groves Wind Farm spreads across Dawson and neighboring Arrowsmith and Cheney's Grove townships, so expect wind turbines in the area and plan altitude accordingly. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal is about 9 nautical miles to the west and has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control if you fly that way. The terrain is flat, and visibility is usually good outside summer haze or winter low overcast.
