# Central Catholic High School (Bloomington, Illinois)

From a first graduating class said to number just two to a new campus beside Bloomington's airport, Central Catholic has carried a parish school's traditions through three names, two buildings and state football titles in four different classes.

**Location:** 40.4931, -88.9228

**Categories:** Schools in Bloomington–Normal, Diocese of Peoria, Catholic secondary schools in Illinois, Educational institutions established in 1886, 1886 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Visiting teams dreaded "The Pit." By the 1970s, that was the nickname for Central Catholic's old gymnasium on Center Street in Bloomington, a place known for its stifling heat, pipes that banged with loud water hammer, and stands pressed so close to the floor that opponents felt the crowd breathing down their necks. The building is gone now, torn down after the school moved out in 2003. But the school that filled it has kept going for well over a century, and its story begins with a first graduating class said to number just two.

## Two Graduates and a Parish Dream

Central Catholic's roots reach back to St. Mary's School, built by Holy Trinity Parish in 1884 during the pastorate of Michael Weldon. It started as a grade school only; in 1886 the building was altered to add high school classes. The school's own history dates its first graduates to 1889, while another account puts the first graduating class, of exactly two students, in 1898. The parish paid off the school's building debt in 1909, and in 1912 both Illinois State University and the University of Illinois recognized St. Mary's high school curriculum. That spring's graduating class had grown to twenty. Sinsinawa Dominican sisters taught at St. Mary's, and they would stay on through the school's next chapter. By the school's count, St. Mary's High School had sent 406 graduates into the world by 1928.

## Trinity on Center Street

In the summer of 1922, the parish bought land for a separate high school building from the Dominican sisters for $25,000, a site next to Holy Trinity Church at 712 North Center Street. The cornerstone was laid on September 25, 1927, and the new Trinity High School earned accreditation from the North Central Association in 1929. Trinity graduated 2,482 students between 1929 and 1967. When the diocese asked other parishes in the region to join in governing and paying for the school, it became a diocesan institution and took the name it carries today: Central Catholic High School. Today it is one of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Peoria's high schools and the only Catholic high school in McLean County.

## A New Campus by the Runways

In 1996, church officials began looking for a site for a new school building. They first considered the old Chicago & Alton railroad yards on Bloomington's west side, but rejected them because of the extensive environmental cleanup required. In 1998 the pastors' board reserved fifteen acres of Deneen family farmland in east Bloomington, land that was gifted to the diocese, and an $11 million fundraising campaign began in 2001. Construction started on August 12, 2002. When renovating the old building proved too expensive, the parish decided to demolish it. The new school at 1201 Airport Road opened on August 25, 2003, with 323 students, and within two years the Center Street building, Pit and all, came down. Bill Hundman Memorial Field was completed in 2005.

## Titles in Four Classes

Sport runs deep here: more than eighty percent of students play on a school team. Before the Illinois High School Association allowed private schools to take part in 1941, the boys' basketball team won State Catholic Tournament championships, in 1927 and 1928 under coach Bennett, in 1933 under John Callans, and in 1940 and 1941 under Essau Dotlich. On the football field, Central Catholic won IHSA state titles in four different classes, 2A in 1982, 1A in 1987, 3A in 1994 and 4A in 2008, and is described as the first Illinois school to do so. Among its alumni is Michael Hoomanawanui, a former NFL tight end and a Super Bowl XLIX champion, and John Rave, an outfielder in the Kansas City Royals organization.

## Can Sculptures and Blue Ribbons

Some traditions are pure fun. Homecoming brings a can sculpture competition and a car caravan, and during National Catholic Schools Week, student teams face off in a school-wide Olympic-style tournament before the week closes with a Mass shared with students from the area's Catholic elementary schools. Prom has been held in spring at the Miller Park pavilion. Other milestones are more formal. In 2017 Central Catholic was named a National Blue Ribbon School, one of 342 honored nationwide. By 2014 the school, under its three names, had graduated 6,556 students, and its listed alumni include Bill Brady, who became minority leader of the Illinois Senate, and Dan Brady, a former state representative who became mayor of Bloomington in 2025.

## Flight Context

Central Catholic stands at 40.4931°N, 88.9228°W, at 1201 Airport Road on the east side of Bloomington, bordered by Airport Road on the west, Cornelius Drive on the south, and McGraw Park on the north and east. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) lies only about a nautical mile to the south-southeast, so the campus is best seen on approach or departure from KBMI rather than from a dedicated orbit. KBMI has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Look for the large two-wing school building, which encloses more than 100,000 square feet, beside its athletic field and the open green of McGraw Park. The surrounding terrain is flat, and the Bloomington-Normal urban area spreads to the west.
