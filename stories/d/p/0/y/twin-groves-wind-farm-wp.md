# Twin Groves Wind Farm

Two hundred and forty turbines spread across 22,000 acres east of Bloomington made Twin Groves the largest utility-scale wind farm east of the Mississippi River when it was completed in 2008, though the machines occupy only about 1 percent of the land.

**Location:** 40.4817, -88.7072

**Categories:** Energy infrastructure completed in 2008, Buildings and structures in McLean County, Illinois, Wind farms in Illinois

## Introduction

Twin Groves Wind Farm spreads across 22,000 acres of McLean County, yet its turbines occupy only a sliver of that ground: between 150 and 200 acres, about 1 percent of the whole. Nearly everything else about the place is big. There are 240 turbines, each on a tower more than 250 feet tall and each turning three blades longer than 100 feet, spread east of Bloomington along Illinois Route 9 near the villages of Arrowsmith, Saybrook and Ellsworth. When construction wrapped up in February 2008, Twin Groves was the largest utility-scale wind farm east of the Mississippi River.

## Towers on the Moraines

Horizon Wind Energy, which owned and operated Twin Groves in its early years, described the first half of the project as sitting "on two prominent moraines in eastern McLean County," ridges left behind by glaciers. The farm begins just east of Bloomington and sits along Illinois Route 9, and the towers can be seen from both Route 9 and McLean County Route 21. Every turbine is a Vestas machine rated at 1.65 megawatts. A wind farm of this size also needs its own working infrastructure, and Twin Groves has gravel roads out to the towers, a switchyard and a control building. Today the turbines, roads and buildings are owned and operated by EDP Renewables North America.

## One Farm, Built in Halves

Twin Groves went up in two equal halves. The first, Twin Groves I, brought 120 Vestas V82 turbines into commercial operation in March 2007, with an installed capacity of 198 megawatts that Horizon said could power approximately 60,000 Illinois homes. Twin Groves II added another 198 megawatts. In federal electricity data the halves go by other names, High Trail Wind for the first and Old Trail Wind for the second. Together they make 240 turbines and 396 megawatts, built at an estimated cost of approximately $700 million. That scale brought a regional distinction. Upon completion, Twin Groves was the largest utility-scale wind farm east of the Mississippi, and in February 2009 Peoria Magazine was still describing it as the largest. The title did not last long: Indiana's Fowler Ridge, at 400 megawatts, became fully operational in March 2009.

## What the Wind Pays

For the people who own the land, royalties of around $5,000 to $6,000 per turbine were typical, Peoria Magazine reported in 2009, and EDP Renewables says it had paid landowners $36.9 million through 2025. Local schools were in line for a share as well. In 2007, the Ridgeview School District was set to start collecting $300,000 a year in property taxes from the wind farm, while the Tri-Valley School District expected between $200,000 and $250,000 a year. That same year, the owner was weighing whether to go bigger. Plans under consideration called for 170 more turbines, which would bring the total to 410 and lift the farm's capacity from 396 to 680 megawatts, an increase of more than two-thirds. Test wind turbines were placed in eastern McLean County to determine whether the area could support them. Twin Groves is still counted at 240 turbines.

## Eleven Years on the Meter

Early projections put the farm's output at about 1.3 billion kilowatt-hours a year, enough for roughly 120,000 homes. Twin Groves I, in commercial operation from March, produced 324,962 megawatt-hours on its own in 2007. Federal records for 2008 through 2018, the years when both halves reported, show an average of 1,162,480 megawatt-hours a year, or about 1.16 billion kilowatt-hours, somewhat below the early estimate. Output proved remarkably consistent. The strongest year was 2008, at 1,215,011 megawatt-hours, and the weakest was 2018, at 1,080,173. Over those eleven years the farm's average capacity factor was 33.5 percent. Put another way, the turbines delivered about a third of what they could have produced had every one of them spun at full rated power through every hour of every year. Everything depends on the wind that crosses eastern McLean County, and from 2008 through 2018 it added up to more than 12.7 billion kilowatt-hours.

## Flight Context

Twin Groves Wind Farm is centered near 40.4817°N, 88.7072°W in eastern McLean County, with 240 turbines spread across about 22,000 acres around Arrowsmith, Saybrook and Ellsworth along Illinois Route 9. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI), at 40.4771°N, 88.9159°W, lies about 9.5 nm west of that center point, and Route 9 leads east from the Bloomington airport area toward the array. The towers alone exceed 250 feet and the blade tips reach higher, so treat the whole area as a field of tall obstructions; 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL gives a good overview of the layout. KBMI's tower (124.6, part-time 0600-2200; CTAF 124.6 when closed) is the nearest controlled field. Other airports nearby include Pontiac Municipal (KPNT), 30 nm northeast of KBMI, and Rantoul National Aviation Center (KTIP), 37 nm east of KBMI. Watch for haze and low ceilings when picking out individual towers.
