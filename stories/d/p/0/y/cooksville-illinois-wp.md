# Cooksville, Illinois

Laid out in 1882 as Kochsville by a Westphalian-born Bloomington land dealer, tiny Cooksville grew up around a railroad whose tracks were eventually pulled up.

**Location:** 40.5422, -88.7164

**Categories:** Villages in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1882, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

The name on the original plat was Kochsville. Frederick Wilhelm Koch, a Bloomington real estate dealer, laid out the town on December 4, 1882, and gave it his own name. Within a year it had become Cooksville, the name it still carries. That was not even the only place in McLean County named for him. Today the village is home to 157 people (2020 census), a small grid in the farm country of eastern McLean County with a founding story that stretches from Westphalia to the Illinois prairie.

## One Man, Two Namesakes

Koch was born in Westphalia, in what is now Germany, and arrived in the United States on November 6, 1854. By 1860 he was living in Bloomington, where he went into real estate. He sold thirty or forty lots near his own home on the west side of the city, and that neighborhood soon became known as Kochsville as well. The result was an unusual distinction: two McLean County places named in his honor, one a Bloomington neighborhood and the other a railroad town about fifteen miles to the east-northeast. The spelling of the second changed almost immediately, but the town has kept his name ever since.

## Waiting on the Rails

Cooksville owes its existence to a railroad that arrived in two installments. In 1880 the line from Kankakee reached Colfax, and the towns of Cropsey, Anchor and Colfax were founded along it. The rest of the route, from Colfax on to Bloomington, was delayed for two years. When it was finally finished, Cooksville was laid out along the new track. The line eventually came under Illinois Central control and was sometimes known as the Bloomer Line. Grain came first: in 1883, the year after the town was platted, William G. Darnell established its first grain elevator.

## Fifty-Five Lots on a Slant

The original town was a rectangle on the north side of the tracks, about 55 lots in all. Because the railroad did not run exactly east and west, its southern blocks were slightly truncated where they met the angled right-of-way. The depot stood on the north side, and the two early grain elevators on the south. Small additions soon spread south of the tracks and along the east side of town. Harvey W. Crumbacker moved to Cooksville in 1883 and opened a hardware store, and in 1902 he put up a two-story brick building in town. Cooksville incorporated as a village in December 1901, and its population peaked in 1910 at 332 people.

## When the Rails Came Up

The railroad that created Cooksville did not last forever. By local accounts, when the town's grain elevators asked to join Alliance Grain, the operator of the Bloomer Line, their requests were denied, and the tracks between Colfax and Cooksville were removed. From then on, road transportation was the only way to move grain out of town. The village has stayed small, covering just over a fifth of a square mile, with 213 residents in 2000 and 157 in 2020. Its children attend schools in the Ridgeview School District. Today Illinois Route 165, which touches the village's northwest corner, is its link to Bloomington.

## Flight Context

Cooksville lies at 40.5422°N, 88.7164°W in eastern McLean County, about 13 nautical miles east-northeast of Bloomington. Illinois Route 165 touches the northwest part of the village and is the best line to follow, running west-southwest toward Bloomington and east-northeast past Colfax, which is roughly 5 nautical miles away, toward Sibley. The village is tiny, just 0.22 square miles, so drop to about 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL to pick out its small grid amid open farm fields. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal is about 10 nautical miles to the west-southwest and has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control if you fly that way. Pontiac Municipal Airport (KPNT) is about 23 nautical miles to the north. The surrounding prairie is flat and unobstructed.
