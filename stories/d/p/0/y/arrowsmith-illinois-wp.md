# Arrowsmith, Illinois

Laid out in 1871 by two Bloomington land dealers who kept their names secret for two years, Arrowsmith grew up around a railroad its township paid to attract, and, by one local account, one of its first stores arrived dragged across the prairie by 32 horses.

**Location:** 40.4489, -88.6317

**Categories:** Villages in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1871, 1871 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

By one local account, thirty-two horses leaned into their harnesses as a merchant named Thompson began moving his store. Taking advantage of cold weather, he skidded the entire building from the old settlement of Senex, about three miles to the southwest near the edge of Old Town Timber, to the brand-new town of Arrowsmith, and the trip took just over two hours. In the same telling, the Senex blacksmith shop and post office soon followed. It was an emphatic vote of confidence in a town that, at the time, officially had no owners anyone could name.

## The Proprietors Who Stayed Anonymous

Arrowsmith was laid out on December 7, 1871, by Charles W. Holder and Owen T. Reeves, but for two years nobody outside the deal knew it. The founders kept their identities hidden until the plat was officially recorded. Both men lived in Bloomington, both were experienced land dealers, and both had a hand in creating the Lafayette, Bloomington and Muncie Railroad, the line their new town was built to serve. Holder had already been Jesse Fell's partner in founding Towanda and one of his partners in developing Normal, and a McLean County town called Holder would soon carry his name. Reeves was a Bloomington lawyer and judge who, during the Civil War, had been colonel of the short-lived 70th Illinois Infantry.

## A Station Bought and Paid For

The railroad did not simply wander through. Arrowsmith Township voted $30,000 in bonds to support its construction, on one condition: a station had to be built within the township. The town that rose around that promised stop was at first expected to be called Weldon, until word came that a newly founded town in DeWitt County had already claimed the name. So the new place took the name of its township instead, which honored the early settler Ezekiel Arrowsmith. By that account Senex contributed its store, its smithy and its post office, but the new town was not simply Senex relocated. Most of Arrowsmith's first buildings were newly built, and many of its early merchants came from somewhere else entirely.

## Sixteen Blocks and a Wide Right-of-Way

The original town was designed around a broad strip of railroad ground, with eight blocks laid out north of the tracks and eight to the south. Most of the early businesses lined Main Street on the north side, where both grain elevators and the first stockyards also stood, while the depot sat across the tracks on the south side. Grain and livestock moved out on the line that had given the town its reason to exist. In 1876 the Lafayette, Bloomington and Muncie was leased to the Lake Erie and Western Railroad, which took it over in 1879, and it is usually remembered by that later name.

## Small by Design

Arrowsmith never became a boomtown. It incorporated as a village in April 1890, and a decade later it held only 317 people. The village still covers just a fifth of a square mile, set in eastern McLean County roughly 19 miles east of Bloomington and 14 miles west of Gibson City in a straight line. The census counted 298 residents in 2000 and 276 in 2020. Arrowsmith's story still begins with the outline the anonymous proprietors drew in 1871: sixteen blocks split by a railroad right-of-way, in a township that paid good money to put itself on the map.

## Flight Context

Arrowsmith sits at 40.4489°N, 88.6317°W in eastern McLean County, about 17 nautical miles east of Bloomington. The village covers only 0.20 square miles, so look for a small, compact grid set in open, flat farmland; a viewing altitude of 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL keeps it in sight. The village of Ellsworth lies roughly 4 nautical miles due west, and Gibson City is about 12 nautical miles to the east. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal is about 13 nautical miles to the west. The terrain is flat prairie with few vertical obstructions near town, and visibility is typically limited only by haze in summer or low overcast in winter.
