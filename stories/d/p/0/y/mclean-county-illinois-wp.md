# McLean County, Illinois

Created on Christmas Day 1830, McLean County is the largest county in Illinois by land area, a patchwork of 31 townships and prairie towns wrapped around Bloomington-Normal that in 2023 out-produced every other U.S. county in both corn and soybeans.

**Location:** 40.4900, -88.8500

**Categories:** McLean County, Illinois, Illinois counties, 1830 establishments in Illinois, Populated places established in 1830

## Introduction

McLean County was born on Christmas Day. On December 25, 1830, Illinois carved it out of Tazewell County and named it for John McLean, a United States senator from Illinois who had died that same year. The first Euro-American settlers had arrived only around 1821, and the first settlement, Blooming Grove, took root in 1822 near what is now Bloomington, the county seat. Nearly two centuries later the county is home to 170,954 people (2020 census) and covers more land than any other county in the state.

## The Biggest Map in Illinois

McLean County spans about 1,186 square miles, of which some 1,183 are land and less than three are water. That makes it the largest county in Illinois by land area, and third largest by total area, behind only Cook and Lake counties. It is divided into 31 townships with names that read like a prairie roll call: Money Creek, Dry Grove, Funk's Grove, Cheney's Grove, Old Town, White Oak. Bloomington is the seat, the town of Normal is its next-door neighbor, and Illinois State University lies within the county. Around them lie the cities of Chenoa, El Paso, Le Roy and Lexington, sixteen villages from Anchor to Towanda, some two dozen unincorporated communities, and three ghost towns: Allin, Benjaminville and Kumler.

## Black Soil, Big Harvests

Out beyond the cities, the land is the story. McLean farmers have ranked at the very top of the nation: for 2023, the county was first in the United States in total corn production, with more than 70 million bushels, and first in soybeans too, at 21.6 million bushels. The weather that grows those crops swings hard across the seasons. In Bloomington, average temperatures have run from a January low of 14°F to a July high of 86°F, and average monthly precipitation ranges from 1.71 inches in February to 4.52 inches in May. Farming shaped more than the fields. George J. Mecherle, a farmer from the McLean County hamlet of Merna, founded State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Company in June 1922, aiming to sell lower-cost auto insurance to farmers and others in low-risk rural areas. The company is still headquartered in Bloomington.

## A Growing Twin-City Core

For all its farmland, McLean County is mostly an urban place now. Its population has climbed steadily, from 119,149 in 1980 to 150,433 in 2000 and 169,572 in 2010, before edging up to 170,954 in 2020. The county has grown more diverse along the way: the share of residents identifying as non-Hispanic white alone fell from about 94 percent in 1980 to under 76 percent in 2020, while the county's Asian, Black and Hispanic communities all grew. Governing all of it is a twenty-member county board elected from ten districts, two members apiece. Three districts cover everything outside Bloomington and Normal, three lie within Normal, and four within Bloomington, a map that reflects where the people now live.

## A County That Changes Its Mind

Like most of central Illinois, McLean County has historically leaned Republican. Before the twenty-first century, only Franklin D. Roosevelt in 1932 and 1936 and Lyndon Johnson in 1964, by a mere 1.2 percent, won an outright majority of its vote for the Democrats; Woodrow Wilson in 1912 and Barack Obama in 2008 carried it with narrow pluralities. Since then the county has become a genuine swing territory. It is one of only thirteen counties that voted for Obama in 2008, Mitt Romney in 2012, Donald Trump in 2016 and Joe Biden in 2020. In 2022 Governor J. B. Pritzker carried it by 1.3 percent, the first time since 1948 the county backed a Democrat for governor, and in 2024 Kamala Harris won it as well.

## Names Worth Remembering

The county has sent a remarkable range of people into public life. Adlai Stevenson II, governor of Illinois from 1949 to 1953, twice the Democratic nominee for president and later ambassador to the United Nations, is counted among its notable figures, as is Archbishop Fulton J. Sheen, host of the popular 1950s and 1960s television program Life Is Worth Living and head of the Catholic Diocese of Rochester, New York. Recording artist and television personality Bonnie Lou and musician and songwriter Pokey LaFarge are on the list, too, alongside State Farm's founder and William H. Rowe, a farmer, businessman and politician. It is a fitting mix for a county that is equal parts courthouse square, college town and cornfield.

## Flight Context

McLean County centers near 40.49°N, 88.85°W, with the Bloomington-Normal urban area at its heart; the county itself stretches across roughly 1,183 square miles of flat, intensively farmed prairie. From 4,000 to 6,000 feet AGL on a clear day, the Bloomington-Normal cityscape stands out sharply against a checkerboard of corn and soybean fields and small grain-elevator towns in every direction. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) on the east side of Bloomington-Normal is the main airport, about 3 nautical miles from the county's reference point, and it has a control tower, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control nearby; Pontiac Municipal Airport (KPNT) lies about 28 nautical miles to the north-northeast, beyond the county line. Expect summer haze and convective weather in warm months and low ceilings in winter; the terrain itself offers no obstacles beyond towers, grain elevators and the wind turbines of the eastern townships.
