# I-74 Rivalry

Forty miles of interstate separate Illinois State in Normal and Bradley in Peoria, whose athletic rivalry began with a 1905 basketball game the two schools still date differently.

**Location:** 40.6050, -89.3050

**Categories:** 1905 establishments in Illinois, Bradley Braves baseball, Bradley Braves men's basketball, Bradley Braves women's basketball, Bradley Braves softball, Bradley Braves women's volleyball, Illinois State Redbirds baseball, Illinois State Redbirds men's basketball, Illinois State Redbirds women's basketball, Illinois State Redbirds football

## Introduction

Illinois State and Bradley have been playing each other since 1905, and they still cannot agree on when that first basketball game took place. Illinois State's records put it on January 25, 1905. Bradley's list it as February 4, 1905, in Normal. The discrepancies run on through the early decades, game after game, a small reminder of just how long ago this rivalry began. Today the series between the Illinois State Redbirds and the Bradley Braves is best known as the War on 74, and is also called the I-74 Rivalry.

## Forty Miles Apart

The name comes from geography. Illinois State University in Normal and Bradley University in Peoria sit about 40 miles apart by road, 35 as the crow flies, and both are served by Interstate 74, which runs from Cincinnati through central Indiana and central Illinois before ending in Davenport, Iowa. The series is best known as "The War on 74," a name the two schools made official in September 2026 with sponsorship from Country Financial; "Battle for 74" and "I-74 Rivalry" are alternate names that never caught on as widely. They are different kinds of schools. Illinois State is a public university founded in 1857, while Bradley is a private institution founded in 1897. They have been in-state rivals since their earliest meetings, but the competition sharpened in 1970, when Illinois State moved up from the NCAA's College Division to the University Division, the forerunner of today's Division I.

## Sports Come and Go

Basketball and football both began in 1905. Football lasted until 1970, when Bradley stopped sponsoring the sport and the series was suspended. Baseball followed in 1921 and men's tennis in 1925; the tennis series ran until 2014, when Bradley dropped the program. Men's soccer had a shorter life, starting in 1987 and ending after 1994, when Illinois State stopped sponsoring the sport. In the Missouri Valley record book, through the 2024-25 season, Bradley's soccer program holds four regular-season titles, three tournament titles and seven NCAA tournament appearances; Illinois State's holds none. Baseball has been closer: Bradley holds four conference regular-season titles to Illinois State's three and five tournament titles to its two, with seven NCAA appearances to the Redbirds' four.

## The Valley Standoff

Both schools compete in the Missouri Valley Conference for men's sports. Bradley first joined in 1948, left in 1952 and rejoined in 1955. Illinois State arrived in 1980. In men's basketball the ledger is remarkably even. Between them they have won more than a dozen Valley regular-season championships, with Bradley holding a narrow edge, and they have split eight conference tournament titles four apiece. Through the 2024-25 season, Bradley leads in postseason appearances, 38 to 22, including nine NCAA tournaments and 24 NITs to Illinois State's six and fourteen. Bradley's 2020 season came with an asterisk: the Braves earned an automatic bid to the NCAA Tournament by winning their conference tournament, only for the event to be canceled because of COVID-19.

## A Redbird Advantage

The women's side of the rivalry began with volleyball in 1974, while women's college sports still ran under the Association for Intercollegiate Athletics for Women. From 1982 to 1992 both schools played in the Gateway Collegiate Athletic Conference, a women's-only league, until the Missouri Valley took on women's sports and absorbed it for 1992-93. Through the 2024-25 season, the balance tilts sharply toward Normal. In volleyball, Illinois State has won eleven Valley regular-season titles and thirteen tournament titles; Bradley has won none. The Redbirds also hold fourteen regular-season crowns in softball and eight in women's basketball, again to none for the Braves. Bradley's women have their own trophies, though: a women's basketball tournament title, two softball tournament titles and four women's golf championships, against fifteen in golf for Illinois State.

## Flight Context

The rivalry's marker sits at 40.605 N, 89.305 W on the Interstate 74 corridor in central Illinois, roughly midway between Bradley University in Peoria, about 15 nautical miles to the west-northwest, and Illinois State University in Normal, about 15 nautical miles to the east-southeast. From 3,000-4,500 feet AGL, follow I-74 across flat farmland from the Illinois River valley at Peoria to the Bloomington-Normal urban area. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) lies about 18 nautical miles to the west, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington-Normal about 19 nautical miles to the east-southeast. Peoria is Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control near KPIA. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the prairie.
