# Palmer, Illinois

A one-square-mile Christian County railroad village founded in 1869 and named for Governor John M. Palmer, a Civil War general who spent his career refusing to belong to any one party.

**Location:** 39.4575, -89.4061

**Categories:** Villages in Christian County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1873, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

"I had my own views. I was not a slave of any party." The man credited with saying that, John M. Palmer, switched political parties more than once over a long career, and in 1869, while he was governor of Illinois, a new settlement in Christian County took his name. More than 150 years later the village of Palmer is still here: one square mile of prairie town on a rail line, home to about 216 people at the 2020 census, carrying the name of one of the state's most independent-minded politicians.

## A Town for the Governor

Palmer was established in 1869 and named for then-governor John M. Palmer. The village grew quickly enough on paper to earn the markers of permanence. A post office opened on January 10, 1871, and the village was incorporated on March 30 of that year, then reincorporated under Illinois's general incorporation law in 1873. Within two years the new settlement had a post office to connect it to the wider world and a legal charter to let it govern itself, and the man it honored was still sitting in the governor's office in Springfield when the first charter was granted.

## A Man of His Own Views

John M. Palmer was born in Scott County, Kentucky, on September 13, 1817. During the Civil War he served as a Union officer, rose to major general, commanded the XIV Corps for most of the Atlanta Campaign until he resigned in August 1864, and served as military governor of Kentucky in 1865. He was the 15th governor of Illinois from 1869 to 1873, later represented Illinois in the U.S. Senate as a Democrat from 1891 to 1897, and in 1896 ran for president as the nominee of the National Democratic Party, a splinter group that opposed free silver. That ticket won about one percent of the vote. Across his career Palmer moved among the Democratic, Republican, Liberal Republican and National Democratic parties. He died in Springfield on September 25, 1900, and was buried in Carlinville.

## Rails and Route 48

The geography of Palmer is easy to read. A railroad line operated by Norfolk Southern runs through town from southwest to northeast, and Illinois Route 48 parallels it, so the village is threaded on the same line as its transportation. It covers 1.00 square mile, all of it land. At the 2020 census Palmer counted about 216 people, down from roughly 230 in 2010. It is a small, settled place, the kind of village where a founding-era name and a rail line still define the map more than any building does.

## Flight Context

Palmer sits at 39.4575 N, 89.4061 W in Christian County, Illinois, at roughly 620 feet above sea level. From 2,000-3,500 feet AGL, follow the Norfolk Southern rail line and parallel Illinois Route 48 running southwest-northeast through flat farmland; the village is a small grid on that corridor. Nearest airport: Taylorville Municipal Airport (KTAZ), about 6 nautical miles to the northeast, with a 4,001-foot asphalt runway 18/36. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) at Springfield lies about 26 nautical miles to the north-northwest. Summer haze and convective storms are common over central Illinois.
