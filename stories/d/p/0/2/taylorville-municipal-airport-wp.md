# Taylorville Municipal Airport

A city-owned general aviation field in central Illinois, active since 1947, with two paved runways and a turf strip, a code it shares with Turkmenistan, and a skydiving history that includes two fatal crashes and some very narrow escapes.

**Location:** 39.5328, -89.3256

**Categories:** Airports in Illinois, Transportation in Christian County, Illinois, Buildings and structures in Christian County, Illinois, Aviation in Illinois

## Introduction

Three letters identify this airport to the Federal Aviation Administration: TAZ. The International Air Transport Association, which assigns the codes printed on airline tickets, gave those same three letters to Daşoguz Airport in Turkmenistan, and Taylorville has no IATA code at all. It is a small irony for a field that has never needed one. Taylorville Municipal Airport, owned by the City of Taylorville and active since March 1947, is a general aviation airport through and through, a place for local flyers, flight training, maintenance and skydiving on the flat farmland of Christian County, Illinois.

## Two Paved Runways and a Grass One

The airfield has three runways, and one of them is grass. Runway 18/36 is the longest, 4,001 by 75 feet of asphalt. Runway 9R/27L is 3,501 by 60 feet, also asphalt. Beside it runs 9L/27R, a turf strip just 1,933 feet long but a generous 165 feet wide. During the COVID-19 pandemic the airport received $1.4 million through the Rebuild Illinois initiative for runway rehabilitation, money aimed specifically at repairing runway 9/27. Of that total, $1.26 million came from the state and $140,000 from local funds. The city runs its own fixed-base operator, with a terminal, self-serve fuel, a courtesy car and major airframe and powerplant repair, and several hotels and motels sit nearby.

## Twenty-Five a Day

Taylorville is not a busy airport by any big-city standard, but it is a working one. In the 12 months ending August 31, 2022, it logged 9,125 aircraft operations, an average of 25 a day. Ninety-nine percent of those were general aviation, split between 67 percent local flights and 32 percent transient visitors, with the last one percent air taxi. Sixteen aircraft were based on the field, all airplanes. It is a modest field that sits within reach of two larger neighbors: Springfield's Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport and Decatur Airport.

## When a Jump Goes Wrong

Skydiving flights have operated from Taylorville, and twice they have gone badly wrong in the air. On October 24, 2004, a Cessna P206 that had taken off from Taylorville on a jump flight reached 10,500 feet with five parachutists aboard. As they prepared to exit, one jumper's main parachute came free of its container, and the canopy was caught in the slipstream and entangled around the right main landing gear strut, blocking airflow over the elevator and rudder. The aircraft pitched down, rolled inverted and entered a flat inverted spin; the pilot fought it down to about 6,000 feet without recovering. The pilot and the remaining parachutists jumped, and the airplane crashed inverted in a field. The entangled jumper could not be freed and was killed. On August 11, 2012, a Beechcraft G18S climbed to 11,000 feet with twelve skydivers aboard. Several heard the stall warning before the airplane suddenly rolled, and all twelve got out, the last of them reporting that the airplane was inverted or partly inverted as they went through the door. The airplane came down in a residential neighborhood. The skydivers were uninjured; the pilot was killed. Investigators blamed the pilot's failure to maintain airspeed and use the appropriate flap setting, and found that allowing more than four jumpers into the door area, against company guidance, had shifted the center of gravity aft.

## The Last Landing of the Week

Some Taylorville stories end with nothing worse than a scare. On June 12, a Beechcraft Baron was on the final landing of the final day of a four-day training course, flown by a student preparing for a multi-engine practical exam. The instructor had set up a simulated engine failure, which the student handled correctly, then pulled the other engine to idle as well. About three miles out, the instructor tried to restart the feathered engine and believed it had caught when its propeller began turning. On the straight-in approach, the instructor noticed they were lined up on a taxiway. Full power, gear up, and then a left turn the student could not control: the Baron "barely cleared a hangar," crossed the highway, passed under power lines and landed in a cornfield. Neither pilot was hurt.

## Flight Context

Taylorville Municipal Airport (KTAZ) is at 39.5328 N, 89.3256 W, field elevation about 622 feet MSL, on the edge of Taylorville in Christian County, Illinois. Runways: 18/36, 4,001 x 75 ft asphalt; 9R/27L, 3,501 x 60 ft asphalt; and 9L/27R, 1,933 x 165 ft turf. Self-serve fuel, a terminal and a courtesy car are available from the city-run FBO. Skydiving operations have been conducted here, so listen for jump-aircraft calls and watch for parachutists. For a scenic pass, 2,000-3,000 feet AGL shows the airport against the surrounding cropland and the town beside it. Nearby airports: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 25 nm northwest at Springfield, and Decatur Airport (KDEC), about 28 nm northeast. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Illinois.
