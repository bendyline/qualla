# Clayville, Illinois

A vanished frontier hamlet west of Springfield whose brick tavern, dated to 1824 or 1834 depending on the source and widely called the oldest brick building in Sangamon County, still stands on its original foundation, rescued twice by its neighbors.

**Location:** 39.8667, -89.8969

**Categories:** Ghost towns in Illinois, National Register of Historic Places in Sangamon County, Illinois, Tourist attractions in Sangamon County, Illinois

## Introduction

On the Illinois frontier, a glass window was a kind of alarm clock. At the Broadwell Tavern, the fireplaces kept the brick rooms warm, but its glass windows helped coax guests out of bed early so they would get back on the road. The tavern still stands on its original foundation west of Springfield, the last real trace of Clayville, a roadside hamlet that was never large, was inhabited from 1824 into the 1850s, and then all but disappeared.

## A Bet on Springfield

The Broadwell family settled here in 1820 and built a home and traveler's inn by 1824. The surviving brick tavern is usually dated to 1824 as well, though the Illinois State Historical Society's roadside marker says John Broadwell built it in the spring of 1834, the one building left of several raised around 1824. John, an innkeeper and land developer, saw the tavern as an investment. He sensed that nearby Springfield, then the county seat, would grow, and that the people living there would need to travel in and out. He placed his bet on the main road between Springfield and Beardstown, the Illinois River port to the west. The wager proved sound. In 1837 the General Assembly voted to move the state capital itself to Springfield, and the road past Broadwell's door became a route between a capital city and a river port. A small settlement gathered around the tavern, firmly centered on it, and for a generation Clayville was a working stop on that main road.

## Beds, Barns and a Liquor License

A frontier tavern in the 1830s was less a bar than a logistics hub. Teamsters driving slow, horse-drawn drays needed somewhere to stop for the night where their horses could be fed and watered, and Illinois law made that service a condition of a tavern's license to sell liquor by the drink. Hospitality at the Broadwell Tavern was never luxurious. It was, however, solid. Broadwell built in a vernacular Federal style, similar to the country inns of England, and he built in brick. The result is now widely described as the oldest brick building in Sangamon County; the state marker calls it one of the first.

## Named for Henry Clay

The community took the name Clayville in 1842, in honor of Henry Clay, the Kentucky statesman and Whig presidential candidate. The tavern was tied to local Whig Party activity in those years, a gathering place for a party whose hero gave the hamlet its name. It served as a stagecoach stop into the late 1840s. The hamlet did not outlast it by much; by the 1850s, Clayville's years as an inhabited settlement were over. It became what it is today: a ghost town with one building left standing to explain it.

## Saved, and Saved Again

The tavern has been rescued more than once. Dr. Emmett Pearson and his wife, Mary, began restoring Clayville in 1961, and in May 1973 the tavern was added to the National Register of Historic Places. Later the building fell into abandonment. Preservation efforts led by neighbors in nearby Pleasant Plains resumed in 2009, and in 2010 a purpose-organized nonprofit, Clayville Historic Site, closely affiliated with the Pleasant Plains Historical Society, acquired the tavern and adjacent land. It restored the exterior and partly restored the interior to the way it looked in operation in the 1840s. As of 2015 the site hosted a spring festival, a summer folk music gathering, a fall festival, car cruise-ins and a haunted house festival, and its September 2024 Fall Festival marked the Broadwell Inn's 200th anniversary, bringing people back to the ghost townsite for reasons John Broadwell could never have imagined.

## Flight Context

Clayville lies at 39.8667 N, 89.8969 W in Cartwright Township, Sangamon County, just east of the village of Pleasant Plains and roughly 12 nautical miles west-northwest of downtown Springfield. From 1,500-3,000 feet AGL, look for a lone historic brick building and its grounds in open farmland outside Pleasant Plains; the town's grid is the nearest landmark. Springfield lies to the east-southeast. Nearest major airport: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 10 nautical miles to the east. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Illinois.
