# Illinois Governor's Mansion

Completed in 1855 a few blocks from Lincoln's home, Springfield's Italianate Governor's Mansion is one of the oldest governor's residences in the country, a public museum with a private apartment upstairs that governors are free to ignore.

**Location:** 39.7966, -89.6500

## Introduction

The governor of Illinois does not have to live in the Governor's Mansion. The state provides a private apartment of about seven rooms for the governor and family, in the south wing created by the 1970s restoration, but residence is optional, and not every governor has made it a full-time home. What it has never stopped being is a stage. Since Joel Matteson threw open its doors for a grand opening on January 10, 1856, this Italianate house in Springfield has hosted state dinners, presidents, Abraham Lincoln and, in recent decades, a long line of people working to keep it in repair.

## Eighteen Thousand Dollars

The state's original appropriation for the house was $18,000. For that, Chicago architect John M. Van Osdel designed a mansion in the then-popular Italianate style, worked in some Greek Revival features, and laid it out in a modified I-shape, with a long central section running from front to back. The 16-room manor was completed in 1855, and Governor Matteson became its first occupant. In 1889 a remodel overseen by Springfield architect George Helmle painted the red-brick exterior a light stone color, in imitation of the White House. The mansion's own history calls it the third-oldest state governor's residence in the United States and the oldest in the Midwest, and it counts among the three oldest governor's mansions in the country that have been continuously occupied. Today it measures approximately 45,000 square feet.

## Lincoln Down the Street

The mansion sits near the Illinois State Capitol and the Lincoln Home National Historic Site, and in 1860 Abraham Lincoln walked into its history. That year he came to the mansion to see Governor William H. Bissell. Later occupants carried their own stories. Richard Yates Jr., who would himself become governor in 1901, spent part of his boyhood living here while his father, Richard Yates Sr., served as Illinois's Civil War governor. Presidents Ulysses S. Grant, Rutherford B. Hayes, Theodore Roosevelt and Franklin Delano Roosevelt all appear on the mansion's list of notable visitors. For a house built on an $18,000 budget in a prairie capital, it has kept remarkably distinguished company.

## Pipes, Polar Vortex and Private Money

Old houses demand attention, and this one has needed a great deal. A $3 million restoration in the 1970s preserved the original plan, and in 1972 the Illinois Governor's Mansion Association was founded as a charity to help with upkeep and programming. By 2011 a multimillion-dollar renovation was being planned, because it had gone decades without major work since the restoration that began in 1971. Then came the 2014 polar vortex, which caused significant water damage. Governor Pat Quinn, who lived in the mansion part-time, allocated about $40,000 for emergency repairs. His successor, Bruce Rauner, announced shortly after his election that he and his wife would put their own money toward repairs so they could live in the house. That privately funded work, which the mansion's history puts at $15 million, added a visitor center, brought the building into compliance with the Americans with Disabilities Act, earned a LEED Silver designation, and was completed for the Illinois bicentennial in 2018.

## Parlors for the Public

The refurbishing has continued under Governor J.B. Pritzker. Privately funded work on guest rooms, fixtures and plumbing followed in the fall of 2019, and in 2023 First Lady M.K. Pritzker, working with designer Michael S. Smith, completed an updated refurbishment of the interior. M.K. Pritzker also wrote a book about the house, A House That Made History: The Illinois Governors Mansion, published by Rizzoli in 2023 with a foreword by Smith. Earlier, Governor George Ryan and First Lady Lura Lynn Ryan had refurbished much of the furniture with private donations during their 1999 to 2003 tenure. Through all of it the mansion has remained a house museum, open to the public through free guided tours that require a reservation. Its libraries, bedrooms, parlors and sitting rooms are kept in 19th-century style, blended with later and modern pieces, and at Christmas the house fills with decorations, including more than a dozen Christmas trees.

## Flight Context

The Illinois Governor's Mansion stands at 39.7966 N, 89.6500 W in downtown Springfield, Illinois, a few blocks east-southeast of the Illinois State Capitol and close to the Lincoln Home National Historic Site. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, use the Capitol's dome as the anchor and look just to the east for the mansion and its grounds among downtown blocks; the Dana–Thomas House lies a short distance to the south. Nearest airport: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 3 nautical miles to the north-northwest; KSPI has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart for its limits and expect to work with approach control. Winter visits around the holidays bring the mansion's Christmas decorations; summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms to central Illinois.
