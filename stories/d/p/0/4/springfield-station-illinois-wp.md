# Springfield station (Illinois)

Springfield's 1895 Chicago and Alton brick depot, crowned inside by a Gulf, Mobile and Ohio route-map mural, still carries Amtrak passengers while its replacement rises across town.

**Location:** 39.8023, -89.6515

**Categories:** Amtrak stations in Illinois, Railway stations in the United States opened in 1895, Former Chicago and Alton Railroad stations, Buildings and structures in Springfield, Illinois, Transportation in Sangamon County, Illinois

## Introduction

Look up while you wait for your train. Painted on the wall above the ticket office of Springfield's Amtrak station is a small mural: a route map of the Gulf, Mobile and Ohio Railroad as it stood after 1947, framed by the state seals of Alabama, Illinois, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, Missouri and Tennessee. Chicago artist Louis Grell painted it, and it is a portrait of a railroad that no longer exists, hanging in a brick depot that has outlasted the railroads that built and ran it and is still sending passengers out along the rails toward Chicago and St. Louis.

## Built for the Alton

The Chicago and Alton Railroad built the station in 1895. It is one of three historic railroad stations still standing in Springfield, along with the Lincoln Depot, built by the Great Western Railroad in 1852, and Springfield Union Station, built by the Illinois Central in 1898. Before Amtrak started up on May 1, 1971, the depot was run by the Alton's successor, the Gulf, Mobile and Ohio, and the trains that stopped here carried names with swagger: the Alton Limited, the Abraham Lincoln and the Midnight Special. When Amtrak arrived, it gathered Springfield's passenger service here, in the former Chicago and Alton depot, three blocks west of the grander Union Station. The brick building on the edge of downtown became the city's one front door to the national rail network.

## The Station That Could Not Turn a Train

Amtrak's early plans made Springfield the southwestern end of the line for the State House, the train that became today's Lincoln Service. There was one problem. The old Chicago and Alton station had never been designed to turn trainsets around, so rather than end the run in Springfield, Amtrak paid to extend the train to St. Louis at its own expense. The limits of an 1895 depot had put the capital in the middle of the route instead of at its end. The depot sits at mile 185 on Amtrak's Illinois and Missouri Route, and as of 2007 it was served by five daily round trips each way: the Texas Eagle and four Lincoln Service frequencies. It is still served by the Lincoln Service and the Texas Eagle. The track and the platform itself belong to the Union Pacific Railroad.

## Polishing the Old Depot

Around 2011, the station got a thorough overhaul. Workers restored the Gulf, Mobile and Ohio mural, relandscaped the trackside, replaced passenger seating, improved access for travelers with disabilities, repaved the parking lot and put a new roof on the building. The $714,500 project was paid for by the city and Amtrak, with Springfield contributing $571,500 drawn from the Central Area Tax Increment Financing district that covers much of downtown. The Illinois Historic Preservation Agency reviewed the plans to make sure the station's historic elements survived the work, which is why Grell's map of a vanished railroad still presides over the ticket office. For a building then more than a century old, it was a vote of confidence in its future.

## The Last Years Before the Hub

That future is now limited. In October 2021, ground was broken on the Springfield-Sangamon Transportation Center, known as the Hub, a center meant to bring Amtrak trains and Springfield Mass Transit District buses together in one place. It was supposed to open in 2025, but in late 2023 local officials were unable to secure a $138 million federal grant needed to finish the work, pushing the hoped-for opening to around 2027, though Sangamon County's own Hub page gives no opening date. In November 2024, federal officials announced $157 million in final grant funds for the wider rail project, which moves the Third Street corridor's rail traffic onto 10th Street and is expected to be complete in fall 2027, and in May 2025 the Department of Transportation said it had fully obligated that funding. When the new station opens, the old depot is expected to lose its trains; until then, the county says, Amtrak passengers keep boarding at 100 North Third Street. It may find a new role, though. The station could become an activity hub for the projected Third Street Greenway, a 6-mile urban greenway that would use the current Amtrak right-of-way through Springfield.

## Flight Context

Springfield's Amtrak station sits at 39.8023°N, 89.6515°W on the western edge of downtown Springfield, Illinois, beside the north-south Third Street rail corridor. From 1,500 to 2,000 feet AGL, follow the rail line through the city center; the depot lies just west of the Old State Capitol's square, and the former Union Station, now part of the Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum complex, is about three blocks to the east. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is about 2.8 nautical miles to the north-northwest, so downtown sits close to its traffic; KSPI has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with approach control. Clear conditions make the rail line an easy north-south guide through the downtown grid.
