# Sacred Heart-Griffin High School

Born in 1988 when an all-girls academy and the all-boys school across the street became one, one of Springfield's largest private schools fills two campuses and a crowded trophy case.

**Location:** 39.8011, -89.6742

**Categories:** Diocese of Springfield in Illinois, Catholic secondary schools in Illinois, Educational institutions established in 1988, Schools in Springfield, Illinois, 1988 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

For years, a street was all that separated them. On one side stood Sacred Heart Academy, a Catholic high school for girls; on the other, Griffin High School, a Catholic high school for boys. The line between them was never quite absolute, since even before they joined, some classes at both schools were taught to boys and girls together. In the fall of 1988 the two finally merged, and Sacred Heart-Griffin High School in Springfield, Illinois, often shortened to SHG, opened its doors on both sides of the road.

## Two Campuses, One School

The merger kept both sets of buildings and gave each a new job. The old Griffin side became the West Campus, home to most of the school's athletic facilities. The former Sacred Heart Academy became the East Campus, where most of the academic work happens. The East Campus sits beside something no gymnasium could match: the motherhouse of a Dominican order of religious sisters, a reminder that this is a school with deep roots in religious life. SHG belongs to the Diocese of Springfield in Illinois, and it is one of the city's largest private schools. Its football field on the West Campus is named for longtime coach Ken Leonard, a measure of how seriously the school takes its football.

## The Cyclones

Every SHG team is known as the Cyclones, a name with a history older than the merger itself. Cathedral High School, an all-boys predecessor to SHG, also fielded teams called the Cyclones, and in 1939 they are reported to have won a Catholic state championship in basketball. The modern Cyclones cover nearly the entire athletic calendar. In the fall, students play football, run cross country, and compete in boys' soccer, boys' and girls' golf, girls' swimming, volleyball and girls' tennis. Winter brings boys' and girls' basketball, cheerleading, hockey, poms, boys' swimming and wrestling. Spring belongs to baseball, softball, girls' soccer, boys' and girls' track and boys' tennis. It is an enormous menu for a single high school, and the results suggest the students take full advantage of it.

## A Crowded Trophy Case

Football leads the way. The Cyclones have won state championships in 2005, 2006, 2008, 2013, 2014 and 2022, with runner-up finishes in 1975, 1982, 1995, 2003, 2016 and 2021. Baseball has claimed state titles in 1964 and 1987, both won under the Griffin name before the merger, and again in 2025. Soccer brought home state crowns in 1998 and 2002, and the boys' golf team won in 1979 and 1980, also as Griffin, and in 2023, while the girls' golf team took a title in 1996. The girls' cross country team won back-to-back AA championships in 2007 and 2008. Some of the most memorable seasons were about waiting. In 2020 the boys' basketball team qualified for the state semifinals, only to have the last weekend of the tournament canceled because of the COVID-19 pandemic. Two years later, in 2022, the boys finally won the school's first state championship in basketball.

## Out Into the World

The roster of notable people associated with SHG and the schools that became it runs well beyond Springfield. Swimmer Ryan Held won Olympic gold at the 2016 Games. Matt Mitrione played in the NFL before turning to mixed martial arts, and Malik Turner, Albert Okwuegbunam and Eric Peterman also reached professional football. On the baseball side, Jeff Fassero, Joe Slusarski and Ryan O'Malley pitched in the major leagues, and Dick Schofield played there too. Not every path ran through a stadium. Stephen Glosecki became a scholar of Old English, Edmund Hull a diplomat, and John E. Franz a chemist. Kevin Vann became Bishop of Fort Worth, Texas, and later Bishop of Orange in California. Two names on the list went on to lead Springfield itself: J. Michael Houston served as mayor of Springfield, and Karen Hasara served as a state senator, a state representative and mayor of Springfield.

## Flight Context

Sacred Heart-Griffin High School sits at 39.8011°N, 89.6742°W on the west side of Springfield, Illinois, about 1 nautical mile west of the Old State Capitol downtown. From 1,500 to 2,000 feet AGL, look for a pair of school campuses facing each other across a street, with most of the athletic facilities on the western side. Washington Park, home to the Thomas Rees Memorial Carillon, lies a little under a nautical mile to the south-southwest and makes a useful reference. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is about 2.5 nautical miles to the north, so the school lies close to the airport's approach and departure areas; KSPI has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with approach control. Clear days give the best view of the campus layout.
