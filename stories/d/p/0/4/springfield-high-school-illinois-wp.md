# Springfield High School (Illinois)

Springfield's oldest public high school opened in 1857, hosted a remote PLATO III computer terminal, and sent out Vachel Lindsay, a Postmaster General, a celebrated translator of Greek texts and the jurist who negotiated the end of Wounded Knee.

## Introduction

PLATO, the first generalized computer-assisted instruction system, was built at the University of Illinois, and the version known as PLATO III is said to have had only one remote terminal. That terminal was not in a university lab. It sat in Springfield High School, in the Illinois state capital, linked to the distant computer by a video connection and a separate dedicated line that carried keyboard data. It was a fitting outpost for a school that has spent more than a century and a half sending people out into the wider world.

## A School on the Move

Springfield High School opened on September 4, 1857, in a small building on Market Street, the road now called Capitol Avenue. It stayed there only a single school year. Next came the Academy Building on South 5th Street near Monroe, which housed the school until 1864, and in 1865 a new $65,000 building was completed for it at 4th and Madison streets. The building students know today opened in 1917. SHS is the oldest of the three high schools in Springfield Public Schools District 186, alongside Southeast and Lanphier, and it draws mainly from the west side of the city. PLATO, meanwhile, earned its own place in computing history. Launched at the University of Illinois under Don Bitzer in 1960, it came to host an email-like notes system, a chatroom called Talkomatic, and Empire, a game considered one of the first networked multiplayer games.

## Senators on Stage and Field

SHS teams are the Senators, a nickname that suits a school so close to the statehouse. They compete in the Central State Eight Conference under the Illinois High School Association, with teams for young men and women in basketball, cross country, golf, soccer, swimming and diving, tennis, and track and field, plus baseball, football and wrestling for boys and cheerleading, softball and volleyball for girls. The arts run just as deep. A marching band plays home football games, two pep bands trade off at basketball games, and the concert season brings a concert band, symphonic band and wind ensemble, along with two jazz bands, a flute choir and a clarinet choir. Since 2011, the IN Session show choir has blended singing and dance and competed around the Midwest. Each autumn brings a play and each spring a musical, from Cats to Legally Blonde, Hairspray and Beauty and the Beast.

## Poets, Players and Public Servants

The writer most associated with the school is Vachel Lindsay, class of 1897, whose writing, from The Golden Book of Springfield to "Abraham Lincoln Walks at Midnight," returned again and again to his hometown. A year before him, Thomas A. Broady graduated and went on to compose and perform ragtime piano. Robert Fitzgerald, class of 1928, translated many Greek texts into English versions that many consider standard, and in 1984 and 1985 he served as Consultant in Poetry to the Library of Congress, the equivalent of Poet Laureate of the United States. Hiram Sherman acted on television and Broadway and twice won a Tony Award. Sports produced Chicago Bears quarterback Joey Sternaman, Pro Bowl tight end Bob Trumpy and basketball's Dave Robisch, whose number 40 the University of Kansas retired. Even Hall of Fame pitcher Robin Roberts spent two years at SHS before a boundary change sent him to Lanphier.

## Into the Halls of Power

Public service runs through the alumni list. J. Edward Day, class of 1932, became Illinois State Insurance Commissioner and then U.S. Postmaster General from 1961 to 1963 under President John F. Kennedy. John Porter East, class of 1949, served as a U.S. Senator from North Carolina from 1981 to 1986, and Cheri Bustos, class of 1979, represented Illinois's 17th congressional district from 2013 to 2023. Harlington Wood Jr., class of 1938, became a federal judge, and while working for the Justice Department in 1973 he led the negotiations that ended the Wounded Knee incident. N. Ronald Thunman, a Naval Academy graduate of 1954 who served as regimental commander of the midshipmen that year, rose to vice admiral and retired in 1988. Ruth Ellis, an LGBT rights activist and one of the oldest known open lesbians, was the subject of the documentary Living With Pride: Ruth C. Ellis @ 100, and her name lives on in the Ruth Ellis Center, a social service agency for LGBTQ teens and young adults experiencing homelessness.

## Flight Context

Springfield High School sits at 39.8010°N, 89.6610°W on the west edge of downtown Springfield, Illinois, only blocks from the Illinois State Capitol. From 1,500 to 2,000 feet AGL, the domed State Capitol is the easiest landmark in the city; the school lies just to its west, with the Old State Capitol's square a little farther east. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is about 2.5 nautical miles to the north-northwest, putting downtown close to the airport's traffic; KSPI has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with approach control. The surrounding terrain is flat, and clear days make the downtown grid and the capitol dome easy to pick out from well outside the city.
