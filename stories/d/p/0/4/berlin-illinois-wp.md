# Berlin, Illinois

The Sangamon County village locals call "Old Berlin": laid out in the 1830s by a governor's grandfather, the principal village of Island Grove Township in 1904, and home to about 140 people a mile and a half from its larger namesake.

**Location:** 39.7569, -89.9033

**Categories:** Villages in Sangamon County, Illinois, Springfield metropolitan area, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Around here it goes by "Old Berlin," a nickname that exists because a larger village called New Berlin sits just 1.4 miles to the south. The name fits better than most nicknames do. Berlin was laid out in the 1830s, with a 1904 county history giving 1836 for the plat, while New Berlin was platted in October 1838 and incorporated in 1865. In every census since 1880 Berlin has counted fewer than three hundred people, holding its ground in western Sangamon County while its neighbor grew past it.

## Settlers at the Grove

Berlin was the principal village of Island Grove Township, and the grove came first. According to a 1904 history of Sangamon County, the first settler at the grove was John Roberts, who came from Tennessee and built a cabin in its northeastern part in 1818. A year or two later others followed: his son Jerry Roberts, David and Fred Troxell, Andrew Scott, William Hart and Josiah Hedges. Those names belong to the generation that broke ground here before there was a village to live in. By the time Berlin was platted, the grove had nearly two decades of settlement behind it.

## A Governor's Grandfather

The man who laid out Berlin was Henry Yates, who had opened a store in western Sangamon County in 1832. The 1904 county history says he platted the town in 1836, though the Sangamon County Historical Society and the Papers of Abraham Lincoln's biography of Yates date the layout to 1833. When the 1904 county history was written, it identified him as the grandfather of the sitting governor, Richard Yates Jr., who served as Illinois's 22nd governor from 1901 to 1905. The governor's family had already produced one famous officeholder. The younger Yates's father, Richard Yates Sr., was Illinois's Civil War governor from 1861 to 1865 and went on to serve in the U.S. Senate, and the future governor spent part of his boyhood living in the Executive Mansion in Springfield, a short ride east of the village his grandfather had platted.

## A Village That Held Steady

Berlin's census record reads like a long, slow exhale. The village counted 287 residents in 1880, 256 in 1900, 215 in 1940 and 175 in 1970. Then it rebounded to 210 in 1980, slipped to 140 in 2000, climbed to 180 in 2010, and stood at 141 in 2020. It covers about one square mile, all of it land, and today it is counted as part of the Springfield metropolitan area. In 2000 the median age was 44, and more than one in five residents was 65 or older. Through every one of those counts, Berlin has remained what it is today: a small grid of homes on the prairie, with a larger namesake just down the road.

## Flight Context

Berlin sits at 39.7569 N, 89.9033 W in western Sangamon County, Illinois, at about 640 feet above sea level and roughly 12 nautical miles west-southwest of downtown Springfield. From 2,000-3,500 feet AGL, look for two villages close together in open farmland: the small grid of Berlin to the north and the larger village of New Berlin about 1.2 nautical miles to the south. Nearest major airport: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 12 nautical miles to the east-northeast, northwest of Springfield. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over central Illinois.
