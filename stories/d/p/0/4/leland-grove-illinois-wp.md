# Leland Grove, Illinois

A small, prosperous city on the edge of Springfield that incorporated in 1950 to pave its dirt roads, took its name from a prominent farmer's estate, and has fended off every attempt by the capital to absorb it.

**Location:** 39.7772, -89.6844

**Categories:** Cities in Sangamon County, Illinois, Cities in Illinois

## Introduction

Leland Grove became a city because of its roads. In 1950 most of the streets in this cluster of subdivisions on Springfield's edge were still unpaved dirt, and residents decided to fix that by governing themselves. In April of that year they voted by referendum to incorporate. Within a year and a half the new city had paved nine miles of road, and run up a significant budget deficit doing it. It would not be the last time the people known as "Grovers" chose to go their own way.

## Nine Miles of Pavement

The referendum brought together residents of several subdivisions in Woodside Township, and the new city started with 1,060 people. On June 20, 1950, voters elected its first government, a mayor, clerk, treasurer and six aldermen, all of them unopposed. Leland Grove later grew by annexing more subdivisions nearby. Its early years had a homemade quality. For its first twenty years the city had no police department at all. When a delivery truck broke the traffic laws, residents would call the city government to report it, and a city officer would then contact the delivery company to complain. It was law enforcement by telephone.

## Jerome Leland's Grove

The city's name reaches back to the Leland farm, more than 600 acres that included what is now Leland Grove. Its best-known farmer, Jerome Leland (1874-1937), hosted picnics, receptions and fairs there; the neighboring village of Jerome takes his first name, and Wikipedia says Leland Grove takes his last. The Sangamon County Historical Society tells a slightly different story, tracing the "Grove" to a grove of oaks believed to have stood on or near the farm, though the historian Carrie Jane Schlitt wrote that the link "seems to exist more in legend than in fact." Much of the food served at Springfield's Leland Hotel was grown on the Leland farm. The farmhouse itself survived in Leland Grove until 2021, when its owner demolished it. One piece escaped. In 2022 the farmhouse's summer kitchen outbuilding was moved off the site to Washington Park in Springfield, where it survives as a physical link to the farm that named the town.

## The Grovers Say No

In 1957 Springfield tried to annex Leland Grove. Both city governments favored the merger, and the question went to voters in both places. Springfield's voters approved it five to one. Leland Grove's voted it down three to one, and that was that. Springfield kept trying, with fresh annexation efforts aimed at Leland Grove and its neighboring villages in 1960, 1971 and 1976. Every one of them failed. The result is a small city that sits right up against the state capital, connected to downtown by Sangamon Mass Transit District bus service, yet stubbornly its own place, with its own government, its own name and a population of 1,454 at the 2020 census.

## An Older, Wealthier Enclave

The 2000 census drew a portrait of an established, affluent community. Leland Grove had 1,592 residents in 693 households, and nearly two-thirds of households were married couples. The median age was 47, and about one in five residents was 65 or older. Per capita income stood at $51,714, a figure that Wikipedia says was the highest of any city outside the Chicago metropolitan area. No families in Leland Grove were below the poverty line. The roads that once needed paving now run through a community that has governed itself for more than seventy years.

## Flight Context

Leland Grove sits at 39.7772 N, 89.6844 W on the southwest side of Springfield, Illinois, about 2 nautical miles southwest of the Illinois State Capitol. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL it is hard to separate from the surrounding city; use the Capitol's dome to the northeast as a reference and look for the residential neighborhoods just southwest of downtown. Nearest airport: Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 4 nautical miles to the north; KSPI has Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart for its limits and expect to work with approach control.
