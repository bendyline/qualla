# White Oaks Mall (Springfield, Illinois)

Opened in 1977 with Ed McMahon and Billy Carter on hand, one of Central Illinois's largest malls lost every traditional anchor and reinvented itself around state offices, fitness and entertainment.

**Location:** 39.7672, -89.7036

## Introduction

On August 24, 1977, Ed McMahon and Billy Carter came to the southwest edge of Springfield, Illinois, to help open a shopping mall. It was supposed to be called Westroads Mall; it opened as White Oaks, with four department stores as its anchors. Nearly half a century later, all four of those stores are gone, along with the ones that replaced them. In their place are a fitness club, a sporting goods store, a craft retailer, an entertainment center, and, in the old Sears, the offices of the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency.

## Westroads Becomes White Oaks

Construction began in 1974 on a site about three and a half miles southwest of downtown Springfield, as the crow flies, where Illinois Route 4, known as Veterans Parkway, meets Wabash Avenue. By the time the doors opened in 1977, the project had shed its planned name of Westroads Mall. Its four original anchors were a roster of the era's department stores: Sears, Montgomery Ward, Famous-Barr and Myers Brothers. The lineup began shifting almost at once, when Bergner's bought Myers Brothers in 1978, and it put its own name on the store in 1983. In the early 1990s, a $14 million renovation reworked the mall and added a new food court, and in 1997 a carousel arrived. White Oaks now lists more than 75 stores in about 928,772 square feet of retail space, which Wikipedia describes as the largest shopping mall in Central Illinois. Its operator, the Simon Property Group, which Wikipedia lists as owning 80.7 percent of it, calls it a "two-level indoor regional shopping destination."

## Losing the Anchors

The first to go was Montgomery Ward, which closed in 2001 when the chain went out of business. In 2006 Famous-Barr was rebranded as Macy's along with every other Famous-Barr store. Then came a brutal 2018. That summer the Bergner's store closed when its parent company, Bon-Ton, liquidated, and the Sears anchor closed the same year as the struggling chain shut stores across the country. Of the four original anchor spaces, only one still operated as a department store, under the Macy's name. On January 9, 2025, Macy's announced that its White Oaks store would close as part of a plan to shutter 66 stores nationwide. It closed in early 2025, leaving the mall with no traditional department-store anchors at all.

## A Mall Reinvented

Even before Macy's left, White Oaks had begun turning into something else: part workplace, part gym, part entertainment venue. In 2020 the Illinois Department of Central Management Services, the custodian of state government real estate, bought the 120,000-square-foot Sears building to remodel as office space for the Illinois Department of Innovation and Technology, the Illinois Environmental Protection Agency and the Illinois Pollution Control Board. After remodeling work that Wikipedia puts at about $80 million, Illinois EPA employees began moving in in January 2025. The former Bergner's space became Malibu Jack's, an entertainment facility, in 2023, and Simon now lists it among the mall's anchors. A fitness club fills much of the old Montgomery Ward space, and Simon also lists Dick's Sporting Goods and Michaels as anchors. The mall that Ed McMahon helped open now leans on entertainment and office space rather than department stores.

## Flight Context

White Oaks Mall sits at 39.7672°N, 89.7036°W on the southwest side of Springfield, Illinois, about three nautical miles from downtown at the junction of Illinois Route 4 (Veterans Parkway) and Wabash Avenue. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the broad roofs of a large mall ringed by parking lots beside the intersection of those two arterial roads; downtown and the domed Illinois State Capitol lie to the northeast. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is roughly five nautical miles to the north-northeast, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. The mall's large rooftops and paved lots make it easy to spot in almost any visibility.
