# Pleasant Plains High School

A 1927 township high school west of Springfield that took in the students of neighboring Salisbury when that town's high school closed in 1941, making a village of about 800 people the hub for the surrounding countryside.

**Location:** 39.8769, -89.9189

**Categories:** Educational institutions established in 1927, Public high schools in Illinois, Schools in Sangamon County, Illinois, 1927 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

In 1941 a neighboring town lost its high school to Pleasant Plains, Illinois. Salisbury High School, in a small community on Illinois Route 97 about seven miles to the east, closed in May of that year, and its students transferred to what was then Pleasant Plains Township High School. One account credits new buses bought for the Pleasant Plains school that year, and Salisbury's school district formally consolidated with Pleasant Plains in September 1948. From then on the road between the two places became part of the school day.

## The Township School

The school building went up in 1927, and for years the institution carried the name Pleasant Plains Township High School. Today it is simply Pleasant Plains High School, a public school for grades nine through twelve at 500 North Cartwright Street, run by Pleasant Plains Community Unit School District No. 8. The village around it is small. Pleasant Plains was incorporated in 1876 and counted just over 800 residents at the 2020 census, which means the high school's enrollment amounts to about half the population of the village itself. That is the arithmetic of a rural school: it serves the farms and crossroads for miles around, a reach that dates to the 1941 closing of Salisbury's high school.

## Cardinals in Red and Blue

The teams are the Cardinals, and the school colors are red and blue. Pleasant Plains competes in the Sangamo conference, with a calendar that turns with the seasons: golf, cross country, football, soccer and volleyball in the fall; boys' and girls' basketball through the winter; baseball, soccer, softball and track when spring arrives. The school sums up what it asks of students in a three-word motto, "Respect, Responsibility, Integrity." For a school of a few hundred students, it is a full slate.

## A Small School, Counted

The numbers tell a story of modest, steady scale. In the 2024 to 2025 school year, federal education data put enrollment at 416, taught by the equivalent of 31 full-time teachers, a ratio of about thirteen students per teacher. The student body is predominantly white, much like the village itself.

## Flight Context

Pleasant Plains High School sits at 39.8769°N, 89.9189°W on the north side of the village of Pleasant Plains, roughly 13 nautical miles west-northwest of downtown Springfield, Illinois. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the small village grid surrounded by open farmland, with the school's buildings and athletic fields at its northern edge. Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI) is about 11 nautical miles to the east and makes the obvious reference point for the area. The flat, open terrain gives excellent visibility on clear days, and the village is easy to pick out against the surrounding fields.
