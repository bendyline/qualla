# Before the Water Came

A Kaskaskia River reservoir that began with relocated mines, rerouted pipelines and a demolished power plant, and now fills 11,100 acres across two central Illinois counties.

**Location:** 39.4717, -88.7139

**Categories:** Protected areas of Moultrie County, Illinois, Protected areas of Shelby County, Illinois, Reservoirs in Illinois, Bodies of water of Moultrie County, Illinois, Bodies of water of Shelby County, Illinois, 1970 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Before the first yard of dam could rise on the Kaskaskia River, the valley had to be cleared. Several old mines in the area were relocated completely. Two gas and oil pipelines and the roads were rerouted, the old Shelby Power Plant was demolished, and land on the west side of the channel, which hugs the bluff east of the river bottom, was cleared and leveled. Those four jobs read like an inventory of what the valley had held before the lake: mines, pipelines, roads and a power plant. Only then could the real work begin. When it was done, the Kaskaskia had been dammed at Shelbyville, and behind the dam lay Lake Shelbyville, a reservoir that today spreads across parts of Shelby County and Moultrie County in central Illinois.

## From Groundbreaking to Dedication

Congress authorized the Shelbyville Reservoir Project in the Flood Control Act of 1958, and $57 million was appropriated for the dam and lake (the state's park history calls it a $56 million project). Groundbreaking on the dam came on May 4, 1963. When the work was finished, the dam stood 3,025 feet long, a little over half a mile, and about 108 feet tall (the state's park pages describe an earthen embankment rising 110 feet above the original stream bed), with the normal pool sitting 17 feet below its top. Filling of the reservoir began on August 1, 1970. Six weeks later, on September 12, 1970, the lake was officially dedicated. The lake was built as part of a plan for the Kaskaskia basin that took in flood control, recreation, water supply, and fish and wildlife conservation. The United States Army Corps of Engineers manages the lake, while the Illinois Department of Natural Resources manages the wildlife.

## Eleven Thousand Acres, Deep for Illinois

At its normal surface pool, Lake Shelbyville covers 11,100 acres at an elevation of 183 meters. Its maximum depth is 67 feet, and at normal pool it averages roughly 16 to 18 feet deep, which makes it popular with boaters during the summer. Islands, coves, peninsulas and inlets give it a wooded shoreline that the Illinois Department of Natural Resources puts at 250 miles, though another account puts it at 172 miles. Its shoreline is also said to be subject to severe erosion. Away from the main channel, the many coves are well suited to fishing, and the lake and the uplands around it form a backdrop for a variety of outdoor recreation.

## Parks, Campgrounds and Man-Made Beaches

Two state parks border the lake, Wolf Creek State Park and Eagle Creek State Park, along with federal campgrounds that include Coon Creek, Opossum Creek, Lithia Springs, Lone Point, Forest (Bo) Woods and Wilborn Creek; the Corps of Engineers' visitor page counts six federal campgrounds in all. The lake has four public swimming beaches by one count, reportedly at Dam West in Shelbyville, Wilborn Creek, Wolf Creek State Park and Sullivan Beach. Beach and boat-ramp availability changes with water levels and maintenance, so check with the Corps of Engineers' Lake Shelbyville project office at 217-774-3951 before planning a swim. Illinois manages its parkland here on a long-term lease from the federal government that began in 1968, and by 1972 the area was open to the public with primitive camping facilities. The state has since made extensive improvements to the campgrounds, boat launches, day-use areas and hiking trails.

## Marinas and the Wild Edges

The Corps of Engineers lists three marinas on the lake: Findlay, Lithia Springs and Sullivan Marina and Campground. Findlay Marina bills itself as centrally located in the heart of the lake, Lithia Springs Marina rents boats and has a floating bar and grill, and Sullivan Marina and Campground sits on the north end of the lake with camping and a lakeside pub. Beyond the docks, the land around the lake is the Shelbyville State Fish and Wildlife Area, managed primarily to promote diverse habitats for a wide variety of wildlife species and the recreation that goes with them. It is divided into two units: the eastern Kaskaskia Unit, at 3,700 acres, and the western West Okaw Unit, at about 2,500 acres. Because the focus there is on hunting and fishing, the area has no camping, picnicking or day-use facilities. Lake Shelbyville has a partner on the river: Carlyle Lake is the other primary reservoir in the Kaskaskia River system.

## Flight Context

Lake Shelbyville is centered near 39.4717 N, 88.7139 W, in Shelby and Moultrie Counties, Illinois, with its surface at about 600 feet MSL. From 3,000-4,000 feet AGL, look for the 3,025-foot dam at Shelbyville and the branching, wooded coves that run from there toward Sullivan, with Sullivan Marina on the lake's north end. Nearby airports: Coles County Memorial (KMTO), about 20 nm east; Decatur Airport (KDEC), about 23 nm north-northwest; Taylorville Municipal (KTAZ), about 29 nm west; Abraham Lincoln Capital Airport (KSPI), about 50 nm west-northwest. Summer haze and convective storms are common over central Illinois, but the open water of the reservoir stays easy to pick out when the horizon is murky.
