# Dry Point and Cold Spring

Founded in 1827 and named for a Kentucky governor, this central Illinois county has had three courthouses, 24 townships and one very large reservoir.

**Location:** 39.3900, -88.8100

**Categories:** Shelby County, Illinois, Illinois counties, 1827 establishments in Illinois, Populated places established in 1827

## Introduction

Shelbyville's thermometer has seen both ends of the dial. By one weather-site summary, it reached 111°F in July 1936, and its record low was recorded in January 1915. Ordinary years are gentler. Average temperatures in the county seat run from a low of 19°F in January to a high of 87°F in July, and average monthly precipitation climbs from 1.89 inches in February to 4.05 inches in June. That is the weather of Shelby County, Illinois, 768 square miles of the central part of the state, where townships carry names like Dry Point, Cold Spring and Flat Branch, and where a county founded in 1827 has had three courthouses and one very large lake.

## A Name Carried from Kentucky

Shelby County was established in 1827 out of Fayette County and named for Isaac Shelby, a governor of Kentucky and a participant in the American Revolutionary War. Shelby was Kentucky's first governor and, in a later term, its fifth. He played a pivotal role in the American victory at the Battle of Kings Mountain, and his men gave him the nickname "Old Kings Mountain." He died in July 1826, which means the Illinois county that took his name was created the year after his death. Counties in nine states honor him, and this is one of them.

## Three Courthouses

The 1827 law that created the county provided for a temporary county seat at the home of one Barnet Bone, and it directed that the permanent seat be named Shelbyville. By the end of that year, with a location chosen, the county commissioners were preparing to build a courthouse. William Hall finished it in 1828 and was paid $110 for a log building two stories tall, 20 by 24 feet. In 1832 county officials paid $1,094 for a second courthouse, a two-story square forty feet on each side, with a courtroom on the first floor and county offices above. The courthouse that stands in Shelbyville today was completed in 1881, the third in the county's history: brick, in the Second Empire style, with mansard roofs and corner towers. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 22, 1976, as part of the Shelbyville Historic District.

## Townships with Plain Names

The county is divided into 24 townships, and some of the names have a plainspoken ring: Dry Point, Cold Spring, Flat Branch, Big Spring, Tower Hill. In all, the county covers 768 square miles, of which 759 are land and 9.5 are water, and eight counties border it, from Macon on the north to Christian on the west. Shelbyville is the county seat and its largest city. Windsor is a city too, and the villages include Cowden, Findlay, Herrick, Moweaqua, Oconee, Stewardson, Strasburg and Tower Hill. The Census Bureau recognizes one census-designated place, Westervelt. U.S. Highway 51 and Illinois Routes 16, 32 and 128 serve the county, and Interstate 57 and U.S. Highway 45 pass briefly through it with no major junctions in the county.

## The Lake Behind the Dam

Lake Shelbyville was made by damming the Kaskaskia River at Shelbyville, and it spreads across Shelby and Moultrie counties. Ground was broken on the dam on May 4, 1963, with $57 million appropriated for the dam and lake project (the state's park history calls it a $56 million project). Filling of the reservoir began on August 1, 1970, and the lake was officially dedicated six weeks later, on September 12. The dam is 3,025 feet long and 108 feet tall, and at normal pool the lake covers 11,100 acres. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers manages the lake, the Illinois Department of Natural Resources manages its wildlife, and two state parks, Wolf Creek and Eagle Creek, border the water.

## A County Counting Itself

The 2010 census counted 22,363 people in Shelby County, living in 9,216 households that included 6,376 families, at a density of 29.5 people per square mile. The median age was 43.5. By 2020 the count had fallen to 20,990, down from 23,923 in 1980. The 2020 census found 8,914 households, with 28.9 percent of them made up of one person. Of the county's residents, 76.5 percent lived in rural areas and 23.5 percent in urban ones. Most of them live under the same wide weather that produced the 111°F reading of July 1936.
