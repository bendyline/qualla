# Normal, Illinois

Born where Jesse Fell bent a railroad to cross the Illinois Central, Normal took its name from a teachers' college and has grown into a university town that now builds electric vehicles.

**Location:** 40.5122, -88.9886

**Categories:** Normal, Illinois, Bloomington–Normal, 1867 establishments in Illinois, Populated places established in 1867, Towns in McLean County, Illinois, Towns in Illinois

## Introduction

Normal exists because a railroad bent. Jesse W. Fell arranged for a new line, soon to become the Chicago and Alton Railroad, to pass west of Bloomington and then curve to cross the Illinois Central at a point where he owned or controlled land. On June 7, 1854, Joseph Parkinson laid out a town at that crossing under the name North Bloomington, and from the start it was generally recognized that Fell was the force behind it. Fell, his brothers and their associates quickly laid out addition after addition. The borrowed name did not last. Today the town is Normal, Illinois, home to 52,736 people at the 2020 census, Illinois State University and Heartland Community College.

## A Name From a Teachers' College

The town was renamed Normal in February 1865 and officially incorporated on February 25, 1867. The name came from Illinois State Normal University, a normal school, meaning a teacher-training institution, located there. The school has since become a general four-year university and was renamed Illinois State University, but the town kept the name. Founded in 1857, ISU is Illinois's oldest public university and one of the Midwest's older institutions of higher education. Its 350-acre campus holds more than 60 major buildings, among them Watterson Towers, which the university says is one of the tallest dormitory buildings in the world. Heartland Community College, founded in 1990 and among the state's youngest community colleges, enrolled 4,964 credit students in fall 2025. Together with Bloomington next door, the town is known as the "Twin Cities," Bloomington–Normal, BN or, more playfully, BloNo.

## The Children's School

Among the town's oldest institutions was the Illinois Soldiers' and Sailors' Children's School, which operated from 1865 until it officially closed in 1979. Its end came from rising per-child costs, deteriorating buildings and fewer children referred by state agencies. Some of the original structures were abandoned and left derelict, but others found new lives as a seniors community, a commercial pool offering swim lessons, and a community football field. Normal has eight listings on the National Register of Historic Places, among them the Camelback Bridge, John W. Cook Hall, the Normal Theater, the Children's Village campus of that old school and Sprague's Super Service. Route 66 left its mark here too. In December 2010, a Route 66 Wayside Exhibit was installed at the historic Sprague's Super Service building, telling the story of the highway's significance to the community, and visitors are welcome anytime during daylight hours.

## From Diamond-Star to Rivian

Normal's economy has had some surprising turns. The original Steak 'n Shake restaurant opened here in 1934. In 1986, Mitsubishi Motors North America and Chrysler Corporation established a manufacturing plant under their joint venture, Diamond-Star Motors, and it became Mitsubishi's only North American car manufacturing facility. That plant closed on May 31, 2016. Within months it had a new owner: Rivian Automotive bought it in January 2017, and since 2021 it has built all-electric vehicles there. It is Rivian's only production factory so far, R2 production began there in April 2026, and Amazon agreed in 2019 to buy 100,000 electric delivery vans. The New York Times has described the transformational effect the company has had on Normal. Not every plan has moved so quickly. Trail East, a five-story, $30 million Uptown building once meant to hold the headquarters of the Farnsworth Group, stalled for years; in July 2026 town staff laid out incentives for a new $66.1 million plan from Carlson Brothers for two six-story buildings, Trail East and Trail West.

## Uptown and the Art Deco Screen

In 2007, the town council voted to call its downtown "Uptown Normal," and the district now centers on a roundabout ringed by hotels, the town hall, Uptown Station, shops and restaurants. The Children's Discovery Museum fills three floors with hands-on exhibits, including a two-story mesh climber that children can scale to the third floor. Nearby, the Normal Theater opened in 1937, and local histories call it the first theater in Bloomington–Normal built specifically for sound films; its bold Art Moderne design was very avant-garde for a small Illinois town. Restored and owned by the town, it shows classic, arthouse and contemporary films. Uptown fills up for festivals, too: the juried Sugar Creek Arts Festival held its 42nd annual edition in 2025, with about 102 artists, and each August a free sweet corn festival, billed in 2026 as the Sweet Corn Circus, sells cooked ears for a dollar and gives college students a free one.

## Redbirds and CornBelters

Game days belong to the Illinois State Redbirds. Their football team plays in 13,391-seat Hancock Stadium, which also hosts the state's invitational high school marching band championship and the Special Olympics State Summer Games. Basketball and volleyball fill CEFCU Arena, formerly Redbird Arena, which seats 10,200. Baseball belongs to the Normal CornBelters of the Prospect League, who play in a stadium reported to hold 7,000 and carrying a fittingly agricultural name: the Corn Crib. And stitching it all together, the Constitution Trail, which the Town of Normal counted at 37 miles by 2014 and which has grown since, runs its north–south spine along an abandoned Illinois Central Gulf railroad from Kerrick Road in Normal to Grove Street in Bloomington, an old rail line put back to work carrying walkers and cyclists.

## Flight Context

Normal lies at 40.5122°N, 88.9886°W, directly north of Bloomington in McLean County, Illinois. From 3,000 to 4,000 feet AGL, the two towns merge into one urban area; Normal is the northern half, with the Illinois State University campus and the tall Watterson Towers dormitories as prominent landmarks and Hancock Stadium nearby. Interstates 39, 55 and 74 all serve the area. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) in Bloomington is only about 4 nautical miles to the southeast, so expect airline traffic and observe its airspace. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 33 nautical miles to the west. The surrounding prairie is flat, with good visibility in clear weather.
