# The Camouflage Station

A 1931 Tudor Revival gas station on Route 66, built to fit in with the surrounding homes.

**Location:** 40.5175, -88.9808

**Categories:** National Register of Historic Places in McLean County, Illinois, Gas stations on U.S. Route 66, U.S. Route 66 in Illinois, Commercial buildings completed in 1931, Retail buildings in Illinois, Gas stations on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois, Transportation buildings and structures in McLean County, Illinois, Normal, Illinois, Energy infrastructure completed in 1931, 1931 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

To a passing traveler in 1931, the building at 305 East Pine Street in Normal, Illinois, appeared to be part manor house and part gas station. Featuring a broad, red roofline with steep gables, stucco with decorative swirls, and contrasting half timbering on its second story, the structure was designed by general contractor William Sprague to follow the architectural conventions of a home so it would blend into its residential neighborhood. Today, it stands as the largest of only three remaining two-story fuel station buildings on US 66.

## The Homey Hub

William Sprague opened the station in 1931, creating a structure that housed both commercial and residential spaces. The main floor housed a Cities Service station and a garage where mechanics worked in pits built into the floor, selling tires, oil, and fan belts. Unusually for 1931, Sprague also included a café on the ground floor to serve Route 66 travelers. Living quarters occupied the second story: a spacious apartment with a sun room perched over the gas pump canopy for Sprague and his family, and a second apartment for the station attendant. The National Park Service says the Tudor Revival style Sprague chose helped give the building a "local, homey identity."

## The Slow Fade

The station's stability wavered during the 1940s. Due to a scarcity of gasoline and repair parts, the building sat vacant for part of World War II between 1941 and 1946. Around 1940, Route 66 was also rerouted to the beltline, bypassing the heart of the Twin Cities. Despite this shift, Sprague's Super Service survived long after the rerouting. It continued to operate through several brand changes, including stints as Sloan's Cities Service from 1959 to 1962, Sloan's Texaco in 1963, a Zephyr station with Irene's Cafe in 1964, and a Gulf station during the 1960s. However, the era of fuel was ending; the last gallon of gas was pumped in 1971, and by 1979, the original pumps were gone and the underground gas tanks were removed.

## A History of Mismatched Tenants

Over time, the manor transitioned into a hub of unrelated businesses. In the mid-1970s, a cake gallery and bridal boutiques like Bridal World shared the site with Hodge's Catering. Other tenants also occupied the space, including Corn Belt Manufacturing, Joe's Welding and Boiler Company, Yellow Cab, and Avis Rent-a-Car. Over time, a computer repair business and a photography studio also rented the space.

In October 2006, Terri Ryburn, an Illinois State University instructor and Route 66 historical author, purchased the station with her husband William Sanders for $220,000. Ryburn spent a decade working to restore the relic, though her plans for a café, diner, soda fountain, bed and breakfast and event space were delayed by an estimated total cost of $1.2 million. Restoration was supported by a $10,000 state grant for bathrooms, a $46,695 town grant for a temporary furnace and roof, and two National Park Service grants totaling $30,000 for the ceiling, duct work, HVAC system, and windows. In 2010, proceeds from the sale of David Schwartz's "Pics on Route 66" 2011 Calendar were donated to the effort. Ryburn eventually sold the station to the town of Normal in 2016. Following additional renovations, the building reopened on August 12, 2017, as Ryburn Place—a visitor center and gift shop featuring local art.
