# The Soil of Normal

A former joint-venture sports car plant in Illinois evolved from a symbol of international cooperation into a hub for electric vehicle manufacturing.

**Location:** 40.5055, -89.0594

**Categories:** Buildings and structures completed in 1988, Vehicle manufacturing companies established in 1985, Vehicle manufacturing companies disestablished in 2015, Mitsubishi Motors factories, Companies based in Bloomington–Normal, Former joint ventures, Motor vehicle assembly plants in Illinois, Chrysler factories, 1985 establishments in Illinois, 2015 disestablishments in Illinois

## Introduction

On October 7, 1985, a Shinto priest stood in Normal, Illinois to perform a blessing. The ceremony sealed the selection of a new industrial site through the "commingling of Japanese soil and the prairie earth of Normal, Illinois." This act marked the birth of Diamond-Star Motors, a joint venture between Mitsubishi and Chrysler. The company’s name was a literal fusion of its parents' logos: the three diamonds of Mitsubishi and the pentastar of Chrysler.

## The Era of the Triplets

Ground was broken in April 1986 on a production facility spanning 1,900,000 square feet, which was completed in March 1988. By 1989, three co-designed 2+2 sports cars—the Mitsubishi Eclipse, Plymouth Laser, and Eagle Talon—rolled off the assembly line. Known as the "DSM Triplets," these cars featured modern styling, optional all-wheel drive, and an available 2.0-liter 4G63T turbocharged four-cylinder engine. They were originally intended to be value performance coupes, but they soon became "the vehicle of choice for tuning enthusiasts."

The facility began with 470 robots and 1,250 workers, later increasing to 2,900 people. By August 1989, the workers had formed UAW Local 2488 and ratified their first contract. The plant’s capacity reached up to 240,000 vehicles per year, producing the triplets alongside homebrewed models like the original Mirage, which were rebranded as the Dodge Colt and Eagle Summit.

## Fading Stars

The partnership did not last. In October 1991, Mitsubishi purchased Chrysler's 50 percent interest in the joint venture for a reputed $200 to $300 million. Mitsubishi took sole control of the plant for four years before the Diamond-Star Motors name was officially replaced on July 1, 1995, by Mitsubishi Motor Manufacturing of America.

The facility continued to produce the Eclipse—including the second-generation Eclipse Spyder convertible—as well as the midsize Galant sedan and Endeavor SUV. It also manufactured the Chrysler Sebring coupe and the Dodge Avenger and Stratus coupes, which were essentially Mitsubishi Eclipses. By October 1995, the plant celebrated the production of its 1,000,000th vehicle.

## From Tuners to Electric Vehicles

By 2015, the plant had been operating well below capacity for several years. In July 2015, Mitsubishi announced it would end production in Normal, and the final car was manufactured on November 30. On that day, 900 out of 1,200 workers were let go. The plant finally closed in May 2016.

Then, a new era began. In January 2017, the EV brand Rivian acquired the plant and its manufacturing contents for $16 million to serve as its primary North American manufacturing facility. This site remains Rivian's sole manufacturing location until a Georgia facility comes online in 2028. In May 2024, Rivian announced a planned $1.5 billion expansion to produce the R2 midsize SUV. The expansion spans more than 2.1 million square feet, including a new 1.1 million-square-foot building, bringing annual capacity to approximately 215,000 vehicles—a massive investment compared to the site's 2016 closure and its original commingling of soil.
