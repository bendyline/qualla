# Moraine View State Recreation Area

A 1,687-acre state recreation area near Le Roy sits on a glacial ridge where two branches of the Sangamon River system begin on opposite slopes, with 158-acre Dawson Lake at its heart.

**Location:** 40.4114, -88.7322

**Categories:** Protected areas of McLean County, Illinois, State parks of Illinois, Protected areas established in 1959, Landforms of McLean County, Illinois, Reservoirs in Illinois, 1959 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Two streams are born on this low ridge in McLean County, Illinois, on opposite sides of the same hill. The North Fork of Salt Creek begins inside Moraine View State Recreation Area. The main stem of the Sangamon River rises on the northeastern face of the same ridge, just outside the park. Each flows away on its own course, and they run more than 80 miles before they finally meet again, near Greenview. The ridge that divides them is a moraine, left behind by a vanished ice sheet, and it gives this 1,687-acre park near Le Roy its name.

## What the Ice Left Behind

A moraine is a low, rolling ridge formed where a melting ice sheet dumped the ground rock and till it had carried. They are common post-glacial features and can be found throughout the U.S. Midwest. This one was left near the close of the Wisconsin glaciation, which ran from about 70,000 to 10,000 years ago; a local history dates such moraines to roughly 20,000 to 25,000 years back. The ice is long gone, but its leftovers still shape where water goes. Salt Creek, whose North Fork begins here, is a major tributary of the Sangamon, one of the largest rivers in central Illinois. That makes this gentle rise a true headwaters, the place where a big river system starts as a trickle. Walk the park's higher ground and you are standing on rock and till that the ice carried here and left behind.

## A Lake Built for Leisure

The centerpiece of the park is Dawson Lake, a 158-acre artificial reservoir built in 1962 and 1963. The Illinois Department of Natural Resources stocks it with largemouth bass and bluegill, the state fish of Illinois, along with sunfish, bullhead, crappie, channel catfish, walleye, yellow perch and northern pike. The park around it traces its roots to the McLean County Conservation Area, established in 1959. It was renamed Moraine View State Park in 1975 and became Moraine View State Recreation Area in 1995, a title that fits how it is managed: for active recreation. Visitors come to camp, hike, swim, boat, hunt and fish. Campers can choose Class A sites with electricity and nearby modern toilets and showers, group camps, or primitive backpacking sites for those who would rather carry everything on their backs. In season, hunters use the park for whitetail deer, pheasant and wild turkey.

## The Winter the Gates Closed

In 2008, Moraine View nearly disappeared from the map of places Illinoisans could go. It was one of eleven state parks slated to close indefinitely on November 1, 2008, because of budget cuts ordered by then-Governor Rod Blagojevich. A reprieve delayed the shutdown, and Moraine View was among seven parks that finally closed at the end of the day on November 30. The gates stayed shut through the winter. Then politics turned over. After Blagojevich was impeached and removed from office, his successor, Pat Quinn, moved to reopen the closed state parks in late February 2009, and Moraine View was on the list. The lake, the ridge and the campgrounds had never gone anywhere, of course. For a few cold months, though, nobody could use them.

## Flight Context

Moraine View State Recreation Area lies at 40.4114°N, 88.7322°W in southeastern McLean County, Illinois, about 4 nautical miles north-northeast of Le Roy. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, Dawson Lake is the unmistakable landmark: a 158-acre reservoir surrounded by parkland, standing out against the flat, open country around it. The moraine itself is a gentle rise rather than a dramatic ridge, so look for the lake rather than for relief. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 9 nautical miles to the west-northwest, and University of Illinois Willard Airport (KCMI) near Champaign is about 30 nautical miles to the southeast. Water shows well in clear weather and low sun.
