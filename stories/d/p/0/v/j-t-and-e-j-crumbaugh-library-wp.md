# J. T. and E. J. Crumbaugh Library

A LeRoy couple who lost their only son as an infant left their fortune for a Spiritualist church and a free public library, which opened under one roof in 1927 after years of litigation and still serves the town.

**Location:** 40.3471, -88.7580

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in McLean County, Illinois, Libraries in Illinois, Museums in McLean County, Illinois, History museums in Illinois, Library buildings completed in 1927, Churches completed in 1927

## Introduction

The Crumbaughs' only child lived for seven weeks. Born on July 2, 1865, he died on August 20 of that same summer, and he is known today only by a tender nickname, "Bright Eyes." For James Thomas and Elizabeth Jane Crumbaugh, the loss deepened an interest in spiritualism that stayed with them for the rest of their lives. Decades later, that grief took the form of a building. In LeRoy, Illinois, a single brick structure houses both a Spiritualist church and a public library, opened on January 1, 1927, as the living memorial the couple asked for in their will.

## Twins, Farms and a Bank

James Thomas Crumbaugh, known as J. T., was born on January 24, 1832, in Empire Township in McLean County, to parents of German descent. He grew up with a twin brother, Daniel T. Crumbaugh, and the two later became partners on their farm. After a few years J. T. rented out his farms and moved into LeRoy, where he built a house at 313 East Center Street. Together with another brother, Leonard A. Crumbaugh, and a friend, James Bonnette, he helped establish the Citizens Bank, which later became The First National Bank. On September 13, 1862, he married Elizabeth Jane Wiley, born November 17, 1841, and also raised in McLean County. Three years later came the brief life of their son, and with it the turn toward spiritualism that would shape everything the couple left behind.

## A Will Worth Fighting Over

J. T. died on April 3, 1905. Elizabeth followed less than a year later, on January 24, 1906, which happened to be the date of her husband's birth. Their will laid out a clear wish: a library and a Spiritualist church, built as a living memorial. Their heirs did not accept it quietly. Nephews and nieces contested the will in court, arguing that his belief in spiritualism was an insane delusion. The Illinois Supreme Court rejected that argument in 1907, ruling that belief in spiritualism is not evidence of insanity, and the Crumbaughs' wishes were reportedly upheld only after five years of litigation. The building still took years to become real. When it opened on New Year's Day 1927, more than two decades had passed since J. T.'s death, and both the church and the library it housed are still in use today.

## Danville Brick and Bedford Stone

The design came from A. L. Pillsbury of Bloomington, and construction went to F. C. Swartz of Villa Grove for $60,000. The plan originally called for two separate buildings, but the two were joined instead, with one wing for the church and another for the library. The result is a colonial design of Danville red brick finished with Bedford stone. The stone carvings are the work of Joseph Petarde, a carver originally from Rome, Italy, who later lived in Peoria and Bloomington. His hand shows up across central Illinois: at the Illinois Wesleyan School of Music, at the Bloomington Center for the Performing Arts (formerly the Consistory), at the McBarnes Memorial Building in Bloomington, at a gymnasium at Illinois State University and at St. Peter's Church in Peoria.

## Where a Town Keeps Its Memory

Inside, the library holds an estimated 14,000 volumes. Because it is endowed, anyone can get a library card for free, and there are computers, internet access and a printer alongside the shelves. The town newspaper, the LeRoy Journal/Leader, is on microfilm from the early 1900s and has been digitized through 2020. The genealogy section began in the fall of 1977, when the Crumbaugh family Bibles and family history were placed on file. It has since gathered more than 400 local family histories, along with LeRoy High School yearbooks back to 1916, city directories from 1895 to 2005 and records for the Oak Grove, Old Oak Grove and Gilmore cemeteries. Downstairs, the Empire Township Historical Museum preserves memorabilia from local businesses, schools and organizations. The LeRoy Historical Society first opened it, and after the society disbanded in 2006, community volunteers reopened it in 2008. A couple who lost their only child ended up keeping the family records of an entire town.
