# Downs, Illinois

A McLean County railroad village that the station men named Downs while neighbors kept calling it Priceville, now a growing town between U.S. 150 and I-74 with state-champion Vikings and Blue Ribbon schools.

**Location:** 40.3972, -88.8797

**Categories:** Villages in McLean County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1870, 1870 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

For years this place had two names. When the Indianapolis, Bloomington & Western Railroad set a station here in 1870, the railroad officials called it Downs, and so did the post office. The neighbors had other ideas. As an old McLean County history records, the neighborhood was called Priceville, and it still popularly kept that name even though the station and post office said Downs. In the end, the railroad's choice won, though local accounts say it was mail clerks confusing Priceville with Princeville that settled the matter, and Wikipedia dates the change to 1902. Downs, a village of 1,201 people nine miles southeast of Bloomington in McLean County, Illinois, carries the name of Lawson Downs, one of the area's pioneer settlers.

## Diamond Grove and Priceville

Lawson Downs, originally from Tennessee, arrived in 1829 and took up a claim at a place called Diamond Grove. Other early families left their mark too. The Price family gave the neighborhood its popular name, and when the village was platted in 1870, it was P. B. Price, the son of the settler remembered as "old Father Price," who laid out the town and platted fifteen blocks north of the railroad. The railroad was the reason for it all. A station near the center of Section 4 turned a rural neighborhood into a platted town, and the men who ran the line gave that station a pioneer's surname. The Prices laid out the streets, but the village took its name from Downs.

## Between the Highway and the Interstate

Downs sits in southern McLean County, squeezed between two major roads. U.S. Route 150 passes along the northeast side of the village, leading northwest to Bloomington and about 7 miles southeast to Le Roy. Interstate 74 skirts the southwest side, reached from Exit 142, running northwest to Bloomington and 40 miles southeast to Champaign. In recent decades the village has grown quickly. Downs counted 776 people in 2000, 1,005 in 2010 and 1,201 in 2020, growth of more than half in two decades across a village of just 3 square miles. At the turn of the century it was a young place, with a median age of 33 and nearly a third of residents under 18. Many of those young residents fill the classrooms of a small school district with a big reputation.

## Vikings and Blue Ribbons

Most children in Downs attend the Tri-Valley schools of District 3, whose offices sit along Washington Street. The schools are smaller than most in the surrounding area, and the elementary, middle and high schools stand right next to each other. The elementary school was named a National Blue Ribbon School by the U.S. Department of Education in 2016 and the middle school in 2019. The high school, formed in 1963 by merging Downs Community High School with Ellsworth's, moved into its current building in 1979. The pride extends to the football field. In 2015 the Tri-Valley Vikings beat Auburn in the Class 2A state final, putting a small-town district on top of its class in Illinois.

## Flight Context

Downs lies at 40.3972°N, 88.8797°W in southern McLean County, Illinois, about 7 nautical miles southeast of Bloomington. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the village tucked between U.S. Route 150 on its northeast side and Interstate 74 on its southwest side, with Exit 142 at the edge of town; the village water tower is a useful landmark. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is only about 5 nautical miles to the north-northwest, so expect traffic in the area and check the sectional chart for the airport's controlled airspace. Le Roy lies about 6 nautical miles southeast along US 150. The flat terrain gives good visibility in clear weather.
