# Delavan, Illinois

A Tazewell County city founded in 1837 by New England settlers and named for a temperance crusader, Delavan later found new revenue in a cannabis cultivation facility on its outskirts.

**Location:** 40.3708, -89.5456

**Categories:** Cities in Illinois, Cities in Tazewell County, Illinois, Peoria metropolitan area, Illinois, 1837 establishments in Illinois, Populated places established in 1837

## Introduction

Edward Cornelius Delavan did not drink, and he wanted the rest of the country to stop drinking too. The Albany, New York, businessman was a temperance advocate and a land speculator, and when settlers from New England laid out a town on the Illinois prairie in 1837, they named it for him. Nearly two centuries later, the city that carries his name has drawn new revenue from a cannabis cultivation facility on its edge, an irony the Chicago Sun-Times did not miss.

## Settlers from New England

Delavan was founded by a group of settlers from New England who came west to the Illinois prairie in 1837. Their namesake, Edward C. Delavan of Albany, was a teetotaler who also helped establish the American Temperance Union. A post office opened in 1840, and it has been operating ever since. Over the following century, Delavan grew into a bustling corn-belt city in Tazewell County, today counted as part of the Peoria metropolitan area. It never grew large in area, though. The whole city covers about 1.4 square miles, all of it land, a compact grid set down in open farm country.

## The Locust Street Question

Delavan's downtown was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1991, but by then its commercial life was already thinning. Businesses closed from the 1980s onward, and by 2019 most of the 26 buildings lining the main two-block stretch of Locust Street stood vacant. Population followed a gentler slope, from 1,825 residents in 2000 to 1,689 in 2010. The 2000 count found a settled, family town: 705 households, nearly two-thirds of them married couples, and a median age of 38. Then an unlikely employer arrived. Since 2015, Revolution Enterprises has operated a cannabis cultivation facility here, a 75,000-square-foot building reported in 2019 to hold up to 10,000 marijuana plants at a time and to employ 56 people; that year the company announced plans for a major expansion. The city was receiving more than $300,000 a year in tax revenue from the operation, according to 2019 reporting, no small sum for a city of about 1,600 people.

## A Small Town's Long Reach

Delavan keeps its schools close together. A single consolidated district, number 703, educates students from preschool through high school in different parts of one common campus. The town's alumni have traveled far. Harriet Creighton, born here in 1909, became a botanist and, working with Barbara McClintock at Cornell University, published an influential 1931 paper demonstrating chromosomal crossover, key evidence that chromosomes carry and exchange genetic information. The city's roll of notable people also includes John T. Culbertson Jr., who served as a justice of the Illinois Supreme Court; the racing driver Johnny McDowell; U.S. Army Major General Archibald H. Sunderland; and the artist Julia Thecla. For a city of well under two thousand people, it is a remarkably varied roster.

## Flight Context

Delavan lies at 40.3708 N, 89.5456 W in southern Tazewell County, Illinois, about 620 feet above sea level and roughly 19 nautical miles south of downtown Peoria. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for a compact small-city grid in flat, open cropland, with the two-block Locust Street business district near its center. The nearest major airport is General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA), about 19 nautical miles to the north-northwest; Pekin Municipal Airport (FAA identifier C15, no ICAO code) is closer, roughly 9 nautical miles to the northwest. KPIA is Class C airspace, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control near Peoria. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the prairie.
