# Emden, Illinois

A Logan County railroad village named by German immigrants for Emden on the River Ems, now the hub of a 67-turbine wind farm.

**Location:** 40.2978, -89.4853

**Categories:** Villages in Logan County, Illinois, German-American history, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Sixty-seven wind turbines stand in the fields of northern Logan and southern Tazewell counties, and the village at the center of them all covers less than a quarter of a square mile. Emden has fewer than five hundred residents, a grid of streets laid out for a railroad that arrived in 1871, and a name carried across the Atlantic by the German families who settled here. It took that name from Emden, a town on the River Ems in Germany, because so many of the people who settled this corner of Illinois had left villages along that river.

## A Name from the River Ems

The German roots run deep in the record of this Orvil Township village. An early history of Logan County, describing a village of about two hundred people, noted that its young Methodist congregation was mostly German, and it explained the town's name plainly: many of the Germans in the locality had emigrated from Emden, on the River Ems. Modern accounts say much the same, tracing the name to the large number of residents who came from villages along the Ems. The community soon had a voice of its own in print. The first newspaper published here, The Emden Star, appeared in 1893.

## Surveyed for the Railroad

Emden exists because of a railroad. The Pekin, Lincoln & Decatur Railroad passed through Orvil Township and was completed in 1871, and on June 15 county surveyor Thomas G. Gardner surveyed the town for its original proprietor, John M. Gill of Pekin. B. F. Burnett built the first store and served as both the first postmaster and the depot agent. Grain followed quickly. A. W. Ray built the first elevator in the summer of 1873, and Smith, Kippen & Co. added a second in 1875. The county history recorded corn shipments from Emden reaching fully half a million bushels a year, a remarkable volume for a village of its size.

## Turbines Around the Town

Today the Rail Splitter Wind Farm is centered on Emden. Built in 2008 and 2009 and dedicated on July 21, 2009, it spreads its 67 General Electric turbines across northern Logan County and southern Tazewell County, near Emden, Delavan and Hopedale, with a nameplate capacity of 100.5 megawatts. It is operated by EDP Renewables North America, formerly Horizon Wind Energy. The village itself has stayed small and settled. Emden counted roughly 520 people in 2000, 485 in 2010 and about 466 in 2020.

## Flight Context

Emden sits at 40.2978 N, 89.4853 W in northern Logan County, Illinois, at roughly 589 feet above sea level. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, the Rail Splitter Wind Farm's 67 turbines make the area easy to identify, with the tiny village grid at their center and Delavan about 5 nautical miles to the north-northwest. Stay well clear of the turbine field at low altitude. Logan County Airport (KAAA) at Lincoln is about 11 nautical miles to the southeast; General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is roughly 24 nautical miles to the north-northwest, and its Class C airspace means you should check the sectional chart and expect to work with the tower or approach control. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the flat cropland.
