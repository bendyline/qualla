# Eureka College

Founded by Kentucky abolitionists and among the first colleges to educate women equally with men, tiny Eureka College heard Lincoln in 1856 and sent Ronald Reagan into the world in 1932.

## Introduction

"Everything that has been good in my life began here." Ronald Reagan said it to a Eureka College audience in 1982, the same year he stood in the college gym that bore his family's name and used the school's commencement to announce his START treaty proposal. He had arrived as a 17-year-old freshman from Dixon, Illinois, in 1928, and he never really left. Eureka, a private college in Eureka, Illinois, says it is the smallest of only 24 colleges and universities to award a bachelor's degree to a future U.S. president. But its story starts long before Reagan, with a band of Kentucky abolitionists.

## Abolitionists at Walnut Grove

The college traces its roots to 1848, when abolitionists who had left Kentucky because of their opposition to slavery founded Walnut Grove Academy; the Illinois legislature chartered the college in 1855, the year the college itself counts as its founding. From the start it broke with convention: by its own account it was the first college in Illinois, and only the third in the United States, to educate women on an equal basis with men. In 1856 Abraham Lincoln spoke on campus, reportedly making Eureka one of only three colleges where he spoke. Abingdon College merged with Eureka in 1885. The college remains related by covenant to the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ), and in 2010 its campus was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Eureka College Campus Historic District. Over the years its graduates have included forty-two college and university presidents and seven governors and members of Congress, among them Frank Frantz, a Rough Rider who became the final Governor of Oklahoma Territory.

## The Freshman From Dixon

Reagan graduated on June 10, 1932, with a joint major in economics and sociology. His ties to the place ran deep. He stayed connected to his fraternity, Tau Kappa Epsilon, kept in touch with his football coach and mentor, Ralph "Mac" McKinzie, and served three terms on the board of trustees. He returned on twelve recorded occasions, gave commencement addresses in 1952, 1957, 1982 and 1992, and gave his own money to the college's fund-raising drives. In 1967 he dedicated the Melick Library building. In 1970 he and his brother Neil, also an alumnus, dedicated the Reagan Athletic Complex named in their honor. When Reagan died in 2004, his family designated Eureka as one of three official recipients of memorial gifts. The college had already created the Ronald W. Reagan Leadership Program in 1982, offering four-year full-tuition scholarships to students named Reagan Fellows.

## A Piece of the Berlin Wall

Today the Ronald Reagan Museum and Peace Garden, inside the Donald B. Cerf Center, displays memorabilia largely donated by Reagan himself, spanning his years as a student, actor, athlete, Governor of California and President of the United States. Admission is free. The garden holds a section of the Berlin Wall, and on March 27, 2009, a remarkable visitor came to see it: Mikhail Gorbachev, the former leader of the Soviet Union. Eureka gave him an honorary degree at a convocation where students asked him questions directly. That same year the college granted Nancy Reagan an honorary degree at a private ceremony at the Ronald Reagan Presidential Library in Simi Valley, California. For the Reagan Centennial in 2011, the college opened the Mark R. Shenkman Reagan Research Center and College Archives, which is collecting every book and doctoral dissertation written about him. James A. Baker III, Sandra Day O'Connor and George P. Shultz were later named Honorary Reagan Fellows.

## Red Devils and a Record

Eureka's teams are the Red Devils, competing in NCAA Division III, mostly in the St. Louis Intercollegiate Athletic Conference since 2006–07 (football plays in the Northern Athletics Collegiate Conference), and fielding 14 varsity sports, including wrestling for both men and women. The school has had its big moments. Coached by Dave Darnell, the men's basketball team won the 1994 NAIA Division II national championship. And on September 1, 2012, quarterback Sam Durley threw for 736 yards, an NCAA record, as Eureka beat Knox College 62–55. Off the field, the college is small by any measure: about 560 students in fall 2024, split nearly evenly between men and women, most of them from Illinois, still gathered on the campus that abolitionists founded more than 170 years ago.

## Flight Context

Eureka College lies at 40.7139°N, 89.2675°W in the city of Eureka, Woodford County, Illinois, east of Peoria. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the compact campus within the town's grid. General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 20 nautical miles to the west, and Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 21 nautical miles to the southeast. The surrounding open country offers good visibility on clear days.
