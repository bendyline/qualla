# The Salesman's Addition

A sixteen-year-old Elbert Hubbard once rode his horse through McLean County to obtain cash, materials, and promises of labor from his father's clientele.

**Location:** 40.6089, -88.9875

**Categories:** Houses in McLean County, Illinois, National Register of Historic Places in McLean County, Illinois, I-houses in Illinois, Houses on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois, 1857 establishments in Illinois, Homes of American writers

## Introduction

Long before he was known at the turn of the century as the Sage of East Aurora, Elbert Hubbard lived in a "little gray house" in Hudson, Illinois. In summer, coral-red honeysuckle and rambler roses overran the clapboards of the dwelling, while lilacs, syringa, and flowering almond bloomed outside the door. Behind the home lay flower-covered prairies and yellow cornfields.

## The Doctor's Cottage

The residence began as a smaller structure. In 1857, Silas Hubbard, one of the first doctors to practice medicine in Hudson, built the original east wing. Elbert's parents moved into this frame house when he was a year old. Within these walls, Elbert first learned to write and learned how to make things with his hands, pursuits that later led him to become a writer and found the Roycroft Shops.

## The Great Expansion

By around 1872, Mrs. Hubbard felt the family had outgrown the small cottage that had occupied the site since 1857. According to the writings of her daughter Mary, Mrs. Hubbard planned a two-story addition at the front elevation of the building. She envisioned a central front hall with a stairway flanked by walnut balustrades, an open parlor with folding doors opening into the living room, a spare bedroom, and two new children's bedrooms on the second floor.

Elbert and his mother searched Silas' records for people they thought might be able to pay via labor or materials. Elbert, riding throughout McLean County, sought cash, materials, and promises of labor from his father's clientele. After these efforts to secure donations, the new section was built in 1872.

## The Finished Form

This two-story addition was constructed in a typical I-house design and attached to the west side of the original house. The addition faced Broadway Street, Hudson's main north-south thoroughfare. Sometime after both sections were completed, a bay window was added in the angle between the wings. Elbert's room was in the attic.

## Preserving the Memorial

The house has faced threats from nature, specifically severe termite damage. The home, which had been listed on the National Register of Historic Places since 1979, underwent extensive remodeling in 2008. This process included reframing all walls and raising the roof of the original portion of the house. The kitchen was also fully remodeled at this time, and modern windows were replaced with double-hung, 4 over 4 windows consistent in size and style with the rest of the home. Today, the house remains significant as the boyhood home of Elbert Hubbard.
