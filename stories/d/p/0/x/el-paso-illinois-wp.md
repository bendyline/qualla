# El Paso, Illinois

A railroad-crossing town on the Woodford–McLean county line where a barber cast a landmark ballot in 1870, Archbishop Fulton Sheen was born, and an elephant slide still anchors one of the original parks.

**Location:** 40.7389, -89.0161

**Categories:** Cities in Illinois, Cities in Woodford County, Illinois, Cities in McLean County, Illinois, Peoria metropolitan area, Illinois

## Introduction

On April 4, 1870, El Paso held its city election, and a barber named David A. Strother came to vote. The 15th Amendment had recently passed, but the officials at the polls, as the story is told, were nonplussed at his demand. Eventually apologies were made, and Strother cast his vote. The Project XV Museum in El Paso, Illinois, now honors him as the first African American to vote in Illinois after the amendment's passage, and his story is bound up with the downtown building where his barber shop once operated. It is one of several surprises in a city of 2,756 people at the 2020 census that straddles Woodford and McLean counties.

## Where Two Railroads Crossed

El Paso was founded in the 1850s by landowners George Gibson and James Wathen, who successfully competed to secure the site where the Illinois Central Railroad crossed the Toledo, Peoria and Western. Gibson gave it a Spanish name. Nobody is quite sure why: it may honor El Paso County, Texas, the El Paso Mountains in California, or simply the nearby railroad junction. The founders also gave the town its first parks. Jefferson Park, established on May 26, 1857, came from Wathen, and in 1877 it was enclosed with an iron fence to keep out wandering cattle. Its bandstand became the focus of summer concerts, and during a 1914 improvement effort, 227 wagonloads of dirt were hauled in to raise its grade. Franklin Park, donated by the Gibson family, got 536 loads that day, gained an elephant-shaped slide in 1962, and has been "Elephant Park" to locals ever since. The tracks also split the young town. By 1859 the Illinois Central divided it into East and West sides, each with its own rival school district.

## The Barber's Ballot

Strother, who lived from 1843 to 1905, was in El Paso by January 1864, when Jonathan D. Parks let him set up a barber's chair in a corner of his insurance and justice-of-the-peace office. He stayed for the rest of his life. Later his shop moved into the basement of the Eagle Block, built in 1874 at 1 West Front Street. A fire in 1894 destroyed that building along with much of Front Street, and its replacement was finished in 1895; it is now called the Legacy Building. Today the building houses local businesses and a restored opera house used for weddings and special events. The Project XV Museum, whose name refers to the 15th Amendment, describes itself as Illinois's first voting rights museum, with visits by appointment, and tells Strother's story along with the local figures and events tied to that amendment. In a small Illinois town, one barber's insistence on his right to vote has become the heart of a museum about voting rights.

## The Depot and the Archbishop

At 8-10 East Front Street once stood the freight house that served both railroads, known to locals simply as "The Depot." Built in 1889 and 1890 after the original was destroyed, it shipped the area's agricultural products and helped drive the local economy until rail traffic declined in the 1930s and ended by the 1960s. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on August 16, 1994, and it kept that status after it was moved to 17 East Main Street. In 2000 it reportedly gained a new purpose as the Archbishop Fulton John Sheen Spiritual Centre. Sheen was born in El Paso in 1895 and became a prominent Catholic figure, known for his popular television programs; he was beatified in St. Louis in September 2026.

## Carnegie Books and an Old Rail Bed

The El Paso District Library was built with a grant from Andrew Carnegie and has served the town continuously since 1907. Its two-story building shares the Jefferson Park block, near the district's schools. Those schools reflect a later merger: in 2004, El Paso joined with Gridley to form Community Unit School District 11, with the high school in El Paso. The town has a habit of repurposing its past. Corn Belt Park began as the clay pit of the Fursman & Straight tile factory, which the city bought in 1920 and filled with refuse before leveling it into a park in 1951. Beginning in 1989, a former railroad right-of-way became the 5.4-mile Bike Trail & Walking Path, a flat route that runs from South Pointe Park on the south side through town and out to a creek north of El Paso among cultivated fields.

## Flight Context

El Paso lies at 40.7389°N, 89.0161°W, straddling the Woodford–McLean county line in central Illinois, north of Bloomington–Normal. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the compact town grid and the straight line of the former railroad right-of-way, now the Bike Trail & Walking Path, running north–south through town. Central Illinois Regional Airport (KBMI) at Bloomington is about 16 nautical miles to the south-southeast, and General Wayne A. Downing Peoria International Airport (KPIA) is about 31 nautical miles to the west. The terrain is flat and open, with good visibility in clear weather.
