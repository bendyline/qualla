# Frederick Delius

A young Englishman sent to grow oranges on the St. Johns River found his vocation instead at Solano Grove, where the music of Florida shaped a composer whose work would be loved, as his amanuensis put it, "dearly loved, by the few."

**Location:** 29.8747, -81.5761

**Categories:** English classical composers, 19th-century English classical composers, 20th-century English classical composers, English expatriates in the United States, St. Johns River, Blind classical musicians

## Introduction

Whenever a steamship passed Solano Grove, singing drifted across the St. Johns River to the veranda of a young Englishman who was supposed to be growing oranges. Fritz Delius, born in Bradford in 1862 to a prosperous wool-merchant family, arrived here in the spring of 1884 to run an orange plantation about 25 miles south of Jacksonville. He never forgot that music. The roughly eighteen months he spent on this riverbank turned a merchant's son into a composer, and the man the world came to know as Frederick Delius carried Florida in his music for the rest of his life.

## Counterpoint in Jacksonville

Delius later liked to call his Solano Grove house "a shanty," though it was in fact a substantial four-room cottage with plenty of room for guests. Jacksonville, up the river, had a rich musical life that was unorthodox to European ears. African American waiters in the city's hotels doubled as singers, performing daily for patrons and passers-by, and it was through them that Delius first heard spirituals. In Jacksonville he also met Thomas Ward, who became his teacher in counterpoint and composition. Delius would later say that Ward's lessons were the only useful musical instruction he ever received. In late 1885 he left a caretaker in charge of the grove and moved to Danville, Virginia, where a newspaper advertisement announced that "Fritz Delius will begin at once giving instruction in Piano, Violin, Theory and Composition." From then on, his career was wholly musical.

## Florida in the Score

He went on to study at the conservatory in Leipzig, where he met the Norwegian composer Edvard Grieg. Like Ward before him, Grieg saw what Delius could become, and at a London dinner party in April 1888 he finally convinced Julius Delius that his son's future lay in music. The young composer moved to Paris, met the German painter Jelka Rosen in 1897, married her in 1903 and lived with her in the village of Grez-sur-Loing for the rest of his life. Yet the St. Johns kept surfacing in his work. His Florida Suite, written in 1887 and revised in 1889, drew on his months on the river, and Appalachia: Variations on an Old Slave Song, first written in 1896 and rewritten for voices and orchestra in 1902-03 before its 1904 premiere, was also inspired by his Florida years. Decades later, Herbert Stothart arranged music from Appalachia for the 1946 Hollywood film The Yearling.

## Music by Dictation

Success came with pieces such as Brigg Fair and On Hearing the First Cuckoo in Spring, championed above all by the conductor Thomas Beecham. But by the end of the First World War, Delius was showing symptoms of syphilis, probably contracted in the 1880s. By 1922 he walked with two sticks, and by 1928 he was paralysed and blind. That year a young English admirer, Eric Fenby, heard that Delius was trying to compose by dictating to Jelka and volunteered to help, unpaid. For five years Fenby took down new works from dictation, among them Cynara, A Late Lark, A Song of Summer and The Songs of Farewell, settings of Walt Whitman dedicated to Jelka. The two men also shared a love of cricket, following the 1930 England-Australia Test series closely and regaling a bemused Jelka with stories of their boyhood exploits in the game.

## The Last Music He Heard

In May 1934, with Delius close to death, Fenby played him Geoffrey Toye's recording of In a Summer Garden. According to Fenby, it was the last music Delius ever heard. He died at Grez on June 10, 1934, aged 72. Though an atheist, he had wished to lie "in some country churchyard in the south of England, where people could place wild flowers," and in May 1935 Jelka had him reburied at St Peter's Church in Limpsfield, Surrey. She died two days after the ceremony and was buried in the same grave. The Delius Trust, established after her death to oversee the editing and recording of his main works, operated largely under Beecham's direction.

## Back to the River

Florida did not forget him either. The Solano Grove house fell into decay after Delius left, but in 1961 Jacksonville University rescued it, moved it to its campus and restored it. A small memorial placed in 1992 by the university and the Delius Association of Florida marks Solano Grove, now called Solano Cove. From 1961 the association held an annual Delius Festival in Jacksonville, which one local history says ran until 2004, and the university's music faculty has awarded a Delius Composition Prize. His music has never been fashionable, and Fenby never pretended otherwise. "It is an art which will never enjoy an appeal to the many," he wrote, "but one which will always be loved, and dearly loved, by the few."

## Flight Context

Solano Grove lies on the St. Johns River in northeast Florida at approximately 29.8747°N, 81.5761°W, about 25 miles south of Jacksonville. The broad, slow St. Johns, flowing north toward Jacksonville, is the obvious visual reference; the grove site itself is marked only by a small memorial and is not visible from the air. A viewing altitude of 1,500–2,500 feet AGL gives a good view of the river's wide bends. Northeast Florida Regional Airport at St. Augustine (KSGJ) is about 13 nm to the east-northeast, and Palatka Municipal Airport (FAA identifier 28J) is about 14 nm to the south-southwest. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms; cooler months usually offer the clearest skies.
