# Newberry Six lynchings

In August 1916, after a constable was killed serving a warrant in Newberry, Florida, a posse and a mob killed six Black people, none of whom was ever tried, and no one was ever arrested for their deaths.

**Location:** 29.6513, -82.5980

**Categories:** Lynching deaths in Florida, Racially motivated violence against African Americans in Florida, Alachua County, Florida, 1916 murders in the United States, African-American history

## Introduction

At the intersection of Newberry Lane and Alachua County Route 235, about a mile from Newberry, Florida, there is no longer an oak tree. On the morning of August 18, 1916, there was, and a mob of about 200 people hanged five Black men and women from its branches. A sixth person had already been shot dead by a posse. None of the six had been tried for anything. Most were accused only of helping a relative or friend get away. Their names were Jim Dennis, Bert Dennis, Mary Dennis, Stella Young, Andrew McHenry and Josh Baskin.

## A Warrant for Hogs

It began late on August 17, when Newberry constable George Wynne went to serve a warrant on Boisey Long, an African American man accused of stealing hogs. Accounts differ about how the confrontation started and who fired first. What is known is that Long shot and killed Wynne and wounded L. G. Harris, the man who had come with him. Long escaped. Two days later he surrendered to the authorities.

In the meantime, the sheriff organized a posse, and Jim Dennis, a friend of Long's, was shot and killed. The sheriff said Dennis had resisted arrest; the Equal Justice Initiative says a mob took him from his home and shot him. Relatives and friends of Long were rounded up and jailed, accused of helping him escape: Bert Dennis and Mary Dennis, Long's wife Stella Young, and two friends of Dennis, Andrew McHenry and the Reverend Josh Baskin.

## The Oak a Mile From Town

On the morning of August 18, a mob of about 200 took those five people from the jail. They were brought to an oak tree one mile from Newberry and hanged from it together. With Jim Dennis, already dead at the posse's hands, they are remembered as the Newberry Six. Newspapers of the day called the killings "a lynching bee."

When word of the hangings reached the surrounding Black community, so many African Americans gathered that white residents said they feared a "race war." The tree itself no longer stands. It grew at what is now the intersection of Newberry Lane and Alachua County Route 235.

## Seven Minutes

No one was ever arrested for the six deaths. The Ocala Evening Star reported a rumor that the coroner's jury had ruled the victims died in freak accidents, such as running into a barbed-wire fence and bleeding to death, or falling out of a tree and choking or breaking their necks. Archivist Rebecca Fitzsimmons of the Matheson History Museum has noted that legal documents pertaining to the lynchings have never been found.

Boisey Long did go to court. He was tried on September 7, and an all-white jury deliberated for seven minutes before finding him guilty. Sentenced to hang, he was executed in the yard of the Alachua County jail on October 27, 1916. Even speaking out carried a price: a man who had voiced approval of Wynne's killing was forced to leave town.

## Counting the Dead

Six is the number in the name, but it may not be the full toll. According to historian Patricia Hilliard-Nunn, as many as nine people may have been lynched or shot to death during those days. And Newberry was not an isolated case: research presented by the Alachua County Historical Commission in 2018 concluded that at least 43 lynchings took place in Alachua County, and later accounts cite 46 between 1867 and 1926.

Three of the victims' headstones survive at the cemetery of Pleasant Plain United Methodist Church in Jonesville. In 2019 a marker was unveiled in remembrance of the lynchings and to acknowledge the Black men and women who were murdered, more than a century after the morning at the oak tree.

## Flight Context

Newberry lies at about 29.651 N, 82.598 W in western Alachua County, Florida, west of Gainesville along State Road 26. From 1,500-2,500 ft the small town is a compact grid surrounded by farmland and pasture; the lynching site was about a mile from town, at the intersection of Newberry Lane and County Road 235, and nothing of the tree remains. Nearby airports: Gainesville Regional (KGNV), about 17 nm east; Williston Municipal (KX60), about 19 nm south-southeast; the small Flying Ten airstrip near Archer (FAA identifier 0J8, no ICAO code) lies about 5 nm east-southeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
