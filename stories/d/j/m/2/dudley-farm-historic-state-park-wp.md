# Dudley Farm Historic State Park

Near Newberry, Florida, a family farm worked across three generations survives with its farmhouse, store and cane syrup mill still furnished, a National Historic Landmark where Cracker cattle still graze.

**Location:** 29.6563, -82.5407

**Categories:** State parks of Florida, Farm museums in Florida, National Historic Landmarks in Florida, Open-air museums in Florida, Parks in Alachua County, Florida

## Introduction

When Myrtle Dudley died in 1996, she was the youngest and last living member of the third generation of her family to live on the farm outside Newberry, Florida. The Florida Park Service had first acquired the farm in 1983, and with it came something rare: not a single restored house, but a complete working farm, its buildings still holding their original furnishings and equipment. Today Dudley Farm Historic State Park is a National Historic Landmark, a particularly fine and well-preserved example of how a Florida farm looked and worked from the mid-19th century to the mid-20th.

## Three Generations on the Newberry Road

The farm traces Florida agriculture from the late 1850s through the mid-1940s. After that its commercial life wound down, and a few third-generation family members kept farming only for their own needs into the 1990s. Visitors reach the historic farmstead by a trail from the visitor center, which leads onto a surviving segment of the original, unpaved Gainesville-Newberry Road.

The farm was added to the National Register of Historic Places on October 4, 2002, and designated a National Historic Landmark in January 2021.

## Everything Left in Place

The park covers about 327 acres and holds more than twenty historic buildings and structures. Among them are the family farmhouse, a kitchen outbuilding from the 1880s, a general store and post office, and a cane syrup complex, all still fitted out with their original furnishings and equipment.

The southern part of the park, on land the Dudleys never owned, serves as the entrance. The visitor center was originally a house built in Newberry in 1918. The gift shop is an exact duplicate of a commissary once found on a 19th-century Florida turpentine plantation. Old barns and farm equipment stand nearby, and a 0.3-mile nature walk winds through a Florida hammock.

## Cracker Cattle and Duncan Grapefruit

The farm is still stocked. It maintains a registered herd of Cracker cattle and, currently, three Florida Cracker horses. Chickens and turkeys scratch around the yard, specimens of the poultry breeds common on American farms in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; at one point the Dudley farm was a large local egg producer.

The orchard tells its own story. Several citrus trees, including two large heritage Duncan grapefruit trees, recall the large commercial presence of citrus in north Florida between the 1840s and 1890s. Other fruit crops the family grew are represented too: figs, bananas, peaches, pecans and black walnuts.

## Cane Boils and Corn Grinding

Dudley Farm is meant to be experienced, not just viewed. Throughout the year it hosts living history in both first and third person, cane boils, corn grinding, paint-ins, children's days, weddings, and ranger-led tours for visitors and school classes. Many of these events are organized with the Friends of Dudley Farm, a state-recognized citizen support organization that works closely with park staff.

The old buildings have found one more use as well: they have made the farm a popular spot for family and professional photography.

## Flight Context

Dudley Farm lies at about 29.656 N, 82.541 W along State Road 26 (West Newberry Road) between Gainesville and Newberry, in western Alachua County, Florida. From 1,500-2,500 ft look for a cluster of farm buildings among pasture and woodland beside SR 26, in otherwise open rural country. Nearby airports: Gainesville Regional (KGNV), about 14 nm east; Williston Municipal (KX60), about 18.5 nm south; the small Flying Ten airstrip near Archer (FAA identifier 0J8, no ICAO code) lies about 3 nm southeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
