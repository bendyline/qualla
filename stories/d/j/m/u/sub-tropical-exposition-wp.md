# Sub-Tropical Exposition

For three winter seasons beginning in 1888, Jacksonville showed off Florida's fruit, flowers and farm machinery in a sprawling exposition hall that President Grover Cleveland visited in 1888, until fever, fire and demolition left a reservoir in its place.

**Location:** 30.3355, -81.6552

**Categories:** Jacksonville, Florida, Demolished buildings and structures in Florida, Buildings and structures demolished in 1897

## Introduction

Where Jacksonville later built a reservoir, the city once staged a showcase of what Florida could grow. The Sub-Tropical Exposition opened in January 1888 and ran until May, filling an expansive exhibition building with fruit, flowers, farm crops and machinery. President Grover Cleveland visited that February. The exposition returned for two more seasons. Then yellow fever, a fire and the wrecking crew brought the story to an end, and within a decade of its grand opening the building was gone.

## A Winter Fair in Bamboo

The firm of Ellis & McClure designed the main exposition building, and the fair also featured a building made of bamboo. Visitors found fruits, trees, flowers, farm crops and grasses on display, along with a zoological collection, an aquarium, sugar mills and cotton gins, and a camp built in the Seminole style. Taken together, the displays put Florida's harvests and gardens in front of the visiting public.

## Strawberries in Print

The fair lived on in print as well. DaCosta Printing and Publishing produced a 47-page brochure covering railroad information, fruit crops, flowers and hotels, with even a note on strawberries. Harper's Weekly published illustrations from the fair in 1888, and the photographer O. Pierre Havens issued his cabinet photo of the bamboo building.

## Fever, Fire and a Reservoir

The exposition's run proved short. A yellow fever epidemic hurt tourism in the years after it opened, although the fair was held seasonally in the two years following 1888. In 1891 a fire damaged the expansive building, and it never reopened. It stood closed until 1897, when it was torn down. A reservoir took its place, a working piece of city infrastructure on the ground where Jacksonville had once put Florida's bounty on display.

## Flight Context

The site of the Sub-Tropical Exposition lies at 30.3355 N, 81.6552 W in Jacksonville, a short distance north of the downtown core, on ground that became part of the city's waterworks after the building came down. Nothing of the exposition remains visible, so navigate by the downtown high-rises nearby. Downtown Jacksonville is surrounded by busy civil and military airspace, so coordinate with Jacksonville approach. Naval Air Station Jacksonville (KNIP) is about 6 nm to the south-southwest, Jacksonville Executive at Craig (KCRG) about 7.3 nm to the east, and Jacksonville International (KJAX) about 9.7 nm to the north. A viewing altitude of 2,000 to 3,000 feet MSL, as cleared, works well. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
