# USS Orleck

Named for a lieutenant who went down with his ship at Salerno, the destroyer USS Orleck served two navies, rode out a hurricane on a Louisiana river and now rests as a museum on the Jacksonville riverfront.

**Location:** 30.3230, -81.6507

**Categories:** Gearing-class destroyers of the United States Navy, Ships built in Orange, Texas, Korean War destroyers of the United States, Vietnam War destroyers of the United States, Gearing-class destroyers of the Turkish Navy, Museum ships

## Introduction

In May 1945, the widow of a Navy lieutenant sponsored the launch of a new destroyer at Orange, Texas, a warship that would carry her late husband's name. More than eight decades later, USS Orleck (DD-886) is still afloat. The Gearing-class destroyer served the United States Navy from 1945 to 1982, spent years in the Turkish Navy as Yücetepe, broke loose in a hurricane while waiting to be towed, and finally found a home as a naval museum on the downtown Jacksonville riverfront.

## The Sailor Behind the Name

Joseph Orleck was born in Columbus, Ohio, on January 22, 1906, and enlisted in the Navy in 1924. He climbed through the enlisted ranks, was warranted a boatswain in 1938, and became an ensign in June 1942. During the invasion of Casablanca he earned the Navy and Marine Corps Medal for rescue work. On May 28, 1943, now a lieutenant, he took command of USS Nauset.

He had that command for barely three and a half months. On September 9, 1943, during the Salerno assault, a Luftwaffe bomber attacked his ship in the Gulf of Salerno. Orleck led the fight against fires and flooding in an effort to prevent the ship's total loss, and he went down with her. The Navy awarded him the Navy Cross posthumously for those efforts.

## Thirty-Seven Years in Navy Gray

The Consolidated Steel Corporation laid down the destroyer at Orange, Texas, on November 28, 1944. She was launched on May 12, 1945, with Lieutenant Orleck's widow as sponsor, and commissioned that September 15.

Her career spanned two wars and the early space age. During the Korean War she operated with the Seventh Fleet in support of United Nations forces. In 1962 she went through an extensive FRAM I overhaul, and on June 7, 1965, she assisted in the recovery of the Gemini IV space capsule alongside the carrier Wasp, the prime recovery ship. In the Vietnam War she served as plane guard for aircraft carriers on Yankee Station in the Gulf of Tonkin, took part in Operation Sea Dragon, patrolled on search-and-rescue duty, and fired naval gunfire support missions. In October 1982, after 37 years in American service, she was sold to Turkey.

## A Second Flag and a Long Wait

The Turkish Navy renamed her Yücetepe (D 345). When her Turkish service finally ended, the Turkish government transferred the ship to the Southeast Texas War Memorial and Heritage Foundation, which berthed her as a museum ship at Orange, Texas, the same town where she had been built. The Orleck Foundation later moved her to the Calcasieu River at Lake Charles, Louisiana, where she arrived on May 20, 2010.

In February 2020, a move to Jacksonville was confirmed. The ship closed to visitors on March 1, 2020, to prepare for a Coast Guard inspection ahead of the tow, but the COVID-19 pandemic delayed the trip. Then Hurricane Laura struck. The old destroyer broke loose from her mooring on the Calcasieu, drifted about a mile downstream and ran aground, suffering some damage.

## Home on the St. Johns

In August 2021, the Jacksonville City Council voted unanimously to let the ship moor downtown. That December she was towed from Lake Charles to Port Arthur, Texas, for drydocking, where she proved to be in much better shape than expected; the restoration work done there extended her life by 15 years. Ownership passed officially to the museum association on January 11, 2022.

Having passed every Coast Guard inspection, Orleck left Port Arthur under tow on March 17, 2022, and reached Jacksonville nine days later, on March 26. She officially opened to visitors on October 5, 2022, and served as a naval museum on the downtown riverfront until April 3, 2023, when she moved to her permanent berth at the Shipyards West.

## Flight Context

USS Orleck is moored near 30.323°N, 81.651°W on the downtown Jacksonville riverfront of the St. Johns River, about half a mile northeast of Friendship Fountain. A slow pass at 1,000–1,500 feet shows the destroyer's gray hull moored along the riverfront. Nearby airports: Jacksonville Naval Air Station (Towers Field, KNIP, military) about 6 miles south-southwest, Jacksonville Executive at Craig Airport (KCRG) about 8 miles east, and Jacksonville International Airport (KJAX) about 12 miles north. Jacksonville International's busy airspace lies just to the north.
