# Carrabelle, Florida

At the mouth of the Carrabelle River, where the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway begins, a fishing town named for a founder of the Grange rebuilt after a hurricane left only a handful of homes standing, trained soldiers for a world war and made a phone booth its police station.

**Location:** 29.8539, -84.6658

## Introduction

Carrabelle's police headquarters once fit inside a telephone booth. On March 10, 1963, the police phone was moved into an old booth on U.S. 98, which stopped unauthorized long-distance calls on it and meant the officer on duty would no longer get drenched answering calls in the rain. The little glass box went on to appear on The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson and in Ripley's Believe It or Not as the "World's Smallest Police Station." It is a fitting kind of fame for a small, practical fishing town at the mouth of the Carrabelle River, where the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway reaches its eastern end.

## Named for a Niece

Long before the town existed, travelers and raiders knew this shore. In 1528 the Spanish expedition of Pánfilo de Narváez passed through the area, and in the late 17th century Carrabelle, Dog Island and St. George Island served as staging points for raids on local ports, including San Marcos de Apalache in 1677 and 1682. In 1875 the explorer Nathaniel Holmes Bishop of Medford, Massachusetts, navigated the Crooked River through the lowlands east to the Ochlockonee. A year later another New Englander, Oliver Hudson Kelley, founded the town and named it "Rio Carrabella" after his niece, Caroline Hall. The name honored a remarkable woman. Kelley was a key founder of the National Grange, the farmers' organization, and Hall was one of its eight founders; she also served as the new town's first postmaster. The Florida Legislature chartered Carrabelle as a city in May 1893.

## Nine Houses and a Lighthouse

On August 1, 1899, the second hurricane of the season struck, almost destroying Carrabelle and leaving, by one count, just nine homes standing; other accounts put the survivors at no more than about twenty. The town rebuilt on the same exposed stretch of coast, where summers are hot and humid and storms arrive off the Gulf. Four years before that hurricane, in 1895, the Crooked River Lighthouse had risen about two miles west of town. At 103 feet, it is the tallest lighthouse on the Forgotten Coast, and the Carrabelle Lighthouse Association now runs the Keeper's House Museum at its foot. Inland sprawls Tate's Hell State Forest, while offshore the waterway leads out toward Dog Island and St. George Sound.

## Practice Beaches for a World War

During World War II, the quiet shoreline became a rehearsal stage. Camp Gordon Johnston, the Army's major amphibious training center, operated here from 1942 until 1946, and soldiers stormed Dog Island and St. George Island in practice landings. The training was famously hard, and the columnist Walter Winchell is said to have called the camp the "Alcatraz of the Army." It was named after Colonel Gordon Johnston, an American soldier who served in the Spanish-American War, the Philippine-American War and World War I. Its story is kept today at the Camp Gordon Johnston World War II Museum at 1873 Highway 98 West, across the street from Carrabelle Public Beach. Admission is free.

## A Hall of Famer's Hometown

John Jordan "Buck" O'Neil was born in Carrabelle on November 13, 1911. He grew up to become a Negro league baseball player and coach for the Kansas City Monarchs, in an era when segregation barred Black players from the major leagues, and he was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 2022. The Carrabelle History Museum, which opened in April 2009 in a former city hall built in 1938, has told his story in an exhibit on O'Neil and Black baseball. Another local name reaches into natural history: Jack Rudloe, the naturalist who cofounded the Gulf Specimen Marine Laboratory in nearby Panacea.

## Small Town, Big Waterway

Carrabelle counted 2,606 residents in the 2020 census. Its population had jumped from 1,303 in 2000 to 2,778 in 2010 because the city limits expanded northeast to take in Franklin Correctional Institution, a state prison. Students attend the Franklin County K-12 School in Eastpoint, built in 2008. The famous phone booth has had a rough life: vandals have ripped out its phones and shot holes in its glass, a pickup truck knocked it over, and Hurricane Kate damaged it. A replica now stands on U.S. 98 across from the chamber of commerce. The calendar still makes room for a Boat Parade of Lights and the Carrabelle Riverfront Festival, fitting celebrations for an old deepwater fishing village that has always faced the water where its river meets the Gulf.

## Flight Context

Coordinates: 29.8539°N, 84.6658°W, at the mouth of the Carrabelle River on the Florida Panhandle's Gulf coast, east of Apalachicola. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet, follow U.S. 98 along the shore; the 103-foot Crooked River Lighthouse stands about two miles west of town, and Dog Island lies offshore across St. George Sound. Nearby airports: Carrabelle-Thompson Airport (FAA identifier X13, no ICAO code), about two nautical miles west-southwest of town; Apalachicola Regional (KAAF); and Tallahassee International (KTLH) to the northeast. Summers are hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms and hurricane season to watch.
