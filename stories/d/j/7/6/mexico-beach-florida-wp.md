# Mexico Beach, Florida

A small Panhandle beach town took the direct hit of Hurricane Michael, the first Category 5 to strike the United States in 26 years, and has been rebuilding ever since.

**Location:** 29.9414, -85.4064

**Categories:** Cities in Bay County, Florida, Populated coastal places in Florida on the Gulf of Mexico, Hurricane Michael, Natural disasters, Beach towns

## Introduction

Mexico Beach has had to start over before. In 1985 a hurricane came ashore right next to the town. But nothing in its history compared with the afternoon of October 10, 2018, when Hurricane Michael came ashore nearby. Afterward, Brock Long, the administrator of the Federal Emergency Management Agency, called Mexico Beach "probably ground zero." For this small Gulf city on the Florida Panhandle, 25 miles southeast of Panama City, life now divides into before and after that day.

## A Town Built for the Shore

Mexico Beach began in 1946, when real estate development started along this stretch of Bay County's coast, and it was incorporated as a city in 1967. It stayed small. At the 2010 census it counted 1,072 residents but 1,852 housing units, and its households averaged fewer than two people. Although it lies in Bay County, its schooling runs through neighboring Gulf County: Bay District Schools pays the Gulf County district tuition for Mexico Beach residents. Storms were part of life here long before 2018. In late November 1985, Hurricane Kate came ashore near Mexico Beach as a Category 2 storm, and its winds and tides destroyed beachfront homes and businesses.

## Just Before One O'Clock

Michael made landfall near Mexico Beach at about 12:30 p.m. local time, with maximum sustained winds of 160 miles per hour. It was the first Category 5 hurricane to reach the United States since Andrew in 1992, and it ranks as the third-strongest landfalling storm in the continental United States by pressure and the fourth-strongest by wind speed; its minimum central pressure at landfall was 919 millibars. Water rose to 14 feet above ground level in the town. Of the city's 1,692 buildings, 1,584 were damaged and 809 destroyed. The elementary school and city hall were devastated, the pier washed away and the water tower came down. A state report estimated that about 285 people in Mexico Beach had declined to evacuate, though some may have left before the peak. Three people from Mexico Beach were among those who died. Nearby Tyndall Air Force Base was also badly damaged.

## The Long Way Back

In the first days after the storm, Mayor Al Cathey warned residents it might be months before electricity, water and plumbing returned. Rebuilding followed, paid for with federal aid and the city's own money, and a town that FEMA's administrator had called ground zero began to take shape again. The scars show in the census: Mexico Beach counted 916 residents in 2020, down from 1,072 a decade earlier. Yet the wider county's population had rebounded by 2024, and the town has been rebuilding. Five years after the storm it still lacked a permanent fire station and police station, and in early 2026 the city was still at the design stage for replacing them and its lost civic center, with the fire department working out of the public works building. In September 2026 it was also still working through environmental and funding hurdles to rebuild the Al Cathey Municipal Pier that Michael destroyed. Some things the storm could not change. Summers here are still hot and humid, winters generally mild, and the Gulf still rolls up onto the same sand.

## Flight Context

Mexico Beach lies at about 29.941 N, 85.406 W on the Gulf shore of the Florida Panhandle, about 21 miles (18 nm) southeast of Panama City. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet, follow the coastal highway and the thin line of rebuilt homes along the beach; St. Joseph Bay and Port St. Joe lie to the southeast. Tyndall Air Force Base (KPAM) is about 12 nm northwest, so stay clear of its restricted and military airspace. Costin Airport at Port St. Joe (FAA identifier A51, no ICAO code) lies to the southeast, near the port. Hurricane season runs through late fall here, and summer thunderstorms are frequent.
