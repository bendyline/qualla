# Bottle Creek Indian Mounds

Hidden on a swampy island deep in the Mobile-Tensaw Delta, eighteen or more earthen mounds mark the largest mound complex on the northern Gulf coastal plain, a ceremonial and trading center of the Pensacola culture reachable, then and now, only by water.

**Location:** 31.0088, -87.9406

## Introduction

Visitors cannot simply drive to Bottle Creek. To reach it you go by water, threading the branches and bayous of the Mobile-Tensaw River Delta until a low, swampy island rises out of the cypress. For the people who built it, that was never an obstacle. Between 1250 and 1550, the Pensacola culture, a regional variant of the Mississippian culture, raised eighteen or more earthen platform mounds here and made this island what the Alabama Historical Commission calls the largest mound complex on the northern Gulf coastal plain. They came and went by dugout canoe, the primary way of getting around the delta.

## Eighteen Mounds on Mound Island

The site sits on Mound Island, north of present-day Mobile, Alabama. Of its eighteen or more platform mounds, the tallest rises roughly 52 feet above the swamp by the Alabama Historical Commission's measure, though some sources say 45. Five of them are arranged around a central plaza, the heart of the community. From about 1250 until 1550, this was described as the largest Mississippian chiefdom on the north-central Gulf Coast. It was a ceremonial center for the Pensacola people and also a social, political, religious and trade center for the whole Mobile Delta region and the central Gulf Coast. Archaeologists know the place by its site number, 1BA2. To the Pensacola people it was a center of ceremony, power and exchange.

## A Gateway in the Swamp

Seen on a modern map, Bottle Creek looks remote. Seen from a canoe, it looks central. The site lies within the Mobile-Tensaw Delta, downstream of the confluence of the Tombigbee and Alabama rivers, where the streams break into a maze of branches and bayous on their way to the head of Mobile Bay. Walking in would have been difficult, but for people whose primary mode of transportation was the dugout canoe, the island was easy to reach. That made Bottle Creek a focal point for interaction with other Mississippian culture areas, both along the coast and in the interior of the Southeast. Despite its seemingly remote location, it served as a gateway to Pensacola society, a meeting place where the rivers of the interior met the Gulf.

## Protected by Cypress

The swamp that once connected Bottle Creek to the world later hid it. The site was first mapped in the 1880s, but its position in the middle of the delta, completely surrounded by swamp and screened by immense cypress trees, kept it hard to reach. That isolation spared it most of the plundering that damaged similar sites elsewhere in the Southeast. Professional investigation began in 1932, when David L. DeJarnette of the Alabama Museum of Natural History started work to learn whether Bottle Creek had a cultural relationship with Moundville, which lies to the north along a connecting river system. The site was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974 and designated a National Historic Landmark on March 10, 1995. It is owned by the state of Alabama and administered by the Alabama Historical Commission.

## Arriving the Old Way

Visitors still reach Bottle Creek much as its builders did, on the water. Because of its remote location, the site is reached only by boat. The Alabama Historical Commission offers seasonal tours and advises against visiting on your own, and Historic Blakeley State Park runs guided excursions that board at Live Oak Landing near Stockton, Alabama. A recent excursion, listed for January 2026, ran about three and a half hours and included a hike of roughly half a mile at the site, and trips can be rescheduled when high water floods Mound Island. Older accounts describe guided trips of about four hours by boat, or six to eight hours by canoe or kayak, so check the current schedule before planning a visit. Standing among the mounds, with swamp and water on every side, it becomes clear how the island could be both hidden and at the center of things.

## Flight Context

Bottle Creek Indian Mounds lie at approximately 31.009 N, 87.941 W on Mound Island in the Mobile-Tensaw River Delta, north of Mobile, Alabama. From 2,000-3,000 ft MSL, the delta is a vast green maze of rivers, bayous and swamp forest; the mounds are hidden beneath trees and very hard to spot, so enjoy the setting of channels winding south toward the head of Mobile Bay. Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown) is about 24 nm south-southwest and Mobile Regional (KMOB) about 25 nm southwest. H.L. Sonny Callahan Airport (KCQF) in Fairhope lies about 33 nm south. Navy outlying training fields are scattered across Baldwin County to the south-southeast, so watch for training traffic. The delta is prone to morning fog and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
