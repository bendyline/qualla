# Harbour Town Light

Ridiculed by locals while it was going up in 1969, the privately built, red-and-white striped Harbour Town Lighthouse became Hilton Head Island's best-known symbol and the backdrop to one of golf's most recognizable finishing holes.

**Location:** 32.1386, -80.8128

**Categories:** Lighthouses in South Carolina, Buildings and structures in Beaufort County, South Carolina, Tourist attractions in Beaufort County, South Carolina, Hilton Head Island, South Carolina

## Introduction

Hilton Head Island's most famous lighthouse was never built by a government, and while it was going up, the people who lived nearby laughed at it. Charles Fraser started the tower at Harbour Town Marina in 1969 and finished it in 1970, a privately built light, and through the planning and construction local residents ridiculed the idea. Then it was finished, and the story flipped. The Harbour Town Lighthouse became instantly popular, and today it is widely seen as the most recognizable symbol of both Hilton Head Island and Sea Pines Resort.

## Laughed At, Then Loved

The tower was built as part of something larger: the Harbour Town Marina and the Sea Pines Plantation development that surrounds it. It is still a working light, though a private one. The lighthouse is a privately operated aid to navigation, and its white light flashes over the marina, since March 2022 through a replica fourth-order Fresnel lens. What the early skeptics missed was how quickly a striped tower could give a new waterfront an identity. The ridicule faded once the light stood finished at the water's edge, and the building people had scoffed at became the image the whole island is known by. Today it also serves as a tourist attraction and a wedding venue, with ceremonies staged beside the marina. Reaching it takes a small fee to enter the plantation and another to go into the lighthouse itself.

## Stripes Over Plywood

Up close, the tower reveals its modern recipe. It rises roughly 90 feet from a concrete foundation, an octagonal column finished in stucco laid over metal lath and plywood. Alternating red and white bands wrap the shaft, and those stripes serve as its daymark, the pattern that lets a mariner identify a lighthouse by daylight. Near the top, a red observation deck circles the tower just beneath the lantern. Climbing is part of the visit. A museum inside displays historic photographs of Harbour Town, and gift shops wait at both the bottom and the top of the light, so the reward for the stairs is a view over the marina and a souvenir counter at the same time. For a building that began as a local joke, it has settled comfortably into the role of attraction.

## The Backdrop to the Eighteenth

Most people have seen the lighthouse without ever setting foot on Hilton Head, thanks to golf. Harbour Town Golf Links, designed by Pete Dye in 1967 with help from Jack Nicklaus, has hosted the Heritage on the PGA Tour since 1969, usually in mid-April, the week after the Masters. The annually televised tournament has carried the lighthouse's fame to millions of golf fans around the world. The reason is the course's final hole. The eighteenth heads northward along Calibogue Sound, whose water guards its entire left side, while out-of-bounds stakes line the right, and because the sound rises and falls with the tides, the waterline shifts through the day. Behind the green stands the striped lighthouse, often a target for players' approach shots. Together they have made the eighteenth one of the most popular and recognizable finishing holes in golf.

## Flight Context

Harbour Town Light stands at about 32.1386 N, 80.8128 W on the western shore of Hilton Head Island, beside Harbour Town Marina and the finishing holes of Harbour Town Golf Links, which run along Calibogue Sound. Look for the red-and-white striped tower at the marina's edge; from 1,500 to 2,000 feet the harbor basin and the fairways beside the sound are easy to pick out. The Stoney-Baynard ruins lie a little over half a nautical mile to the south. Hilton Head Airport (KHXD) is about 8 nm northeast, Beaufort Executive Airport (KARW) about 19 nm north-northeast, and Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) about 20 nm west. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms along the coast.
