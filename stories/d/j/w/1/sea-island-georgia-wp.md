# Sea Island, Georgia

A young barrier island in Georgia's Golden Isles became a private resort where presidents plant oak trees, where Eugene O'Neill wrote a play, and where the leaders of the G8 met behind a single causeway in 2004.

**Location:** 31.2002, -81.3332

**Categories:** Georgia (U.S. state) Sea Islands, Islands of Glynn County, Georgia, Private islands of the United States, Resorts, Walker Cup venues, G8 summits

## Introduction

In 1928, visiting for the Christmas holidays, President Calvin Coolidge planted a live oak on Sea Island, Georgia. He started something. Over the decades that followed, a procession of distinguished guests added trees of their own: Presidents Gerald Ford, Jimmy Carter, George H. W. Bush and Bill Clinton, Queen Juliana of the Netherlands, and Prime Ministers Margaret Thatcher and Tony Blair. In 2004 President George W. Bush planted one during a summit of world leaders. Only a single tree in the collection is not a live oak. In 1931 the island's developer, Howard Coffin, planted an English oak acorn from General Oglethorpe's estate in England, to honor the founder of the Georgia colony. It is a fitting emblem for a privately owned resort island that has spent nearly a century hosting the powerful and the famous.

## Fifth Creek Island

Geologically, Sea Island is young. Like many of Georgia's easternmost barrier islands, it formed about 5,000 years ago, after the last major ice age, and it is far younger than the Pleistocene-age islands just to its west. Little is known about early human activity here, and most of its recorded history dates from the twentieth century. Its original English name was Fifth Creek Island. The first known white settler was Captain James Mackay, who served under Oglethorpe at Fort Frederica, and later owners, a series of St. Simons Island planters, used the land mainly for grazing. The island lies along the Atlantic just east of St. Simons Island, reached by a causeway, about 60 miles from both Jacksonville, Florida, and Savannah. It belongs to the Golden Isles of Georgia and to the long chain of Sea Islands that stretches along the coasts of South Carolina, Georgia and northern Florida.

## The Little Friendly Hotel

The automobile magnate Howard Coffin bought the island in 1926. Two years later, on the advice of friends who saw promise in oceanfront property, he opened the Cloister Hotel, soon nicknamed the "Little Friendly Hotel," and handed its management to his cousin, Alfred W. Jones, who took over fully when Coffin died in 1937. The Cloister succeeded from the start. Coolidge's Christmas visit brought it national attention, and it marketed itself as a quiet, worry-free alternative to Florida's high-energy resorts. Businessmen, politicians and celebrities came, among them New York Mayor Jimmy Walker, Edsel Ford, John D. Rockefeller Jr. and Eddie Rickenbacker. Coffin wanted residents too, so he brought in electricity, water and telephone service, improved the causeways, and encouraged guests to build "cottages." Eugene O'Neill lived there in the early 1930s, at a house called Casa Genotta, and is said to have written his play Ah, Wilderness! on the island. In 1949 Sarah Churchill married the photographer Anthony Beauchamp on the island, and among its thousands of honeymooners were George and Barbara Bush.

## Summit Behind a Causeway

From June 8 to 10, 2004, President George W. Bush brought the Group of Eight to Sea Island. Canada's Paul Martin, France's Jacques Chirac, Germany's Gerhard Schroeder, Italy's Silvio Berlusconi, Japan's Junichiro Koizumi, Russia's Vladimir Putin and Britain's Tony Blair attended, along with European Commission President Romano Prodi and other invited heads of state. The island was chosen partly because its isolation made the extensive security arrangements easier. The Department of Homeland Security designated the gathering a National Special Security Event, access was tightly controlled, and the press was headquartered in Savannah, some 60 miles north. Protests were marginalized, in part because the venue was so hard to reach. Behind those barriers, the leaders discussed the problems facing developing countries in Africa and elsewhere, from peacekeeping and HIV/AIDS vaccine development to famine relief, debt reduction and the eradication of polio.

## Rebuilt, Then Nearly Undone

The original Cloister was eventually demolished and replaced over three years with a much larger building inspired by it, and the beach club and spa were upgraded. Just two years after the grand reopening, the financial panic of 2008 and the Great Recession struck from several directions. Expected property sales in a new upscale development never materialized, resort visits declined, and home purchases stalled. Cloister revenue fell 31 percent from 2007 to 2009, and about a quarter of the workforce was laid off. After a restructured loan package from Synovus and Bank of America in 2009, the Sea Island Company defaulted again in 2010 and filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. The company was acquired by a consortium of investors, who formed Sea Island Acquisitions to turn the business around. In April 2016 they completed a $40 million Garden Wing with 63 rooms, and in June 2016 the Anschutz family of Denver, headed by Philip Anschutz, bought out the other three investors.

## Resort and Fairways Today

Today the island holds two resorts with limited public access and a gated community of around 500 single-family homes. The Cloister, on the island's southwestern side along the Black Banks River, has 200 rooms, a spa and The Georgian Rooms, a restaurant with a long run of Forbes Five-Star ratings. The oceanfront Beach Club, a short distance away along Sea Island Drive, has several pools and even a bowling alley. Golf grew into a centerpiece after 2001, when Ocean Forest Golf Club hosted the Walker Cup, opened by George H. W. Bush, and later the annual Jones Cup Invitational. With St. Simons Island resident Davis Love III as host, the PGA Tour event now known as the RSM Classic, first played as the McGladrey Classic, was founded on the Plantation and Seaside courses.

## Flight Context

Sea Island lies at about 31.2002 N, 81.3332 W on the Atlantic coast of Georgia, just east of St. Simons Island and linked to it by a causeway. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet, look for the long ocean beach on the east side, the Cloister along the Black Banks River on the southwest side, and the golf courses nearby. The island is privately owned and largely gated, so enjoy it from the air. McKinnon St. Simons Island Airport (KSSI) is about 4 nm to the southwest, Brunswick Golden Isles Airport (KBQK) about 8 nm west-northwest, and Jekyll Island Airport (FAA identifier 09J, no ICAO code) about 9 nm south-southwest. Coastal summers bring haze and afternoon thunderstorms; watch for sea fog in the cooler months.
