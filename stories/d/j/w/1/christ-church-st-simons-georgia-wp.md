# Christ Church (St. Simons, Georgia)

Beside Fort Frederica on St. Simons Island, an Episcopal parish that began with Charles Wesley's services in 1736 once paid for repairs with honey from its steeple, and its 1884 church was raised by Anson Dodge Jr. as a memorial to his wife.

**Location:** 31.2201, -81.3874

**Categories:** Episcopal church buildings in Georgia (U.S. state), 1736 establishments in the Thirteen Colonies, Religious organizations established in the 1730s, St. Simons, Georgia

## Introduction

In 1840, the people of Christ Church on St. Simons Island found bees in their steeple. The parishioners put the hive to work, collecting and selling the honey to pay for repairs the building badly needed. It is a charming footnote in a parish history that stretches back more than a century before it, to the founding of a fort, a chaplain named Wesley and services held in a building of tabby. Christ Church still stands beside Fort Frederica National Monument, though the building worshippers enter today is the one a husband raised in 1884 in memory of his wife, on the site of the church the Civil War left damaged beyond repair.

## Wesley at Frederica

On February 15, 1736, James Oglethorpe, founder of the Province of Georgia, established the town and fort of Frederica on St. Simons Island. About a month later came the Reverend Charles Wesley, a Church of England priest serving as Oglethorpe's chaplain and brother of John Wesley, then the priest at Christ Church in Savannah. Charles held services in a tabby structure on the island, ministering as a missionary until July of that year. His stay was short, but it planted a congregation. After Wesley left, clergymen from the society now known as the United Society Partners in the Gospel served the island through the rest of the 1700s, keeping a church presence alive on St. Simons long before there was a permanent building to hold it.

## A Parish Takes Shape

The state legislature incorporated the parish on December 22, 1808, with William Best as its first rector. In 1810 Edmund Matthews became rector, a post he held until 1827, and his tenure brought two milestones. In 1820 the first church building rose on the site, and in 1823 the parish joined Christ Church in Savannah and Saint Paul's Church in Augusta as one of the first to form the Episcopal Diocese of Georgia. In 1831 Christ Church hosted its first convention for the diocese, and five years later the congregation celebrated the centennial of the island's first religious service. Then came the bees in 1840, and with them the honey that helped keep the building sound.

## Ruin and a Widower's Gift

In the Civil War, Union troops occupied the island and the little church, and when families returned they found it damaged beyond repair. For years afterward there was no regular minister; lay preachers and the occasional visiting clergyman kept services going until 1879, when Anson Greene Phelps Dodge Jr. first came to the island and began reorganizing the parish; he was named its rector in 1884. Dodge's connection to the church was deeply personal. His wife Ellen had died in India in 1883, and parish accounts say she was buried on the church grounds. In 1884 he had the church rebuilt on the site of the old one as a memorial to her. That building still stands and remains the congregation's place of worship. When Dodge died in 1898, he left an endowment to the diocese that funded much of its missionary work at the time, so his generosity left a mark well beyond this island.

## St. Ignatius and Anna Alexander

Dodge's work reached beyond his own congregation. In 1886 he established St. Ignatius as an outreach to people on the island who had been freed from slavery, a community whose members had endured bondage within living memory. Some accounts call Anna Alexander its deaconess, though her best-documented ministry was at Good Shepherd in Pennick, west of Brunswick. She became the first Black deaconess in the Episcopal Church. The church eventually honored that service. The Diocese of Georgia came to honor her as a saint, and in 2018 General Convention added her to the Episcopal Church's calendar of saints. Her story sits beside Wesley's in the Episcopal history of this coast, and it deserves equal weight: one the brief ministry of a colonial chaplain, the other the service of the first Black woman the Episcopal Church made a deaconess.

## Flight Context

Christ Church stands on St. Simons Island, Georgia, at 31.2201N, 81.3874W, immediately adjacent to Fort Frederica National Monument, whose grounds make the easiest reference from the air. A slow pass at 1,500-2,000 feet shows the church site beside the fort. St. Simons Island Airport (KSSI) is only about 4 nm south, and Brunswick Golden Isles Airport (KBQK) about 4.7 nm west-northwest. Jekyll Island Airport (FAA code 09J) lies about 9 nm south-southwest. Jacksonville International (KJAX) is about 46 nm south-southwest. Coastal fog on winter mornings and summer afternoon thunderstorms are the main weather concerns.
