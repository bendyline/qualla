# Sapelo Island dock collapse

In October 2024, a ferry gangway on Georgia's Sapelo Island gave way at the end of a celebration of the island's Gullah-Geechee community, and seven people died.

**Location:** 31.4180, -81.2962

## Introduction

By late afternoon on October 19, 2024, Cultural Day on Sapelo Island was winding down and the ferry was waiting. More than 200 people had gathered on this Georgia barrier island for the annual celebration of its Gullah-Geechee community, and many of them were lining up to go home. Around 4 p.m., the aluminum gangway that led down to the boat crumpled into the water. A day meant to honor survival and heritage ended with seven people dead.

## An Island Reached Only by Water

Sapelo has no bridge. The island is accessible only by boat, and the primary ferry runs from the Sapelo Island Visitors Center in McIntosh County, a crossing that takes roughly 20 to 30 minutes. About 97 percent of the land belongs to the state of Georgia and is managed by the Department of Natural Resources. The island is also home to Hog Hammock, where many residents are Gullah-Geechee - specifically Saltwater Geechees - who descend from West Africans enslaved and brought to the island in the 1700s and 1800s to work its plantations. By a 2009 estimate, only about 47 full-time Gullah-Geechee residents remained. Every third Saturday in October, the community's cultural and revitalization society hosts Cultural Day, drawing descendants and visitors back across the water. For nearly everyone who came, the ferry dock was both the way in and the only way out.

## The Ramp Between the Docks

The gangway was a ramp connecting two docks, and on that afternoon it was crowded with people waiting to board. When it failed, at least twenty of them fell into the water, and some eyewitnesses put the number higher. Local reporting noted that many of those on the ramp were elderly, some using walkers. What happened next came from the crowd itself. Festival-goers jumped in and pulled people out by hand, while the ferry's crew threw life preservers to anyone they could reach. Seven people died. Eight more were taken to hospitals, at least six of them initially reported in critical condition. Behind each of those numbers was someone who had come to celebrate alongside family and neighbors, and who had simply been waiting for the boat home.

## Searching, and Asking Why

Rescue crews converged on the island. Personnel from the Georgia Department of Natural Resources, the United States Coast Guard and the McIntosh County Fire Department began searching for anyone still in the water, and several of those recovered were taken to regional hospitals. That same evening, a spokesperson for the Georgia Bureau of Investigation said the Department of Natural Resources - the agency that runs the ferry - was leading the investigation into what had gone wrong. For a community whose children ride the ferry to reach school on the mainland, the dock was no minor piece of infrastructure. Afterward, a small but telling change appeared at the ferry docks: within days, signs limiting how many people could use the gangway at once, eight by one account, were posted at the mainland Meridian dock. The Attorney General hired an independent engineering firm to investigate, and in December 2024 the state installed two new aluminum gangways, one at the island's Marsh Landing dock where the collapse happened and one at Meridian as a precaution. In June 2025, relatives of the seven who died and dozens of survivors filed suit in Gwinnett County State Court against four private firms that designed, built and fabricated the dock and walkway for the state, alleging poor welding, inadequate reinforcement and failure to follow the design plans. The state agency was not named, and at that point the state's investigations into the cause were still open.

## Words From Afar

Messages of grief arrived from far beyond McIntosh County. President Joe Biden and First Lady Jill Biden said they were heartbroken, and their statement captured the cruel shape of the day: "What should have been a joyous celebration of Gullah-Geechee culture and history instead turned into tragedy and devastation." Georgia Governor Brian Kemp and his wife, Marty Kemp, asked Georgians to pray "for those lost, for those still in harm's way, and for their families." On an island where the Gullah-Geechee story reaches back to the 1700s, Cultural Day exists to gather people together. In 2024, the gathering became a vigil as well.

## Flight Context

The ferry dock sits on the western side of Sapelo Island at roughly 31.418 N, 81.296 W, the side of the island bordered by the Sapelo Island National Estuarine Research Reserve and facing the McIntosh County mainland. From 2,000-3,000 feet the barrier island stands out clearly from the mainland across the water; the Hog Hammock community lies about 1.8 nm east-northeast of the dock, and the Sapelo Island lighthouse stands near the island's southern tip, about 1.7 nm south-southeast. The nearest airstrip is Sapelo Island Airport (FAA identifier 08GA), under a mile northeast; it is a private-use, state-owned turf strip that requires permission before landing. For public airports, Brunswick Golden Isles Airport (KBQK) is about 13 nm southwest and St. Simons Island Airport (KSSI) about 17 nm south-southwest. The coast here is low and flat, so haze over the marshes matters more than terrain.
