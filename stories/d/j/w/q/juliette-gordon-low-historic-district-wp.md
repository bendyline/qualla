# Juliette Gordon Low Historic District

Three Savannah buildings, a Federal-style birthplace, a Greek Revival mansion guarded by iron lions and a remodeled carriage house, trace how Juliette Gordon Low brought Girl Guides home to America in 1912.

**Location:** 32.0771, -81.0924

**Categories:** National Historic Landmarks in Savannah, Georgia, Girl Scouts of the USA, Historic house museums in Georgia (U.S. state), Scouting museums in the United States, Historic districts on the National Register of Historic Places in Georgia (U.S. state)

## Introduction

The first headquarters of the Girl Scouts was built for carriages. Around 1849, a stuccoed brick carriage house and servants' quarters went up on the Andrew Low House property in Savannah, and in 1912 Juliette Gordon Low remodeled it into a meeting place for girls. She closed in the carriage doors facing Drayton Street, turned two doorways into windows and reworked the interior. That modest building, together with the mansion in front of it and the house nearby where Low was born, makes up the Juliette Gordon Low Historic District, three places that together tell how a Savannah woman set the Girl Scouts of the USA in motion.

## Born on Oglethorpe Avenue

Juliette Gordon was born in Savannah on October 31, 1860, in the house at 10 East Oglethorpe Avenue now known as the Wayne–Gordon House. Built between about 1818 and 1821 and attributed to the architect William Jay, it is a Federal-style residence of two stories over a raised basement, with two curved bay windows at the back, plaster cornices and ceiling medallions, and marble fireplaces. In 1886 she married William Mackay Low. When his father died, William inherited the family's Savannah mansion, and the couple kept it as their American residence. Their married life took them to England, and it was there, still living abroad after her husband's death in 1905, that Juliette Low met Robert Baden-Powell, founder of the Boy Scouts, and his sister Agnes, who oversaw the Girl Guides.

## Iron Lions on Abercorn Street

The mansion William inherited, the Andrew Low House at 329 Abercorn Street, was designed by John Norris and built in the late 1840s. It greets visitors with an entry staircase of brown sandstone flanked by two cast-iron lions. Pilasters with capitals decorated with acanthus and lotus leaves frame the door. Along the south side, a cast-iron balcony with guilloche patterns and a pagoda-style roof runs the length of the parlor's six windows, and a three-story porch on stuccoed posts rises at the back. Inside, a passion flower medallion decorates the central hall ceiling, the doorknobs are sterling silver, and pediments and pilasters echo the Tower of the Winds motif of the exterior. The parlors, dining room and library have mantels of black Egyptian marble. Upstairs, beside five bedrooms, is an original bathing room with running water, fed from a 500-gallon attic tank that was filled by hand pump from a rainwater cistern.

## Eighteen Girls in March

Low came home to Savannah with an idea. On March 12, 1912, at the Andrew Low House, she organized the first troop of American Girl Guides, registering 18 girls that March. That same year Low remodeled the carriage house as their meeting place, turning a building made for carriages and servants into the first Girl Scout headquarters. In 1913 the organization became known as the Girl Scouts of the United States. Low lived another fifteen years. She died in Savannah on January 17, 1927, and left the carriage house to the Savannah Area Girl Scout Council, so the first headquarters stayed with the movement she had started.

## Three Houses, One Landmark

Recognition followed decades later. In 1965 the birthplace and the carriage-house headquarters were designated a National Historic Landmark. The Andrew Low House, where Juliette lived with her husband, was added to the National Register application afterward, creating the Juliette Gordon Low Landmark District in 1966. All three properties also lie within the larger Savannah Historic District. The three sit within a short walk of one another, from the Federal rooms of the birthplace on Oglethorpe Avenue, to the lions and silver doorknobs of the Abercorn Street mansion, to the plain stuccoed walls on Drayton Street where the first troop gathered.

## Flight Context

The district centers on about 32.0771 N, 81.0924 W in Savannah's historic district, among the grid of tree-filled squares a few blocks south of the Savannah River. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet, follow Bull Street, the district's north-south spine, and look just east of it near Oglethorpe Avenue; the three buildings sit within a few blocks of one another. Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) is about 6 nm west-northwest, and Hunter Army Air Field (KSVN), a military field, is about 5 nm southwest, so downtown sightseeing flights operate in busy airspace; coordinate with Savannah approach control.
