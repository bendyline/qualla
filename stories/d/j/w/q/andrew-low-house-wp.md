# Andrew Low House

The oldest building on Savannah's Lafayette Square was a merchant's family home, then the house where Juliette Gordon Low lived as she launched the Girl Scouts from the carriage house out back.

**Location:** 32.0731, -81.0925

**Categories:** Houses in Savannah, Georgia, Houses completed in 1849, Savannah Historic District, Lafayette Square (Savannah, Georgia) buildings, 1849 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state), Girl Scouts of the USA

## Introduction

Sarah Cecil Hunter Low never got to live in the house at 329 Abercorn Street. She died in 1849 from complications after a miscarriage, shortly before construction on the home was completed. The house went on without her, and in the nearly eight decades that followed it would hold a merchant's second marriage, a famous daughter-in-law and, in its carriage house out back, the first headquarters of the Girl Scouts.

## First on the Square

The Andrew Low House stands on Lafayette Square in the heart of Savannah, Georgia. Architect John S. Norris built it between 1848 and 1849, and it is described as the oldest building on the square today. Its owner, Andrew Low II, was a young Scottish immigrant who became one of the most successful businessmen in Georgia. He inherited his uncle's cotton businesses when the uncle died in 1849, and by Wikipedia's account the house too. Five years after losing Sarah, he married Mary Cowper Stiles, and the couple maintained the house as their American residence. Their years together were happy ones, by the museum's account, but they ended with Mary's death in 1863. Andrew Low lived until 1886. By then the house was already tied to the next generation of the family, and to a woman whose work would reach far beyond Savannah.

## Enter Daisy

Andrew Low's son, William Mackay Low, married Juliette Gordon, known as Daisy, in Savannah on December 21, 1886 - the same date as her parents' wedding anniversary. The couple leased property in London and Scotland, and in time Juliette inherited the Savannah house from her husband. Andrew Low was her father-in-law. It was here, in the house on Lafayette Square, that she lived during the early formation of what became the Girl Scouts of the USA. In March 1912, she formed the first two American Girl Guides patrols in Savannah, registering 18 girls. From that small start grew the Girl Scouts of the USA, and much of its earliest life unfolded within a few steps of this house.

## Headquarters in the Carriage House

The movement needed a base, and Low found one on her own property. She established the first headquarters in a remodeled carriage house at 330 Drayton Street, directly behind the home she had inherited and adjacent to it on the west. The carriage house served as Girl Scout headquarters until about 1913, and the building stayed in Girl Scout use for generations after Low left it to the Girl Scouts of Savannah. Low died in Savannah on January 17, 1927, at the age of 66. A year later, on January 19, 1928, the National Society of the Colonial Dames of America in the State of Georgia bought the house from her family for $37,500.

## Open Doors

The Colonial Dames opened the home for limited public tours in 1952, and by 1966 visitors could tour the Andrew Low House seven days a week. The society still governs the museum. The house is part of both the Savannah Historic District and the Juliette Gordon Low Historic District, a double designation that reflects its two lives: first as the showpiece home of a successful merchant, and then as the place where a determined woman built something new for girls across the country. Visitors touring it today move through the rooms where Daisy Low lived as the Girl Scouts took shape.
