# Christ Church (Savannah, Georgia)

Founded the year Savannah was, Christ Church on Johnson Square - "the Mother Church of Georgia" - outlasted fire and hurricane and counts John Wesley and George Whitefield among its early rectors.

**Location:** 32.0795, -81.0908

**Categories:** Episcopal church buildings in Georgia (U.S. state), Churches completed in 1838, 1733 establishments in the Thirteen Colonies, Churches in Savannah, Georgia, Johnson Square (Savannah, Georgia) buildings, Savannah Historic District

## Introduction

For its first years, the first church in Georgia had no church building at all. Colonists from England founded Savannah on February 12, 1733, and a Church of England priest named Henry Herbert came with them. James Oglethorpe had plotted a lot for a church, but the parish worshipped in the open air, and later in the city's courthouse. The building that finally rose would burn, and the next would be wrecked by a hurricane before it was finished. The church on Johnson Square today came later still.

## Wesley's Difficult Years

Herbert left Georgia in late 1733, and a series of missionaries followed. The most famous was John Wesley, who served in Savannah from February 1736 to December 1737. His time here mattered more than its length suggests. Wesley founded one of the first Sunday schools in what became the United States, and he held services at his house - gatherings he later cited as an important moment in the creation of the Methodist movement. Yet the posting went badly. Wesley struggled to evangelize the Native Americans of the region and clashed with residents of the city, and he left less than two years after arriving. His successor, George Whitefield, became parish priest in December 1738 and founded the Bethesda Orphanage near Savannah in 1740.

## Fire, Then Hurricane

In 1744, soon after Whitefield's tenure, the cornerstone was laid for Christ Church's first permanent building, and the church was dedicated in 1750. In 1796, it burned in a great fire that destroyed most of the city. Work on a second church began in the early 1800s, but in 1804 a great hurricane destroyed the unfinished building. The congregation kept going. When the rebuilt church was consecrated on April 26, 1815, by Bishop Theodore Dehon of South Carolina, the occasion marked the first visit of a bishop to Georgia. Four years later, in 1819, the parish hung a bell made by Revere and Son of Boston - a bell that is still in use today. In 1823, Christ Church became one of the three original parishes of the Episcopal Diocese of Georgia, alongside Christ Church on St. Simons Island and St. Paul's Church in Augusta.

## A Temple on Johnson Square

On February 26, 1838, construction began on a new building at the same location - the church that stands today. It was designed by James Hamilton Couper, a noted Georgia planter, with Amos Scudder responsible for the brickwork and Gilbert Butler for the woodwork, and it was consecrated in 1840. Couper's design is Neo-Classical. The church's two bell towers were deliberately kept short, so they cannot be seen from the front and the facade can hold the calm lines of a Roman or Greek temple. Seen from Johnson Square, the result is a church front with no visible steeple at all. The Revere bell hangs in the north tower.

## The Mother Church

Because it was founded in 1733 as the first church in the Province of Georgia, Christ Church earned the nickname "the Mother Church of Georgia." Its list of rectors reads like a chronicle of the colony and the state, running from Herbert, Wesley and Whitefield through the 18th and 19th centuries and into the present day. The church has also served the whole city in moments of grief. In 1968, it held a city-wide memorial service in memory of the Rev. Martin Luther King Jr. Nearly three centuries after its founding, the congregation still gathers at 28 Bull Street, on a lot laid out for it when Savannah itself was brand new.

## Flight Context

Christ Church stands at 32.0795 N, 81.0908 W on Johnson Square at 28 Bull Street in Savannah's historic district, Georgia. From 1,500-2,000 feet, look for Savannah's grid of green squares just south of the Savannah River waterfront; Johnson Square lies close to the river end of Bull Street. Hunter Army Airfield (KSVN), a military field, is about 5 nm southwest, and Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) about 6.4 nm west-northwest, so expect controlled airspace over downtown. Hilton Head Airport (KHXD) is about 22 nm east-northeast. The terrain is low and coastal, and haze is the most common visibility limit.
