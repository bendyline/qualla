# Mrs. Wilkes' Dining Room

In the ground floor of an 1870 Savannah townhouse, strangers have lined up since 1943 to sit ten to a table and pass Southern home cooking around like family - a tradition begun by boardinghouse keeper Sema Wilkes.

**Location:** 32.0725, -81.0958

**Categories:** 1870 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state), 1943 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state), Houses completed in 1870, Restaurants established in 1943, Restaurants in Savannah, Georgia, America's Classics winners

## Introduction

There is no menu. At Mrs. Wilkes' Dining Room, the kitchen decides what you will eat, and it changes every day. Guests wait in line outside a historic townhouse on West Jones Street, and when their turn comes they are led in groups of ten to long tables already crowded with freshly laid dishes. Then the strangers sit down together and do what families do: they pass the bowls.

## Tables for Ten

The ritual is the whole point. Diners are escorted into the dining room in shifts of ten and seated at one of several long tables, each shared by people who, a few minutes earlier, were strangers in the same queue. A spread of Southern home cooking is already set out, chosen by the restaurant rather than the guest. Everyone serves themselves and hands the dish along. The line to get in is usually long, and the restaurant serves lunch only, on weekdays, and closes for a winter break from New Year's Day to mid-February, which makes the wait part of the experience. It is dining as a small act of trust: you give up the menu, and you share the table.

## Mrs. Wilkes' Boardinghouse

The dining room began as part of something else. In 1943, a young Sema Wilkes took over a boardinghouse in downtown Savannah, known as the Wilkes House, offering comfortable lodging and homestyle Southern cooking served family style in the downstairs dining room. The house itself, at 107 West Jones Street, dates to 1870. Over time the meals outlasted the boardinghouse, and today the restaurant still occupies the ground floor of the same building. Sema Wilkes owned and managed it for 59 years, from 1943 until her death in 2002 at the age of 95. Along the way she published a cookbook, Mrs. Wilkes' Boardinghouse Cookbook, in 2001, carrying her kitchen's recipes well beyond Jones Street. Author William Schemmel captured the place neatly, calling it "a treasure hidden away in a historic district town-house."

## Famous Faces at Lunch

Mrs. Wilkes and her restaurant have been the subject of newspaper and magazine articles, and the dining room has drawn well-known guests as well as the daily line outside. Newsman David Brinkley once profiled the restaurant on the evening news, and Robert Duvall, Kate Smith and Gregory Peck are all said to have eaten here. On March 2, 2010, President Barack Obama dropped in for a surprise lunch at a table with Savannah Mayor Otis Johnson and other guests, and fried chicken was on the menu. It was the same kind of homestyle Southern cooking that Sema Wilkes had set out for her boarders in 1943.
