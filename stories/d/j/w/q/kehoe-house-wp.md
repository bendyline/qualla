# Kehoe House

An Irish-born iron founder built this 1892 mansion on Savannah's Columbia Square with cast-iron columns, stairways and gates - a house that later served as a funeral parlor, belonged for a time to Joe Namath, and is now an inn.

**Location:** 32.0771, -81.0883

**Categories:** Houses completed in 1892, Houses in Savannah, Georgia, Columbia Square (Savannah, Georgia) buildings, Savannah Historic District

## Introduction

Look closely at the columns, the stairways and the window treatments of the Kehoe House, and then look again. None of them are wood. The stairways, window treatments, columns, fences and gates are all cast iron, a showcase for its builder's foundry. William Kehoe, the man who built it on Savannah's Columbia Square in 1892, had spent his working life turning molten metal into things people could use, and he made sure his home said so.

## From Wexford to the Foundry Floor

Kehoe was eight years old in 1851 when he immigrated to America from County Wexford, Ireland, with his parents, four brothers and three sisters. He apprenticed in an iron foundry and worked his way up to foreman, and eventually he bought the foundry himself. In 1868, he married Anne Flood, and the couple soon started a family. The Kehoes first lived on the square at 130 Habersham Street, a house built in 1885 that is now known as the William J. Kehoe House. As both the business and the family kept growing, they needed more room, so Kehoe built a larger home diagonally across the square.

## Iron in Place of Wood

Architect DeWitt Bruyn designed the new house, and it was completed in May 1892 at a cost of $25,000. The Kehoes moved in with their 10 children. What sets the building apart is the material Kehoe chose for details a visitor would expect to be wood. The exterior stairways, the window treatments, the columns and the fences and gates are all made of cast iron, a tribute, as the inn describes it, to the foundry's excellence in pattern making and casting. In a sense, the house doubled as an advertisement: anyone walking past on the square could see Kehoe ironwork at the scale of a family mansion.

## Funeral Parlor, Quarterback, Inn

The Kehoe family sold the house in the 1920s or around 1930; accounts differ. Over the following decades it served as a family home and as a funeral parlor, and for a time it was held by Joe Namath, the Alabama and New York Jets football star. By the inn's account, Namath sold the property in 1990, and after a two-year renovation it opened as a historic bed and breakfast inn named The William Kehoe House. Today the inn has 13 guest rooms and multiple private and semi-private balconies. It now operates under the Verdigreen Hotels & Residences brand, alongside Savannah's East Bay Inn, Eliza Thompson House and The Gastonian.
