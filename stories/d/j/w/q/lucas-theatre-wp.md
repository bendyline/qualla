# Lucas Theatre

Savannah's 1921 "Jewel" on Reynolds Square went dark in 1976, nearly became a parking garage, and returned in 2000 as a home for the city's philharmonic, film festival and a rescued Wurlitzer organ.

## Introduction

To build the Lucas Theatre, Savannah gave up the home of its first elected mayor. John Houstoun had lived in a house on this site that dated to around 1784, and in 1920 it was demolished to make way for a movie palace. For decades, the theater held its ground. Then the theater itself went dark, and it came within reach of the same fate: a wrecking ball, and a parking garage in its place.

## The Jewel of Savannah

The theater was the idea of Arthur Lucas, an Atlanta businessman who named it after himself. Architect Claude K. Howell designed it in the Spanish Baroque style, and it opened to the public on December 26, 1921, on Abercorn Street in Reynolds Square. It seated well over a thousand people; the restored house holds about 1,200. Inside, audiences found a large dome overhead and marble floors underfoot, and some called the place the "Jewel of Savannah." Its original marquee was wrought iron. Sometime in the 1930s it gave way to a neon sign, a brighter face for a building that would keep its doors open for more than half a century, until 1976.

## Dark Years

After the Lucas closed, the building did not stay empty, but its later tenants, a comedy club and a restaurant, were a long way from the grand opening nights of the 1920s. By the mid-1980s the theater was slated for demolition, reportedly to be replaced by a parking garage. That prospect moved a group of Savannahians to act. They organized to prevent the demolition, bought the building and began extensive renovations. The renovations were extensive and stretched across more than a decade. Help also came from an unexpected quarter in 1997, when Hollywood came to town to film Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil. Members of the film crew, including director Clint Eastwood and lead actor Kevin Spacey, took part in fundraising events for the theater.

## Curtain Up Again

The Lucas Theatre reopened in 2000 with a screening of Gone with the Wind. The Savannah College of Art and Design now manages it as the Lucas Theatre for the Arts, and it is the home venue of the Savannah Philharmonic Orchestra. It also hosts the annual Savannah Film Festival and the Savannah Music Festival, so the building that once showed the latest pictures now helps put them before new audiences. The most recent chapter belongs to the theater's Wurlitzer organ, built in 1925. It had been removed decades earlier and stored in an Atlanta barn, exposed to the elements. With help from the American Theatre Organ Society, it was restored and reinstalled in 2023, and seven new ranks of pipes were added to the original eleven. After years of silence, the Lucas once again has a voice of its own.

## Flight Context

The Lucas Theatre stands at 32.0789 N, 81.0896 W on Abercorn Street at Reynolds Square in Savannah's historic district, Georgia, a short walk from the Savannah River waterfront. From 1,500-2,000 feet, the grid of green squares is the reference; Reynolds Square sits in the northernmost row, just east of Johnson Square. Hunter Army Airfield (KSVN), a military field, lies about 5 nm southwest, and Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) about 6.4 nm west-northwest, so expect controlled airspace over downtown. Hilton Head Airport (KHXD) is about 22 nm east-northeast. Low coastal terrain means haze is the usual visibility limit.
