# Chippewa Square (Savannah, Georgia)

Laid out in 1815 and named for soldiers who fell in the War of 1812, Savannah's Chippewa Square holds a sword-drawn Oglethorpe, a theater that opened in 1818, and the spot where Forrest Gump's fiberglass bench once sat.

**Location:** 32.0758, -81.0928

**Categories:** Squares in Savannah, Georgia, Savannah Historic District, 1815 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state), Monuments and memorials in Savannah, Georgia, Filming locations

## Introduction

The bench was never really here. When the opening scene of the 1994 film Forrest Gump was shot on the north side of Chippewa Square, the bench where the story begins was a fiberglass prop, not one of the park's own. The square around it, though, was entirely real. Chippewa Square has been part of the city since 1815, and the Savannah Theatre at 222 Bull Street first opened on its edge in 1818. For all its fame on screen, the square has a much older story to tell.

## The Spelling Is Correct

Savannah has 22 squares, arranged in five rows, and Chippewa sits in the middle row, where Bull Street meets McDonough Street. Wright Square lies to the north, Madison Square to the south, Orleans Square to the west and Colonial Park Cemetery to the east. The square was laid out in 1815 and named in honor of American soldiers killed in the Battle of Chippawa during the War of 1812. The battle is spelled with an "a"; the square takes an "e," and in its case "Chippewa" is considered correct. Like its neighbors, the square follows what is now known as the Oglethorpe Plan, with eight blocks around it: four residential "tything" blocks and four civic "trust" blocks. The plan gives every square the same rhythm, a green room framed by houses and public buildings, and Chippewa's frame is filled with buildings raised between the 1810s and the early 1900s.

## Oglethorpe Faces South

At the center of the square stands the James Oglethorpe Monument, the work of sculptor Daniel Chester French and architect Henry Bacon, unveiled in 1910. The founder of Georgia faces south, toward the colony's one-time enemy in Spanish Florida, and his sword is drawn. Making room for him meant clearing the square of earlier tributes: busts of the Confederate figures Francis Stebbins Bartow and Lafayette McLaws were moved from Chippewa Square to Forsyth Park. Because of the monument, Savannahians sometimes call this Oglethorpe Square. That name belongs to a different square, however, which lies just to the northeast.

## Curtain, Pulpit and Schoolhouse

Public life crowds the square's edges. The Savannah Theatre at 222 Bull Street opened in December 1818 and is counted among the oldest continually operating theatres in the United States; fires in 1906 and 1948 mean the Art Deco building seen today is a remodel of the original. The First Baptist Church at 223 Bull Street dates to 1833. At 207 Bull Street rises the Independent Presbyterian Church, a John Holden Greene design, dedicated in 1819, that was destroyed in the fire of 1889 and rebuilt in 1891; its school building, designed by Charles Henry, followed in 1894. The Board of Education Building stands at 208 Bull Street. On the southwestern civic block is the Philbrick–Eastman House of 1847, a design by Charles B. Cluskey. Within a few steps of Oglethorpe's statue, a visitor can pass a theater, two churches and the Board of Education Building.

## A Walk Down the Decades

The houses around the square read like a timeline. The Hetty, Abbie and Phillipa Minis House at 11 East Perry Street dates to about 1820. The house at 3 West Perry Street, built in 1831, was once the home of Joseph Frederick Waring. The John Stoddard House at 15 West Perry Street went up in 1867, and the Julius Koox Duplex at 230–232 Bull Street in 1871. Later decades added 240 Bull Street in 1890 and 234 Bull Street around 1900. On the northwestern block, the Honora Foley Property at 14 West Hull Street, designed by Henry Urban in 1896, is known today as the Foley House Inn, and the Julius Perlinski House nearby at 22 West Hull Street dates to about 1903. Taken together, they span nearly a century of Savannah building, all facing the same patch of green.

## Flight Context

Chippewa Square lies at about 32.0758 N, 81.0928 W in the heart of Savannah's historic district, a few blocks south of the Savannah River waterfront. From the air, the city's grid of squares is unmistakable: follow Bull Street, the north-south spine, south from the river and count the leafy squares; Chippewa is the third, after Johnson and Wright, with Forsyth Park's large rectangle of green further south. A viewing altitude of 2,000 to 2,500 feet shows the pattern best. Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) is about 6 nm west-northwest, and Hunter Army Air Field (KSVN), a military field, is about 5 nm southwest, so downtown sightseeing flights operate in busy airspace; coordinate with Savannah approach control. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
