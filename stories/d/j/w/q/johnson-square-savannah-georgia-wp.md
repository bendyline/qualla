# Johnson Square (Savannah, Georgia)

The first square laid out in Savannah in 1733, Johnson Square is still the largest - home to Nathanael Greene's obelisk, a sundial honoring the man who surveyed the city grid, and nearly two centuries of banks, hotels and church spires around its edges.

**Location:** 32.0800, -81.0914

**Categories:** Johnson Square (Savannah, Georgia), 1733 establishments in the Thirteen Colonies

## Introduction

Savannah's famous grid of squares started here. In 1733, Johnson Square became the first of the city's squares to be laid out, and it remains the largest of all 22. Nearly three centuries later it is ringed by banks and office buildings, but its center still holds a general's tomb, a pair of fountains and a sundial that quietly credits the man who drew the city's streets.

## Square One

Johnson Square sits in the northernmost of the city's five rows of squares, on Bull Street and St. Julian Street, with Ellis Square to the west, Reynolds Square to the east and Wright Square to the south. Its name honors Robert Johnson, colonial governor of South Carolina and a friend of General James Oglethorpe. The choice fit the moment: as the square itself records, at least one South Carolinian played a central part in laying out the new town. Over the following centuries, the square's edges filled with a church, hotels and banks, turning the first square into a busy center of city life.

## Colonel Bull's Sundial

One of those South Carolinian helpers is remembered right in the square. A sundial here is dedicated to Colonel William Bull, the namesake of Bull Street. Bull assisted Oglethorpe in establishing Savannah and, as a surveyor, laid out the original street grid - the grid that still organizes downtown Savannah. The sundial stands on a square granite base with a panel on each of its four sides, and its bronze dial is set atop a marble shaft. One of the panels reproduces a 1734 map of Savannah, a snapshot of the town barely a year after it was founded. Two fountains complete the square's furnishings, and a Georgia Historical Commission marker was erected in 1955.

## A General in the Center

At the center of the square rises an obelisk, a memorial that first honored both Revolutionary War generals Nathanael Greene and Casimir Pulaski and now honors Greene alone. Its cornerstone was laid in 1825 by the Marquis de Lafayette. Greene himself had died in 1786 and was buried in Savannah's Colonial Park Cemetery, but that was not his final resting place. His remains were later moved, and today he lies beneath the monument in Johnson Square. That makes the city's first square something more than a public garden or a crossroads. It is also a grave, and every visitor who crosses it passes the resting place of one of the Revolution's generals.

## Hotels, Banks and a Church

Buildings around the square have come and gone. The Pulaski House Hotel operated at the northwest corner from 1835 to 1948 and was torn down eight years later; by 2023 a Regions Bank stood in its place. The Screven House Hotel stood at the corner of Bull and Congress Streets in the late 19th and early 20th centuries before it was demolished and replaced in 1913 by the Savannah Hotel, which became the Manger Building. Banking left its mark: the 1907 Citizens and Southern Bank at 22 Bull Street later became a Bank of America, and the 1895 Citizens Bank Building at 15 Drayton Street later became Propes Hall of the Savannah College of Art and Design. Christ Church, at 28 Bull Street, anchors the southeastern side. The oldest surviving building on the square is said to be the Ann Hamilton House on East Bryan Street, which dates to 1824.

## Flight Context

Johnson Square is at 32.0800 N, 81.0914 W in the northernmost row of squares in downtown Savannah, Georgia, a few blocks south of the Savannah River waterfront. From 1,500-2,000 feet, the grid of green squares is easy to read; Johnson Square is the largest of them, sitting on Bull Street. Hunter Army Airfield (KSVN), a military field, lies about 5 nm southwest, and Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) about 6.3 nm west-northwest, so expect controlled airspace over downtown. Hilton Head Airport (KHXD) is about 22 nm east-northeast. Low coastal terrain means haze is the usual visibility limit.
