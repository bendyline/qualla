# Monterey Square (Savannah, Georgia)

Named for an 1846 battle in Mexico, Savannah's Monterey Square keeps nearly all of its original buildings, a monument to Casimir Pulaski, a Gothic synagogue and the mansion made famous by Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil.

**Location:** 32.0714, -81.0947

**Categories:** Monterey Square (Savannah, Georgia), 1847 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state)

## Introduction

Savannah's Monterey Square honors a battle fought in Mexico, and it even spells the name differently. In 1846, during the Mexican-American War, American forces under General Zachary Taylor captured the Mexican city of Monterrey. The next year, Savannah laid out a new square and named it for the victory - with one "r," which remains the correct spelling for the square to this day.

## A Monument at the Southern Edge

Laid out in 1847, Monterey Square sits in the southernmost of the city's five rows of squares, on Bull Street and Wayne Street. Madison Square lies to its north, Taylor Square to its east and Chatham Square to its west, and just to the south the grid gives way to Forsyth Park. Spanish moss hangs from the square's trees. What makes Monterey Square unusual, even in a city famous for its squares, is how little has changed around it. Nearly all of the buildings surrounding it are original to the square; the lone exception is the United Way Building at 428 Bull Street. Among the oldest is the Herman Kuhlman Duplex at 22-24 West Taylor Street, built in 1851. In the middle of the square stands a monument to General Casimir Pulaski, whose cornerstone was laid on October 11, 1853, and which was unveiled in the mid-1850s. It carries a mystery beneath it. Remains found at Greenwich Plantation, near Savannah, were believed to be Pulaski's and were reinterred at the monument; after a forensic study that ended inconclusively, they were buried there again with military honors in 2005. A 2019 Smithsonian-funded study concluded the skeleton was likely Pulaski's, a conclusion some historians dispute. The result is a memorial to a Revolutionary general that may, or may not, also be his grave.

## Row Houses and a Gothic Synagogue

The buildings around the square read like a catalog of 19th-century Savannah. On the southeastern block, Scudder's Row joins five homes, 1 through 9 East Gordon Street, built in 1852 and 1853 by the brothers John and Ephraim Scudder. On the other side of Bull Street, 11 West Gordon Street was designed by architect John S. Norris. On the southeastern corner rises Congregation Mickve Israel, dating from 1878, one of the few Gothic-style synagogues in America. Its presence on the square is a reminder that Savannah's Jewish community is almost as old as the city itself. Other houses around the square are still known by the names of the people they were built for, among them the Reverend Charles W. Rogers, Nicholas Cruger and William Hunter.

## Midnight on Monterey Square

The most famous house on the square is Mercer House, begun in 1860 for Hugh Mercer (who never lived there) and later the home of antiques dealer and conservator Jim Williams. It fills an entire block, the southwestern trust lot, and today operates as the Mercer Williams House Museum at 429 Bull Street. The house and the square were featured prominently in John Berendt's 1994 true crime book Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil, and they appeared again in the 1997 film version, along with the Comer House at 2 East Taylor Street. That was not the square's first time on screen. It also served as a setting for the television film The Ordeal of Dr. Mudd, starring Dennis Weaver.

## Flight Context

Monterey Square is at 32.0714 N, 81.0947 W on Bull Street in the southernmost row of squares in Savannah's historic district, Georgia. From 1,500-2,000 feet, find the large green rectangle of Forsyth Park, then look just north of it along Bull Street for Monterey Square. Hunter Army Airfield (KSVN), a military field, is about 4.5 nm southwest, and Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) about 6.4 nm west-northwest, so expect controlled airspace over downtown. Hilton Head Airport (KHXD) lies about 22 nm east-northeast. The terrain is low and coastal; haze is the main visibility concern.
