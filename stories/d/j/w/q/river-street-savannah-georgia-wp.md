# River Street (Savannah, Georgia)

Below Savannah's bluff, River Street's cobbled lane of old cotton warehouses has become one of the city's major tourist promenades - where ocean-going ships slide past within yards of the cafés.

**Location:** 32.0858, -81.0995

**Categories:** Roads in Savannah, Georgia, Savannah River, Streets in Georgia (U.S. state), Cobblestone architecture

## Introduction

The ships are the surprise. You can be sitting at a café table on River Street when a tanker or container ship bound for the Port of Savannah slides past, sometimes within yards of the promenade, its hull filling the view where you expected open water. Behind you rise the brick walls of old cotton warehouses. River Street is where Savannah's commercial past and its tourist present share a single strip of Belgian block paving along the water.

## Two Miles of Waterfront

River Street runs for 2 miles along the southern edge of the Savannah River, from the merging of North and East Lathrop Avenues in the west to East Bay Street in the east. Its best-known stretch begins at the Talmadge Memorial Bridge and passes below City Hall and Yamacraw Bluff to the street's eastern end. It is West River Street until it reaches the point where the Hyatt Regency Savannah spans it; there, around 40 feet below Bay Street, it becomes East River Street. Today East River Street is mostly restaurants, cafés and craft shops, and it ranks among the city's major tourist attractions. Its half-mile pedestrian promenade, the John P. Rousakis Riverfront Plaza, honors Savannah's longest-serving mayor, who held office from 1970 to 1992. Riverboats including the Georgia Queen and the Savannah River Queen, and the free Belles Ferry across to Hutchinson Island, tie up alongside.

## King Cotton's Back Door

The buildings along the south side tell the street's original purpose. They are terraces of former cotton warehouses, the industrial rear portions of more fashionable buildings that face Bay Street on the bluff above. Some are very old: the Archibald Smith Stores date to 1810, and the William Taylor Stores to 1806 and 1818. Factors Row, a bluffside line of red-brick buildings where cotton brokers bargained during the crop's heyday, preserves that trade in its name, and Factors Walk is "built on the middle level of a sloping bluff with warehouses beneath and Bay Street above." These buildings also hold a harder memory. By accounts handed down, some of the warehouses and archways were used to hold enslaved Africans - people held against their will in the same buildings through which the port's trade flowed. That history sits just behind today's shopfronts.

## Rails in the Cobblestones

Look down and the street's transport history is set into the pavement. The rails embedded in River Street's Belgian block paving first carried horsecars, then electric streetcars from 1890 to 1946. The Norfolk Southern Railway owned the River Street branch line for years and ran a local freight train, the River Street Rambler, until 2003. The following year, the City of Savannah bought the line's right-of-way for about $600,000. From 2009 to 2015, a heritage streetcar served six stops between Montgomery Street and East Broad Street. Today visitors arrive on foot, by tour trolley, or down the Lincoln Street ramp beside the western end of Factors Walk; no city buses stop on River Street itself, though the fare-free downtown loop runs nearby.

## Blocks Lost and Found

Not everything survived. Long before the Hyatt Regency occupied its lot around 1980, two ranges of buildings, Bolton Range and Habersham & Harris Range, stood there. They gave way to the Neal Blun Building, which stood from 1889 until 1969, and the Moses Ferst and Company grocery store to its west. Around 1970, the 1890 Commercial Row and its neighbors were knocked down. What had been the most tight-knit block on River Street lay empty throughout the 1970s until the Rousakis Plaza and the Hyatt filled the gap. Change continues. Kevin Barry's Pub closed at the turn of 2020, with its last night on New Year's Eve, after nearly forty years in business, and in 2020 the large mixed-use Plant Riverside District opened at the street's west end. River Street even has a digital double: it appears as a location in the 2012 video game The Walking Dead.

## Flight Context

River Street runs along the south bank of the Savannah River at downtown Savannah, Georgia, centered near 32.0858 N, 81.0995 W. From 1,500-2,500 feet, follow the river: the Talmadge Memorial Bridge marks the western end of the best-known stretch, and the waterfront buildings line the bank below the bluff and City Hall to the east. Watch for large container ships and tankers in the channel. Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) lies about 5.8 nm west-northwest and Hunter Army Airfield (KSVN), a military field, about 5 nm south-southwest, so expect busy controlled airspace. Hilton Head Airport (KHXD) is about 22 nm east-northeast. Low coastal terrain means haze is the usual visibility limit.
