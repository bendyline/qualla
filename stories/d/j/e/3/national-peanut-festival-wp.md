# National Peanut Festival

Every fall since 1938, with pauses for war and pandemic, Dothan, Alabama, has thrown the nation's largest peanut festival, a celebration that began with George Washington Carver as its first guest speaker.

## Introduction

The first National Peanut Festival, held in Dothan, Alabama, in 1938, was a cold one; the festival remembers it as a near-freezing day. Its guest speaker was Dr. George Washington Carver. That choice was no accident. In the festival's own telling, Carver introduced peanuts to the Wiregrass region, and peanuts saved the area after boll weevils destroyed its cotton crops. More than eight decades later, the United States' largest peanut festival still fills its fairgrounds south of Dothan each fall, honoring peanut growers and celebrating the harvest season.

## A Crop That Saved a Region

The festival was built on gratitude. Its purpose from the start was to honor the people who grew peanuts and to mark the harvest, and in 1938 that meant celebrating a crop the region had come to depend on. Harry P. Hall served as the first festival president. The first pageant winner was Elizabeth Johnson of Headland, Alabama, who later became the first Miss Peanut. Early pageants had a practical twist: contestants were sponsored by local businesses, and whoever sold the most tickets to the festival won the title. It was a contest of salesmanship as much as poise.

## War, Revival and a Parade of Stars

World War II interrupted the celebration; the Encyclopedia of Alabama counts 1945 and 1946 as the missed years, while other accounts say there was no festival between 1942 and 1946. In 1947 the Jaycees and the Chamber of Commerce brought it back. By 1950 the festival had star power: Miss America attended, Johnny Mack Brown rode his horse in the parade, and Eddie Arnold appeared in concert. The festival has also moved several times. In 1959 it moved to the Houston County Farm Center, in 1993 to 150 acres on Highway 231 South, and in 1999 to its current fairgrounds on Highway 231 South, three miles south of the Ross Clark Circle. The COVID-19 pandemic brought another pause in 2020, when the festival was canceled; instead, the carnival that hosted it set up at the local mall with some acts and performances.

## Midway Lights and the Biggest Peanut

Today's festival is a full-scale fall fair. A large midway offers games and amusement rides, and there are animal acts, agricultural displays, arts and crafts, food and beauty pageants. An outdoor amphitheater hosts live concerts by national recording artists, and the parade runs a full two hours. Agriculture remains at the heart of it. The festival sponsors field crop exhibits and awards prizes to every exhibitor, and its competitions range from sewing, cake decorating, photography, cooking and art to one that could only belong here: who has grown the biggest peanut. Every year crowds come to enjoy it, and every year the humble legume that Carver championed takes center stage.

## Flight Context

The National Peanut Festival fairgrounds lie at approximately 31.147 N, 85.407 W on U.S. Highway 231 South, about three miles south of Dothan's Ross Clark Circle in Houston County, Alabama. From 1,500-2,500 ft MSL, follow Highway 231 south out of Dothan; in festival season, the midway rides and parking fields make the grounds easy to pick out amid the surrounding farmland. Dothan Regional Airport (KDHN) is about 11 nm north-northwest, and Headland Municipal Airport (KHDL) about 14 nm north-northeast. Cairns Army Airfield (KOZR), about 18 nm west-northwest, is a military field, so expect busy Army training traffic in the region. Fall usually brings more settled weather than summer's afternoon thunderstorms, though the first festival day in 1938 was near freezing.
