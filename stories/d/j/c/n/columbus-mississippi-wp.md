# Columbus, Mississippi

A Tombigbee River town once mistaken for part of Alabama, Columbus nursed the wounded of Shiloh, decorated Union and Confederate graves alike in an early example of Memorial Day, and was the birthplace of Tennessee Williams.

**Location:** 33.5017, -88.4150

**Categories:** Cities in Mississippi, County seats in Mississippi, American Civil War, Antebellum architecture, Tennessee Williams, Memorial Day

## Introduction

In the spring of 1866, barely a year after the Civil War ended, a group of women in Columbus, Mississippi, decided to lay flowers on the graves of Union and Confederate soldiers alike. The gesture reached the New York newspapers, where the poet Francis Miles Finch read about it and wrote "The Blue and the Grey" in its honor. That day in Columbus is counted as an early example of what became Memorial Day. It is a fitting emblem for a river town whose past is full of crossings, between states, between armies, and between the peoples who have shared this high ground above the Tombigbee.

## Possum Town on the Military Road

Hernando de Soto is reputed to have crossed the nearby Tombigbee River, but the place enters the steady record of American history in December 1810, when John Pitchlynn, the U.S. Indian agent and interpreter for the Choctaw Nation, built a home and farm at Plymouth Bluff. After the Battle of New Orleans, Andrew Jackson ordered a road from Nashville to New Orleans, and in 1817 his surveyor, Captain Hugh Young, chose this crossing, where high ground met the river on both sides. The town grew where Jackson's Military Road crossed the Tombigbee. Local Choctaw and Chickasaw people called it Possum Town, a nickname locals still use. Silas McBee suggested the name Columbus, and a small creek was named for him in return. Founded in 1819, the town was first recognized by an act of the Alabama legislature, since it was believed to be in Alabama, until the state line was corrected.

## Hospital Town

Columbus was building early institutions even before it knew its own state. Its founders established Franklin Academy, known as Mississippi's first public school, opened in 1821 for white boys, and it served as a public school until the district closed it as an active school at the end of the 2025-26 school year. During the Civil War, Columbus became a hospital town. Many of the wounded from the Battle of Shiloh were brought here, and thousands were eventually buried in Friendship Cemetery. Annunciation Catholic Church, built in 1863 and still in use, served as one of the hospitals. The town's arsenal made gunpowder, handguns and a few cannons, which drew a Union invasion that Confederate General Nathan Bedford Forrest turned back. When Union troops neared Jackson, the state capital briefly moved to Columbus before settling in Macon.

## The Houses That Survived

Because Union forces never reached the town, its antebellum homes escaped burning, and Columbus is said to hold the most extensive collection of them in Mississippi after Natchez. Each year during the Pilgrimage, residents open their doors to visitors from across the country. The town's literary claim is just as strong. Tennessee Williams was born in the rectory of St. Paul's Episcopal Church on Main Street, where his grandfather was the priest; the rectory was moved in 1993 to preserve it and is now the Tennessee Williams Home Museum and Welcome Center. For the post office, Beulah Bettersworth completed the mural Out of the Soil in 1939, commissioned through the Treasury Department's Section of Painting and Sculpture. In 2010 Columbus won a Great American Main Street Award from the National Trust for Historic Preservation.

## Harder Chapters

Not every memory here is gentle. In 1915 a mob of white men raped and lynched Cordella Stevenson, a Black woman, in Columbus. Stephen D. Lee High School desegregated in 1970, as Columbus's schools came under a federal desegregation decree; local accounts say that before it did, the school formed a committee of Black and white students to work through problems, and that it later received a state award for its race relations. The school also sorted students into three tracks by achievement test scores, a practice that, by the same accounts, a federal court later ruled unconstitutional because the testing was biased and ignored differences in earlier preparation. Disaster has visited too. In 1990 a fireworks factory explosion killed two workers and was felt 30 miles away, and in February 2019 an EF-3 tornado tore through homes and businesses and killed a woman when a structure fell on her.

## Wings Over the Golden Triangle

Columbus Air Force Base has trained pilots since World War II. During the 1950s and 1960s it served as a Strategic Air Command base, enough to earn Columbus a place on Soviet target lists, before returning to flight training. The mid-twentieth century brought mattress, furniture and textile plants, along with Sanderson Plumbing Products, then described as the world's largest toilet seat maker, though most of those had closed by 2000. New plants near Golden Triangle Regional Airport, building aircraft, engines and steel, have since helped revive the economy. About 24,000 people live here today, beside a river that is now part of the Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway, with Columbus Lake held back by the John C. Stennis Lock and Dam just north of downtown.

## Flight Context

Columbus sits at about 33.502 N, 88.415 W on the east bank of the Tombigbee River in Lowndes County, roughly 10 miles west of the Alabama line. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet, the river and the Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway make the best landmark: Columbus Lake lies about two miles north of downtown behind the John C. Stennis Lock and Dam, and Luxapalila Creek winds through town to join the Tombigbee south of the center. Columbus Lowndes County Airport (KUBS) is about 3 nm southeast, Golden Triangle Regional Airport (KGTR) about 9 nm west-southwest, and Columbus Air Force Base (KCBM) about 8 nm north. Expect heavy military training traffic around the base. Spring and summer thunderstorms, including severe weather, are common.
