# Paul W. Bryant Museum

On the University of Alabama campus, a museum born of Bear Bryant's own suggestion keeps a crystal houndstooth hat, a retired national championship trophy and a list of hundreds of people named in his honor.

**Location:** 33.2056, -87.5353

**Categories:** Sports museums in Alabama, American football museums and halls of fame, University museums in Alabama, Alabama Crimson Tide football, Museums in Tuscaloosa, Alabama

## Introduction

Every fall the Paul W. Bryant Museum hosts an unusual reunion. The guests answer to Bryant, Paul, Bear and even Paula, and they share those names because former players and fans chose to name their children after one football coach. Hundreds of namesakes have attended the annual event over the years. It is a fitting ritual for a museum on the University of Alabama campus in Tuscaloosa that was opened to "house the history of Alabama football, with special emphasis on the legendary coach" Paul William "Bear" Bryant.

## The Coach's Idea

The museum began with Bryant himself. At his suggestion, a planning committee formed in 1981 to create a museum honoring the former coaches and players who had helped him set what was then the intercollegiate coaching record for the most victories. The committee took the idea further, giving the museum two missions: "inclusion of the entire football history from the first team in 1892 and creating a collections component establishing the foundation of our current institution." Founded in 1985, it opened its doors in 1988. Bryant still dominates, with about a third of the collection devoted to him, but the museum has grown in staff, services and exhibits into a central source for journalists and writers researching the university's athletic history. It also offers educational programs and tours for school groups, and it belongs to the University of Alabama Museums, alongside the Alabama Museum of Natural History, Gorgas House and Moundville Archaeological Museum.

## Crystal Houndstooth and a Trophy Kept for Good

The coach's famous headwear appears here in an unexpected material: a houndstooth hat made of Waterford Crystal. The collection also includes the Daniel Moore painting used to create the 32-cent U.S. postage stamp that celebrated Bryant's life. One trophy tells a story about winning often enough. The Associated Press Trophy, a traveling national championship award presented from 1957 to 1965, became Alabama's to keep when the team won it for the third time in 1965. For those who want the full record, the museum's research room lets visitors watch and study Alabama's games, the victories, the defeats and even the embarrassments. Officials claim more than 1,000 such videos.

## Named for the Bear

The museum keeps a running list of people named for Bryant. The oldest namesake, apart from the coach's own son, Paul Bryant Jr., is reportedly Bryant Darrell Brown. His father, Darrell Brown, was one of the Junction Boys, the survivors of the brutal 10-day summer camp Bryant ran in Junction, Texas, in 1954 while coaching at Texas A&M. Academically ineligible to play in 1956, Brown surprised even Bryant by coming back for his last year of eligibility on the 1957 Texas A&M team, which reached No. 1 in the polls that fall, shortly before Bryant left for Alabama. Reportedly, he was the only Junction Boy who also played in Bryant's final season at A&M. The name has carried into another generation: Darrell Brown's grandson, Bryant Andrew Brown, is a second-generation namesake. With hundreds of names the list is not definitive, but it makes the point plainly enough: many Alabamians admired the coach enough to name their children after him.

## Flight Context

The Paul W. Bryant Museum is on the University of Alabama campus in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, at approximately 33.206°N, 87.535°W, south of the Black Warrior River. The museum building itself is small from the air; look instead for the broad university campus from 1,500 to 2,500 feet. Tuscaloosa National Airport (KTCL) is about 4 nm to the west-northwest across the river. On home football game days, expect stadium temporary flight restrictions and heavy traffic around the city. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common.
