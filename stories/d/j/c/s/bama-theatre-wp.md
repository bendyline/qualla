# Bama Theatre

Built by the New Deal as both a movie palace and Tuscaloosa's city hall, the Bama Theatre still shows films and stages performances beneath a ceiling of painted clouds and twinkling stars.

**Location:** 33.2085, -87.5681

**Categories:** Cinemas and movie theaters in Alabama, Buildings and structures in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, Streamline Moderne architecture in Alabama, Public Works Administration in Alabama, Theatres on the National Register of Historic Places in Alabama

## Introduction

For roughly three decades, Tuscaloosa's city government and a movie palace shared the same roof. The Bama Theatre was built in 1937 and 1938 as a Public Works Administration project under Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal, designed as a combination theater and city hall. City officials worked in one part of the building while audiences settled into the other, looking up at a ceiling where stars twinkled among clouds. The offices left long ago, but the stars are still there, over what is now the city's performing arts center.

## A Palace Under Painted Skies

The Birmingham architect David O. Whilldin, who designed other historically significant buildings in downtown Tuscaloosa, gave the three-story brick and limestone building two personalities. Outside, it wears the Streamline Moderne style popular in the period. Inside, the auditorium is a lavish "atmospheric" hall that mixes Moderne and Renaissance elements, with twinkling stars and clouds on the ceiling to create the feeling of watching a show in the open air. The Bama opened as a theater on April 12, 1938, and it was one of the last movie palaces built in the South. Its name had history behind it. An earlier Bama had shown silent films and vaudeville through the arrival of sound pictures; when the new theater opened, the old one was renamed the Druid Theater, after Tuscaloosa's nickname, the "Druid City."

## From City Hall to Center Stage

City hall moved out after Tuscaloosa acquired and renovated the former United States Post Office, built in 1909, in 1968. In 1976 the Bama became a performing arts center, and it has since been completely renovated for live theater and concerts. Its modern life is the product of cooperation between the Arts Council of Tuscaloosa and the Tuscaloosa County Parks and Recreation Authority. The building was added to the National Register of Historic Places on August 30, 1984. Change has continued: in March 2017 the central panel of the marquee was replaced with an LED screen, and a major renovation in 2022 brought new seats, new carpeting and other upgrades.

## Premieres and Recitals

After theater manager David Allgood arrived in 2003, the Bama became a sought-after stop for touring artists, and its film program was reinvented as the Bama Art House Film Series, devoted to independent, foreign and documentary movies. Past screenings have included Boyhood, Moonrise Kingdom, Room and Turn Me On, Dammit!, along with a one-night screening of John Sayles's Honeydripper, a film shot in Alabama. Sayles attended that screening, which drew nearly 900 people. In the 2020s the program mixes concerts, movies, dance and live theater. The stage also belongs to local performers. The Tuscaloosa Children's Theatre and the Tuscaloosa Community Dancers each perform here two or three times a year, dance schools such as The Dance Centre and The Academy of Ballet and Jazz hold their end-of-year recitals here, and school drama groups from Tuscaloosa Academy, Tuscaloosa County High School and Brookwood have staged productions under the painted sky.

## Flight Context

The Bama Theatre stands at the corner of Greensboro Avenue and Gary Fitts Street (formerly Sixth Street) in downtown Tuscaloosa, Alabama, at approximately 33.208°N, 87.568°W, a few blocks south of the Black Warrior River. The building is hard to pick out from the air; use the river and the downtown grid as references from 1,500 to 2,500 feet, with the University of Alabama campus spreading out to the east. Tuscaloosa National Airport (KTCL) lies about 2.3 nm to the west-northwest, across the river. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common in west Alabama.
