# Denny Chimes

Built by University of Alabama students in 1929 to honor a president they feared was leaving, this 115-foot Art Deco bell tower now rings over the Quad and a walk of football captains' handprints.

**Location:** 33.2097, -87.5467

**Categories:** Bell towers in the United States, Carillons, Art Deco architecture in Alabama, University of Alabama buildings and structures, Alabama Crimson Tide football, Towers completed in 1929

## Introduction

A rumor built this tower. In the late 1920s, word spread across the University of Alabama campus that its president, George H. Denny, was thinking of leaving Tuscaloosa and going home to his native Virginia. Students responded by raising money for a monument in his name. The result, dedicated on May 27, 1929, is Denny Chimes, a 115-foot campanile on the south side of The Quad and one of the most visible landmarks on campus. Denny, as it turned out, stayed. He led the university until 1936 and came back as interim president from 1941 through 1942.

## From War Memorial to Thank-You Note

The idea of a bell tower was older than the rumor. In 1919 it was proposed as a memorial to those who had fought in World War I, but there was no money to build it, and the plan went nowhere. A decade later the students succeeded where the war memorial had failed, though the tower they paid for honored a living president rather than the war dead. Skinner, Maxwell and Company built it for a final cost of $40,000, and Governor Bibb Graves attended the dedication. Even the materials carry a message. The limestone was quarried in Alabama, while the bricks came from Virginia, a tribute to Denny's home state.

## Limestone, Brick and Bronze

The tower is Art Deco from base to peak. A square base of white limestone, with pilasters at each corner and recessed bays between them, rises to a molded cornice, where an eagle with partly outstretched wings perches at each of the four corners. Above the eagles the red brick shaft begins, tapering gradually to a limestone crown. There the belfry opens between square pillars and partly open neoclassical grilles, all capped by a stepped pyramidal roof of limestone. Inside hang 25 cast bronze bells, the largest about 11 feet around and 3 feet tall. Westminster chimes ring every 15 minutes and on the hour, and the alma mater and other songs sound for university celebrations and holidays. An automatic player in the base plays roll music, and a keyboard console is brought into use for special occasions. By 1945 the original bell system had been converted to an electronic one, which was modernized in 1966; after the tower fell silent in the mid-1980s, a 1986 renovation installed the current 25-bell bronze carillon, funded by alumni and friends. For many years Warren Hutton, a professor of organ at the university, served as carillonneur for memorial services and special events, and today faculty and students alike take their turns at the manual console.

## Handprints at the Base

Around the foot of the tower runs the Walk of Fame. Since 1948, captains of the Alabama football team have pressed their hands and feet into cement slabs here, in a ceremony held each year during the A-Day festivities to honor the previous season's leaders. The roll reads like a history of the program: Harry Gilmer from 1947, Joe Namath in 1965, Kenny Stabler in 1967, Ozzie Newsome in 1977, Derrick Thomas in 1988, and, in more recent years, Tua Tagovailoa, Bryce Young and Jalen Milroe. The entry for 1963 reads simply "Not awarded." Every quarter hour, the tower the students paid for rings out over their names.

## Flight Context

Denny Chimes stands at about 33.210 N, 87.547 W on the south side of The Quad at the University of Alabama in Tuscaloosa. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet, look for the long rectangular green of The Quad with the slender red brick tower and its pale limestone crown at its southern edge; the Black Warrior River to the north helps with orientation. Tuscaloosa National Airport (KTCL) is only about 3.3 nm west-northwest, so stay aware of its traffic pattern. Summer thunderstorms and spring severe weather are common in west Alabama.
