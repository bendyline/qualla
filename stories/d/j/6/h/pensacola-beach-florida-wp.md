# Pensacola Beach, Florida

On a barrier island where nobody owns the land, Pensacola Beach pairs famously white sand, a 1,471-foot Gulf pier and Blue Angels flyovers with a long history of rebuilding after hurricanes.

**Location:** 30.3333, -87.1375

**Categories:** Beaches of Florida, Santa Rosa Island (Florida), Unincorporated communities in Escambia County, Florida, Populated coastal places in Florida on the Gulf of Mexico, Hurricanes

## Introduction

Nobody in Pensacola Beach owns the ground under their feet. The land here is bound by a 1947 deed from the United States Department of the Interior requiring that it be administered in the public interest by the county or leased, but never "disposed" of, so every resident and business on this stretch of Santa Rosa Island is a long-term leaseholder rather than a property owner. What they lease is remarkable: a slice of low-lying barrier island in the Gulf of Mexico, reached by bridges from Pensacola and Gulf Breeze, and famous for its ultra-white sand.

## Spanish Sails and a Coastal Fort

Europeans arrived early. Francisco Maldonado, a lieutenant under the conquistador Hernando de Soto, anchored in Pensacola Bay for the winter of 1539–1540. In 1559 Tristán de Luna y Arellano led the first settlement of the region, and within weeks, on the night of September 19, a hurricane struck the colony and destroyed most of its ships and cargo. It was an early lesson in the power of Gulf storms. Later generations fortified the island's western tip. Fort Pickens, completed in 1834, stayed in use until World War II, when modern weapons made traditional coastal defenses obsolete, and it is now open to visitors as part of Gulf Islands National Seashore.

## An Island That Keeps Rebuilding

Storms have written much of the community's modern history. Landfalling hurricanes can drive storm surge right over the island, wrecking buildings and eroding the beach, and residents have faced mandatory evacuations during some of them. In 1995 two hurricanes made landfall here: Erin in August, then Opal just two months later, leveling dunes and destroying homes. On September 16, 2004, Hurricane Ivan devastated the area, destroying hundreds of homes and damaging many others. Hurricane Dennis came ashore between Pensacola Beach and eastern Navarre Beach on July 10, 2005, though, like Erin, it did less damage than predicted. In 2020 Hurricane Sally hit Pensacola Beach hard with wind, surge flooding and more than 20 inches of rain, and a section of the Pensacola Bay Bridge, known locally as the Three Mile Bridge, was destroyed. Some residents build to fight back. The "Dome of a Home," a monolithic concrete dome completed in 2002 and designed to withstand storm surge and hurricane-force winds, came through both Ivan and Dennis.

## Tar on White Sand

Not every disaster came with wind. On April 20, 2010, the BP-operated Deepwater Horizon drilling rig exploded in the Gulf off the Louisiana coast, and the well eventually released almost 5 million barrels of oil. The slick reached Pensacola's beaches on June 4. Crews stationed along the Escambia County coastline quickly cleaned up much of the visible oil, but tourism suffered through the summer of 2010. BBC News reported that swimmers at Pensacola Beach "encountered an oil sheen and children picked up tar blobs as big as tennis balls."

## Pier, Boardwalk and a Beach Ball

In calmer seasons, life gathers at Casino Beach, named for the original casino that once stood on the spot. Restaurants, volleyball courts and lifeguard stands surround it, and in summer the Gulfside Pavilion hosts the "Bands on the Beach" concert series. Beside it, the 1,471-foot Pensacola Beach Gulf Pier, once billed as the longest pier on the Gulf of Mexico (the 1,545-foot Navarre Beach pier, farther east along the barrier island, now carries that title), draws anglers and wildlife watchers looking for dolphins and stingrays; storm damage has closed it for repairs several times. Directly across the island, on the Santa Rosa Sound side, the Quietwater Beach Boardwalk is lined with shops, nightclubs, street musicians and sidewalk artists. Overhead, the Blue Angels from nearby Naval Air Station Pensacola are known for their flyovers, and a Pensacola Beach air show is held most summers.

## Domes, Towers and a Spaceship

The skyline has its own quirks. Towers such as Portofino Towers and Verandas Tower reach 255 feet, among the tallest buildings between Tallahassee and Mobile, while the retired water tower painted like a beach ball is preserved as a landmark. The island also collects novelty houses. Besides the Dome of a Home, sometimes called the "Flintstone Home," there is the "Spaceship House," topped by a UFO-shaped Futuro, a design created in the 1960s by the Finnish architect Matti Suuronen, of which about a hundred were built. Just outside, in Gulf Breeze, a 1960s-era welcome sign points drivers toward the beach; when it was renovated in 2019, its neon gave way to LED lights made to look exactly like the original.

## Flight Context

Pensacola Beach lies on Santa Rosa Island at approximately 30.333°N, 87.138°W, a thin white barrier island between the Gulf of Mexico and Santa Rosa Sound, south of Gulf Breeze and Pensacola. From 1,000 to 2,000 feet along the shoreline, look for the Gulf Pier jutting 1,471 feet into the Gulf, the high-rise towers and the bridges crossing the sound. Fort Pickens marks the island's western tip at the entrance to Pensacola Bay. Pensacola International Airport (KPNS) is about 9 nm to the north-northwest, and Naval Air Station Pensacola Forrest Sherman Field (KNPA) lies about 9.5 nm to the west; expect busy military training traffic, and note that Blue Angels air shows may bring temporary flight restrictions. Summer brings afternoon thunderstorms, and hurricane season runs through the late summer and fall.
