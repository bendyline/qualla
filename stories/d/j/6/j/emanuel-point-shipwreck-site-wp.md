# Emanuel Point Shipwreck Site

Off Emanuel Point in Pensacola Bay lies a Spanish ship from Tristán de Luna's 1559 colonizing fleet, sunk by a hurricane barely a month after the colonists arrived and found again by archaeologists in 1992.

**Location:** 30.4222, -87.1808

**Categories:** National Register of Historic Places in Escambia County, Florida, Shipwrecks of the Florida coast

## Introduction

Tristán de Luna's colonists had been on the shores of Pensacola Bay for about a month when the hurricane came. In the summer of 1559 their fleet had delivered some 1,500 people to begin Spain's colonization of the northern Gulf Coast. Then, in September, the storm struck, and most of the fleet went down. One of those ships lies off Emanuel Point, near Pensacola, where archaeologists found it more than four centuries later. They call it Emanuel Point I.

## Eleven Ships from Veracruz

The expedition was large and deliberate. The conquistador Tristán de Luna led an army and a colony to La Florida, and the University of West Florida describes the company as 1,500 soldiers, colonists, enslaved people and Aztec people, carried in 11 ships from Veracruz, Mexico, to Pensacola. They came to stay. Alongside the soldiers sailed colonists hoping to build new lives, and people who had been given no choice about making the voyage at all, every one of them now dependent on the success of a new Spanish outpost on the Gulf. The settlement itself would last from 1559 to 1561. Most of its fleet, however, did not survive the first autumn.

## One Month

The hurricane hit Pensacola one month after the colonists arrived. It sank most of the fleet and devastated their food supplies, a double blow for a colony that had barely begun. The ship off Emanuel Point went down with the others. Whatever the survivors had planned for that first season on the Gulf, they now faced it with most of their ships gone and much of their food lost. The colony held on for about two more years, but its life was short, ending in 1561. The wreck on the bay floor is a record of the moment everything changed, left in place since September 1559.

## Found by Remote Sensing

For centuries the ship lay unseen. Then, in 1992, archaeologists with the Florida Bureau of Archaeological Research located it during a remote sensing survey of Pensacola Bay, making it one of the earliest shipwrecks yet discovered in the Gulf. The Bureau and the University of West Florida went on to investigate it underwater, working on what researchers described as the lower hull of a Spanish vessel from the Luna expedition. On March 4, 1996, the Emanuel Point Shipwreck Site was added to the U.S. National Register of Historic Places. The listing honors a historic place that no visitor can walk into, preserved beneath the water of the bay.

## Sister Ships on the Bay Floor

The first wreck turned out not to be alone. A University of West Florida summer field school discovered Emanuel Point II in 2006. On June 20, 2016, UWF archaeologists and students on a field methods course found Emanuel Point III, uncovering ballast stones, iron concretions, remnants of ceramics and articulated hull timbers with their frames and planking still together. The colony itself has been found too: its land site lies in a developed Pensacola neighborhood, on what UWF reported as a privately owned residential lot, within view of the shipwrecks in the bay. From that shore, the view takes in the same water where Luna's fleet foundered, and where some of those ships still rest.

## Flight Context

The story's coordinates, 30.422 N, 87.181 W, mark Emanuel Point on the north shore of Pensacola Bay in Pensacola, Florida, off which the wreck lies; the wreck's exact position is not published. The wreck is submerged and invisible from the air, so this is a flight for the setting rather than the site: at 1,500-2,500 ft MSL, trace the Pensacola waterfront and the broad bay opening toward Pensacola Pass and the Gulf of Mexico to the southwest. Pensacola International Airport (KPNS) is about 3 nm north and Naval Air Station Pensacola (KNPA) about 8 nm west-southwest; the NAS is busy military airspace, so coordinate with ATC. Gulf Shores International Airport (Jack Edwards Field, KJKA) lies about 27 nm west-southwest. Expect afternoon thunderstorms in summer and early autumn, the season in which the 1559 hurricane struck.
