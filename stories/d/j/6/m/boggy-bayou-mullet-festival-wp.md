# Boggy Bayou Mullet Festival

For 43 years, Niceville, Florida, celebrated a humble local fish with fried mullet, country stars and crowds of more than 40,000.

**Location:** 30.5352, -86.4923

**Categories:** Food and drink festivals in Florida, Seafood festivals, Festivals in Florida, Music festivals, Okaloosa County, Florida

## Introduction

Let's settle it first: the haircut had nothing to do with it. The Boggy Bayou Mullet Festival was named for the fish, the mullet that swims in abundance in the waters around Niceville, Florida, and not for the famous hairstyle, however common that assumption became. For 43 years, from 1977 to 2019, this Panhandle city built a weekend around that fish. The formula was simple and durable: music, rides, a wide spread of arts and crafts, and above all, fried mullet.

## Boggy Before Nice

The town itself started out with a less flattering name. When mail service began in 1868, the settlement on Boggy Bayou, which opens into Choctawhatchee Bay, was simply called Boggy; the name Niceville, chosen by the postmaster's daughter, became official on November 5, 1910. The bayou kept its old name, and so did the festival. Walter Francis Spence Jr. founded it in 1976, and the first Mullet Festival took place on September 24, 1977. It soon grew into a three-day affair that opened on the third Friday in October and ran until after dark on Sunday, with no alcohol served on the final day. In its later years, weekend attendance typically topped 40,000.

## Mullet on Morning Television

National attention arrived on Friday, October 20, 1989, when Chantal, the Hollywood entertainment reporter for ABC's Good Morning America, broadcast from the festival grounds. During the broadcast, founder Francis Spence and a local man, James Campbell, showed viewers how to catch and fry a mullet. The festival's merchandise traveled even further. In 2009, a T-shirt from the third annual Boggy Bayou Mullet Festival, made by the Tailgate Clothing Company, turned up in season two of HBO's True Blood, in the episode "Scratches"; a representative from the show said she had found it on a website she used to outfit some of the cast. Niceville's city manager, Lannie Corbin, reported that a Mullet Festival shirt had also appeared in the season four premiere of the CBS comedy How I Met Your Mother.

## Country Stars on the Bayou

The stage drew some of country music's best-known names. Little Big Town and Ricky Skaggs played in 2002; the Charlie Daniels Band came in 2003 and again in 2005, and Dierks Bentley shared the 2003 bill with an Air Force Jazz Ensemble. Blake Shelton appeared in 2006 and 2009, Florida Georgia Line in 2012, Vince Gill in 2013, Martina McBride in 2014 and Tanya Tucker in 2017, while Montgomery Gentry played both the 2003 festival and the 2018 edition, one of the last. Other acts, including Dr. Zarr's Amazing Funk Monster, which turned up on bill after bill, filled out the weekends between headliners. The 2019 festival, by then billed as Boggy Fest, was the 43rd and the last held. The next year the COVID-19 pandemic arrived on top of several years of declining revenues and attendance, and together they ended the run. Promoters have since pitched Niceville officials on reviving it, and one group said in 2024 that it hoped for a comeback in 2025, but the city's own history page still speaks of the festival in the past tense.

## Flight Context

The festival was held in Niceville, Florida, at about 30.535 N, 86.492 W, on the north side of Choctawhatchee Bay near Eglin Air Force Base. From 2,000 feet, look for Boggy Bayou opening south into the broad bay, with Destin and the Gulf beaches beyond. Destin-Fort Walton Beach Airport (KVPS) is only about 3.5 nm south-southwest, and Duke Field (KEGI) lies about 7 nm north, so expect restricted and military airspace throughout this area and coordinate with controllers. Destin Executive Airport (KDTS) is about 8 nm south. Sea breezes and summer thunderstorms are common along the Emerald Coast.
