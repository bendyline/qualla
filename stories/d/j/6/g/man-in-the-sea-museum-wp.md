# Man in the Sea Museum

In Panama City Beach, a military diving museum keeps SEALAB I, the Navy habitat where four divers lived 192 feet below the sea off Bermuda in 1964, on outdoor display near the waters where it was first tested.

**Location:** 30.2323, -85.8934

**Categories:** Museums in Bay County, Florida, Sports museums in Florida, Maritime museums in Florida

## Introduction

In July 1964, four Navy divers climbed into a habitat lowered 192 feet beneath the ocean off Bermuda and made it their home. SEALAB I let them live on the seafloor instead of merely visiting it. Today that same habitat sits outdoors along Panama City Beach Parkway, the prize exhibit of the Man in the Sea Museum, a military diving museum that has been preserving the history of diving and underwater exploration since 1982. It rests not far from the offshore waters where it was first tested before its voyage into history.

## Papa Topside's Experiment

SEALAB I went into the water off the coast of Bermuda on July 20, 1964. It was commanded by Captain Bond, who became known as "Papa Topside," a nickname that tells you where he worked while his crew lived below. That crew numbered four: Lieutenant Commander Robert Thompson, a medical officer; Gunner's Mate First Class Lester Anderson; Chief Quartermaster Robert A. Barth; and Chief Hospital Corpsman Sanders Manning. Their assignment was to live at 192 feet rather than dive there and come back up. The ocean, however, kept its own calendar. After 11 days, with a tropical storm approaching, the experiment was halted, and the four men's stay on the bottom came to an end ahead of plan.

## Home Waters

SEALAB I's resting place is fitting. The habitat was initially tested offshore near Panama City Beach before it was deployed to Bermuda, and it now stands on outdoor display at the Man in the Sea Museum, at 17314 Panama City Beach Parkway. Visitors can see up close the structure that four men once called home on the seafloor, now resting on dry land instead of 192 feet down. The habitat anchors a museum devoted specifically to military diving, and its presence here ties this stretch of the Florida Panhandle to the Navy's experiments in living beneath the sea.

## Helmets, Masks and Submersibles

Inside, the museum traces the evolution of diving technology and underwater exploration. Its collection includes military diving equipment, underwater submersibles and an assortment of underwater masks and helmets, and many of the exhibits are hands-on, inviting visitors to handle the tools rather than simply peer at them through glass. The museum also looks beyond its walls. It hosts special events, often focused on particular chapters of diving history or marine science, and it runs educational programs; tours of the exhibits are self-guided. For a place built around a sealed habitat, it is remarkably open to the people who come through its doors.

## Flight Context

From 1,500-2,500 ft MSL, follow the line of the Gulf shoreline and the parkway running parallel to it; the museum grounds are small, so use the beach strip and road grid to orient. Tyndall Air Force Base (KPAM) is about 19 nm east-southeast and is military airspace to avoid, and Destin Executive Airport (KDTS) is about 32 nm west-northwest.
