# Jackie Robinson Ballpark

When other Florida cities turned Jackie Robinson away in 1946, this island ballpark in Daytona Beach let Jackie Robinson play, and more than a century after opening it is still hosting games.

**Location:** 29.2094, -81.0167

## Introduction

In the spring of 1946, two Florida cities refused to let Jackie Robinson play under Jim Crow rules against "mixed" athletics. Jacksonville padlocked its stadium on game day, and in Sanford the police chief threatened to cancel games and, on April 7, walked onto the field to order Robinson and teammate Johnny Wright out of an exhibition with the Montreal Royals. Scheduled games were cancelled. Daytona Beach did not turn him away. On March 17, 1946, the game went ahead here on City Island, in the Halifax River, and Daytona Beach became the first Florida city to let Robinson play in a game that spring.

## The Road to Brooklyn

Robinson had signed to play for the Montreal Royals, the Triple-A club that held spring training in Florida with the Brooklyn Dodgers. By permitting the game, Daytona Beach helped clear the path that led Robinson to break Major League Baseball's color barrier the following year, when he joined the Dodgers. In 1947 the Dodgers and their top farm clubs trained in Havana, Cuba, and the club soon built Dodgertown in Vero Beach, which opened in 1948. A statue of Robinson now stands at the ballpark's south entrance, and a law signed in January 2025 designated the park the Jackie Robinson Ballpark National Commemorative Site.

## Wooden Bleachers to Grandstand

The ballpark was already decades old when Robinson played there. It opened in 1914 as City Island Ball Park, little more than a baseball field and a set of wooden bleachers. The present grandstand and press box went up in 1962. Over the years it has been home to a long list of minor league teams, including the Daytona Beach Islanders across several stretches between 1920 and 1986, the Daytona Beach Dodgers from 1968 to 1973 and the Daytona Beach Astros from 1978 to 1984. The major league Montreal Expos held spring training here from 1973 to 1980. As of the 2021 season, it was the oldest active ballpark in Minor League Baseball, and it has been on the National Register of Historic Places since October 22, 1998.

## Weathering the Storms

An island ballpark on the Halifax River takes its share of weather. Hurricane Donna caused heavy damage in 1960. In 1999, Hurricane Floyd ripped the metal roofs off the seating, which sped up a $2 million historic renovation already in the works. Hurricane Charley brought moderate damage in 2004, enough that several home games had to move inland to Melching Field at Conrad Park in DeLand. The park keeps coming back, and it has hosted more than baseball: on May 12, 2018, the rapper Nelly headlined a concert here, with Bone Thugs-n-Harmony and Juvenile opening.

## Two Home Teams

Today the ballpark belongs to two teams. The Daytona Tortugas, a franchise founded in 1993, have won six Florida State League championships, in 1995, 2000, 2004 (shared with Tampa), 2008, 2011 and 2013. The Bethune–Cookman Wildcats have piled up their own titles, including six straight Mid-Eastern Athletic Conference baseball championships from 1999 to 2004 and seven more between 2006 and 2012. Both play their home games on the same island where, in 1946, a single exhibition helped change who was allowed to play. A renovation reported at $32 million or more, adding a player development facility, new clubhouses and museum space, was ready for the Tortugas' 2026 season opener on April 2, with more work planned along the third-base side.

## Flight Context

Jackie Robinson Ballpark sits at 29.2094°N, 81.0167°W on City Island in the Halifax River, in downtown Daytona Beach. From 1,500–3,000 feet, look for the small island between the mainland and the beachside peninsula, with the ballfield's diamond easy to spot and bridges crossing the river nearby; the Atlantic beaches lie just to the east. Daytona Beach International (KDAB) is only about 3 nm southwest, so this area sits inside Daytona's Class C airspace; contact approach before overflying. Ormond Beach Municipal (KOMN) is about 7.5 nm northwest and New Smyrna Beach Municipal (KEVB) about 10 nm south-southeast. Summer sea-breeze thunderstorms often build in the afternoon.
