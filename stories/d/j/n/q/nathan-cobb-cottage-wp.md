# Nathan Cobb Cottage

In 1897 an Ormond man built a cottage from the wreck of the schooner Nathan F. Cobb, and its walls of salvaged railroad ties still stand.

**Location:** 29.2928, -81.0483

**Categories:** Ormond Beach, Florida, 1897 establishments in Florida, Houses in Volusia County, Florida, Vernacular architecture in Florida, Tourist attractions in Florida, Houses completed in 1897

## Introduction

The walls of this little cottage in Ormond Beach are railroad ties, set on end, that came out of a shipwreck. In 1897 a local man named William Fagen built the house almost entirely from the salvaged cargo and timbers of the schooner Nathan F. Cobb, which had run aground offshore the winter before: ties for the walls, ship's flooring, balustrades and stairs. More than 120 years later, the cottage is still standing, and the ship has not entirely vanished either: remnants of its hull occasionally surface when the waves disturb the sandbars.

## Four Days Adrift

Trouble found the Nathan F. Cobb far to the north. On December 1, 1896, a nor'easter battered the schooner off the coast of North Carolina, and two of her crewmen drowned. The crippled ship then drifted for about four days, some 375 miles by the historical marker's count, until on December 5 it ran aground on a sandbar off Ormond, now Ormond Beach. Six crewmen were still aboard. Freeman Waterhouse, a bookkeeper at the Ormond Hotel, drowned while trying to rescue them; his body was never recovered. The six sailors were eventually brought ashore. Their ship never sailed again. It stayed marooned off the coast, and its cargo and most of its wooden parts were stripped and salvaged.

## A House Made From a Ship

Fagen built in the vernacular style, with what the wreck gave him. The exterior walls were vertical railroad ties. Heart pine floorboards ran the length of the cottage, and two bedrooms sat under the roof in the upper half story. A breezeway known as a dog-trot connected the house to a separate wooden kitchen, all of it built from the schooner's wood. Out front, a full-length porch was laid with planks from the ship's sole, or floor, and railed with salvaged ship balustrades. A wood shake roof with a gable dormer topped it off, and a brick chimney and fireplace, both still original, completed the house. On the south wall hung a section of the ship's quarter board, the name Nathan F. Cobb carved into it.

## Rot, Repairs and Survival

Salvaged ship timber does not last forever, and much of the cottage's history is a story of patching. The outer kitchen and dog-trot came down in the 1920s, probably because of rot, and the front porch followed in the 1940s. Around 1955, an eight-foot section of the south side was removed, the south bedroom was taken out to open a vaulted ceiling over the living room, and a bathroom was added. The shake roof and dormer gave way to composite shingles around 1965, a brick porch arrived in the 1990s, and 2014 brought a modern kitchen, bath and a rear deck. Today the house rests on brick piers, measuring roughly 400 square feet, its railroad-tie walls mostly hidden under cedar shakes. The carved quarter board now hangs inside, on the living room wall.

## A Marker on Orchard Lane

In May 2021, the Florida Bureau of Historic Preservation approved a Florida Historical Marker for the cottage, which also recognizes it as a Florida Heritage Landmark, and the marker went up in front of the house at 137 Orchard Lane on November 17, 2021; the Ormond Beach Historical Society, which owns the cottage, held its dedication ceremony on March 26, 2022. The marker describes the cottage as one of the last standing structures in Florida built from salvaged ship lumber and freight. The marker also explains why there are unlikely to be any more: wooden schooners have not been built since the 1920s, and almost all of them were decommissioned and salvaged decades ago. What remains here is a house that was once a working ship.

## Flight Context

The Nathan Cobb Cottage stands at 137 Orchard Lane in Ormond Beach, at about 29.2928°N, 81.0483°W near the Halifax River. From 1,500–2,500 feet over Ormond Beach the house itself is too small to pick out, so use the Halifax River and the Atlantic shoreline, where remnants of the Nathan F. Cobb's hull occasionally appear in the surf, as references. Ormond Beach Municipal (KOMN) is about 3.5 nm west, Daytona Beach International (KDAB) about 7 nm south, and Flagler Executive (KFIN) about 13 nm northwest. The area lies near Daytona's Class C airspace, so contact approach before overflying. Afternoon sea-breeze thunderstorms are common in summer.
