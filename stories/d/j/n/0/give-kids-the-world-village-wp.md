# Give Kids the World Village

Founded by Holocaust survivor and hotelier Henri Landwirth after a sick child's wish came too late, this 89-acre Kissimmee resort gives critically ill children and their families week-long vacations at no cost.

**Location:** 28.2919, -81.4584

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Kissimmee, Florida, Children's charities based in the United States, Resorts in Florida, Tourist attractions in Osceola County, Florida, 1989 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

On the ceiling of the Castle of Miracles, every child who has stayed at Give Kids The World Village has a star. There are a great many of them by now. This 89-acre nonprofit resort in Kissimmee, Florida, gives critically ill children and their families week-long wish vacations at no cost, and over its history it has welcomed more than 200,000 families from all 50 U.S. states and 77 countries. The village exists because of one little girl who never got her trip, and one man who had known far too much of loss to let it happen again.

## A Survivor Learns Hospitality

Henri Landwirth was born in Antwerp, Belgium, and spent five years of World War II in the Auschwitz and Mauthausen concentration camps. By the war's end both of his parents had been killed, but Henri and his twin sister, Margot, survived and found each other again. He crossed to America on a freight ship, was soon drafted into the U.S. Army and served during the Korean War. Afterward he used the G.I. Bill to study hotel management while working the night desk at Manhattan's Wellington Hotel. Having lived through so much, he promised himself that one day he would help spare others the kind of suffering he had known. In 1954 he moved to Florida to manage the 100-room Starlite Motel in Cocoa Beach, near Cape Canaveral, and he later opened a franchised Holiday Inn in Orlando.

## The Wish That Came Too Late

In the 1980s Landwirth began offering free hotel rooms to children with life-threatening illnesses who wanted to visit Walt Disney World. Then came Amy. She had leukemia, and her wish was to visit Orlando's theme parks. A respected hotelier was asked to host her family and gladly agreed, but the rest of the travel plans took too long to arrange. Time ran out, and Amy died before her wish could be granted. Landwirth was moved to vow that no child in need would ever be failed that way again. In 1986, with the support of many partners and individuals, he founded Give Kids The World, determined that no child would have to go through what Amy had. The nonprofit began small, operating out of a closet in his hotel, and the Village itself opened in 1989.

## A Storybook Village

Families stay in 166 private storybook villas, and all their meals and snacks come at no cost, made possible by donors and community partners. Around the villas spreads a small world built for children of every ability: four accessible rides and attractions such as the Enchanted Carousel, the Jurassic Junction train experience, Kelly's Sunny Swing and Lori's Magical Flight. There are two wheelchair-accessible pools, Jack's Wacky WaterWorks splash pad, horseback riding, a nature trail, gardens and a koi pond. Children can eat at the Gingerbread House Restaurant, catch a show on the pirate-themed Serendipity stage, putt at Marc's Dino Putt, fish in the Happy Harbor pond, or play a giant game of Candy Land at Matthew's Boundless Playground. Henri's Starlite Scoops, a space-themed accessible ice cream parlor, shares its name with the motel where Landwirth began in Florida.

## A Village Run on Kindness

None of it runs without people. In a typical year, volunteers fill roughly 1,800 shifts every week, by one count. Children aged 3 to 18 who have been diagnosed with a critical illness by a licensed doctor are eligible for a wish, and more than 250 wish-granting organizations around the world determine each child's eligibility and work with the village to arrange the trip. Roughly half of the critically ill children eligible for a wish are reported to choose Central Florida and its theme parks, and Give Kids The World exists to make that wish come true, adding each child's star to the ceiling of the Castle of Miracles.

## Flight Context

Give Kids The World Village lies at 28.2919 N, 81.4584 W in Kissimmee, Florida, south of Orlando. The village sits only about 1 nm west of Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM), so treat the area as the airport's traffic environment and coordinate with ATC; the runways make an easy reference for spotting the village grounds just to their west. Orlando International (KMCO) is about 11 nm to the northeast and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 17 nm to the north-northeast. Walt Disney World's permanent flight restriction lies to the northwest.
