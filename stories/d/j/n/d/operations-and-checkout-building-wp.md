# Operations and Checkout Building

Astronauts sleep, suit up and walk out to the launch pad from this Kennedy Space Center building, whose high bay has tested Apollo spacecraft, Spacelab, station modules and Orion.

**Location:** 28.5239, -80.6461

**Categories:** Kennedy Space Center, Buildings and structures in Merritt Island, Florida, Manufacturing plants, Manufacturing buildings and structures, Manufacturing plants in the United States, National Register of Historic Places in Brevard County, Florida, Historic American Engineering Record in Florida, 1964 establishments in Florida, Neil Armstrong

## Introduction

Before launch day, astronauts sleep here. The Neil Armstrong Operations and Checkout Building holds the dormitories where crews spend the night before they fly and the rooms where they suit up, and a doorway on the north wall of its rear building is where astronauts would board the Astrovan for the ride to Launch Complex 39. Among the photographs tied to the building is one from the morning of July 16, 1969, showing Neil Armstrong, Michael Collins and Buzz Aldrin at the van for their transfer to the launch pad. Forty-five years later, in 2014, a ceremony on the anniversary of Apollo 11 gave the building Armstrong's name.

## Two Blocks, Two Jobs

The five-story building stands in the Industrial Area of NASA's Kennedy Space Center on Merritt Island, designed in the International Style by architect Charles Luckman. It works as two linked blocks. One holds the crew quarters and suit-up rooms. The other is a vast spacecraft workshop for building and checking out crewed spacecraft. Its name has shifted with the eras. Planners called it the Operations and Checkout Building, but on completion in 1964 it became the Manned Spacecraft Operations Building, processing Gemini and Apollo spacecraft. During the Shuttle program it went back to its original name, known simply as the O&C. It joined the National Register of Historic Places on January 21, 2000.

## Thin Air on Demand

In 1965, two altitude chambers were installed in the High Bay to test the environmental and life support systems of the Apollo Command and Service Module and the Lunar Module. Each chamber stands 58 feet tall, with 28 feet of clear working height and an interior diameter of 33 feet. Human-rated, they could simulate altitudes up to 250,000 feet and reach that thinnest setting in one hour. Prime and backup crews used them, from Apollo 1 in October 1966 through the Apollo-Soyuz Test Project in July 1975. On October 18, 1966, Gus Grissom, Ed White and Roger Chaffee climbed into their spacecraft here for a chamber test. Barely three months later, on January 27, 1967, all three were killed when fire broke out in their spacecraft during a launch rehearsal test at Launch Complex 34.

## From Spacelab to Orion

After Apollo, the workshop kept its purpose while the spacecraft changed shape. Through the 1980s and 1990s, the O&C housed and tested Spacelab science modules before they flew aboard the Space Shuttle. In the late 1990s and 2000s, some of the modules and trusses bound for the International Space Station were checked out under the same roof. Then, on January 30, 2007, NASA held a ceremony handing the high bay to the Constellation program, which planned to make the building the final assembly facility for its Orion crew vehicle. To get ready, the state of Florida paid to clear about 50 short tons of steel stands, structures and equipment, and $55 million in renovations followed between June 2007 and January 2009. Lockheed Martin then took over as operator for Orion production. The Orion spacecraft for Artemis 1 finished assembly here and moved to the Multi-Payload Processing Facility on January 16, 2021. The Orion for Artemis II received its final testing in the building, and on April 1, 2026, Artemis II launched with Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch and Jeremy Hansen aboard on the first crewed flight beyond low Earth orbit since 1972. The Artemis III Orion is now taking shape in the same building, where technicians joined its crew and service modules on July 30, 2026.

## Flight Context

The Neil Armstrong Operations and Checkout Building stands at 28.5239°N, 80.6461°W in the Industrial Area of Kennedy Space Center on Merritt Island. From 3,000–5,000 feet, look for its large rectangular high bay among the center's cluster of office and processing buildings; the Vehicle Assembly Building rises unmistakably to the north, and the Banana River and Indian River Lagoon frame the island. Space Coast Regional (KTIX) is about 8 nm west, the Cape Canaveral Skid Strip (KXMR) about 5 nm southeast, and the NASA Shuttle Landing Facility (KTTS) about 6 nm north-northwest. Kennedy Space Center airspace is restricted and closed around launches, so check NOTAMs. Summer thunderstorms build most afternoons.
