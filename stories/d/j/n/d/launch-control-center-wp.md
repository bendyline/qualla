# Launch Control Center

Beside the Vehicle Assembly Building, the four-story Launch Control Center has run NASA's countdowns from Pad 39 since Apollo 4, then handed each rocket to Houston seconds after liftoff.

**Location:** 28.5853, -80.6493

**Categories:** Kennedy Space Center, Buildings and structures in Merritt Island, Florida, National Register of Historic Places in Brevard County, Florida

## Introduction

For roughly the first ten to fifteen seconds of a launch from Pad 39, the rocket belongs to Florida. Controllers here in the Launch Control Center run the pre-launch checks, the booster and the spacecraft, right up to the moment the vehicle climbs clear of its tower. Then control passes away: to the Christopher C. Kraft Jr. Mission Control Center in Houston for NASA flights, or to SpaceX's mission control in Hawthorne, California, for SpaceX launches. For NASA's launches, everything before that handoff happens in this building.

## Four Stories Beside a Giant

The Launch Control Center is modest only by comparison. It stands four stories tall, attached to the southeast corner of the Vehicle Assembly Building on Merritt Island, and it holds offices, telemetry, tracking and instrumentation equipment, and the control rooms known as firing rooms. Dating to the mid-1960s and built in the International Style, it manages launches from Kennedy Space Center's Launch Complex 39 and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Since February 2022 it has formally carried a name as well: the Rocco A. Petrone Launch Control Center, honoring NASA's former director of launch operations.

## From Apollo 4 to Artemis

The building's first launch came on November 9, 1967, when it sent the uncrewed Apollo 4, designated Apollo-Saturn 501, aloft. Just over a year later, on December 21, 1968, it launched its first human crew, on Apollo 8. The Space Shuttle program ran its countdowns from here too. NASA then renovated the center for the Space Launch System, whose missions began in 2022 with Artemis 1. On April 1, 2026, Artemis II lifted off from Pad 39B carrying Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch and Jeremy Hansen on the first crewed flight beyond low Earth orbit since Apollo 17 in 1972.

## Who Says Go

A launch team works through a chain of roles, and the Shuttle era defined many of them. At the top sits the Launch Director, who polls the team and makes the final "go" or "no go" call; eight different people held that job for the Shuttle between 1981 and 2011. The NASA Test Director oversees all pre-launch testing and answers for the safety of everyone on the pad once fueling has begun. The Superintendent of Range Operations makes sure tracking and communications are ready, keeps downrange airspace and splashdown areas clear, and watches the weather near the pad. The Flow Director, responsible for readying the spacecraft, stays in the room as an adviser, while contractor test conductors monitor the orbiter's systems and the payloads it carries.

## Nine Minutes to Go

The final minutes of a Shuttle countdown belonged to a machine. From T-minus 9 minutes, the automated Ground Launch Sequencer ran the clock, watched by a dedicated engineer who could call a manual hold up to T-minus 31 seconds. After that, only an automatic cutoff could stop the launch, and it would recycle the clock back to T-minus 20 minutes. That usually pushed the launch beyond its window, forcing a scrub and a 24-hour turnaround. Behind all of it stood the Launch Processing System, a highly automated, computer-controlled system that oversaw the entire checkout and launch process.

## A Firing Room for Rent

The firing rooms have been rebuilt as the rockets changed. Extensive renovation of Firing Room 4 was completed in 2006, and a 2018 photograph shows the freshly renovated Firing Room 1. Firing Room 4 also took on a commercial tenant. SpaceX leased it as its launch control center for Pad 39A and ran launches from it until mid-2023, when the company began using a launch control center of its own at its Hangar X facility, in time for the Crew-7 mission in August.

## Flight Context

The Launch Control Center sits at 28.5853°N, 80.6493°W, attached to the southeast corner of the Vehicle Assembly Building, whose enormous cube is the unmistakable landmark of Kennedy Space Center from almost any altitude. From 3,000–5,000 feet, the crawlerway runs east from the VAB toward Pads 39A and 39B on the Atlantic shore. The NASA Shuttle Landing Facility (KTTS) is about 3 nm northwest, Space Coast Regional (KTIX) about 9 nm west-southwest, and the Cape Canaveral Skid Strip (KXMR) about 8 nm south-southeast. Kennedy Space Center airspace is restricted and subject to temporary flight restrictions around launches, so check NOTAMs and plan to view the complex from outside the restricted area. Summer afternoons often bring thunderstorms.
