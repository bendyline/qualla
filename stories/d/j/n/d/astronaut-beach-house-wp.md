# Astronaut Beach House

NASA bought out an entire Cape Canaveral subdivision in 1963 and kept one house, which became the place astronauts and their families gather before launch.

**Location:** 28.5742, -80.5719

**Categories:** Kennedy Space Center, Houses completed in 1962, Houses in Brevard County, Florida

## Introduction

In 1963, NASA bought an entire neighborhood and then spared a single house. The Neptune Beach subdivision at Cape Canaveral stood in the way of an expanding Kennedy Space Center, so the agency acquired it through eminent domain for $31,500 and cleared the private homes away. A nearby gas station and store did not survive either. One two-story house, built only the year before, was left standing, and for decades since it has been where astronauts come to gather by the sea before they launch.

## Three Names for One House

On paper, the house has long gone by other names. NASA first designated it the Astronaut Training and Rehabilitation Building, a title that sounds like a clinic. A 1997 remodel made it the Center Director's Conference Center, which sounds like an office park, and NASA's current name for it is the Kennedy Space Center Beach House and Conference Center. Throughout its time with NASA, though, the people who used it gave it a plainer name: the Astronaut Beach House, or simply The Beach House. It was built in 1962 as an ordinary home in a subdivision meant for ordinary residents, and the nickname keeps that origin in view.

## Neighbors That Roar

Location is what makes the house remarkable. It stands about 50 meters back from the shoreline, directly between Launch Pads 40 and 41. Pad 41 has hosted Titan rockets starting in 1965, then Atlas V from 2002 and Vulcan Centaur from 2024. In 2024 it also launched Boeing's Crew Flight Test, carrying astronauts Barry Wilmore and Sunita Williams to the International Space Station, the first crewed launch from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station since Apollo 7 in 1968. So a quiet beach house, with the Atlantic on one side, has launch towers on either flank.

## Barbecues Before Liftoff

What happens inside is more human than technical. The house serves as a private conference center, but its best-known role is social. It has long been a gathering point for astronauts before launch, and crews have brought their families here for private get-togethers and barbecues before they fly. NASA said in 2018 that astronauts preparing for Commercial Crew and Orion flights planned to bring their families too, and it also uses the house for retreats and program reviews. The house holds memorabilia, and one collection stands out: wine bottles signed by crew members, each identified by its mission decal. Together, the bottles add up to a record of the people who passed through on their way to space, kept not in a museum but in a house by the beach.

## Flight Context

The Astronaut Beach House sits at 28.5742°N, 80.5719°W on the Atlantic shore of Cape Canaveral, roughly midway between Launch Complexes 40 and 41. From 3,000–5,000 feet, the pads' tall service structures are the obvious landmarks along this stretch of beach, with the Banana River and Merritt Island's marshes to the west. The Cape Canaveral Skid Strip (KXMR) is about 6 nm south, the NASA Shuttle Landing Facility (KTTS) about 7 nm west-northwest, and Space Coast Regional (KTIX) about 12.5 nm west-southwest. This coast sits inside launch-related restricted airspace that is activated frequently; check NOTAMs before planning a pass. Summer afternoons often bring thunderstorms, so mornings usually give the best visibility.
