# Shuttle Landing Facility

Kennedy Space Center's 15,000-foot runway welcomed 78 Space Shuttle landings, then found new life with spaceplanes, lunar-lander tests, speed records and basking alligators.

**Location:** 28.6150, -80.6945

**Categories:** Airports in Brevard County, Florida, Space Shuttle program, Kennedy Space Center, Buildings and structures in Merritt Island, Florida

## Introduction

Locals have a nickname for the runway at the Shuttle Landing Facility: the "gator tanning facility." Kennedy Space Center is home to some 4,000 alligators, and some of them regularly haul out onto the warm pavement to bask in the sun. It is a strangely peaceful image for a strip built to bring spacecraft home from orbit, and it captures the place well. For all its history, the runway has always shared its ground with the wildlife of Merritt Island.

## Nearly Three Miles, Kept Clear

The numbers explain why a Shuttle could land here. The facility covers 500 acres around a single runway, 15/33, which runs 15,000 feet, nearly three miles, and spans 300 feet across, making it one of the longest runways in the world. Another 305 meters of paved overrun extends from each end. Just off the southern end stood the Mate-Demate Device, used whenever a Shuttle traveled by the Shuttle Carrier Aircraft. The field also served NASA training jets and civilian aircraft, and since 2015, Space Florida has managed and operated it under a 30-year lease from NASA, with a control tower and the services needed for horizontal launch and landing. A runway surrounded by wildlife needs defending. The contractor EG&G provided air traffic control and managed hazards to landing aircraft, above all birds. During Shuttle landings, a Bird Team kept both local and migratory birds away using pyrotechnics, blank rounds fired from shotguns, and a ring of 25 propane cannons arranged around the facility. It was an elaborate ritual of noise, performed so that a returning orbiter would meet an empty sky.

## Landing Where It Launched

Columbia was the first Shuttle to arrive, riding in on the Shuttle Carrier Aircraft on March 24, 1979. It would be years before one landed here under its own wings. Early Shuttle missions came down at Edwards Air Force Base in California, except STS-3, which landed at White Sands Space Harbor, while the Florida runway continued testing and crews built their skills where the margin for error was far wider than at the SLF, with its surrounding water hazards. Then, on February 11, 1984, Challenger returned from STS-41-B and touched down here, the first time a spacecraft had landed at its own launch site. Discovery made the first night landing at the SLF on September 22, 1993, at the end of STS-51. In all, 78 Shuttle missions landed on this runway before the program's final landing in July 2011.

## Life After the Shuttle

The runway never went quiet. In 2012 the first vehicle of NASA's Project Morpheus, an experimental lander, arrived for advanced testing. During an August 9, 2012, test at the SLF, a vehicle exploded, though no one was hurt, and new vehicles and upgraded iterations carried on, flying tests including free flights here in 2013 and 2014. After NASA signed an agreement in October 2014, Boeing upgraded Orbiter Processing Facility 1 for the X-37B program, and on May 7, 2017, the X-37B's OTV-4 mission landed on Runway 15; OTV-5 and OTV-6 later came home on Runway 33. Zero Gravity Corporation has flown passengers from here into brief periods of weightlessness, the Virgin Atlantic GlobalFlyer made record-setting attempts, and today Starfighters Space flies supersonic F-104 jets from the runway as it works toward launching small satellites from them.

## 283 Miles an Hour

A runway this long also tempts people with fast cars. NASCAR teams tested vehicles here in 2010, and automakers including Tesla and Volvo use the runway for aerodynamic testing. In 2012, Johnny Bohmer of Performance Power drove a modified Ford GT down the SLF and set the Guinness World Records mark for the fastest standing mile by a street car, at 283 mph, a record that still stood as of March 2023. Bohmer credited the partnership with NASA, which let his team test technologies and collect engineering data, saying NASA had given them access to "a one-of-a-kind facility."

## Flight Context

The Shuttle Landing Facility (KTTS) lies at 28.615°N, 80.6945°W on Merritt Island, with its single runway, 15/33, running 15,000 by 300 feet, and it is unmistakable from the air: a vast, straight strip cutting through marsh and scrub northwest of the Vehicle Assembly Building, with the Indian River to the west and the launch pads on the Atlantic shore to the east. Space Coast Regional (KTIX) is about 8 nm southwest, Arthur Dunn Airpark (X21) about 7 nm west, and the Cape Canaveral Skid Strip (KXMR) about 11 nm southeast. Kennedy Space Center airspace is restricted and closed around launches, so check NOTAMs and current facility procedures before planning any flight nearby. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
