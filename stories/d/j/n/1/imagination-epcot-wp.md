# Imagination! (Epcot)

Epcot's glass-pyramid pavilion devoted to creativity has cycled through Michael Jackson, Rick Moranis and three versions of its dark ride, and learned the hard way not to sideline a small purple dragon.

**Location:** 28.3729, -81.5512

**Categories:** Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Epcot, Imagination! (Epcot), Future World (Epcot), World Celebration, 1982 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

In 1999, Disney reopened the ride at the heart of its Imagination pavilion and gave its most beloved character almost nothing to do. Figment, a small purple dragon with a runaway imagination, appeared only in cameos until a short line of dialogue at the very end, and his companion Dreamfinder, a jolly, wizard-like scientist, was gone altogether. Fans were upset. By the end of 2001, Disney had closed the ride to rework it and put Figment back where he belonged. It is a fitting episode for a pavilion themed to human imagination, creativity and the arts, a place that has spent four decades reinventing itself at Epcot.

## Magic Journeys and a Slide Show

The pavilion opened with Epcot in 1982, in what is now the park's World Celebration section at the Walt Disney World Resort in Bay Lake, Florida. At first it was essentially a theater. The 3-D film Magic Journeys began playing on October 1, 1982, set to a song of the same name by the Sherman Brothers, and a slide presentation called Makin' Memories, a history of photography, served as its pre-show. Kodak, the pavilion's title sponsor, put its name on the building from the start.

The namesake ride arrived the following spring. Journey Into Imagination opened on March 5, 1983, and the building became the Journey Into Imagination Pavilion. From 1983 to 1986 it held the ride, the 3-D film and an upstairs play space called ImageWorks, all beneath the pavilion's glass pyramids, with leap frog fountains outside.

## Michael Jackson, Then Rick Moranis

The pavilion's Magic Eye Theater became a showcase for ever more ambitious 3-D films. Magic Journeys closed in February 1986, and on September 12, 1986, the theater reopened with Captain EO, a more advanced 3-D film executive produced by George Lucas, directed by Francis Ford Coppola and starring Michael Jackson in the title role. It ran until July 6, 1994, and that November it was succeeded by Honey, I Shrunk the Audience!, with Rick Moranis as Professor Wayne Szalinski and Eric Idle as Dr. Nigel Channing, chairman of the fictional Imagination Institute.

Then history circled back. After Michael Jackson died on June 25, 2009, Disney officials weighed bringing Captain EO back. The film, retitled Captain EO Tribute, returned to Disneyland in February 2010 and to the Imagination! pavilion on July 2, 2010, for a limited run. It lasted until December 6, 2015. Since December 23, 2015, the theater has hosted the Disney & Pixar Short Film Festival.

## Where Did Figment Go?

In October 1998, the original Journey Into Imagination closed for renovation, and the pavilion changed almost everywhere a visitor could look. Its purple and blue exterior was repainted in new colors led by navy blue. The original glass sign gave way to one of wood and metal. The leap frog fountains were shrunk to make room for a new gift shop at the ride's exit. The glass pyramids survived, but the upstairs ImageWorks closed, replaced downstairs by ImageWorks: The "What If" Labs.

The new ride, Journey Into Your Imagination, opened on October 1, 1999, and the pavilion was renamed Imagination! It did not last. On October 8, 2001, Disney closed the ride for modifications. Its replacement, Journey into Imagination with Figment, opened on June 2, 2002, with the dragon back in a prominent role. Dreamfinder, however, did not return, at least not for more than two decades: at the D23 Expo on August 15, 2026, Disney announced a new Journey Into Imagination at Epcot that will reunite Dreamfinder and Figment, without giving an opening date.

## Upstairs, Behind the Glass

For a brief time after Figment's comeback, a walk-around Figment greeted visitors in the former Kodak shop between the main pavilion and the theater. That spot is now unlabeled, though its décor can still be glimpsed through the windows. In the summer of 2006, the closed upstairs ImageWorks briefly became Dr. Drakken's base, the finale of a test run of a Kim Possible interactive adventure, with "Kimunicators" loaned out at a station in World Showcase.

In August 2010, Kodak ended its sponsorship after nearly 28 years, a partnership that dated to the pavilion's opening. The old upstairs space finally found a permanent use in June 2016, when it reopened as a lounge exclusively for Disney Vacation Club members.

## Flight Context

The Imagination! pavilion stands on the western side of Epcot's World Celebration area at approximately 28.3729°N, 81.5512°W, within Walt Disney World southwest of Orlando, Florida. From 3,000–4,000 feet, orient on Epcot's geodesic sphere, Spaceship Earth, and look just to its southwest for the pavilion's glass pyramids. Nearby airports: Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 9 statute miles southeast and Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 15 statute miles east-northeast, inside Orlando's Class B airspace. Check current NOTAMs for flight restrictions over the Disney parks. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are frequent over central Florida.
