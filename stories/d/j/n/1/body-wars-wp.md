# Body Wars

Epcot's Body Wars shrank riders to cell size and sent them careening through a human bloodstream, a Leonard Nimoy-directed thrill ride that some loved and others could barely stomach.

**Location:** 28.3752, -81.5467

**Categories:** Amusement rides introduced in 1989, Amusement rides closed in 2007, Amusement rides manufactured by Rediffusion Simulation, 1989 films, Human body, Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Epcot, Simulator rides, Walt Disney Parks and Resorts films, Future World (Epcot)

## Introduction

The patient had a splinter in his left index finger, and that was the whole reason for the trip. At Body Wars, a motion simulator inside Epcot's Wonders of Life pavilion, guests signed on with the fictional Miniaturized Exploration Technologies corporation, styled MET, to be shrunk down and injected beneath a volunteer's skin, where they would watch white blood cells go to work on the splinter. Things did not go to plan. They never do on a ride called Body Wars.

## A Pavilion for the Human Body

On January 22, 1988, Epcot announced a new pavilion for Future World East. Called Wonders of Life and themed to health care, it would sit between Universe of Energy and Horizons, with new restaurants, stores and several attractions, all sponsored by MetLife. Construction started that February. The headline attraction was to be a motion simulator, built by Rediffusion Simulation and running on the Advanced Technology Leisure Application Simulator technology already carrying guests through Star Tours at Disneyland.

Wonders of Life opened to the public on October 19, 1989, and Body Wars drew a 90-minute wait on its first day. Body Wars did not have the concept to itself for long. Just two months later a similar ride, Star Tours, opened at Disney's Hollywood Studios.

## Pioneering the Universe Within

The fiction started in the queue, which ran off the left side of the Wonders of Life dome into a separate wing of the building. Announcements welcomed guests as "MET Observation Team Members," and until 1993 signs informed them that the company had been founded in 2063, under the motto "Pioneering the Universe Within." A preshow video in the queue briefed them on the mission, and along the way they passed through two "Dermatopic Purification" stations separated by a hallway lined with TV sets.

That mission was a rendezvous. Dr. Cynthia Lair had volunteered to be miniaturized to observe the splinter, and guests aboard vehicle Bravo 229 were to meet up with her and bring her back out. Their pilot was Captain Braddock. The briefing added one memorable statistic: their "LGS 250" probe weighed about 26 tons at full size, but once miniaturized, it would weigh less than a drop of water.

## A Wrong Turn at the Heart

Bravo 229 rolled from its bay into the miniaturization room, where technicians focused a "particle reducer" on the ship, and the crew slipped under the subject's skin. At the splinter, Dr. Lair began a cell count and was suddenly sucked into a capillary. Braddock went after her into an unauthorized area, steering past the heart into the right ventricle and on into the lungs, where a white blood cell had Lair in its grip. He freed her with the ship's lasers, but by then power was nearly gone. Lair's solution was audacious: ride an artery up to the brain and borrow some of its energy. A neuron made contact with the ship and restored its power, and the crew de-miniaturized outside the body to congratulations from Mission Control.

Tim Matheson played Braddock, Elisabeth Shue played Lair and Dakin Matthews voiced Mission Control. Leonard Nimoy directed the film. It was the shortest he had ever directed, he said, and to intensify the sense of motion his crew built a set that actually moved, rocking it during filming to match the simulator's pitching and rolling.

## From Thrill Ride to Storeroom

Body Wars earned a mixed reception from guests. Some praised the thrilling experience, while others complained of motion sickness and nausea, both because it was considered a rough ride and because of the subject matter itself: not everyone wants to hurtle through a stranger's bloodstream. Its popularity declined over the years, and in 2004 the park announced that it would operate only seasonally. The entire Wonders of Life pavilion closed on January 1, 2007.

Body Wars was never replaced so much as slowly emptied. By November 2014, all four simulators had been dismantled and removed, and the show building was being used for storage for the Epcot Food & Wine Festival and the Flower & Garden Festival. The queue survived longer, stripped of most of its lighting and electronics, but by November 2016 it too was being taken apart piece by piece. Red archive tags went onto the MET sign at the entrance and the Body Wars safety sign near the exit. On February 21, 2019, a new Play! Pavilion was announced to replace all of Wonders of Life, ride included. That plan stalled: in January 2023 Disney said it was reevaluating the concept and removed the pavilion from park maps, and by late 2025 the Play! Pavilion was reported to be abandoned. Disney has made no formal announcement either way, and in June 2026 the pavilion's flag was reported to have been taken down from Epcot's entrance plaza. The old building remains closed.

## Flight Context

The former Body Wars show building is part of the Wonders of Life pavilion on the east side of Epcot's Future World, at approximately 28.3752°N, 81.5467°W, within Walt Disney World southwest of Orlando, Florida. From 3,000–4,000 feet, orient on Epcot's geodesic sphere, Spaceship Earth, and look to its east for the pavilion's dome.
