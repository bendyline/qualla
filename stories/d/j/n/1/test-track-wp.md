# Test Track

Born from a General Motors pavilion and nearly two years late after its wheels and its control software failed, Epcot's Test Track sends riders racing around the outside of its building at about 65 miles per hour, Walt Disney World's fastest ride.

**Location:** 28.3733, -81.5477

**Categories:** Epcot, Amusement rides introduced in 1999, General Motors, Chevrolet, Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions

## Introduction

The wheels were the first thing to give out. When Walt Disney Imagineering set out to build a ride that would hurl cars around the outside of a building at highway speed, the wheels on the ride vehicles could not stand up to the demands of the course and the speed. Fixing that, and the problems that followed (including a control system that could run only six cars at a time), pushed Test Track's opening at Epcot back by nearly two years. When it finally worked, it delivered a speed trial on a track around the outside of the building at a top speed of about 65 miles per hour, making it Walt Disney World's fastest ride.

## Out of the World of Motion

Test Track occupies the building that once held World of Motion, a General Motors-sponsored ride through a fanciful history of transportation, from animal travel in prehistoric times to automobiles, trains and planes. When that sponsorship expired, GM was in the middle of layoffs and cutbacks and had to decide whether to sign again. It did, and GM wanted Disney to build a new ride on the site focused specifically on its automobiles. Imagineers had visited GM's Milford Proving Grounds in Michigan in 1976, and they made a second trip while designing the new ride. Epcot announced the transformation in November 1995. World of Motion closed on January 2, 1996, and its interior was stripped bare, while outside and behind the building the elevated track for the high-speed finale was already rising.

## Crash Dummies and a Surprise Test

The ride was supposed to open in May 1997. On October 15 of that year, park officials announced a delay until at least 1998. Test Track finally soft-opened on December 19, 1998, but it was still prone to breakdowns and did not officially open until March 17, 1999. The original version cast guests as passengers in a GM testing facility. The queue wound past tire and car-door tests, an anechoic chamber for radio receivers and a station for crash test dummies. In a briefing room, the host, Bill McKim, played by John Michael Higgins, told a technician named Sherrie which tests to set up, ending with one final "surprise test" as a video of a car smashing into a barrier played and the doors opened. Afterward, a walk-through post-show called The Assembly Experience placed riders on the simulated floor of a vast assembly plant, where doors, seats and engines glided overhead, the floor rumbled with each die press, and real GM workers talked on video about their products and their work.

## Design Your Own Car

The ride closed on April 15, 2012, and reopened that December under Chevrolet sponsorship with a new premise. Guests became "designers," designing their own Chevrolet Custom Concept Vehicle in a design studio, then uploading it to a "sim-car" that was scored in four tests: capability, efficiency, responsiveness and power. This version was full of nods to the building's past. One roadside sign reads "Motion Lane 82, General Motorway 99, Bowtie Boulevard 12," pointing to the three attractions that have occupied the building and their opening years. A tire bears the words "WED Performance," honoring Walter Elias Disney's initials and the original name of Walt Disney Imagineering, and World of Motion logos turn up on trash bins, murals and the attraction's main sign.

## Back to the Future of Motion

In September 2023, Disney announced another retheming, this time inspired by World of Motion itself. Test Track closed on June 17, 2024, General Motors announced that December that it would return as sponsor, and the third version reopened in July 2025. The design studio is gone. Guests now wind through a car showroom before riding with a male narrator and a female AI car through a digital city, a futuristic house where mirrors transform the car's look in an ode to a similar effect in World of Motion, and a wooded highway where the AI takes the wheel and declares, "It's always fun to be free." After a near miss with a semi truck in a tunnel and a pass through a giant projection dome of flying cars, the car bursts outside into the high-speed loop, the one element every version has kept. The exit mural nods to the style of circa-1982 EPCOT Center, and the World of Motion logo is now the attraction's official symbol.

## Flight Context

Test Track stands at about 28.373°N, 81.548°W at Epcot in the Walt Disney World Resort in Bay Lake, southwest of Orlando; its outdoor loop of elevated track wraps around the exterior of the ride building. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a standing NOTAM, reported as a 3 nm radius up to 3,000 feet AGL), so view the resort only from outside the restricted area and check the current NOTAM text before planning a nearby route. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) lies about 8 nm to the southeast and Orlando International (KMCO) about 13 nm to the east-northeast. Central Florida summers bring near-daily afternoon thunderstorms; mornings and the drier winter months are clearer.
