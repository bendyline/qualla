# Dragon Challenge

For eighteen years, two intertwined inverted coasters at Islands of Adventure chased each other as dueling dragons, first as Fire and Ice, then as a Chinese Fireball and a Hungarian Horntail from the world of Harry Potter.

**Location:** 28.4736, -81.4733

**Categories:** 1999 establishments in Florida, 2017 disestablishments in Florida, Amusement rides based on film franchises, Former roller coasters in Florida, Harry Potter in amusement parks, Inverted roller coasters manufactured by Bolliger & Mabillard, Universal Islands of Adventure, Licensed properties at Universal Destinations & Experiences, Roller coasters in Orlando, Florida, Roller coasters opened in 1999

## Introduction

Before every duel, the dragons stepped on a scale. Once riders had loaded, each train was weighed so that operators could adjust the dispatch times, and if one train came out heavier, it left the station after its lighter rival. The goal was precise choreography: two inverted roller coasters, launched side by side, had to arrive at three near-miss points on their tangled courses at the same moment. For much of its life, that was the thrill of Dueling Dragons, later renamed Dragon Challenge, at Universal's Islands of Adventure in Orlando, Florida.

## Fire and Ice on the Lost Continent

Universal announced the ride in 1997 as part of Islands of Adventure, a new second theme park at the Universal Orlando Resort. Swiss firm Bolliger & Mabillard designed it as a dueling inverted coaster, and it was set in the Merlinwood area of the Lost Continent, a fairy-tale land. It was finished on time and debuted with the park on May 28, 1999. At the time, it was the only fully inverted dueling roller coaster in the world.

The original story was simple and fierce. Two dragons, Fire and Ice, had wrecked a castle, and the queue wound through its ruins, down into dungeons dressed with human skeletons, torches and cobwebs. At an intersection, riders chose their dragon: left for Fire, right for Ice.

## Choreographing a Near Miss

The two trains climbed adjacent 125-foot lift hills, then split onto separate courses of roughly 3,200 feet of steel track each. The fire dragon's red track dropped 115 feet and reached 60 miles per hour, while the ice dragon's blue track dropped 95 feet and topped out at 55. Both courses packed five inversions into an identical ride time of 2 minutes and 25 seconds, and both rose on white supports.

The courses were built to flirt. The fire train came up an Immelmann and over an air-time hill as the ice train rolled through a zero-gravity roll, the first of three meeting points. The trains met again at a vertical loop, and a final time at a corkscrew. Each eight-car train carried 32 riders and was styled as a dragon, red for fire and blue for ice, with the front seat shaped like a mouth and the rest resembling hands.

## Enter the Triwizard Tournament

On May 31, 2007, Universal announced The Wizarding World of Harry Potter, and Dueling Dragons was folded into the new land. Queue work began in the third quarter of 2009, the ride closed in early 2010 for its refurbishment, and it reopened in mid-March 2010. On June 18, 2010, when the whole Wizarding World opened, it officially became Dragon Challenge. Fire became the Chinese Fireball and Ice became the Hungarian Horntail.

The castle ruins gave way to the Triwizard Tournament. Banners cheered on its four champions, and guests walked past the Weasleys' crashed flying Ford Anglia into the Champions' Tent, where the Triwizard Cup glowed on a large pedestal. Dark tunnels led to the stations, and at the final fork riders still had to choose, Fireball to the left and Horntail to the right, before stepping beneath dragons projected on the ceiling.

## The Duel Called Off

The near misses that made the ride famous also created its gravest problem. On July 31, 2011, an unidentified object struck a rider in the eye, and he lost the sight of it; one account says it was his only good eye. The ride was closed for less than 24 hours before Universal concluded it was safe. Then, around August 10, 2011, another rider was hit by an object and hurt. Universal stopped running the two coasters simultaneously while both incidents were investigated, and in October 2011 officials ended the dueling permanently.

The crackdown on loose objects kept tightening. In April 2015, permanent metal detectors went in at the entrance, staffed by security officers with wands, under a zero-tolerance policy for phones, wallets, coins, watches and cameras. Violators could be sent to the lockers or even ejected from the park without a refund.

## Scrapyard and Tribute

Without its duel, the ride had lost its signature trick. On July 24, 2017, Universal announced a new roller coaster for 2019, and officials, citing that Dragon Challenge had reached the end of its service life, closed it permanently on September 4, 2017. By late that month it was being dismantled and sent to the scrapyard. Its replacement, Hagrid's Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure, opened on June 13, 2019, leaving The Incredible Hulk Coaster as the park's only remaining Bolliger & Mabillard coaster.

The dragons are not entirely forgotten. In Hagrid's queue, just past the room with cages on the ceiling, a mural on the left wall shows fire and ice dragons beside the words "Dueling Club." And in 2023, Halloween Horror Nights built a haunted house on the original concept, "Dueling Dragons: Choose Thy Fate," in which guests once again pick a path and a victor.

## Flight Context

The former Dragon Challenge site, now occupied by Hagrid's Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure, is in The Wizarding World of Harry Potter area of Universal Islands of Adventure at approximately 28.4736°N, 81.4733°W, southwest of downtown Orlando, Florida. From 2,500–3,500 feet, look for the tightly packed Universal Orlando Resort parks and the looping track of The Incredible Hulk Coaster in the same park. Nearby airports: Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 10 statute miles east-northeast and Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 10 statute miles east-southeast, beneath Orlando's Class B airspace.
