# The Amazing Adventures of Spider-Man

Islands of Adventure's Spider-Man ride fused a roaming motion-base vehicle with moving 3D projection in 1999, and won Best Dark Ride a decade running.

**Location:** 28.4701, -81.4696

**Categories:** Universal Islands of Adventure, Dark rides, Marvel Comics in amusement parks, Amusement rides introduced in 1999, Licensed properties at Universal Destinations & Experiences

## Introduction

Early in the ride, a trash truck nearly flattens your vehicle, and the man at the wheel, in a quick cameo, is Stan Lee. The ride has not forgotten him. After Lee died on November 12, 2018, a desk in the attraction's queue was decorated with Spider-Man items, flowers and photographs of him. The Amazing Adventures of Spider-Man, a 3D motion simulator and dark ride at Universal Islands of Adventure in Orlando, opened to the public on May 28, 1999, as part of the park's debut. It has since collected many of the amusement industry's top awards.

## Cub Reporters in the Scoop

The adventure starts inside the Daily Bugle building on Marvel Super Hero Island. Riders become "cub reporters," don "night vision goggles" that are really 3D glasses, and board the Scoop, the newspaper's new 12-passenger reporting vehicle. Their assignment leads straight into trouble: the Sinister Syndicate is holding the Statue of Liberty hostage in a warehouse.

The villains come one after another. Electro tries to shock the Scoop with a sparking wire, and the vehicle absorbs the charge and shudders. Scream lunges before Doctor Octopus pulls her aside and fires his anti-gravity ray. In the sewers, Hydro-Man slams a pipe into the car while Spider-Man fights him off. Doctor Octopus smashes through a brick wall and grabs the front bumper, only to stumble back when it breaks loose. By the river, Hobgoblin hurls pumpkin bombs, and a fireball explodes overhead. In the climax, Doctor Octopus uses his anti-gravity cannon to lift the Scoop to a simulated height of 400 feet.

## A Ride System Built From Scratch

Development began in 1996, at a time when, as Allen Ambrosini of At The Park Magazine put it, theme park guests were growing more sophisticated and wanted theming and thrills combined in a single immersive experience. The original idea was an Omnimover with 3D and 4D effects. What emerged instead was a prototype: a motion-base vehicle mounted on a track-roaming platform.

The platform carries each car forward through the show scenes, while a yaw motor and a Stewart platform let the vehicle turn a full 360 degrees and tilt at different angles along the way. Philip Hettema, William Mason and Gary Goddard are credited as the system's inventors, Oceaneering International co-developed the vehicles, Dynamic Structures built the track switches and Thierry Coup designed the Scoop itself. The system outgrew its first home: Universal later used a similar system for Transformers: The Ride 3D in Singapore, Hollywood, Florida and Beijing.

## Bending the Picture

Combining 3D film with moving viewers created a problem, because an image looks distorted from an angle. The ride's polarized system pairs two projectors for each screen, with filters that work with each lens of the glasses to overcome the way off-center seats weaken traditional 3D. Kleiser-Walczak, the studio that supplied the stereoscopic animation and custom software, went further with a process called "squinching," which Universal later patented. The process calculated how distorted an image would look from a particular viewing angle, then built the same distortion into the projection in the opposite direction to cancel it out. Models mapped the ride path and the vehicle's point of view before full-scale prototypes were built.

The screens have plenty of help. Fog, fire, wind, heat, mist, strobe lights and water spray all fire under a central control system that times them to a thirtieth of a second.

## A Decade at the Top

Critics were enthusiastic. Howard Shapiro of The Philadelphia Inquirer predicted it was "bound to become one of the all-time attractions of theme parks anywhere," and the Los Angeles Times' Brady MacDonald once ranked it his second favorite ride in the world, behind Disneyland's Indiana Jones Adventure. It won Amusement Today's Golden Ticket Award for Best Dark Ride every year from 2001 through 2010, and by some counts from 1999, until Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey, in the same park, took the top spot in 2011. It also won a Thea Award in 2000.

A second version opened at Universal Studios Japan in Osaka on January 23, 2004, and that park's attendance reportedly rose from 8.8 million in 2003 to 9.9 million in 2004. It closed on January 22, 2024. The Orlando original kept evolving: a refurbishment remastered its film in high definition, installed Infitec digital projectors and reopened it on March 8, 2012, in time for The Amazing Spider-Man.

## Flight Context

The ride's show building stands near 28.470°N, 81.470°W on Marvel Super Hero Island in Universal Islands of Adventure, part of the Universal Orlando complex in Orlando, Florida, directly beside Universal Studios Florida. From about 2,500–3,500 feet, look for the park's loop of themed islands; the working Pharos Lighthouse at the Port of Entry, lit each night, marks the main gate. Nearby airports: Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 10 miles east-northeast, Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 10 miles east-southeast (check the Orlando terminal-area chart for airspace before overflying), and Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 13 miles south. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
