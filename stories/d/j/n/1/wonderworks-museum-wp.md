# WonderWorks (museum)

The original WonderWorks in Orlando looks like a building that a storm tore from the ground and dropped on its roof onto a warehouse, and inside, visitors get to feel hurricanes, earthquakes and a bed of 3,500 nails for themselves.

**Location:** 28.4339, -81.4715

## Introduction

The building appears to have lost a fight with the weather. Its roof is on the bottom, its foundation points at the sky, and it sits on top of a warehouse as though a storm ripped it free of the ground and dropped it there upside down. That is exactly the illusion WonderWorks intends. The Orlando location, which opened in March 1998, was the original home of these science-themed entertainment centers, and its topsy-turvy design became the signature of every WonderWorks that followed.

## Upside Down by Design

The idea came from Orlando architect Terry O. Nichoson of Nichoson Design International, who is credited with the upside-down design at the original Orlando site. Nichoson went on to serve as the main design consultant for all later WonderWorks locations, and the concept traveled well. The company now has six locations in the United States, including Pigeon Forge, Tennessee; Panama City Beach, Florida; Myrtle Beach, South Carolina; Syracuse, New York, inside Destiny USA; and Branson, Missouri, which opened in 2020.

Each site plays the same joke with a local accent. The WonderWorks lab always lands on top of some other building. In Orlando it rests on a warehouse. In Pigeon Forge it is a theater, in Panama City Beach a surf shack, in Myrtle Beach a crab shack, and in Branson a log cabin. In Syracuse, it occupies the third floor of Destiny USA.

## Weather You Can Feel

Inside, the fun is labeled "edutainment," a blend of education and entertainment built around interactive exhibits on space, physics and math. Fittingly for a building that was supposedly thrown by a storm, one of its zones is devoted to natural disasters. At the Hurricane Shack, visitors stand in a hurricane wind of 74 miles per hour. The Earthquake Cafe shakes them with a quake of magnitude 5.3 on the Richter scale. A Tesla coil crackles with 100,000 volts of electricity, and at the "How Cold Is It?" station, guests hold their hands in icy water at 28°F. A trivia wall quizzes them on weather and disasters before they move on.

## Nails, Bubbles and a Giant Piano

Other zones test the body. On the Bed of Nails, visitors lie down on 3,500 nails, and on a gigantic pin wall they press out three-dimensional images of themselves. The Bubble Lab lets them create bubbles big enough to fit inside, and at Pulley Power they haul themselves up by rope. Virtual Sports tests their athletic skills.

The light and sound zone is playful in a different way. Guests can test their reflexes at Speed of Light, watch their shadows multiply into different colors and patterns on a wall, and jump from key to key on a giant piano.

## Countdown in the Space Zone

The Space Discovery zone reaches for orbit. Visitors can examine a life-sized replica of an EVA suit, climb into a replica Mercury capsule, work the controls to land a Space Shuttle, feel the weightlessness astronauts experience in space, and ride the Wonder Coaster, a roller coaster they design themselves.

There is more for those who linger; a full visit takes about three to four hours. The Imagination Lab offers gear puzzles, digital painting, an alien-stomping game and a forensic science exhibition that explores the real work of a forensic scientist. The Far-Out Art Gallery hides objects inside illusion paintings. Most locations also have laser tag and a multi-story indoor ropes course, and some add a 4D XD motion theater or the Outta Control Magic Comedy Dinner Show. Most WonderWorks locations stay open every day of the year.

## Flight Context

The original WonderWorks is at approximately 28.4339°N, 81.4715°W, in Orlando's tourist corridor southwest of downtown, a short distance south of the Universal Orlando Resort. From 1,500–2,500 feet in good light, look for the unmistakable upside-down building perched on its warehouse base. Nearby airports: Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 10 statute miles east, inside Orlando's Class B airspace; Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 10 statute miles south-southeast; and Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 11 statute miles northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are frequent over central Florida, so morning flights usually offer the clearest views.
