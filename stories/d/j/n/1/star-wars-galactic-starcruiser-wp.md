# Star Wars: Galactic Starcruiser

Disney's Star Wars: Galactic Starcruiser sold two-night voyages on a starship that never left Florida, won an industry award, drew sharp criticism for its price, and closed after nineteen months.

**Location:** 28.3507, -81.5614

## Introduction

The starship never left the ground, and its voyage lasted just nineteen months. From March 1, 2022, until September 30, 2023, Star Wars: Galactic Starcruiser invited guests aboard the Halcyon, a starcruiser docked beside Disney's Hollywood Studios in the Epcot Resort Area of Walt Disney World in Bay Lake, Florida. It was not quite a hotel and not quite a theme park ride. It was a two-night live-action role-playing adventure, part cruise, part choose-your-own-adventure story, in which the guests themselves were cast in the Star Wars galaxy. The fare started at roughly $4,800 for two people.

## The Most Experiential Concept Ever

The idea was tested before it was built. In April 2017, Disney hired the website Swagbucks to survey its visitors about a possible Star Wars-themed hotel, and that year at the D23 Expo in Anaheim, California, the company announced it alongside 22 other additions to its parks around the world. Bob Chapek, then Disney's head of Parks and Resorts, promised the company's "most experiential concept ever."

The plans read like science fiction made real: creatures and droids as hotel staff, guests in Star Wars costumes, and a building that would respond as if visitors had truly entered the galaxy. Marketing suggested lightsaber training and duels. A writer for The Verge called it a "Westworld for Star Wars fans." Early renderings showed a hotel shaped like a starship, and on May 30, 2018, Disney placed it just south of Disney's Hollywood Studios, east of World Drive. Estimates of its cost ranged from $350 million to $1 billion. The COVID-19 pandemic pushed its planned 2021 opening back to March 1, 2022.

## Aboard the Halcyon

Guests stayed aboard the Halcyon for two days and two nights. The ship was described as having 13 decks, four of them open to passengers. Deck 4 held the Crown of Corellia Dining Room, a Lightsaber Training Pod, a Climate Simulator, the Transport Dock to Batuu, the Cargo Hold, the Brig and the Engineering Room. Deck 6 opened onto the Atrium, which led to the Bridge, the Sublight Lounge, the Chandrila Collection souvenir shop and the Passenger Services desk. Cabins filled Decks 4, 5 and 7, fitted with full-size bunk beds and a queen bed, and some had a pull-down berth for a fifth traveler.

On day two, guests traveled to Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge by "shuttle-pod," in reality a cargo box on a flatbed truck whose interior resembled a shuttle, arriving at a terminal beside the First Order ship in Docking Bay 9. That walkway is still visible in the park, though closed to guests. Arrivals by car used valet parking only, and guests could leave the ship at any point during their stay.

## Choose Your Side

The fiction gave every passenger a reason to be on board. The Halcyon's crew and passengers were celebrating the 275th anniversary of the starline, but the ship's itinerary coincided with Resistance activity, and the First Order came aboard to investigate. From there, guests chose the storyline they would follow: Resistance, Smuggler or First Order. The whole experience was thematically linked to Galaxy's Edge, the Star Wars land in Disney's Hollywood Studios, and the day-two shuttle trip carried the story out of the hotel and into the park.

## A Death Star-Sized Price Tag

Trouble started before the first voyage. Early photos of the small, windowless cabins went viral, and many commentators on Twitter argued they were not suitable luxury accommodation. After opening, the price drew continued criticism. Esquire wrote that "the real villain here is Galactic Starcruiser's Death Star-sized price tag," and one commentator noted that the cost kept many people away, while the single storyline gave those who did come little reason to return.

The industry saw something else. In November 2022, the Themed Entertainment Association named the Galactic Starcruiser a Thea Award winner for outstanding achievement in brand experience.

## The Final Jump

In May 2023, Disney announced that the last two-day voyage would depart on September 28 and that the Starcruiser would then close for good. It officially shut down at 10 a.m. on September 30, 2023. At a JP Morgan media conference in May 2023, Disney's Josh D'Amaro said the company expected $100 to $150 million in accelerated depreciation in each of the next two fiscal quarters, and interim CFO Kevin Lansberry later put the figure at $250 million.

The ship's story kept circulating after it closed. In May 2024, a four-hour review by YouTuber Jenny Nicholson went viral, describing customer-service problems and app glitches that kept her from fully engaging with the story, and concluding that the experience did not justify its cost. In January 2025, it was reported that the building was being turned into office space for Walt Disney Imagineering's upcoming park projects, including a Disney Villains land at Magic Kingdom, a Monsters, Inc. land at Disney's Hollywood Studios and a Tropical Americas land at Disney's Animal Kingdom. As of April 2026, crews were still gutting and renovating the building, which is not expected to be open to guests.
