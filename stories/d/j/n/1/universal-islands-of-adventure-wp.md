# Universal Islands of Adventure

Built on the site of a cancelled shopping mall, Universal Islands of Adventure opened in 1999 as a loop of themed islands and found its footing with superheroes, dinosaurs and, in 2010, a wizarding village.

**Location:** 28.4717, -81.4714

**Categories:** Universal Islands of Adventure, Amusement parks in Orlando, Florida, Amusement parks opened in 1999, Universal Destinations & Experiences attractions by name, Tourist attractions in Orange County, Florida

## Introduction

Every night, a working lighthouse at the entrance to an Orlando theme park sends out a bright beam to guide visitors to and from the gates. The Pharos Lighthouse is the centerpiece of Port of Entry, the first of the "islands" that make up Universal Islands of Adventure, a park built around the idea of exploration. From there, the park runs clockwise through Marvel Super Hero Island, Toon Lagoon, Skull Island, Jurassic Park, The Wizarding World of Harry Potter, The Lost Continent and Seuss Landing, each one a separate world. In 2024 it welcomed an estimated 9.4 million visitors, placing it among the most visited theme parks on the planet.

## The Mall That Never Was

The land was first meant for shopping. Plans for a mall called Galleria Orlando were drawn up in 1985, but the project was cancelled in 1990, the same year Universal Studios Florida opened next door. Universal instead built a second theme park, its largest investment since the resort's founding.

Two years before opening, it gave guests a preview. The Islands of Adventure Preview Center, inside the Paradise Theater building in the New York section of Universal Studios Florida, led visitors through rooms themed to each island and ended with the park's opening date and Universal's plans for the resort. A soft opening began on March 27, 1999, with warnings that rides might open and close without notice as staff added the finishing touches. Planned for mid-May, the official opening slipped to May 28, 1999, when the park debuted alongside Universal CityWalk.

## Lost in the Name

Universal had spent heavily on the new park, CityWalk and resort hotels in hopes of overtaking nearby Walt Disney World in attendance. Its branding got in the way. The expanded complex was called Universal Studios Escape, and visitors seem to have been confused, assuming Islands of Adventure was simply a new section of the existing studio park. For the first two years, attendance did not rise as expected.

By 2001, Universal had revamped its marketing to make clear that Islands of Adventure was a completely separate park. The resort became Universal Orlando Resort, the park became Universal's Islands of Adventure, a name it kept until 2023.

## No Straight Lines

Each island has its own texture. On Marvel Super Hero Island, building interiors are drawn in comic-book perspective with exaggerated lines and angles, many exteriors wear a special paint that shifts between purple and orange depending on where you stand, and the buildings carry blunt labels like "Store," "Food" and "Comics." Toon Lagoon, built around King Features and Jay Ward characters, focuses on water rides. Jurassic Park poses as John Hammond's dinosaur park from the films, and The Lost Continent divides into an Arabian marketplace called Sindbad's Bazaar and a Grecian, Atlantis-like Lost City, and it is home to Mythos, voted best theme park restaurant by Theme Park Insider six years running from 2003 to 2008. In May 2026 Universal announced that the Lost Continent will close in phases and Mythos will close permanently in 2027 to make way for a new themed land.

Seuss Landing takes the books literally. There are almost no straight lines anywhere, and even the palm trees, bent by the winds of Hurricane Andrew, were planted to keep the theme. Skull Island arrived in the summer of 2016 with Skull Island: Reign of Kong, bringing King Kong back to the resort.

## Spider-Man's Side of the Line

Marvel Super Hero Island rests on a contract signed in 1994 that gave Universal exclusive rights to certain Marvel characters. When Disney, Universal's main theme-park competitor, moved to acquire Marvel Entertainment in 2009, Universal announced the island would be unaffected, and Disney CEO Bob Iger said Disney would honor Marvel's existing contracts. As a result, Walt Disney World cannot use Spider-Man, the Avengers, the Fantastic Four, the X-Men, their villains and related characters east of the Mississippi River, a geographic exclusivity that Universal's own financial filings describe. Universal is also said to hold theme park marketing rights to the Marvel name in the United States, and Disney's Avengers Campus in California does not use it in its branding. Other Marvel characters, such as Star-Lord and Gamora, remain available to Disney.

## A Castle in the Middle of It All

The park's fortunes changed with a wizard. On May 31, 2007, Universal announced it had secured the rights from Warner Bros. and J. K. Rowling to bring Harry Potter to Islands of Adventure. The Wizarding World of Harry Potter – Hogsmeade opened on June 18, 2010, covering 20 acres with Hogsmeade Village, the Forbidden Forest and Hogwarts Castle, and it brought a notable increase in attendance. Most of The Lost Continent's medieval Merlinwood area was absorbed in the process, and the old Dueling Dragons coaster, renamed Dragon Challenge, was eventually replaced by Hagrid's Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure. In 2014 the story crossed the fence: a Diagon Alley expansion opened at Universal Studios Florida, and the Hogwarts Express now links the two halves.

## Flight Context

Universal Islands of Adventure is centered near 28.472°N, 81.471°W in Orlando, Florida, directly beside Universal Studios Florida in the Universal Orlando Resort. From about 2,500–3,500 feet, look for the loop of themed islands and the castle of the Harry Potter area; the Pharos Lighthouse at Port of Entry shines each night. Nearby airports: Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 10 miles east-northeast, Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 10 miles east-southeast (check the Orlando terminal-area chart for airspace before overflying), and Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 13 miles south. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
