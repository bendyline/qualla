# Pleasure Island (Walt Disney World)

Walt Disney World's Pleasure Island celebrated New Year's Eve every night for sixteen years, and after its clubs closed in 2008 the island became The Landing at the heart of Disney Springs.

**Location:** 28.3710, -81.5180

## Introduction

Some of them started lining up at nine in the morning. On September 27, 2008, Pleasure Island's last night, the capacity crowd included many of the actors and cast members who had worked there over the years, determined not to miss what became a very emotional evening. Frankie and the West End Boys played the waterfront stage. At midnight, the island celebrated New Year's Eve one last time, with fireworks set to songs guests had heard there for years. Inside Mannequins, the final track was Rozalla's "Everybody's Free (To Feel Good)." With that, Pleasure Island's nightclubs were officially shut down, and by the next day workers were already removing or painting over the club signs.

## The Legend of Merriweather Pleasure

Pleasure Island opened on May 1, 1989, beside the shopping complex that Disney later branded Downtown Disney. Its name came from the island in Walt Disney's 1940 film Pinocchio, but its business model came from Church Street Station in downtown Orlando, then a highly successful gated attraction that offered admission to multiple clubs for one price.

Disney wrapped the district in an invented legend. The island, the story went, belonged to Merriweather Adam Pleasure, an industrialist and explorer who moved his family to Florida and ran a sailmaking and yacht-building business there from the 1920s into the early 1940s. He loved throwing after-hours parties for employees and friends, until he was lost at sea, his sons ran the company into failure and a storm mostly destroyed the island. Disney, in the tale, rediscovered the ruins and opened a nightclub district to carry on his merrymaking spirit. Even the Portobello restaurant played along, cast as the former Pleasure family home.

## Midnight, Every Night

From 1990 through New Year's Eve 2005, Pleasure Island rang in the new year every single night at midnight with fireworks. The clubs gave each crowd its own world. The Adventurers Club was a 1930s-style British explorers' club staffed by flamboyant characters played by improv actors. The Comedy Warehouse began with a parody show called "Forbidden Disney" before switching to improv. Mannequins Dance Palace spun dancers on a revolving lighted floor, with light shows and live human "mannequins." Over the years the lineup also included the Rock 'n' Roll Beach Club, the country-music club Neon Armadillo, a jazz club, and Videopolis East, which played new wave music on 170 video screens.

A thirty-foot, two-sided neon Jessica Rabbit with a swinging leg hung over one shop and later over the West End Stage. Performers ranged from New Kids on the Block to Johnny Cash, and *NSYNC filmed a demo at the Rock 'n' Roll Beach Club in 1995. A 1994 Simpsons episode parodying Walt Disney World even sent Homer and Marge to a "Parents' Island" whose '70s-themed dance club celebrated New Year's Eve every few minutes.

## Last Call

By the mid-2000s attendance was falling. In March 2006, Disney made major renovations to reverse the decline. With no entry fee anymore, the free entertainment on the West End and Hub stages had been drawing large groups of local teens, which the company considered undesirable, so both stages were closed. The Jessica Rabbit sign came down that June. From January 1, 2007, the dance clubs returned to a 21-and-over policy.

In late June 2008, Disney announced that the six remaining nightclubs would close by September 28 to make room for more family-oriented entertainment, offering annual-pass holders pro-rated refunds. Officials said the change responded to guests who wanted broader dining and retail. The clubs closed on September 27, while the island's shops and restaurants stayed open.

## From Hyperion Wharf to The Landing

The island spent years searching for a new identity. In 2009 three former clubs, including the Adventurers Club, were offered for private parties, and on November 10, 2010, Disney announced a new name, Hyperion Wharf, with an early 20th-century nautical warehouse theme, $3.2 million in new lighting and a lighthouse. The buildings of Motion and the Rock 'n' Roll Beach Club were demolished between December 2010 and February 2011, but the Hyperion Wharf trademark was allowed to expire on December 3, 2012.

Instead, in April 2015, the area reopened as The Landing, the middle link of what is now Disney Springs, focused on shopping and restaurants. Merriweather Pleasure was not forgotten. Jock Lindsey's Hangar Bar, themed to the pilot from Raiders of the Lost Ark, ties the area to the Society of Explorers and Adventurers, and a fez in Magic Kingdom's Skipper Canteen establishes Pleasure as a member, turning his Adventurers Club, retroactively, into his own private chapter of the Society.
