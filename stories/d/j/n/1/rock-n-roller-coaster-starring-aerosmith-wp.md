# Rock 'n' Roller Coaster Starring Aerosmith

For more than a quarter century, Disney's Hollywood Studios launched riders from 0 to 57 mph in 2.8 seconds in a "super stretch" limo blasting Aerosmith, before the Muppets took over the stage in 2026.

**Location:** 28.3594, -81.5607

**Categories:** Roller coasters at Disney's Hollywood Studios, Enclosed roller coasters, Cultural depictions of Aerosmith, Licensed properties at Walt Disney Parks & Resorts, 1999 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

The license plates gave fair warning: 2FAST4U, H8TRFFC, BUHBYE, UGOBABE, 1QKLIMO. Each belonged to a "super stretch" limousine at Rock 'n' Roller Coaster Starring Aerosmith, and each played its own Aerosmith soundtrack as it rocketed through the dark. A sixth limo carried no plate at all and was always "in refurbishment." The indoor coaster, built by Vekoma, opened at what was then Disney-MGM Studios, now Disney's Hollywood Studios, on July 29, 1999. Its linear motors fired riders from 0 to 57 miles per hour in 2.8 seconds, and for more than 26 years a rock band served as the excuse for going that fast.

## Backstage Passes

The story began in a record company. Guests entered a high-ceilinged circular room themed to the fictional G-Force Records, its walls lined with ceiling-high guitars and its floor laid out as a giant record. Digital posters could sometimes display guests' own names as recording artists, read from their MagicBands.

In the pre-show, guests filed into Studio C, where Aerosmith was working on an instrumental version of "Walk This Way." Their manager, played by Illeana Douglas, burst in to say the band was late for a show across town. Steven Tyler refused to leave the guests behind. When the manager sarcastically asked whether she should send them all along, Tyler paused: "Wait a minute. I love that idea. How about some backstage passes?" A "super stretch" limo was ordered, described as a "really fast car," and "Walk This Way" kicked back in, this time with vocals. In the most recent version, Joe Perry asked a cast member named Chris to grab his black Les Paul, and, on rare occasions, a real cast member physically carried a guitar signed by Perry off the set.

## Zero to 57 in 2.8 Seconds

Then came the launch. Linear motor electromagnetic technology hurled each limo down the track, and riders felt up to 5 Gs as they went through three inversions, including a sea serpent and, as the third and final one, a corkscrew. Neon signs along the track mimicked road signs, as if the limo were racing across town to the show, and the ride finished with banked turns and a camelback hill before pulling into a VIP backstage area, where riders stepped out onto a red carpet.

The music was built into the seats. Each train carried 120 speakers, five surrounding every rider: two mid-range speakers, two tweeters and a subwoofer under the seat. Walt Disney Imagineering worked with Aerosmith on a special soundtrack, with some songs given new lyrics for the ride, so that "Love in an Elevator" became "Love in a Rollercoaster." Depending on your limo, you might hear "Nine Lives," "Sweet Emotion," "Back in the Saddle" or "Dude (Looks Like a Lady)."

## Limos and Soundtrackers

Disney announced the coaster in April 1998, although construction had begun two months earlier, and the track layout was complete by June. At the invitation-only grand opening party on July 29, 1999, Aerosmith were guests of honor. Contest winners arrived by stretch limousine, watched a performance by painter Denny Dent and got the chance to ride with a member of the band. A photo from that night was put on display near the ride's exit.

A twin with an identical track opened at Disneyland Paris for the debut of Walt Disney Studios Park on March 16, 2002, as one of the park's only three opening-day rides. There, guests entered the fictional Tour de Force Records and rode "Soundtrackers" instead of limos, each with its own color of light show, while lighting rigs, projectors, strobes and smoke replaced the road signs. That version closed on September 2, 2019, and returned on July 20, 2022, as Avengers Assemble: Flight Force.

## The Muppets Take the Wheel

Even in Florida the Aerosmith theme occasionally stepped aside. On December 17, 2015, a special event promoting Star Wars: The Force Awakens hung movie posters in the queue, disabled the pre-show and filled the speakers with music from the film, and the overlay returned on April 14, 2017.

The bigger change was announced on November 22, 2024, alongside the closure of Muppet*Vision 3D: the coaster would be re-themed around the Muppets while keeping its name. The Aerosmith pre-show was removed in December 2025, and the Aerosmith version ran its last day on March 1, 2026, closing officially on March 2. On the same track, Rock 'n' Roller Coaster Starring The Muppets opened on May 26, 2026, with a soundtrack of cover versions of five well-known songs performed by the Electric Mayhem.

## Flight Context

Rock 'n' Roller Coaster sits near 28.359°N, 81.561°W in Disney's Hollywood Studios at Walt Disney World, about 1.3 miles southwest of Epcot's Spaceship Earth, southwest of Orlando, Florida. The coaster is fully enclosed, so from 3,000–4,000 feet look for its large show building within the park rather than any visible track. Check current NOTAMs and charts for flight restrictions over the Walt Disney World parks and stay outside any restricted area. Nearby airports: Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 9 miles east-southeast, Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 16 miles east-northeast, and Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 19 miles northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
