# Alfred Hitchcock: The Art of Making Movies

For nearly thirteen years, Universal Studios Florida let guests walk through a Hitchcock scrapbook of 3-D crows, the Bates Motel and a lesson in how the shower scene in Psycho was cut together.

**Location:** 28.4758, -81.4680

**Categories:** Universal Studios Florida, Former Universal Studios Florida attractions, Alfred Hitchcock, Amusement park attractions introduced in 1990, Licensed properties at Universal Destinations & Experiences, 1990 establishments in Florida, 2003 disestablishments in Florida, Universal Destinations & Experiences attractions by name

## Introduction

A scene from Dial M for Murder was playing in 3-D when the birds arrived. Crows and seagulls from The Birds swarmed across the screen and toward the audience, and as the flock broke apart, one crow morphed into a silhouette nobody could mistake: the round profile of Alfred Hitchcock. That was only the pre-show. Alfred Hitchcock: The Art of Making Movies, also billed as The Art of Alfred Hitchcock or Hitchcock's 3-D Theater, was part 3-D film and part live-action show, and one of the original attractions of Universal Studios Florida. It opened in 1990 and ran until January 3, 2003.

## Forty-Three Years on the Lot

The show was a tribute to a working relationship. The attraction was billed as a commemoration of Hitchcock's 43-year association with Universal Studios, and it set out to honor that long partnership in the studio's new Florida park. Susan Lustig directed and produced it, with Peter N. Alexander as associate producer, and Kodak signed on as sponsor. The creative consultants included the actor Norman Lloyd and Anthony Perkins, the man forever linked with Norman Bates.

The tribute began before guests reached the door. Waiting outside, they passed posters for Rear Window, The Birds, Vertigo, Rope and Psycho. Inside stood a large collage of props from Hitchcock's films, a kind of three-dimensional scrapbook: a flock of crows from The Birds, a black telephone from Dial M for Murder, and a cymbal player from The Man Who Knew Too Much.

## Out of the Screen, Into the Room

The pre-show film opened with a scene from The Wrong Man, in which Hitchcock himself told the audience that the new film was very extraordinary. From there it slid into a montage of his work: Rear Window, The Man Who Knew Too Much, Vertigo, Marnie and Frenzy, one suspense sequence handing off to the next.

Then came Dial M for Murder in 3-D, and the interruption. Just as the audience settled in, a swarm of attacking crows and seagulls from The Birds broke through and turned on the viewers, stereoscopic wings and all. By the time the lone crow became the director's silhouette, the room had been properly introduced to its host, who appeared in archive footage.

## Checking In at the Bates Motel

Guests then filed into the main theater, built as the set of the Bates Motel and the looming house of Norman Bates. Here the attraction slowed down and became a film lesson. Anthony Perkins narrated the story of how Hitchcock made Psycho, a film the show presented as the most frightening movie in cinematic history, and he walked the audience through the camera techniques behind the shower scene. Then the scene itself played, with Janet Leigh appearing as Marion Crane in archive footage.

Perkins was not the only familiar face. Norman Lloyd, Shirley MacLaine, John Forsythe and James Stewart all appeared in the show as themselves.

## Your Turn in the Frame

The last act handed the camera to the guests. An interactive post-show area let them re-enact scenes from Saboteur, Strangers on a Train and Rear Window, playing the roles that audiences had watched for decades. The exit ran, fittingly, through the Bates Motel Gift Shop, stocked with Hitchcock gifts and souvenirs.

The show closed on January 3, 2003. Later that year Shrek 4-D replaced it, and the crows and the shower curtain gave way to a very different kind of 3-D film.
