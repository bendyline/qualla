# The Wizarding World of Harry Potter (Universal Orlando Resort)

Disney once had a letter of intent for Harry Potter, but it was Universal that raised Hogwarts over Orlando in 2010, then ran a working Hogwarts Express between two parks and spread the wizarding world across three.

**Location:** 28.4728, -81.4734

**Categories:** Harry Potter in amusement parks, Universal Islands of Adventure, Universal Studios Florida, Universal Epic Universe, 2010 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

Hogwarts almost ended up in a corner of Fantasyland. In 2004, J. K. Rowling signed a letter of intent with the Walt Disney Company, which planned a Harry Potter section inside the Magic Kingdom. Then Disney reportedly walked away, deciding that Rowling's creative influence and the terms set by Warner Bros. were too stringent. Universal, partnering with Warner Bros. under an exclusive license, got the castle instead. Today the Wizarding World of Harry Potter spreads across three theme parks at the Universal Orlando Resort, and the castle that Disney passed on rises above Islands of Adventure, where it has pulled in crowds since 2010.

## Project Strong Arm

Rumors began in January 2007, when a report claimed that Islands of Adventure's Lost Continent area would be rethemed to "one of the most popular children's franchises." Within days, other sources linked the project to Harry Potter and gave its working name, "Project Strong Arm," a nod to the KUKA robotic arm technology it would use. On May 31, 2007, Universal and Warner Bros. made it official: a 20-acre land recreating Hogwarts, the Forbidden Forest and the village of Hogsmeade. Construction started in January 2008. Existing rides were stripped and reborn, as the Flying Unicorn family coaster became Flight of the Hippogriff and the two-sided Dueling Dragons coaster became Dragon Challenge, which ran until 2017. The top shell of Hogwarts Castle was finished by September 2009. At the grand opening ceremony on June 16, 2010, Rowling stood alongside Daniel Radcliffe, Rupert Grint, Emma Watson and other cast members while John Williams, composer of the films' original scores, conducted the Orlando Philharmonic Orchestra.

## A Bench on a Robot Arm

The flagship ride, Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey, begins on foot. Visitors walk through the castle, hearing a speech from Dumbledore and getting directions from Harry, Hermione and Ron, before boarding an enchanted bench mounted on a KUKA robotic arm. From there the ride blends simulated flight with lifelike animatronics: the Whomping Willow, a swarm of dementors, giant spiders, a Hungarian Horntail dragon and a Quidditch match. Outside, Hogsmeade is full of small rituals. At Ollivanders, guests enter in groups of twenty so a wand keeper can demonstrate that "the wand chooses the wizard," testing wands on a volunteer as flowers dry out or a thunderstorm erupts inside the shop. The Owl Post mails letters with a Hogsmeade postmark, and the Three Broomsticks pours non-alcoholic Butterbeer, served regular or frozen. It worked. Attendance at Islands of Adventure rose 36 percent in 2010.

## Platform Nine and Three-Quarters

Success demanded an expansion, and in December 2011 Universal announced it, days after revealing that its classic Jaws ride would close for good. The Jaws site in neighboring Universal Studios Florida was leveled, its lagoon reclaimed, and in its place rose London and Diagon Alley, which opened on July 8, 2014. The flagship coaster, Harry Potter and the Escape from Gringotts, carries riders deep into the wizarding bank's vaults, hosted by Domhnall Gleeson reprising his role as Bill Weasley. At the Gringotts Money Exchange, guests can trade American dollars for wizarding banknotes. The real showpiece, though, connects the two parks. A fully functioning, full-scale replica of the Hogwarts Express, built as a funicular people mover by Doppelmayr Garaventa, runs between King's Cross Station and Hogsmeade Station. To board in London, guests walk into the wall between Platforms 9 and 10, just as Harry did in the first film.

## Paris, 1920s

The Wizarding World later reached a third park, Universal Epic Universe. There, guests step into Place Cachée, French for "Hidden Place," a 1920s Parisian counterpart to Diagon Alley drawn from the Fantastic Beasts films. A Métro-Floo carries them forward in time to the British Ministry of Magic of the 1990s for Harry Potter and the Battle at the Ministry, a ride built around the trial of Dolores Umbridge. Its vehicles sit atop scissor lifts, in the tradition of Universal's Spider-Man and Transformers rides. Nearby, the stage show Le Cirque Arcanus follows a ringmaster who steals Newt Scamander's suitcase of magical creatures in a bid to restore his circus to its former glory, brought to life with live performers, puppetry and special effects.

## A Mayor's Jealous Irritation

Not everyone was delighted to see Hogwarts in Florida. Boris Johnson, then Mayor of London, confessed his "jealous irritation" that the park had gone to Orlando, calling it "utterly mad" to leave it to the Americans "to make money from a great British invention." Britain may yet catch up. A Universal theme park for the United Kingdom was announced in 2023, and in April 2025 Prime Minister Keir Starmer confirmed government support. Planning permission was granted in December 2025 for the site near Bedford, early enabling works were under way by September 2026 on what is now called Universal United Kingdom Resort, and an opening is planned for 2031. Critics, meanwhile, were largely won over. Wired's Matt Blum wrote that "every detail is finely crafted to enhance the experience," and the Orlando Sentinel called Forbidden Journey a one-of-a-kind ride that could not easily be described.

## Flight Context

Hogsmeade and Hogwarts Castle stand in Universal's Islands of Adventure at approximately 28.4728°N, 81.4734°W, in southwest Orlando near Interstate 4. The castle's spires, the surrounding theme parks and the resort's lagoons are readily visible from the air. Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) is about 8.5 nm to the east-northeast, and Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 9 nm to the east-southeast; the area lies beneath Orlando's busy Class B airspace, so coordinate with Orlando approach and check NOTAMs for any temporary flight restrictions. A viewing altitude of 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, where airspace allows, gives a good look at the park. Central Florida summer afternoons frequently bring thunderstorms; mornings and the dry winter months offer the best visibility.
