# The Incredible Hulk Coaster

Born from a prototype of welded boxcars and a dune buggy, the Incredible Hulk Coaster fires riders out of a tunnel at Islands of Adventure and has been rattling nerves since 1999.

**Location:** 28.4711, -81.4683

## Introduction

"Everything looks good... I think... I think this time it's... going to work!" In the station, Dr. Bruce Banner sounded anything but sure. A female voice warned that coolant levels were dropping, alarms went off, and Banner began to scream, "No! No! No! No!" The last "No!" was timed to the launch. With it, the train shot out of the station and up at an angle, from a standstill to 40 miles per hour in two seconds. For its first sixteen years, that was how the Incredible Hulk Coaster began at Universal Islands of Adventure in Orlando, Florida, and the launch at its heart took years of experiments to get right.

## Fired From a Cannon

Planning for a new park beside Universal Studios Florida began in 1991, and by the end of 1993 one of its areas was set to be themed after Marvel Comics, with the Hulk chosen for a thrill ride. The designers wanted riders to feel as though they were being fired out of a cannon, so they started building a launch.

Universal Creative and MTS Systems Corporation had a working prototype by January 1995, and it was gloriously improvised: a series of boxcars welded together into a launch tunnel, with a track-mounted dune buggy propelled through it by a weight-drop mechanism. The team experimented with rates of acceleration of between one and five times the force of gravity. The final tire-propelled system sends trains up a 30-degree incline through a 150-foot tunnel, pulling 1 G. Werner Stengel designed the coaster and Bolliger & Mabillard built it, making it the first B&M coaster themed to a Marvel superhero and the first to feature a launch.

## Seven Inversions Over the Water

The Incredible Hulk Coaster opened with Islands of Adventure on May 28, 1999. After the launch, its 3,670 feet of track pack in seven inversions: a zero-G roll, a cobra roll, two vertical loops and two corkscrews. The coaster reaches a maximum height of 110 feet, drops 105 feet and tops out at 67 miles per hour over a ride of about two and a quarter minutes. Two dives plunge below ground level, part of the ride is built over water, and at night the whole thing glows green.

Each train has eight cars seating riders four abreast, 32 people in all, held by ratcheting over-the-shoulder restraints. Riders must be at least 54 inches tall. At full tilt, the ride can theoretically carry 1,920 people an hour.

In the original queue, guests wound through Bruce Banner's laboratory, where televisions played a cartoon of the Hulk's story featuring Bruce, General Thunderbolt Ross, Betty Ross Banner and the Rhino. After boarding, trains rolled into a part of the station themed as a gamma-ray accelerator.

## Rebuilt From the Track Up

The Hulk closed on September 8, 2015, for a major overhaul. It came back on August 4, 2016, after several days of technical rehearsals, with new track sections, new trains carrying on-board audio, an updated queue and a repainted black launch tunnel. Most of the old track and trains were recycled for scrap, but not all of it. At the new entrance, a statue of the Hulk holds a ride vehicle over his head beneath arching tracks taken from the original coaster.

The story changed, too. Guests now enter a perilous experiment led by General Thaddeus Ross, where televisions show computer-animated test subjects turning into Hulk-like creatures under gamma radiation. Trains leave Ross's launching platform into a gamma core as a female voice intones, "Initiating Gamma Exposure. Hulk Transformation: Accelerating. Do not be afraid." Then the tunnel fires. The layout is the same, rebuilt with new pieces, now set to an original score by Patrick Stump of Fall Out Boy.

## It Rattles Our Nerves

The Hulk has been well received from the start. In its debut year, Discovery Channel viewers voted it the best roller coaster, and it appeared in the documentary Thrills, Chills and Spills. Amusement Business named it one of the park's two world-class coasters, alongside Dueling Dragons. The Orlando Sentinel's Dewayne Bevil ranked it his eighth favorite attraction in Orlando and called it "too intense to take back-to-back trips. It rattles our nerves, in a good way." Arthur Levine of About.com gave it 9 out of 10, calling it "both terrifying and exhilarating." In Amusement Today's Golden Ticket Awards, it debuted at number 19 in 1999, peaked at number 9 in 2001 and stayed on the list for more than a decade.

It nearly gained a twin. Concept art for Universal Studios Beijing in 2015 showed a clone of the Hulk, but in 2019 Universal clarified that the coaster, Decepticoaster, would be themed to Transformers instead. It opened in September 2021 as a similar launched coaster.

## Flight Context

The Incredible Hulk Coaster is in Universal Islands of Adventure at approximately 28.4711°N, 81.4683°W, in the Universal Orlando Resort southwest of downtown Orlando, Florida. From 2,500–3,500 feet, look for looping coaster track built partly over the water; at night the coaster is lit green. Nearby airports: Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 10 statute miles east-northeast and Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 10 statute miles east-southeast, beneath Orlando's Class B airspace.
