# Honey, I Shrunk the Audience!

For more than fifteen years, Epcot's Honey, I Shrunk the Audience! shrank its guests, lifted their theater and sneezed on them, before being replaced by the very show it had once replaced.

**Location:** 28.3726, -81.5509

**Categories:** Epcot, Imagination! (Epcot), Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, 3D short films, Films directed by Randal Kleiser

## Introduction

Somewhere in the middle of the show, a camera flash goes off in your face, and a giant child picks up the entire theater. That is Adam, Wayne Szalinski's youngest son, and he wants to show the little people to his mother. A minute or two later a snake nearly eats you. Honey, I Shrunk the Audience!, a 4D spin-off of the Honey, I Shrunk the Kids films, opened at Epcot in 1994 and spent more than fifteen years convincing audiences that they had been miniaturized. Guests wore 3D glasses while the theater shook and rocked beneath them, creating the illusion of moving with the characters on screen.

## The Theater That Became the Audience

In November 1993, Epcot announced that a new attraction themed to the Honey, I Shrunk the Kids franchise would replace Captain EO for the 1994 season. It was to be called Honey, I Shrunk the Theater, a 3D film with effects such as vibrating chairs and water sprays. In February 1994 the name changed to Honey, I Shrunk the Audience. Captain EO closed on July 6, 1994, work began swiftly, and the new show opened to the public on November 21, 1994. It was an instant hit.

The film was directed by Randal Kleiser, who had directed the franchise's Honey, I Blew Up the Kid as well as Grease, The Blue Lagoon and Flight of the Navigator. Rick Moranis played inventor Wayne Szalinski and Marcia Strassman as his wife Diane, with Eric Idle as Dr. Nigel Channing. Kodak sponsored the show.

## Inventor of the Year

The premise put guests in the Imagination Institute's theater for the Inventor of the Year Award Ceremony, where they were asked to don "safety goggles" for the scientific demonstrations. Things went wrong almost immediately. Wayne flew in miniaturized aboard a Hoverpod and dropped his control box, and the runaway machine later crashed into the neon award sign above the audience.

With the guest of honor missing, his son Nick filled time by demonstrating a copy machine called the Dimensional Duplicator. His younger brother Adam put his pet mouse inside, and it multiplied into hundreds of copies that scurried under the audience's seats. The screaming was real; the mice under the seats were leg ticklers. A Holo Pet Generator meant to scare them away projected a cat, which then turned into a lion swiping at the audience's faces.

## Four Inches of Earthquake

Then came the title moment. Wayne's shrinking machine went completely out of control and shrank the whole audience, along with Nick. When Diane walked closer to look, the ground thudded under each footstep, and when she fainted, the seats jerked from the impact. Adam lifted the theater for his tour, his pet snake lunged hungrily, and the family dog, Quark, chased the snake away. After Wayne restored everyone to normal size, the beam caught Quark by accident. "Honey, I did!" Wayne confessed. "I blew up the dog!" The giant dog pushed through the curtain and sneezed on the audience, firing hidden water sprayers in the seat backs.

The physical tricks were modest in scale. The entire audience sat on a platform that could move up to four inches to simulate the floor shaking, while polarized glasses and projectors delivered the 3D images.

## Shrinking Around the World

Epcot's success spread. Tokyo Disneyland opened its version, called MicroAdventure!, on April 15, 1997. Disneyland's installation opened on May 22, 1998, alongside a remodeled Tomorrowland, and Disneyland Paris followed on March 28, 1999, as Chérie, j'ai rétréci le public. In every park it replaced Captain EO.

The original pre-show doubled as a Kodak advertisement set to "True Colors," sung by various artists including Luther Vandross in the American parks and by Cyndi Lauper in Paris. For the 2003 season it was replaced with a new pre-show about making memories, featuring scenes such as a child who could not find his dog and a frog causing mayhem at a wedding, with Eric Idle delivering the safety instructions at the end.

## Replaced by the Show It Replaced

History then ran in reverse. After Michael Jackson died on June 25, 2009, Captain EO found new popularity, and on December 18, 2009, Disney announced that EO was coming back to Disneyland. Honey, I Shrunk the Audience! closed there at midnight on January 4, 2010, officially only temporarily, but it never returned. Paris closed its version on May 3, 2010, Epcot on May 8 or 9 (sources differ) and Tokyo on May 10. All four were replaced by the Captain EO Tribute, which at Epcot borrowed the shrinking show's own hydraulics, leg ticklers and water sprayers to bring back the film it had displaced sixteen years earlier.

## Flight Context

The show played at Epcot's Imagination! pavilion, near 28.373°N, 81.551°W at Walt Disney World southwest of Orlando, Florida. From 3,000–4,000 feet, use Epcot's 180-foot geodesic sphere, Spaceship Earth, as the reference point. Check current NOTAMs and charts for flight restrictions over the Walt Disney World parks and stay outside any restricted area. Nearby airports: Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 9 miles southeast, Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 15 miles east-northeast, and Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 18 miles northeast.
