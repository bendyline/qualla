# The Land (Epcot)

Epcot's The Land pavilion has mixed boat rides, nutrition shows and an indoor hang-gliding simulator with a working agricultural research facility since 1982.

**Location:** 28.3740, -81.5517

**Categories:** Epcot, World Nature, Future World (Epcot), Agricultural research, Buildings and structures with revolving restaurants, 1982 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

Most of Epcot is built to entertain. The Land pavilion also does real work. Some 43,000 square feet of it are given over to experimental horticulture, testing hydroponics, irrigation methods and integrated pest management, and a boat ride passes right through its greenhouses. Opened on October 1, 1982, with what was then EPCOT Center, the pavilion is devoted to how people interact with the Earth: agriculture, conservation and travel, and the ways humans can use the land for their benefit or destroy it. Today it sits in Epcot's World Nature neighborhood, and for more than four decades it has balanced entertainment with a serious purpose.

## Seeds of an Idea

The Land opened with Kraft Foods as its sponsor and three original attractions. Listen to the Land carried guests on connected boats through exhibits about agriculture. Kitchen Kabaret was an Audio-Animatronics stage show about nutrition. Symbiosis, a film, weighed both the positive and negative sides of humanity's relationship with the land.

The growing systems behind the glass owed much to Dr. Merle Jensen, a professor emeritus from the University of Arizona's School of Plant Science and a specialist in controlled environment agriculture. As project leader for the pavilion's agricultural systems, he helped design and implement advanced growing methods meant to show sustainable farming and the future of agriculture. His career has included developing intensive food production systems for use on Earth and beyond it.

The building is large. At about 254,000 square feet, The Land is roughly the size of Fantasyland at Magic Kingdom, with a reported capacity of 3,600 people.

## Farming on the Ride

The pavilion is a demonstration, production and research facility as well as an attraction. Its main ride, now called Living with the Land, still carries boats through the greenhouses, with narration by Mike Brassell. For an extra fee, the roughly one-hour Behind the Seeds tour takes guests into the research facility to explain what is being studied and how. Visitors can even take a little of it home as Mickey's Mini Gardens, tiny plants grown through tissue culture in nutrient gel and sealed in sterile tubes.

The ride has been updated along the way. From August to October 2009 its vehicles were replaced with newer, more wheelchair-friendly boats that raised capacity from 36 to 40 guests each, and in 2011 Chiquita signed on as sponsor. Diners at the Garden Grill look out over the ride's dark-ride section from a revolving seating area. When the floor sat still for several months in the spring of 2014, rumors of a permanent stoppage spread, but it began turning again on June 17, 2014.

## A New Sponsor, a New Recipe

Nestlé took over sponsorship in 1993 and co-financed a gradual refurbishment with Walt Disney World. Brighter colors and fabrics freshened the restaurants and shops, and several names changed. Listen to the Land reopened, mostly unchanged, as Living with the Land on December 10, 1993. Kitchen Kabaret closed on January 3, 1994, and gave way to Food Rocks, a more modern nutrition show built on parodies of classic songs. Symbiosis closed on January 1, 1995, replaced that month by Circle of Life: An Environmental Fable, in which Simba explains symbiosis to Timon and Pumbaa. Both new shows recycled pieces of their predecessors while aiming for younger audiences.

Nestlé renewed in 2003 and oversaw a second major makeover between 2004 and 2005: a white, yellow and green color scheme, new foliage, wider walkways, new carpeting, fresh signage and a completely redesigned food court. The pavilion's original dedication plaque stayed exactly as it was.

## Hang Gliding Indoors

The most dramatic change came when Food Rocks closed on January 3, 2004, and was demolished entirely. In its place came Soarin', a ride originally designed and built for Disney California Adventure that simulates hang gliding over a series of landscapes. Its massive screens and ride mechanics demanded a large physical addition to the building. Soarin' opened on May 5, 2005, the same day The Land officially reopened. Since May 26, 2026, it has been showing Soarin' Across America, a limited-run film made for the United States' 250th anniversary.

Nestlé withdrew as sponsor on February 13, 2009, and in 2018 Circle of Life gave way to Awesome Planet in the Harvest Theater. Through all of it, the pavilion's purpose has held steady, and beyond the hang-gliding theater the greenhouses remain a working research facility.

## Flight Context

The Land pavilion is near 28.374°N, 81.552°W in Epcot's World Nature area at Walt Disney World, southwest of Orlando, Florida. From 3,000–4,000 feet, use Epcot's 180-foot geodesic sphere, Spaceship Earth, as the reference point; The Land's large pavilion lies close by. Check current NOTAMs and charts for flight restrictions over the Walt Disney World parks and stay outside any restricted area. Nearby airports: Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 9 miles southeast, Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 15 miles east-northeast, and Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 18 miles northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are frequent over central Florida.
