# Star Tours – The Adventures Continue

Behind an Ewok-village facade at Disney's Hollywood Studios, a hydraulic flight simulator piloted by a reluctant C-3PO shuffles randomized trips across every era of the Star Wars saga, so that almost no two rides are the same.

**Location:** 28.3552, -81.5587

**Categories:** Disney's Hollywood Studios, Attractions based on Star Wars, Amusement rides introduced in 2011, Audio-Animatronic attractions, Motion simulator rides

## Introduction

The Imagineers had a problem with Hoth. They wanted riders to drop onto the ice planet in the middle of the famous Imperial assault, but the new attraction was set before the original Star Wars film, and that battle hadn't happened yet. So they swapped it for a quieter encounter with the planet's tauntauns and wampas and showed the idea to George Lucas. He liked it, but asked for the battle anyway, even if it bent his own saga's history. Perhaps, Lucas offered, there had been an earlier scuffle there, and the Rebels "later decided to build a base there figuring the Empire wouldn't think the rebels would return to that same location." That spirit of cheerful rule-bending runs through all of Star Tours – The Adventures Continue.

## A Spaceport in the Forest

Each of the four versions of the ride, at Disney's Hollywood Studios in Florida, Disneyland, Tokyo Disneyland and Disneyland Paris, has its own exterior. The Florida version stands out: it is inspired by an Ewok village on the forest moon of Endor, while the others are modeled on a Tomorrowland-style space port. Inside, the queue becomes a spaceport terminal. Posters advertise trips to distant planets, and a large screen lists flight statuses and planetary weather forecasts in both spoken Basic and the Aurebesh alphabet. Audio-Animatronic figures of C-3PO, R2-D2 and two Mon Calamari officers banter with each other and with guests. In the cargo bay sits Captain Rex, pilot of the original attraction, who in the ride's timeline hasn't been used yet and is being shipped back to the factory as defective. A droid scanning luggage reveals bags packed with in-jokes from Star Wars, Disney and Pixar films before guests collect their 3-D "flight glasses" and, in Florida, head to one of six boarding gates.

## Waiting for the Prequels

Disney first planned an upgrade in 1998, timed to Star Wars: Episode I – The Phantom Menace, with flights to the Boonta Eve podrace on Tatooine and 3-D glasses styled like Anakin Skywalker's goggles. The idea was shelved because two more films were coming with new characters and places, and a ride built around Episode I would soon be outdated. In April 2005, at Star Wars Celebration III in Indianapolis, Lucas confirmed that a second Star Tours was in production, and by May 2009 filming was underway in West Hollywood. The original ride in Florida closed on September 7, 2010. In June 2010 Disney announced that the new version would be set between Revenge of the Sith and A New Hope, which is why riders now board a "StarSpeeder 1000" rather than the original attraction's StarSpeeder 3000.

## C-3PO at the Controls

The pre-show sets up the premise: C-3PO, assigned to maintenance on your StarSpeeder, gets trapped in the cockpit when the captain steps out, and the ship takes off with him protesting that he is not the captain. Anthony Daniels, who played C-3PO in all the Star Wars films, returned to portray him as a live-action suit performer, through motion capture and as the voice of the Audio-Animatronic figure. The flight itself is assembled at random from an opening, a destination, a hologram message and a final stop, and one rider is always singled out as a Rebel spy whose photo flashes on screen. You might be caught in Darth Vader's Force grip, luge down a Hoth canyon or win the Boonta Eve podrace against Sebulba. Combined, the segments allow for many possible journeys: Disney cited 54 at launch, and the count has grown with each added destination. Underneath it all is the same hydraulic motion base that powered the original, a simulator patented as the Advanced Technology Leisure Application Simulator and first built by Rediffusion Simulation.

## A Galaxy That Keeps Expanding

In Florida, soft openings began on May 14, 2011, and the ride officially opened at midnight on May 20. Disneyland followed on June 3, Tokyo in 2013 and Paris in 2017. In 2012 the Themed Entertainment Association honored it as the most "Outstanding Attraction Refresh." The ride has never stopped growing. The desert planet Jakku from The Force Awakens arrived in November 2015. The mineral planet Crait and the remote outpost of Batuu followed in 2017, and Kef Bir and Exegol from The Rise of Skywalker were added on December 20, 2019, the day the film was released. More destinations came in April 2024. Today the attraction draws on characters and locations from all nine films of the Skywalker saga, with one sensible rule: sequel-trilogy scenes are never mixed with other eras.

## Flight Context

Star Tours sits at about 28.355°N, 81.559°W inside Disney's Hollywood Studios, part of the Walt Disney World Resort southwest of Orlando. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a standing NOTAM, reported as a 3 nm radius up to 3,000 feet AGL), so view the resort only from outside the restricted area and check the current NOTAM text before planning a route nearby. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 7.5 nm to the east-southeast, and Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east-northeast. Central Florida summers bring near-daily afternoon thunderstorms; mornings and the drier winter months are clearer.
