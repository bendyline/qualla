# Universal Epic Universe

Universal sold the land, bought most of it back, pushed through a pandemic and a hurricane, and in 2025 opened Epic Universe, a hub of starlit Celestial Park ringed by portals into four themed worlds.

**Location:** 28.4422, -81.4492

**Categories:** Universal Epic Universe, Amusement parks in Orlando, Florida, Tourist attractions in Greater Orlando, 2025 establishments in Florida, Compositions by Danny Elfman, Amusement parks opened in 2025

## Introduction

In 2003, Universal sold 1,800 acres of land near the Orange County Convention Center to a real estate developer for $70 million. A dozen or so years later it started buying the land back, parcel by parcel, beginning with roughly 475 acres for $130 million. The patience paid off. On May 22, 2025, Universal Epic Universe opened on that ground as the third theme park, and fourth park overall, at the Universal Orlando Resort in Orlando, Florida. Tom Williams, then chairman and CEO of Universal Parks & Resorts, called it the "most immersive and innovative theme park" the company had ever created.

## The Long Way Around

The story begins in 1998, when Universal paid Lockheed Martin $63 million for 2,100 acres near the convention center, planning a theme park, hotels and a sports complex. Instead, it sold pieces off to convention-related development: 230 acres to Orange County for $69 million in 1999 to expand the convention center, 26 acres to Hilton in 2000, 44 acres to Hyatt Hotels in 2001, and finally the remaining 1,800 acres to developer Stan Thomas in 2003.

The reversal started with that first purchase. In 2017 came 101 acres along Sand Lake Road for $27.5 million, and in 2018 Universal bought 135 acres from Stan Thomas in a deal that also resolved disputed deed restrictions, clearing the way for up to two new theme parks. More parcels followed for road and transit projects. Universal now owns 750 acres there, only part of it developed into the park, and it has given land to regional rail: 13 acres toward the Brightline route in 2022, with parcels set aside for a future SunRail station.

## Delays, a Pandemic and a Hurricane

Construction began as early as August 2019, aiming for a 2023 opening. The COVID-19 pandemic intervened, halting work from July 2020 to March 2021 and helping push the debut to 2025. Around the site, Universal built new infrastructure, including a $315 million, 1.7-mile extension of Kirkman Road to connect with Universal Boulevard.

The name changed along the way. In March 2023, when Universal Parks & Resorts rebranded as Universal Destinations & Experiences, Universal's Epic Universe lost its possessive "s" and gained a new logo, a simplified golden compass framing a starry backdrop. In October 2024, Hurricane Milton caused minor damage and a brief pause in construction, and later that month Universal confirmed the May 22, 2025, opening date, about two weeks before Universal Orlando's 35th anniversary. Employee previews began on March 1, 2025, followed by early entry for pass holders and Universal hotel guests in April, with a celebrity and executive preview the day before the grand opening.

## A Park Built Around the Stars

Epic Universe uses a hub-and-spoke layout. At its center is Celestial Park, unveiled in January 2024 and the first world guests encounter, with seven million individually controllable LEDs embedded throughout the area, and Universal planned fireworks and light shows there. On July 7, 2026, a nightly show called Universal Celestial Goodnight added fireworks to the fountains, music and lights. Its rides include the Constellation Carousel and Stardust Racers, a dueling launch coaster from Mack Rides. Around the hub sit walk-up bars and small eateries such as Frosty Moon, Meteor Astropub and Pizza Moon.

From Celestial Park, themed portals with digital LED signage open onto four lands. Each one carries its theme into everything from the food and cutlery to the music, staff uniforms and infrastructure. Out beyond it all is something unusual for Universal Orlando: an open-air parking lot on the southeast end of the site, where the other parks use garages.

## Four Worlds Through Four Portals

Clockwise from the entrance, the first portal is a Warp Pipe into Super Nintendo World, which unlike its counterparts in Hollywood and Japan includes escalators. Alongside Mario Kart: Bowser's Challenge and Yoshi's Adventure, its Donkey Kong Country section holds Mine-Cart Madness, a patented "Boom Coaster" whose trains ride a hidden track beneath a false one, creating the illusion that they leap over gaps.

Next comes Dark Universe, set in the dreary Darkmoor Village, where the Universal Classic Monsters return through Dr. Victoria Frankenstein, great-great-great granddaughter of Henry Frankenstein. Its name revives Universal's abandoned monster-film franchise, dropped after The Mummy (2017) struggled at the box office, and its Monsters Unchained: The Frankenstein Experiment uses KUKA robotic-arm technology.

The Wizarding World of Harry Potter: Ministry of Magic, set in 1920s Paris and inspired by the Fantastic Beasts films, is home to Harry Potter and the Battle at the Ministry. Finally, How to Train Your Dragon: Isle of Berk, the largest of the four, is set between the second and third films, with the Hiccup's Wing Gliders family coaster and a stage show, The Untrainable Dragon.

## Flight Context

Universal Epic Universe is at approximately 28.4422°N, 81.4492°W, south of Sand Lake Road and east of Universal Boulevard, about 2.5 miles south-southeast of the original Universal Orlando parks and near the Orange County Convention Center. From 2,500–3,500 feet, look for the ring of four themed lands around the central Celestial Park hub and the large open-air parking lot on the site's southeast end. Nearby airports: Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 8.5 statute miles east, inside Orlando's Class B airspace; Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 10 statute miles northeast; and Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 10.5 statute miles south. Check current NOTAMs for flight restrictions over the theme parks. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common across central Florida.
