# Maelstrom (ride)

For 26 years, Epcot's Maelstrom sent longship boats past Odin and a three-headed troll, spun them backward toward the edge of the Norway pavilion, and dropped them into a stormy North Sea.

**Location:** 28.3708, -81.5464

**Categories:** Amusement rides introduced in 1988, Amusement rides closed in 2014, Audio-Animatronic attractions, Dark rides, Water rides, Epcot, Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Works set in Norway, Walt Disney Parks and Resorts gentle boat rides, World Showcase

## Introduction

For a moment, the boat hung backward over the edge. Guests strolling the Norway pavilion's main thoroughfare at Epcot could look up and see the stern of a longship poking out through the building's facade, perched at the lip of a waterfall. Inside, riders had just been cursed by trolls and shoved into reverse. Then the track pivoted, the boat swung forward again, and it plunged down a 28-foot flume into a storm. That was Maelstrom, a reversing boat dark ride with a flume drop (Wikipedia files it under Shoot the Chute) that ran in Epcot's World Showcase from 1988 to 2014.

## The Ride Once Called SeaVenture

Walt Disney Imagineering designed Maelstrom as something unusual, a mix of log chute and traditional film attraction. Visitors rode boats patterned after longships through scenes populated by Audio-Animatronics figures. It very nearly carried a different name. During construction, the entrance sign read "SeaVenture," and only sometime between March 1988 and opening day did the title change. Maelstrom opened on July 5, 1988, in the Norway Pavilion of the park's World Showcase.

## Odin, a Nokken and a Three-Headed Troll

The journey began quietly. Boats left the dock, turned a corner into a dark tunnel and climbed the flume's lift hill as a voice set the terms: "those who seek the spirit of Norway face peril and adventure, but more often find beauty and charm." At the top, the lit face of the Norse god Odin hovered overhead. From there the boats drifted past seafarers and maritime villages, a mythological version of Norway's Viking days.

The peril came in a marsh. There the boat met Audio-Animatronic depictions of a Nokken and a three-headed troll, and the trolls, angered by the trespassers, cast a spell. Suddenly the boat began moving backward, fast, driven by conveyor belts hidden beneath the water's surface.

## Backward Into the North Sea

Traveling in reverse, riders slid briskly past Atlantic puffins, polar bears and living trees before stopping at the edge of another waterfall, the moment when the boat's backward end peeked out through the facade above the pavilion. The track pivoted, and correctly oriented at last, the boat plunged forward down the 28-foot flume into a stormy depiction of the North Sea. It passed very close to an oil rig before coming to an abrupt stop in the calm harbor of a small village, where the narrator delivered the moral: "Norway's spirit has always been, and will always be adventure."

On the way out, guests could stay for a short tourism film shot in 70mm, about five to six minutes long. The Spirit of Norway highlighted the real country's attractions, including skiing, hiking and Kjerag mountain.

## Mickey in the Mural, Then a Frozen Farewell

Sharp-eyed riders found a few jokes hidden in the scenery. The loading area featured a large painted mural crowded with people and elements from Norway's history, and hidden among them were a Viking whose helmet sported Mickey Mouse ears and another figure wearing a Mickey Mouse watch. In the stave church, King Olaf II wore a tiny Mickey embroidered on his tunic, near his right thigh.

In September 2014, Disney announced that Maelstrom would be replaced by an attraction based on Disney Animation's 2013 film Frozen. The trolls cast their last spell on October 5, 2014. Frozen Ever After opened in its place on June 21, 2016.
