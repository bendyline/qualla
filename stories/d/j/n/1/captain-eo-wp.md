# Captain EO

Michael Jackson, Francis Ford Coppola and George Lucas made Captain EO, a 17-minute 3D space musical that was among the first "4D" films and returned to Epcot as a tribute after Jackson's death.

**Location:** 28.3726, -81.5509

**Categories:** Epcot, Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, 3D short films, Michael Jackson, Amusement park attractions introduced in 1986, Amusement park attractions that closed in 2015

## Introduction

Seventeen minutes of film, a reported $23.7 million to $30 million. When Captain EO arrived in Disney theme parks in 1986, it was billed as the most expensive film ever produced on a per-minute basis, with estimates running as high as about $1.76 million for every minute on screen. The money bought an unlikely creative summit: Michael Jackson as the star, Francis Ford Coppola as director and George Lucas as executive producer. It also bought a new kind of show. Asteroids, lasers, smoke and starfields spilled off the screen and into the theater, making Captain EO one of the first so-called "4D" films, a 3D movie synchronized with in-theater effects.

## Filmed in Secret

Captain EO was a co-production between Walt Disney Studios and Lucasfilm, made in collaboration with Walt Disney Imagineering. Rusty Lemorande produced it and wrote the script with Lucas and Coppola, working from a story idea by Imagineering's artists; Lemorande also designed and created two of the creatures and helped edit the film. Jeffrey Hornaday and Jackson choreographed the dance numbers, and cinematographer Vittorio Storaro served as lighting director for much of the principal photography.

Filming began on July 15, 1985. Imagineering and Eastman Kodak shot it using 70mm 3-D photography, and at the request of Jackson and Lucas, the work was carried out in considerable secrecy. James Horner wrote the orchestral score, and Jackson wrote and performed two songs for the film, "We Are Here to Change the World" and "Another Part of Me."

## A Song for the Supreme Leader

The story is a space fable. Captain EO and his ragtag crew fly to a world of rotting, twisted metal and steaming vents to deliver a gift to a witch known as the Supreme Leader. The crew includes a small flying cat-like sidekick named Fuzzball, a two-headed navigator called Geek, the robots Major Domo and Minor Domo, and Hooter, a clumsy elephant-like shipmate who always manages to upset the mission.

Captured and condemned, EO tells the Supreme Leader that he sees the beauty hidden inside her and has brought the key to unlock it: a song. The robots transform into instruments and the crew starts to play, until Hooter trips over EO's cape and breaks his instrument, stopping the music. He repairs it just in time. EO turns the hulking guards into dancers, Fuzzball ties the whips of two Whip Warriors together, and at last EO transforms the Supreme Leader into a beautiful woman and her lair into a peaceful Greek temple. The crew flies off to "Another Part of Me," a song that later appeared, remixed, on Jackson's 1987 album Bad.

## Gone, Then Missed

At Epcot, Captain EO closed on July 6, 1994, to make way for Honey, I Shrunk the Audience!, and over the next few years the other parks replaced it too. In 1995 it made its only television appearance, on MTV, in a down-converted 2-D version. It has never been officially released on home video, though bootleg copies exist.

For several years a small group of fans petitioned Disney to bring it back. After Michael Jackson died on June 25, 2009, Captain EO found new popularity online and the campaign reached its peak. Disney officials reportedly held a private screening at Disneyland's Magic Eye Theater to decide whether it could be shown again. On September 10, Disney CEO Bob Iger said there were no plans to bring it back at that time but that the company was "looking at it," adding that if Disney did bring it back, "we'd want to make sure we do it right."

## The Tribute Years

On December 18, 2009, Disney announced EO's return. It reopened at Disneyland on February 23, 2010, then at Disneyland Paris on June 12 and Tokyo Disneyland on July 1. At Epcot, the film had a soft opening on June 30 before officially returning on July 2.

Billed as the "Captain EO Tribute," the revival was not quite the original. The enormous fiber-optic starfield wall, the smoke and the lasers had been removed when the show first closed, and they did not come back. Instead, the tribute borrowed the hardware of the attraction it replaced. Hydraulics built for Honey, I Shrunk the Audience! shook the seats along with EO's spaceship and bounced them to the beat of Jackson's music, that show's hidden water sprayers fired when Hooter sneezed, and its leg ticklers were reused for the Whip Warriors.

## The Final Curtain

The second run proved shorter than the first. Tokyo Disneyland closed Captain EO on June 30, 2014. Days later, on July 4, Disneyland began using its theater for a sneak peek of Marvel Studios' Guardians of the Galaxy, initially expecting EO to return; it never did, and the venue was later renamed the Tomorrowland Theater. On November 13, 2015, Disney announced that Epcot's presentation would end as well, making way for a Disney and Pixar short film festival. On December 6, 2015, Captain EO was shown for the final time at Epcot.
