# The Great Movie Ride

Housed in a replica of Grauman's Chinese Theatre, the Great Movie Ride carried Disney guests through a century of cinema, from Busby Berkeley to Alien, for 28 years, and it was so strong an idea that Disney built an entire theme park around it.

**Location:** 28.3565, -81.5605

**Categories:** Disney's Hollywood Studios, Dark rides, Audio-Animatronic attractions, Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Amusement rides introduced in 1989, Amusement rides closed in 2017

## Introduction

The Great Movie Ride was supposed to be one attraction in one pavilion. Walt Disney Imagineering first sketched it as the centerpiece of a show-business pavilion at Epcot called "Great Moments at the Movies." Then Disney's new chief executive, Michael Eisner, and Imagineering president Marty Sklar decided the idea was too big for a pavilion. It deserved a whole park. The concept grew into the Disney-MGM Studios, and when that park opened on May 1, 1989, the ride sat at its heart, inside a replica of Grauman's Chinese Theatre, one of Hollywood's most famous movie palaces.

## Hooray for Hollywood

Guests wound through a recreated theater lobby lined with glass cases of real costumes and props. Over the years those cases held a pair of ruby slippers from The Wizard of Oz, Sam's piano from Casablanca and a Dejarik board from the original Star Wars. The line ended in a 1930s Hollywood soundstage, complete with a neon marquee and a painted panorama of the Hollywood Hills crowned by the original Hollywoodland sign. As "Hooray for Hollywood" played, riders settled into open, theater-style vehicles with a live tour guide standing in the front cab. The journey began with musicals: a tiered cake of starlets from Busby Berkeley's Footlight Parade, Gene Kelly swinging from a lamppost in Singin' in the Rain, and Mary Poppins and Bert on the rooftops of London. To represent the broad sweep of American film, Disney licensed films from outside its own library, through licensing deals that included MGM and Turner Entertainment.

## Hijacked

Then the ride turned into a heist. In the gangster scene, riders rolled through the shadowy back streets of 1930s Chicago past an Audio-Animatronic James Cagney from The Public Enemy, and a live gangster sometimes burst out, traded shots with rival mobsters, chased off the guide and commandeered the vehicle. For riders in the other vehicle, a live bandit did the same in a Western town watched over by Clint Eastwood and John Wayne, setting the bank ablaze with dynamite on the way out. The hijacker drove on through the Nostromo from Alien, where Ripley stood ready with a flamethrower and a Xenomorph lunged from the ceiling, and into a snake-filled Egyptian tomb from Raiders of the Lost Ark. There, ignoring a temple guard's warning, the villain reached for a priceless jewel. Fire and smoke erupted, a skeleton appeared where the thief had stood, and the robed guard turned out to be the original tour guide, who climbed back aboard to finish the tour.

## Munchkinland and Beyond

The final act moved through a burial chamber of stirring mummies, a jungle where Tarzan swung past Jane seated on an elephant, and the airport farewell of Casablanca, with Humphrey Bogart and Ingrid Bergman beside a waiting plane. After a glimpse of Mickey Mouse as the Sorcerer's Apprentice, the vehicles rolled into Munchkinland, where Dorothy's house had just landed on the Wicked Witch of the East. Munchkins emerged singing, until a plume of smoke delivered the Wicked Witch of the West. That witch was later upgraded to a far more lifelike figure using Sarcos technology, capable of quicker and more varied movement. The ride ended in a darkened theater with a montage of great film moments drawn from nearly every major studio, with one pointed exception. Nothing released by Universal Pictures appeared, since its parent company runs the rival Universal Orlando Resort.

## Behind the Scenery

Recreating famous faces raised delicate questions. At the request of John Wayne's family, actor Chad Everett voiced Wayne's figure. The Western scene was originally planned to include Lee Marvin's inebriated Kid Shelleen from Cat Ballou, but Marvin's family felt that portrayal was inappropriate, so Clint Eastwood took his place. Engineering proved just as tricky. For about the first year, the Footlight Parade cake actually rotated and sprayed water like the one in the film, but the mechanism kept breaking and the pumps kept failing and flooding the ride path, so the cake was frozen in place and animated with light instead. Even the Casablanca airplane carried a myth. The Lockheed Electra Junior was often claimed to be the actual plane from the film, yet no full-size plane was used in the filming at all.

## The Final Reel

In 2014 Turner Classic Movies became the ride's sponsor, and in 2015 TCM host Robert Osborne began welcoming guests in new pre-show and onboard narration. The partnership did not last long. On July 15, 2017, Disney announced that the Great Movie Ride would close to make way for Mickey & Minnie's Runaway Railway. The last riders boarded at 9:30 p.m. on August 13, and by the next morning signs for its replacement were already going up. The Chinese Theatre facade survived, and so did its forecourt, where handprints and signatures from Audrey Hepburn, Bob Hope, George Lucas and Mickey Mouse himself were left in place. Inside the new attraction, careful eyes can spot three tributes to the old one: a poster for "The Great Moving Ride," a mailbox bearing the words "No Place Like Home," and an alley cat whose yowl once echoed through the gangster scene.
