# Hontoon Island State Park

A car-free island in the St. Johns River, reached only by boat or ferry, where an ancient shell mound waits at the end of a shaded trail.

**Location:** 28.9669, -81.3631

**Categories:** Parks in Volusia County, Florida, State parks of Florida, Florida Native American Heritage Trail, DeLand, Florida

## Introduction

You cannot drive onto Hontoon Island. The only ways across are by private boat or aboard the park's own passenger ferry, and once you land, no motorized vehicles are allowed at all, with the single exception of wheelchairs. That one rule sets the pace for everything else here. On 1,648 acres wrapped by the St. Johns River, the Hontoon Dead River and Snake Creek, you move by foot, by bicycle or by paddle, and the island moves at the speed of the water around it.

## The Mound at Trail's End

Long before there was a ferry, the island had people. The Mayaca, also called Mayacan, are named as its first known inhabitants, and the clearest evidence of its early people is a large shell mound that still stands at the end of the Hammock Trail. Getting there means a walk of 1.7 miles each way, most of it in shade, through a landscape that shifts as you go: pine flatwoods, hammocks of palm and oak, bald cypress swamps and open marsh. In places, boardwalks carry visitors above the ground to protect the fragile soil. The reward at the end is not a monument built for visitors but the mound itself, built up over centuries by the island's early residents and still standing.

## Nine and a Half Miles Around

The water is the island's other trail. Paddlers can circle Hontoon entirely in a day trip of about nine and a half miles, and Blue Spring State Park lies a short paddle of 3.5 miles up the St. Johns. Canoes and kayaks are available to rent on the island, along with bicycles for the bike paths. Birdlife is a big part of the draw. Sandhill cranes have been photographed on the island itself, and along the St. Johns nearby, visitors have captured limpkins, yellow-crowned night herons, red-shouldered hawks and American black vultures, a barred owl near the island, and one tree crowded with American white ibis. Black-crowned night herons and pileated woodpeckers turn up too, for anyone patient enough to drift and watch.

## A Night Without Traffic

Staying over has long been part of the appeal. The park lists twelve primitive tent sites and six rustic cabins, plus boat slips for those who arrive under their own power. Hurricane damage closed the park for about three years before it reopened on October 20, 2025, so check with the park for current overnight availability. Picnic tables, playground equipment and a 3.4-mile nature trail fill the daytime hours, alongside fishing, hiking, horseshoes and simple nature photography. There is even a lending library stocked with picture books for children aged four to nine. There is no swimming, though. The park opens at 8 a.m. every day of the year, and the ferry's last run leaves about an hour before sunset.

## Flight Context

Hontoon Island State Park lies at 28.9669°N, 81.3631°W in Volusia County, about six miles west of DeLand off State Road 44. From 2,000–3,000 feet, look for a wooded island ringed by the broad, winding St. Johns River on one side and the narrower Hontoon Dead River and Snake Creek on the others, with marshland spreading out around it; Blue Spring State Park is a few miles upriver. DeLand Municipal (KDED) is about 7 nm northeast, Orlando Sanford International (KSFB) about 13 nm south-southeast, and Daytona Beach International (KDAB) about 20 nm northeast. Orlando Sanford's Class C airspace lies to the south-southeast. Central Florida summers bring daily afternoon thunderstorms, so morning flights offer the calmest air and clearest views.
