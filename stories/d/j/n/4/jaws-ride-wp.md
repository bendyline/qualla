# Jaws (ride)

Universal Studios Florida's Jaws boat ride stalked guests through Amity Harbor from 1990 to 2012, surviving a disastrous debut and a total rebuild before giving way to Harry Potter's Diagon Alley.

**Location:** 28.4799, -81.4699

**Categories:** Universal Studios Florida, Jaws (franchise), Former Universal Studios Florida attractions, Animatronic attractions, Amusement rides based on film franchises

## Introduction

"Ten minutes? We'll be shark bait in ten minutes!" For years, a boat skipper at Universal Studios Florida delivered that line after Chief Brody radioed that help was on the way, then grabbed a grenade launcher as a great white shark lunged at the boat again. Jaws, which opened with the park on June 7, 1990, took guests on what began as a leisurely harbor tour of the fictional Amity Island and turned, abruptly, into a fight for survival. The Florida original closed in 2012, but its twin still sails at Universal Studios Japan.

## Captain Jake's Amity Boat Tours

The ride had its own backstory. In the attraction's version of events, Chief Brody, Matt Hooper and Quint killed the shark in 1974, the incident inspired Steven Spielberg's Hollywood movie, and fear of sharks drove tourists away from Amity Island. To bring them back, local seaman Jake Grundy started a boat tour to the places where the attacks had happened.

Guests waited in a string of boathouses near Amity Harbor, filled with fishing gear and nautical artifacts, while overhead monitors played the local station, WJWS13, "The Station That BITES," with a low-budget talk show called "Hey There, Amity!", ads for local businesses and promos for classic films and shows. Aboard, the skipper pointed out the army-surplus 40mm grenade launcher and promised it would not be needed, because no sharks had been seen in the area since the attacks.

## Something in the Water

The tour rolled past Chief Brody's house and the harbor shops toward a lighthouse on a rocky jetty. Then the radio crackled: a fellow skipper named Gordon was calling for help, and his voice dissolved into screams and silence. Rounding the jetty, guests found his boat, Amity 3, sinking. A dorsal fin rose beside the wreck, dove, and rocked the boat from below. The skipper fired twice and missed.

Hiding in a boathouse only made it worse. The walls shook as the shark battered them, and the boat refused to go into gear until the last possible second, just as the shark's head burst inside and grazed the hull. Back in the open, a wild grenade hit Bridewell's Gas Dock, which erupted in flames. The skipper made for an old fishing pier beside a high-voltage barge, where the shark bit into a submerged power cable and electrocuted itself. The smell of roasted shark filled the air, and when the burnt creature rose for one last lunge, the skipper finally hit it.

## The Shark That Wouldn't Work

The ride grew from a small scene on the Studio Tour at Universal Studios Hollywood, where trams have passed through Jaws-inspired sets since 1976. For Florida, MCA/Universal Planning and Development worked with Ride & Show Engineering, the company behind those original tour scenes, and Spielberg served as creative consultant.

The debut was rough. Like fellow opening-day rides Kongfrontation and Earthquake: The Big One, Jaws broke down often, but unlike them its problems persisted, and it was often evacuated on a daily basis. In July 1990 a 39-year-old man fell into the water after a boat railing allegedly broke; according to his lawyer, other riders applauded as he was pulled back aboard, thinking it was part of the show. He was not seriously hurt and sued Universal for $1 million. In August 1990 Universal shut the ride down and sued Ride & Show Engineering over its design.

## A New Shark for 1993

Partial fixes through 1992 had only limited success, so Universal brought in Totally Fun Company, ITEC Entertainment, Intamin and Oceaneering International to install an entirely new ride system. Oceaneering built the animatronic shark, its first theme park project. The opening scene, in which the shark bit the boat and spun it 180 degrees, became the gas dock explosion. The finale, originally modeled on the first film's rifle shot into an oxygen tank, was replaced with the electrocution scene loosely based on Jaws 2. The rebuilt ride reopened in the summer of 1993 with Roy Scheider, Lorraine Gary and Steven Spielberg on hand.

A second Jaws opened at Universal Studios Japan on March 31, 2001, using a ride system developed by MTS Systems Corporation.

## Last Boat Out of Amity

The pyrotechnics and boats ran on petroleum, and in January 2005, with fuel costs rising, Universal temporarily closed the ride. It returned that December as a seasonal attraction, and guest complaints brought it back full-time in February 2007. Then, on December 2, 2011, Universal announced that Jaws and the whole Amity area would close for good, and a few days later it revealed that the site would make room for the second phase of The Wizarding World of Harry Potter. On the evening of January 2, 2012, a skipper took the last 48 guests on the final voyage. By the next morning Amity was walled off, and it was demolished in the months that followed; Diagon Alley opened on the site on July 8, 2014.

A few traces survive. The hanging shark statue from Amity's town square now hangs in the park's Fisherman's Wharf area, and a building still reads Amity Island Lobster Co.

## Flight Context

The former Jaws site, now Diagon Alley, lies near 28.480°N, 81.470°W in Universal Studios Florida, part of the Universal Orlando Resort, Orlando, Florida. From about 2,500–3,500 feet, look for the studio park's soundstages, with Universal Islands of Adventure just to the south. Nearby airports: Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 9.5 miles east-northeast, Orlando International Airport (KMCO) about 10 miles east-southeast (check the Orlando terminal-area chart for airspace before overflying), and Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 13 miles south. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
