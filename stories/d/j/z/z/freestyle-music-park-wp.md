# Freestyle Music Park

Myrtle Beach's rock-and-roll theme park opened into the teeth of the 2008 recession, collapsed twice in two seasons, and saw its rides shipped to Vietnam and its land taken over by a FedEx distribution center.

**Location:** 33.7140, -78.9340

**Categories:** Defunct amusement parks in the United States, Amusement parks in South Carolina, Tourist attractions in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, Amusement parks closed in 2009, Business history

## Introduction

In the summer of 2008, a roller coaster named for Led Zeppelin carried riders beside the Intracoastal Waterway in Myrtle Beach. Fourteen years later, FedEx announced a nearly $64 million investment on the same ground for a distribution operation. In between lies one of the stranger stories in American amusement parks: a music-themed park that opened as Hard Rock Park, went bankrupt within months, came back as Freestyle Music Park, failed again, and left behind rides that ended up on the other side of the world.

## A Late-Night Email

The idea took several shapes before it found its tune. Early on, Jon Binkowski and Steven Goodwin considered a park themed to the four seasons, until investors called that too generic. A partnership with Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer for a movie-themed park proved too costly. Then, in 2002, Binkowski proposed a Hard Rock Cafe-themed park in a late-night email to Goodwin, who had worked for Hard Rock Cafe in the late 1990s. Getting it built was another matter. When the developers sought $25 million to $30 million from Horry County but were reluctant to submit detailed business plans, the county council refused to approve the bond financing. A licensing deal with Hard Rock finally came together in 2006. The financing, mostly junk bonds, included a loan of $385 million for a park whose construction cost was reported as $225 million (press accounts put the total investment nearer $400 million), and a feasibility study predicted 3 million visitors in the first year. Many locals, having watched earlier plans for the site go nowhere, doubted it would ever open.

## Nights in White Satin

It did. When its promoters announced it in 2007, Hard Rock Park was billed as the first major new amusement park in the United States since Legoland California in 1999. Its signature attraction, Led Zeppelin The Ride, topped out in July 2007. The 55-acre park at the junction of US 501 and the Intracoastal Waterway opened to the public in April 2008 and held its official opening on June 2 with a concert by the Eagles and The Moody Blues. There were rides, live shows, gardens, play areas and a 10,000-seat amphitheater. Associated Press travel editor Beth J. Harpaz named Nights in White Satin: The Trip one of her all-time favorite rides at any park. Skeptics had warned early. Amusement industry expert Dennis Spiegel called the plan "questionable" because it could not operate year-round, and local businesspeople thought the guest projections were far too high. Their doubts were about to be tested.

## The Worst Possible Timing

The 2008 financial crisis froze credit markets just as the park needed money to advertise. High gas and hotel prices kept travelers home, and with little advertising, attendance fell far short. By one count the park drew only about 370,000 guests that season, against a projected 3 million. In September, majority owner Africa Israel Investments wrote off its entire $10 million investment. The park ended its season more than a month early, laid off most of its employees and filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, converting to Chapter 7 in January 2009. The following month a Delaware court approved its sale for $25 million to FPI MB Entertainment. The new owners dropped the Hard Rock name, which was now tied to bankruptcy and not considered family-oriented, and the court required every Hard Rock souvenir to be destroyed. Renamed Freestyle Music Park, promising genres from reggae to beach music, it reopened on May 23, 2009. Attendance again fell below projections, and it closed for good at the end of that season. Martin Durham, its former vice president for entertainment, later said the biggest culprit was the recession that hit right as it opened.

## Coasters Bound for Vietnam

The years after were a slow unwinding. Foreclosure proceedings began in 2010, and court filings traced the park's mortgage holder to a division of the Moscow-based MT Development. In 2013 a local Christian nonprofit arts group, Abiding Village, tried to raise $10 million to buy the park without its rides and turn it into an education and entertainment complex; with five days left it had raised $1 million. That November, the rides went up for sale. The Wave Swinger and Balloon Race went to Seabreeze Amusement Park, and by February 2015 everything had been dismantled. Most of the rides were reassembled at Asia Park in Da Nang, Vietnam. Three roller coasters, among them the former Led Zeppelin ride, were set up there but never operated, and were dismantled in 2017. Coaster track from the park later turned up at Dragon Park Ha Long, where a ride opened in 2017 as Dragon's Run. Back in Myrtle Beach, Medieval Times bought about four acres it had used before 2008 to exercise and graze its horses.

## Not Another Theme Park

Even empty, the park found one more role: it served as a set for the TV series Revolution, playing a post-apocalyptic amusement park. In December 2018 former Myrtle Beach mayor John Rhodes bought the property, saying only that any development would take advantage of the waterway and that "it will not be another theme park." Three fires broke out on the grounds in 2019, and a preliminary investigation found that two of them were not "of an accidental ignition." Horry County weighed zoning changes in 2019 and 2021 to allow uses ranging from equipment maintenance to boat storage and distribution. In 2022, a permit application for an approximately 250,000-square-foot FedEx facility went under review, and that October Horry County approved an agreement for a nearly $64 million FedEx investment on the former park site. Local news reporting since describes the distribution center as built and operating. The road outside, once Outlet Boulevard and then Hard Rock Parkway, has been Fantasy Harbour Boulevard since 2009.

## Flight Context

Coordinates: 33.714 N, 78.934 W, where US 501 crosses the Atlantic Intracoastal Waterway on the inland side of Myrtle Beach, South Carolina. The site sits just north of Myrtle Beach International Airport (KMYR, about 2 nm south), so you will be inside its Class C airspace; coordinate with approach and fly at pattern-safe altitudes, around 1,500-2,000 ft. Look for the waterway and the US 501 bridge as your references.
