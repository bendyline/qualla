# Ocean Forest Hotel

Designed by Rockefeller Center architect Raymond Hood and opened in 1930, Myrtle Beach's grand Ocean Forest Hotel bankrupted its founder, reigned as the Grand Strand's glamorous stopover, and was imploded on Friday, September 13, 1974.

**Location:** 33.7295, -78.8352

## Introduction

The Ocean Forest Hotel came down on Friday, September 13, 1974. The ten-story tower that had once been a Myrtle Beach showpiece was imploded, clearing the oceanfront for condominiums. The date seemed to suit a building whose luck had run bad from the start. It opened in 1930 with marble columns, crystal chandeliers and ballrooms, the dream of a textile magnate who had already lost most of his fortune in the stock market crash before the doors opened. For four decades it hosted travelers between New York and Miami. Today little more than a roundabout marks where its grand entrance once stood.

## A Textile Magnate's Vision

The hotel was the vision of John T. Woodside, a textile magnate from Greenville, South Carolina. Woodside's company bought 65,000 acres from the Myrtle Beach Farms Company, including oceanfront land for a hotel, and he set out to build a resort for the upper class. He started with golf. The Ocean Forest Country Club and its golf course were completed in 1928, and Woodside then turned his attention to the hotel that would anchor the whole enterprise. His timing could hardly have been worse. As construction advanced, the stock market crash of 1929 wiped out most of his fortune, leaving him with a nearly finished luxury hotel and no way to pay for it. The building would open anyway, a monument to ambitions formed in a very different economy.

## Marble on the Grand Strand

The hotel was designed by Raymond Hood, one of the architects of New York's Rockefeller Center. The Ocean Forest formally opened on February 21, 1930, after costing about $1 million to build. Its ten-story main tower rose between two five-story wings, and inside it offered marble columns, crystal chandeliers, large ballrooms and elevators, features that set it apart from every other hotel then operating in Myrtle Beach. The property spread across 13 acres of gardens, stables and pools on the oceanfront, near present-day Porcher Drive. It also enjoyed a strategic spot. Located halfway between New York City and Miami, the Ocean Forest came to be known as "the Grand Central Station of the area," a place where well-heeled travelers paused on their way up and down the coast.

## Foreclosure and Fading Glamour

Grandeur could not cover the debts. Once the hotel was complete, Woodside was unable to make the mortgage payments, and his bank, Iselin and Company of New York, foreclosed on the hotel, the country club and the surrounding property. The bank held them for several years. A group of independent investors eventually bought the hotel and country club, while the Myrtle Beach Farms Company repossessed much of the land. The hotel itself outlasted these reversals, but after World War II the building did not receive the maintenance and upkeep it required. Year by year, the once-elegant resort became an aging giant, with the costs of keeping it running only growing.

## Friday the Thirteenth

In 1973 new owners bought the hotel and began selling off its contents. The lighthouse that once stood on top of the building reportedly found a second life at Family Kingdom Amusement Park. The cost of running the Ocean Forest had grown too high, and a Sun News article at the time reported that it was "unable to meet the requirements of its insurers." On Friday, September 13, 1974, the hotel was imploded to make way for a condominium development. Traces remain for those who know where to look. A roundabout marks the spot where the hotel's entrance stood, with roads extending toward Pine Lakes Country Club, once the Ocean Forest Country Club. A 1924 family beach house that had stood beside the hotel was moved south to Springmaid Beach and became the Franklin G. Burroughs-Simeon B. Chapin Art Museum, and Ocean Forest Villas stands on the hotel's site today.

## Flight Context

The Ocean Forest Hotel site is at 33.7295°N, 78.8352°W on the Myrtle Beach oceanfront along the Grand Strand, near present-day Porcher Drive, where Ocean Forest Villas now stands. From 1,000–2,000 feet over the beach, the hotel is gone, so look for the roundabout that marked its entrance and the golf course of Pine Lakes Country Club, the former Ocean Forest Country Club, inland from it. The Myrtle Beach Pavilion site is about 3.2 nm southwest along the beach, Family Kingdom Amusement Park about 4 nm southwest, and Springmaid Beach about 6 nm southwest.
