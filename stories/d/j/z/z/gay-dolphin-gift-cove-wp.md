# Gay Dolphin Gift Cove

On Myrtle Beach's oceanfront since the 1940s, the Gay Dolphin Gift Cove calls itself "the nation's largest gift shop," a four-story maze of 50 themed coves, a glass tower and a family history that outlasted Hurricane Hazel.

**Location:** 33.6928, -78.8783

**Categories:** Tourist attractions in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, Buildings and structures in Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, 1946 establishments in South Carolina

## Introduction

The best-selling item in what the store bills as "the nation's largest gift shop" is not a T-shirt or a seashell. It is a name tag. At the Gay Dolphin Gift Cove on Myrtle Beach's oceanfront, co-owner Michelle Plyler has said the top seller is name tags, displayed on a wall holding 3,000 names that is updated every year and ordered using the Social Security Administration's list of top names. The store at 916 North Ocean Boulevard is four stories tall, and as of 2011 it covered 26,000 square feet and averaged 70,000 items, according to store owner Justin "Buz" Plyler; a 2026 local news report describes it as about 30,000 square feet divided into eight offset levels.

## Fifty Coves of Everything

Inside, the Gay Dolphin is divided into 50 sections called "coves," each its own small world of merchandise. The Elvis Cove lets shoppers pose for a photo with a life-size Elvis Presley. The inventory has included "sea shells, brushes for bald men and noisy seat cushions," and Myrtle Beach T-shirts have been another popular item. Buz Plyler has reached for big comparisons: in 2023 he said the store had "more items than Sears Roebuck had in its heyday" and "more items than you'll find in a Walmart Superstore," with about 50 people working there. Over the years the store has also sold live animals. It once carried baby alligators, until they became too expensive, and later sold small turtles and hermit crabs.

## Rebuilt After Hazel

The store opened in 1946, three years before Buz Plyler was born in 1949. Its first building did not last. Hurricane Hazel destroyed the original store in 1954, and it was rebuilt on the same site, starting near the beach and eventually adding a six-story glass tower beside Ocean Boulevard. Buz started working for his parents at the age of eight, taking on the jobs his father disliked, and eventually became the buyer for all of the store's merchandise. By 2011 he had dealt with some sellers for fifty years, and he also bought goods from bankrupt suppliers at a discount. Eloise Plyler worked for the store until she died in 2009.

## The Tower, the Falls and the Dolphin Sign

For years the glass tower, at one time among the tallest buildings in the city, was an attraction of its own. Visitors could climb its circular steel steps to the roof for a view of Myrtle Beach, at a cost of $1 as of 2000, though anyone who could not find their name on the wall of bicycle license plates could climb for free. The tower once housed the Wonder Falls, oil-rain structures that created the illusion of waterfalls, running through barrels on the ceiling down to a wishing well below. When the pump broke in the early 2000s and the parts could not be replaced, the falls were removed, and liability and insurance concerns ended the tower climbs in 2006. Lights were added to the tower in 2019. Outside, a sign with wooden dolphins went up in the 1970s and came down for Hurricane Hugo. A 1979 zoning law said such a sign could not be replaced once taken down, but it went back up in 1994 and has stayed despite the city's efforts to remove it. The Gay Dolphin story also includes an amusement park with a Wild Mouse roller coaster, which was less fortunate: it closed in the 1970s because of one man's reckless behavior on the coaster.

## A Park for the Founder

The store's co-founder, Justin Plyler Sr., died in March 2002. Two years earlier, in 2000, the Myrtle Beach City Council had approved buying 0.85 acres by the ocean at the end of what became Mr. Joe White Avenue, where a motel, a wax museum and two beachwear stores stood, trading several alleys plus cash for the land. On June 3, 2005, at the Sun Fun Festival, the new park was dedicated to the elder Plyler. A concrete walkway called the Boardwalk had long run from the Myrtle Beach Pavilion past the Gay Dolphin to the park, and when the Myrtle Beach Boardwalk was completed in 2010, the store improved its boardwalk entrance. Plyler Park became home to the "Hot Summer Nights" concert series, and in 2011 the Myrtle Beach SkyWheel, the tallest Ferris wheel in the eastern United States when it was built, opened next door.

## Flight Context

The Gay Dolphin stands at 916 North Ocean Boulevard, 33.6928°N, 78.8783°W, on the Myrtle Beach oceanfront along the Grand Strand. From 1,000–2,000 feet over the beach, look for the boardwalk, Plyler Park and the large SkyWheel Ferris wheel beside it; the Gay Dolphin's glass tower sits just along the boardwalk. Myrtle Beach International Airport (KMYR) is only about 3 nm west-southwest, so this stretch lies within the airport's busy terminal area; coordinate with KMYR before sightseeing low along the beach. Grand Strand Airport (KCRE) in North Myrtle Beach is about 10 nm northeast. Expect sea breezes, beach haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
