# The Progressive Club

Esau Jenkins's Progressive Club on Johns Island was a grocery store, child care center, dormitory and meeting hall rolled into one, built in 1963 to house a Citizenship School that taught African-American neighbors to register and vote.

**Location:** 32.6764, -80.0422

**Categories:** African-American history of South Carolina, Clubhouses on the National Register of Historic Places in South Carolina, Buildings and structures completed in 1963, Buildings and structures in Charleston County, South Carolina, National Register of Historic Places in Charleston County, South Carolina

## Introduction

Esau Jenkins taught reading on a bus. In the 1940s he bought buses to carry his own children and others from the Sea Islands to school in Charleston, and during those rides he and his wife helped passengers learn the literacy skills they needed to register to vote. The Progressive Club on Johns Island carried that same work forward. Its clubhouse, built in 1963 on River Road, was part grocery store, part schoolhouse and part refuge, and it was designed around one purpose above all: a Citizenship School.

## A Co-op of Families

The Progressive Club began in 1948 as a co-op started by Jenkins and other families on Johns Island. The co-op gave residents a way to trade goods and services and to help one another in times of need, and it housed a community grocery store and space for recreation, sleeping and classes. When the clubhouse was finally built in 1963, it held the club's legal and financial assistance program, an adult education program and dormitory lodging. It also served as a recreation space, a child care center, a meeting place and a grocery store. Under one roof, a neighbor could buy groceries, leave a child in care, get help with a legal or financial problem and sit down for a class.

## Learning to Vote

At the heart of the building was the Citizenship School. Its adult education classes and workshops enabled African-American citizens to register, to vote and to understand the political processes of their own communities. The idea had deep roots on this island. At Septima Clark's invitation, Jenkins traveled to Highlander to meet Myles Horton, and the first citizenship school was established on Johns Island at the Progressive Club, opening in 1957 in a former schoolhouse the club had bought. That school became a model for similar programs throughout the South. The 1963 clubhouse, built to house a Citizenship School, gave the work a permanent home. The lessons Jenkins had begun on a moving bus now had walls around them and a place in the community's daily life, sharing a roof with the grocery counter and the child care rooms.

## Saving What Remains

Esau Jenkins died in 1972, and the building he helped bring into being has not fared as well as his legacy. Hurricane Hugo badly damaged the building in 1989, and the uninsured club could not afford to repair it. Photographs of the site show what remains today, alongside a sign about its restoration and a public appeal to save it, and talk of rebuilding has so far produced little visible progress. A historical marker now stands at the site to tell passersby what happened here. The Progressive Club was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2007, recognition that this modest clubhouse at 3377 River Road belongs to the larger story of the civil rights movement. What makes it matter is not architecture but use. Here a grocery store and a classroom shared a roof, and neighbors came to learn how to claim their full place in democracy.
