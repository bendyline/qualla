# Mosquito Beach Historic District

Barely a beach at all, this strip of creekside land on James Island became a weekend refuge for thousands of African Americans during the height of Jim Crow, and its Pine Tree Hotel is being rebuilt after a 2022 fire.

**Location:** 32.6742, -79.9819

**Categories:** Historic districts on the National Register of Historic Places in South Carolina, African-American history of South Carolina

## Introduction

It was never much of a beach. The land on James Island that people came to call Mosquito Beach was closer to a dirt road running along a creek than a stretch of natural sand. Yet during the height of Jim Crow, it was the weekend destination for thousands of African Americans, because it offered something the open beaches nearby did not: a place where they were welcome. Today the 8.36-acre site between two creeks is a historic district, and the effort to save what remains has already survived one fire.

## A Shoreline of Their Own

The land had belonged to a member of the Legare family. In the 1950s, African Americans began using it for recreation, turning the creekside strip into a gathering place when segregation shut them out of so much else. Four structures and their corresponding pavilions stood on the site, and together they became a safe refuge for the community. It is worth pausing on that phrase. A weekend by the water is an ordinary pleasure, but under Jim Crow even ordinary pleasures had to be found in places set apart, and the people who made Mosquito Beach their own built a social life on land that was rough, low and far from glamorous. That thousands of people made it their weekend destination is a measure of how much such a place mattered.

## When the Crowds Moved On

The same change that ended the need for a refuge also emptied it. After the Civil Rights Act of 1964, once nearby Folly Beach was desegregated, use of Mosquito Beach declined, as the people who had once been barred from the open shoreline could finally go there. Recognition came decades later. On September 23, 2019, Mosquito Beach was added to the National Register of Historic Places as a historic district, formally marking its place in the African-American history of South Carolina. The listing acknowledged what the site had been for the people who gathered there: not a destination chosen for its scenery, but one chosen because it was theirs.

## Fire at the Pine Tree Hotel

Among the structures is the Pine Tree Hotel, a 14-room lodging house built in the early 1960s, and in 2019 the Historic Charleston Foundation received a $490,861 grant from the National Park Service's African American Civil Rights grant program to restore and renovate it. The plan was ambitious. The building would be almost completely rebuilt and raised higher to protect it from the floods that affect the area. Then, on April 15, 2022, fire destroyed the structure. The loss could have been total, but timing intervened. Because the Historic Charleston Foundation was already in the middle of reconstructing the hotel, windows, roof trusses and other materials had been removed from the building before the fire. Those salvaged pieces are to be used in rebuilding the hotel, carrying something of the original Pine Tree into its next life. In 2025 the foundation announced a $1.25 million Mellon Foundation grant to help rehabilitate the hotel and the neighboring Skeeta Beach Lounge, and no reopening date had been given.

## Flight Context

Mosquito Beach lies at about 32.6742 N, 79.9819 W on James Island, south of Charleston, between two creeks. The site is small and low, so use broader landmarks: Folly Beach is about 2 nm to the south, and the Stono River lies about 3 nm to the southwest. A viewing altitude of 1,500 to 2,000 feet shows the creeks and the nearby coast well. Charleston Executive Airport (KJZI) is very close, about 2 nm north-northwest, so expect traffic in its pattern; Charleston International Airport (KCHS) is about 14 nm north-northwest. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common along this coast.
