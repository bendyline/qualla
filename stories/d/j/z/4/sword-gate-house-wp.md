# Sword Gate House

By local legend, a gatemaker's misunderstanding of the word "pair" gave Charleston's Sword Gate House its famous iron gates, and the house itself went on to be a girls' school, a guest house, a subdivided estate and, in 2020, a $10 million sale that set a South of Broad record.

**Location:** 32.7736, -79.9342

**Categories:** Houses in Charleston, South Carolina, Houses on the National Register of Historic Places in South Carolina, National Register of Historic Places in Charleston, South Carolina

## Introduction

The popular story turns on one word. When the City of Charleston hired the ironworker Christopher Werner, reportedly in 1838, to make a "pair" of gates for its new Guard House at Meeting and Broad Streets, home to the city police, the city meant one set, a left panel and a right. Werner understood a pair to mean two matching sets, and he made four panels, two lefts and two rights. The city did not need the extras, so the tale goes, Werner sold them to George Hopley, the British consul, who had them installed in the late 1840s in the high brick wall of his house on Legare Street. Some accounts dispute the tale and say the city balked at the price instead. Each half carries a central cross formed where the points of two vertical spears meet in the middle of a horizontal broadsword, and those swords gave the Sword Gate House its popular name. Sword-gate panels wrought by Werner are now part of the main entrance of The Citadel, near Hampton Park.

## A House Built in Stages

The Sword Gate House grew over time. Its main portion is believed to have been built around 1803, possibly by the French Huguenots James LaRoche and J. Lardent, though other accounts credit Solomon Legare, replacing a simpler house shown on a plat from that year. A brick wing is believed to have been added around 1818, and in 1819 the property was acquired by Andre Talvande and Madame Talvande, who used it as a school for girls. It was during their ownership that the high masonry walls around the property went up. Hopley bought and redecorated the house in 1849. The building is two and a half stories over a high basement, with a slate-covered hip roof, three pedimented dormers on the north and one on the west, stucco over brick walls, paneled shutters on the first-floor windows and louvered shutters above. Many additions and alterations followed in the late 19th century.

## Empty Rooms and Divided Lots

By the middle of the 20th century the house had fallen quiet, standing vacant for almost two decades before the Gaud family bought it in 1949. They repaired and furnished the buildings and ran a guest house there for a time. By 1959, to make ends meet, they chose to subdivide the property and sell two lots facing Legare Street. Neighbors strongly opposed the plan, but a city zoning board approved the subdivision in late 1959. In 1960 Mrs. H.C. Shackelford bought most of the property from Henry T. Gaud for $47,500, and in 1963 George E. Roosevelt bought the house from her and set about reversing the subdivisions. By 1970 the old brick wing had been separated from the house with its own address, 109 Tradd Street, though it kept its architectural integrity. On December 18, 1970, the Sword Gate House was listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

## Charleston's Priciest Address

In 2002 an extensive restoration brought the property back together, reassembling three lots that had been divided into a single parcel and converting the entire structure back into a single-family house. In April 2009 it went on the market at a far higher price, reportedly $23 million, and the asking price fell over time. When it finally sold in June 2020 for $10 million, according to county records, it set a record for a South of Broad residence, beating the $8.625 million paid the year before for 23 Legare Street. Only one residence on the peninsula had fetched more: a penthouse in the People's Office Building, which sold for $12 million in March 2020, though a penthouse is not a house. For a property whose name began with a misunderstood order, it was a remarkable price.

## Flight Context

The Sword Gate House sits on Legare Street near Tradd Street at 32.7736°N, 79.9342°W, in the lower Charleston peninsula a few blocks northwest of the Battery and White Point Garden. From 1,000–2,000 feet, individual houses are hard to pick out, so orient on the Battery seawall at the peninsula's tip and the steeple of St. Michael's Church to the northeast. Charleston Executive Airport (KJZI) is about 6 nm southwest, Charleston International Airport (KCHS) about 9 nm northwest, and Mount Pleasant Regional Airport (KLRO) about 11 nm northeast. Watch for KCHS traffic; summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common.
