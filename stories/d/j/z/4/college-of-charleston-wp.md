# College of Charleston

Founded in 1770 by men who went on to sign the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution, the College of Charleston is South Carolina's oldest university, a campus of century-old buildings whose history includes both founding-era ideals and the long exclusion of Black students.

**Location:** 32.7842, -79.9381

## Introduction

Thomas Heyward Jr., Arthur Middleton and Edward Rutledge signed the Declaration of Independence. Charles Pinckney, Charles Cotesworth Pinckney and John Rutledge signed the Constitution of the United States. All six were among the founders of the College of Charleston, a school that opened its story in 1770 and received its charter in 1785. It is the oldest university in South Carolina, the 13th-oldest institution of higher learning in the United States and the country's oldest municipal college. Today it is a public university spread through the streets of downtown Charleston, where the average campus building is more than a hundred years old.

## From Barracks to Randolph Hall

The college's first structure stood where Randolph Hall stands now, and it was designed much like a barracks. In March 1785 the South Carolina General Assembly granted a charter, but classes did not officially begin until 1790, and the first commencement followed in 1794. Robert Smith served as the first president from 1790 to 1797. A second charter in 1791 stipulated that the college would not discriminate on the basis of religion. In the decades before the Civil War the college grew, adding Randolph Hall, now its oldest building, and the President's House. In 1837 the Charleston municipal government assumed control of the college, beginning a long era as a city institution.

## The Doors That Stayed Shut

The handsome buildings carry a harder history. Randolph Hall and the President's House were both built by enslaved laborers, men and women whose work still stands at the heart of a campus that would long refuse to admit Black students. In the mid-20th century, several African Americans applied to the racially segregated college as part of the Double V campaign against racism in the United States, and every one of them was rejected. In 1949 the college went so far as to return to private status in order to avoid racial integration. The resistance did not hold. Under external pressure, the college began admitting Black students in 1967, nearly two centuries after its founding and 176 years after it had promised not to turn anyone away for their religion.

## Where the Average Building Is a Century Old

The downtown campus holds 156 buildings, a mix of modern and historic structures built between 1770 and 2015, and 20 of them are protected by historic easements. The campus is a National Historic Landmark (designated in 1971) and so is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, as is the William Blacklock House. Readers of Travel + Leisure voted it "America's Most Beautiful College Campus" in 2017, Country Living named it one of the most beautiful campuses in the South, and MSN named it the most beautiful campus in South Carolina in 2019. Tucked among the old buildings is the Mace Brown Museum of Natural History, a public museum focused on the paleontology of North American mammals, especially those of the South Carolina Lowcountry, with more than 30,000 vertebrate and invertebrate fossils. Beyond the peninsula the college also runs the Grice Marine Lab on James Island, a sailing center and athletic complex in Mount Pleasant, and the 881-acre Stono Preserve.

## Cougars, Candidates and Concerts

Campus life spills well beyond the classroom. The college is organized into eight academic schools plus an Honors College and a Graduate School, and its Bully Pulpit Series invites announced presidential candidates to speak. During the 2020 campaign alone, a dozen Democratic candidates visited, including Joe Biden, Bernie Sanders, Elizabeth Warren, Pete Buttigieg, Cory Booker and Amy Klobuchar. Each May and June, advanced students perform in the Piccolo Spoleto Young Artists Series, part of Charleston's Piccolo Spoleto Festival, in programs ranging from classical and jazz ensembles to voice recitals and a steel band. The college's 19 varsity teams compete in NCAA Division I's Coastal Athletic Association as the Cougars. They were once the Maroons, until students voted during the 1970–71 school year to honor a cougar that had recently arrived at the Animal Forest at nearby Charles Towne Landing. Clyde the Cougar carries on the tradition today.

## Flight Context

The College of Charleston's main campus lies in downtown Charleston around 32.7842°N, 79.9381°W, on the Charleston peninsula between the Ashley and Cooper Rivers. From 1,500–2,000 feet, look for the cluster of campus buildings roughly half a mile north of Broad Street and the steeple of St. Michael's Church. Charleston Executive Airport (KJZI) is about 6 nm south-southwest, Charleston International Airport (KCHS) about 9 nm northwest, and Mount Pleasant Regional Airport (KLRO) about 10 nm northeast. Traffic into KCHS is steady; plan around summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
