# Francis Marion Hotel

Opened in 1924 as the grandest hotel in the Carolinas, Charleston's Francis Marion Hotel sank through decades of debt and near-conversion before a mayor, a hurricane-battered city and a $12 million restoration brought it back to life on upper King Street.

**Location:** 32.7859, -79.9364

**Categories:** Hotels in Charleston, South Carolina, Skyscraper hotels in South Carolina, Hotel buildings completed in 1924, Historic Hotels of America, Buildings and structures in Charleston, South Carolina

## Introduction

For about a decade, one of the tallest buildings in Charleston had a general living at the top. When Mark W. Clark retired as president of The Citadel in 1965, he and his wife moved into the penthouse of the Francis Marion Hotel at 387 King Street, and Clark stayed on until about 1975. The hotel beneath them was already four decades old, built to be the grandest in the Carolinas, and harder times lay ahead of it: near-conversion, heavy debt, foreclosure and a hurricane. The Francis Marion came through all of it, and today it is a member of Historic Hotels of America, its story a lesson in how close a landmark can come to being lost.

## The Grandest Hotel in the Carolinas

The hotel began with a group of local investors who formed on March 13, 1920. Organized as the Marion Square Realty Co. and headed by former Charleston mayor T.T. Hyde, they commissioned plans from the noted New York architect William Lee Stoddart and spent $1.5 million on construction. They named the building for Francis Marion, the Revolutionary War hero. When it opened on February 7, 1924, the Francis Marion was the largest and grandest hotel in the Carolinas. In 1952 it added another first, becoming the first fully air-conditioned hotel in Charleston.

## Enter the Swamp Fox

The Jack Tar Hotels Corp. bought the hotel in 1954 and soon launched a major renovation in 1955 and 1956. Its signature addition was the Swamp Fox Room, a ground-floor restaurant named for Francis Marion's famous nickname, and during the same work the ground floor of the exterior was refaced in red granite. Ambitions outran results. In 1961 the hotel announced plans for a three-story parking deck on King Street, north of the building, drawn by Augustus Constantine, but the deck was never completed. From 1965 its most distinguished resident was General Clark, whose penthouse years would last about a decade.

## Years of Red Ink

By the mid-1970s the Francis Marion was in trouble. In 1975 its owners considered converting it into a home for the elderly, and in 1976, with the hotel doing poorly, they weighed selling out, at a time when the hotel held about a quarter of all the hotel rooms on the peninsula. A group of investors looked at buying it in 1977, but restoring it as a first-class destination would have cost about $3 million, a price judged too high. In 1984 the hotel was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as part of a boundary increase to the Charleston Historic District. That December, Hospitality Associates of Charleston bought it for $8.5 million and ran it first as a Ramada and then as a Radisson. The company had borrowed heavily from Prudential Insurance and from Dr. Julian Youmans, who had acquired the hotel in 1981, and from April 1986 it fell behind on its payments. In 1988 Prudential bought the hotel at a public sale for $6 million.

## A Mayor's Bet on Upper King

In 1989, shortly after Hurricane Hugo hit Charleston, a new group of investors bought the hotel for $3.2 million with a $12 million restoration plan, only to find that an economic downturn left them unable to finance it. Mayor Joseph P. Riley saw the building as a landmark that could spark the renovation of upper King Street, the stretch between Calhoun and Cannon Streets, and he lobbied local banks until they contributed $5.5 million; the city itself put up more than $4 million. In 1991 the city even considered buying the hotel for $3,420,000 and leasing it to the College of Charleston as student housing. In 1996 the Francis Marion reopened as a Clarion hotel after a meticulous $12 million restoration that won an award from the National Trust for Historic Preservation. A further $2 million renovation followed in 1997, and on June 18, 1998, it became The Westin Francis Marion. It left Westin on January 1, 2003, and has run independently since, with 235 guest rooms and suites.

## Flight Context

The Francis Marion Hotel stands at 387 King Street, 32.7859°N, 79.9364°W, on the upper King Street corridor of the Charleston peninsula. As one of the tallest buildings in Charleston, its tower rises above the low roofline of the historic district and is a useful reference from 1,500–2,500 feet. Charleston Executive Airport (KJZI) is about 6 nm south-southwest, Charleston International Airport (KCHS) about 9 nm northwest, and Mount Pleasant Regional Airport (KLRO) about 10 nm northeast. Expect steady traffic into KCHS and frequent summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
