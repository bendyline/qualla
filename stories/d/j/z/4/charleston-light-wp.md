# Charleston Light

Sullivan's Island's triangular steel-and-aluminum lighthouse, first lit in 1962 to replace the eroding Morris Island Light, is billed as the only U.S. lighthouse with an elevator and air conditioning, and once shone so brightly that it had to be turned down.

**Location:** 32.7579, -79.8432

**Categories:** Lighthouses in South Carolina, Buildings and structures in Charleston County, South Carolina, Lighthouses completed in 1962

## Introduction

The keepers of Charleston Light hardly had to climb. Inside the tower, an elevator makes a leisurely seventy-four-second trip up, with only a short ladder left for the final stretch to the lantern room, and the tower is air-conditioned, which park guides say makes it the only lighthouse in the United States with both an elevator and air conditioning. It is a fitting distinction for a tower that looks like no traditional lighthouse at all. Rising from Sullivan's Island at the northern entrance to Charleston Harbor, it is a slim, triangular shaft of steel and aluminum, built in the early 1960s and recorded as the last major lighthouse built by the federal government.

## A Replacement Across the Harbor

The new light existed because an old one was losing its ground. Across the harbor entrance, erosion threatened the Morris Island Light, and the decision was made to build a replacement beacon on Sullivan's Island. The old tower was not lost after all: it still stands and was stabilized in 2010, though it no longer guides ships. Designed by Coast Guard architect Jack Graham, the new lighthouse was begun in 1960 and first lit on June 15, 1962. Its materials were thoroughly modern. A steel frame with an aluminum alloy skin rises from a concrete foundation in a triangular cross section, engineered to withstand winds of 125 miles per hour. The National Park Service gives the tower's height as 162.5 feet (Wikipedia lists 140 feet for the tower and 163 feet for the light's focal plane above mean sea level).

## Too Bright for Its Neighbors

When it switched on in 1962, Charleston Light burned with an astonishing twenty-eight million candlepower. The beam proved dangerous to the keepers who tended it and bothersome to the people living nearby, and some years later (sources say five to ten) it was downgraded to a light of just over a million candlepower. The tower's first coat of paint was no more popular. Originally it wore white and red-orange, a color scheme so disliked that its daymark was soon changed to the scheme it wears today, black on the upper half and white below. Even toned down, the light remains powerful. It flashes white twice every 30 seconds, two brief flashes separated by a pause of just under five seconds, and it is visible for 26 nautical miles.

## From Keepers to Custodians

The keepers who enjoyed the elevator and the air conditioning did not stay forever. The light was automated in the mid-1970s, ending its years as a manned station. On May 29, 2008, the lighthouse was transferred from the Coast Guard to the National Park Service. Today the responsibilities are shared: the Coast Guard maintains the light itself, while the Park Service looks after the structure's historic preservation. The tower remains closed to the public, with no plans to reopen it because of elevator problems (the last tours were in 2018), though visitors can view it from the surrounding grounds. For those standing at its base, the view up is of a modern monolith of metal and paint, still flashing its signal over the harbor it has watched since 1962.

## Flight Context

Charleston Light stands at about 32.7579 N, 79.8433 W on Sullivan's Island, on the northern side of the entrance to Charleston Harbor. Look for the slim triangular tower, black above and white below. Fort Moultrie is less than a mile to the west, Fort Sumter sits in the harbor mouth about 1.6 nm west-southwest, and the older Morris Island Light stands about 4 nm south-southwest across the entrance, making for a two-lighthouse view from 1,500 to 2,500 feet. Mount Pleasant Regional Airport (KLRO) is about 9 nm north-northeast, Charleston Executive Airport (KJZI) about 9 nm west-southwest and Charleston International Airport (KCHS) about 13 nm northwest. Expect summer haze, sea breezes and afternoon thunderstorms.
