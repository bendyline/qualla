# The Battery (Charleston)

At the tip of the Charleston peninsula, where the Ashley and Cooper Rivers meet, the Battery seawall and promenade has shifted from fortress to public park to a line of grand homes, and now to the city's front line against the sea.

**Location:** 32.7692, -79.9288

**Categories:** Parks in Charleston, South Carolina, Waterfronts, South Carolina in the American Civil War, Artillery battery fortifications in the United States

## Introduction

Long before it had a seawall, the tip of the Charleston peninsula was white. Piles of bleached oyster shells gathered on the point, and people called it White Point, or Oyster Point, for the pale mounds along the shore. Today the same ground is the Battery, a landmark defensive seawall and promenade stretching along the lower shores of the peninsula, where the Ashley and Cooper Rivers meet to form Charleston Harbor. It takes its name from the coastal defense artillery once mounted here, and for more than two centuries the point has been fortified, landscaped, admired and tested by the water it faces.

## A Point Built for Guns

The point was fortified early. Fort Broughton, later called Fort Wilkins, stood here from the 1730s (accounts give about 1735 or 1737), and the point stayed fortified through the American Revolution. In the 18th century, rocks and heavy materials were piled to fortify the shore of the Cooper River along the eastern side of the peninsula. During the Siege of Charleston, British forces had to contend with Fort Johnson, Broughton's Battery, Dorrell's Fort, Wilkins' Fort and Gibbs' Fort, which stood at the foot of King Street. Colleton Bastion was located near the intersection of Water and Meeting Streets. Around 1795, during the Quasi-War, Fort Mechanic stood where the Edmondston-Alston House stands today. The area now known as White Point Garden was first used as a public park in 1837, and a year later the stretch along the seawall became a promenade. War returned the garden to its old purpose: during the Civil War it became a place for artillery, occupied by Battery Ramsay.

## Mansions Along the Promenade

To most visitors, the Battery means houses. Its stately, mainly antebellum homes are among the most famous in Charleston, lined up along East Battery and South Battery. Along East Battery stand the Louis DeSaussure House at number 1, the Roper House at 9, the William Ravenel House at 13, the Edmondston-Alston House at 21, the Charles Drayton House at 25 and the George Chisolm House at 39. South Battery holds the Villa Margherita at 4, the William Washington House at 8, the Col. John A.S. Ashe House at 26, the James Spear House at 30 and the Col. John Ashe House at 32. The higher part of the promenade, running beside East Battery to Murray Boulevard, is known as High Battery. Historically, the Battery has been understood to run from the start of the seawall at the site of the former Omar Shrine Temple, at 40–44 East Bay Street, to the intersection of Murray Boulevard and King Street.

## A View of Forts and Ships

Stand on High Battery and the harbor's history spreads out before you. Fort Sumter is visible from the Cooper River side and from the point, and so are Castle Pinckney, Fort Moultrie, Sullivan's Island and the World War II aircraft carrier USS Yorktown (CV-10). The promenade also carries a small naming quarrel. Guidebooks, websites and everyday speech often call the Battery and White Point Garden "Battery Park," but the City of Charleston does not regard the park and the seawall promenade as a single entity, and "Battery Park" is not an official name.

## Where the Water Pushes Back

A wall built against enemies now faces a different threat. In 2004 a structural report by the City of Charleston found that the Battery was suffering serious problems and could fail to protect the southeastern portion of the city during hurricanes. In 2012 the City announced a $3.2 million restoration project at the juncture of High Battery, along East Battery, and Low Battery, along Murray Boulevard. The sea tested the wall again on August 30, 2023, when a surge from Tropical Storm Idalia, on top of a king tide that crested near 9.2 feet, poured over the lower Battery wall into White Point Garden and flooded portions of downtown. A much larger federal flood-defense project, the Battery Extension, is still in design: in September 2026 the Army Corps of Engineers awarded design contracts, not construction. The old oyster point, once shaped by shells and guns, is now defined as much by the storms it must hold back.

## Flight Context

The Battery wraps the southern tip of the Charleston peninsula around 32.7692°N, 79.9288°W, where the Ashley River (west) and Cooper River (east) meet to form Charleston Harbor. It is one of the easiest landmarks in the area to spot: from 1,000–2,500 feet, look for the seawall curving around the peninsula's point, the green of White Point Garden, and the row of large houses facing the water. Fort Sumter sits out in the harbor. Charleston Executive Airport (KJZI) is about 6 nm southwest, Charleston International Airport (KCHS) about 10 nm northwest, and Mount Pleasant Regional Airport (KLRO) about 11 nm northeast across the harbor. Watch for KCHS traffic; summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common.
