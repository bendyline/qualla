# Hell Hole Swamp

A wilderness swamp in the Francis Marion National Forest whose unforgettable name is older than every legend told to explain it, Hell Hole Swamp lent its name to a festival, a road race and a proud circle of South Carolina politicians.

**Location:** 33.2278, -79.7033

**Categories:** Protected areas of Berkeley County, South Carolina, IUCN Category Ib, Swamps of South Carolina, Wilderness areas of South Carolina, Landforms of Berkeley County, South Carolina

## Introduction

"I'm from Gumville. It's near Hell Hole Swamp. If you don't know where it is, look it up." That was how L. Mendel Rivers, the longtime congressman from South Carolina's 1st District, liked to answer reporters who asked where he was born. He was proud of the remoteness, and prouder still of the name. Hell Hole Swamp is a real place, and its heart is the Hell Hole Bay Wilderness, about 2,000 acres of designated wilderness tucked within the Francis Marion National Forest in northeastern Berkeley County. Its name has inspired colorful stories about British officers and Prohibition bootleggers. The surprise is that the name is older than all of them.

## Older Than Its Legends

Ask around and you may hear that Colonel Banastre Tarleton named the swamp during the Revolutionary War, supposedly because the British had so much trouble finding the elusive General Francis Marion. Others say it earned the name during Prohibition, when bootleggers used it as a hiding place. Both stories are appealing, but neither can explain the name. It appears on a 1757 map of the South Carolina and Georgia coastline, and it turns up again on James Cook's 1773 map of South Carolina, well before either Tarleton or Prohibition could have had a hand in it. On March 3, 1761, a man named James Colladon received a grant of 500 acres on Hell Hole Swamp, another sign that the name was already in use. The Forest Service suggests the name may come from a large opening in the forest, possibly cleared by wildfire long before the 1700s, but where it truly came from remains unknown.

## Wilderness in the Swamp Fox's Forest

The wilderness at the heart of Hell Hole Swamp lies inside the Francis Marion National Forest, named for the same Revolutionary War hero the British knew as the Swamp Fox. There is a certain poetry in that. The legend that falsely credited Tarleton with naming the swamp was built around Marion's skill at disappearing into country like this, and today the swamp and the forest both carry reminders of him. The Hell Hole Bay Wilderness, designated by Congress in 1980, covers about 2,000 acres (the Forest Service lists 2,125 acres, Wilderness.net 1,980), set aside within a forest that honors a man remembered for his elusiveness. The name may sound forbidding, but the place itself is a protected piece of the Lowcountry's wild interior, far quieter than its reputation.

## The Hell Hole Swamp Gang

Several prominent South Carolina politicians claimed the swamp as a badge of honor. A group of native sons of Berkeley County boasted of membership in what they called the Hell Hole Swamp Gang. Its roster was remarkable for such a rural corner of the state: Representative L. Mendel Rivers of the 1st Congressional District, Governor Robert Evander McNair, state Senator Rembert Dennis and Columbia mayor Lester Bates. Rivers was the most enthusiastic of them. Born in Gumville, a small community about two miles west of Jamestown on U.S. Route 17 Alternate, he relished telling reporters exactly where he came from and daring them to find it on a map. For these men, a name that might embarrass others became a mark of authenticity.

## Gator Trot and Festival Days

Each year in early May, the town of Jamestown, on the northern edge of the swamp, hosts the Hell Hole Swamp Festival, listed as May 1-2 for 2026. It is a small-town Southern celebration with a spitting contest, arm wrestling, beauty contests, a parade and plenty more. One event stands apart. The Hell Hole Gator Trot 10K, known locally as the Redneck Run, began in 1976 and is billed as the Lowcountry's oldest 10K; its organizers count 2026 as the 50th running, and this year it is scheduled for October 17. Runners who line up for it are keeping alive a tradition that is now half a century old, all in the name of a swamp whose own name is older than the nation. It is a fitting kind of fame for a place that has always been better known for what it is called than for what it is.

## Flight Context

Hell Hole Swamp is centered near 33.2278°N, 79.7033°W in northeastern Berkeley County, inside the Francis Marion National Forest. From 2,000–3,000 feet, look for unbroken forest and wetland; the small town of Jamestown sits about 3.5 nm north at the swamp's northern edge, and Gumville about 3.4 nm north-northwest along U.S. Route 17 Alternate. Few other landmarks break the forest canopy, so cross-check with GPS. Nearby airports: Robert F. Swinnie Airport at Andrews (KPHH) about 16 nm north-northeast, Berkeley County Airport at Moncks Corner (KMKS) about 17 nm west, and Georgetown County Airport (KGGE) about 20 nm east-northeast. The area is a designated wilderness; fly high and quietly overhead. Expect humid haze, morning fog over the wetlands and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
