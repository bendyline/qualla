# Pawleys Island, South Carolina

A three-mile barrier island at the southern end of the Grand Strand, Pawleys Island began as a summer refuge for rice planters and remains one of the oldest resort areas on the East Coast, a tiny town that bans commerce and keeps a ghost on watch.

**Location:** 33.4258, -79.1250

**Categories:** Towns in Georgetown County, South Carolina, Towns in South Carolina, Populated coastal places in South Carolina

## Introduction

Pawleys Island has a ghost with a job. The Gray Man, a famous purported ghost local to the island, is said to have walked the coastline for nearly 200 years, and his presence is said to warn of hurricanes and other dangers. It is a fitting legend for a place that lives so close to the sea. Pawleys Island is both a barrier island and the small town that occupies it, in Georgetown County at the southern end of the Grand Strand. The 2020 census counted just 130 residents. Yet this sliver of sand is one of the oldest resort areas on the East Coast of the United States, and its history runs far deeper than its population suggests.

## Shell Mounds and Summer Refuges

The earliest known inhabitants of the area were the Waccamaw and Winyah peoples. The Waccamaw lived in communities along the Waccamaw River, in a territory stretching from Lake Waccamaw in North Carolina to Winyah Bay at Georgetown, and the shell middens they left behind show that clams and oysters were popular foods. European colonization devastated both peoples, many of whom were captured and enslaved or died of introduced diseases. The Winyah had disappeared as a distinct people by 1720, and today the Waccamaw Indian People, a state-recognized tribe based in Conway, claim descent from the historic Waccamaw, a claim that researchers and the Waccamaw Siouan of North Carolina dispute. The island itself takes its name from George Pawley, who inherited it through a land grant to his father. He sold parcels to wealthy rice planters, whose fortunes rested on the labor of enslaved people, who wanted a coastal summer refuge from the mosquitoes that carried malaria. In 1791 President George Washington toured the Grand Strand along the King's Highway, stopping at an unincorporated part of Pawleys Island to visit the Alstons, who owned several plantations in the area.

## An Island That Said No

What sets Pawleys apart today is what it lacks. The island bans commercial and industrial buildings, with only a few exceptions: a condominium complex from the 1970s and a couple of grandfathered inns, the SeaView Inn and the PCJ Weston House, now the Pelican Inn. The rest is cottages and beach. The sea has tested that character. When Hurricane Hugo struck in 1989, some island cottages were swept away, and they have since been replaced. In September 2022 Hurricane Ian's storm surge washed away the island's dunes, though a beach renourishment two years earlier kept the damage to buildings far smaller. The town government was incorporated only in the mid-1980s, and it governs the island alone; the nearby commercial strip along U.S. Route 17 and the neighborhoods stretching toward the Waccamaw River share the Pawleys Island postal address but lie outside the town. All Saints Church, the Cedar Grove Plantation Chapel and the Pawleys Island Historic District are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

## Three Miles of Sand

The island is a little over three miles long, a sandy barrier lying off the Waccamaw Neck, the long, narrow peninsula between the Atlantic and the Waccamaw River. Two bridges, the North Causeway and the South Causeway, tie it to the mainland. Dunes rise to about 15 feet at the northern end, while the southern end lies very low, and behind the island runs a tidal creek and marsh. The town covers about one square mile, nearly a third of it water. From May to October the ocean is comfortably warm, and for most of the year the island offers fishing, crabbing, shrimping and birdwatching. Elizabeth Allston Pringle, the plantation owner and writer who lived from 1845 to 1921, was born at her family's summer home near the island.

## Flight Context

Pawleys Island lies at about 33.4258°N, 79.1250°W, a thin barrier island along the Atlantic coast at the southern end of the Grand Strand, roughly 10 miles from Georgetown. From 1,500–2,500 feet, look for the narrow strip of beach and cottages separated from the mainland by tidal marsh, with the two causeways crossing to U.S. Route 17 and the Waccamaw River beyond. Georgetown County Airport (KGGE) is about 12 nm southwest, and Myrtle Beach International Airport (KMYR) about 18 nm north-northeast. Expect sea breezes, coastal haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
