# East Lake Golf Club

Billed as Atlanta's oldest golf course, Bobby Jones's home club, slid into decline alongside its neighborhood before being reborn as "Golf with a Purpose," sending its profits back into the community and hosting the PGA Tour's season finale.

**Location:** 33.7430, -84.3030

**Categories:** Golf clubs and courses in Georgia (U.S. state), Golf clubs and courses designed by Donald Ross, Ryder Cup venues, Sports venues in Atlanta, Bobby Jones, Community development

## Introduction

Charlie Yates grew up on Second Avenue, the street that separated East Lake's main course from its No. 2 course. His boyhood hero was Bobby Jones, and at East Lake he did more than admire him from a distance: he knew Jones and played with him on these fairways. In 1938 Yates won the British Amateur at Royal Troon. Sixty years later, the old No. 2 course across Second Avenue was transformed into a nine-hole public course bearing his name, built to teach golf to Atlanta's children. The road between those two moments runs through triumph, decline and a remarkable second act at what is billed as the oldest golf course in Atlanta.

## Heisman's Club, Bendelow's Holes

The Atlanta Athletic Club was formed in 1898 and proved so popular that it gained 700 members in only four years. The director of its athletic program was John Heisman, the football coach for whom the Heisman Trophy is named. In 1904 the club bought property at East Lake, five miles east of downtown, for a country club with a golf course, and asked Tom Bendelow to lay it out. The first holes opened in 1906, just seven at first, then nine. By the summer of 1907 there were 17, and later that year the 18th was built. That same year East Lake hosted its first significant tournament, the Southern Amateur, won by Nelson Whitney. Bendelow opened a second course, known as No. 2, in 1908. In 1913 the celebrated architect Donald Ross redesigned the main course so that each nine finished at the clubhouse, and in 1928 he reworked No. 2 as well.

## Bobby Jones's Home Course

Bobby Jones is said to have played his first and last rounds of golf at East Lake. He won the Southern Amateur here in 1922 and the Southern Open in 1927, and in 1930 he won golf's Grand Slam, taking the U.S. Amateur, U.S. Open, British Amateur and British Open in a single year. His father, "Colonel" Robert P. Jones, served as the club's president from 1937 to 1942 and as a director for 38 years, and Bobby himself was president in 1946 and 1947. Jones had company. Amateurs Watts Gunn, Perry Adair, Charlie Yates and Alexa Stirling Fraser all played here around the same time, many of them helped along by East Lake's golf professional, Stewart Maiden. When a fire destroyed the original clubhouse in 1925, the club hired architect Philip Shutze, known for Buckhead's Swan House, to build the two-story Tudor-style clubhouse that still stands. Much of it now serves as a tribute to Jones.

## Palmer's Ryder Cup and the Long Slide

In 1963 East Lake hosted the 15th biennial Ryder Cup, where Arnold Palmer served as playing captain of the winning U.S. team. It was a high point just before a long fall. In the 1960s the surrounding neighborhood deteriorated and fell victim to suburban flight. The Atlanta Athletic Club sold the No. 2 course to developers and moved to its current home in Johns Creek. A group of 25 members, led by Atlanta businessman Paul Grigsby, saved the original course and clubhouse by buying them and creating East Lake Country Club in 1968. Two years later the East Lake Meadows public housing project was built on the site of the No. 2 course, and it became a center of poverty, drugs and violence for the families who lived there. Middle-income homeowners left the surrounding neighborhood and low-income renters replaced them. By the 1980s East Lake was a mostly forgotten golf course in a neighborhood that seemed, to many, beyond hope.

## Golf with a Purpose

Everything changed in 1993, when a local charitable foundation headed by Tom Cousins bought East Lake, intending to restore it as a tribute to Bobby Jones and the club's other great amateurs. The East Lake Foundation was created alongside it and used the renovation as a catalyst for revitalizing the surrounding community. In 1994 Rees Jones, son of architect Robert Trent Jones, restored Donald Ross's original design to the layout played today. The club's model is simple and unusual: all of its profits go to the East Lake Foundation, which supports the health, education, safety and productivity of the neighborhood. Hence the motto, "Golf with a Purpose." In 1998 the No. 2 course became the Charlie Yates Golf Course, a nine-hole executive public course whose profits also go to the foundation. The model spread. In 2009 a new organization, Purpose Built Communities, was founded to help fight concentrated poverty in other communities, and it now supports more than 60 local community organizations across the country.

## Where the Season Ends

The Tour Championship came to East Lake for the first time in 1998, and the club has been its permanent home since 2004 (2005 by one account), hosting the finale of the PGA Tour Playoffs for the FedEx Cup. That arrangement is ending: in June 2026 the PGA Tour announced that the Tour Championship will rotate among courses beginning in 2028, with East Lake still under consideration for the rotation. The tournament has also been one of the foundation's largest supporters. On September 15, 2007, Zach Johnson shot a 60, ten under par, to set the course record at the Tour Championship. The club also hosts the East Lake Cup, a collegiate match-play championship launched in 2015. In 2008 the clubhouse grew by a 20,000-square-foot addition designed to match the 1926 building. The maker of the original B. Mifflin Hood roof tiles had gone out of business in the 1940s, so Ludowici recreated them. Construction began that February and was finished in time for the Tour Championship in September.

## Flight Context

East Lake Golf Club lies at approximately 33.743°N, 84.303°W, about five miles east of downtown Atlanta, Georgia. From 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, look for the open green of the 18-hole course amid dense residential neighborhoods, with the downtown skyline to the west. The area lies beneath Atlanta's busy Class B airspace, so plan altitudes and communications carefully. DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) is about 8 nautical miles to the north, Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (KATL) about 9 nautical miles to the southwest, and Fulton County Airport-Brown Field (KFTY) about 11 nautical miles to the west-northwest. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms to the Atlanta area.
