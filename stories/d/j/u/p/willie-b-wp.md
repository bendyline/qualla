# Willie B.

A western lowland gorilla who spent 27 years alone behind glass with a television and a tire swing, Willie B. finally stepped outside in 1988 before 25,000 onlookers and became the father of a family and Atlanta's most beloved ape.

**Location:** 33.7319, -84.3706

**Categories:** Individual gorillas, Individual primates in the United States, Tourist attractions in Atlanta, Zoo Atlanta, 2000 animal deaths

## Introduction

In 1979, just before that year's NFL playoffs, someone stole Willie B.'s television. For a western lowland gorilla at the Atlanta zoo, it was a real loss, because the television and a tire swing were nearly all he had. For much of his life he lived alone in a glass-enclosed cage, and he spent 27 years in isolation before anyone let him meet another gorilla. His story would become the story of how a city learned to see its zoo differently.

## Named for the Mayor

Willie B. was born around 1958 in the western lowlands of Africa. In 1960 an animal dealer from Kansas City, Dr. Deets Pickett, captured him in Cameroon and later sold him to the Atlanta zoo, which wanted to replace its original gorilla at the request of Mayor William Berry Hartsfield. The young gorilla, only about three years old, arrived in 1961 and was named for the mayor himself. There he stayed, behind glass, for decades. Visitors watched him in his solitary cage while he watched his television, a routine that stretched through the 1960s and 1970s with no other gorillas for company.

## A City Pushes for Change

The theft of the television drew media attention, and pressure mounted as Atlanta residents called for the zoo to be reformed. Mayor Andrew Young, the civil rights activist, worked to improve the zoo's facilities. Part of that effort was a change in leadership: zoo director Steve Dobbs was replaced by Terry L. Maple, then a professor at the Georgia Institute of Technology. The payoff came on May 13, 1988, when Willie B. stepped into a new outdoor habitat for the first time. Nearly 25,000 people gathered to watch, an attendance the zoo had never seen before. After 27 years alone, he was finally outdoors.

## Learning to Be a Gorilla

Decades of solitude meant Willie B. had to be taught how to live among other gorillas. The effort was judged a success when Kinyani, a smaller young female, began sitting very close to him and staring into his eyes, and the pair soon mated, though the zoo said she was too young to become pregnant. Maple called it a significant milestone for a gorilla who was now social, and said the experiment had exceeded expectations. Willie B. embraced the role of silverback and leader of a troop, and he went on to father five offspring at the zoo: Kudzoo, Olympia, Sukari, Willie B. Jr. (formerly Kidogo) and Lulu. His newfound sociability helped earn him his status as "Atlanta's iconic ape."

## A Silverback in Bronze

In January 2000, Willie B. came down with influenza and pneumonia at the same time, which placed immense strain on a heart already weakened by cardiomyopathy. He died on February 2, 2000, after 39 years at the zoo, one of its oldest animals. More than 5,000 people attended his memorial ceremony, and Andrew Young delivered a eulogy. Shortly afterward the zoo commissioned the artist Ed Bogucki to create a life-sized bronze statue, which now stands outside the gorilla exhibit. Willie B.'s body was cremated. By Wikipedia's account, eighty percent of his remains rest inside the statue, and the other twenty percent were flown back to Africa, where his life began.

## Flight Context

Willie B.'s statue stands at Zoo Atlanta, 33.7319°N, 84.3706°W, southeast of downtown Atlanta. From 2,500–3,500 feet, the zoo's leafy park setting and the downtown skyline to the northwest are the best references. This is busy airspace: Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL), named in part for the same mayor Willie B. was named after, is about 7 miles south-southwest. Fulton County Airport–Brown Field (KFTY) is about 9 miles west-northwest and DeKalb-Peachtree (KPDK) about 11 miles north-northeast. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common.
