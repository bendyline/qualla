# Monastery of the Holy Spirit

Outside Conyers, Georgia, a community of Trappist monks who once lived in a barn keeps a day of prayer that begins at 4 a.m., paid for by stained glass, fruitcake and fudge.

**Location:** 33.5865, -84.0660

**Categories:** Trappist monasteries in the United States, Catholic Church in Georgia (U.S. state), Christian organizations established in 1944, Buildings and structures in Rockdale County, Georgia, Tourist attractions in Rockdale County, Georgia

## Introduction

At 4 a.m., long before the roads around Conyers fill with commuters, the monks of the Monastery of the Holy Spirit are already at prayer. Vigils come first, followed by half an hour of silent contemplation, and by the time most of Georgia wakes they have broken, returned for Mass at 7, and begun a day of work that will be interrupted again and again by the call to the church. This is a Trappist monastery, officially the Monastery of Our Lady of the Holy Spirit, and its own website describes a community of 28 monks, one count among several (Wikipedia gave 22 as of 2024), who sustain themselves through businesses on site. Its grounds belong to the Arabia Mountain National Heritage Area and mark the southernmost point of the Arabia Mountain Path, so a trail that winds through the heritage area reaches its southern end at a cloister.

## Monks in a Barn

The community began on March 21, 1944, when about 20 monks (21 by the monastery's own count) arrived from the Abbey of Gethsemani in Kentucky. The land, about 1,400 acres of the old Honey Creek Plantation that once belonged to the silent film star Colleen Moore, was by Wikipedia's account a gift from her and the Catholic Church in Georgia. There was no monastery waiting for them. The first monks lived in a barn while they built, with their own hands, a temporary home that became known as the "pine board" monastery. They lived in it from 1944 to 1959, working all the while on the permanent monastery that stands today, a concrete structure complete with a retreat house and a cloister. The pine board building found a second life housing the monks' carpentry and stained glass businesses until a fire destroyed it in 2005. As for the barn, it is now part of the Monastic Heritage Center, a welcome center that opened on May 6, 2011, and tells the story of the monastery and its work.

## A Day Measured in Prayer

Prayer is the monks' primary work, and it shapes every hour. After vigils and morning prayer and Mass at 7, they pause again at midday at 12:15, for vespers at 5:20 in the evening, and for compline, the night prayer, at 7:30. After 8 p.m. comes the grand silence, which binds monks and guests alike. Visitors staying at the retreat house are encouraged to join the monks in the church. At least half of the community are priests, and they take turns hearing confession at the retreat house. Monks also offer spiritual direction, and they welcome people of every faith, including those who hold no faith at all. The abbot, Augustine Myslinski, received his abbatial blessing on August 15, 2016, the solemnity of the Assumption, and was re-elected in 2022. He follows a line that includes Augustine Moore, Armand Veilleux, Bernard Johnson, Basil Pennington and Francis Michael Stiteler.

## Fruitcake, Fudge and Stained Glass

The monks follow the Rule of St. Benedict, and in particular its precept that a monastery should sustain itself. That means running businesses. They produce stained glass and sell it through an online store, and they make fruitcakes and fudge. They also operate a green cemetery on the monastery property, a quiet echo of the monks' own graveyard that lies behind the church. The retreat house is part of that self-sufficiency. Dormitory-style and adjoining the church, it gives men and women separate floors and hosts individuals and groups on retreats scheduled almost year-round, with classes on religious topics for those who want them.

## Beyond the Walls

The monastery's reach extends past its own community. A small group of lay people associated with Holy Spirit, led by Fr. Anthony Delisi, began what became the Lay Associate movement, forming prayerful communities grounded in Cistercian spirituality. Their members make annual retreats to the monastery, which is now the motherhouse to five groups of Lay Cistercians, part of a worldwide network of lay people affiliated with monasteries near where their groups gather. Around the church, the grounds offer their own invitation: cloister gardens with a statue of Jesus beside the walkway, a lake, a sweeping front lawn, and a trail bridge nearby on the Rockdale River Trail.

## Flight Context

The monastery sits at 33.5865 N, 84.0660 W, south of Conyers in Rockdale County, about 18 nm east of Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (KATL). Look for the church and cloister set among open lawns, gardens and a lake within the Arabia Mountain National Heritage Area. A pass at around 2,500 to 3,000 feet MSL shows the grounds well, but this area lies close to Atlanta's busy Class B airspace, so check your sectional and stay clear of arrival and departure flows. Covington Municipal Airport (KCVC) is about 11 nm to the east-northeast and DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) about 21 nm to the northwest. Summer haze over metro Atlanta can cut visibility; spring and fall mornings are usually clearer.
