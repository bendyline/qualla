# Nu-Way Weiners

Opened by a Greek immigrant in 1916, the same year as Nathan's Famous, Macon's Nu-Way still serves its trademark red hot dogs under a name that has been cheerfully misspelled for more than a century.

**Location:** 32.8368, -83.6306

**Categories:** Hot dog restaurants in the United States, Restaurants in Georgia (U.S. state), Companies based in Macon, Georgia, Restaurants established in 1916, Regional restaurant chains in the United States

## Introduction

The name says "Weiners." The dictionary says "wieners." In Macon, Georgia, nobody has cared for more than a hundred years. Nu-Way Weiners has been serving its hot dogs, famous for their bright red color, since February 27, 1916, and the misspelling has become as much a part of the brand as the chili. One author even titled his history of the place There Is More than One Way to Spell WIENER.

## A Stand on Cotton Avenue

The story begins with James Mallis, a Greek American immigrant, who opened a hot dog stand on Cotton Avenue in downtown Macon in 1916. That was the same year Nathan's Famous opened its stand in New York City, which puts Nu-Way among the oldest hot dog restaurants in the United States. The business grew slowly and steadily. By 1962 there were three Nu-Way restaurants, and expansion, including franchising, brought the count to eight by 1972. As of late 2025 there were still eight in the Central Georgia area (five in Macon, two in Warner Robins and one in Fort Valley, by one count), run by third-generation owners James G. Cacavias and Spyros N. Dermatas. On the morning of March 13, 2015, fire destroyed the Cotton Avenue flagship, which Dermatas called "the original mother store." The family said it would rebuild in time for the 100th anniversary, and the company has operated continuously since, though a 2025 profile describes the original location as closed since the fire.

## Red Dogs and Mega-Burgers

The red hot dog is the signature, but the menu reaches well beyond it. Mornings bring egg platters, bacon and egg sandwiches, pancakes and spicy dog biscuits. Later in the day come the hamburgers Nu-Way is also known for: chili-burgers, cheeseburgers and the Mega-Burger, a deluxe quarter-pounder whose name, like Nu-Way itself, is a registered trademark. So is the slogan, a promise of loyalty delivered with a wink: "I'd Go a Long Way For a Nu-Way." Food critics have praised the restaurants for their authentic Southern cooking and their nostalgic atmosphere, qualities that are hard to fake in a business that has been open since the First World War era.

## One of Oprah's Favorite Things

Recognition has piled up over the decades. Nu-Way has appeared on the PBS special A Hot Dog Program, a tour of 20 locations across the country billed as featuring "some of the finest hot dog houses in the country." Praise has also come from Southern Living, Gourmet, the Travel Channel, The Atlanta Journal-Constitution, NBC's Today Show, CNN, Georgia Trend and Every Day with Rachael Ray, from food writer John T. Edge of the Southern Foodways Alliance in his book Southern Belly, and from the Southern humorist Lewis Grizzard. The brightest spotlight arrived in November 2007, when Oprah Winfrey visited while in Macon to tape the annual Oprah's Favorite Things episode of her show and said Nu-Way was one of her favorite things about the city. In 2012 the author Ed Grisamore gave the restaurant a detailed written history of its own.

## Flight Context

Nu-Way's original Cotton Avenue site is at 32.8368°N, 83.6306°W in downtown Macon, Georgia. From 1,500–2,500 feet, downtown Macon's street grid is the reference; the restaurant itself is too small to pick out from the air. Macon Downtown Airport (KMAC) is about 4 miles east-southeast, and Middle Georgia Regional Airport (KMCN) is about 10 miles south. Robins Air Force Base lies farther south, so check military airspace before heading that way. Summers are hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms.
