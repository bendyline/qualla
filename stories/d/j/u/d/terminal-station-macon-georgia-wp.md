# Terminal Station (Macon, Georgia)

Macon's limestone and pink-marble Terminal Station once handled some 100 trains a day, fell silent in 1971 when the last Nancy Hanks departed, and now serves the city again as its bus hub.

**Location:** 32.8336, -83.6240

**Categories:** Former railway stations in Georgia (U.S. state), Railway stations on the National Register of Historic Places in Georgia (U.S. state), Former Central of Georgia Railway stations, Repurposed railway stations in the United States, Railway stations in the United States opened in 1916

## Introduction

On April 30, 1971, the Nancy Hanks made its final run through Macon, Georgia, and with it ended 125 years of intercity passenger rail service in the city. The train pulled away from Terminal Station, a Beaux-Arts building of limestone and pink Tennessee marble at the end of Cherry Street that had once sent off and welcomed an estimated 100 trains a day. Then the station went quiet.

## A Lawyer Demands a Station

The station began with a legal filing. On September 28, 1912, Col. Robert L. Berner, a prominent Macon attorney and former state legislator, petitioned the Georgia Railroad Commission to require the railroads serving Macon to build an adequate union passenger station. His campaign succeeded, and Terminal Station was completed on 5th Street in 1916. It was designed by the architect Alfred T. Fellheimer, who had worked on Grand Central Terminal in New York City and later designed Cincinnati Union Terminal. Outside, the walls were limestone; inside, the main lobby and waiting areas were finished with floors and walls of pink Tennessee marble. Owned by the Macon Terminal Company, the complex spread across 13 acres.

## Trains with Names

By the mid-1920s the station was dispatching an estimated 100 arrivals and departures a day, and the trains that passed through carried names that read like a map of the eastern United States. The Central of Georgia's Nancy Hanks ran between Atlanta and Savannah. The Flamingo linked Cincinnati with Jacksonville, and the Southland ran from Chicago to St. Petersburg, Sarasota and Miami. The Kansas City–Florida Special came through from Kansas City by way of Memphis and Birmingham, and the Southern Railway's Florida Sunbeam connected Chicago, Detroit and Cleveland with Miami. The Ponce de Leon and the Royal Palm both ran between Cincinnati and Jacksonville, and in the early 1950s the Central also ran a Birmingham–Columbus–Savannah night train through Macon. One by one they disappeared. The Royal Palm made its last run from Macon in 1970, and the Nancy Hanks followed the next year.

## Second Lives

After the last trains left, the building closed (most sources say 1971, some 1975) and sat empty. In 1982 Georgia Power Company bought it and used it for offices until the 1990s. The City of Macon purchased the station in 2002 with federal transportation funds and completed a restoration in 2010, and since 2014 it has served as the hub of the Macon Transit Authority's buses, today the Macon-Bibb County Transit Authority, which keeps its headquarters in the building, so travelers once again come and go at the old station. The building is part of the Macon Historic District on the National Register of Historic Places. Scenes for the Amazon series The Underground Railroad were reportedly filmed there in 2020. A couple of miles away, Norfolk Southern's Brosnan Yard, opened in 1967 and named for William Brosnan, then president of the Southern Railway, remains a major classification yard handling about 10,000 railcars a week. Its hump operation was idled in 2020 as the railroad shifted to precision railroading, but Norfolk Southern began restoring hump operations in 2022.

## Flight Context

Terminal Station stands at 32.8336°N, 83.6240°W at the foot of Cherry Street in downtown Macon, Georgia. From 1,500–2,500 feet, look for the rail lines converging at the edge of downtown, with the big Brosnan Yard about two miles out. Macon Downtown Airport (KMAC) is about 4 miles east-southeast, and Middle Georgia Regional Airport (KMCN) is about 10 miles south. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common; clear fall and winter days offer the best visibility.
