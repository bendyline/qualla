# D'Lo, Mississippi

A Strong River town with a name that may have started as a curse, D'Lo boomed as a sawmill city, sent its men to World War II in such numbers that Life magazine came calling, and later lent its riverbank to Hollywood's sirens.

**Location:** 31.9867, -89.9011

**Categories:** Towns in Simpson County, Mississippi, Jackson metropolitan area, Mississippi, Sawmill towns, World War II home front, Filming locations

## Introduction

Ask how D'Lo, Mississippi, got its name and you may hear three different answers. One popular version holds that an early settler looked at this flood-prone stretch of the Strong River and called it "too damn low." When the time came for a post office, the story goes, postal authorities refused to approve the profanity, so someone suggested "D-Low," which over the years was shortened to D'Lo. It is a fittingly irreverent origin for a town of 373 people that has been, at different moments, a booming sawmill city, a symbol of wartime sacrifice and a backdrop for Hollywood sirens.

## A Name Written in Script

The other explanations are no less colorful. One says the name Millhaven was rejected by postal authorities, who purportedly sent the villagers a list of four-letter alternatives. They chose "Delo," and a penman wrote back in a script that may have set the little "e" above the "lo," where it was probably misread as an apostrophe. A third version traces the name to a French phrase. What the records show is plainer. A post office named "Dlo" was approved on June 17, 1881, with Mary Frances May, the wife of the town's founder, R. W. May, as the first postmaster, working out of the Mays' home. May registered a town plat on March 1, 1900. D'Lo was incorporated as a village in 1901 and raised to a town in 1905, and the post office's name changed from Dlo to D'Lo on January 1, 1950.

## The Million-Dollar Mill

In 1916 the Finkbine Lumber Company chose the town for a $1,000,000 sawmill plant, and everything changed. Finkbine built many houses for its workers, along with the Kew Mercantile Company, a single store that sold dry goods, hardware, groceries, farm supplies and seed. Between 1916 and 1930, by local accounts, the mill town of Millhaven was the largest town between Jackson and Hattiesburg, with a population estimated at around 5,000 and a claim to be the second-largest milling town in the United States. Those figures are unsourced, and the census counted only 646 people in D'Lo in 1920. Local lore credits the boom town with two large YMCA buildings, a movie theater, ten grocery stores, seven butcher's markets, five cafes, three pharmacies, a bank, lighted basketball courts, professional basketball and baseball teams and its own newspaper, The D'Lo Herald. Its three-story brick school held 550 students and 17 teachers, and the three-story Pine View Hospital was considered the best between Jackson and the Mississippi Gulf Coast for equipment and doctors.

## When the Pines Ran Out

The boom consumed its own foundation. By 1927, the Finkbine mill had cut almost all the virgin pine in the surrounding counties, and the D'Lo plant closed for good in August 1929. The town lost its jobs, and its population went into steep decline. Recovery took several years and came along a new road: the construction of U.S. Route 49 through town let lumber companies reach second-growth timber in the area, and a new hardwood sawmill reportedly supplied timber to firms building weapons and equipment for World War II. That mill kept the town going for many years, but it was never large enough to bring back the lost thousands. By the 1940s, D'Lo had around 400 residents, roughly where it has stayed ever since.

## The Men Have Gone Off to War

Small as it had become, D'Lo gave a remarkable share of itself to the war. Local accounts say that, of a population of about 400, around 150 men served in the armed forces during World War II, and about 46 volunteered even before the bombing of Pearl Harbor. The town became known for sending more men per capita than any other town of its size, with 38 percent of the men who lived there serving. Life magazine told the story in its July 6, 1942, edition under the headline "The Young Men of D'Lo Have Gone to Fight." The article began: "In many a Southern town today the streets are empty of young men." A monument at the community center is said to list the townspeople who served.

## Sirens on the Strong River

Today the Strong River draws people for gentler reasons. D'Lo Water Park sits on its banks, and it was there that part of the 2000 film O Brother, Where Art Thou? was shot, the stretch known as the "Siren Scene." The town's one-screen Lux Theatre reportedly closed in the 1950s, but D'Lo found the screen again in 2016, when it was featured on the UP reality series Small Town, Big Mayor. The Simpson County Historical Society, based in nearby Mendenhall, is also pictured with a building in D'Lo, a quiet guardian for the story of a place that has been both much bigger and much busier than it looks.

## Flight Context

Coordinates: 31.9867°N, 89.9011°W, in Simpson County, Mississippi, along the Strong River. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet, look for U.S. Route 49 passing through the small town and the bends of the Strong River beside it. Nearby airports: Jackson-Medgar Wiley Evers International (KJAN), about 21 nautical miles to the north-northwest, and Hawkins Field (KHKS), about 27 nautical miles to the northwest, both in the Jackson area, and Hattiesburg-Laurel Regional (KPIB), about 42 nautical miles to the southeast. Summers are hot and humid, with afternoon thunderstorms.
