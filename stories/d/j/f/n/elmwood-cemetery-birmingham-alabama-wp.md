# Elmwood Cemetery (Birmingham, Alabama)

Birmingham's sprawling Elmwood Cemetery, founded in 1900, holds some 130,000 burials, from Bear Bryant and Sun Ra to the inventor of the windshield wiper, along with faux-wood concrete sculptures and a history of segregation broken by a Vietnam soldier's family in 1970.

**Location:** 33.4886, -86.8461

## Introduction

Paul "Bear" Bryant, the University of Alabama's football coach, is buried here. So is Sun Ra, the jazz musician. Eddie Kendricks, a co-founder of The Temptations, lies in the same grounds, as does Mary Anderson, the inventor of the windshield wiper. Elmwood Cemetery in Birmingham, Alabama, holds about 130,000 burials across several hundred acres, and walking its roads is like paging through the city's century: its industrialists and governors, its athletes and musicians, its reformers and its civil rights figures, and also a long chapter in which the cemetery itself was closed to Black families.

## From Elm Leaf to Elmwood

A group of fraternal organizations founded the cemetery in 1900 as Elm Leaf Cemetery, northwest of Homewood, and it took the name Elmwood in 1906. It began with 40 acres and kept growing: 40 more in 1904, 80 in 1909, another 80 in 1910 and 43 in 1924, reaching 286 acres by 1928. Over those decades it gradually eclipsed the older Oak Hill Cemetery as one of Birmingham's most prominent burial places. Today the grounds are divided into about fifty large blocks of varying shapes and sizes, each holding up to several hundred lots, with as many as eight or ten burial plots to a lot. Among them are at least two sections set aside for newborns and infants, marked with brass plaques that read "BABYLAND," and four mausoleums.

## A Log That Isn't Wood

Some of Elmwood's most surprising features are not graves at all. In the late 1930s, the Mexican sculptor Dionicio Rodriguez created a series of large concrete sculptures for the cemetery. Among them are a bridge, a large mushroom and a fallen log "carved" into a bench, all shaped from concrete to look like something else entirely. Visitors who sit down to rest on what looks like a fallen log are, in fact, sitting on a piece of sculpture. The effect is gentle and a little playful, a touch of artistry in a landscape of stone.

## A Gate Forced Open

For its first seven decades, Elmwood was a whites-only cemetery. That changed in 1970, when the family of a Black soldier who had died in Vietnam went to federal court and won a lawsuit forcing the cemetery to allow their son to be buried there. A soldier who had died in his country's service could be buried there only after his family fought for it in court. Elmwood's later history carries other reminders of the civil rights era. In August 2007, the parents of Denise McNair, a victim of the 16th Street Baptist Church bombing, had her remains moved here from Shadow Lawn Memorial Park. The cemetery is also the resting place of Abraham Woods, a minister and civil rights activist, and of Milton L. Grafman, a former rabbi of Temple Emanuel and a civil rights figure.

## A Roll Call of Birmingham

The list of notable burials reads like a history of Alabama. There are governors, including B. B. Comer and Joseph Forney Johnston, senators such as Oscar Underwood, and congressmen such as Truman H. Aldrich, who served in 1896 and 1897. Industry is here in Henry T. DeBardeleben, a coal magnate, and Erskine Ramsay, an inventor, engineer and philanthropist. Rufus N. Rhodes, founder of the Birmingham News, lies here, and so does Patti Ruffner Jacobs, a social reformer. Henry Eugene "Red" Erwin Sr., a World War II Medal of Honor recipient, rests at Elmwood, along with Father James Coyle, the priest of St. Paul's church who was assassinated in 1921. Music runs deep, too: besides Sun Ra and Eddie Kendricks, the trumpeter and composer Erskine Hawkins is buried here. Baseball players and managers, football players and the Birmingham television celebrity Cliff Holman round out a gathering that no single era could have assembled.

## Finding Your Way In

Elmwood is roughly bounded by Martin Luther King Jr. Drive, Dennison Avenue Southwest, 14th Place Southwest and a set of railroad tracks. The main entrance sits directly across from 6th Avenue Southwest, with a secondary entrance on Martin Luther King Drive. Johns-Ridout's Mortuary, a sister funeral home on nearby Dennison Avenue established in 1962 by the Lackey family, handles arrangements. The cemetery has been part of the Dignity Memorial network since 1987 and is still very much a working place of burial. Wander its blocks with no particular destination and you will keep finding names you recognize, in a place that was once open only to some of the city it now remembers.

## Flight Context

Elmwood Cemetery lies at approximately 33.489 N, 86.846 W in southwestern Birmingham, Alabama, northwest of Homewood. From 2,000-3,000 ft MSL, look for a very large green expanse of lawns and trees bordered by railroad tracks, southwest of Birmingham's downtown towers. Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International Airport (KBHM) is only about 6.5 nm northeast, so the cemetery sits within its busy terminal airspace; contact approach before maneuvering here. Bessemer Airport (KEKY) is about 11 nm south-southwest, and Shelby County Airport (KEET) about 19 nm south. Summer afternoons bring thunderstorms, and haze can settle in the valley.
