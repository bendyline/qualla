# Legion Field

Birmingham's Old Gray Lady gave the Iron Bowl its home and its name, hosted Joe Namath, the Rolling Stones and Olympic soccer, and still carries the memory of the days when it called itself the Football Capital of the South.

**Location:** 33.5114, -86.8428

**Categories:** Stadiums in Birmingham, Alabama, Venues of the 1996 Summer Olympics, NCAA bowl game venues, Iron Bowl, Birmingham Stallions stadiums

## Introduction

The biggest crowd ever to fill the self-proclaimed "Football Capital of the South" did not come for football. In 1996, 83,810 spectators poured into Birmingham's Legion Field to watch the United States play Argentina in the opening soccer match of the Atlanta Summer Olympics, a record no Iron Bowl, bowl game or rock concert ever topped. It is a fitting quirk for a stadium that has hosted almost everything over the past century. Locals know it as the Old Gray Lady, or the Gray Lady on Graymont, and for decades many of the most important games in the state's football history were played here.

## A Memorial That Kept Growing

Construction began in 1926 on a 21,000-seat stadium costing $439,000, named as a war memorial for the recently founded American Legion, the veterans' organization. On November 19, 1927, its first crowd of 16,800 watched Howard College, now Samford University, beat Birmingham–Southern 9–0. The designer had envisioned a monumental horseshoe-shaped amphitheater, but the stadium grew in its own way. Capacity reached 25,000 in 1934 and 45,000 in 1948, when the bowl was enclosed. A 9,000-seat upper deck rose on the east side in 1961, and a new press box in 1965 helped push the count to 68,821. On October 4, 1969, Ole Miss and Alabama met here in the stadium's first nationally televised night game. Further expansions lifted capacity to 83,091 by 1991, with "Football Capital of the South" emblazoned across the upper deck facade.

## Named for the Mills

From 1948 through 1988, Alabama and Auburn closed each regular season here, and because Birmingham's economy ran on iron and steel, the game became the Iron Bowl. The teams alternated as nominal home side and split the tickets evenly, and from the late 1950s Auburn always took the west sideline and Alabama the east. Neutral ground was partly a necessity. Neither campus stadium was nearly big enough for the crowds, even in the 1950s, and Birmingham was far easier to reach from the rest of the state. By the 1980s, though, Jordan-Hare Stadium seated more than 80,000, and Auburn fans pushed to bring the game home, since Alabama was the only major Auburn rival never to have played on the Plains. Auburn moved its home games in the series to campus in 1989, apart from one return to Birmingham in 1991, and Alabama followed suit in 2000. The Crimson Tide left holding a 32–15 edge in Iron Bowls at Legion Field.

## Everybody's Home Field

For much of the twentieth century, most of Alabama's "home" football history actually unfolded in Birmingham, and Auburn also played home games here well into the 1970s. But the Gray Lady belonged to many. The Magic City Classic between Alabama A&M and Alabama State has been played here since 1946, and the Southeastern Conference staged its first two championship games here in 1992 and 1993. A parade of professional teams came and went: the Birmingham Americans of the World Football League, the Stallions of the USFL, the Barracudas of the Canadian Football League's brief American expansion, the XFL's Thunderbolts and the Birmingham Iron. In 1968 the Boston Patriots played a "home" game here against the New York Jets, and the Jets, featuring former Alabama quarterback Joe Namath, won 47–31. A 1974 high school game between Banks and Woodlawn drew a record crowd of more than 40,000. UAB made Legion Field its home from 1991 through 2020.

## Grass, Turf and the World's Game

The playing surface has changed almost as often as the tenants. Natural grass gave way to Poly-Turf in 1970 and AstroTurf in 1975, then returned as Bermuda grass in 1995 so that the stadium could host Olympic soccer. The stadium also hosted exhibitions by the U.S. men's and women's national teams, and a men's World Cup qualifier against Guatemala on March 30, 2005. When the city switched to FieldTurf in 2006, U.S. Soccer announced it would no longer schedule men's national team games here. Music found a place too. U2, Pink Floyd and the Rolling Stones all played Legion Field, and the Drum Corps International World Championships came in 1979 and 1980.

## The Gray Lady Steps Back

In 2004 a structural evaluation found that the east upper deck would need major work to meet modern building codes. With little prospect of repairs, the University of Alabama withdrew the few home games it still scheduled in Birmingham, and the city removed the deck in 2005, leaving room for about 71,594. A 2015 renovation added a larger video scoreboard and a new sound system, but construction on a new stadium downtown had begun in 2019. UAB moved into the new 47,000-seat Protective Stadium in 2021. Legion Field still had work to do, sharing the 2022 USFL season with its younger neighbor and hosting flag football at the 2022 World Games. It remains the home of the Magic City Classic, and in October 2025 the city council approved about $1 million in repairs, from restrooms to stadium walls, as the Gray Lady nears its 100th birthday in 2027.

## Flight Context

Legion Field sits at 33.511N, 86.843W on Graymont Avenue, just west of downtown Birmingham, at a field elevation of about 570 feet. From 3,000 to 4,000 feet, look for the open gray concrete bowl with its north–south field west of the downtown towers; the newer Protective Stadium lies on the downtown side to the east. Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International (KBHM) is only about 5.5 nm to the northeast, so the stadium sits beneath Birmingham's Class C airspace; contact approach before maneuvering. Bessemer Airport (KEKY) lies about 13 nm to the south.
