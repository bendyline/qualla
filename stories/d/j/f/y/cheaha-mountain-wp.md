# Cheaha Mountain

Alabama's highest natural point rises from hard, ancient quartzite, crowned by a stone Civilian Conservation Corps tower and a forest of broadcast antennas.

**Location:** 33.4855, -85.8087

**Categories:** Mountains of Alabama, Highest points of U.S. states, State parks of Alabama, Civilian Conservation Corps in Alabama, Landforms of Cleburne County, Alabama

## Introduction

Stand on the summit of Cheaha Mountain and there is no higher ground for more than a hundred miles. The nearest taller peak, Brushy Top in Gilmer County, Georgia, lies about 107 miles away. At about 2,410 feet (surveys give figures from 2,405 to 2,413), Cheaha is the highest natural point in Alabama, and its name seems to say as much: it is thought to come from the Choctaw word chaha, meaning "high." The mountain rises a few miles northwest of the town of Delta, inside Cheaha State Park, where the old lodge has been torn down and a replacement hotel was slated to open in 2026 (check with the park for its current status), and where campgrounds, cabins and the historic Bald Rock Lodge remain.

## Stone Work on the Summit

The very top of Alabama is marked by a USGS benchmark set in front of Bunker Tower, a stone building raised by the Civilian Conservation Corps with an observation deck on its roof. The CCC also built a road up the mountain. It has been closed for years, but the old route, known as CC Road, still holds interesting ruins along the way. The mountain opened to the public as part of Cheaha State Park on June 7, 1939. Near the peak, Bald Rock now has a wheelchair-accessible wooden walkway leading to an overlook that takes in the surrounding region, opening one of the finest views in the park to visitors who use wheelchairs.

## Why It Feels Higher

Cheaha belongs to the Talladega Mountains, a small section of the Ridge and Valley province, unlike the other high ground of north Alabama, which is part of the Cumberland Plateau. It is the highest point in the eastern Sun Belt, the region east of the Mississippi River, south of Interstate 20 and north of the Gulf of Mexico. The summit also feels loftier than its elevation, partly because the land to the west sits so low; the top stands more than 1,700 feet above the city of Oxford near its base. The mountain owes its height to tough rock. It is made of the Cheaha quartzite, weakly metamorphosed sandstones and conglomerates of Silurian or Devonian age that resist erosion, and large quartzite cliffs show on the upper slopes. Over it lies a moderately deep brown stony silt loam, well drained and very strongly acidic.

## Antennas Among the Trees

The summit is not purely wild. Several commercial and public service transmitters share the mountaintop, and their antennas, along with assorted structures left over from commercial schemes by the state of Alabama in the 1970s, stand in stark contrast to the natural setting around them. The Calhoun County Amateur Radio Association keeps a repeater near the peak, and Alabama Public Television broadcasts WCIQ TV 7 from a tower 176 meters tall. That tower is a replacement. In January 1982 an ice storm sent the previous one crashing to the ground, a reminder that even in Alabama, winter at the top of the state can bite.

## Flight Context

Cheaha Mountain rises to about 2,410 feet MSL at approximately 33.486°N, 85.809°W in Cheaha State Park, Cleburne County, Alabama, a few miles northwest of Delta. The summit carries broadcast towers, including the 176-meter (about 577-foot) WCIQ tower, so obstructions reach roughly 3,000 feet MSL; stay well above them, at 4,000 feet MSL or higher. The quartzite cliffs on the upper slopes and the drop to the low country on the west side make the ridge easy to identify. Nearby airports: Anniston Regional Airport (KANB), about 6.6 nm to the north-northwest; Ashland/Lineville Airport (FAA identifier 26A), about 12 nm south; and Talladega Municipal Airport (KASN), about 13 nm west-northwest. Expect mountain turbulence on windy days, clouds that can cap the summit, and occasional winter icing.
