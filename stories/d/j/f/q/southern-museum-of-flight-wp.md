# Southern Museum of Flight

Beside the runways of Birmingham's airport, the Southern Museum of Flight keeps roughly 100 aircraft - from a Wright Flyer replica to a Lockheed A-12 - plus a B-25 bomber raised from the bottom of a South Carolina lake.

**Location:** 33.5635, -86.7381

**Categories:** Aerospace museums in Alabama, Museums in Birmingham, Alabama, Museums established in 1966, 1966 establishments in Alabama, Birmingham–Shuttlesworth International Airport

## Introduction

For more than six decades, a B-25C Mitchell bomber sat nearly 150 feet down on the bottom of Lake Murray in South Carolina. It had gone into the water during a 1943 training exercise, losing its right engine in the crash - though all five crew members escaped before it sank. Divers came to know it as a wreck site. Then, in September 2005, it was raised from the depths and moved to Birmingham, to become part of the collection at the Southern Museum of Flight.

## Raising the Mitchell

The recovery was the work of the Lake Murray B-25 Rescue Project, formed by Dr. Bob Seigler, who spearheaded the effort, together with John Hodge and Dr. Bill Vartorella. For years the bomber had been a destination for local technical divers, a piece of wartime history resting nearly 150 feet below the surface. Bringing it up meant turning a dive site back into an artifact. After the aircraft broke the surface in September 2005, its remains were moved to the Southern Museum of Flight for conservation and eventual museum display. The operation drew cameras, too: a video crew that included maritime video specialists from Nautilus Productions documented the recovery for the Mega Movers series on the History Channel. The museum displays the Mitchell's forward section, deliberately left unrestored - preserved, as a museum official put it in 2018, "just as it was sitting at the bottom of the lake" - a survivor of both the crash and six decades underwater.

## Nearly a Hundred Airframes

Founded in 1966 by Mary Alice Beatty, the Southern Museum of Flight is a civilian aviation museum with roughly 100 aircraft, along with engines, models, artifacts, photographs, and paintings. The range is striking. Among the military aircraft are a Fokker D.VII, a Fairchild PT-19, a Douglas B-26 Invader, a MiG-15, an F-86 Sabre, an F-4 Phantom II, an F-14 Tomcat, and a Lockheed A-12. In April 2012 the museum received a Hind helicopter and an An-2, and its collection includes a Mi-25 captured in Operation Mount Hope III. The civilian side is just as eclectic, with replicas of the Wright Flyer and the Curtiss Model D, a Huff-Daland crop duster, an Aeronca Sedan on floats, a Beechcraft Starship, a Rutan VariViggen, and a Bede BD-5B. Walking the floor is like paging through much of the history of flight, from replicas of the earliest flying machines to jet fighters.

## Heroes in Collective Biography

The museum also houses the Alabama Aviation Hall of Fame, which presents the state's aviation history through collective biography - the idea that a story of flight is really a story of the people who made it. That approach runs through the exhibits. A diorama honors Alabama's famed Tuskegee Airmen, and the Korean War Jets Exhibit and Vietnam War Helicopters Exhibit place the aircraft in the context of the conflicts their crews flew through. Here the airplanes are not just machines on display but reminders of the people who designed, maintained, and flew them.

## Staying by the Runway

The museum sits at 4343 73rd Street North, on the grounds of Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International Airport, with the runways close at hand. Its future address has been a moving target. In 2017 the museum announced plans to move to a new site near Barber Motorsports Park, but the move has been delayed for years. Accounts differ on where the plan stands now - one says the museum decided in 2023 to keep its original campus and treat the new location as an expansion rather than a replacement. For now, then, the Mitchell raised from Lake Murray and its companions remain right where an aviation museum arguably belongs - next to a working airfield.

## Flight Context

The Southern Museum of Flight sits on the grounds of Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International Airport (KBHM) at approximately 33.563°N, 86.738°W, about 0.7 nautical mile east of the airport's reference point. KBHM has two runways, 6/24 (12,007 feet) and 18/36 (7,099 feet), so the museum is best seen on an arrival or departure rather than from an overflight - coordinate with Birmingham ATC. Downtown Birmingham lies about five miles to the southwest and makes an easy visual reference. Nearby alternates include Parker Field (AL18) in Gardendale, about 9 nautical miles northwest, and Anniston Regional Airport (KANB), about 44 nautical miles east.
