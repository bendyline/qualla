# Kymulga Mill & Covered Bridge

A Civil War-era gristmill that Union troops missed and an 1861 covered bridge still standing where it was built share a quiet park on Talladega Creek near Childersburg, Alabama.

**Location:** 33.3344, -86.2996

## Introduction

Major George H. Forney never saw his mill finished. The Confederate officer had hired a German contractor, G.E. Morris, to build a gristmill beside Talladega Creek in early 1864, but Forney died at the Battle of the Wilderness in Virginia on May 5 of that year, before the work was done (some accounts say he had risen to lieutenant colonel and was leading the 1st Confederate Infantry Battalion). Morris went on to complete it, largely with enslaved workers he leased. More than a century and a half later, Kymulga Mill still stands, its machinery now run by electricity, and the covered bridge beside it still carries people across the creek.

## The Mill the Raiders Missed

During the Civil War, Union Army soldiers burned most of the gristmills throughout this part of Alabama. Kymulga Mill was missed. That stroke of luck gave it a long working life. Three water-powered turbines ran the multistory mill, driving its lights, its grain elevator, and the millstones that ground the grain. At its height the mill had five sets of stones, two of them French buhrs. Ownership changed hands four times over the decades, yet the mill stayed in active service through many of those years. In 1988 the Childersburg Heritage Committee bought it from its latest owner, Edward Donahoo. Today the mill's machinery runs on electricity rather than water, and the building serves as a tourist attraction with a gift shop and the park office; one account says it still makes corn meal with its huge millstones.

## A Bridge That Never Moved

Just east of the mill, the Kymulga Covered Bridge spans Talladega Creek. Built around 1861, it is a 105-foot, single-span Howe truss, a wood-and-metal combination that has stood at its crossing through the entire history of the mill beside it. That permanence is rare. The Kymulga bridge is one of only two 19th-century covered bridges still standing at their original sites in Alabama; the other, the Waldo Covered Bridge, is also in Talladega County. Today it carries pedestrian traffic, leading park visitors from the mill grounds to a series of nature trails north of the creek. Walk its length and you cross the same gap travelers have crossed here since about 1861, when this bridge was built.

## The Old Georgia Road

The bridge once gave access to the Old Georgia Road, also called the McIntosh Road, a Native American trade route later used by settlers and frontiersmen venturing into the area. Over time, farms and communities grew up along the former trail. Then, around 1940 and 1941, the landscape changed again. The United States government bought land next to Kymulga Mill for the Alabama Ordnance Works, a military installation, and the settlements within the tract had to be relocated - whole communities uprooted to make way for it. Those who walk the nature trails beyond the bridge today can still find remnants of the Old Georgia Road, along with other traces of the lives that once filled this stretch of the creek.

## Saving Kymulga

Both the mill and the bridge were restored in 1974 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places on October 29, 1976. After the Childersburg Heritage Committee bought the tract in 1988, it worked with the Alabama Historical Commission and the Talladega County Commission to establish Kymulga Park, making further structural renovations along the way. Visitors pay an admission charge, and the proceeds go toward the upkeep of the structures and nature trails. In June 2011 the City of Childersburg acquired the park, which is now managed by the Childersburg Historic Preservation Commission. The park sits on Grist Mill Road (County Road 46) off State Route 76, about four miles northeast of Childersburg.
