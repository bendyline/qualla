# Cramton Bowl

Built on a donated landfill in 1922, Montgomery's Cramton Bowl has hosted big-league spring training, a Negro World Series finale, the South's first night football game and decades of Blue-Gray Classics, and after a $10 million renovation it still hosts football on game days.

## Introduction

"[We] used dishpans for reflectors and sent to California for the lamps," former superintendent D. H. "Sarge" Caraker remembered. On September 23, 1927, Cloverdale took on Pike Road High School at Montgomery's Cramton Bowl in the first football game played "under the lights" in the South, and, by Caraker's account, the novelty drew 7,200 people from all over the South. It was one night among many for a stadium that has seen almost everything: big-league spring training, a Negro World Series finale, a famous Christmas all-star game and, today, college bowl games in front of up to 21,000 fans.

## Built on a Landfill

The stadium exists because one businessman refused to let a good idea die. After a conversation with friends about Montgomery's need for a baseball stadium, Fred J. Cramton donated his sanitary landfill to the city so one could be built there. The city held the land for a while and then handed it back, saying the project was too big for it to take on. Cramton took matters into his own hands. With the help of the Junior Chamber of Commerce he raised the $33,000 needed to build the venue, and the stadium that opened in 1922 still carries his name. The first baseball game on the new field, in May 1922, matched Auburn University against Vanderbilt University.

## Big-League Springs and a World Series Night

Shortly after the park opened, the Philadelphia Athletics moved their spring training from Eagle Pass, Texas, to Montgomery, using Cramton Bowl in 1923 and 1924 before leaving for a newer stadium in Fort Myers, Florida. Minor league baseball followed. The Montgomery Lions played here from 1926 to 1930; after a gap from 1931 to 1936, caused by troubles within the Southeastern League and the Great Depression, the team returned in 1937 as the Montgomery Bombers, later becoming the Montgomery Rebels. World War II's manpower shortage disbanded the Rebels in 1943, and that July the Chattanooga Lookouts moved their home games here to finish the season. The Rebels returned from 1946 through 1949 before moving to the newly built Paterson Field across the street. In 1943 the park also hosted a championship: the eighth and deciding game of the Negro World Series, in which the Homestead Grays beat the Birmingham Black Barons, 8-4.

## Christmas Football, Then Decay

Football eventually became the stadium's main purpose. Seating grew from 7,991 after a 1929 expansion to 12,000 after additions in 1946, and the Alabama State Hornets made Cramton Bowl their home field. The stadium probably achieved its greatest fame, though, as the home of the Blue-Gray Football Classic, a college all-star game held here each December from 1938 until 2001 and usually played on Christmas Day. For decades, Christmas meant football in Montgomery. By the start of the twenty-first century, Cramton Bowl was widely regarded as functionally obsolete for major events. Its size mattered less than its age and condition; the stadium had not been well maintained for most of its history and was, by the new millennium, actually crumbling. Those problems were among the factors, though not the main ones, in the Blue-Gray Game not being held in 2002 and moving in 2003 to Troy University's stadium, about 50 miles away. In mid-2010, the City of Montgomery approved a $10 million renovation that mixed old and new: four entrances, a modern press box, an enhanced plaza, a "Walk of Fame" celebrating Montgomery's sports history and, most impressive of all, a 90,000-square-foot multi-purpose sports facility.

## Bowl Games and Friday Nights

After the renovation, the old stadium found a new role on the national stage. Beginning in 2014 it hosted the Camellia Bowl, a college bowl game with tie-ins to the Sun Belt Conference and the Mid-American Conference, renamed the Salute to Veterans Bowl in 2024. From 2017 through 2025 it hosted the FCS Kickoff, a season-opening Division I game televised nationally on ESPN's networks; the 2026 edition moves to a campus site at Mercer in Macon, Georgia. In December 2020 the stadium staged the one-off Montgomery Bowl during the pandemic. Local football never left. Several Montgomery high schools, including the Johnson Abernathy Graetz Jaguars, the Percy Julian Phoenix, the Park Crossing Thunderbirds and the Carver Wolverines, call Cramton Bowl home. A century after Fred Cramton's landfill became a ballpark, the lights still come on.

## Flight Context

Cramton Bowl sits at approximately 32.379 N, 86.293 W just east of downtown Montgomery, Alabama, bounded by Madison Avenue to the north, Hall Street to the east and Pelham Street to the south. From 1,500-2,500 ft MSL the stadium bowl and its large multi-purpose sports building are easy to spot on the city grid, with Paterson Field across the street. Maxwell Air Force Base (KMXF) is only about 4 nm west, so this is busy military airspace; contact ATC. Montgomery Regional Airport (KMGM) lies about 7 nm southwest and is the main civilian field. Troy Municipal (KTOI), in the city where the Blue-Gray Game moved in 2003, is about 35 nm south-southeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms, and fall game days usually offer clearer skies.
