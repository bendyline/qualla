# Red Lady of Huntingdon College

A Huntingdon College legend tells of a lonely student who surrounded herself with red and, the story goes, still sends crimson light through a transom on the fourth floor of Pratt Hall.

**Location:** 32.3500, -86.2850

**Categories:** American ghosts, Folklore, Huntingdon College, Montgomery, Alabama, Women's education in Alabama

## Introduction

The sign, students say, is a flash of crimson light spilling from the transom above a door on the fourth floor of Pratt Hall. Once a year, the story goes, it marks the return of the Red Lady, the ghost said to haunt the old Pratt Hall dormitory at Huntingdon College in Montgomery, Alabama. Her story is told in 13 Alabama Ghosts and Jeffrey, by Kathryn Tucker Windham, a Huntingdon alumna, and Margaret Gillis Figh, a Huntingdon English professor who wrote the Red Lady chapter. Like all good campus legends, it is equal parts eerie and sad, and nobody has ever been able to prove who she was.

## A Scarlet Parasol in Tuskegee

According to Windham and historian Daniel Barefoot, there have actually been two Red Ladies. The first belongs to the college's earlier life in Tuskegee, where it was founded in 1854 as Tuskegee Female College before moving to Montgomery in 1909 in search of a more populous city. Late one night in the late nineteenth century, the tale says, a young woman in a scarlet dress, carrying a scarlet parasol, walked wordlessly up and down the halls of a women's dormitory, bathed in a red glow. She left the building, passed through a gateway outside and vanished. No one ever determined who she was, and she was apparently never seen again.

## The Girl Who Loved Red

The second Red Lady is the one whose story students still tell. The book calls her Martha; another source says Margaret; no one has ever offered a last name. In the legend she came south reluctantly from New York because her father's will required her to attend Huntingdon, his mother's alma mater. She arrived dressed in red and brought red draperies, a red bedspread, a red prayer rug from Turkey and small red figurines for her shelves. When classmates asked about the color, she never explained. Shy, unhappy and far from home, she could not make friends. Having heard she was wealthy, the other young women mistook her shyness for disdain, and she took her meals alone.

## One Roommate After Another

Her first roommate asked to move out, and so did the next, and the one after that, each lasting only a short time as Martha grew more aloof. At last the dormitory president, known for getting along with everyone, moved in and tried hard to befriend her. When she too gave up and packed her things, Martha, in the book's telling, turned on her with a bitter warning: "for the rest of your life you'll regret leaving this room." After that, the legend says, Martha drifted into gatherings whose members soon found excuses to leave her alone, then retreated to her room, wrapped in her red bedspread. Later she began walking the halls in the darkest hours, silent and staring, opening and closing doors before hurrying away.

## Crimson Light on the Fourth Floor

One day Martha missed all her classes and meals. That evening her former roommate, stricken with guilt, went to her room in an isolated corridor on the top floor and, the story says, saw flashes of red shooting from the transom. Inside she found that Martha had taken her own life. The account says only that this happened "a long time ago." Since then, students have claimed to see the crimson light returning on the anniversary and a figure passing through walls and closed doors on Pratt's fourth floor. The building is no longer a dormitory; one account says it now houses the college's Department of Education and Psychology, while the college's own ghost guide says the Red Lady has since "moved" to another building, Jackson Home. Each October, members of the Chi Omega, Phi Mu and Alpha Omicron Pi sororities keep the tale alive with "The Red Lady Run," painting their faces, dressing in black and racing around a campus that also claims a poltergeist known as Frank the Library Ghost.

## Flight Context

Huntingdon College sits at about 32.35 N, 86.285 W in the Old Cloverdale neighborhood of Montgomery, Alabama, a couple of miles south of downtown. From 2,000-3,000 feet, look for the college green and its cluster of campus buildings within the residential grid south of the downtown skyline. Montgomery Regional Airport (Dannelly Field, KMGM) is about 6 nm to the west-southwest and Maxwell Air Force Base (KMXF, military) about 4.3 nm to the northwest; both lie in controlled airspace, so contact ATC. Wetumpka Municipal (FAA identifier 08A, no ICAO code listed) is a general-aviation alternative about 11 nm to the north. Expect haze and thunderstorms in summer.
