# Phenix City, Alabama

A river town named, perhaps, for a mythical bird that rises from its own ashes lived up to the symbol, going from "Sin City, USA" and martial law in 1954 to an All-America City Award a year later.

**Location:** 32.4728, -85.0200

**Categories:** Cities in Alabama, Cities in Russell County, Alabama, County seats in Alabama, Alabama populated places on the Chattahoochee River, Columbus metropolitan area, Georgia

## Introduction

Cross the Chattahoochee River westbound from Columbus, Georgia, and you leave the Eastern Time Zone for Alabama and Central Time, at least on paper. In Phenix City, the clocks stay put. This city on Alabama's eastern edge is so tightly bound to its much larger neighbor across the water that it keeps Eastern Time on a de facto basis while most of the state runs an hour behind. It is a small quirk for a town with a large story, one that runs from what some call the last battle of the Civil War through a notorious era as "Sin City, USA" to a hard-won reinvention.

## Brownville, Lively, or Phenix

The city began as two towns sitting side by side. Girard lay to the south in Russell County, and Brownville to the north in Lee County. Because Tuscaloosa County already had a Brownville, postal officials insisted on the name Lively, and the nearby railroad depot confusingly went by Knight's Station. The 1880 census, bowing to both camps, listed the town as Brownville and Lively. Incorporated as Brownville in 1883, it was renamed Phenix City by the legislature in 1897, and nobody is quite sure why. It may honor Phenix Mills across the river in Columbus, or it may honor the fabled bird. The city's own history page admits that no definitive source explains the choice, but it has embraced the legend anyway: its seal shows a phoenix burning in its nest. Girard and Phenix City merged on August 9, 1923, and in 1932 the Lee County portion was shifted into Russell County, which later made Phenix City its sole county seat.

## Crossing the Chattahoochee

Girard, which makes up most of today's downtown Phenix City, had already made its mark on history. On April 16, 1865, Union General James Wilson's cavalry fought its way across the Chattahoochee here in an effort to destroy the military industry of Columbus, Georgia, on the far bank. Confederate defenders met them at the crossing. The Battle of Columbus came a week after Robert E. Lee's surrender at Appomattox, and some consider it the last battle of the American Civil War. The river that the soldiers fought over remains the defining feature of the place, the boundary between two states and, officially, two time zones.

## Sin City, USA

In the 1940s and 1950s, Phenix City became notorious as a haven for organized crime, prostitution and gambling, with many of its customers coming from the U.S. Army training center at Fort Benning, Georgia. Jimmie Matthews and Hoyt Shepherd led the crime syndicate. Albert Patterson, from Phenix City, ran for Alabama attorney general promising to clean up his hometown, and he won the Democratic nomination. On June 18, 1954, less than two weeks later, he was shot and killed in Phenix City. Russell County's chief deputy sheriff was convicted of the murder. The state responded forcefully. On July 22, 1954, Governor Gordon Persons declared limited martial law in Russell County, and it lasted until January 17, 1955. Patterson's son, John, took his father's place on the ballot, won, and later served as Governor of Alabama from 1959 to 1963.

## Rising From the Ashes

The town that took the phoenix as its emblem proved remarkably quick to rise. In 1955, the same year the film The Phenix City Story was made, the National Municipal League gave Phenix City its All-America City Award. Local reformers such as Hugh A. Bentley had helped turn the tide, and Margaret Ann Barnes later chronicled the whole saga in The Tragedy and the Triumph of Phenix City, Alabama. The old reputation has never entirely faded, and many people still link the city with its Sin City years. The city has since built a different reputation. In 2007, BusinessWeek named Phenix City the best affordable suburb in the nation in which to raise a family.

## A City on Columbus Time

Today about 38,800 people live in Phenix City, according to the 2020 census, and after being consolidated entirely into Russell County in 1932 the city has grown back north into Lee County. U.S. Routes 80, 280 and 431 thread through it, leading west toward Tuskegee, northwest to Opelika and south toward Eufaula. Chattahoochee Valley Community College is here, Troy University voted in 2025 to close its Phenix City campus by the end of that year, and the Phenix City Express has offered local bus service. Olympic sprinter Harvey Glance was born in Phenix City, while major league pitcher Tim Hudson went to school here and country singer Freddie Hart grew up here; Hart is credited with a song called "Phenix City." Its sister cities stretch from Colomiers in France to Västerås in Sweden.

## Flight Context

Phenix City sits at 32.473N, 85.020W on the west bank of the Chattahoochee River, directly across from Columbus, Georgia. From 3,000 to 5,000 feet, the river running north–south between the two downtowns is the obvious landmark, with Phenix City on the Alabama side. Columbus Airport (KCSG) is about 5 nm to the northeast; coordinate with Columbus approach before overflying the cities. Lawson Army Airfield (KLSF) lies about 8 nm to the south, and the military airspace around Fort Benning should be checked before any flight in that direction. Auburn University Regional Airport (KAUO) is about 23 nm to the west-northwest.
