# Miss Freedom

The hollow copper statue atop Georgia's gold-leafed Capitol dome has held her torch over Atlanta since 1889 - though it stayed dark for seventy years before it finally glowed blue-green.

**Location:** 33.7490, -84.3881

**Categories:** 1889 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state), 1889 sculptures, Colossal statues in the United States, Copper sculptures in the United States, Finial figures, History of Atlanta, Liberty symbols, Outdoor sculptures in Georgia (U.S. state), Statues in Atlanta

## Introduction

For seventy years, the torch in Miss Freedom's hand was a promise unkept. The statue took her place atop the dome of the Georgia State Capitol in 1889, holding a torch that was supposed to shine continuously - yet it stayed dark until it was reconstructed in 1959. Today it works as intended, a mercury-vapor lamp that casts a blue-green light over Atlanta at night, above a dome covered in Georgia gold.

## Copper, Painted White

Miss Freedom was commissioned in 1888. She is a hollow copper statue, painted white. Accounts differ on her size: the New Georgia Encyclopedia gives about 22 feet from head to foot, 26 feet to the tip of her torch, and 1,600 pounds, while a restoration report lists a 15-foot statue weighing 2,000 pounds. She was sculpted with a torch in her right hand and a sword in her left, both raised above the seat of state government. Her name has never been entirely settled. What she was called before her christening is unknown, and over the years she has been known as Miss Freedom, Liberty, and the Goddess of Liberty. She has kindred figures elsewhere: the Statue of Freedom, Thomas Crawford's 1863 sculpture atop the dome of the U.S. Capitol, and a similar Goddess of Liberty by Elijah E. Myers, erected in 1888, that crowned the Texas State Capitol in Austin until a replica replaced it in 1986. Liberty, it seems, was a popular choice for the top of a dome.

## A Capitol for Atlanta

Georgia's government had met in several other places, including Savannah, Augusta, Louisville and Milledgeville, before settling in Atlanta, where this is the third statehouse building. The commission overseeing the new building engaged the Chicago architects Willoughby J. Edbrooke and Franklin Pierce Burnham, and the building was completed in 1889. Like many state capitols, it was designed to echo the Neoclassical style of the United States Capitol, with a dome 75 feet in diameter. Miss Freedom has stood on that dome since the building's opening, and the Capitol itself has since been named a National Historic Landmark. Inside, a museum near the rotunda has been part of the building since 1889, and a replica of the statue is said to be displayed within the Capitol for those who want a closer look than the street allows.

## Under the Gold Dome

The dome Miss Freedom stands on was originally built of terra cotta and covered with tin. In a 1958 renovation it was gilded with native gold leaf from near Dahlonega in Lumpkin County, the region where the first American gold rush took place in the 1830s. The gleam stuck, and so did the nickname: across Georgia, the media often describe legislative business as what is happening "under the Gold Dome." A year after the gilding, Miss Freedom's long-dark torch was finally reconstructed. Keeping it lit takes some ingenuity. Tube and trolley systems were installed inside the statue so the bulb can be changed from within, sparing anyone a climb up the outside of a figure perched on a golden dome.

## Flight Context

Miss Freedom stands atop the gold dome of the Georgia State Capitol in downtown Atlanta at approximately 33.749°N, 84.388°W. The gilded dome is a bright target among the downtown towers, especially in low sun. The site lies only about 7 nautical miles north-northeast of Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (KATL), deep inside Atlanta's Class B airspace, so any overflight requires a clearance and close coordination with ATC; a scenic view is most practical on an arrival or departure. Fulton County Executive Airport-Charlie Brown Field (KFTY, formerly Fulton County Airport-Brown Field) is about 7 nautical miles west-northwest, and DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) about 9 nautical miles north-northeast.
