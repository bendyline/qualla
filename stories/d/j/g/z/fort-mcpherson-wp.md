# Fort McPherson

For more than a century Fort McPherson in southwest Atlanta held prisoners of war, healed the wounded and commanded armies, before the Army lowered its flag in 2011 and much of the post became a film studio.

**Location:** 33.7062, -84.4333

**Categories:** United States Army posts, Military history, Prisoner-of-war camps in the United States, Atlanta, Georgia, Historic districts

## Introduction

Quarters 10 is the centerpiece of Fort McPherson's Staff Row. Completed in 1892, the three-story house has twelve-foot ceilings on its first floor and semicircular windows in its turret. In 1925 it awaited a new post commander, Douglas MacArthur. His wife refused to live in it. The MacArthurs rented an apartment downtown near the Fox Theatre instead, and after 89 days he was reassigned to Baltimore. The house later made room for a more famous visitor: in 1935 a sleeping porch was added to the back for President Franklin D. Roosevelt's stops on his way to Warm Springs. Such stories fill the old Army post four miles southwest of downtown Atlanta.

## A Post Named for a Fallen General

The Army first came in September 1867, setting up McPherson Barracks on the grounds where Spelman College stands today. Through Reconstruction, soldiers of the 2nd, 16th and 18th Infantry and the 5th Artillery garrisoned the post to enforce Union regulations after the Civil War. When Reconstruction ended, Secretary of War Robert T. Lincoln ordered the site given up, and the troops left on December 8, 1881; part of the land went to the Atlanta Baptist Female Seminary, the future Spelman College. In 1885 Congress funded a new ten-company post, and the Army bought land that September, at least 140 acres in five tracts. It took the name of Major General James Birdseye McPherson, commander of the Union Army of the Tennessee, who was killed while on reconnaissance during the Battle of Atlanta on July 22, 1864.

## Guardhouses, Barracks and Bells

The post that rose in the 1880s and 1890s still survives in part. About forty buildings on 33 acres form a historic district listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The chapel began life as the post guardhouse, built between 1886 and 1893 depending on the source, and it gained pews, eleven stained-glass windows and four stained-glass transoms in 1921. Troop Row's barracks went up from 1889, and Staff Row's 19 officers' quarters followed between 1891 and 1910, facing Hedekin Field, a former polo field. The old headquarters, Van Horn Hall, holds a small place in office history. In 1891 the Army reportedly authorized renting a telephone line to Atlanta for $12.50 a month, and in 1893 it approved $17 for electric bells to link the building's offices, the post's first known intercom.

## Prisoners and Patients

War repeatedly turned the fort into something else. During the Spanish-American War it served as a general hospital and trained nearly 20,000 recruits, with barracks overflowing and emergency tents pitched, and by the end of July 1898, 16 Spanish Army prisoners were held in what is now the chapel. In World War I a war prison barracks just west of the post confined German prisoners of war, peaking at 1,411 in July 1918; after it was torn down in 1919, many homes were built on its leftover concrete slabs. At the same time Fort McPherson became General Hospital No. 6, with nearly 2,400 beds, admitting an estimated 10,000-plus patients from August 1917 through December 1918. The fort held Americans too. During the 1934 textile strike, picketers arrested at a cotton mill in Newnan, Georgia, were detained here.

## Where Armies Were Run

In World War II the post processed thousands of men into the service, and when the war ended in August 1945 the flow reversed: Fort McPherson became a separation center for almost 200,000 soldiers heading home. In 1947 it became home to Third Army, the command George S. Patton Jr. had led across France and into Germany. Third Army stayed until 1973, then was reactivated in Atlanta in 1982. When it deployed to Saudi Arabia in August 1990, it commanded the U.S. Army's ground forces in the buildup, drew up the initial defensive plan for Saudi Arabia and later the offensive ground plan. By the 2000s the fort also housed U.S. Army Forces Command, the Army Reserve Command and the Army Ground Forces Band, whose lineage reportedly stretched back to 1845.

## From Parade Ground to Soundstage

The 2005 round of base closures sealed the fort's fate. Fort McPherson closed on September 15, 2011, and its commands scattered to Fort Bragg, Shaw Air Force Base, Fort Eustis and Fort Sam Houston. A local redevelopment authority reportedly bought the land for about $30 million. In June 2015, actor and producer Tyler Perry purchased 330 acres to become the new home of Tyler Perry Studios, and on June 26, 2015, the Fort Mac Local Redevelopment Authority became owner of 145 acres to guide their reuse. Beneath the changes, the land keeps one constant: natural springs here are among the headwaters of South Utoy Creek, which flows northwest to the Chattahoochee River.

## Flight Context

Fort McPherson lies at about 33.706 N, 84.433 W in southwest Atlanta, bordering the northern edge of East Point and about 4 miles southwest of downtown. From the air, look for the open parade ground of Hedekin Field faced by the officers' houses of Staff Row, near the Lakewood/Fort McPherson MARTA station. The site is only about 4 nm north of Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) and sits within Atlanta's busy terminal airspace, so overflight requires ATC clearance and vigilance for airline traffic. Fulton County Executive Airport-Charlie Brown Field (KFTY, formerly Fulton County Airport-Brown Field) is about 6 nm to the northwest, and DeKalb-Peachtree (KPDK) about 12 nm to the north-northeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
