# TomorrowWorld

For three Septembers, a Belgian dance-music fantasy took over the Georgia countryside at Chattahoochee Hills, drawing roughly 150,000 people a year before a weekend of mud and a corporate bankruptcy ended it.

**Location:** 33.6361, -84.7075

**Categories:** Electronic music festivals in the United States, Music festivals in Atlanta, Music festivals established in 2013, Recurring events disestablished in 2016, Chattahoochee Hills, Georgia

## Introduction

The organizers were looking for a piece of Belgium in the American South. Tomorrowland, the electronic music festival, is traditionally held in Boom, Belgium, and when its producers decided to launch an American spinoff, they chose land at Chattahoochee Hills, southwest of Atlanta, specifically because it resembled Boom. For three Septembers, from 2013 to 2015, TomorrowWorld turned that Georgia land into a city of stages, tents and bass, until the weather and the balance sheet brought it to an end.

## The Next Chapter

The festival was announced in March 2013 by Barnette Group, ID&T and SFX Entertainment. It was organized and produced by the promoter ID&T, a wholly owned subsidiary of SFX, the company later renamed LiveStyle. To mark TomorrowWorld as the "next chapter" in the Tomorrowland story, the first edition reused the "Book of Wisdom" main stage design from the 2012 Tomorrowland festival in Belgium. The launch came at an uneasy moment. TomorrowWorld was the first electronic dance music event after two deaths at New York's Electric Zoo Festival, and ticket sales ran slower than expected. Between that, the unconventional rural location and a minimum age of 21, industry insiders feared a flop.

## Dreamville Rises

The doubters were wrong. In the final week of September 2013, roughly 140,000 people gathered at Chattahoochee Hills to hear Armin van Buuren, Tiësto, Hardwell and more than 300 other artists across eight stages. Nearly 30,000 of them stayed on site in a campground called Dreamville, and the edition was reported to have passed without incidents. That first year earned a nomination for Best Music Event at the International Dance Music Awards. A study commissioned by the organizers compared the festival's impact on metro Atlanta to the $70 million generated by the 2013 NCAA Final Four. Research firm Beacon Economics reported that visiting attendees spent $28.7 million directly on lodging, restaurants, sightseeing and more, and estimated $4.3 million in tax revenue for state and local governments from the 2013 event.

## Bigger, and Cashless

The second edition, in September 2014, grew to 150,000 attendees. It introduced something new: every purchase was cashless, handled by the same radio-frequency identification wristbands that served as entry tickets. The first two editions had been billed as Chattahoochee Hills events even though they were held just outside the city limits. That changed in spring 2015, when the City of Chattahoochee Hills annexed 4,920 acres, including the festival property. The city council also approved a land use permit allowing the event to be held there until 2025, at the request of the landowner, Carl Bouckaert, who had asked for it as part of the annexation petition. On paper, TomorrowWorld had a decade ahead of it.

## The Weekend of Mud

The third edition opened on the weekend of September 25, 2015, and the rain came with it. Showers churned the festival grounds into mud and left the entrance roads unusable. On Saturday, because of the road conditions, organizers restricted shuttle service back to Atlanta. Some festivalgoers were stranded at the grounds overnight without shelter; others had to hike for miles to reach places where taxi and Uber drivers were offering rides back to the city at high prices. The next morning, organizers announced that the final day would be open only to those camping on site, and promised refunds to people hit by the transport problems or holding tickets for day three. The backlash lingered. In February 2016, SFX filed for bankruptcy, and on March 2, 2016, the company announced that TomorrowWorld 2016 was cancelled. The festival permitted to run until 2025 had lasted three years.

## Flight Context

The former festival grounds are at 33.6361 N, 84.7075 W in Chattahoochee Hills, Georgia, southwest of downtown Atlanta. The festival was temporary and left no permanent stages to spot, so treat this as a scenic pass over the Chattahoochee Hills countryside rather than a landmark hunt. Fly at 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL and stay well clear of Atlanta's busy airspace to the east. Fulton County Executive Airport - Charlie Brown Field (KFTY, formerly Fulton County Airport - Brown Field) is about 13 nm to the northeast, Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) about 14 nm to the east, and Newnan Coweta County Airport (FAA identifier CCO) about 20 nm to the south. Late-September showers, like the ones that swamped the 2015 festival, can lower visibility.
