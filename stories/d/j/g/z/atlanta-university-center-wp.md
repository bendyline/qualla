# Atlanta University Center

In southwest Atlanta, Clark Atlanta, Spelman, Morehouse and the Morehouse School of Medicine form what the consortium calls the world's oldest and largest association of historically Black colleges and universities, sharing a library, traditions and nearly a century of cooperation.

**Location:** 33.7490, -84.4110

**Categories:** Historically black colleges and universities, Education in Atlanta, College consortia, African American history, Atlanta, Georgia

## Introduction

Every August, before the fall semester begins, the newest students of Clark Atlanta, Morehouse and Spelman, joined since 2023 by Morris Brown, gather for a ceremony called Olive Branch. It is an official welcome, but also a reminder that these schools are neighbors in the most literal sense. Together with the Morehouse School of Medicine, they make up the Atlanta University Center, which the consortium describes as the world's oldest and largest association of historically Black colleges and universities. A student can walk from one campus to the next, sign up for classes across the line, and study in a library they all share.

## Old Roots on the Southwest Side

The schools are older than the consortium that binds them. Atlanta University was founded in 1865, in the year the Civil War ended, and Morehouse began two years later as the Augusta Institute in Augusta, Georgia. Clark College followed in 1869. Spelman started in 1881 as the Atlanta Baptist Female Seminary, meeting in the basement of Friendship Baptist Church, and took its present name in honor of Laura Spelman Rockefeller and her parents. Today Morehouse is a men's liberal arts college and Spelman a women's, and Clark Atlanta University was born in 1988 when Atlanta University merged with Clark College. The campuses cluster in the Atlanta University Center District, near downtown and immediately southwest of Mercedes-Benz Stadium.

## John Hope's Idea

The consortium was the vision of John Hope, who in 1929 was president of both Morehouse College and Atlanta University and saw what the schools could gain by working together. That April, Atlanta University, Morehouse and Spelman signed an affiliation agreement and became the original members. Clark College and Morris Brown College joined in 1957, the Interdenominational Theological Center followed in 1959, and the Morehouse School of Medicine, which had become independent of Morehouse College, joined in 1983. Not every bond held: Morris Brown ended its affiliation after losing its accreditation in December 2002, though after regaining accreditation it rejoined the Olive Branch welcome ceremony in 2023. The theological center is no longer a consortium member, but it still belongs to the library-sharing arrangement of the Robert W. Woodruff Library, which the member schools share.

## Two Degrees in Five Years

Cooperation opened doors that no single college could. In 1969 the consortium launched a dual degree engineering program: students complete a science, technology or mathematics curriculum at an AUC school, then an engineering curriculum at a partner institution, earning two bachelor's degrees in about five years. Partners include Georgia Tech, the University of Michigan, Notre Dame, Auburn and Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute, and more than 1,100 students have completed the program. The model keeps expanding. In 2019 UnitedHealth Group gave $8.25 million to create the AUC Data Science Initiative, aimed partly at bringing more underrepresented minorities into data science, and three AUC schools struck a deal with Syracuse University College of Law for a bachelor's and law degree in about six years. In 2023 Morehouse alumnus Spike Lee helped launch a fellowship for students pursuing entertainment and media careers, including $25,000 toward student loans.

## SpelHouse Weekend

The AUC's traditions spill beyond the classroom. Spelman and Morehouse hold their homecomings in the same week each fall, a celebration known as "SpelHouse Homecoming." Every few years Clark Atlanta's lines up too, and the week becomes "SpArkHouse." The homecomings draw tens of thousands of alumni, students, celebrity guests and visitors, and they help define the center's cultural identity. Art has a home here as well, in the Spelman College Museum of Fine Art and the Clark Atlanta University Museum of Art. Faith has its places, too: the Lyke House Catholic Student Center serves students, and the Absalom Jones Episcopal Student Center and Chapel also houses a Lutheran ministry that serves both students and the surrounding community. Among the many who passed through these campuses was Martin Luther King Jr., a Morehouse graduate.

## Flight Context

The Atlanta University Center lies at about 33.749 N, 84.411 W, just west-southwest of downtown Atlanta. The easiest landmark is Mercedes-Benz Stadium, with the AUC campuses immediately to its southwest; the downtown towers rise just beyond. The area sits beneath Atlanta's busy terminal airspace, less than 7 nm north of Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL), so any flight over the city requires ATC clearance and close attention to airline traffic. Fulton County Executive Airport-Charlie Brown Field (KFTY, formerly Fulton County Airport-Brown Field) is about 6 nm to the west-northwest, and DeKalb-Peachtree (KPDK) about 9 nm to the northeast. A viewing altitude assigned by ATC, typically 2,500-3,500 feet, gives a good look at the campuses. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
