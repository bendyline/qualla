# Solar Total Energy Project

In the 1980s a field of solar collectors outside Newnan, Georgia, powered, cooled and steamed a knitwear factory, billed as the world's first and largest solar thermal cogeneration project with an industrial application.

**Location:** 33.4044, -84.7472

**Categories:** Solar energy in the United States, Georgia Power, Renewable energy history, Coweta County, Georgia, Industrial history

## Introduction

Fly low over Newnan, Georgia, and you may spot an odd rectangle: a fenced enclosure of crumbling pavement with mature pine trees pushing up through the cracks. Nothing about it says "power plant." Yet in the 1980s this ground held the Solar Total Energy Project, known as STEP, billed as the world's first and largest solar thermal cogeneration project with an industrial application. Its job was to turn Georgia sunshine into electricity, air conditioning and steam for the knitwear factory next door.

## An Answer to the Oil Shocks

STEP grew out of the National Solar Thermal Energy Program, created after the oil crises of the 1970s. The U.S. Department of Energy and Georgia Power financed it jointly to push nonconventional renewable technology forward. The goal was ambitious and practical at once: design, build, operate and evaluate a solar thermal system that could supply electric power, process steam and absorption air conditioning to a working factory. In 1977 the Department of Energy chose a joint proposal from Georgia Power and the Westinghouse Advanced Energy Systems Division, picking it from a field of 16 competitors in 14 states. Design work ran from 1978 to 1980, and the project was formally dedicated and began test operations in May 1982.

## The Physicist at the Center

The idea belonged to Edward J. Ney, a Westinghouse energy physicist who later became a nationally recognized expert on solar energy systems. Ney served as the project integrator while at Westinghouse, then joined Georgia Power as its Manager of Solar Operations and STEP project manager. He brought the parties together into a joint-venture agreement, and he stayed with the project through every stage of its life: design, construction, test operations, commercial operations, and finally its decommissioning and disassembly. He saw STEP from its first handshake to its final teardown.

## Sunshine for a Knitwear Mill

The customer was a knitwear factory operated by Bleyle of America Inc. STEP supplied a large part of its electricity, displacing the fossil fuels that would normally have generated that power. The system also provided process heat for absorption air conditioning inside the building, and steam, drawn off downstream from the turbine, for pressing the finished knitwear. The factory itself was built to sip energy. It was kept low to reduce wall area and interior volume, oriented north to south, wrapped in a four-foot insulating earthen berm, and fitted with heavily insulated walls and roof, high-efficiency fluorescent lighting, efficient production machinery and air conditioning with an economizer cycle. According to Georgia Power, which monitored the plant's energy needs as part of the design process, those conservation features alone cut the factory's energy consumption by 46 percent. STEP was then sized to the factory's established, relatively constant peak loads.

## Pines in the Collector Field

Georgia Power took over full responsibility for commercial operation in 1984 and kept the project running beyond 1987. By 1991, STEP had been shut down and dismantled. The five-plus-acre site sits on Solar Circle, along Amlajack Boulevard, in a commercial park that was once part of the planned community of Shenandoah and is now part of the city of Newnan. The fenced collector field remains, slowly surrendering to the pines. Between the old field and the boulevard, an education center at 7 Solar Circle once housed the University of West Georgia's Newnan Center, which taught classes on ground where sunlight once ran a factory before the university moved its Newnan campus to a renovated historic hospital on West Jackson Street downtown.

## Flight Context

The former STEP site is at 33.4044 N, 84.7472 W in the former Shenandoah planned community, now part of Newnan, Georgia, southwest of Atlanta. Look for a still-fenced rectangle of old pavement with pine trees growing through it, beside a commercial park on Solar Circle and Amlajack Boulevard. It is small, so 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL gives the best chance of picking it out. Newnan Coweta County Airport (FAA identifier CCO) is about 6 nm away and Atlanta Regional Airport-Falcon Field (formerly Peachtree City Falcon Field; FAA identifier FFC) about 9 nm to the east-southeast. Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) is about 21 nm to the northeast, so expect busy Atlanta airspace. Best viewed in clear weather with the sun high enough to light the enclosure.
