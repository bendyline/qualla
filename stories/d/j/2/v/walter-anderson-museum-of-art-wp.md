# Walter Anderson Museum of Art

In Ocean Springs, Mississippi, a museum built in the style of the family's Shearwater compound gathers the work of reclusive Gulf Coast painter Walter Inglis Anderson and his brothers, including the secret mural room found after his death.

**Location:** 30.4105, -88.8275

## Introduction

After Walter Anderson died in 1965, a locked room in his cottage at Shearwater was opened, and its walls were found alive with paint. Around the small space, he had painted a single day on the Gulf Coast, beginning with sunrise on the east wall and moving through noon, sunset, and night. It was the most private work he ever made. Today that room, known as the Little Room, has been installed at the Walter Anderson Museum of Art in Ocean Springs, where anyone can step inside a vision Anderson kept to himself.

## The Recluse of Shearwater

Walter Inglis Anderson, who lived from 1903 to 1965, painted the plants, animals, landscapes, and people of the Mississippi coast so distinctively that he is counted among the most singular artists of the twentieth century. From the mid-1940s until his death he lived a reclusive life at Shearwater, the family's Ocean Springs compound, working as a decorator at the family pottery. He also made frequent trips in a rowboat, sometimes rigged with a sail, out to Horn Island, where he lived in primitive conditions and recorded the life around him, from birds and sea creatures to trees and landscapes, in radiant watercolors and a series of logbooks.

## Three Brothers

The museum does not tell Walter's story alone. It is also dedicated to his brothers: Peter Anderson, who lived from 1901 to 1984 and was a potter and the founder of Shearwater Pottery, and James McConnell Anderson, from 1907 to 1998, a painter and ceramist. Together, the three brothers make the museum a portrait of a remarkable creative family rooted in one coastal town. Its mission, in its own words, is to "empower lifelong curiosity and connection to the natural world through the art of Walter Anderson and kindred artists."

## Built by Friends

The museum began as an idea shared by a group of people in Ocean Springs and Jackson who wanted to preserve Anderson's art and culture. The Friends of Walter Anderson was chartered in 1974, and through their efforts, funds and grant money were raised to build a museum on Washington Avenue in Ocean Springs, on land leased from Jackson County. Mark A. Tullos Jr. became its first director in 1990, and the museum was dedicated on May 4, 1991, at an estimated cost of $1.3 million. Its conceptual architect, Edward Pickard, the former husband of Anderson's eldest child, Mary Anderson, designed the building in keeping with the style of the Shearwater compound, so that the museum itself echoes the place where the art was made.

## Public Walls, Private Walls

The museum links Anderson's most public project with his most private one. The Ocean Springs Community Center, now part of the museum, holds some 3,000 square feet of murals that Anderson painted there. On one wall he depicted the landing of the seventeenth-century French explorer Pierre Le Moyne d'Iberville at Ocean Springs. On the opposite wall he painted what he called the Seven Climates, casting the Gulf Coast as a small world containing all of them. Those murals have inspired new art of their own. Since 2016, Mississippi hill country blues musician Luther Dickinson has staged nearly annual performances of music inspired by the Seven Climates of Ocean Springs in the Community Center, letting Anderson's colors echo in sound.

## Flight Context

The Walter Anderson Museum of Art stands at 30.4105°N, 88.8275°W on Washington Avenue in Ocean Springs, Mississippi, a city about two miles east of Biloxi. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, Biloxi and the coast make easy references for finding Ocean Springs. Horn Island, the long, thin barrier island where Anderson painted, lies about 13 nm to the southeast, and a flight out over the water retraces the journey he made by rowboat. Keesler Air Force Base (KBIX) is only about 5 nm west, so observe its airspace, and Gulfport–Biloxi International Airport (KGPT) lies about 13 nm west. Trent Lott International Airport (KPQL) at Pascagoula is about 16 nm east-northeast.
