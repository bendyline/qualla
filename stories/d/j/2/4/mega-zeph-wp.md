# Mega Zeph

Jazzland's signature wooden coaster ran for barely five years before Hurricane Katrina flooded its park, then stood rotting over eastern New Orleans for nearly twenty years, with one last turn as a movie prop, until it came down in 2025.

**Location:** 30.0532, -89.9375

**Categories:** Former roller coasters in Louisiana, Six Flags New Orleans, Roller coasters closed in 2005, Hurricane Katrina, Abandoned places

## Introduction

For the cameras, it came back to life one more time. When a film crew arrived to shoot Percy Jackson: Sea of Monsters, Mega Zeph had sat unused since Hurricane Katrina flooded its park in August 2005. The crew lined the old coaster with lights and resurrected it just enough for a car to zoom in and out of frame. On screen, the abandoned Six Flags New Orleans became Circeland, a park built by the goddess Circe and destroyed by the cyclops Polyphemus. Off screen, the ride went on decaying in eastern New Orleans until it was finally torn down in 2025.

## Jazzland's Big Hill

Mega Zeph borrowed its name from an older local legend, the Zephyr, or Big Zephyr, a coaster that operated at the now-defunct Pontchartrain Beach amusement park. The new ride celebrated its topping out on September 10, 1999, when the underpinnings of its highest hump were installed, and it opened on May 20, 2000, as the signature ride of Jazzland, on the same day as the park's grand opening. Its construction was unusual: a steel structure carrying a wooden track. Riders left the station and climbed a 110-foot lift hill to the first drop, then made a quick descent toward a high-speed turnaround near the lake's edge.

## Rides That Got Away

Katrina's floodwaters shut the park, by then Six Flags New Orleans, in August 2005, and it never reopened. In 2007, Six Flags began removing rides to other parks. Batman: The Ride went first, to Six Flags Fiesta Texas, where it reopened as Goliath on April 18, 2008. Bayou Blaster and Sonic Slam went to the Great Escape in Queensbury, New York, reopening as Sasquatch on May 10, 2009. The Road Runner Express left last, removed in 2009 and reopened at Six Flags Magic Mountain under its old name on May 28, 2011. Mega Zeph stayed behind. Its only train was sent to Six Flags St. Louis, where it went into storage beneath that park's Batman: The Ride.

## A Slow Collapse

Left to the weather, the coaster came apart piece by piece. Most of its wood decayed and its steel rusted, and a large section of track fell to the ground entirely. Its brief return to the spotlight came in the middle of that decline. Percy Jackson: Sea of Monsters, released in the United States on August 7, 2013, used several of the dead park's rides, including Ozarka Splash, with Mega Zeph lit up for its cameo. Some of it was saved: the National Roller Coaster Museum and Archives preserved the neon sign from the ride's entrance at its facility in Plainview, Texas. The end came quickly in 2025. On January 18, a section of the coaster caught fire as demolition crews cut steel, though the blaze posed no threat to public safety, and by February 2 Mega Zeph had been torn down. It had carried riders for a little more than five years, then stood for nearly twenty more after its park closed.

## Flight Context

The former Six Flags New Orleans site lies at approximately 30.05 N, 89.94 W in eastern New Orleans, on flat, low-lying ground near the south shore of Lake Pontchartrain. With the coaster gone, look from 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL for the broad footprint of the former park amid the surrounding development. Lakefront Airport (KNEW) is about 5 nm west, making this a quick sightseeing hop along the lakeshore; Louis Armstrong New Orleans International (KMSY) is about 17 nm west-southwest, beneath the New Orleans Class B airspace, and New Orleans NAS JRB/Alvin Callender Field (KNBG), a military field, is about 15 nm south-southwest. Expect afternoon thunderstorms in summer and hazy, humid conditions much of the year.
