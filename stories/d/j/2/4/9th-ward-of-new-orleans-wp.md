# 9th Ward of New Orleans

New Orleans' largest ward stretches from the Mississippi to Lake Pontchartrain, home to Fats Domino, Ruby Bridges' first school day and the Lower Ninth, where Katrina's floodwaters hit hardest.

**Location:** 30.0470, -89.8850

**Categories:** Wards of New Orleans, Neighborhoods in New Orleans, Hurricane Katrina, Civil rights movement, African-American history of Louisiana

## Introduction

On Fat Tuesday 2006, hundreds of people gathered near the Florida housing project in New Orleans' Ninth Ward. Six months after Hurricane Katrina, the neighborhoods around them were still devastated, and for many residents this was their first trip back. They came anyway, in the spirit of a jazz funeral, and threw a massive block party in the streets where they used to live. It was mourning and celebration at once, the kind of gathering that makes sense in a place that has flooded, rebuilt and kept its music again and again.

## From River to Lake

The Ninth Ward is the easternmost, downriver end of New Orleans and the largest, by area, of the city's 17 wards. It runs from the Mississippi River north to Lake Pontchartrain, with St. Bernard Parish and Lake Borgne to the southeast and the Rigolets at the parish's far eastern edge. The name dates from 1852, when the city's three municipalities were merged into one government and the wards were redrawn. Settlement began along the riverfront in the early 19th century, then spread to the natural high ground of Gentilly Ridge; most of the swamp between the ridge and the lake was not drained until the mid and late 20th century. Along the lakefront, fishing camps stood on piers, and the Little Woods camps lasted longest, many surviving until Hurricane Georges in 1998. In the early 1920s the Industrial Canal was dredged straight through the ward, splitting it into the Upper and Lower Ninth.

## Holy Cross to Desire

Below the canal, along the river, the Lower Ninth holds the Holy Cross neighborhood, the twin Doullut Steamboat Houses and Jackson Barracks. Until Katrina it had the highest percentage of Black homeownership in the city. Above the canal lie Bywater, St. Claude, Florida and Desire, once home to the Desire and Florida housing projects, both since demolished. Nearby, the old Agriculture Street Landfill was covered over and built up as low-income housing, then became a Superfund toxic cleanup site. Under segregation, Black New Orleanians went to Lincoln Beach, a lakefront amusement park, while whites went to Pontchartrain Beach. The ward's roll of natives and residents includes Fats Domino, trumpeter Kermit Ruffins, the Batiste musical family, NFL player Marshall Faulk and actor John Larroquette. In 1960, Ruby Bridges became the first Black student at the all-white William Frantz Elementary School here, launching the New Orleans school desegregation crisis; the school now honors her with a statue and has preserved her classroom.

## Water Through the Walls

Floods were not new. In the 1915 hurricane, a levee break at Florida Avenue gave the area behind St. Claude Avenue some of the city's most significant and longest-standing flooding, parts of the ward flooded in Hurricane Flossy in 1956, and Hurricane Betsy brought catastrophic flooding to the Lower Ninth in 1965. Katrina in 2005 was worse still. Storm surge caused most of the damage, pouring through multiple severe levee breaks along the Mississippi River Gulf Outlet and the Industrial Canal. In the Lower Ninth, two large breaches in the canal's flood protection unleashed currents so violent they smashed houses and pushed them off their foundations, and floodwaters carried the barge ING 4727 over into the neighborhood. Only the old high ground of Bywater, on the river side of St. Claude Avenue, escaped significant flooding. The disaster thrust the ward into the national spotlight, though live coverage by reporters unfamiliar with the city often muddled it, calling the Lower Ninth simply "the 9th Ward." A month later, Hurricane Rita flooded the Lower Ninth again before it had even dried out.

## An Uneven Return

Recovery has come in patches. Streets neglected for years before the storm, including St. Claude and Poland avenues, Chartres Street and parts of Desire Street, have been resurfaced, while many smaller neighborhood roads remain a patchwork of potholes, dips and humps. In New Orleans East, the largest part of the ward, the Vietnamese community has built a dining destination and commercial hub, even as Vietnamese and other fishermen farther down the parish suffered from the 2010 Deepwater Horizon oil spill. And on the ward's eastern side, NASA's Michoud Assembly Facility, which laid off workers in 2010 as the space shuttle fleet retired, still provides jobs as the place where the core stage of the Space Launch System is fabricated.

## Flight Context

The coordinates (30.047 N, 89.885 W) fall in the eastern reaches of the ward in New Orleans East; the ward as a whole runs from the Mississippi River north to Lake Pontchartrain. From 2,000-3,000 feet the key references are the Industrial Canal cutting north-south between the Upper and Lower Ninth, the Gulf Intracoastal Waterway and Mississippi River Gulf Outlet channel running east from the canal, the lakeshore to the north, and the large Michoud Assembly Facility complex in the east. New Orleans Lakefront Airport (KNEW) is about 7.5 nm west on the lakeshore, Louis Armstrong New Orleans International (KMSY) about 20 nm west, and Slidell Airport (KASD) about 18 nm north across the lake. Check New Orleans Class B airspace and coordinate with Lakefront tower when near KNEW. Expect summer thunderstorms, low haze over the water and the June-November hurricane season.
