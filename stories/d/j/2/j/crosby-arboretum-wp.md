# Crosby Arboretum

Created by a family to honor their father, Mississippi State University's Crosby Arboretum in Picayune protects the native plant life of the Pearl River basin across a 64-acre interpretive center and seven natural areas, and is home to E. Fay Jones's acclaimed Pinecote Pavilion.

**Location:** 30.5036, -89.6662

**Categories:** Arboreta in Mississippi, Botanical gardens in Mississippi, Protected areas of Pearl River County, Mississippi, Mississippi State University, Nature reserves in Mississippi

## Introduction

On a sloping bog along Dead Tiger Creek, pitcher plants wait for insects. Orchids grow among them, along with most of the state's species of holly, in a savanna set aside to be studied and protected. It is one of seven wild tracts that belong to the Crosby Arboretum, a Picayune institution built on a simple, generous idea: that a father could be honored by saving the native landscape of a region.

## A Family's Tribute

The arboretum began as a family project. Lynn Crosby Gammill, her husband Stewart Gammill, and her brother Osmond Crosby set it in motion to honor their father, L. O. Crosby Jr., who lived from 1907 to 1978. Their aim was to conserve the region's biological diversity and to showcase the plants native to the Pearl River drainage basin. Today the arboretum is affiliated with Mississippi State University. It covers about 64 acres at Pinecote, its interpretive center in Picayune (the university's own site counts the whole center at 104 acres), plus about 700 acres in seven additional natural areas, and together these lands shelter over 300 species of indigenous plants.

## Pinecote

The heart of the arboretum is Pinecote, the 64-acre interpretive center, carefully designed by the consultants Andropogon Associates with site master planner Edward L. Blake Jr. Walking routes lead visitors through 20 acres of biologically enhanced savanna exhibits, 40 acres of woodland succession, and nearly four acres of created waterways, so that a single visit moves through several of the region's landscapes in miniature. The design has won high honors. It received a 1991 Honor Award from the American Society of Landscape Architects, followed by an ASLA Medallion Award in 1999.

## A Pavilion in the Pines

Rising within Pinecote is the Pinecote Pavilion, the work of the late architect E. Fay Jones. Jones had been an apprentice to Frank Lloyd Wright and carried many of Wright's design principles into his own buildings, and his pavilion is counted among the architectural jewels of Mississippi. The American Institute of Architects gave it an Honor Award in 1990, and the landscape architects honored the grounds around it the next year, so that building and setting were both recognized.

## Seven Wild Places

Beyond Pinecote, the seven natural areas in Pearl River, Hancock, and Lamar counties were chosen for the diversity of their vegetation and are preserved and managed for research. Each has its own character. The 70-acre Hillside Bog in northern Hancock County mixes bog with longleaf pine and scrub oak. Dead Tiger Creek Hammock is a low hardwood swamp home to species such as Coreopsis nudata and Macranthera flammea. Red Bluff spreads across 320 acres along Catahoula Creek, with a clear-water stream, sandy white beaches, oxbow lakes, and titi-lined banks. At Talowah, 120 acres of longleaf pine ridges are maintained by periodic burning. Mill Creek's mature beech-magnolia woods hold all five magnolia species found in the state, and Steep Hollow shelters quaking bogs among its longleaf slopes.

## Flight Context

The Crosby Arboretum's Pinecote interpretive center lies at 30.5036°N, 89.6662°W in Picayune, Pearl River County, Mississippi. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the savanna and woodland of the 64-acre grounds. Picayune Municipal Airport (MJD) is only about 1 nm southeast, making this an easy local sightseeing loop. Stennis International Airport (HSA) lies about 14 nm southeast and Slidell Airport (ASD) about 12 nm southwest. Clear days give the best view of the grounds.
