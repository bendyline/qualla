# I-10 Twin Span Bridge

Hurricane Katrina tore dozens of concrete segments off the twin bridges carrying I-10 across Lake Pontchartrain; their replacements now bear the name of a New Orleans TV personality who often fished nearby, while pieces of the old spans live on as a fishing pier and a reef.

**Location:** 30.1826, -89.8249

**Categories:** Bridges in New Orleans, Road bridges in Louisiana, Interstate 10, Hurricane Katrina, Slidell, Louisiana, Trestle bridges in the United States

## Introduction

When the water went down after Hurricane Katrina, sixty-four pieces of the Twin Span were simply gone. The storm surge of August 29, 2005, had pulled or shifted concrete segments off their piers along both parallel bridges that carry Interstate 10 across the eastern end of Lake Pontchartrain, from New Orleans to Slidell. The spans that cross those nearly 5.5 miles today are replacements, finished in the years that followed. The originals have not entirely disappeared, though. Part of them was kept as a fishing pier, closed since Hurricane Ida damaged it, and debris from the rest lines the lake floor as a reef.

## Four Hundred Thirty-Three Segments

The original bridges opened at a short ceremony on December 21, 1965. Each was built from 433 concrete segments, each 65 feet long, and each carried two lanes of traffic. For most of their length the spans rode low over the water, with a clearance of just 8.5 feet, rising to 65 feet at the navigation channel so boats could pass beneath. For four decades the paired spans carried Interstate 10 traffic across the lake on those low concrete trestles, segment after identical segment, until the hurricane of 2005 tested how firmly those segments sat on their piers.

## Pulled From the Piers

Katrina's surge left the eastbound span missing 38 segments, with another 170 knocked out of alignment. The westbound span lost 26 segments, and 265 more were misaligned. With the Twin Span broken and U.S. Route 90 to the east also damaged, only one road remained into New Orleans from the east: the U.S. Route 11 bridge just west of the Twin Spans. The Louisiana Department of Transportation and Development moved quickly. On September 9, 2005, it accepted a $30.9 million bid from Boh Brothers Construction Company to reconstruct the bridges. The first goal was to restore two-way traffic on the eastbound span within 45 days, and crews did it by salvaging segments from the westbound span to fill the gaps. On October 14, 2005, the eastbound span reopened with one lane in each direction.

## A Life Lost Above the Lake

Permanent replacement spans rose in the second half of the decade, and the work came at a cost. On October 30, 2008, as construction workers were preparing a form for a concrete pour, a girder collapsed and sent 10 workers plunging into Lake Pontchartrain, about 30 feet below. One of them, Eric Troy Blackmon, was tethered to the girder, and he died in the accident. The first new span opened to eastbound traffic on July 9, 2009. On April 7, 2010, the second span opened and the old Twin Spans were closed to traffic for good. After a ribbon-cutting on September 8, 2011, for the westbound approaches, all six lanes opened the next morning, with traffic cameras and electronic message boards to warn drivers of problems ahead.

## A Pier, a Reef and a Fisherman's Name

The original bridges were demolished shortly after the new ones opened, but not every piece was hauled away. A short section near the northern end was preserved as a public fishing pier in Slidell, though the pier has been closed since extensive damage from Hurricane Ida in 2021 and St. Tammany Parish is building a new one, expected by early 2027. Debris from the demolition was used to line the nearby lake bottom as an underwater reef. On August 1, 2014, the new crossing was officially named the Frank Davis "Naturally N'Awlins" Memorial Bridge. It honors Frank Joseph Davis, the former WWL-TV news personality who died in 2013 and who often fished near the bridge. Anglers who cast from the old spans stood on the same concrete that once carried the interstate.

## Flight Context

The Twin Span's midpoint lies at approximately 30.18 N, 89.82 W over the eastern end of Lake Pontchartrain, between eastern New Orleans to the south and Slidell to the north. From 1,500 to 3,000 feet AGL the two parallel trestles are unmistakable, running nearly 5.5 miles across open water, with the old fishing-pier remnant near the northern end and the U.S. Route 11 bridge just to the west. Slidell Airport (KASD) is about 10 nm north, Lakefront Airport (KNEW) about 13.5 nm southwest, and St. Tammany Regional Airport at Covington (FAA identifier L31; no ICAO code) about 18 nm north-northwest. Watch for summer thunderstorms building over the lake and for haze and low visibility on humid mornings.
