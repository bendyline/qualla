# Biloxi Lighthouse

Kept for decades by a mother and daughter, Biloxi's cast-iron lighthouse has leaned, gone dark, been repainted, flooded and restored, and still carries blue lines marking every great storm surge up to Katrina's.

**Location:** 30.3946, -88.9015

**Categories:** Lighthouses on the National Register of Historic Places in Mississippi, Buildings and structures in Biloxi, Mississippi, Lighthouses completed in 1848, Mississippi Landmarks, Civil rights history

## Introduction

Several feet of water stood in the room where Maria Younghans lived, and still the light kept burning. When a hurricane struck Biloxi on October 1, 1893, washing away the seawall and threatening the tower, the New Orleans Daily Picayune took note of "the plucky woman who was in charge of the light," who "kept a light going all through the storm." Her lighthouse still stands beside the Mississippi Sound, a tower described as having been kept by women for more years than any other lighthouse in the United States.

## An Iron Tower in the Sand

The tower was completed and placed in operation in 1848, a cast-iron structure roughly 60 feet tall (published heights range from 45 to 64 feet) with brick foundation and backing. Its footing proved less steady than its walls. During a storm in 1860, part of the sand beneath it eroded and the lighthouse began to lean, and it was straightened by removing sand from the opposite side. On June 18, 1861, local authorities ordered the light extinguished. It was repaired and back in service by November 15, 1866. Reports at that time said the tower had been painted with coal tar to protect it from rust, not, as has since been claimed, to mourn the assassination of President Abraham Lincoln. The coating was for protection, not grief.

## Mother and Daughter

Perry Younghans was appointed keeper on November 14, 1866, but fell ill soon afterward, and his wife, Maria, took over tending the light. After he died, she was named keeper herself on December 6, 1867. Her early years tested her. In 1868 the tower was painted white, and a hurricane that year nearly brought it down. The old keeper's house was razed and rebuilt in 1880, and in 1893 came the storm that flooded her quarters while the lamp stayed lit. Maria Younghans retired on December 31, 1918, after more than half a century at the light. Her daughter, Miranda, succeeded her and remained keeper until 1929.

## A Wade-In on the Beach

The sand at the foot of the tower holds another kind of history. In April 1960, the beach near the light was the site of a "wade-in," a demonstration against the segregation of Mississippi's beaches. Fifty years later, in 2010, a civil rights historical marker was placed at the lighthouse to commemorate the demonstration. The tower that has long guided ships along the coast now also marks the place where people demanded equal access to the water in front of it.

## Blue Lines on the Wall

The lighthouse has survived a score of hurricanes, including the great storms of 1947 and 1969, when Hurricane Camille destroyed the keeper's house. Inside, blue lines painted on the wall mark historic storm surges above mean sea level. The hurricanes of 1855 and 1906 reached 14 feet, a 1909 storm crested at 15 feet, and Camille set a record of 17.5 feet. On August 29, 2005, Hurricane Katrina broke that record with a surge of 21.5 feet, and the tower stood through it. Restoration costing more than $400,000 brought rewiring, rebuilt interior brickwork, new exterior lights and a restored decorative fence, and the light was relit on February 19, 2010. Owned by the City of Biloxi and operated as a private aid to navigation, it appeared on Mississippi license plates from 2007 to 2012, recognized as a landmark of the Gulf Coast. In August 2026 the city began the lighthouse's first major rehabilitation since Katrina, replacing the upper deck and catwalk decking and removing rust from the lantern room, and tours to the top were paused until the work is finished.

## Flight Context

Biloxi Lighthouse stands at approximately 30.39 N, 88.90 W on the beachfront of Biloxi, Mississippi, facing the Mississippi Sound, at the intersection of Beach Boulevard and Porter Avenue. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the slender tower standing between the beachfront road and the sand along Biloxi's long, straight shoreline. Keesler Air Force Base (KBIX) is only about 1.5 nm northwest, so its airspace must be respected; contact or avoid as required. Gulfport-Biloxi International Airport (KGPT) is about 9 nm west, and Ocean Springs Airport (FAA identifier 5R2; no ICAO code) about 8 nm east. Summer afternoons bring thunderstorms and haze, and the coast is exposed to tropical weather in hurricane season.
