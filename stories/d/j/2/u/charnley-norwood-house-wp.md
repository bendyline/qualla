# Charnley–Norwood House

Louis Sullivan dashed off a pair of Gulf Coast vacation cottages in 1890 that he called "two shacks"; the one he built for a Chicago lumber baron survives in Ocean Springs as an early landmark of the Prairie School.

**Location:** 30.3955, -88.8090

**Categories:** Louis Sullivan buildings, Frank Lloyd Wright buildings, Prairie School architecture in Mississippi, Houses in Jackson County, Mississippi, Houses completed in 1890

## Introduction

Louis Sullivan is remembered as the "father of the skyscraper," but on the Mississippi Gulf Coast he designed for sea breezes rather than skylines. In March 1890, by his own account, he quickly drew up what he called "two shacks" for a pair of adjoining gulf-side lots in Ocean Springs, one for himself and one for his friend James Charnley, a wealthy Chicago lumber baron. Sullivan's own bungalow is gone, destroyed by Hurricane Katrina. Charnley's survives, and its style represents an important turn in American residential architecture toward what became known as the Prairie School.

## Two Shacks by the Water

Sullivan was a partner in the Chicago firm of Adler & Sullivan when he and Charnley bought their neighboring properties to build vacation homes. Once the plans were ready, they were handed to a local carpenter to build. The "shack" that resulted was a cottage of roughly 3,000 square feet, set with an octagonal guest cottage on an acre of beachfront along East Beach Drive. It was built as a vacation residence for Charnley, and the house was designed around that purpose, from its sheltering roof to its fireplaces.

## A House That Breathes

The Mississippi summer is muggy and hot, and the house answers it with shade and air. Large roof overhangs and covered porches on the south, west and east sides guard the rooms from the harsh southern sun while opening them to the setting. Numerous doors and operable windows line the exterior, arranged to catch both onshore and offshore breezes and move them through the house. For cooler winter days, there are fireplaces in each bedroom, the entry hall and the dining room. Although the design was strikingly different from typical 19th-century houses, its forms later became hallmarks of modern architecture. The home represents a watershed in residential design, offering historians insight into the forces that powerfully reshaped American houses in the 20th century.

## Whose Hand Drew It?

The house is often credited to both Sullivan and Frank Lloyd Wright, often called the "father of modern architecture." Wright was a young draftsman in Sullivan's office at the time, but there is little evidence that he actually had a hand in the design, and he is said never to have visited the site. Sullivan's autobiography recalls designing the cottages himself. Even so, by one published count of Wright's work, it is among only four homes he designed in Mississippi, out of 532 homes, museums and office buildings he built in his lifetime. Storms have taken two of them. Hurricane Camille destroyed the Welbie L. Fuller Residence in Pass Christian, and Katrina destroyed the Louis Sullivan Bungalow next door in Ocean Springs. Fountainhead in Jackson and the Charnley–Norwood House remain. Charnley–Norwood is the one open to the public today; the Mississippi Museum of Art, which bought Fountainhead in 2025, plans to open it after a restoration.

## Weathering the Storms

A fire damaged the original house in 1897, and it was rebuilt. Hurricane Katrina's surge in 2005 destroyed Sullivan's neighboring cottage and knocked this one off its piers, leaving it so badly damaged that it was slated for demolition. It survived: the state stabilized it from 2008, bought it in 2011 and restored it. The property is now managed by the Mississippi Gulf Coast National Heritage Area, a program designation through the National Park Service that recognizes the conservation and preservation of historic places, under the state's Department of Marine Resources. The house is open weekly for tours and hosts cultural and artistic events, so visitors can walk the porches Sullivan shaded more than a century ago.

## Flight Context

The Charnley–Norwood House sits at approximately 30.40 N, 88.81 W on the beachfront of Ocean Springs, Mississippi, facing the Mississippi Sound on the east side of Biloxi Bay. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow the Ocean Springs shoreline east from the mouth of the bay along East Beach Drive, where the house stands on its acre of beachfront. Ocean Springs Airport (FAA identifier 5R2; no ICAO code) is about 3 nm east. Keesler Air Force Base (KBIX) lies about 6 nm west across the bay, so respect its military airspace, and Gulfport-Biloxi International Airport (KGPT) is about 13.5 nm west. Trent Lott International Airport (KPQL) at Pascagoula is about 15 nm east-northeast. Expect summer thunderstorms and humid haze along the coast.
