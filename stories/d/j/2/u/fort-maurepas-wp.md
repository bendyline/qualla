# Fort Maurepas

Before Mobile and New Orleans, the seat of French Louisiana was a temporary fort built in 1699 on the shore at present-day Ocean Springs; the original, long abandoned, is said to have burned in 1719, and its replica fell to Hurricane Katrina.

**Location:** 30.4056, -88.8292

**Categories:** Louisiana (New France), French forts in the United States, Colonial forts in Mississippi, Parks in Mississippi, 1699 establishments in New France

## Introduction

Before Mobile, and long before New Orleans, the French colony of Louisiana was governed from a fort on the shore of what is now Ocean Springs, Mississippi. Fort Maurepas, later known as Old Biloxi, was developed along the Gulf of Mexico in April 1699 and completed on May 1 of that year. The French colony of La Louisiane began here. Over the next two decades the colonial capital would leave this shore, return to it and leave again, before finally settling on the Mississippi River at New Orleans.

## Iberville's Foothold

The fort was the work of the expedition led by Pierre Le Moyne d'Iberville. After entering the Mississippi's Birdfoot Delta in March 1699, he built a temporary fort at present-day Ocean Springs, left a small garrison under Sauvolle (one account counts 76 men and 10 officers, another about 100 people in all) and sailed back to France in May. Fort Maurepas was designated as the temporary capital of Louisiana that same year. The colony it anchored was part of New France, known in colonial times simply as La Louisiane. In modern times it is called La Louisiane française to distinguish it from the modern state of Louisiana, which bears the same name in French.

## A Capital That Would Not Stay Put

The colony's early records are tangled, and documents from the period often conflict on dates or compress events into simpler stories. In broad outline, the capital moved to Mobile in 1702, and Mobile itself was relocated in 1711. In 1719, during the War of the Quadruple Alliance against Spain, the administrative capital returned to the Biloxi area, as hurricanes and shifting sandbars blocked harbor waters in the early 18th century. Fort Maurepas, abandoned years earlier, is said to have burned in 1719, and the French never rebuilt it. By one account, de l'Épinay and Bienville had already chosen, around 1717, to use the harbor at Ship Island and ordered a new fort built on the mainland opposite, one league west of Old Biloxi across Biloxi Bay. The transport ship Dauphine, commanded by M. Berranger, brought many carpenters and masons, and they raised New Biloxi, also called Fort Louis, while another fort and magazines went up on Ship Island itself.

## To Deeper Water

Biloxi's turn as capital did not last. Between June and August 1722, Governor Bienville moved the capital again, to the deeper waters of the Mississippi River at La Nouvelle-Orléans, a harbor town built for the purpose between 1718 and 1722. Some documents give 1723 as the year New Orleans became the capital, but that was its first full year in the role. Biloxi's name endured in the colony's administration. When the Province of Louisiana was later divided into nine military districts, Biloxi was one of them, alongside Natchez, Yazoo, Alabama, Mobile, New Orleans, Illinois, Arkansas and Natchitoches, most of them named for local Native American peoples.

## Replica, Storm and Park

The original fort site is said to lie on private property. In the 20th century, a replica of the wooden fort was built on Front Beach, about a mile away, and opened to the public. Hurricane Katrina badly damaged it on August 29, 2005. A park opened on the site in October 2009, with a playground, a splash pad, a stage, public restrooms and a two-story structure with a viewing platform overlooking the beach. The work also improved the beach pathways, the sailors memorial, crosswalks and fire pits, and the park connects to parking on the opposite side of the Biloxi Bay Bridge. More than three centuries after Iberville's garrison settled in, the shore near where French Louisiana began is once again a place to stand and look out over the water.

## Flight Context

Fort Maurepas Park lies at approximately 30.41 N, 88.83 W on Front Beach in Ocean Springs, Mississippi, at the eastern end of the Biloxi Bay Bridge. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL the bridge across the mouth of Biloxi Bay is the key landmark, with Biloxi to the west and the Ocean Springs shoreline running east; Ship Island, whose harbor the French used, lies offshore to the south. Keesler Air Force Base (KBIX) is about 5 nm west, so respect its military airspace. Ocean Springs Airport (FAA identifier 5R2; no ICAO code) is about 4 nm east, Gulfport-Biloxi International Airport (KGPT) about 12.5 nm west, and Trent Lott International Airport (KPQL) at Pascagoula about 16 nm east-northeast. Expect summer thunderstorms and haze, and tropical weather risk in hurricane season.
