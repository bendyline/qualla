# Cat Island (Mississippi)

Named by French explorers who mistook its raccoons for cats, this T-shaped barrier island off the Mississippi coast has pine forests, alligator bayous, a troubling World War II dog-training history, and a shoreline steadily reshaped by storms.

**Location:** 30.2266, -89.1031

**Categories:** Barrier islands of Mississippi, Landforms of Harrison County, Mississippi, Gulf Islands National Seashore, Beaches of Mississippi

## Introduction

The French explorers who first named this island were looking at raccoons. Raccoons are native to the Americas and were not established in Europe until the twentieth century, so the animals looked unfamiliar, and the explorers took them for cats. The name stuck. Cat Island still sits off the Gulf Coast of Mississippi, one of the Mississippi–Alabama barrier islands, though nobody knows who first discovered it.

## An Island Shaped Like a T

Cat Island is unusual among its neighbors. Colliding currents in the Gulf of Mexico built it into a distinctive T shape, and unlike the other Mississippi islands, its sand beaches are backed by dense forests of slash pine and live oak. Behind the trees lie bayous and marshes that are home to alligators and a refuge for migratory birds. The island is also fighting a slow battle. Dredging of the Gulfport ship channel cut off the natural westward flow of sediment that once supplied fresh material to replace what erosion carried away, and storms have taken their toll. In 2005, Hurricane Katrina eliminated the island's southern tip and destroyed every home on the island.

## Settlers and Owners

People came to Cat Island in the eighteenth century, when it became home to a number of settlers. The first to acquire a land grant was Nicolas Christian Ladner, and in 1837 his son-in-law sold the island. Ownership has been shifting ever since. The western half and southern tip became part of Gulf Islands National Seashore in 2002, while the rest, including most of the beach, stayed in private hands. After the 2010 Deepwater Horizon oil spill, BP bought 492 acres of the eastern beach from the Boddie family in April 2011 to help with the cleanup. In December 2016, the State of Mississippi acquired that land and added the East Beach section to the Cat Island Coastal Preserve.

## The War Dogs of Cat Island

During World War II, the island became the Cat Island War Dog Reception and Training Center. American families sent their own dogs here to be trained for military service. Part of the program rested on a false and racist premise. Dogs were trained to find and attack people of Japanese ethnicity, for possible use against Japanese forces in the Pacific, based on the belief that such people had a distinct odor. Japanese American soldiers of the 100th Infantry Battalion served as the live targets, hiding in the brush and often getting hurt despite their padding. That experiment failed completely, but the dogs did prove genuinely useful as aids to sentries and advance scouts. The episode, a small but revealing chapter in the wartime suspicion aimed at Japanese Americans, was featured in a June 2009 episode of the PBS series History Detectives.

## Wild by Design

Visiting Cat Island takes some planning. The island has no facilities at all: no staff, no drinking water, no shelter, and no way to communicate once you are there. It can be reached only by private boat, and the National Park Service asks anyone who plans to camp on the national seashore portion to contact a ranger about wilderness area regulations first. Fires are allowed only below the mean high tide line, away from vegetation, and campers must bring and take away their own firewood. For those who make the trip, the reward is a barrier island where pines meet the sand, alligators hold the bayous, and the name still honors a case of mistaken identity.

## Flight Context

Cat Island lies at about 30.2266°N, 89.1031°W in the Gulf of Mexico off the Mississippi coast, within Harrison County. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for its distinctive T shape and the dark band of pine and oak forest behind the beaches, a contrast that sets it apart from the other Mississippi barrier islands. Gulfport–Biloxi International Airport (KGPT) is about 11 nm north, and Keesler Air Force Base (KBIX) about 14 nm northeast, so coordinate with Gulfport approach and respect military traffic. Stennis International Airport (HSA) lies about 20 nm west-northwest. Expect over-water flying and keep an eye on Gulf weather.
