# Fernandina Beach, Florida

Florida's northernmost Atlantic city was the last town Spain ever platted in the Western Hemisphere, and its island has flown eight flags, from a Scottish adventurer's Green Cross to the Stars and Stripes.

**Location:** 30.6694, -81.4617

## Introduction

On Estrada Street in the old part of Fernandina, a house known locally as Captain Bell's House overlooks the Amelia River. In the 1988 film The New Adventures of Pippi Longstocking it played Villa Villekulla, Pippi's home. Directly across the street lies something with a far longer history: the Fernandina Plaza, once the parade ground of a Spanish fort. On Amelia Island, even a children's movie shares the frame with empires.

## The Isle of Eight Flags

Long before any flag, bands of the Timucuan mound-building culture settled the island around 1000 CE and called it Napoyca. They stayed until the early 18th century, when European settlement began. The French, English and Spanish all kept a presence on the island at different times in the 16th, 17th and 18th centuries, and the list of banners that have flown here earned Amelia Island its nickname, the "Isle of 8 Flags": France, Spain, Great Britain, Spain again (counted once), the Republic of East Florida in 1812, the Republic of the Floridas in 1817, Mexico, the Confederate States of America and the United States. It was the Spanish, though, who founded the town.

## Spain's Last Grid

On January 1, 1811, Enrique White, governor of Spain's East Florida province, named a new town in honor of King Ferdinand VII. That first Fernandina stood about a mile from the present city. On May 10, 1811, it became the last town in the Western Hemisphere platted under Spain's Laws of the Indies. It was meant as a bulwark against American expansion. It did not hold for long. In the following years the town was captured and recaptured by a succession of renegades and privateers, and the island became a prize in other people's schemes. The original town is remembered today as the Original Town of Fernandina Historic Site.

## Patriots and a Green Cross

On March 13, 1812, at the start of the Patriot War and with the approval of President James Madison and George Mathews of Georgia, insurgents calling themselves the "Patriots of Amelia Island" seized the island. They raised a Patriot flag, then replaced it with the Stars and Stripes, and American gunboats under Commodore Hugh Campbell kept control of the island. On May 15 the British brig Sappho fired on U.S. Gunboat No. 168, which had fired on the loyalist merchant ship Fernando to stop her leaving; outgunned, the gunboat withdrew, and several vessels slipped out of port. Madison eventually denounced Mathews's filibustering, saying he had violated his instructions, and Spanish pressure forced the Americans out in 1813. Spain built Fort San Carlos in 1816. The next year the Scottish soldier and adventurer Gregor MacGregor took it with just 55 musketeers, claiming the island for "the brethren of Mexico, Buenos Ayres, New Grenada and Venezuela" and raising the Green Cross of Florida.

## Fort Clinch and the Proclamation

On January 8, 1861, two days before Florida seceded, Confederate sympathizers of the Third Regiment of Florida Volunteers took over Fort Clinch, which Federal workers had abandoned partway through enlarging it. The Confederates put batteries on the island's northern end but lacked the means to fortify the fort, and in early 1862 Robert E. Lee, commanding coastal defenses in the Deep South, ordered the cannons and troops withdrawn. The order came too late. On March 3, 1862, Union forces with 28 gunboats under Commodore Samuel Dupont occupied the island and raised the American flag. In January 1863, the 1st South Carolina Volunteers, among the first regiments of formerly enslaved men recruited to fight for the Union, heard Lincoln's Emancipation Proclamation read aloud on January 1 at Camp Saxton, South Carolina. Three weeks later they sailed up the St. Marys River, on the Georgia-Florida line near Fernandina, to take on Confederate forces.

## Shrimp, Parades and Pirates

Today Fernandina Beach is the seat of Nassau County, home to 13,052 people at the 2020 census and the northernmost city on Florida's Atlantic coast, about 25 miles northeast of downtown Jacksonville. Fort Clinch is now a state park, and the city's historic places include the Fairbanks House and the Historic Nassau County Courthouse. Every year over the first weekend in May, the Isle of Eight Flags Shrimp Festival fills the town with seafood vendors, arts and crafts, antiques, live music, the Miss Shrimp Festival pageant and a parade, plus fireworks in most years (a drone show replaced them in 2026 because of a county burn ban). The local high school's teams, fittingly for an island once fought over by privateers, are the Pirates.

## Flight Context

Fernandina Beach sits at 30.6694°N, 81.4617°W on Amelia Island in Florida's far northeastern corner. From 2,000–3,000 feet, look for the Amelia River along the city's western waterfront, the Atlantic beaches on the east side of the island, and Fort Clinch State Park on the island's northern end, with the St. Marys River and the Georgia line just beyond. Fernandina Beach Municipal Airport (KFHB) is about 4 miles south of the city; Jacksonville International (KJAX) is about 18 miles southwest. Expect sea breezes and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
