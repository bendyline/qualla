# St. Marys, Georgia

A river town on Georgia's border with Florida, chartered by twenty neighbors in 1787, that has been captured, shelled and repurposed, and now launches the ferry to Cumberland Island within sight of a Trident submarine base.

## Introduction

For several years after its founding, the town on the north bank of the St. Marys River could not settle on a name. Public documents called it St. Marys, or sometimes St. Patrick's, and people who lived there often just called it "the New Town." Even the name that stuck has a disputed origin. Some say it simply borrowed the river's name, while others trace it to Santa Maria, a seventeenth-century Spanish mission on nearby Amelia Island in Florida. Either way, St. Marys grew up with its feet in the water and its gaze across the river, where Florida begins, and it has been living on that edge ever since.

## Twenty Neighbors and a Charter

The town was born in a meeting on an island. After the Revolutionary War, inhabitants of Camden County gathered on Cumberland Island and, on November 20, 1787, signed a charter for "a town on the St. Marys." Twenty charter members put their names to it. Each received four town lots and one marsh lot, the marsh lots lying outside the town on its eastern side. Their names still stand on a historical marker downtown: Isaac Wheeler, three Ashleys named Nathaniel, William and Lodowick, James and Robert Seagrove, Jonathan Bartlett, Prentis Gallup, Simeon Dillingham and the rest. The original town ran from the present waterfront to Bartlett Street and North Street and a block east of Norris Street, with two public town squares at its heart. Walking those streets today traces the outline of that first plan.

## Spanish Rivers, British Treaties

Long before the charter, the St. Marys River was Spanish ground. Spanish expeditions explored the area in the mid-sixteenth century as part of the settlement of Spanish Florida, with St. Augustine to the south serving as the established capital. For colonial Georgians, settling here only became legal after the Treaty of Paris in 1763, when Britain, having defeated France in the Seven Years' War, exchanged territory with Spain. Over the following decades the area came under British and finally American influence. That layered past explains why the name debate has never quite ended. A town founded by English-speaking Georgians in the 1780s may still carry the memory of a Spanish mission, and a river first explored by Spanish expeditions now marks the line between two states.

## Captured and Shelled

Living on a border has had its price. At the tail end of the War of 1812, in January 1815, the Battle of Fort Peter was fought near the town, at the fort on Point Peter along the St. Marys River. The British captured both the fort and the town, and they held St. Marys for a week or more (accounts differ) before withdrawing to Cumberland Island. Half a century later the guns came from the other direction: during the Civil War, the United States Navy bombarded the town's shoreside buildings. St. Marys recovered each time, and for a stretch it carried the weight of government as well: it served as the seat of Camden County from 1869 until 1923. The courthouse role eventually moved on, and today St. Marys is counted among Georgia's former county seats.

## Ferries, Festivals and Submarines

Modern St. Marys runs on water in several ways at once. It is the departure point for the passenger ferry to Cumberland Island, whose National Seashore lies to the northeast. The city borders Naval Submarine Base Kings Bay, home port for Ohio-class submarines, including fleet ballistic missile boats capable of carrying Trident missiles, and the St. Marys Submarine Museum tells that undersea story to visitors. Crooked River State Park, a waterfront park and a history walk round out the attractions. The calendar stays busy too, with Mardi Gras events, an Independence Day festival, the St. Marys Seafood Festival and a Music in the Park series. The city counted 18,256 people in the 2020 census, up from 17,121 in 2010. The climate is humid subtropical, with long, hot summers and short, mild winters, and the river is never far from anything.

## Flight Context

St. Marys sits on the north bank of the St. Marys River at 30.7206N, 81.5486W, with Florida directly across the water to the south and Kingsland to the west. From 2,000-3,000 feet, the river's curves and the marshes around the town make it easy to identify; Cumberland Island lies to the northeast, and the large Naval Submarine Base Kings Bay adjoins the city. Military airspace and security restrictions surround the base, so check charts and NOTAMs and give it a wide berth. Jacksonville International (KJAX) is about 15.5 nm south-southwest, and Fernandina Beach Municipal Airport (FAA code FHB, GPS ident KFHB) about 8 nm southeast. St. Simons Island Airport (KSSI) is about 27 nm north-northeast. The old St. Marys Airport just north of town closed in 2017 and is no longer usable.
