# Fountain of Youth Archaeological Park

A St. Augustine attraction built on an unproven Ponce de León legend turned out to sit on something real: the site of the first Spanish settlement of 1565.

**Location:** 29.9073, -81.3161

**Categories:** Museums in St. Augustine, Florida, St. Augustine, Florida, Tourist attractions in St. Augustine, Florida, Archaeological parks, Florida Native American Heritage Trail, History museums in Florida, National Register of Historic Places in St. Johns County, Florida, Parks on the National Register of Historic Places in Florida

## Introduction

The Fountain of Youth has a well, a legend and a problem. This 15-acre park in St. Augustine has long been touted as the likely spot where Ponce de León landed in Florida in 1513, and its well as the fabled water he was supposedly seeking. No evidence has ever been found to back up either claim. The twist is that the ground beneath the legend holds something genuinely historic, and it took archaeologists, not promoters, to find it.

## A Legend Without Proof

The park is privately owned and sits along Hospital Creek, part of the Intracoastal Waterway. Its central attraction is a well claimed to be the freshwater source mentioned by Antonio de Herrera y Tordesillas in his Historia general de los hechos de los castellanos en las Islas y Tierra Firme del mar Océano, the spring Ponce de León was said to have sought. There is no supporting evidence for the connection, and the landing claim is contested too. Research by amateur historian Douglas Peck has proposed another possible landing site far to the south, near Melbourne Beach in Brevard County. What visitors find here, in other words, is less a proven site than a long-running story about one.

## Diamond Lil's Spring

The story was built up by showmanship. Beginning in the 1860s, a series of small attractions operated on these grounds, showing off a well described as a "spring" and the pleasant grounds around it. Around 1900 the property passed to Luella Day McConnell, known as "Diamond Lil," who enlarged the attraction (by 1904, by one account) and fabricated tales designed to amuse and appall residents and tourists alike. She kept it up until her death in a car accident in 1927. That same year, the park was bought by Walter B. Fraser, a Georgia native who made St. Augustine his home and became active in local politics.

## The Mayor of Old Things

Fraser also turned his attention to the city's past. In the 1930s he organized St. Augustine's first formal restoration movement, the forerunner of the preservation program that eventually became the Historic St. Augustine Preservation Board. He served on the City Commission from 1933 to 1943, the last seven of those years as mayor. Along the way he bought the Oldest Orange Grove and the Oldest School House, built the radio station WFOY, and purchased the Markland estate next to the Ponce de Leon Hotel. From 1944 to 1948 he served in the Florida Senate.

## What the Digging Found

The real discovery came from the ground itself. Beginning in the mid-1970s, archaeologist Kathleen Deagan led excavations on the park's grounds and, over the following decades, uncovered remains of the first Spanish settlement in St. Augustine, along with its fortifications. That settlement dates to 1565, when Pedro Menéndez de Avilés and his expedition came ashore on September 6 near the Timucua town of Seloy and occupied several of its structures, possibly for their first fortifications and dwellings. The arrangement did not last. In 1566 the Saturiwa burned St. Augustine, and the settlement was relocated. The park was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2016, and it now holds two kinds of history at once: a legend that was never proven, and the buried traces of an encounter between Spanish colonists and the Timucua that certainly happened.

## Flight Context

The Fountain of Youth Archaeological Park lies at 29.9073°N, 81.3161°W on the north side of St. Augustine, along Hospital Creek and the Intracoastal Waterway. From 1,500–2,500 feet, the old city's grid and the star-shaped Castillo de San Marcos are just to the south, with the Matanzas River, Anastasia Island and the St. Augustine Inlet to the east. Northeast Florida Regional Airport (KSGJ) is about 3.5 nm north-northwest, so the park sits beneath its Class D airspace; contact the tower before overflying. Flagler Executive (KFIN) is about 27 nm south-southeast and Jacksonville International (KJAX) about 40 nm north-northwest. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
