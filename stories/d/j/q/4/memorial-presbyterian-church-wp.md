# Memorial Presbyterian Church

Henry Flagler answered his daughter's death by raising a copper-domed Venetian church in St. Augustine, and he now lies there beside her.

**Location:** 29.8938, -81.3169

**Categories:** Churches in St. Augustine, Florida, Historic American Buildings Survey in Florida, Tourist attractions in St. Augustine, Florida, Renaissance Revival architecture in Florida, Churches completed in 1889, Presbyterian churches in Florida, 1889 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

March 1889 was the month Henry Morrison Flagler lost his daughter. Jennie Louise Benedict died at sea after complications from childbirth, and the industrialist answered his grief the way he answered most things in St. Augustine: he built. Within a year, a church inspired by St. Mark's Basilica in Venice stood at the corner of Sevilla and Valencia Streets, a short walk from his grand hotels. Its copper dome still rises above the old city's rooftops, and inside, carved into a font of Siena marble, a dedication to Jennie remains legible well over a century later. Memorial Presbyterian Church is the most personal thing Flagler left in Florida. It is also where he chose to rest.

## A Trade of Land and Faith

Flagler was a Presbyterian all his life, and when Jennie died he picked a plot very near his hotels as the site for a new home for St. Augustine's Presbyterian congregation, the first in Florida. The gift was generous but not simple. The building, the land beneath it and the parsonage of the day were all donated to the Presbyterian Church, which in exchange handed over property it owned on St. George and Hypolita Streets. Flagler did not let that parcel sit idle. He later put up a municipal building for the city on the site, so the church's old address became part of his civic footprint too. The arrangement tells you something about how Flagler operated in St. Augustine, where private mourning, public philanthropy and real estate could all be settled in a single transaction.

## Venice on Sevilla Street

Flagler turned to the architects he already trusted. John M. Carrère and Thomas Hastings of New York had designed his Hotel Ponce de Leon, the Hotel Alcazar and Grace United Methodist Church, yet Memorial Presbyterian looks nothing like its siblings. Its model was St. Mark's Basilica in Venice, which inspired both the Venetian Renaissance style and the sanctuary's Latin cross plan. The materials crossed an ocean to get here. The copper dome was made in Italy, and the floors are Italian marble. Mahogany was carved into pews, while the baptismal font was cut from a single block of Siena marble. Standing inside, the effect is of a small cathedral lifted out of an Italian lagoon city and set down among the palms, a sanctuary built to hold one family's sorrow and an entire congregation's worship at the same time.

## Words Cut in Marble

The font is where the church speaks most plainly. Its inscription reads: "In Memoriam, F. H. B. to J. L. B., March 25th, 1889. I will be a God unto thee and to thy seed after thee," the promise of Genesis 17:7. For a woman who died bringing a child into the world, the choice of verse is hard to read without feeling its weight. The church was dedicated on March 16, 1890, and the ceremony drew national figures, among them First Lady Caroline Harrison and Vice President Levi Morton. Flagler outlived Jennie by twenty-four years. When he died in Palm Beach on May 20, 1913, at 83, he came back to St. Augustine. He was entombed in the Flagler family mausoleum at Memorial Presbyterian, alongside his first wife, his daughter and a granddaughter.

## The Night the Cross Came Down

A copper dome on the Florida coast is a standing invitation to the weather, and the storms eventually accepted it. After Hurricane Irma in 2017, the 900-pound cross that crowned the dome fell, punching several holes through the copper as it came down. What followed was a major restoration of the dome, with the congregation setting out to raise $3.5 million for the repairs. The cross was taken down while crews worked, and in October 2018 a solid copper cross, reported at 700 pounds, was mounted atop the dome again. As of March 2019, the church was still carrying on renovation work to repair damage left by both Hurricane Matthew and Irma. None of it dimmed the building's reputation. In 2012, CNN had already named Memorial Presbyterian one of eight religious wonders worth visiting in the United States, and the congregation has opened the building to visitors for free self-guided tours, letting anyone step under the dome Flagler raised for Jennie.

## Flight Context

Memorial Presbyterian Church stands at 32 Sevilla Street in downtown St. Augustine, Florida, at 29.8938N, 81.3169W. From 1,500-2,500 feet the copper dome and cross-shaped roof stand out among the towers of Flagler's former hotels a block or two away; the Castillo de San Marcos sits on the waterfront less than a kilometer to the northeast. Northeast Florida Regional Airport (KSGJ) at St. Augustine is about 4 nm north-northwest, making this an easy sightseeing loop after departure.
