# Mission Nombre de Dios

On the marshy shore where Spain founded St. Augustine in 1565, a Franciscan mission, a tiny chapel and a 208-foot steel cross mark what is likely the oldest surviving mission in the continental United States. Here, on the west side of Matanzas Bay, the story of Spanish Florida and its first peoples is layered into the quiet grounds.

**Location:** 29.9051, -81.3161

**Categories:** Spanish missions in Florida, Catholic Church in Florida, Florida Native American Heritage Trail, Religious museums in Florida, 1565 establishments in the Spanish Empire, Pre-statehood history of Florida

## Introduction

A stainless-steel cross towers 208 feet above the Matanzas marshes in St. Augustine. It stands on the grounds where, on September 8, 1565, Admiral Pedro Menéndez de Avilés came ashore with a band of settlers to found St. Augustine. The grounds became Mission Nombre de Dios, "Name of God," likely the oldest surviving mission in the continental United States. Here, on the west side of Matanzas Bay, the story of Spanish Florida and its first peoples is layered into the quiet grounds.

## A Mass at the Landing

The expedition's chaplain, Father Francisco López de Mendoza Grajales, celebrated the first Thanksgiving Mass on these grounds. The land was not empty. Nearby stood villages of the Mocama, a Timucua people, and this place lay at the center of an important Mocama chiefdom through the late 16th and 17th centuries. Jesuits and later Franciscans ministered to the Spanish colonists and made some efforts to convert the Mocama and Agua Dulce peoples around St. Augustine. They found their greatest success in the Mocama village called Nombre de Dios, where they converted the chief, a woman, and her daughter. In 1587, as the Franciscans began their first major push to found missions, a formal mission with a resident friar was established here, perhaps the first mission in what would become the continental United States.

## Our Lady of the Milk

Late in the 1500s, a devotion to Our Lady of La Leche, Our Lady of the Milk, crossed the ocean from Spain. Her shrine on the mission grounds is billed as America's oldest Marian shrine. It has not had an easy life. The original chapel was destroyed in 1728 during a British attack on St. Augustine. Bishop Augustin Verot rebuilt it in 1875, but a hurricane later damaged that one too. The chapel visitors see today dates from 1914, a small replica of the earlier coquina chapels, its walls now wrapped in ivy and its pews seating only about 30 people. A canonical coronation planned for October 10, 2020, was postponed a year because of the COVID-19 pandemic, and on October 10, 2021, Cardinal Carlos Osoro Sierra of Madrid crowned the image of Mary and the Child Jesus at a Mass at the Cathedral Basilica of St. Augustine.

## Four Centuries On

The 400th anniversary of the landing brought two of the site's best-known landmarks. The Prince of Peace Votive Church went up in 1965-66 at the San Marco Avenue entrance. In October 1966, during a diocesan Eucharistic Congress, Archbishop Casimiro Morcillo of Madrid dedicated the Great Cross at the direction of Archbishop Joseph P. Hurley. Around them the grounds hold archaeological excavations, a historic cemetery, a bell tower, fountains, a rustic altar and shrines to Our Lady of Perpetual Help and Our Lady of Guadalupe. There are monuments to the Seven Sorrows of Mary and statues of Saint Francis of Assisi, Saint Joseph and Father López himself, the chaplain who said that first Mass. Mission plaques and historical markers around the grounds help visitors piece the long story together.

## Flight Context

Mission Nombre de Dios is at 29.9051°N, 81.3161°W in St. Augustine, on the west side of Matanzas Bay. From 1,500–2,500 feet, look for the 208-foot stainless-steel Great Cross standing above the marshes along the bay, with the historic city spread along the waterfront. Northeast Florida Regional Airport (KSGJ) is about 3.5 nm to the north-northwest. Coastal haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms are common; clear winter days give the best views of the bay and inlet.
