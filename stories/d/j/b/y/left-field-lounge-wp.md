# Left Field Lounge

Beyond the outfield fence at Mississippi State, a tradition that began with one pickup truck grew into college baseball's most famous tailgate.

**Location:** 33.4638, -88.7941

**Categories:** Baseball venues in Mississippi, Mississippi State Bulldogs baseball, College sports traditions, Tailgating, Starkville, Mississippi

## Introduction

According to a 2003 account by a Mississippi State alumnus, sometime in the early 1970s a truck parked beyond the left-field fence at Mississippi State would not start after the game, so its owners simply left it where it sat. When the next game came around, the truck was still there, holding what amounted to a reserved spot. Other fans noticed. Soon trucks and grills were being brought in with no intention of leaving, and dead vehicles were towed into place just to claim ground. That is how the Left Field Lounge took its shape, on its way to becoming one of the most celebrated tailgates in college sports.

## One Pickup in Left Field

By the usual telling, the lounge began in the 1960s, when fans started driving cars and trucks into the open area behind the outfield at Dudy Noble Field to watch the Bulldogs play. Longtime fan Russ Rogers is often credited as the first to drive a pickup into left field and set up shop, though a 2021 Commercial Dispatch profile dates his first truck-seating to 1980, so accounts of the timeline differ. By the late 1960s, people were hauling in grills, tables and ice chests full of drinks for a full tailgate. At first everything came in before the first pitch and went out after the last out. As word spread, a line formed before each game, the space filled, and some fans were turned away, which is exactly why a stranded truck became such a good idea.

## Bleachers in the Truck Bed

Once vehicles stayed put, fans started building up. Homemade seats and bleachers appeared in pickup beds so more people could share a spot, and later motorhomes and flatbed trailers arrived carrying sturdier, more elaborate stands. Eventually the university stepped in with rules. Spaces were rented by the season, a waiting list formed for everyone who missed out, and the trucks, trailers and motorhomes, all still on wheels, had to be brought in by a set day before the season and removed when it ended. Sports Illustrated named the lounge the "#1 place to watch college baseball" and listed it among its "100 things you gotta do before you graduate," and in 2009 ESPN's magazine called it the country's best tailgating experience. The novelist John Grisham, a Mississippi State alumnus and devoted fan, wrote about his own days in the lounge in his introduction to Inside Dudy Noble: A Celebration of Mississippi State Baseball.

## Crowds No Campus Had Seen

Weekend games against Southeastern Conference rivals draw big gatherings, and Mississippi State's crowds have rewritten the record book. In 2007, two Super Regional games against Clemson drew 12,620 and 13,715 fans, at the time the two best-attended Super Regional games in NCAA history. The ballpark itself has changed around the lounge: the stadium that opened in 1967 was mostly leveled in 2017, and an all-new Dudy Noble Field was ready for the 2019 season. The old trucks, trailers and motorhomes did not survive the rebuild: the lounge area is now an elevated party deck with bleachers beside the 12-unit Left Field Lofts, where fans still tailgate. The records kept climbing. On April 15, 2023, a game against Ole Miss drew 16,423 people, the largest on-campus crowd in the history of college baseball. Not bad for a tradition that started with one pickup truck.

## Flight Context

The Left Field Lounge sits beyond the outfield of Dudy Noble Field on the Mississippi State University campus in Starkville, at about 33.464 N, 88.794 W. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet, look for the ballpark among the campus athletic facilities; on game weekends the lots around the ballpark fill with vehicles and the area beyond left field fills with tailgaters. Avoid low passes over crowds. George M. Bryan Airport at Starkville (FAA identifier STF, no ICAO code) is about 3 nm southwest, and Golden Triangle Regional Airport (KGTR) about 10 nm east. Spring baseball season brings frequent thunderstorms to northeast Mississippi.
