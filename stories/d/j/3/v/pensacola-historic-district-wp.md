# Pensacola Historic District

Around Seville Square, a group of determined preservationists saved some of Pensacola's oldest streets, and today some 30 historic properties and museums tell the city's layered story.

**Location:** 30.4106, -87.2100

**Categories:** Pensacola, Florida, Historic districts, Open-air museums in Florida, History museums in Florida, Tourist attractions in Pensacola, Florida

## Introduction

Saving a neighborhood took surveys, lobbying and a real estate purchase. It also took a picnic. Pensacola preservationist Mary Turner Rule and her allies wanted their neighbors to rediscover the worn-down streets around Seville Square, so they created An Evening in Old Seville Square, a hometown Victorian picnic in the park meant to draw people into the district and encourage restoration. Their larger campaign succeeded. On September 29, 1970, the Pensacola Historic District, also known as the Seville Historic District, was designated a U.S. historic district.

## Parade Grounds by the Bay

The district is roughly bounded by Bayfront Parkway and by Tarragona, Romana and Cevallos streets. Within it lie the Historic Pensacola Village, the Pensacola Museum of History and Seville Square itself. The square has military roots. Seville Square and its twin, Plaza Ferdinand VII, served as the parade grounds for the Fort of Pensacola established during British rule. The history here runs deeper still. Elsewhere in Pensacola, on Pensacola Bay, lies the site of Tristán de Luna's settlement of 1559 to 1561, which the University of West Florida, whose archaeologists identified it, calls the earliest multi-year European colonial settlement archaeologically identified in the United States.

## A Picnic for Preservation

In the early 1960s Rule, born Mary Turner Reed, led a group of local preservationists who formed the Pensacola Heritage Foundation, joined the National Trust and surveyed the neighborhood around Seville Square beside Pensacola Bay. Recognizing how much of the city's history was at stake, they nominated the district for the National Register of Historic Places. They did not stop at paperwork. The foundation bought the Dorr House and restored it, then persuaded the city to bring the deteriorated square back to good condition. Rule also had the famous lighthouse at the Naval Air Station listed on the National Register, and she helped create the state board now called the N.W. Florida Preservation Board, charged with protecting the Seville Square Historic District and Pensacola's history. The city, for its part, established an Architecture Review Board to guard that history locally.

## From Commission to Trust

Preservation also found an institutional home. In 1967 the Pensacola Historic Preservation and Restoration Commission was founded to preserve the city's historic monuments and buildings and to educate the public. When the Florida Legislature abolished it in 2001, its collections and buildings passed to the University of West Florida. A 2009 merger and a 2013 renaming produced today's UWF Historic Trust, which manages Historic Pensacola Village, a collection of historic buildings and museums set between Plaza Ferdinand VII and Seville Square. By the Trust's own count, Historic Pensacola covers 8½ acres and 30 properties, 12 of them open to the public.

## A Street Scene from 1900

The village's most immersive corner is the Museum of Commerce, a reconstructed Pensacola street scene of the 1890s era, based on businesses that operated in the city between 1880 and 1910, set inside a brick turn-of-the-century warehouse. Its historic shops include toys, leather goods, hardware, music and household goods, along with an antique trolley car and a print shop. Older visitor guides say that print shop holds one of the most complete collections of antique printing presses and type in the Southeast. Around it, other museums pick up different threads: the Museum of Industry, the Pensacola Museum of Art, the Pensacola Children's Museum, the Voices of Pensacola Multicultural Center and the Colonial Archaeological Trail.

## Houses That Remember Their People

Many of the village's buildings are homes, and several carry the names of the people who lived in them. Julee Cottage was once owned by Julee Panton, a free woman of color, and by other Black families. The Lear-Rocheblave House has been restored to its life as a 1920s boarding house, and the Tivoli High House, another former boarding house, now holds a shop and one of the service desks where tickets are sold. There are the Dorr House that launched the preservation effort, the Lavalle House, the Barkley House, the Manuel Barrios Cottage and the John Appleyard Cottage. Old Christ Church stands among them, and the Bowden Building has housed University of West Florida classrooms downstairs and the village's administrative offices upstairs.

## Flight Context

The Pensacola Historic District lies along the downtown waterfront of Pensacola, Florida, centered near 30.411°N, 87.210°W, between Bayfront Parkway and Pensacola Bay to the south and the downtown blocks to the north. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet, look for the tree-covered green of Seville Square and, a few blocks west, Plaza Ferdinand VII. Pensacola International Airport (KPNS) is about 4 nm to the north-northeast, and Naval Air Station Pensacola Forrest Sherman Field (KNPA) lies about 7 nm to the west-southwest, with busy military training airspace. Coastal haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms are common.
