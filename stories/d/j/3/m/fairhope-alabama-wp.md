# Fairhope, Alabama

Founded in 1894 as a single-tax utopia on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay, Fairhope became a progressive-school pioneer and winter haven for writers before growing into an affluent suburb and boutique resort.

**Location:** 30.5264, -87.8957

**Categories:** Cities in Baldwin County, Alabama, Georgist communities, Utopian communities in the United States, Populated coastal places in Alabama, Progressive education

## Introduction

Before Monopoly, there was The Landlord's Game, and a version dated to 1910 is said to have included a property called "Fairhope Avenue." By then, on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay, a group of idealists had spent 16 years trying to demonstrate that a community could run on a single economic idea. Fairhope, Alabama, began as that experiment, and more than a century later its single-tax corporation still owns and leases roughly a quarter of the city's land.

## Twenty-Eight Believers From Iowa

The founders were 28 followers of the economist Henry George who had incorporated in Des Moines, Iowa, as the Fairhope Industrial Association. On November 15, 1894, they founded their colony on the site of the former Alabama City, after pooling their money to buy land known as "Stapleton's pasture." Rather than sell lots, they divided the land into long-term leaseholds. The corporation paid all government taxes out of the rents its lessees paid, simulating George's proposed "single tax." The goal was to remove the penalties on putting land to productive use and to keep the value of the land for the community. Remarkably, the Fairhope Single Tax Corporation still operates, owning about 4,500 acres in and around the city and leasing it on renewable 99-year terms.

## A School That Caught Dewey's Eye

Ideas drew more ideas. In 1907 the educator Marietta Johnson founded the School for Organic Education in Fairhope, and in 1915 John Dewey praised it in his influential book Schools of Tomorrow. Johnson was among the founders of the Progressive Education Association in 1919, and Dewey later became its honorary president. The Marietta Johnson School of Organic Education still operates as a small private school, with 19 students in 2023, grouping children in life groups that traditionally span the first through eighth grades. The town also became a popular wintering spot for artists and intellectuals. Sherwood Anderson, Clarence Darrow, Wharton Esherick, Carl Zigrosser and Upton Sinclair were among its notable visitors. The town's list of notable people also includes the writers Rick Bragg, Fannie Flagg and Winston Groom, author of Forrest Gump, and the singer Jimmy Buffett.

## When the Bay Gives Up Its Fish

Fairhope sits on a sloping plateau that climbs from sea level at the bay to 122 feet in the city center, six miles south of Daphne. Its shoreline shares in one of Mobile Bay's strangest gifts. On some summer mornings, usually before sunrise, oxygen-poor water rising from the bottom of the bay drives flounder, blue crab, shrimp, eels and stingrays into the shallows along the Eastern Shore, and word spreads with a single cry: "Jubilee!" According to the Alabama Cooperative Extension System, jubilees happen most often along the upper eastern shore between Great Point Clear and just north of Daphne, and while they have been reported elsewhere, Mobile Bay is probably the only body of water where they occur fairly regularly. In just a few hours, residents can gather hundreds of flounder or tubs of crabs.

## Utopia to Boutique

Fairhope's path has run from utopian experiment to artists' and intellectuals' colony to boutique resort and affluent suburb of Mobile, a trajectory that sits uneasily with the single-taxers' ideals. The city has been growing quickly, from 15,326 residents in 2010 to 22,477 in 2020, and developers have built downtown commercial buildings larger than the town historically allowed. Its charm has drawn national notice. In 2019 the New York Times described it as "A Southern Town That's Been Holding On to Its Charm, for More Than a Century." Yet the older Fairhope has not vanished. The single-tax leaseholds still cover thousands of acres, Marietta Johnson's little school still teaches, and on some summer dawns the bay still fills its shallows with fish.

## Flight Context

Fairhope lies at 30.526 N, 87.896 W on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay in Baldwin County, Alabama, with the city of Mobile across the bay to the northwest. From 1,500-2,500 feet follow the Eastern Shore south from Daphne; US 98 runs north-south through town, and the bay's broad open water to the west makes the shoreline easy to trace. H. L. Sonny Callahan Airport (KCQF) is about 4 nm south-southeast, Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown) about 11 nm northwest across the bay, and Mobile Regional (KMOB) about 20 nm west-northwest. Navy outlying fields in the county, such as Barin (KNBJ, about 16 nm east-southeast) and Summerdale (about 13 nm east), are used for training, so watch for military traffic; the Silverhill field, about 5 nm east-northeast, is listed as closed. Summer brings afternoon thunderstorms and hurricane season runs June through November.
