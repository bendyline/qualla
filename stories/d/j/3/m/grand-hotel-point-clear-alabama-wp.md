# Grand Hotel (Point Clear, Alabama)

A resort on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay since 1847, Point Clear's Grand Hotel served as a Confederate hospital, was battered by hurricanes in 1906 and 1916 and replaced in 1942, and in 1944 became headquarters for Operation Ivory Soap.

**Location:** 30.4849, -87.9345

**Categories:** Hotels in Alabama, Baldwin County, Alabama, Historic Hotels of America

## Introduction

Ivory soap floats. That, according to the officer in charge, is how a World War II operation run from a suite at a Mobile Bay resort got its name. In 1944 the Grand Hotel at Point Clear, Alabama, took in soldiers and became the base of Operation Ivory Soap, a program built around experimental aircraft repair units meant to float, just like the soap. It was only one chapter in the long life of a hotel that has stood on this shore, in one form or another, since 1847.

## A Resort on the Eastern Shore

The original Grand Hotel, built in 1847, was a modest two-story building with 40 guest rooms on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay. Its role grew beyond hospitality during the Civil War. During the Civil War part of the hotel became a Confederate hospital for wounded soldiers, which the Encyclopedia of Alabama ties to the Siege of Vicksburg in 1863 and the hotel's own history ties to the 1864 Battle of Mobile Bay; some 300 Confederate soldiers died there and lie in the Confederate Rest cemetery on the grounds. The original building did not last: a hurricane damaged parts of it in 1893, hurricanes in 1906 and 1916 did severe damage, and the old buildings were eventually demolished for a new hotel that opened in 1942.

## Operation Ivory Soap

In the Pacific during World War II, the United States advanced by leapfrogging, capturing strategically valuable islands held by the Japanese. Army air commander Henry "Hap" Arnold understood that American aircraft fighting across those distances needed mobile air depots. The answer was Operation Ivory Soap, and in 1944 its base of operations was the Grand Hotel, with Matthew Thompson, an Army Air Forces officer, overseeing the effort from one of its suites. The name, Thompson explained, came from the fact that Ivory soap floats, like the experimental Aircraft Repair Units the military wanted to deploy.

## Army Seamen

During the operation, the hotel housed soldiers and served as a maritime training school. Troops learned swimming, special calisthenics, marching and drill, navigation, ship identification, signaling, cargo handling, ship orientation, sail making, and amphibious operations, an unusual curriculum for Army men. The school turned out 5,000 trained Army seamen, who went on to take part in operations in the Philippines, Iwo Jima, and Guam.

## Grand Once More

The Grand Hotel is still in operation on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay, and it has grown far beyond its forty-room beginnings. Its official site now lists 405 guestrooms and suites, along with a spa of 20,000 square feet. It has been a member of Historic Hotels of America since 2011 and is part of Marriott's Autograph Collection as the Grand Hotel Golf Resort & Spa. Guests who come for the bay views may never guess that the same shoreline once echoed with drill commands and swimming lessons for men bound for the Pacific.

## Flight Context

The Grand Hotel sits at 30.4849°N, 87.9345°W at Point Clear on the eastern shore of Mobile Bay in Baldwin County, Alabama. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow the eastern shoreline of the bay and look for the resort's buildings along the water. Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown) is about 11 nm northwest across the bay, and Mobile Regional Airport (KMOB) about 20 nm northwest. Several naval outlying fields lie nearby on the eastern shore, including Barin about 17 nm east-southeast, so watch for military training traffic; the Silverhill field about 8 nm northeast is listed as closed. Gulf Shores International Airport (Jack Edwards Field, KJKA) is about 18 nm southeast.
