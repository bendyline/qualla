# Bamahenge

In the woods behind an Alabama marina, a full-scale fiberglass Stonehenge built by artist Mark Cline lines up with the summer solstice sunrise, each 'stone' braced on a wooden spine against Gulf hurricanes.

**Location:** 30.3314, -87.5673

**Categories:** Replicas and derivatives of Stonehenge, Landmarks in Alabama, Outdoor sculptures in Alabama, Buildings and structures in Baldwin County, Alabama, Tourist attractions in Baldwin County, Alabama, Fiberglass sculptures in the United States

## Introduction

Knock on one of these stones and it will not answer the way ancient rock would. Bamahenge is a full-scale fiberglass replica of England's prehistoric Stonehenge, standing in the woods on the grounds of Barber Marina near Josephine, Alabama, and each of its 'stones' hides a wooden pole for a spine. It is a monument born of a hurricane repair job and a dairy magnate's taste for oversized lawn ornaments. Yet it keeps one appointment borrowed faithfully from the original: on the morning of the summer solstice, the sun rises over the center of three lintels on its outer ring.

## Dinosaurs First

The story begins not with stones but with dinosaurs. In 1991, the wealthy dairy magnate George W. Barber commissioned Mark Cline, a Virginia artist of Enchanted Castle Studios, to design and install seven life-size dinosaurs as "lawn ornaments" on one of his properties. Fifteen years later, in 2006, Cline was back on site repairing hurricane damage to those sculptures when Barber made a new request. He wanted a life-size Stonehenge. Barber may have known that Cline had recently installed Foamhenge, his replica in Virginia; either way, he had found an artist who had built one before.

## A Change of Venue

Barber's first thought was to put the monument at his Vintage Motorsports Museum outside Birmingham. The project waited, though, and by the time Cline could begin work in 2012 the plan had shifted south. Barber had relocated four of the dinosaurs to the grounds of Barber Marina, and he decided the stones should join them there. Cline set the circle in the woods to the north of the dinosaurs, arranging the installation, like Stonehenge itself, so that the summer solstice sun rises over the center of three lintels on the outer markers. A monument once meant for a museum near Birmingham ended up in a quiet corner of Baldwin County instead.

## Anchored Against the Wind

A fiberglass monument has a problem its stone ancestor never faced: it is light. Bamahenge sits only about 4 miles from the Gulf of Mexico, squarely in an active tropical storm and hurricane zone, where a lightweight 'stone' is exactly the sort of thing a strong wind would like to carry off. Cline's answer runs deep. Each "stone" is anchored by a wooden pole that extends 8 feet up into it and a similar distance into the ground. The lower portion of the stone, along with a pit dug beneath it around the pole, is filled with concrete. What looks like ancient rock is really something closer to an engineered tree, rooted as firmly as the woods around it.

## Finding the Circle

Reaching Bamahenge is refreshingly simple. The monument stands about 200 yards west of the access road to Barber Marina, and a small parking area sits at the head of the path leading to it. Walk that short trail and the uprights appear through the trees, as unexpected here as dinosaurs on a lawn. Officially the site lies closest to the unincorporated community of Josephine, but it is more often associated with the larger nearby town of Elberta. Come on a clear midsummer morning and you can test Cline's alignment for yourself, watching the sun climb over the lintels of a Stonehenge raised in 2012 rather than in prehistory.

## Flight Context

Bamahenge stands at approximately 30.3314 N, 87.5673 W in southern Baldwin County, Alabama, about 4 miles inland from the Gulf of Mexico. The fiberglass circle is small and set in woods, so it is hard to pick out from altitude; fly low and slow at 1,000-1,500 ft AGL and look for a clearing just west of the Barber Marina access road. Gulf Shores International Airport (Jack Edwards Field, KJKA) lies about 6 nm west-southwest and is the most practical nearby field. Naval Outlying Field Barin (KNBJ) sits about 5 nm northwest and Naval Air Station Pensacola (KNPA) about 13 nm east; both are military fields, so give their training traffic a wide berth. Pensacola International (KPNS) is about 21 nm east-northeast. Summer afternoons along the Gulf coast often bring thunderstorms, and the solstice alignment is a sunrise event.
