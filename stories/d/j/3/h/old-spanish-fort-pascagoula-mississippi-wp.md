# Old Spanish Fort (Pascagoula, Mississippi)

Neither a fort nor Spanish, Pascagoula's LaPointe-Krebs House is a French colonial plantation home of oyster-shell concrete and longleaf pine, dated by its timbers to about 1757 and ranked as Mississippi's oldest standing building.

**Location:** 30.3803, -88.5581

**Categories:** Houses on the National Register of Historic Places in Mississippi, Houses in Jackson County, Mississippi, Pascagoula, Mississippi, Mississippi Landmarks, Tabby buildings, French colonial history

## Introduction

The Old Spanish Fort was never really a fort, and the Spanish did not build it. The low building on the shore of Lake Catahoula, also called Krebs Lake, near Pascagoula, Mississippi, is a one-story plantation house of longleaf pine and oyster-shell concrete, and its proper name is the LaPointe-Krebs House. Tree-ring dating of its oldest timbers suggests construction began around 1757, which is believed to make it Mississippi's oldest surviving historic building and the only structure in the state from the French colonial era. It may also be the oldest scientifically confirmed standing structure on the Gulf Coast of the United States.

## Shell, Moss and Pine

The house measures 37 feet wide and 62.25 feet long, with framing walls 18 inches thick. Its builders framed it in longleaf pine and filled the oldest walls with oyster-shell concrete, while the western addition, the most recent, uses bousillage, a mix of clay and Spanish moss. The original structure held one large room and two smaller ones. Porches supported by square wooden posts wrapped three sides, a gable roof was covered in wooden shingles, and two chimneys of stucco-covered brick served its fireplaces. The floor was oyster-shell concrete until 1820, when it was covered with boards 18 inches up. Each part of the building carries its own date. Timbers in the central tabby room date to 1757, samples from the east addition to 1762 and 1772, and tool marks and building methods place the western addition around 1820. Some older wood may have been recycled, possibly after a hurricane struck in 1772.

## Cattle, Indigo and Enslaved Labor

The land was granted to Joseph Simon dit La Pointe, a French Canadian who, like several other Canadians, came to Pascagoula from the area around Mobile Bay; two neighboring plantations belonged to Canadian families who had moved over from Dauphin Island. La Pointe first used the land to breed cattle, then turned to indigo, grown for blue dye, and wax myrtle, used for candlemaking. The plantation also rested on bondage. La Pointe quickly became invested in chattel slavery, enslaving Native Americans before purchasing enslaved Africans. When he died in 1751, the property passed to his daughter, Marie-Josèphe, and her German husband, Hugo Ernestus Krebs. The oldest part of the house dates from about six years later, during their ownership, and under the Krebs family the plantation used the forced labor of enslaved Africans to produce rice and cotton.

## A Gin Before Whitney

Hugo Krebs left a curious mark on American history. According to the Anglo-Dutch traveler Bernard Romans, Krebs built a roller cotton gin more than two decades before Eli Whitney's 1793 invention; histories of the machine date his modification of the older roller gin to 1772 and note that roller gins handled only long-staple cotton, so Whitney's saw gin was a different machine. The house's misleading nickname came later. During Spanish control of the region in the late 18th century, the building served as the fortified home of Don Enrique Ginarest, an officer in the Spanish Army who had married Hugo Krebs's granddaughter, and the "Old Spanish Fort" name took hold. It has also been called the "Old French Fort." Through all its names, the house stayed in the family: descendants of Hugo Krebs owned and occupied it until 1914.

## Oldest, With a Footnote

The LaPointe-Krebs House was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1971 and designated a Mississippi Landmark in 1984. Its claim as the Gulf Coast's oldest standing building comes with a footnote: the Old Ursuline Convent in New Orleans is known to have been designed by Ignace François Broutin in 1745 and completed by 1753, so the Pascagoula house's distinction rests on its scientifically confirmed date. Today the LaPointe-Krebs Foundation owns and operates the house as a museum of the history of Pascagoula and Jackson County, and a restoration carried out between 2015 and early 2022 has renewed the old house.

## Flight Context

The LaPointe-Krebs House stands at approximately 30.38 N, 88.56 W on the shore of Krebs Lake (Lake Catahoula) in Pascagoula, Mississippi, near the Mississippi Sound. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the small lake just inland from the coast within Pascagoula; the house itself is small and low, so the lake shoreline is the better reference. Trent Lott International Airport (KPQL) is about 5 nm north-northeast. Ocean Springs Airport (FAA identifier 5R2; no ICAO code) is about 10 nm west, and Mobile Regional Airport (KMOB) about 25 nm northeast. Keesler Air Force Base (KBIX) lies about 19 nm west at Biloxi. Expect summer thunderstorms, coastal haze and tropical weather in hurricane season.
