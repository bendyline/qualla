# Battle House Hotel

Mobile's Battle House has hosted a losing presidential candidate on election night, a president who made a famous pledge during his stay, and Mardi Gras balls as far back as 1852; rebuilt in steel after a 1905 fire and shuttered for thirty years, it reopened in 2007 fully restored.

**Location:** 30.6931, -88.0430

**Categories:** National Register of Historic Places in Mobile, Alabama, Hotels in Mobile, Alabama, Hotel buildings completed in 1852, Hotel buildings completed in 1908, Hotels established in 1852, 1852 establishments in Alabama, Sheraton hotels, Historic Hotels of America

## Introduction

On the night Stephen A. Douglas lost the presidency to Abraham Lincoln, he was a guest at the Battle House in Mobile, Alabama. It was a fitting place for a public man to wait out history. The hotel stood on ground where Andrew Jackson had set up a military headquarters during the War of 1812, and over the decades it would take in guests from Jefferson Davis to Oscar Wilde. It would also burn, rise again in steel, fall silent for thirty years, and return.

## Brothers, Brick, and Cast Iron

James Battle and his two half-nephews, John and Samuel, opened the first Battle House Hotel on November 13, 1852. The site already had a history of hotels that did not last: the Franklin Hotel and the Waverly Hotel had both stood there, and both had burned. The Battle family built big, a 200-room, four-story brick building wrapped in a two-story gallery of cast iron. It became a gathering place for the prominent. Jefferson Davis, Millard Fillmore, Oscar Wilde, and Winfield Scott are all listed among its guests. Henry Clay is often named too, though he died in June 1852, months before the hotel opened, so he can only have known an earlier hotel on the site. Mardi Gras found a home here too. The first Mardi Gras ball held at the Battle House was the Strikers Ball in 1852, back when such balls were part of the New Year celebration.

## From Ashes to Steel

The first Battle House kept up with the times. A weather station was established there in 1880, electric lighting arrived in 1884, and the hotel was renovated in 1900. Then, after more than fifty years in service, it was destroyed by fire on February 12, 1905, the same fate that had claimed the hotels before it. This time the proprietors built to last. They hired Frank Mills Andrews of New York City to design a new hotel of steel and concrete, and it reopened in 1908 as one of the earliest steel-frame structures in Alabama. Woodrow Wilson was entertained there in 1913, at a presidential breakfast, and during that Mobile visit, in an address to the Southern Commercial Congress, he declared that "the United States will never again seek one additional foot of territory by conquest." Renovations followed in 1916 and 1949, when air-conditioning reached every guest room and public space.

## Thirty Silent Years

The hotel remained a Mobile landmark through both World Wars, but the postwar decades were harder. Sheraton bought it in 1958 and ran it as the Sheraton-Battle House until 1968, when it sold the property to Gotham Hotels along with seventeen other aging hotels, and the Battle House name returned. In May 1973 a group of local citizens bought it, renamed it the Battle House Royale, and planned a full renovation, but the venture failed and the doors closed in 1974. They stayed closed for thirty years. The empty building joined the National Register of Historic Places in 1975, and by 1980 it was the only building left completely intact on its city block. In 2003 the Retirement Systems of Alabama began restoring it, alongside the new RSA Battle House Tower next door. After a four-year restoration by Smith Dalia Architects, working with TVSA on the tower, the hotel reopened on May 11, 2007.

## Kings on the Walls

The eight-story building wears marble and brick over its steel frame. At street level a one-story portico rests on paired Tuscan columns, cast iron balconies line the upper floors, and a molded cornice with scroll brackets crowns the roof. Inside, the lobby rises to a domed skylight dating to 1908, framed by elaborate plasterwork and walls painted in trompe-l'oeil. Portraits of Louis XIV of France, George III of Great Britain, Ferdinand V of Castile, and George Washington look down on arriving guests. The Trellis Room, a Northern Italian restaurant, sits beneath a barrel-vaulted ceiling with a Tiffany glass skylight. Nearby, the Crystal Ballroom, known as "Mobile's First Harvest," has been restored to its 1908 colors, its agricultural-themed plasterwork once again the backdrop for weddings, meetings, and Mardi Gras balls.

## Flight Context

The Battle House Hotel stands at 30.6931°N, 88.0430°W in downtown Mobile, Alabama, beside the RSA Battle House Tower. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, the skyscraper tower rising above downtown makes an easy reference, with the historic hotel at its base. Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown) is only about 4 nm south-southwest, and Mobile Regional Airport (KMOB) about 10 nm west, so coordinate with Mobile approach. Clear days offer the best views of the downtown skyline.
