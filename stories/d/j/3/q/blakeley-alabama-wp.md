# Blakeley, Alabama

Founded in 1813 on the Tensaw River, Blakeley briefly outgrew Mobile as a rival port before yellow fever and speculation emptied it; one of the last battles of the Civil War was fought here, and the ghost town now lies within Historic Blakeley State Park.

## Introduction

In 1822, Blakeley was bigger than Mobile. The young town on the eastern shore of the Tensaw River counted as many as 2,700 people by that winter and would peak at around 4,000 in the early 1820s, while Mobile's census that year showed only 2,800. Blakeley had a bank, three hotels, a newspaper, and a deep natural harbor that could take ships unable to reach Mobile. Today it is a ghost town, its streets traced only in outline beneath the trees of Historic Blakeley State Park.

## Before Blakeley

Long before the town had a name, people lived here. A burial mound found near the town site was excavated, revealing human remains and copper ornaments, and Blakeley lay along the Mobile-Tensaw delta, where the Mississippian culture flourished at the Bottle Creek site from around AD 1250 to 1500. In the 1700s, while France claimed the Mobile Bay region, the Apalachees, a native group from Florida allied with the French, established a village on this ground. British and Spanish colonial settlers farmed the area in turn, until it came under American control in 1813.

## A Connecticut Man's Town

That same year, Josiah Blakeley made his move. An entrepreneur and adventurer from Connecticut who had come to Mobile in 1806, he bought 7,000 acres at the northeastern edge of Mobile Bay, and in May 1813 he hired a surveyor to lay out a town, selling the first ten lots by June. On January 6, 1814, territorial lawmakers authorized the town of Blakeley, and in 1820 the State of Alabama formally incorporated it. Josiah Blakeley died in 1815 and never saw what followed. The town's great advantage was its deep natural port, reachable by ships that could not cross the Dog River bar, a sandbar that sometimes blocked access to Mobile. For some years, Blakeley competed with Mobile to be the top port in the region.

## Boom Years on the Tensaw

In 1820 Blakeley became the seat of Baldwin County, and it filled out quickly with a post office, a bank, three hotels, and a weekly newspaper, The Blakeley Sun, the fourth newspaper established in Alabama. A weekly ferry connected it with Mobile. Boat-building thrived. The steamers Mississippi and Tensas were built here, and the Tensas was one of the first steamships to ascend the Alabama River as far as Montgomery. Shipbuilders Jacob Bell and David Brown found success in Blakeley after the War of 1812, then returned to New York City around 1820 or 1821, where they ran Brown & Bell, a shipyard famous for its clipper ships and steamships.

## Fever and Decline

The boom did not last. Yellow fever epidemics struck the region in 1822, 1826, and 1828, and they devastated Blakeley's population. So many people died that mass graves were needed to bury them, a grim reversal for a town that had been growing so fast. High land prices and widespread speculation during the 1820s may also have discouraged newcomers from settling. Blakeley held on largely because it remained the county seat, and quarterly court sessions still brought crowds to town. But by the time of the Civil War, only about 100 people lived there.

## The Last Stand at Fort Blakeley

During the Civil War, the Confederacy built a fort at Blakeley with a garrison estimated at 3,500 to 4,000 soldiers, meant to protect Mobile from an attack by land from the east. After a siege that began in the first days of April 1865, some 16,000 Union troops attacked on April 9 and finally breached it, in one of the last battles of the war. Afterward, the county seat moved south to Daphne in 1868, and Blakeley was essentially abandoned. Many of its buildings were taken apart and carried off to Mobile, the rival it had once outgrown. What remains is the cemetery, the ruins of the jailhouse, and the outlines of the old streets. The town was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974, and Historic Blakeley State Park, created in 1981, now protects it with nature trails and annual spring reenactments of the battle.

## Flight Context

The site of Blakeley lies at 30.7422°N, 87.9242°W on the eastern shore of the Tensaw River in Baldwin County, Alabama, north of Spanish Fort and across the delta from Mobile. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the state park along the river's edge, with the waterways of the Mobile-Tensaw delta around it and Mobile to the west. Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown) is about 10 nm southwest and Mobile Regional Airport (KMOB) about 17 nm west. Naval outlying fields lie to the south and southeast in Baldwin County, so watch for military training traffic. Clear days show the delta at its best.
