# Spring Hill College

Founded in 1830 by a French bishop on a hill outside Mobile, Spring Hill College is the oldest private college in Alabama and the first Catholic college in the South.

**Location:** 30.6942, -88.1372

**Categories:** Universities and colleges in Mobile, Alabama, Jesuit universities and colleges in the United States, Catholic universities and colleges in Alabama, Educational institutions established in 1830, Historic places

## Introduction

The first classroom was a rented hotel. On May 1, 1830, Bishop Michael Portier of Mobile opened a college in a hotel beside the land he had bought on a hill near the city, with thirty students enrolled; the bishop himself taught theology to the six ecclesiastical students of that first year. Two months later, on July 4, he laid the cornerstone of a permanent building. Nearly two centuries on, Spring Hill College still occupies that same campus. It is the oldest private college or university in Alabama, the first Catholic college in the South, the fifth-oldest Catholic college in the United States and the third-oldest member of the Association of Jesuit Colleges and Universities.

## A Bishop's Errand to France

Portier, the first bishop of Mobile, was himself from France, and when he needed teachers and money he went home to find them. He came back with two priests and four seminarians to staff the school. A friend, Cardinal Joseph Fesch, Archbishop of Lyon, became a major benefactor, donating his philosophical and theological library along with works of art. Pauline Jaricot, founder of the Society of the Propagation of the Faith, is credited with a gift of 38,000 francs. The first permanent building, begun on that Fourth of July cornerstone, opened for classes in November 1831 on the site where the Administration Building stands today. In 1836 Governor Clement Comer Clay signed the legislative act chartering the college.

## Presidents Called Away

Keeping a leader proved harder than finding students. The college's first two presidents were both called away to become bishops, Mathias Loras to Dubuque, Iowa, and John Stephen Bazin to Vincennes, Indiana. The third, Mauvernay, died after a brief term. Portier then handed the college first to the French Fathers of Mercy and next to the Congregation of Jesus and Mary, but neither group had the teaching and administrative experience the work demanded. In September 1847 a new regime began with Francis Gautrelet as president, and the Jesuits have directed Spring Hill ever since. The historic Administration Building, whose renovation was completed in 2008, now carries the name of the college's 38th president: the Gregory F. Lucey, S.J. Administration Center.

## Oaks, Chapels and The Pit

Several of the campus's buildings are listed on the National Register of Historic Places: the Sodality Chapel, built in 1850; Stewartfield, from 1849; and the Spring Hill College Quadrangle, which gathers the 1869 Administration Building, St. Joseph's Chapel of 1910 and four other structures. Graduation traditionally happens along the Avenue of the Oaks. Sport has an old pedigree here too. Spring Hill has fielded a baseball team since its first intercollegiate game in 1889, and it plays at Sims-Galle Field, formerly Stan Galle Field and known as "The Pit," which the college bills as the oldest active collegiate baseball field in America. Frank Bolling, who won a Gold Glove at second base for the Detroit Tigers in 1958, came through the program, as did his brother Milt. On the water, the college won the North American Intercollegiate Sloop Championship, a national title in sailing, in 1986.

## Doors That Opened

The twentieth century brought milestones of a different kind. Fannie E. Motley became the college's first Black graduate in 1956. On July 27, 1963, Lee Harvey Oswald spoke on campus about life in the Soviet Union, invited by a cousin who was a student there; the talk was controversial in a Cold War America deeply opposed to communism, and it came months before he assassinated President John F. Kennedy. After Hurricane Katrina tore along the central Gulf Coast in 2005, Spring Hill took in 117 displaced students for the rest of the year, most of them from Loyola University in New Orleans, a sister Jesuit school. In 2014 it became the first school that is not a historically Black college or university to join the Southern Intercollegiate Athletic Conference, and it is scheduled to move to the Gulf South Conference in 2027. And on January 1, 2023, Mary H. Van Brunt took office as the college's first female president, 193 years after the hotel classes began.

## Flight Context

Spring Hill College sits at about 30.694 N, 88.137 W in the Spring Hill neighborhood of western Mobile, Alabama. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet, look for a wooded campus with an 18-hole golf course and the oak-lined Avenue of the Oaks, roughly halfway between downtown Mobile and Mobile Regional Airport. Mobile Regional Airport (KMOB) is about 5.5 nm west and Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown) about 5.4 nm southeast, so stay aware of traffic for both fields. Gulf Coast haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms are common.
