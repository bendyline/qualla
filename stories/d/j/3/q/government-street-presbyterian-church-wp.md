# Government Street Presbyterian Church

Built in the 1830s with a Mobile millionaire's backing, this downtown church keeps its original pews and one of the least-altered Greek Revival interiors in the United States.

**Location:** 30.6894, -88.0436

**Categories:** Churches in Mobile, Alabama, Greek Revival church buildings in Alabama, National Historic Landmarks in Alabama, Presbyterian churches in Alabama, Churches completed in 1837

## Introduction

The pews are the originals. So is nearly everything around them. Walk through the portico of Government Street Presbyterian Church in downtown Mobile and you enter a sanctuary whose Greek Revival design survives largely intact, with very little alteration since it was built in the 1830s. That rare continuity makes the building one of the oldest and least-altered Greek Revival churches in the United States, and it is now recognized as a National Historic Landmark.

## An Advertisement for Builders

The congregation formally organized in 1831 and at first worshipped in a frame building on Government Street. In the spring of 1835 it bought a lot at the corner of Government and Jackson streets for $10,000, and that December a church trustee named Henry Hitchcock, a local millionaire, placed an advertisement in the Mobile Daily Register calling for builders. Hitchcock sat on the building committee and paid for much of the construction himself. The drawings and specifications were already in hand, supplied by the architects Gallier and Dakin. The final bill came to $60,000, and the stonework explains where most of it went: $40,000 of that total was paid to the mason, Thomas James. Construction began in February 1836, and the masonry was finished by March 1837.

## Ancient Athens, Indoors

Three doors lead from the portico into the building. The southern main doors open straight into the main vestibule, while the eastern and western doors lead to side stairway vestibules serving the sanctuary and its upper gallery. Inside, two aisles run between the original pews. A U-shaped gallery rests on fluted Corinthian columns of the Tower of the Winds design, and its balcony carries a Greek key molding topped with rosettes. Much of the interior appears to draw on Minard Lafever's pattern book, The Beauties of Modern Architecture. Behind the altar rises a pylon-like screen with an engaged Corinthian tetrastyle crowned by antefixae, similar in design to the Choragic Monument of Lysicrates in Athens. Overhead, the ceiling is coffered in a diagonal diamond pattern.

## Sixty-One Ranks from Krnov

When the church undertook a major renovation in 2000, an organ committee took up the problem of the failing early-1900s Estey organ. It chose the European builder Rieger-Kloss, of Krnov in the Czech Republic, to make an instrument that could lead congregational and choral singing and handle recitals of any organ literature. The result has 61 ranks spread across six divisions: great, swell, positiv, grand choir, en chamade and pedal. It sits in the south gallery in three cabinets designed to match the sanctuary's architecture. The central cabinet holds the positiv division, voiced in the German baroque style, with the 8-foot and 16-foot en chamade pipes set atop the case. A detached four-manual English console on a movable platform stands at the center. In 2018 the console's control system was upgraded.

## Neighbors on Government Street

The church is not a museum. It belongs to the Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) through the Presbytery of South Alabama. Alongside worship, Christian education and a Ministry of Music, it hosts a Tuesday Lunch and Learn series. Its members reach out into downtown Mobile through Meals on Wheels, monthly lunches at McKemie Place, a residence program for homeless women, and support for Family Promise of Coastal Alabama and for nearby Barton Academy. For decades the church also owned Baytreat, a bayside retreat that the Meador family gave it in 1957 and that hosted youth mission trips; it has since passed to Trinity Presbyterian Church of Fairhope. Organists and students can even arrange to rehearse on the Rieger-Kloss by contacting the church office.

## Flight Context

Government Street Presbyterian Church stands at the corner of Government and Jackson streets in downtown Mobile, Alabama, at approximately 30.689°N, 88.044°W. The building itself is hard to single out from the air; use the downtown high-rises and the Mobile River waterfront just to the east as references, and fly at 1,500 to 2,500 feet for a view of the historic street grid. Nearby airports: Mobile International Airport (KBFM, formerly Mobile Downtown), about 4 nm south, and Mobile Regional Airport (KMOB), about 10 nm west. Expect Gulf Coast haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
