# Plaza Ferdinand VII

A two-acre green in downtown Pensacola named for a Spanish king is the traditional site where Spain handed West Florida to the United States in 1821.

**Location:** 30.4075, -87.2139

**Categories:** National Historic Landmarks in Florida, Parks in Escambia County, Florida, Buildings and structures in Pensacola, Florida, Tourist attractions in Pensacola, Florida, History of Florida

## Introduction

It is an odd sort of tribute. This leafy square on Palafox Street carries the name of Ferdinand VII, King of Spain, yet it is the place where Spain let go. In July 1821, in a formal ceremony on or beside the plaza, the Spanish flag was lowered and the American flag raised as West Florida passed to the United States, with Andrew Jackson in attendance. Today Plaza Ferdinand VII is a National Historic Landmark, a quiet garden on the edge of the Historic Pensacola Village area of downtown Pensacola.

## A Plan Drawn in 1765

The square is older than its name. It belongs to a street plan laid out in 1765, when Pensacola was the capital of the British colony of West Florida, and it was once the centerpiece of that plan. The original park was somewhat larger. In 1802, with West Florida back in Spanish hands, significant portions of it were subdivided and sold off, leaving the block that survives today. The British years left more behind than street lines. In 2002 archaeologists working here found evidence of British structures that no one had known existed in the area.

## The Flag Comes Down

Under the Adams–Onís Treaty, signed in 1819, Spain agreed to cede Florida to the United States, though the treaty did not take effect until February 22, 1821. That March, Andrew Jackson was given the powers of governor and the job of overseeing the transfer of East and West Florida from Spanish authorities. The handover came in two ceremonies. The first took place in St. Augustine on July 10, with Jackson's deputy, Robert Butler, serving as the chief United States representative. Jackson came to Pensacola for the second, held on or beside this plaza, where the Spanish flag was lowered and the American flag raised. Afterward he spoke to the townspeople and told them the land now belonged to the United States.

## Fountain, Obelisk and a General

Bounded by Government, Jefferson, Zaragoza and Palafox streets, the plaza covers about 2 acres of grass dotted with trees and crossed by paved walkways. Three features anchor it from north to south. A fountain stands near the northern end. At the center rises an obelisk honoring William Dudley Chipley, a railroad baron who served as mayor of Pensacola from 1887 to 1888. Near the southern end is a memorial to Andrew Jackson, featuring a bust of the man who stood here in 1821. The plaza was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960 and was automatically included in the National Register of Historic Places when that program began in 1966. In 1989 it was also listed in A Guide to Florida's Historic Architecture, published by the University of Florida Press.

## Life Around the Square

The plaza has hosted celebrations as well as ceremonies; it was once the site of Pensacola's Pelican Drop on New Year's Eve, a tradition that later moved to Palafox and Garden streets and was last held downtown in 2019. Historic buildings frame it. The Pensacola Museum of History can be seen from the park, and the Saenger Theatre stands nearby. On Zaragoza Street, the Quayside Art Gallery occupies an 1873 firehouse at number 17 East, and the Federalist House stands at number 11 East.

## Flight Context

Plaza Ferdinand VII is a two-acre green block in downtown Pensacola, Florida, at approximately 30.408°N, 87.214°W, on Palafox Street a short distance north of the Pensacola Bay waterfront. It is small, so look for the tree-dotted rectangle among the downtown blocks from 1,500 to 2,500 feet. Pensacola International Airport (KPNS) is about 4 nm to the north-northeast, and Naval Air Station Pensacola Forrest Sherman Field (KNPA) lies about 6 nm to the west-southwest; stay clear of its busy military training airspace. Expect Gulf Coast haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
