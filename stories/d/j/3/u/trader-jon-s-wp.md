# Trader Jon's

For fifty years a Pensacola bar made famous by a man in mismatched socks was a gathering place and a museum for generations of naval aviators.

**Location:** 30.4071, -87.2142

**Categories:** Bars (establishments), Defunct restaurants in Florida, Pensacola, Florida, Naval aviation, Buildings and structures in Pensacola, Florida

## Introduction

At Trader Jon's, the price of a drink was negotiable, though not by the customer. Martin Weissman, the owner everyone called Trader Jon, handed out discounts or free drinks depending on his mood and how well he knew the person across the bar, a practice that became known as "Tradernomics." From 1953 to 2003 the bar he founded in downtown Pensacola was part watering hole, part shrine to naval aviation, crowded with military memorabilia and with the young aviators training at the bases nearby.

## Shoes, Ship Stores and a Paratrooper

The building was already old when the bar moved in. Erected in 1896, it had spent the decades from the 1890s to the 1950s housing the Samuel Charles Shoe and Leather Establishment, Birgar Testman's ship chandlery and a long list of other tenants. On January 1, 1953, Weissman and his wife, Jackie Bond, opened Trader Jon's there. Weissman had taken a roundabout road to Pensacola. During World War II he trained as a U.S. Army paratrooper, but he broke his ankle before his scheduled deployment to Europe and was discharged. He went on to run bars in Miami and Key West before settling in the Panhandle.

## Mismatched Socks and Gold Wings

Behind the bar, Trader Jon was a character. He was famous for tending bar in shorts and mismatched socks, and for eccentricities that became part of the place's legend. His location did the rest. With Naval Air Station Pensacola, NAS Whiting Field and the former fields at Saufley, Ellyson and Corry all nearby, the bar filled with officer flight students from the Navy, Marine Corps and Coast Guard, including the non-commissioned Naval Aviation Candidates known as NAVCADs, along with flight instructors and senior staff officers. Officer candidates from the Navy's since-disestablished Aviation Officer Candidate School came too, and once the Blue Angels made NAS Pensacola their home base, the Navy's flight demonstration squadron became regulars as well. The bar's large collection of memorabilia, devoted mainly to naval aviation, grew to match its clientele. Celebrities visited too, among them John Wayne and Larry King.

## Smoke in the Blue Angels Room

In September 1997 Weissman suffered a stroke that left him partly paralyzed and with impaired speech. He never fully recovered, and he died on February 18, 2000, at the age of 84. The bar had already closed and gone up for sale in 1998. It reopened on September 1, 2000, months after his death and on what would have been his 85th birthday, under a new owner, a Navy aviator who kept the theme. On January 16, 2001, with six people inside, customers noticed smoke coming from the room that housed the bar's Blue Angels Museum. Two of them ran toward it, saving as many pieces of historical memorabilia as they could, and the Pensacola Fire Department stopped the fire before it could spread through the rest of the building. Trader Jon's held on for nearly three more years before it closed for good in November 2003, some fifty years after it first opened its doors.

## Flight Context

Trader Jon's stood on Palafox Street in downtown Pensacola, Florida, at approximately 30.407°N, 87.214°W, a short distance north of the Pensacola Bay waterfront. The bar itself is gone, but the downtown grid and the bay make a good visual reference from 1,500 to 2,500 feet. The naval air stations that filled its barstools are close by: Naval Air Station Pensacola Forrest Sherman Field (KNPA), home of the Blue Angels, lies about 6 nm west-southwest, and NAS Whiting Field (North Field, KNSE) lies about 21 nm to the north-northeast near Milton; both have busy military training airspace. Pensacola International Airport (KPNS) is about 4 nm to the north-northeast. Expect Gulf Coast haze and summer afternoon thunderstorms.
