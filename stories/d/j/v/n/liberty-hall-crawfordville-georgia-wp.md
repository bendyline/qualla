# Liberty Hall (Crawfordville, Georgia)

The Crawfordville home of Alexander H. Stephens, congressman, Confederate vice president and Georgia governor, where the name Liberty Hall stands in hard contrast to the slave quarters behind the house and the words its owner spoke in 1861.

## Introduction

The house is called Liberty Hall. Its owner, Alexander H. Stephens, stood before an audience in Savannah on March 21, 1861, as vice president of the new Confederate States of America, and declared that its "corner-stone rests, upon the great truth that the negro is not equal to the white man." Behind his Crawfordville home, restored outbuildings include the quarters where enslaved people lived. Few historic houses hold such a stark contradiction in plain view. Liberty Hall, now a National Historic Landmark and the centerpiece of a Georgia state park, is worth visiting precisely because it does not let that contradiction hide.

## An Orphan Comes Home

Stephens was born two miles north of here, in a log cabin on his father's farm, and he was orphaned at the age of fourteen. Loss ran through the family's story; as one account of the house puts it, the family home "had been sold out from under the brothers when their father died." Stephens made Liberty Hall his residence by the mid-1840s (accounts give 1839 or 1845) and kept it as his home until his death in 1883. For a time it went by another name as well; in 1859 it was known as Bachelor's Hall. Between 1872 and 1875 he tore down the old house, keeping only two rear rooms, and built the structure that stands today. It has been renovated to its 1875 appearance, so visitors see the house he built in his later years.

## A Career in Four Offices

Stephens represented Georgia in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1843 to 1859, then served as vice president of the Confederate States of America from 1861 to 1865. His Cornerstone Speech in Savannah, delivered early in that term, set out in plain words the racial hierarchy on which he said the new government was founded. After the Civil War, Georgians sent him back to the U.S. House, where he served from 1873 to 1882, and then elected him governor, an office he held from 1882 until he died in 1883. The house in Crawfordville was his home through nearly all of it, from his years in Washington to his last months as governor.

## Behind the House

Walk around to the rear of Liberty Hall and the working household comes into focus. The restored outbuildings include a wine cellar, a smokehouse, a woodhouse, a washhouse, a chickenhouse and slave quarters, the last housing people whose labor Stephens claimed as his property. Accounts differ on how many people Stephens held in slavery on the eve of emancipation, giving figures from thirty-one to thirty-four, and one account says most of them remained with him after the war ended. The sources used here name only two of them, Eliza and Harry, a married couple who stayed until his death in 1883, and they do not explain why anyone stayed, so it is worth resisting any easy conclusion. They were men, women and children whose lives were bound to this property by law before the war and by circumstance after it, and the small quarters behind the big house are the most direct trace they left here.

## Rio, a Sitting Room and a Park

Stephens's constant companion at Liberty Hall was a large, fluffy white dog named Rio. After Stephens died in office in 1883, his sitting room was preserved as he had left it, and the house passed to surviving relatives. It served as a boardinghouse until 1932, when it was donated to the State of Georgia. The following year, the Civilian Conservation Corps began building most of what became A. H. Stephens Historic Park, a state park maintained by the Georgia Department of Natural Resources with campsites, picnic areas, fishing ponds, a nature trail and rustic cabins. Liberty Hall was added to the National Register of Historic Places on May 13, 1970, and designated a National Historic Landmark on May 4, 1983, a century after its owner's death. The site also includes a memorial to Stephens himself.

## Flight Context

Liberty Hall stands in Crawfordville, Taliaferro County, Georgia, in the eastern Georgia Piedmont, at 33.5578N, 82.8958W. From 1,500-2,500 feet, look for the small town of Crawfordville and the fishing ponds of A. H. Stephens Historic Park. Greene County Regional Airport at Greensboro (FAA code CPP, GPS ident KCPP) is about 12 nm west-northwest, and Washington-Wilkes County Airport (FAA code IIY, GPS ident KIIY) about 14 nm north-northeast. Thomson-McDuffie County Airport (FAA code HQU, GPS ident KHQU) is about 19 nm east. Athens Ben Epps Airport (KAHN) lies about 32 nm northwest and Augusta Regional (KAGS) about 48 nm east-southeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common over the Piedmont.
