# 1986 Masters Tournament

At Augusta National in April 1986, 46-year-old Jack Nicklaus played the back nine in 30 on Sunday to win his sixth Masters and 18th major, becoming the oldest Masters champion.

**Location:** 33.5030, -82.0200

**Categories:** Masters Tournament, 1986 in golf, 1986 in American sports, 1986 in sports in Georgia (U.S. state), April 1986 sports events in the United States

## Introduction

"Maybe... Yes sir!" That was all CBS commentator Verne Lundquist could manage as Jack Nicklaus's 18-foot birdie putt dropped on the 17th green at Augusta National on April 13, 1986. With that putt, a 46-year-old golfer took sole possession of the lead in the Masters for the first time all week. He had started the final round tied for ninth. By the end of the day he had won his sixth Masters and his 18th professional major, both records, and the 50th Masters Tournament had its historic finish.

## An Old Bear in Ninth Place

Nicklaus arrived at the final round at two under par, tied for ninth after 54 holes. The early leaderboard belonged to other men. Seve Ballesteros hit his second shot at the 13th to within six feet and holed the putt for his second eagle of the day, building a three-shot lead. Nicklaus, meanwhile, played his first eight holes in even par. Then he stirred. Birdies at 9, 10 and 11 pulled him into contention, but a bogey at the par-3 12th dropped him three behind the leaders again. No one his age had ever won the Masters. The previous oldest Masters champion, Gary Player, had been 42 when he won in 1978, and Nicklaus's first Masters title had come way back in 1963, less than ten months after his first major victory at the 1962 U.S. Open.

## Thirty on the Back Nine

What followed became golf legend. Nicklaus made eagle at the 15th, and CBS's Ben Wright cried, "Yes sir! The battle is joined. My goodness. There is life in the old Bear yet." Then Ballesteros, in prime position to go for the same green in two, pull-hooked his approach into the water, and Wright called it again: "And the foreign invasion is reeling under the Bear's attack!" Ballesteros bogeyed the hole and later three-putted the 17th to fall out of contention. Nicklaus rolled in a three-foot birdie at 16, prompting Jim Nantz to declare that "the Bear has come out of hibernation," then holed the 18-footer at 17 and parred the last. His back nine of 30, six under par even with that bogey at 12, tied the course record for the second nine, a mark Nick Price had also reached the day before. His final-round 65 left him at 279, nine under par.

## Kite, Norman and the Waiting

Then Nicklaus had to wait while two men tried to catch him. Tom Kite came to the 18th with 12 feet for a birdie that would have tied, and his putt barely slipped past on the low side. Greg Norman's charge was even more dramatic. After a double bogey at the 10th, he birdied 14, 15 and 16, then threaded a shot between two pines at the 17th to eight feet and holed the putt to tie for the lead. A perfect drive on 18 left him needing a birdie for his first major championship, or a par to force a playoff. Instead he pushed his approach into the gallery and missed a 15-foot par putt. Kite and Norman finished one shot back at 280, with Ballesteros fourth and Nick Price fifth. Both runners-up would be remembered for their near-misses at Augusta.

## Records That Still Stand Out

The numbers from that week still astonish. Nicklaus became the oldest winner in Masters history, and at the time the second-oldest winner of any major championship in the modern era, behind Julius Boros, who was 48 when he took the 1968 PGA Championship; both were later passed by Phil Mickelson, who won the 2021 PGA Championship at 50. The victory stretched Nicklaus's span of Masters wins to 23 years and of major wins to 24, both records. His winner's share was $144,000, more than seven times what he earned for his first Augusta victory. The week had other heroes too. A day before Nicklaus's charge, Nick Price shot a third-round 63, a new course record that beat the 64 Lloyd Mangrum had set in 1940, with ten birdies, then a single-round record at Augusta. Gary Koch won the Par 3 Contest, and Sam Randolph was low amateur for the second straight year. But the week belongs, forever, to the Bear.
