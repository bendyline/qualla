# 2019 Masters Tournament

Fourteen years after his last green jacket, a 43-year-old Tiger Woods came from behind on Augusta's back nine to win the 2019 Masters, a victory widely regarded as one of the great comebacks in sports.

**Location:** 33.5030, -82.0200

**Categories:** Masters Tournament, 2019 in golf, Golf history, Augusta, Georgia, April 2019 sports events in the United States

## Introduction

The wind was swirling on the 12th tee. It was Sunday at the 2019 Masters, and the leading group had reached the par-3 12th in Amen Corner, where Rae's Creek waits in front of the green. Francesco Molinari, the reigning Open champion, had held the lead from the start of the round. He aimed at the flag, and his ball landed on the false front of the green and rolled back into the water. Standing on the same tee, Tiger Woods chose not to attack the pin and put his shot in the center of the green. Molinari made double bogey, Woods tied him, and a tournament that had been Molinari's to lose began to turn toward one of the most celebrated comebacks in the history of sport.

## Before the Storm

The 83rd Masters, played April 11 to 14 at Augusta National Golf Club in Augusta, Georgia, opened golf's major season with other names at the top of the betting. Most of the media and the Las Vegas bookmakers made Rory McIlroy, Justin Rose and Dustin Johnson the favorites. McIlroy arrived as the Players champion with seven consecutive top-10 finishes on the PGA Tour but could only tie for 21st, and Rose, the world number one, missed the cut for the first time in his fourteen Masters appearances. The course played slightly longer, too, with the fifth hole, Magnolia, stretched by 40 yards. In the traditional par-3 contest on Wednesday, Matt Wallace beat Sandy Lyle on the third playoff hole after both finished at 22, five under par. Four aces were recorded, by Wallace, Mark O'Meara, Shane Lowry and the amateur Devon Bling.

## Records on the Way In

Woods opened with a 70, one of ten players tied for eleventh. On Friday, thunderstorms forced a 29-minute delay, and when play finished the Masters' rule that anyone within ten shots of the lead makes the cut had set the line at 147, three over par. Sixty-five players advanced, the most since the cut was introduced in 1957, and a handful of amateurs made it. Among those going home was Ian Woosnam, the 1991 champion, who shortly afterward announced his retirement as an active Masters competitor. Saturday brought a scoring spree. Webb Simpson, Patrick Cantlay and Tony Finau each shot 64, the first day in Masters history with three rounds of 64 or better, and the field combined to play the round 80 under par, reported as the lowest in tournament history.

## Water at Twelve, Water at Fifteen

Amen Corner punished more than Molinari on Sunday. Brooks Koepka and Ian Poulter both found Rae's Creek on the 12th and made double bogey, and Tony Finau, playing in the final group, did the same. By the time that group stood on the 15th, five players were tied at twelve under: Molinari, Woods, Xander Schauffele, Dustin Johnson and Koepka. Molinari drove into the trees, followed with a misplaced lay-up, and from an awkward angle clipped an overhanging branch. His ball dropped into the pond guarding the front of the green, and another double bogey followed. Woods made birdie and took the lead alone. On the 16th his tee shot came within inches of going in before stopping a few feet from the cup, and he converted for another birdie. A tap-in par at 17 left him needing only a bogey on the 18th. He chipped on, two-putted, and won by a single stroke over Johnson, Schauffele and Koepka.

## Fourteen Years Between Jackets

The numbers were remarkable. It was Woods's fifth green jacket and fifteenth major title, leaving him one short of Jack Nicklaus's record six Masters wins and three short of Nicklaus's eighteen majors. At 43 he became the second-oldest Masters champion, behind only Nicklaus, who won at 46. Nearly eleven years had passed since his last major, the 2008 U.S. Open, making him the fifth player to go more than a decade between major wins, after Henry Cotton, Julius Boros, Hale Irwin and Ben Crenshaw. The fourteen years since his previous Masters victory broke Gary Player's record gap of thirteen. It was also the first time Woods had won a major without leading after 54 holes. Because of his fame, and his long fall from the top of the game through personal issues and injuries, the victory drew enormous publicity around the world. Elsewhere on the leaderboard, Norway's Viktor Hovland took the Silver Cup as low amateur at three under.

## Now I'm the Dad

In Butler Cabin, where the champion is traditionally interviewed, Woods admitted he was a little hoarse from yelling and at a loss for words. "When I tapped the putt in, I don't know what I did, but I know I screamed," he said. He talked about missing a couple of years of the tournament and feeling lucky just to be playing again the year before. His mother was there, he noted, just as she had been in 1997, the year he first won at Augusta. "To have my kids there, it's come full circle. My dad was here in '97, and now I'm the dad with two kids there."

## Flight Context

Augusta National Golf Club sits at about 33.503 N, 82.020 W in Augusta, Georgia. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the course's fairways, greens and ponds; Rae's Creek runs in front of the 12th green. Daniel Field (KDNL) is about 2.4 nm away and Augusta Regional Airport at Bush Field (KAGS) about 8.5 nm to the south-southeast. In tournament week each April, traffic is heavy and temporary flight restrictions may apply, so check NOTAMs before planning a pass. Spring visibility is usually good, but thunderstorms, like the ones that halted play for 29 minutes in 2019, can build quickly in the afternoon.
