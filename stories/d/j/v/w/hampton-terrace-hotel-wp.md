# Hampton Terrace Hotel

Billed as the most magnificent winter resort in the world and reportedly the largest wooden structure on Earth, North Augusta's Hampton Terrace Hotel hosted Rockefeller and Taft before burning to the ground in the early hours of New Year's Eve 1916.

**Location:** 33.5025, -81.9689

**Categories:** Demolished hotels in the United States, Hotels in South Carolina, North Augusta, South Carolina, 1916 fires in the United States, 1903 establishments in South Carolina

## Introduction

"The Most Magnificent Winter Resort in the World." That was how the advertisements described the Hampton Terrace Hotel when it opened in December 1903 on a hill in North Augusta, South Carolina. Five stories tall, with 300 rooms, it had cost more than $500,000 and was reportedly the largest wooden structure in the world. Thirteen years later, in the early hours of New Year's Eve 1916, it was gone.

## A Town Built Around a Hotel

The hotel was the centerpiece of James U. Jackson's plan to turn North Augusta, the town he founded, into a resort. In April 1902 Jackson's youngest son ceremonially broke ground, with the Woodward Lumber Company as general contractor. The builders chose a hill overlooking the surrounding country, and it was later said that from the top floor visitors could see sights more than 50 miles away. The hotel was supposed to open by January 1, 1903, and that month The Augusta Chronicle was already calling it "One of the Very Finest in the Country." In the end it took nearly the whole year, and the doors opened that December.

## Tycoons and a President-Elect

The Hampton Terrace was a hit from the start, and it drew notable guests: the businessmen Marshall Field, Harvey S. Firestone and John D. Rockefeller, and William Howard Taft, then President-elect of the United States, who was honored with a banquet at the hotel in 1909. Open each year from January to May, it offered guests a 9-hole golf course, later expanded to 18, and a 15,000-acre game preserve, along with gentler pastimes such as shuffleboard and ping-pong. The course's second hole, at 593 yards, was considered one of the longest golf holes in the world at the time. For those few months each year, Jackson's resort-town dream looked very much like it was working.

## New Year's Eve, 1916

In the early morning of December 31, 1916, a watchman discovered fire in the building. At 2:10 a.m. the fire department across the Savannah River in Augusta, Georgia, received the call, and four companies were soon on their way. By 3 a.m. the flames had spread, and firefighters ordered everyone out. The hotel housed about 100 staff members at the time, preparing for the coming season. Low water pressure and hose connections that did not match slowed the firefighting, and witnesses said the fire burned for about five hours, while the coal stored in the basement smoldered far longer. The cause was later reported to be a short circuit in the wiring, on the third floor by one account. Remarkably, no one was seriously hurt or killed. The building, however, was completely destroyed. Jackson at first planned to rebuild, but the grand hotel on the hill never rose again.

## Flight Context

The Hampton Terrace Hotel stood at about 33.5025°N, 81.9689°W on a hill in North Augusta, South Carolina, just across the Savannah River from Augusta, Georgia. Little if anything of the hotel remains (brick chimneys were left standing after the fire), so from 1,500–2,500 feet use the Savannah River and the Augusta skyline across it as references for the North Augusta hillside. Daniel Field (KDNL) is about 5 miles southwest, Augusta Regional at Bush Field (KAGS) about 9 miles south, and Aiken Regional (KAIK) about 19 miles northeast. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common in the river valley.
