# 1997 Masters Tournament

At Augusta National in April 1997, a 21-year-old Tiger Woods recovered from a four-over front nine to win his first major by twelve strokes, the largest margin in Masters history.

**Location:** 33.5030, -82.0200

**Categories:** Masters Tournament, 1997 in golf, Golf history, Augusta, Georgia, April 1997 sports events in the United States

## Introduction

Nine holes into the 1997 Masters, Tiger Woods had shot a 40. At Augusta National, that is four over par, the kind of start that ends a week before it begins. Then, on the back nine of that first round, the 21-year-old made four birdies and an eagle, came home in 30, and signed for a 70. It was the beginning of one of the most lopsided performances the tournament had ever seen. Held April 10 to 13 at Augusta National Golf Club in Augusta, Georgia, the 61st Masters ended with Woods twelve strokes clear of the field and the record book rewritten.

## Forty, Then Thirty

That opening 70 left Woods three shots behind John Huston, who led the first round with a 67, one stroke ahead of Paul Stankowski. Few could have guessed that the leaderboard would never look that close again. On Friday Woods shot 66, and it gave him his first lead in a professional major championship, three shots ahead of Colin Montgomerie of Scotland. Saturday was more decisive still. Woods posted a 65, the best score of the day for the second day running, reaching 201, fifteen under par, while Montgomerie slid to a 74 and a tie for sixth. With eighteen holes left, Woods led by nine.

## A Win for the Ages

A final-round 69 brought Woods home at 270, eighteen under par. That broke the tournament record of 271, set by Jack Nicklaus in 1965 and matched by Raymond Floyd in 1976, and it stood as the Masters scoring record, matched by Jordan Spieth in 2015, until Dustin Johnson bettered it in 2020. His twelve-stroke margin over runner-up Tom Kite remains the largest in the tournament's history. The victory was Woods's first major championship, and it carried meaning well beyond the numbers. At 21 he became the youngest player to win at Augusta, and the first Black player, and the first of Asian heritage, to do so. As his final putt dropped on the 18th green, Jim Nantz of CBS Sports told viewers: "There it is, a win for the ages!"

## More Than Forty Million Watching

The country was paying attention. Woods's victory set television ratings records for golf, and the Sunday broadcast of the final round was seen by an estimated 43 to 44 million viewers in the United States, depending on the source. Many of them were watching a young player beat a field thick with the game's history. Past champions including Arnold Palmer, Jack Nicklaus and Gary Player teed it up that week alongside Seve Ballesteros, Nick Faldo and Tom Watson, while legends such as Ben Hogan, Byron Nelson, Gene Sarazen and Sam Snead held invitations but did not play.

## How He Got In

Woods's path into the field had its own small twist. As U.S. Amateur champion he held an invitation, but he forfeited it when he turned professional. It did not matter. By the time the Masters came around, he had qualified again on other grounds, as a winner of a PGA Tour event since the previous Masters and as one of the top 30 players on the 1996 PGA Tour money list. He arrived at Augusta as a professional, and he left it as the champion.

## Flight Context

Augusta National Golf Club lies at about 33.503 N, 82.020 W within the city of Augusta, Georgia. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet AGL, the course's long fairways, greens and water hazards stand out among the surrounding neighborhoods. Augusta's Daniel Field (KDNL) is only about 2.4 nm away, and Augusta Regional Airport at Bush Field (KAGS) about 8.5 nm to the south-southeast. During tournament week in April, expect heavy traffic and check NOTAMs for any temporary flight restrictions. Spring usually brings good visibility, though afternoon thunderstorms can build.
