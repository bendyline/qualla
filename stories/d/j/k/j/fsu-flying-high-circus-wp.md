# FSU Flying High Circus

Since 1947, Florida State University students have run their own three-ring circus in Tallahassee, flying the trapeze, walking the wire, sewing the costumes and raising the tent themselves.

**Location:** 30.4404, -84.3025

**Categories:** Florida State University, American circuses, Acrobatic sports, Student organizations, Tallahassee, Florida

## Introduction

There are no lions in this circus and no elephants, and no professional crew raises the tent. At Florida State University in Tallahassee, the people hanging the rigging are the same people who will fly from it that night. Every member of the FSU Flying High Circus is a degree-seeking student who earned a place by audition, and together they rig their own equipment, put up the big top, sew their own costumes and run their own lights and sound. Then they climb up and perform a three-ring show of aerial and stage acts that has been running since 1947.

## A Circus for a Co-ed Campus

The circus was founded in 1947 by Jack Haskin, as an extracurricular activity at a newly co-educational institution, and it was created in part to bring the campus's men and women together. Haskin led it until 1960, and the program has had only a handful of directors since: Addison Gilbert, Adrian Catarzi, Richard "Dickie" Brinson, whose tenure ran from 1972 to 2007, and Chad Mathews, who took over in 2007. Today the circus sits under the university's Division of Student Affairs, and students who want a taste without joining can take a one-credit course, "Circus Activities," that covers juggling, hand balancing, aerial ballet and the rigging that keeps it all in the air.

## The Gift of a Whole Circus

One of the stranger chapters in the program's history involves a professional show. In the mid-1950s the Clyde Beatty Circus was bought and merged with the Cole Bros. Circus by Jerry Collins, Frank McClosky, Walter Kernan and Randolph Calhoun. The combined Clyde Beatty-Cole Brothers Circus traded railroad cars for trucks and began wintering in DeLand, Florida.

In 1981 Collins gave the entire circus, valued at $2.5 million, to Florida State. He said he wanted to "benefit people of all ages." The university held on to its gift for about a year. In 1982 FSU sold it to John W. Pugh for $2 million, and Pugh formed The New Cole Bros. Circus.

## Flying Seminoles and a Bicycle Built for Five

Acts change from season to season, but the lineup reads like a catalog of circus tradition. The flying trapeze troupe goes by "The Flying Seminoles." There are high wire and slack wire, Roman rings and Russian bar, a cloud swing and a sky pole, teeterboard, Chinese pole, fire poi, rola bola, and a bicycle built for five.

The circus pays its own way by performing for sponsors across the southeastern United States. Each spring it stages "Homeshows" at the Jack Haskin Circus Complex on campus, traditionally on weekends in April, free to students with an FSU ID. There are performances for Parents Weekend too, and in recent years a Halloween show that puts the classic acts in costume.

## Summers at Callaway Gardens

According to Wikipedia, since 1961 the circus has run a summer recreation program at Callaway Gardens Resort in Georgia. Around 25 students live and work there for twelve weeks, performing several times a week and serving as counselors for the resort's Summer Family Adventure, working with campers from 7 to 18 and running a ropes course for teens and adults. In 2009 the circus added a summer camp on the FSU campus for ages 7 to 17, where campers try flying trapeze, cloud swing, juggling and more; the circus says the camp "will return in 2027."

Not every season has gone to plan. COVID-19 forced the circus to suspend its programs during the pandemic. On May 10, 2024, tornadoes destroyed the circus's big top, and a new, larger tent arrived in March 2025. But the model that Haskin started in 1947 has held: students who learn to fly also learn to build the stage they fly over.

## Flight Context

The Flying High Circus is based on the Florida State University campus at about 30.440 N, 84.303 W, just west of downtown Tallahassee, Florida. From 1,500-2,500 ft the dense campus, with its large stadium, sits beside the capitol district; the circus complex is a small feature within it. Tallahassee International Airport (KTLH) is about 3.6 nm to the southwest, so the campus lies within Tallahassee's busy terminal airspace; coordinate with approach control. Other nearby fields include Cairo-Grady County Airport in Georgia (70J), about 28 nm north-northeast, and Thomasville Regional (KTVI), about 35 nm northeast. Expect afternoon thunderstorms in summer.
