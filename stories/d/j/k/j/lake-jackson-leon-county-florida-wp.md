# Lake Jackson (Leon County, Florida)

Just north of Tallahassee, Lake Jackson has a habit of disappearing, draining through sinkholes in its own bed into the Floridan Aquifer, then slowly filling again.

**Location:** 30.5294, -84.3225

**Categories:** Lakes of Leon County, Florida, Geography of Tallahassee, Florida, Sinkholes, Karst landforms, Wildlife viewing

## Introduction

Every so often, Lake Jackson simply leaves. The water that usually spreads across roughly 16 square kilometers of shallow prairie lake on the north side of Tallahassee funnels down through openings in its own bed and disappears into the Floridan Aquifer, sometimes leaving nothing behind but scattered pools. Newspapers were reporting these disappearances as early as 1829, and each time the lake has returned. But for a while, the drain holes of Lake Jackson, two sinkholes called Porter Sink and Lime Sink, are open for everyone to see.

## Plugs in the Bottom

Lake Jackson sits in the Red Hills Region of north Florida, about 12 kilometers long, and no stream carries its water away. Instead the lake drains downward. Its two major depressions, Porter Sink and Lime Sink, connect to the aquifer below. Most of the time they are partly or completely plugged with sediment. When groundwater levels drop, those plugs can collapse, and the lake water funnels straight into the aquifer, sometimes until the lake is completely empty. The level swings accordingly: over time the lake has ranged from bone dry to a maximum elevation of 96 feet above sea level.

## A Record of Vanishing Acts

Newspaper accounts describe the lake draining in 1829, again in 1840 and again in 1860. It went down a sinkhole in June 1907 and drained once more in the summer of 1909. Then came November 1918, January 1932 and October 1936. It drained in 1956 and 1957, and again in 1981 and 1982.

The pattern has continued into the modern era, with drainings in 1999, May 2007, June 2012 and June 2021. During the 2007 episode, the water poured down Porter Sink, though pools remained scattered across the bed. Since June 2021 the sinkhole has drained repeatedly: the aquatic preserve says the lake was high from December 2023 to October 2025, and that Porter Hole is once again drained, with only small boats able to launch under the current low water.

## Limpkins, Wood Storks and a Wandering Pelican

When the water is in, large numbers of waterbirds gather here. Great blue herons and little blue herons, great egrets and snowy egrets, limpkins, moorhens, American coots and wood storks feed in the shallows, and ospreys, bald eagles, fish crows and least terns are found here too. Brown pelicans, rare so far inland, have turned up on occasion.

American alligators live here too, along with southern chorus frogs, southern leopard frogs and Florida softshell turtles. On the shoreline lives one of the lake's less familiar mammals, the round-tailed muskrat.

## Lotus, Hydrilla and a Preserve

Beneath the surface, submerged plants grow thickly: blue hyssop, coontail, green fanwort, variable-leaf milfoil and bladderwort. In the marshes stand maidencane, pickerelweed, American lotus and slender spikerush, and on the drier ground of the transitional marsh grow sweetgum, several kinds of oak, wax myrtle, Carolina willow and elderberry. Not every plant belongs. Invasive Chinese tallow and hydrilla have taken hold in some areas.

Since 1973 the lake has been part of the Lake Jackson Aquatic Preserve, together with Lake Carr and Mallard Pond. Interstate 10 crosses its southern tip, and beside the lake lies the Lake Jackson Mounds Archaeological State Park, a reminder that people have been drawn to these shores for a very long time.

## Flight Context

Lake Jackson is centered near 30.529 N, 84.323 W, on the north side of Tallahassee, Florida, with Interstate 10 crossing its southern tip. From 2,000-3,000 ft it appears as a long, shallow, marsh-edged lake, its extent varying dramatically depending on whether it has recently drained; the Lake Jackson Mounds lie beside it. Tallahassee International Airport (KTLH) is about 8 nm to the south, and the lake lies within reach of Tallahassee's terminal airspace; coordinate with approach control. Other nearby fields: Cairo-Grady County Airport in Georgia (70J), about 23 nm north-northeast; Decatur County Industrial Air Park at Bainbridge, Georgia (KBGE), about 31 nm north-northwest; Thomasville Regional (KTVI), about 32 nm northeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
