# Alfred B. Maclay Gardens State Park

Cotton plantation, vineyard, quail-hunting estate and finally a New York financier's garden of azaleas and camellias, Maclay Gardens layers more than a century of Tallahassee history around three lakes.

**Location:** 30.5189, -84.2511

## Introduction

For twenty-two years this corner of Tallahassee made wine. A vineyard planted here in 1882 produced it until 1904, when Leon County voted to go dry and the grapes lost their purpose. The land has reinvented itself again and again since then, from quail-hunting estate to a financier's private garden to a public park. Today the Alfred B. Maclay Gardens State Park spreads across about 1,170 acres on Thomasville Road, a botanical garden, historic site and state park rolled into one, where azaleas and camellias bloom beside quiet lakes.

## Cotton, Grapes and Quail

The property's story runs back to the Lafayette Land Grant. In antebellum times it was part of the Andalusia Plantation, which grew cotton in a county where planters built their cotton plantations on enslaved labor. Then, in 1882, came the vineyard, and after its end in 1904, yet another use. The land became a quail-hunting estate called Lac-Cal. That is how Alfred Barmore Maclay found it. In 1923 the New York financier, son of Robert Maclay, and his wife, Louise Fleischmann, bought the 1,935-acre Lac-Cal plantation along with adjoining land, assembling a 3,760-acre estate. Maclay named it Killearn, after his ancestral village in Scotland, the birthplace of his great-grandfather.

## Louise Maclay's Gift

Alfred Maclay shaped the gardens until his death in 1944, and his wife carried the work on without him. Louise Maclay opened the gardens to the public in 1946, and in 1953 she donated some 307 acres of the estate, including the gardens, to a predecessor of the Florida Department of Environmental Protection. The gardens kept the Killearn name until 1965, when a new development called Killearn Estates took shape next door, and to avoid confusion they were renamed in Alfred Maclay's honor. The 1909 Maclay House, where the couple lived, still stands, furnished to look as it did during the Maclays' residence, and it opens for tours during the high blooming season from January through April. In 2002 the grounds were listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Killearn Plantation Archeological and Historic District, counting 18 historic buildings, four structures and four objects.

## A Garden Built on Azaleas

Azaleas and camellias form the backbone of the plantings, and around them grows a rich cast. Among the trees are bald cypress, black gum, dogwood, hickory, holly, redbud, oak, Japanese maple and Torreya taxifolia. Lower down, gardenia, jasmine, wisteria, ginger, mountain laurel, sago palm, palmetto and Oriental magnolia fill the beds. At the front of the gardens, the Magnolia Chapter of the Florida Native Plant Society tends a Native Plant Arboretum devoted to the region's own species, among them Piedmont azaleas, Florida flame azaleas, eastern red columbine, white wild indigo and Florida anise. Winter into spring is the time to come. The house tours are timed to the high blooming season, January through April, when the garden is at its most colorful.

## Three Lakes and Open Trails

Water gives the park its shape. Lake Hall, Lake Overstreet and Lake Elizabeth, also called Gum Pond, lie within its bounds, and beyond the formal gardens the park opens up for recreation. Visitors come to bicycle, hike, ride horses, watch birds, fish, swim, and paddle canoes and kayaks, and there are picnic areas and extensive walking trails. A museum offers interpretive exhibits. From cotton to wine grapes to quail to camellias, the park holds more than a century of changing uses in a single landscape.

## Flight Context

Maclay Gardens lies at 30.5189 N, 84.2511 W on the north side of Tallahassee, along Thomasville Road. From the air look for Lake Hall and the park's other lakes, with the Killearn Estates neighborhood adjacent. A viewing altitude of 1,500 to 2,500 feet MSL shows the lakes and gardens well. Tallahassee International Airport (KTLH) is about 9 nm to the southwest, and Thomasville Regional Airport (KTVI) in Georgia about 30 nm to the northeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions, ideally during the January-to-April blooming season.
