# Ebenezer, Georgia

Founded in 1734 by Protestant exiles from Salzburg who dreamed of a religious utopia on the Georgia frontier, Ebenezer spun silk, briefly served as the state capital and then emptied out, leaving behind what is described as Georgia's oldest church building.

## Introduction

In 1731, Prince-archbishop Count Leopold Anton von Firmian issued an edict that expelled Protestants from the Catholic Archbishopric of Salzburg, in present-day Austria. Three years later, the first groups of those refugees were building a town an ocean away, in the colony of Georgia. They called it Ebenezer. Their pastor wanted nothing less than a religious utopia on the Georgia frontier, and for a while the Salzburgers came remarkably close. Today the town is gone, a ghost town in Effingham County, but the church their community finished in 1769 still has a living congregation.

## Exiles on the River

The Salzburger emigrants arrived in 1734 as Protestant refugees, and their first settlement did not stay put. In 1736, with the consent of James Oglethorpe, who governed the colony, the town, also known as New Ebenezer, was moved closer to the Savannah River, to the site near Ebenezer Creek where its remains lie today. Leading the community was the Reverend Johann Martin Boltzius, the Salzburgers' pastor, whose ambition went beyond survival. He sought to build what has been described as a religious utopia on the Georgia frontier, and the people he was building it with had already lost their homes once for their beliefs.

## Silk Mills and a Brick Church

For a time, the utopian idea worked. The economy thrived, and at the relocated town many silk mills were opened; a historical marker titled Silk Culture at Ebenezer still recalls that industry. The community's crowning achievement came in 1769, when Jerusalem Lutheran Church was completed. It is described as the oldest church building in Georgia and as one of the oldest buildings of any kind in the state. It also houses what the Georgia Historical Society calls the oldest continuing Lutheran congregation in the United States to worship in its historic building. The building's survival is the more striking given what happened to the rest of Ebenezer: while the town around it faded, the congregation never stopped gathering. The church the Salzburgers' community raised has outlasted the silk mills, the town and the utopian dream itself.

## War, a Capital, and a Slow Emptying

The American Revolutionary War broke the town's momentum. After the British invasion of 1778, Ebenezer was severely damaged, and it never fully recovered. Yet it had one more moment on the larger stage. In 1782 Ebenezer briefly served as the capital of Georgia. The town also produced one of the state's founding figures: John A. Treutlen, the first governor elected under Georgia's state constitution, is among the most notable people to come from Ebenezer. Civic importance lingered a little longer. Ebenezer became the county seat of Effingham County in 1797, but two years later the seat moved to Springfield, taking much of the county's business with it. The decline was steady from there. By the time Ebenezer was abandoned in 1855, the town that had once aspired to be a model community covered only a quarter of a square mile.

## Digging for a Vanished Town

What remains is protected. The Ebenezer Townsite and Jerusalem Lutheran Church were listed together on the National Register of Historic Places on December 4, 1974. Three years later, in 1977, the New Ebenezer Camp was established on the grounds of the former town, bringing new activity to the grounds of a town abandoned more than a century earlier. Archaeologists came too. In the late 1980s the LAMAR Institute began an archaeological study of Ebenezer, and several of its researchers' reports on the town are available online. Historical markers now guide visitors through the story, recalling the town itself, the old church, the silk culture, and the Reverends Boltzius and Israel Christian Gronau. Standing on the site, you are at the center of a community that was built on hope, prospered, was battered by war and slowly let go.

## Flight Context

Ebenezer lies at about 32.3767 N, 81.1809 W in Effingham County, Georgia, on the Georgia bank of the Savannah River near Ebenezer Creek, roughly 18 nm north-northwest of downtown Savannah. The townsite itself is small; from 1,500 to 2,500 feet, follow the Savannah River north from the city and look along its Georgia bank near Ebenezer Creek for the church and camp grounds. Savannah/Hilton Head International Airport (KSAV) is about 15 nm south, and Ridgeland-Claude Dean Airport (FAA: 3J1), in South Carolina, is about 12 nm northeast. Small private strips such as Swaids Field (FAA: 2GA2) lie closer, about 5 nm west-northwest. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
