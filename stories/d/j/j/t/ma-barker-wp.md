# Ma Barker

On the shore of Lake Weir, the FBI killed Kate Barker and her son Fred in a 1935 shootout, then turned a poor, uneducated mother into the most famous criminal mastermind who probably never was.

**Location:** 29.0413, -81.9334

**Categories:** American gangsters, American gangsters of the interwar period, Public enemy era, FBI history, Marion County, Florida, People shot dead by law enforcement officers in Florida

## Introduction

An alligator gave them away. When FBI agents arrested Arthur "Doc" Barker in Chicago on January 8, 1935, he was carrying a map that pointed toward Ocklawaha, Florida. His mother had been writing to him about a big gator in Lake Weir that everyone called "Gator Joe," and that small-town detail led agents to a lakeside vacation house rented under the name "Blackburn." Eight days later the house was surrounded. Inside were Fred Barker and his 61-year-old mother, Kate, the woman J. Edgar Hoover would make famous as Ma Barker, criminal mastermind. Historians now widely regard that reputation as largely fiction.

## Arrie of Ash Grove

She was born Arizona Donnie Clark on October 8, 1873, in Ash Grove, Missouri, and her family called her "Arrie." In 1892 she married George Barker in Lawrence County, and the couple raised four sons: Herman, Lloyd, Arthur and Fred. Census records and Tulsa city directories show George moving through low-paying work as a water company hand, farmer, watchman and clerk. The boys moved in a darker direction. In 1910 Herman was arrested for highway robbery after running over a child in the getaway car, and his brothers followed him into crimes of mounting seriousness, drawn into bigger work by Tulsa's Central Park gang. Herman took his own life in Wichita in August 1927, badly wounded after a confrontation with police in which an officer was killed. By 1928 the other three sons sat in three different prisons. George left, or was thrown out; accounts differ. From 1928 to 1930, Arrie lived alone in what was described as "miserable poverty" in a "dirt-floor shack."

## The Mother Kept Out of the Room

Fred walked out of the Kansas State Prison in 1931 having met a fellow inmate named Alvin Karpis, and when Doc was released in 1932 the core of the Barker–Karpis Gang was complete. The crimes that followed were real and bloody. The gang robbed banks in Kansas, Minnesota and Nebraska, kidnapped William Hamm of the Hamm's brewing family in 1933 and Edward Bremer in 1934, and left a trail of dead men behind. Minneapolis patrolmen Ira Leon Evans and Leo Gorski were shot during a bank robbery in December 1932; South St. Paul officer Leo Pavlak was killed in a payroll robbery the following August. And Ma? In Wisconsin, Fred hid her in a series of hotels and hideouts, partly to keep her from learning much about the gang's crimes and partly to separate her from the men's girlfriends, with whom she did not get along. Later, near Chicago, the gang rented apartments for her while they tried to launder the ransom money.

## A Morning on Lake Weir

Agents surrounded the house on the morning of January 16, 1935. They did not know that Karpis and the others had left three days earlier, leaving only Fred and his mother behind. Ordered to surrender, Fred opened fire, and an intense gun battle dragged on for hours. Local people allegedly gathered to watch, some even holding picnics while bullets flew. When the house finally went quiet, the agents sent in a local estate handyman, Willie Woodbury, wearing a bulletproof vest. He reported that no one inside was alive. Both bodies lay in the same front bedroom. Fred's was riddled with bullets, while Ma appeared to have died from a single wound. Their bodies were put on public display and then lay unclaimed for months, until relatives buried them on October 1, 1935, in Williams Timberhill Cemetery in Welch, Oklahoma, beside Herman.

## Building a Mastermind

After her death, Hoover called Kate Barker "the most vicious, dangerous, and resourceful criminal brain of the last decade." The men who actually knew her told another story. In his autobiography, Alvin Karpis called the idea that Ma ran the gang "the most ridiculous story in the annals of crime." "She wasn't a leader of criminals or even a criminal herself," he wrote. "When we traveled together, we moved as a mother and her sons. What could look more innocent?" Bank robber Harvey Bailey, who knew the family well, was blunter: she "couldn't plan breakfast." Those close to her believed Hoover built the myth to justify his agents' killing of an old woman, and later reports consistently conclude that her role was largely a creation of the FBI and of newspapers eager to sell copies. The legend has outrun every correction. Films such as Bloody Mama (1970) cast her as a monstrous matriarch, and in 1977 Boney M. turned her into the disco hit "Ma Baker."

## The House Crosses the Water

The house itself had stood on its lakeside lot since 1930. When the owners sold the land, the Barker death house was loaded onto a barge in October 2016 and floated across Lake Weir to Carney Island in Marion County, where the county now preserves it and offers tours by advance request. Just before the move, a team with metal detectors won permission to sweep the property and turned up a vintage ring engraved with the initials "F.B." It is presumed to have belonged to Fred, and it joined a collection of bullet casings and other relics. The alligator lives on too, after a fashion: the local restaurant Gator Joe's takes its name from the reptile in Ma's letters. For many years in the 1980s the Lake Weir Chamber of Commerce staged a reenactment of the shootout, with Marion County deputies playing the FBI agents.

## Flight Context

The shootout site lies on the shore of Lake Weir near Ocklawaha in Marion County, Florida, at approximately 29.0413°N, 81.9334°W. Lake Weir is the dominant visual reference, a large open lake amid central Florida's lake country; Carney Island, where the house was moved in 2016, sits in the lake itself. A viewing altitude of 1,500–2,500 feet AGL gives a good picture of the shoreline and the island. Ocala International Airport - Jim Taylor Field (KOCF) is about 17 nm to the west-northwest, and Leesburg International Airport (KLEE) is about 15 nm to the south-southeast.
