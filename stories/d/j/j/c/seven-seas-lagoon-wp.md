# Seven Seas Lagoon

Walt Disney never drew it, yet the man-made Seven Seas Lagoon became the front door of the Magic Kingdom, its dug-out earth buried beneath the park and its waters home to ferries, fireworks islands and wild Florida.

**Location:** 28.4113, -81.5824

**Categories:** Walt Disney World, Lakes of Orange County, Florida, Artificial lakes of the United States, Lakes of Florida, 1960s establishments in Florida

## Introduction

Everyone who crosses the Magic Kingdom is walking over the Seven Seas Lagoon, or at least over what used to fill it. More than seven million cubic yards of earth were moved to create this artificial lake, and that soil went to cover the utilidor beneath the park. What remains is the lagoon itself, a sheet of water up to 14 feet deep that separates the Magic Kingdom from its parking lot, connects with neighboring Bay Lake, and gives visitors arriving by ferry a long look across the water toward the park.

## A Lake Walt Never Drew

The original master plan for Walt Disney World, drawn up around 1966 and 1967, had no Seven Seas Lagoon at all. Walt Disney had pictured an expanded Bay Lake as the resort's great open body of water. The ground where the lagoon now lies was meant for themed resorts, relatively landlocked and separated from the Magic Kingdom by golf courses, an ice rink and a roller skating dome. A monorail was to run in a straight line to the park entrance, with three WEDway PeopleMover lines linking the hotels. After Walt's death the design changed substantially. Most likely because the land was swampy, Bay Lake was expanded and the lagoon was created from the excess water displaced by the park, resorts, parking lots and golf courses. The monorail became a loop around the new lagoon, with parking and a transportation center on its south shore.

## Ferries Named for Builders

Three Disney Transport ferryboats shuttle guests between the park and the Transportation and Ticket Center: the Admiral Joe Fowler, once Magic Kingdom I; the Richard F. Irvine, once Magic Kingdom II; and, since 1976, the General Joe Potter, formerly the Kingdom Queen. All three carry the names of people who were instrumental in creating Walt Disney World. Older boats had more old-fashioned charm. The steam-powered sidewheelers Southern Seas and Ports O' Call worked the water until 1975 and 1982, and a slightly larger, diesel-powered Southern Seas II took over in 1977 and ran until 1996. Of all the hotels on Walt Disney World property, only the resorts along these shores, the Grand Floridian and the Polynesian Village among them, are served directly by the monorail.

## Kites, Pyramids and a Wave Machine

In the 1970s the lagoon was a stage. The Walt Disney World Water Ski Show ran five times a day across Seven Seas Lagoon and Bay Lake, featuring an eight-person, three-tiered human pyramid, flex-wing kites flown 300 feet above the water and skiers jumping a five-and-a-half-foot ramp, with Dumbo, Goofy and Pinocchio in the cast. Out on Beachcomber Island, an artificial wave machine made surf in the early 1970s. Beach erosion, temperamental technology, cost and trouble for passing boats shut it down. A 1985 revival brought more erosion and a permanent shutdown, and because removal would have been so expensive, remnants of the machine still lie under the surface. The lagoon's other islands have their own roles. Blackbeard Island sits near the water bridge to Bay Lake, and Castaway Cay, which shares its name with Disney Cruise Line's island in the Bahamas, serves as the launch point for perimeter fireworks during special events and holidays.

## Wild Florida in a Man-Made Lake

For all its engineering, the lagoon is not tame. Alligators, lizards, snakes and turtles, all native Florida species, live in and around it. Fish were released in 1973, though fishing was not permitted until the mid-1990s. Swimming was once allowed but has since been banned, a policy usually explained by the rental boats and by Naegleria fowleri, a single-celled amoeba that occurs in warm Florida fresh water. The lagoon is also home to the Electrical Water Pageant. The lagoon's darkest evening came on June 14, 2016, when a two-year-old boy wading at the Grand Floridian's beach was seized by an alligator. His parents tried to save him, but he died, and he was found the next afternoon a short distance from shore. Afterward the beaches and walkways were rebuilt with large rocks and roped-off gates between guests and the water, and warning signs went up around the shoreline.

## Flight Context

Seven Seas Lagoon is centered near 28.4113 N, 81.5824 W, directly south of the Magic Kingdom, and makes an unmistakable visual reference: a broad sheet of water with Cinderella Castle on its north side, the Transportation and Ticket Center and its vast parking lots to the south, the Grand Floridian's red roofs on its western shore and the Polynesian's longhouses to the south. The monorail beam traces a loop around it, and Bay Lake lies to the east, joined to the lagoon by a water bridge. The lagoon lies within Walt Disney World's permanent flight restriction (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface to 3,000 feet above ground level), so view it from above that ceiling, around 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL, or from outside the ring, and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast.
