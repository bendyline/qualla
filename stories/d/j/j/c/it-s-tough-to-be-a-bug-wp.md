# It's Tough to Be a Bug!

Hidden inside the Tree of Life for nearly 27 years, Pixar's first theme park attraction sprayed, poked, tickled and stank its way into Animal Kingdom lore before giving way to Zootopia in 2025.

**Location:** 28.3578, -81.5910

**Categories:** Disney's Animal Kingdom, Discovery Island (Disney's Animal Kingdom), Pixar in amusement parks, Audio-Animatronic attractions, 4D films, Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions

## Introduction

The posters in the lobby promised a thriving theater scene: "The Grass Menagerie," "Barefoot in the Bark," "Little Shop of Hoppers," "My Fair Ladybug." Down in this "underground" foyer, deep inside the Tree of Life at Disney's Animal Kingdom, the overture was Broadway played by insects. It's Tough to Be a Bug! was a 3D show with a sense of humor and a mean streak, and for nearly 27 years it made guests feel, sometimes quite literally, what life is like at six-legged scale.

## A Show Inside a Tree

About a year before Disney's Animal Kingdom opened in 1998, Imagineers decided to put a show inside the park's centerpiece, the Tree of Life, but struggled to find the right concept. Disney CEO Michael Eisner suggested a tie-in with a Pixar film then in the works, A Bug's Life. Visual effects studio Rhythm and Hues produced the 3D animation, while Disney's own effects teams built everything else: Audio-Animatronic characters, wind, water and foul smells. Directed by Chris Bailey and Andrew Schmidt, the show opened with the park on April 22, 1998, seven months before A Bug's Life reached theaters, and it was Pixar's first presence in a Disney park. Dave Foley voiced the ant Flik in both the attraction and the feature, and the film's screenwriter and co-director, Andrew Stanton, voiced the villainous grasshopper Hopper for the park version.

## Broadway for Bugs

The approach was part of the show. Animals were carved into the tree's exterior between its roots, visible only from the queue. Posters for the bug acts led guests down to the lobby, where queue music arranged by George Wilkins gave insect twists to Broadway standards: "One" from "A Cockroach Line," "Tomorrow" from "Antie," "I Feel Pretty" from "Web Side Story" and "Hello Dung Lovers" from "The Dung and I." Just outside the lobby doors, a plaque honored Dr. Jane Goodall, a consultant on the design of Disney's Animal Kingdom, beside a larger-than-life sculpted likeness of David Greybeard, the chimpanzee she studied. Bruce Broughton composed and conducted the score, and Wilkins wrote the title song with Kevin Rafferty.

## Acts of Nature

Once guests donned their "bug eyes," the 3D glasses, an Audio-Animatronic Flik emerged from a hole in the ceiling to explain that insects matter to human lives in ways people cannot see. A curtain of butterflies parted and the acts began. Chili, a tarantula voiced by Cheech Marin, showed off his skill as a quill shooter, until Weevil Kneevil, voiced by Jason Alexander, launched acorns from a slingshot, courtesy of hidden air cannons. The Termite-ator, voiced by French Stewart, sprayed "acid" at a taunting flea and then at the audience, which meant hidden water jets, until he ran dry. Then came Claire de Room, a stinkbug with a "silent but deadly" talent. When a fleeing Weevil knocked her target flower toward the crowd, her gas hit the audience instead, delivered by hidden smell cannons.

## Hopper Declares War

An explosion announced Hopper, in Audio-Animatronic form, disgusted by Flik's sympathy for humans. Hornets turned an extermination flyer into a screen for clips of old giant-insect monster movies, and a huge fly swatter swung at the audience. Finding them still alive, Hopper declared war. A hand with a can of bug spray filled the air with fog, the theater went black, and hornets "stung" everyone through small pokers in the seat backs while black widow spiders dropped over guests' heads. Hopper's victory speech was cut short when a chameleon snapped him up with its tongue. Flik noted that reptiles do not care that it is tough to be a bug, and bees, dragonflies and the singing Dung Brothers, voiced by Tom Kenny, closed with the title song. The last gag came as guests rose: an announcer asked everyone to stay seated so the other insects could leave, and rubber wheels rolled beneath the seats.

## Curtain Call

A second version opened with Disney California Adventure on February 8, 2001, in the Bountiful Valley Farm area that later became A Bug's Land. It closed there on March 19, 2018, and the area was eventually redeveloped as Avengers Campus, where Web Slingers: A Spider-Man Adventure replaced it. In Florida, Disney announced at its Destination D23 event in September 2023 that a show based on the Zootopia franchise would move into the Tree of Life. It's Tough to Be a Bug! gave its last show on March 16, 2025, with Disney listing March 17 as the official closing date, and Zootopia: Better Zoogether! opened in the same theater on November 7, 2025.

## Flight Context

The Tree of Life, which housed the theater, stands at the center of Disney's Animal Kingdom near 28.3578 N, 81.5910 W, about 20 miles southwest of Orlando; the park's icon is a distinctive landmark from outside the resort. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL, centered near the Magic Kingdom about 3.4 nm north-northeast of this site, with limited exceptions), so keep well clear, view the park only from outside the restricted area and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 9 nm to the east-southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 15 nm to the east-northeast, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 18 nm to the northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
