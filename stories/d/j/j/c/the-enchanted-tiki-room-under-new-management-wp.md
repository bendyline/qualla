# The Enchanted Tiki Room (Under New Management)

For nearly 13 years, Aladdin's wisecracking parrot Iago took over the Magic Kingdom's Tiki Room, angered a Tiki goddess and divided fans, until a small attic fire in 2011 opened the door for the original show's return.

**Location:** 28.4184, -81.5839

**Categories:** Former Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Adventureland (Disney), Magic Kingdom, Audio-Animatronic attractions, Tiki culture

## Introduction

Disney kept quiet about exactly what the fire did to Iago. On January 12, 2011, a small blaze broke out in the attic of the Magic Kingdom's Enchanted Tiki Room, and while the cause and the extent of the damage were kept quiet during the investigation, the rumor was that the Audio-Animatronic parrot who first interrupted the show had been badly burned. It was a strangely fitting end. For almost 13 years, Iago's whole act had been getting scorched by the Tiki gods.

## Agents Behind the Waterfall

The Tiki Room came to Florida from Disneyland on October 1, 1971, as Tropical Serenade, a near duplicate of the California show, sponsored by Florida Citrus Growers until 1986. It closed on September 1, 1997, for updating, and reopened on April 5, 1998, as The Enchanted Tiki Room: Under New Management. The new version kept all the original birds, flowers and Tiki gods, but added two famous feathered stars as the room's new owners: Iago from Aladdin, voiced by Gilbert Gottfried, and Zazu from The Lion King, voiced by Michael Gough. The joke started outside. While guests waited, a cylinder behind a waterfall opened to reveal two toucans, William, voiced by Don Rickles, and Morris, voiced by Phil Hartman, who were the new co-owners' agents. Morris boasted he was about to sign Donald Duck; William bragged he had the Mighty Ducks.

## Iago Takes the Stage

Inside, the four familiar hosts, José, Michael, Pierre and Fritz, voiced by Wally Boag, Fulton Burley, Jerry Orbach and Thurl Ravenscroft, launched into "The Tiki Tiki Tiki Room" as always. Midway through, Iago descended from a hole in the ceiling on a pillow, megaphone in hand, demanding they stop the music; the old songs, he said, made him want to "toss his crackers." Zazu dropped in on a perch to beg him not to toy with the Tiki Room. The hosts had no idea who either bird was, since they never "flew to the movies"; they were rather "attached to the place." Iago announced that he and Zazu were the new owners, sang a number to the tune of "Friend Like Me," called himself a "cele-birdy" and warned that the old act had better get hip. Then, despite Zazu's warnings, he insulted the Tiki gods.

## The Goddess of Disaster

Lightning struck and the lights went out. The birds sang that things had suddenly gotten "Hot Hot Hot," the carved faces on the Tiki poles chanted "Uh-Oa!" and, out of the smoking fountain in the center of the room, rose Uh-Oa, the green Tiki Goddess of Disaster, voiced by Armelia McQueen. She sang Iago a warning about pushing his luck, cursed him, and blasted him away in a cloud of dark smoke. The Tiki gods then got their own spotlight with "In the Still of the Night," while the flowers rapped lyrics from the original show. Iago reappeared over the exit, burnt, bandaged and leaning on a crutch, now a fan of the gods. When Zazu explained "Hakuna Matata," Iago heard "Hunky-Tuna Tostada," and the room broke into a conga as the Bird Mobile descended. Guests left to "Get on Your Feet" and "Heigh-Ho," while Iago insulted them on the way out and announced he was off to nap in the Hall of Presidents. In all, the show used 225 Audio-Animatronics.

## The Original Returns

Not everyone was charmed. Reception among guests was mixed. Some called the makeover "disrespectful" to a classic, one park-goer was quoted as saying "they ruined it," and many found it strange that a once-calming attraction had been replaced by what critics called a "jarring and cynical" take, with jokes some reviewers considered dated and crass. When the fire came in 2011, no guests were hurt; the sprinkler system that put out the flames left other show elements with minor damage. Rather than bring the new management back, Disney restored the original. On August 15, 2011, the attraction reopened as Walt Disney's Enchanted Tiki Room, an edited and trimmed version of the original show featuring all but one of the songs from its 1963 debut. Some now count Under New Management among the worst attraction replacements in Magic Kingdom history.

## Flight Context

The Tiki Room stands in Adventureland at the Magic Kingdom, near 28.4184 N, 81.5839 W, about 15 miles southwest of downtown Orlando; Bay Lake lies immediately east of the park. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a standing NOTAM, reported as a 3 nm radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL), so view the resort only from outside the restricted area and check the current NOTAM text before planning a route nearby. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
