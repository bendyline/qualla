# Disney's Grand Floridian Resort & Spa

Built from the memory of Florida's Victorian beach hotels, Disney's flagship resort has red gables, white walls and a lagoon that has brought it both glamour and grief.

**Location:** 28.4114, -81.5868

**Categories:** Commercial buildings completed in 1988, Hotels established in 1988, Hotel buildings completed in 1988, Hotels in Walt Disney World Resort, Destination spas, Convention centers in Florida, 1988 establishments in Florida, Disney Vacation Club

## Introduction

Look closely at the Grand Floridian and you are seeing three other hotels at once. Its red gabled roofs and white walls echo the Mount Washington Resort in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, and the Hotel del Coronado in Coronado, California, while still more ideas came from the former Belleview-Biltmore Hotel in Belleair, Florida. Disney wove those borrowings into a Victorian-themed hotel and spa on the shore of the Seven Seas Lagoon, opened it on June 28, 1988, and has treated it ever since as its flagship and its most opulent resort.

## A Seaside Era, Rebuilt by a Lagoon

The inspiration was a style of Florida holiday that had long since passed: the Victorian-era beach resorts built along the state's east coast in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Disney's version sits not on the Atlantic but on a man-made lagoon beside the Magic Kingdom, and the ground beneath it once had a different future. During the initial development of Walt Disney World in the late 1960s, this land was earmarked for an Asian-themed resort. That hotel was never built here. When the Victorian one opened instead, it carried the name Grand Floridian Beach Resort, and it kept that title until the fall of 1997, when it became Disney's Grand Floridian Resort & Spa. Disney classes it as a "deluxe" resort, and even within that tier it stands apart as the company's showpiece.

## Kokomo on the Seven Seas

In its opening year of 1988, the hotel had famous company: the Beach Boys performed at the Grand Floridian to film the music video for their hit "Kokomo." What the cameras found was a resort of real scale: 867 rooms spread among six buildings, each room averaging about 400 square feet. Seen from the entrance of the Magic Kingdom, or from a monorail gliding past, the complex reads as a long run of white walls beneath red roofs across the water, with the Polynesian Village Resort sharing the same lagoon shore. Business followed leisure. In 1992 a 40,000-square-foot convention center opened next to the hotel, complete with a business center, bringing conferences to a building whose whole design looks back to an unhurried age of seaside holidays.

## Sorrow at the Water's Edge

The lagoon that frames the resort has also brought grief. On October 9, 1989, a 33-year-old woman from Glen Cove, New York, was killed when the small speedboat she was in collided with a ferry. She and her eight-year-old son had been trying to videotape friends and family members water skiing when the ferry broadsided them. A crew member and a visitor aboard the ferry dove in and rescued the boy, who was unhurt, and the family later sued Disney for $240 million, arguing that the ferry's operators should have seen the speedboat sooner. On June 14, 2016, a two-year-old boy was dragged by an American alligator from the shore of the resort's beach into the lagoon, and his body was recovered the next day. In 2017 a lighthouse sculpture honoring him was unveiled near the place where he was lost. Along the lagoon, large rocks, roped-off barriers and warning signs now stand between guests and the water.

## Flight Context

Disney's Grand Floridian sits at 28.4114 N, 81.5868 W on the shore of the Seven Seas Lagoon, just south of the Magic Kingdom. Look for a long cluster of white buildings with red gabled roofs at the water's edge, with the Polynesian Village Resort's longhouses to the east and the monorail beam looping around the lagoon. Walt Disney World sits beneath a permanent flight restriction extending 3 nautical miles from its center, from the surface to 3,000 feet above ground level, so view the resort from above that ceiling (around 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL) or from outside the ring, and check current NOTAMs before flying. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 15 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 16 nm to the east-northeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
