# Walt Disney World Railroad

Four Baldwin steam engines rescued from a Mexican railroad boneyard now work the Magic Kingdom's narrow-gauge line, which was the first attraction finished in the park and has been rerouted around its newest rides ever since.

**Location:** 28.4164, -81.5812

## Introduction

In 1969, two Disney railroad men walked into a boneyard in Mérida, in Mexico's Yucatán, to inspect the worn-out steam locomotives left there. Roger Broggie and Earl Vilmer were searching for engines for a park that did not yet exist, and among the derelicts of the Ferrocarriles Unidos de Yucatán they found four Baldwin locomotives they believed could be saved. Those same engines pull the Walt Disney World Railroad, a 3-foot narrow-gauge line whose full circuit runs 1.5 miles through the Magic Kingdom, stops at stations in three different parts of the park, and takes roughly 15 to 20 minutes to complete a round trip. Since July 2025, though, the trains have shuttled only between Main Street, U.S.A. and Fantasyland.

## Five Engines for $32,750

The hunt began in 1968, when Broggie contacted rail historian Gerald M. Best about locomotives sitting in the Mérida boneyard. The engines had been built by the Baldwin Locomotive Works in Philadelphia, and after the 1969 inspection trip Broggie bought them, paying $8,000 apiece for the four in the boneyard and another $750 for a fifth locomotive, a total of $32,750. They were shipped to the Tampa Ship Repair & Dry Dock Company in Tampa, Florida. At the time, it was the closest facility to the Walt Disney World site with the space and equipment to handle full-size railroad rolling stock. The fifth, an ALCO engine, was not among those restored; its remains were stored at WED Enterprises in Glendale, California, until they were sold to an unknown locomotive broker in the mid-1980s.

## Making Old Engines Look Older

The goal of the restoration was a kind of time travel: to make the locomotives look as if they had been built in the 1880s. Each received a diamond-shaped smokestack and a square headlamp. Their dilapidated boilers gave way to new, smaller ones from Dixon Boiler Works in Los Angeles, and the worn wood-and-steel cabs were replaced with fiberglass. New tenders rode on the trucks salvaged from the originals, and the fireboxes were converted to burn ultra-low-sulfur diesel oil instead of bunker oil. Restoring each locomotive and its tender cost around $125,000. The four engines carry the names Walter E. Disney, Lilly Belle, Roger E. Broggie and Roy O. Disney. George Britton, who was instrumental in getting them refurbished, served as the railroad's foreman from opening day until he retired on April 6, 2006.

## The First Ride Finished

The first set of five passenger cars reached the Magic Kingdom in April 1971, and the first completed locomotive arrived on May 15, 1971, months ahead of opening. Echoing the Disneyland Railroad, whose steam trains ran on that park's opening day in 1955, the Florida trains were the first attraction in the Magic Kingdom to be finished, and they have run since the park opened on October 1, 1971. At first there was only one stop, Main Street, U.S.A. Station at the park entrance, modeled after the former Victorian-style station in Saratoga Springs, New York, so passengers could ride only full circuits. A Frontierland station followed on May 1, 1972. Riders needed a D ticket until 1982, when pay-one-price admission arrived, and the line grew into one of the most popular steam-powered railroads in the world, carrying about 3.7 million passengers a year.

## Rerouted Around Every New Idea

The railroad has had to adapt again and again to its neighbors. A third station opened on June 18, 1988, for Mickey's Birthdayland, and the train was briefly renamed the Mickey's Birthdayland Express. In November 1990 the original Frontierland Station was demolished to make room for Splash Mountain, and during construction the railroad became the Backtrack Express, a single train shuttling back and forth. On March 12, 2012, a new Fantasyland Station opened in an area nicknamed Carolwood Park, paying tribute to Walt Disney's Carolwood Pacific Railroad. Then came TRON. The railroad closed on December 3, 2018, and reopened on December 23, 2022, with a new tunnel beside the new attraction and composite plastic ties expected to last another 25 years. Splash Mountain's closure in 2023 led to Tiana's Bayou Adventure in 2024, and from July 7, 2025, the trains returned to shuttle mode for the Beyond Big Thunder expansion, running only between Main Street and Fantasyland while the Frontierland Station stays closed. In September 2026 the railroad closed for a routine refurbishment, with a reopening expected in late October or November.

## Flight Context

The railroad's Main Street, U.S.A. Station sits at the Magic Kingdom's entrance near 28.4164 N, 81.5812 W, just north of the Seven Seas Lagoon, with Cinderella Castle rising a short distance to the north. The Magic Kingdom lies within Walt Disney World's permanent flight restriction (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface to 3,000 feet above ground level), so view the park from above that ceiling, around 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL, or from outside the ring, and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
