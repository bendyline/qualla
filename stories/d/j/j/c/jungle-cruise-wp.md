# Jungle Cruise

Conceived as a serious nature voyage and remembered for its skippers' terrible puns, Disney's Jungle Cruise has carried guests down simulated jungle rivers since 1955, with a Magic Kingdom version that winds from the Amazon to the Mekong.

**Location:** 28.4180, -81.5834

**Categories:** Theme park attractions, Magic Kingdom, Walt Disney World, Adventureland, Boat rides

## Introduction

What good is a jungle ride if the animals sleep all day? Disney's planners asked themselves exactly that while designing an attraction for Disneyland's opening in 1955. They had intended to use real animals, then realized the creatures would likely doze through park hours. So the Jungle Cruise got mechanical animals instead, and a live skipper at the wheel of every boat. More than seventy years later, at Magic Kingdom in Florida and at Disney parks across the Pacific, those skippers are still steering guests past charging hippos and bathing elephants, delivering jokes that are bad on purpose.

## A Jungle on a Budget

The ride drew on two films: The African Lion, a 1955 True-Life Adventures documentary, and the 1951 adventure The African Queen. Imagineer Harper Goff referred to The African Queen frequently and modeled the boats on its steamer. Landscaper Bill Evans, who planted Disneyland and most of Walt Disney World, was handed the tricky job of growing a realistic jungle on a limited budget. Alongside genuine tropical plants he used "character plants" that looked exotic without being so, and he relied on a well-known trick: uprooting local orange trees, replanting them upside down, and growing vines over the exposed roots. Even the water plays a role. Its murkiness is carefully controlled to hide the boats' guidance system and the platforms beneath the bathing elephants and hippos, and its color has shifted over the years from brown to green to a bluish-green.

## From Documentary to Punchlines

The Jungle Cruise was meant to be serious. The original intent was a realistic, believable voyage through the world's jungles, and the first skippers' narration sounded much like a nature documentary. Over the decades that script gave way to the comedic, loosely rehearsed patter that defines the ride today, full of intentionally bad puns delivered by a cast member who acts as both guide and captain. The attraction has changed in other ways, too. Disney has added enhanced audio, updated the storyline, and removed material now recognized as culturally insensitive. During a 1991 refurbishment the ride gained Albert Awol, a fictional Depression-era disc jockey billed as the "Voice of the Jungle," who broadcasts news, quizzes and period music to waiting guests.

## From the Amazon to the Mekong

Magic Kingdom's version is set at a Depression-era British outpost on the Amazon, run by the fictional Jungle Navigation Co. Boats push off past butterflies with one-foot wingspans, or, as the skipper might put it, twelve inches. Inspiration Falls leads into the Congo, where western gorillas have ransacked a camp, and then onto the Nile. On the African veldt, giraffes, zebras and wildebeests watch a pride of lions, while a lost safari party, chased up a pole by an angry black rhinoceros, is trapped and surrounded by spotted hyenas. Beyond Schweitzer Falls lies the tail of a crashed plane, the back half of a Lockheed Model 12 Electra Junior that once appeared in the Casablanca scene of the Great Movie Ride. Hippos threaten a charge until the skipper scares them off, and the Mekong brings an earthquake-shattered temple with cobras and a Bengal tiger before a pool of Indian elephants nearly soaks everyone aboard.

## Plants for a Florida Winter

Florida posed its own landscaping problem. Bill Evans chose every plant variety so it could survive the state's hot summers and relatively cool winters while still looking like equatorial jungle. The queue, meanwhile, was designed to resemble a colonial outpost where river expeditions could be booked, winding back and forth past period artifacts, tools and photographs, with the office of Albert Awol as its highlight. Fifteen boats carry names such as Amazon Annie, Senegal Sal and Zambezi Zelda, and each takes a holiday name at Christmas, so Nile Nellie becomes Noel Nellie. In December 2015 the Skipper Canteen restaurant opened nearby and expanded the legend, making Dr. Albert Falls the founder of the Jungle Navigation Co. in 1911 and his granddaughter Alberta its leader in the 1930s.

## Rivers Around the World

The cruise has spread far from Anaheim. Tokyo Disneyland's version, closely resembling Florida's, runs clockwise rather than counterclockwise, departs from a station themed to an upscale African city, and is narrated in Japanese; since 2014 it has been called Jungle Cruise: Wildlife Expeditions. Hong Kong Disneyland's route circles Tarzan's Treehouse and builds to a finale in a canyon where feuding fire and water gods turn the river to a cloud of steam. It once ran separate queues for Cantonese, English and Mandarin, but by 2024 the three language queues had reportedly been merged into one, with a single script. The ride even reached the big screen: after years of planning, a film adaptation opened in the United States on July 30, 2021.

## Flight Context

The Jungle Cruise lies at about 28.418 N, 81.583 W in Adventureland at Magic Kingdom, Walt Disney World, southwest of Orlando, Florida, close to Frontierland's Big Thunder Mountain. From the air individual rides are hard to pick out, so use the Magic Kingdom park as a whole as the landmark. After the September 11, 2001 attacks the airspace over Walt Disney World was restricted, and the restriction was later made permanent (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL, with limited exceptions), so view the resort from outside the restricted area and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 15 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
