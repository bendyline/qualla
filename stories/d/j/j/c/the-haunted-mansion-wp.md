# The Haunted Mansion

Built alongside its California twin and then made bigger, the Magic Kingdom's Haunted Mansion has carried guests in Doom Buggies past a clock that strikes thirteen, a medium in a crystal ball and a graveyard full of singing ghosts since the park opened in 1971.

**Location:** 28.4208, -81.5830

**Categories:** Haunted Mansion, Dark rides, Audio-Animatronic attractions, Haunted attractions (simulated), Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions

## Introduction

"Is this haunted room actually stretching?" asks the unseen Ghost Host, as the walls of a dim chamber seem to grow and the portraits on them lengthen to reveal their subjects' grim, comic fates. At Disneyland in California the answer is a trick of engineering: the room is an elevator, sinking guests below the railroad berm to reach the ride building. At the Magic Kingdom in Florida, where there was room to spare, the floor stays put and the ceiling rises instead. That small difference says a lot about the Haunted Mansion of Liberty Square, an opening-day attraction designed as a longer, more elaborate twin of the original.

## A House Walt Wouldn't Let Decay

The idea of a haunted house predates Disneyland itself. An early sketch by Disney Legend Harper Goff showed a crooked street leading past a church and graveyard to a run-down manor on a hill above Main Street. Imagineer Ken Anderson later studied New Orleans and old plantations, settled on the Shipley-Lydecker House in Baltimore as a model, and drew a decaying antebellum manor overgrown with weeds, dead trees and swarms of bats, with a screeching cat for a weathervane. Walt Disney rejected the idea of having a run-down building in his park. Disneyland's mansion exterior was finished in 1963, years before the ride inside was ready. The project stretched on for more than a decade after Disneyland opened, becoming one of the last developed under Walt's direction. The California mansion finally opened in 1969, and it has since inspired Phantom Manor in Paris and Mystic Manor in Hong Kong.

## Built Twice, Then Bigger

While Imagineers were building the props and Audio-Animatronics for Disneyland's mansion, they made duplicates of everything for Walt Disney World. The Florida version was planned to be slightly longer and richer, with extra scenes such as a library and a music room, and Paul Frees recorded additional lines as the Ghost Host to narrate them. With ample space inside the new park, the show building could be much larger and was not squeezed by any railroad berm. Riders board Omnimover cars called Doom Buggies, pass under a staircase where a candelabra floats, glide past the changing portraits, and roll into the library, where busts of ghost writers stare and follow them as they pass. In the music room an invisible pianist plays a sinister "Grim Grinning Ghosts," and on the Endless Staircase footprints appear while unseen ghosts blow out candles.

## Thirteen O'Clock

Bat-eyed wallpaper glows in the dark beside the Endless Hallway, where the Hatbox Ghost appears from a blue door. In a conservatory, a corpse tries to get out, crying for help. Beyond a corridor of doors, a demonic grandfather clock chimes thirteen. Then comes the séance room, where the medium Madame Leota floats, reciting her spell while instruments play and a green specter drifts among them. The Doom Buggies roll onto a mezzanine above a ballroom where ghosts have gathered for a party, then up into the attic, where the pianist can be heard but not seen. Florida's bride, Constance Hatchaway, recites twisted wedding vows and holds a hatchet.

## Happy Haunts in the Graveyard

The mood lifts, in its way, as the buggies descend into the cemetery, where ghosts have risen to sing "Grim Grinning Ghosts," the attraction's theme. One of that song's vocalists, Thurl Ravenscroft, is honored in the queue by a pipe organ from the fictional Ravenscroft company. Next comes the crypt, where the three Hitchhiking Ghosts play tricks in the mirrors as riders pass. As guests step out, Little Leota appears above the vehicles, and the exit path leads past a mausoleum and a pet cemetery. That cemetery holds a sly memorial of its own: a statue of Mr. Toad, whose Wild Ride closed at the Magic Kingdom in 1998.

## A Mansion That Keeps Changing

A 2007 renovation brought over the changing portraits and lightning-lit windows from Disneyland, gave the graveyard ghosts new vocals, and filled a formerly dark space with the Endless Staircase. The Sinister 11 portraits, whose eyes once followed guests down the hall, moved to the loading area. In March 2011 an interactive queue opened with crypts honoring Imagineers, a murder mystery about the sinister Dread Family, a Composer Crypt whose instruments play when touched, the brine-filled sepulcher of the Mariner, whose ghost sings and sneezes, and the tomb of the poetess Prudence Pock, where guests help finish her poems. Since 2019 a hidden camera photographs riders, its flash disguised as lightning. Unlike its California and Tokyo siblings, the Florida mansion never takes a holiday makeover; it simply stays haunted all year.

## Flight Context

The Haunted Mansion sits in Liberty Square at the Magic Kingdom, about 28.421N, 81.583W, on the northwest side of the park at Walt Disney World, southwest of Orlando, Florida. From above, find Cinderella Castle at the park's center and the former Rivers of America waterway to its west, closed in July 2025 and now a construction zone; the mansion lies along that edge. Walt Disney World is protected by permanent flight restrictions from the surface to 3,000 feet AGL within 3 nm, so stay above 3,000 feet AGL or outside that ring; from about 4,500 to 5,500 feet the park is easy to read in clear weather. Orlando's Class B airspace overlies much of the area, so contact Orlando Approach. Orlando International Airport (KMCO) is about 14 nm to the east and Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) about 11 nm to the southeast.
