# Liberty Square (Magic Kingdom)

The Magic Kingdom's smallest land recreates Revolutionary-era America, from a transplanted Liberty Tree and a cast Liberty Bell to Paul Revere's lanterns, with the Haunted Mansion and the Hall of Presidents as its neighbors.

**Location:** 28.4194, -81.5825

**Categories:** Magic Kingdom, Themed areas in Walt Disney Parks and Resorts, Colonial America in popular culture, American Revolution, 1971 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

The great oak at the center of Liberty Square did not start out there. It was a 100-year-old tree found on the Walt Disney World property and transplanted to this spot, with a younger oak grafted into its base, and it now serves as the square's Liberty Tree. Nearby stands a replica Liberty Bell, and in an upstairs window two lanterns recall Paul Revere's "two if by sea." Liberty Square is the Magic Kingdom's smallest land, and it is crowded with symbols.

## A Street That Became a Square

The idea began in Anaheim as Liberty Street, a proposed annex to Main Street, U.S.A. at Disneyland. It resurfaced in the late 1960s as the Magic Kingdom was being designed and planners needed an area that would do for Florida what New Orleans Square did for Disneyland, without copying it. Walt Disney Imagineering chose an early American, 18th-century theme centered on the Revolutionary War, with the nation's Bicentennial approaching in 1976. Liberty Square opened with the park on October 1, 1971, as one of its six original lands. It sits in the park's northwest corner, bordering Fantasyland and Frontierland, with bridges to the central hub and to Adventureland. It is also the smallest land in the Magic Kingdom by guest area.

## A Walk Through American Time

The theming is meticulous, from architectural homages down to the antiques and artifacts scattered through its attractions and restaurants. The land also begins a journey through American history and geography. It starts at the Haunted Mansion, styled after the 1770s or 1780s in upstate New York, and continues clockwise around the former Rivers of America into Frontierland, ending in the 1880s at Big Thunder Mountain Railroad. Buildings represent each of the original Thirteen Colonies, and the flags of those 13 states fly with the American flag in a central plaza. A replica of the House of Burgesses holds Revere's lanterns. The Liberty Bell replica was cast in 1989 from a mold of the original. Legends cling to it: one claims it is one of only two bells ever made from that mold, though many others exist, and another says Pennsylvania handed over its 1976 state replica. In fact, Pennsylvania's replica is in Allentown.

## Ghosts, Presidents and Turkey Legs

Two very different attractions anchor the square. The Haunted Mansion, one of the Magic Kingdom's most popular attractions, draws steady crowds, while the Hall of Presidents presides over the square with its Audio-Animatronic American history show, historic artifacts from presidents and first ladies on display in its lobby. From 2016 to 2019, the Muppets added their own version of the story outside the Hall in The Muppets Present... Great Moments in American History, complete with a new song. Food follows the colonial theme. Liberty Tree Tavern serves Americana in a colonial setting, Columbia Harbour House offers quick meals across two nautical-themed floors, Sleepy Hollow Refreshments takes its name from The Legend of Sleepy Hollow, which Disney adapted in its 1949 film The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad, and the outdoor Liberty Square Market is the place for turkey legs. A Christmas shop does business year-round, and Memento Mori caters to Haunted Mansion fans.

## When the River Ran Dry

For more than half a century, Liberty Square's western edge was water. The Rivers of America carried the Admiral Joe Fowler riverboat from 1971 to 1980, the Mike Fink Keel Boats from 1971 to 2001 and the Liberty Belle riverboat from 1973 onward. That era ended in the 2020s. At the D23 Expo in August 2024, Disney announced that part of Frontierland would be redeveloped into an American-wilderness area themed to Pixar's Cars franchise, replacing Tom Sawyer Island, the Rivers of America and the Liberty Belle. In June 2025 came the closing date, and on July 7, 2025, the river and its riverboat went quiet. The square's oak, its bell and its lanterns remain, but the view to the west is changing.

## Flight Context

Liberty Square occupies the northwest corner of the Magic Kingdom near 28.4194 N, 81.5825 W, about 15 miles southwest of downtown Orlando; the former Rivers of America to its west is slated for redevelopment, and Bay Lake lies immediately east of the park. Walt Disney World is covered by permanently restricted airspace, and flying over the property requires FAA approval, so view the park only from outside the restricted area. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
