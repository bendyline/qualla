# Tree of Life (Disney)

A 145-foot concrete baobab wrapped in more than 300 carved animals, the Tree of Life anchors Disney's Animal Kingdom, hides a theater in its roots and carries a chimpanzee added at Jane Goodall's request.

**Location:** 28.3579, -81.5906

**Categories:** 1998 sculptures, Disney's Animal Kingdom, Discovery Island (Disney's Animal Kingdom), Walt Disney Parks and Resorts icons, 1998 establishments in Florida, Buildings and structures completed in 1998, Artificial trees, Trees of life

## Introduction

When Jane Goodall looked over the animals being carved into Disney's giant tree, she noticed someone missing. The primate researcher, a consultant to the new park, asked that a chimpanzee join the menagerie, and not just any chimpanzee: David Greybeard, one of the chimpanzees she had famously worked with. So he was added to the trunk, one face among more than 300 carvings of living and extinct species covering the trunk and roots of the Tree of Life, the 145-foot sculpted baobab at the heart of Disney's Animal Kingdom.

## Carving Against the Clock

The tree took 18 months to make, rising between 1997 and 1998 in time for the park's opening on April 22, 1998. Early concepts came from Disney Imagineers Dave Minichiello, Dan Goozee, Ben Tripp, Gerry Dunn and Joe Rohde. The work took three Disney Imagineers and ten artists, and the team that designed and sculpted the animals included senior production designer Zsolt Hormay along with Vinnie Byrne, Fabrice Kennel, Eric Kovach and Joe Welborn. Their biggest enemy was time. The tree is made of concrete, and the cement dried so quickly that the sculptors had only a narrow window to carve and detail each creature before it set. Every creature on the trunk was shaped under that pressure, and together they echo the mythological Tree of Life that gave the sculpture its name.

## A Baobab Built in Houston

The design followed the natural forms of real baobab trees, and the structure was designed and fabricated at the Greens Bayou Fabrication Yard in Houston. The numbers are enormous. The tree soars nearly fourteen stories, 145 feet, and spreads roughly 165 to 170 feet wide, with more than 8,000 branches of wildly different sizes. Its canopy holds roughly 102,000 artificial leaves made of thermoplastic Kynar. It stands on Discovery Island, roughly in the center of Disney's Animal Kingdom. A walking trail, the Tree of Life Garden, winds around the base for a closer look at the carvings. Sharp-eyed visitors hunt there for a Hidden Mickey, which sits upside-down just above the hippopotamus's eye.

## A Theater in the Roots

The designers weighed other uses for the space inside the trunk. At one stage they considered a restaurant at the bottom of the tree, or even letting guests climb up to the top. They settled on a theater instead. The 428-seat Tree of Life Theater, tucked into the tree's interior base, opened with the park and housed the 3D film It's Tough to Be a Bug! for nearly 27 years, until March 16, 2025. On November 7, 2025, a new 3D film took its place: Zootopia: Better Zoogether! The carved animals outside have now watched over the theater's audiences for more than a quarter century.

## When the Bark Wakes Up

At night the carvings come alive in light. Tree of Life Awakenings, a series of projection-mapping shows, debuted on May 27, 2016, when the park began staying open after dark, following a media preview that April. The original four presentations each told a small story across the trunk. In Journey, a playful deer meets a watchful hawk. Rendezvous pairs a hummingbird with flowers, and Gift Giver follows a red fox through a winter scene, while a Disney Medley conjures animals from films including The Lion King, The Jungle Book, Bambi and Finding Nemo. The repertoire has kept growing, with First Snow and Northern Lights for the holidays from 2019, a Beacon of Magic transformation for Walt Disney World's 50th anniversary from October 2021 to April 2023, and features inspired by Avatar: The Way of Water in 2022 and Mufasa in 2024.

## Flight Context

The Tree of Life stands at 28.3579 N, 81.5906 W on Discovery Island, roughly in the center of Disney's Animal Kingdom, south of the Magic Kingdom. From the air look for a broad canopy roughly 165 to 170 feet across, with the open savanna of Kilimanjaro Safaris just to the northwest. Walt Disney World sits beneath a permanent flight restriction (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface to 3,000 feet above ground level); stay above 3,000 feet AGL, around 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL, and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 9 nm to the east-southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 16 nm to the east-northeast, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 18 nm to the northeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
