# Shades of Green (resort)

Tucked between two golf courses near the Magic Kingdom, a former Disney golf hotel became a Department of Defense resort where room rates are set by military rank and the motto is "Serving Those Who Serve."

**Location:** 28.4021, -81.5924

**Categories:** Armed Forces Recreation Centers, Hotels in Walt Disney World Resort, Military recreation, Golf resorts, 1973 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

Most hotels price a room by its size or its view. At Shades of Green, the price also depends on your rank. Room rates here run on a sliding scale that rises with rank and pay grade, because this resort, a short distance from the Magic Kingdom, belongs not to Disney but to the United States Department of Defense. Its name refers to the colors of U.S. military uniforms, and its motto, "Serving Those Who Serve," explains who it is for.

## Fried Ice Cream and Fairways

The resort began as a Disney property. It opened in December 1973 as the Golf Resort, with 151 rooms set between the Palm and Magnolia golf courses and built of wood and volcanic rock to look like a country club. Its main restaurant, the Trophy Room, was known for a dessert of "French fried ice cream," and a duo called Amos and Charles entertained diners with a blend of soft rock, bluegrass, country and folk. Occupancy ran at 60 to 75 percent for most of the year, so Disney went looking for guests beyond golfers. In February 1986 the hotel was expanded by 150 rooms, given a Snow White theme and renamed The Disney Inn, which billed itself as having "the rustic charm of a quiet country inn." By the early 1990s, rooms ranged from $195 a night to $500 for suites.

## The Inn Enlists

On February 1, 1994, the Department of Defense secured a 100-year lease on the resort and restricted it to eligible guests under the military's Morale, Welfare, and Recreation program. The Disney Inn became Shades of Green. The arrangement worked well enough that in 1996 the Department of Defense bought the resort for $43 million, while Disney kept ownership of the land. On April 1, 2002, the resort closed so the original building could be demolished and replaced with a new one designed by KBJ Architects; in the meantime, guests stayed at Disney's Contemporary Resort at Shades of Green rates. When it reopened on March 31, 2004, the room count had doubled to 586, including 11 family suites, spread across the Magnolia and Palm wings, with meeting space, a fitness center and a 500-space parking garage.

## Flags Over the Waterfall

Arriving guests are greeted by a flag array atop a rock formation with waterfalls, where each branch of the U.S. military is represented by a large American flag and a smaller POW/MIA flag above it. Unlike its Disney neighbors, Shades of Green has no theme; it keeps a modern country-club look, surrounded by woods, waterfalls and tropical gardens. It is one of five Armed Forces Recreation Centers resorts, funding itself from non-appropriated funds and reinvesting all profits. Merchandise is free of sales tax and rooms of hotel tax, yet the lease still requires the resort to meet Disney standards. It is not served by the monorail. Instead, a fleet of buses run by a private contractor, separate from Disney's own transport, carries guests to Disney's Animal Kingdom, Disney's Hollywood Studios, Disney Springs, the water parks and the Transportation and Ticket Center, where they can change to the monorail for the Magic Kingdom and Epcot.

## Who May Stay

Shades of Green is not open to the general public. Reservations are limited to active-duty, Reserve and National Guard members and their retirees; current and retired civilian employees of the Department of Defense and the Coast Guard; Medal of Honor and Purple Heart recipients; former prisoners of war; veterans with service-connected disabilities documented by the Department of Veterans Affairs; and designated family caregivers of veterans. Several of those groups were added in January 2020, when the Department of Defense widened its access rules. Others may stay when traveling with an eligible sponsor. Since 2010, the Salute to Our Veterans program has opened the resort each January and September to all honorably discharged veterans, and a Survivors' Family Program serves families whose sponsor died on active duty, whatever the cause.

## Still a Golf Resort at Heart

The fairways that gave the hotel its first identity still wrap around it. Disney's Palm and Magnolia courses envelop the resort, and it adds a nine-hole course of its own, the par-36 Oak Trail, with holes ranging from 132 to 517 yards, plus driving ranges and putting greens. The pro shop offers lessons for golfers of every age and skill level. Two pools, a children's pool, a hot tub, two tennis courts, jogging routes and an arcade fill out the grounds. For gatherings, there are 7,500 square feet of banquet space, and the Magnolia Ballroom holds up to 350 guests, all a short bus ride from the parks.

## Flight Context

Shades of Green is near 28.4021 N, 81.5924 W, southwest of Disney's Polynesian Village Resort and near the Magic Kingdom, about 16 miles southwest of downtown Orlando. From outside the property, the Palm and Magnolia golf courses that surround it make a broad green landmark. Walt Disney World is covered by permanently restricted airspace, and flying over the property requires FAA approval, so view the area only from outside the restricted zone. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 15 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 16 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
