# Disney's Contemporary Resort

One of Walt Disney World's two original hotels, the Contemporary is a steel A-frame with a monorail gliding through its atrium, and the unlikely stage where Richard Nixon told America, "I am not a crook."

**Location:** 28.4150, -81.5740

**Categories:** Hotels in Walt Disney World Resort, Hotels established in 1971, Modernist architecture in Florida, Welton Becket buildings, Watergate scandal, Disney Vacation Club

## Introduction

On November 17, 1973, the President of the United States stood before a convention of newspaper editors in a Disney hotel, a building with a monorail running straight through its middle, and delivered one of the most famous press-conference lines in modern American politics: "I am not a crook." The setting was Disney's Contemporary Resort, which had opened two years earlier as one of the first two hotels at Walt Disney World. Before and since, it has been known less for presidents than for its sloping steel A-frame and the trains that glide through its lobby.

## Rooms Built on an Assembly Line

The hotel, once planned as the Tempo Bay Hotel, opened on October 1, 1971, alongside Disney's Polynesian Village Resort. Its tower, the most prominent of the resort's three stand-alone buildings, is an A-frame whose outer walls slope inward around an inner atrium. The design was a collaboration between Disney, the United States Steel Corporation and Welton Becket, the architect of the Xerox Tower. Construction followed a modular method. Steel frames went up on site, and modular rooms, built in advance to designs by California architect Donald Wexler, were lifted into place by crane. The original buildings at the Polynesian, the Court of Flags Resort and a residential property at Bay Hill, which used the first four units off the assembly lines, were all built the same way, except that their rooms were stacked instead of slid in.

## Trains in the Atrium

Inside, the main hall is the Grand Canyon Concourse, lined with restaurants and shops and presided over by a multi-story mosaic designed by Mary Blair. The Walt Disney World Monorail runs right through the building, with its own station for resort guests. The trains have even taken shelter indoors; during Hurricane Ian in 2022, monorails were parked inside the Contemporary. Some 383 guest rooms line the tower's outer walls, and a three-story wing to the south holds 250 more. Chef Mickey's character buffet and the Contempo Café share the concourse, while California Grill occupies the 15th floor, in the space once called the Top of the World Lounge. Steakhouse 71 takes both its name and its theming from the resort's opening year. A convention center with more than 90,000 square feet of space opened on November 11, 1991, and in 2021 the rooms were redesigned to blend monorail motifs with characters from The Incredibles.

## A President Under Pressure

Nixon came to the Contemporary for the annual convention of the Associated Press Managing Editors Association, where he held a live, televised press conference lasting an hour. His job approval had been falling steadily for more than a year as the Watergate scandal deepened. After an earlier question about his taxes, he took on claims that he had profited from public service. "I earned every cent," he said. "And in all of my years of public life, I have never obstructed justice... People have got to know whether or not their President is a crook. Well, I am not a crook. I have earned everything I have got." The words outlived the moment. Less than a year later, facing almost certain impeachment and removal, Nixon announced his resignation in a national address on August 8, 1974. It took effect the next day.

## The Mystery Building Next Door

Disney began an extensive renovation of the Contemporary in 2005 that ran until 2009. The Racquet Club was demolished by January 30, 2007, and the North Garden Wing came down between January 31 and April 6 of that year. Construction on its site continued through 2007 without Disney saying what was being built, although a condominium association had been incorporated for the property that January. The answer was unveiled on September 16, 2008: Bay Lake Tower, a 15-story Disney Vacation Club building with 295 timeshare units. It opened on August 4, 2009, sharing design features with the original resort and linked to it by a skyway from the main tower's fourth floor to its own fifth. At their initial offering, its units were among the most expensive in the Disney Vacation Club, presumably because the Magic Kingdom sits right next door.

## Flight Context

The Contemporary stands near 28.4150 N, 81.5740 W on the shore of Bay Lake, adjacent to the Magic Kingdom, about 20 miles southwest of Orlando. Its A-frame tower and the 15-story Bay Lake Tower beside it are unmistakable, and the monorail beam can be traced straight into the building. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL, centered close to this site, with limited exceptions), so view it from outside the restricted area and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 10 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
