# The Hall of Presidents

Dreamed up by Walt Disney in the 1950s and opened with the Magic Kingdom in 1971, the Hall of Presidents gathers lifelike figures of every U.S. president on one stage, and each new inauguration sends Imagineers back to work.

**Location:** 28.4195, -81.5822

**Categories:** Magic Kingdom, Liberty Square (Magic Kingdom), Audio-Animatronic attractions, Cultural depictions of presidents of the United States, 1971 establishments in Florida

## Introduction

Weeks after his inauguration in 2009, Barack Obama sat for an unusual recording session in the White House Map Room. The audience was not the nation but a theater in Florida. Walt Disney Imagineering had come to capture his speech and his recitation of the oath of office, and to study his facial expressions and body movements so that an Audio-Animatronic Obama could join the Hall of Presidents. Every president since has recorded the oath for this stage, where figures of all 45 people who have held the office appear together in Liberty Square at the Magic Kingdom.

## A Hall for Liberty Street

The idea is older than Walt Disney World. In 1957, with Disneyland flourishing, Walt Disney proposed an extension of Main Street, U.S.A. called Liberty Street, a re-creation of colonial Boston on the eve of the American Revolution, timed to coincide with Disney's film adaptation of Johnny Tremain. It was to host two stage shows: a re-creation of the signing of the Declaration of Independence with lifelike figures of Benjamin Franklin, Thomas Jefferson and others, and "The Hall of the Presidents of the United States," with an immersive film and a figure of every president. Liberty Street was never built in Anaheim, but the research survived. Over more than a decade of development, Imagineers consulted thousands of paintings, photographs, periodicals and books, and traveled to Washington, D.C., and Colonial Williamsburg. The Hall of Presidents finally opened with the Magic Kingdom on October 1, 1971.

## The Constitution on Trial

The original show, narrated by actor Lawrence Dobkin, was about the Constitution: how it was made, how it preserves liberty and how it has been tested. After a recitation of the preamble, the film opened at the Constitutional Convention of 1787, where George Washington and Benjamin Franklin urged the delegates toward ratification, then followed the young republic into its first test, the Whiskey Rebellion, which Washington was willing to put down by personally leading the federal militia. A 1993 overhaul brought a new script and score, with Maya Angelou narrating and calling the roll of presidents. For the first time, the incumbent spoke, with Bill Clinton recording his speech at the White House. Lincoln got a revised speech, eyeglasses and a sheet of paper to glance at, which some criticized as diminishing an iconic figure. Royal Dano, Lincoln's original voice, was ill, so voice actor Peter Renaday took over the role.

## The Sculptor's Hand

As each new president was inaugurated after 1971, sculptor Blaine Gibson returned to create the new figure, aiming, in his words, to "render the uniqueness of an individual." His last contribution was George W. Bush in 2001. For that update, actor J. D. Hall succeeded Angelou as narrator, reading the 1993 script, and the Clinton figure's Audio-Animatronic was recycled for Bush. The hall closed on October 31, 2008, before that year's election, for an extensive upgrade of its audio and visual systems, and returned as The Hall of Presidents: A Celebration of Liberty's Leaders. After Gibson's retirement, his apprentice, Valerie Edwards, sculpted the Obama figure. The show reopened on July 4, 2009, the 233rd anniversary of American independence. Pam Fisher, a senior show writer for Imagineering, described being present as the president recorded his speech as "quite an experience."

## An Oath for Every Era

A newer film, The Idea of a President, premiered with Donald Trump's first-term version, tracing the presidencies of Washington, Andrew Jackson, Lincoln, Theodore Roosevelt, Franklin D. Roosevelt and John F. Kennedy before the roll call and a short speech by Washington. Clinton, George W. Bush, Obama and Trump, in his first term, each recorded their own remarks. Joe Biden's version, which reopened on August 3, 2021, kept the film; Biden recited the oath but added no speech. After Trump's second inauguration, the attraction closed on January 20, 2025, and reopened on June 29 with little fanfare, the changes made without press releases. It largely reinstated the first Trump version, reportedly reusing his 2017 recording of the oath without the speech that had followed it, and it was the first time a former president had returned to center stage. The hall has since been featured in the Disney+ series Behind the Attraction, and briefly parodied in the 2020 Animaniacs reboot.

## Flight Context

The Hall of Presidents presides over Liberty Square in the northwest part of the Magic Kingdom, near 28.4195 N, 81.5822 W, about 15 miles southwest of downtown Orlando; Bay Lake lies immediately east of the park. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a standing NOTAM, reported as a 3 nm radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL), so view the park only from outside the restricted area and check the current NOTAM text before planning a route nearby. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
