# The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh (attraction)

Built inside the old Mr. Toad's Wild Ride building, the Magic Kingdom's honey-pot dark ride carries guests through a blustery day, a dream of Heffalumps and Woozles and a flood in the Hundred Acre Wood.

**Location:** 28.4201, -81.5804

**Categories:** Magic Kingdom, Fantasyland, Dark rides, Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Winnie-the-Pooh

## Introduction

Look closely at the jumble of pictures in Owl's house and one stands out: J. Thaddeus Toad handing Owl a deed. Nearby, flat on the floor, lies a picture of Pooh greeting Moley, Toad's sidekick. The joke is an inside one. The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh, which opened at the Magic Kingdom on June 5, 1999, moved into the building that had housed Mr. Toad's Wild Ride, and the Imagineers left the previous tenant a quiet farewell in the Hundred Acre Wood.

## A Bear in Waiting

Pooh took a long time to find a ride. After Walt Disney's Winnie the Pooh films, based on A. A. Milne's books, grew in popularity, Imagineers planned a Pooh attraction in the late 1970s for Disneyland's soon-to-be renovated Fantasyland, but when that land reopened in 1983, Pooh was not there. After the success of Who Framed Roger Rabbit in 1988, a Pooh dark ride with spinning honey pots was planned for Disneyland's new Mickey's Toontown; it was canceled, and its space and vehicle design were reworked into Roger Rabbit's Car Toon Spin. Seven years later, with the character enjoying a resurgence, a Pooh attraction was finally approved, this time for Walt Disney World's Magic Kingdom. Rather than build anew, planners used an existing Fantasyland structure, the home of Mr. Toad's Wild Ride. Toad got one more tribute nearby: a statue of him stands in the pet cemetery outside the Haunted Mansion in Liberty Square.

## A Very Blustery Day

Riders board honey pots and glide past a giant storybook showing Pooh and Christopher Robin, then enter the Hundred Acre Wood in the middle of a windy day, the story of Winnie the Pooh and the Blustery Day rather than the tale of the honey tree. Piglet clings to a broom as he spins, Pooh holds a balloon while reaching for honey as Eeyore looks on, and Kanga hangs on to Roo as he starts to blow away. After Owl's wind-tossed home comes a second storybook page, and out bounces Tigger. The vehicles begin to bounce too, following him through the woods as he pops up here and there, at one point upside down. At Pooh's house, Tigger pins Pooh to the floor and warns him about Heffalumps and Woozles. Then Pooh falls asleep and, through a Pepper's ghost illusion, floats up into a sky lit by fiber optics.

## Heffalumps and Woozles

What follows is Pooh's dream, and the ride's strangest room. Honey pots grow eyes and mouths. Giant Woozles on jack-in-the-box necks move in front of the vehicles. A purple Woozle lights up a Heffalump, sending a huge smoke ring from its trunk, while another Heffalump plucks a harp made out of honey. Dancing Heffalumps and Woozles change colors and patterns on the walls, balloons drift upward, and at last two watering cans pour fake rain on the riders. The rain turns real, at least in the story. Thunder and lightning crash in a room painted with clouds, and the vehicles re-enter a flooded Hundred Acre Wood, appearing to float although they are simply moving at a steady speed. Eeyore complains about the wind and then the rain, Gopher squirts water, and Kanga, Roo, Rabbit, Tigger and Owl try to keep Piglet from floating away while Pooh is blown about by the wind.

## The End, With Honey

In the final scene, the storm has passed and everyone except Pooh is celebrating. Piglet is a moving sculpted figure, while the others are painted on the wall behind him. Pooh, naturally, is enjoying a load of honey. The vehicles pass a page that reads "The End" and return to the load area. Peter Renaday narrates, and the ride holds a small place in voice-acting history: it was among the last productions in which Paul Winchell voiced Tigger. The attraction survived the later Fantasyland expansion, and in 2010 it gained Pooh's Interactive Queue, a Hundred Acre Wood with children's games that lets some of a group play while others hold their place in line.

## Pooh Around the World

Versions of the ride followed elsewhere. Disneyland's opened in 2003 in Critter Country, now renamed Bayou Country, replacing the Country Bear Jamboree at a reported cost of $30 million. It uses beehive vehicles, and the mounted heads of Max, Buff and Melvin from the old bear show still hang inside. Hong Kong Disneyland opened on September 12, 2005, with a version most similar to the original Magic Kingdom ride. It was the only dark ride in that park's Fantasyland until It's a Small World arrived in 2008. Shanghai Disneyland opened with its own version on June 16, 2016.

## Flight Context

The ride occupies a Fantasyland show building at the Magic Kingdom near 28.4201 N, 81.5804 W, about 15 miles southwest of downtown Orlando; Bay Lake lies immediately east of the park. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a standing NOTAM, reported as a 3 nm radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL), so view the resort only from outside the restricted area and check the current NOTAM text before planning a route nearby. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Florida.
