# Disney's Polynesian Village Resort

One of Walt Disney World's two original hotels, the Polynesian is a South Seas fantasy of longhouses and over-the-water bungalows, and the place where John Lennon signed the papers that ended the Beatles.

**Location:** 28.4057, -81.5843

**Categories:** Hotels in Walt Disney World Resort, Hotel buildings completed in 1971, Hotels established in 1971, 1971 establishments in Florida, Disney Vacation Club

## Introduction

On December 29, 1974, John Lennon signed the paperwork that officially broke up the Beatles. He did it at a South Seas-themed resort on a man-made lagoon in central Florida, where guests sleep in longhouses named for Pacific islands and the monorail glides past the beach. Disney's Polynesian Village Resort had opened three years earlier, on October 1, 1971, as one of Walt Disney World's first two on-site hotels. In the half-century since, it has grown, changed its name twice and kept retelling its island story.

## Rooms Lowered by Crane

Behind the island fantasy was an industrial experiment. Welton Becket and Associates designed the resort, and US Steel Realty Development built it using United States Steel's then newly developed construction process. Steel frames went up on site, and pre-constructed modular rooms were lifted into them by crane, the same approach used at Disney's Contemporary Resort. The Polynesian opened with roughly 490 rooms (sources give 484 or 492). Growth came in waves. In 1978 the resort added the Oahu longhouse, the Tangaroa Terrace restaurant and support facility, and a second pool, and in 1985 two more longhouses, Moorea and Pago Pago, joined the village. That same year the resort shortened its name to Disney's Polynesian Resort, a title it would keep for almost three decades.

## A Map of the Pacific on Dry Land

Everything revolves around the Great Ceremonial House, designed after a Tahitian royal assembly lodge. It holds guest services and most of the dining and shops, but no rooms. Those are spread across longhouses and water bungalows scattered through the grounds. On October 28, 1999, ten of the eleven longhouses were renamed, all except Fiji. The new names came from islands on the Polynesian isle map, chosen so that each building's place on the property mirrors the relative position of its namesake in the Pacific. Walk from Tonga to Samoa, in other words, and you are tracing a rough chart of the ocean. For decades the Great Ceremonial House also sheltered a tropical waterfall in its atrium, surrounded by more than 75 species of plants. In 2014 renovations removed it, citing health and safety concerns and a wish to turn the lobby into a social space.

## Bungalows over the Lagoon

On September 17, 2013, Disney Vacation Club announced that its next location would be the Polynesian. In May 2014 Disney announced that the resort would return to its old name, Disney's Polynesian Village Resort, and on April 1, 2015, the third major expansion opened. Its most striking addition sits on the water itself: between 2013 and 2015, twenty over-the-water bungalows were built in a new area called Bora Bora. The changes kept coming. In March 2021 the rooms were refreshed with Moana-themed details, colors and textures. A year later Disney confirmed that the Spirit of Aloha Dinner Show, the resort's luau, which had closed two years earlier during the COVID-19 pandemic, would not return, its space needed for further vacation club growth. That growth arrived on December 17, 2024, when the Island Tower opened.

## Family Dinners and Dole Whips

For many visitors the Polynesian is remembered by taste. Ohana, whose name means "family" in Hawaiian, serves an all-you-care-to-enjoy island-style meal while Stitch, Lilo, Pluto and Mickey work the room. The restaurant is older than its most famous host; it already appeared in The Walt Disney World Explorer in 1996, years before Lilo & Stitch arrived in 2002. Pineapple Lanai is a stand devoted entirely to Dole Whips. After dark, Trader Sam's Grog Grotto fills with tiki-bar theatrics from its servers, with the Tiki Terrace as its outdoor counterpart (closed for refurbishment since April 2026, with Disney saying it would reopen in late October), while the Tambu Lounge pours tropical drinks. Getting here is part of the show. The resort sits on the Magic Kingdom monorail loop, with the Transportation and Ticket Center to the east and the Grand Floridian to the west, and the Magic Kingdom resort water launches call here too.

## Flight Context

The Polynesian sits at 28.4057 N, 81.5843 W on the southern shore of the Seven Seas Lagoon, directly south of the Magic Kingdom. From above, look for low longhouses spread along the beach, the monorail beam curving past, the Transportation and Ticket Center and its parking lots to the east and the red roofs of the Grand Floridian to the west. Walt Disney World sits beneath a permanent flight restriction extending 3 nautical miles from its center, from the surface to 3,000 feet above ground level, so view the resort from above that ceiling (around 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL) or from outside the ring, and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 10 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 15 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 16 nm to the east-northeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
