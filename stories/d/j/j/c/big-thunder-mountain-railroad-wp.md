# Big Thunder Mountain Railroad

Disney's runaway mine train was carved out of a cancelled Western mega-attraction, built rocks-first with early computer design, and now runs its trains through painted desert rockwork at four Disney parks around the world.

**Location:** 28.4205, -81.5848

**Categories:** Roller coasters, Magic Kingdom, Walt Disney World, Frontierland, Theme park attractions

## Introduction

Sometime in the late 1800s, the story goes, gold was found on Big Thunder Mountain. The mining camp of Tumbleweed boomed under a greedy company owner named Barnabas T. Bullion, and a line of mine trains hauled the ore, until a flash flood struck, the town was abandoned, and the locomotives were found racing around the mountain on their own, with no engineer at the throttle. None of it happened, of course. The tale lives in Disney's park literature and training materials rather than in anything guests are told, but it explains why, in Frontierland at Magic Kingdom, a train full of riders appears to have no one in charge.

## Rescued from a Cancelled Frontier

Big Thunder began as a piece of something much bigger. Imagineer Marc Davis had sketched the Western River Expedition, a Magic Kingdom pavilion designed to look like an enormous plateau and hold several rides, among them a runaway mine train roller coaster. After the construction and 1973 opening of Pirates of the Caribbean, the whole pavilion was judged too expensive. Imagineer Tony Baxter, who had worked on Davis's concept, proposed cutting the mine train loose and building it on its own, and he designed it with ride engineer Bill Watkins. The project stalled again in 1974 as people and money went to Space Mountain. The delay may have helped: when work resumed, computers were entering attraction design, and Big Thunder became one of the first Disney rides to use computer-aided design.

## Rocks First, Track Second

Earlier mine rides had been built track-first, with rockwork sculpted around the rails. Imagineers reversed the idea, making it look as if the rocks had always been there and the railroad had been threaded through them. Although the Florida design came first, Disneyland's version opened first, on September 2, 1979, with rounded, muted rockwork modeled on the hoodoos of Bryce Canyon in Utah. Magic Kingdom's followed on November 15, 1980, drawing instead on the sharp buttes and vivid colors of Monument Valley. Florida had more room, so its mountain covers 2.5 acres, 25 percent more than Disneyland's. Hemmed in by the Rivers of America (which closed in 2025) on the east and the Walt Disney World Railroad on the west, the ride long kept its maintenance shop across the railroad tracks, reached by a swing bridge; that original shed has since been removed to make room for the planned Cars and Villains Land expansions.

## A Ride Through Tumbleweed

As the Florida ride was laid out before its 2025 refurbishment, trains left an enclosed station on a hillside, ducked into a tunnel full of bats and climbed to pass beneath a waterfall before dropping away to the left. They ran through the flooded town of Tumbleweed beside the Walt Disney World Railroad and rattled over a decaying trestle, where the track is slightly banked from side to side so the cars seem to sway, into Davy Jones Mine, and later spiraled down a 540-degree helix over a broken trestle. The third lift climbed past veins of gold that lit the tunnel. The finale swept toward the Rivers of America, through the ribcage of a Tyrannosaurus skeleton, and past geysers and hot springs before the brakes brought the runaway to rest.

## Four Mountains, Four Personalities

Each park's mountain has its own character. Disneyland's replaced the gentle Mine Train Through Nature's Wonderland and honors it with the village of Rainbow Ridge, reused animal figures and glowing Rainbow Caverns pools; even the name "Big Thunder" once belonged to a waterfall on that older ride. It is also the only version with an outdoor station. Tokyo Disneyland's opened on July 4, 1987, with its own final twist. Paris's, which opened on April 12, 1992, is the only one to have debuted on its park's opening day and described as the largest of them all, rising from an island in the Rivers of the Far West that trains reach through a tunnel under the water. There has been sorrow, too. On September 5, 2003, a 22-year-old man was killed at Disneyland when a locomotive derailed in a tunnel because of improper maintenance; ten other riders were injured.

## Thunder Beyond the Tracks

The mountain's reach extends well past the parks. Sounds recorded from Disneyland's trains became effects for the mine cart chase in the 1984 film Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom. In October 2016 the Journal of the American Osteopathic Association published a study in which a model kidney riding Big Thunder was far more likely to pass a stone in the back row than in the front, while Space Mountain and Rock 'n' Roller Coaster reportedly produced no such result. A feature film based on the ride was announced as in development in 2022. At Magic Kingdom, the ride closed in early January 2025 for a complete refurbishment with new track and refreshed trains and scenes, and it reopened on May 3, 2026, its runaway trains once again racing around a mountain with no one at the controls.

## Flight Context

Big Thunder Mountain stands at about 28.421 N, 81.585 W in Frontierland at Magic Kingdom, Walt Disney World, southwest of Orlando, Florida. Its red Monument Valley-style buttes rise on the western shore of the former Rivers of America, which closed in 2025. After the September 11, 2001 attacks, the airspace over Walt Disney World was restricted, and the restriction was later made permanent (a 3-nautical-mile radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL, with limited exceptions), so view the park from outside the restricted area and check current NOTAMs. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 15 nm to the east in busy terminal airspace, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 15 nm to the east-northeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
