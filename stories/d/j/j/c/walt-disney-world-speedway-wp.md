# Walt Disney World Speedway

Nicknamed "The Mickyard," Disney's one-mile oval hosted the first-ever Indy Racing League race in 1996 and nearly two decades of fan driving experiences before it was razed in 2015 for Magic Kingdom parking and a lake.

**Location:** 28.3967, -81.5777

**Categories:** Motorsport venues in Florida, IndyCar Series tracks, NASCAR tracks, Walt Disney World, Demolished sports venues

## Introduction

Journalists at AutoWeek and the Orlando Sentinel gave it a name that stuck: "The Mickyard," a mash-up of Mickey Mouse and the "Brickyard," the famous nickname of the Indianapolis Motor Speedway. The joke carried a real connection. Walt Disney World Speedway was built by a subsidiary of the Indianapolis Motor Speedway Corporation, and its groundbreaking featured an original paving brick carried down from Indianapolis. For twenty years this compact oval beside the Magic Kingdom parking lot roared with Indy cars, stock cars and ordinary fans living out their racing dreams. Then it vanished beneath a lake and a sea of parked cars.

## A Brick from Indianapolis

The site, chosen in September 1994, was a triangular plot next to the Magic Kingdom parking lot near Disney's Polynesian Resort, and the track was designed to fit inside the existing roads with minimal rerouting. Plans were announced on January 23, 1995. At the groundbreaking on June 27, Indianapolis Motor Speedway chairman Mari Hulman George presented one of the original Brickyard bricks. The one-mile, three-turn tri-oval, drawn up by speedway chief engineer Kevin Forbes, took 5,200 tons of asphalt and 10 miles of safety cabling. Crews beat the weather, too: despite a record-wet June and July in Orlando that included Hurricane Erin, final paving was done on October 18, 1995. At the dedication on November 28, five Indy Racing League drivers, among them Arie Luyendyk and Lyn St. James, drove a five-car "Flying V" formation amid fireworks.

## Racing on a Shoestring

The Speedway was built on a very limited budget, and it showed by design. Only the racing surface, retaining wall and catch fence were permanent. Seating, restrooms and race support areas went up temporarily each year, starting in November for the January races, and teams worked out of their transporters because there were no permanent garages. The approach, borrowed from street circuits such as Long Beach, had never been tried on an oval. It saved money at first, but the temporary grandstands occupied part of the Magic Kingdom parking lot for nearly three months, right through the busy Christmas and New Year's season. The infield held a signature touch: three linked ponds in the shape of Mickey Mouse, nicknamed "Lake Mickey," added a year after opening to solve drainage problems.

## The First IRL Race, and the Traffic

Racing began on January 26, 1996, with a Formula Ford 2000 support race, and the next day the Speedway hosted the Indy 200, the first race the Indy Racing League ever ran. That month Buddy Lazier set the unofficial track record, a lap of 19.450 seconds at 185.089 mph. Off the track, the location soon proved a headache. Race fans and theme park guests arrived at the same time and place, so in 1996 and 1997 Disney parked race spectators at the Magic Kingdom and sent park guests to Epcot's lot, where a roundabout monorail and bus trip drew many complaints. In 1997 a sudden rainstorm cut the race short, and fans rushing to their cars caused hours of gridlock on World Drive. From 1998 race spectators were shuttled in from Epcot instead, at considerable cost.

## Hard Walls

Orlando's warm winters made the Speedway a popular year-round test track, but it was also unforgiving. On January 11, 1997, Eliseo Salazar crashed in turn one during preseason testing and fractured his lower back. Twelve days later Davy Jones broke his neck in a practice crash in turn three. On January 6, 2000, Sam Schmidt was paralyzed in a testing crash. The track was built before the SAFER barrier was introduced, which many believe might have lessened the injuries in all three cases. The racing calendar turned against the Speedway as well. IRL officials felt the January date came too early for teams, Disney worried about a clash with Super Bowl XXXV in Tampa, and talks on a new date quietly ended. The NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series had already left after 1998, and after the 2000 season the Speedway hosted no more major series races.

## Fans at the Wheel

The oval found its lasting purpose with the public. The Richard Petty Driving Experience opened on February 7, 1997, letting fans drive real stock cars or ride beside professionals, and it was said to be the only track in that program operating roughly 365 days a year, weather permitting. A one-lane tunnel eased access, and in 1999 the operation traded trailers and tents for a visitor center, gift shop and modest garage. The Indy Racing Experience made the track its permanent home after 2008, and in 2012 the Exotic Driving Experience added a clockwise infield course for supercars. The oval even carried runners in some years of the Walt Disney World Marathon. In February 2015 the Richard Petty Driving Experience announced it was leaving and Disney said the land would go to transportation improvements. The Speedway's final day was August 9, 2015, and it was then razed to expand and reconfigure Magic Kingdom parking. A large lake now covers part of the site.

## Flight Context

The former Speedway site lies at about 28.397 N, 81.578 W, beside the Magic Kingdom parking area near Disney's Polynesian Resort in Bay Lake, southwest of Orlando, Florida. The oval is gone; look instead for the expanded parking lots and the lake created on the site. Walt Disney World lies under a permanent FAA flight restriction (a standing NOTAM, reported as a 3 nm radius from the surface up to 3,000 feet AGL), so view the resort only from outside the restricted area and check the current NOTAM text before planning a route nearby. Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 10 nm to the southeast, Orlando International (KMCO) about 14 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 16 nm to the northeast. Summer afternoons bring frequent thunderstorms.
