# Mr. Toad's Wild Ride

For 27 years, Magic Kingdom guests took the wheel as the reckless Mr. Toad, smashing through barns, pubs and prison cells on two different tracks before a head-on collision with a train sent them straight to a cartoon hell.

**Location:** 28.4199, -81.5802

**Categories:** Walt Disney Parks and Resorts attractions, Dark rides, Fantasyland, Works based on The Wind in the Willows, 1998 disestablishments in Florida

## Introduction

Every trip on Mr. Toad's Wild Ride ended the same way: with the death of its passengers. Riders took the wheel as the motor-mad Mr. Toad, careening through the English countryside, crashing through doors and walls, until their little car bounced onto railroad tracks in the dark, the headlight of a locomotive swelled ahead, and the train hit them. What came next was a tongue-in-cheek vision of hell, complete with laughing demons. At the Magic Kingdom in Florida, this cheerfully morbid dark ride welcomed guests from the park's opening day in 1971 until 1998.

## A Toad Behind the Wheel

The ride grew from Disney's take on Kenneth Grahame's 1908 novel The Wind in the Willows, one half of the 1949 animated package film The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad. It began at Disneyland, where it opened with the park in July 1955. It was first envisioned as a roller coaster, but Walt Disney worried a coaster might not suit young children or the elderly, so it became a dark ride instead. Unlike its Fantasyland neighbors, it never simply retold its film. Guests became Toad himself, racing a period motorcar through the countryside and the streets of London. In that original, waiting riders heard Toad's film voice, actor Eric Blore, calling out, "Come along! Hop on up here! We'll go for a jolly ride!" and promising "Travel! Change! Excitement!" Much of the scenery was painted plywood flats rather than sculpted figures, and the ride has always remained overwhelmingly flat in its presentation.

## Two Tracks, Two Adventures

When the Magic Kingdom opened on October 1, 1971, its version of Mr. Toad had a twist Disneyland's lacked: two separate boarding areas, each feeding its own track along a different route, so riders got a somewhat different adventure depending on where they boarded. Both were built from highly ornate plywood characters and sets, layered in a way that recalled the multiplane camera work of Disney's animated films. The Right Track burst out of Toad Hall's library into a barnyard, past a sheep, a pig, cows and a bull, and into a barn where chickens attacked. After a turn through a central plaza, riders rolled into a courtroom to face a judge who was really a policeman holding a gavel, then wound through prison cells where weasels were trying to break out, and past a shootout between police and weasels rendered in flashing red lights.

## Mugs in Midair

The Left Track took a different path through Toad Hall, visiting Toad's trophy room and a kitchen before joining the plaza. Instead of the courtroom, it swerved into Winky's Tavern, where Mr. Winky the bartender ducked out of sight and left two beer mugs spinning in the air. The gag was later recycled for the rebuilt 1983 Disneyland ride. Around the corner, weasels hid among the ale barrels, and then riders emerged into the night countryside, passing Ratty's house while Moley sat in a boat. Both tracks ended the same way. A railroad crossing gate swung aside, the car turned onto the tracks, and the approaching locomotive's headlight grew until impact. On the Right Track, a door then opened on volcanoes, flashing lights, laughing demons and a giant flat of Satan that popped up at riders. The Left Track's hell was its mirror image. Neither the film nor the novel contains anything like it.

## Pooh Takes the Keys

In October 1997 rumors began circulating that an attraction based on Winnie the Pooh would take Toad's place. On August 27, 1998, Walt Disney World made it official, and Mr. Toad's Wild Ride closed on September 7, 1998. The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh opened in the space on June 5, 1999. Toad was not entirely evicted. Pictures of Mr. Toad and Moley appear inside the Pooh attraction, and a statue of Mr. Toad stands in the pet cemetery outside the Haunted Mansion in Liberty Square, a quiet memorial for a ride that liked to kill its guests. His original ride lives on across the country. Disneyland's version, rebuilt in 1983, is one of about a dozen attractions still running from that park's opening in 1955.

## Flight Context

The former site of Mr. Toad's Wild Ride, now home to The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh, is in Fantasyland at the Magic Kingdom, about 28.420N, 81.580W, at the northern end of the Walt Disney World resort, southwest of Orlando, Florida. From above, Cinderella Castle at the park's center and the broad Seven Seas Lagoon just to the south are the landmarks. Walt Disney World is protected by permanent flight restrictions from the surface to 3,000 feet AGL within 3 nm, so stay above 3,000 feet AGL or outside that ring; from about 4,500 to 5,500 feet the park is easy to see in clear weather. Orlando's busy Class B airspace overlies much of the area, so contact Orlando Approach. Orlando International Airport (KMCO) is about 14 nm to the east, and Kissimmee Gateway Airport (KISM) is about 11 nm to the southeast.
