# Saint Leo Abbey

Florida's first Benedictine arrived in 1886 to serve German immigrants in a Catholic colony in Pasco County; the monastery that grew from his mission founded parishes across the state, a university, and a Romanesque abbey church.

## Introduction

Florida's Benedictine story began with a language problem. In the 1880s, a Catholic colony in Florida's Pasco County had a growing German-immigrant population, and the bishop of St. Augustine, John Moore, asked for a German-speaking priest to serve it. The answer came from Pennsylvania: in 1886 Father Gerard Pilz, O.S.B., arrived as the first Benedictine in Florida. From that single assignment grew Saint Leo Abbey, a monastery of Benedictine monks of the American-Cassinese congregation, along with a university and a string of parishes reaching far beyond the colony that called for him.

## The Colony of San Antonio

The abbey traces its beginnings to 1882, when settlers began arriving in the Catholic Colony of San Antonio that Judge Edmund F. Dunne had established the year before. Father Pilz came south at the direction of Archabbot Boniface Wimmer of Saint Vincent Archabbey in Latrobe, Pennsylvania, answering Bishop Moore's request. Two years later, in 1888, Saint Vincent Archabbey handed responsibility for the colony's ministry to Mary Help of Christians Abbey in Belmont, North Carolina. Women religious followed. In February 1889 Benedictine nuns arrived from Allegheny, Pennsylvania, and founded Holy Name Monastery. That same year brought another lasting foundation: a school.

## A School by Charter

On June 4, 1889, the Florida Legislature approved a charter allowing the Catholic religious order to build and operate a school. That school eventually became Saint Leo University. For eight decades the monks held title to it, until 1969, when the Benedictines transferred the university to an independent board. Abbey and campus remain intertwined on the ground. The St. Leo Abbey Historic District, added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 7, 1998, lies on the campus of Saint Leo University at 33701 County Road 52. It encompasses 100 acres and contains three historic buildings, among them the abbey church.

## Priests on the Move

The monks did not stay put. Besides providing priests for the churches of the Catholic Colony, they established Catholic parishes in nearby Dade City and in Zephyrhills, New Port Richey, Brooksville, Crystal River, and Ocala. Their reach extended much further: they founded parishes as far away as Farmingdale, New York, and Cuba. In the early 20th century, Saint Leo's Benedictine monks could be found in churches and missions throughout the northern half of peninsular Florida, and in the second half of the century the abbey sent missionaries to Argentina. Closer to home, Saint Leo continued to supply priests for Catholic congregations throughout Pasco, Hernando, and Citrus counties until the last decade of the 20th century - more than a hundred years after Father Pilz first arrived to minister to the colony in German.

## Thirteen Years to Consecration

In 1935, Abbot Francis Sadlier commissioned an abbey church. Tampa architect Frank Parziole designed it in the Romanesque style, and thirteen years passed between the commission and the day the Church of the Holy Cross was consecrated, January 29, 1948. A mosaic sits above its entrance, and in 1998 the church was added to the National Register of Historic Places as part of the St. Leo Abbey Historic District. The community it serves is still very much alive. As of 2019, the monks of Saint Leo came from all over the United States and ranged in age from twenty-three to seventy-four - heirs to a mission that began with a single German-speaking priest.

## Flight Context

Saint Leo Abbey stands on the campus of Saint Leo University in Pasco County, Florida, at approximately 28.337°N, 82.260°W. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, look for the university campus and the abbey church among the surrounding countryside. Zephyrhills Municipal Airport (KZPH) is about 9 nautical miles to the southeast, fittingly near one of the towns where Saint Leo's monks founded a parish, and Brooksville-Tampa Bay Regional Airport (KBKV) is about 13 nautical miles to the northwest. Tampa International (KTPA) lies about 26 nautical miles south-southwest. Summer afternoons in central Florida often bring thunderstorms.
