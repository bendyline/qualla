# Sugarloaf Mountain (Florida)

The highest point on the Florida peninsula began as coastal sand dunes two million years ago, and has since worn pine forest, vineyards, orange groves and a scenic byway that cyclists climb for the challenge.

**Location:** 28.6494, -81.7331

**Categories:** Hills of Florida, Landforms of Lake County, Florida

## Introduction

Florida's most prominent summit started out as a beach. Sugarloaf Mountain is built from relict sand ridges and dunes laid down along a marine shoreline about two million years ago, in the Pleistocene, and today that old coastline stands 312 feet above sea level in Lake County. It is the highest point on the geographic Florida Peninsula and the most prominent point in the entire state. The label "mountain" invites a smile from visitors who know the Rockies, but on a peninsula this low, a sand hill with a view is a genuine summit.

## Rooftop of the Peninsula

Sugarloaf's geology ties it to Florida's sand ridges, and specifically to the Lake Wales Ridge. Measured by height alone, it is only the fifth-highest named point in Florida. The state's true high point, Britton Hill, rises to 345 feet in the Florida Panhandle, making Sugarloaf about 10 percent lower. What Sugarloaf has instead is prominence. Its summit rises more distinctly above the surrounding land than any other peak in the state, and it offers panoramic views of the land around it. Those views would eventually shape the mountain's fortunes.

## Pines, Grapes and Citrus

Before the 20th century the mountain was wilderness, dominated by sandhill and flatwood pine forests. By the 1920s, logging had stripped away its hardwood vegetation and permanently altered its native environment. Scrub crept up the slopes, and for a decade or so farmers planted grapes, mostly on the land south of the mountain. Fungal diseases brought grape growing into decline by the 1940s, and citrus took its place. Citrus groves flourished on Sugarloaf's flanks and carried the local economy until the 1980s, when freezes began to devastate them. As citrus faded, the mountain's property was rezoned for homes, its value now measured in panoramic views. Other lands abutting the mountain, mainly along Lake Apopka, were preserved with the aim of restoring long-absent ecologies. Only a few citrus plantations and vineyards survive today.

## The Climb on Two Wheels

Florida designated the Green Mountain Scenic Byway, which runs mostly along the mountain's eastern side, offering sweeping views of the surrounding landscape and access to newly acquired ecological preserves and historic sites. The byway and the other roads over the mountain have become popular with cyclists, drawn by terrain that is relatively rugged compared with the surrounding area. Not every plan for the slopes worked out. A golf course closed in 2012, undone by low turnout and slow development in the wake of the 2008 housing bubble. Sugarloaf has even found a place in fiction. In Omar El Akkad's speculative novel American War, climate-driven flooding turns the mountain into an island, which becomes a detention facility modeled on Guantanamo Bay during a second American civil war.

## Flight Context

Sugarloaf Mountain's summit lies at 28.6494 N, 81.7331 W in Lake County, rising 312 feet above sea level. The relief is gentle, so look for the patchwork of neighborhoods, remaining groves and preserve land on a broad ridge beside Lake Apopka rather than a distinct peak; a low sun angle helps pick out the rise. A viewing altitude of 2,000 to 3,000 feet MSL gives a good sense of the ridge against the surrounding lowlands. Leesburg International Airport (KLEE) is about 11 nm to the north-northwest and Orlando Executive Airport (KORL) about 22 nm to the east-southeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
