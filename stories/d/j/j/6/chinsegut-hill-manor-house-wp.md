# Chinsegut Hill Manor House

On one of peninsular Florida's highest hills, a manor built with enslaved labor became the home of reformers who hosted Edison and Jane Addams, befriended Lenin, and left behind a plaque that dragged the house into the Red Scare.

**Location:** 28.6186, -82.3650

**Categories:** Historic houses, National Register of Historic Places in Florida, Plantations, Hernando County, Florida, Red Scare

## Introduction

In 1961 a group of Boy Scouts on a hilltop north of Brooksville, Florida, found a plaque on the ground near an oak tree. It honored Vladimir Lenin. They reported it to the Tampa Tribune, and a media firestorm followed. The University of South Florida, which ran the property, claimed to know nothing about it, and on May 4, 1961, the caretaker was summoned before a grand jury to testify about her own activities and those of the couple who had once owned the place. The plaque was only the strangest chapter in the long history of Chinsegut Hill Manor House, a house that has been a plantation, a salon for reformers and a university retreat.

## High Ground in Central Florida

At 269 feet, Chinsegut Hill is one of the highest points in peninsular Florida. Its plantation history begins with the end of the Second Seminole War and the Armed Occupation Act of August 4, 1842, after which plantations spread rapidly across an interior that had seen no white settlement a decade before. Colonel Byrd Pearson of South Carolina claimed 160 acres here, named his plantation Mount Airy and began growing sugarcane with the labor of enslaved people. His cabin, completed two years after Florida became a state, stood on the site of the manor's present East Wing. In 1851 Pearson sold the land to Francis Higgins Ederington, another South Carolinian, who arrived the next year with his family, livestock and goods, and with 30 enslaved people forced to make the journey with them. Between 1852 and 1854 Ederington built the manor house, which is believed to be the oldest house remaining in Hernando County.

## Frozen Groves and Snow Hill

Mount Airy produced sugar cane, cotton and corn, then expanded into citrus, livestock and timber. When the Great Freeze of 1895 killed groves across the region, several trees on top of the hill survived, and their buds helped supply nurseries and groves as Florida's citrus industry restarted. Ederington's daughter Charlotte married Dr. J. R. Snow, who practiced dentistry in the manor house; the couple bought out her sisters' interests, renamed the property Snow Hill and added porches on the first and second floors. Generations of Ederingtons and Snows were born on the hill, and many of their descendants still live in Hernando County.

## A Salon for Reformers

In 1904 Raymond Robins and his sister Elizabeth acquired the property and renamed it Chinsegut Hill. Raymond, later joined by his wife Margaret, turned it into a gathering place for the powerful and the principled. He served as an adviser to presidents from Theodore Roosevelt to Franklin Roosevelt, and during the Russian Revolution he served as commissioner of the American Red Cross Mission to Russia, where he met Kerensky, Trotsky and Lenin, the last of them repeatedly. Margaret spent 15 years as president of the National Women's Trade Union League. Their guests included Jane Addams, William Jennings Bryan, Thomas Edison, James Cash Penney, Marjorie Kinnan Rawlings, Senator Claude Pepper, Interior Secretary Harold L. Ickes and Soviet ambassadors.

## The Gift and the Grand Jury

The Robinses' generosity cost them dearly. After the 1929 crash they gave almost $250,000 to keep the First National Bank in Brooksville from failing. Short of money, Raymond used his connections in the Hoover administration to donate the estate to the federal government on condition that the couple could live there, free of property taxes, for the rest of their lives. Margaret died in 1945 and Raymond in 1954. Five years later the caretaker, family friend Lisa von Borowsky, honored Raymond's wish to commemorate Lenin with the plaque near the oak Margaret had planted in 1918. When it was found in 1961, the grand jury inquiry followed, forty-three years and three days after the oak went into the ground.

## Saving the Hill

Universities kept the house alive, if not always gracefully. The University of Florida leased it in 1954, hoping to use Raymond Robins' 8,000 books as a branch library, but abandoned it when the lease expired in 1958. The University of South Florida took over and turned the property into a conference center, removing the leaking widow's walk in 1963 and adding cabins, a dining room and a classroom over the following decades. In 1982 the Department of Agriculture transferred title to the university, and on November 21, 2003, USF faculty succeeded in adding the house to the National Register of Historic Places. The university returned it to the state in 2008. The Friends of Chinsegut Hill then led a $1.5 million renovation and ran the manor as a museum from 2015 to 2019, and beginning in 2019 or early 2020 (sources differ) the Tampa Bay History Center partnered with Hernando County to open the house for weekend tours and provide curatorial and interpretive services.

## Flight Context

Chinsegut Hill lies at about 28.619 N, 82.365 W, roughly five miles northeast of Brooksville in Hernando County, Florida. The hill rises to 269 feet, a noticeable high point in the flat central Florida landscape; from 2,000-3,000 feet, look for the hilltop manor house and its scattered cabins north of Brooksville. Brooksville-Tampa Bay Regional Airport (KBKV; FAA identifier BKV) is about 10 nm to the south-southwest and is the most convenient field. Leesburg International (KLEE) lies about 32 nm to the east-northeast, and Tampa International (KTPA) about 40 nm to the south-southwest, within busy terminal airspace. Expect summer afternoon thunderstorms and haze.
