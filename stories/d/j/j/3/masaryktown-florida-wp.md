# Masaryktown, Florida

Founded in 1924 by Slovak settlers and named for Czechoslovakia's first president, this small Florida community still carries the names of Slovak heroes on its street signs.

**Location:** 28.4417, -82.4606

**Categories:** Census-designated places in Hernando County, Florida, Slovak-American history, Czech-American history, Populated places established in 1924, Immigrant communities

## Introduction

Drive the east-west streets of this small community north of Tampa and the signs read like a roll call of a nation far away: Štúr, Hurban, Kollár, Štefánik, Hodža, Hviezdoslav, Mudron. Cross them with the north-south streets and you meet Wilson, Jefferson, Madison and Lincoln. Down the middle runs a boulevard named for Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, the man who gave the town its name. Few of the Slovaks who laid out this grid remain, but the map they drew still tells their story.

## Sixty Slovaks and One Czech

Masaryktown was founded in 1924. When the 61 people who launched it first met, 60 of them were reportedly Slovak and just one was Czech. They named their settlement for Masaryk, the founder and first president of Czechoslovakia, who had served in that office since 1918, the year he traveled to the United States to seek President Woodrow Wilson's support and presented him with the Washington Declaration proclaiming Czechoslovak independence. The founders formed the Hernando Plantation Company and sold shares at $1,000 apiece. Each share gave its owner the right to buy 20 acres of Florida land. There is an American thread in the name itself, too: Masaryk shared his middle name with the surname of his wife, Charlotte Garrigue, who was born in Brooklyn.

## Oranges, Frost and Chickens

The settlers' first bet was on citrus. They planted oranges. Then killer frosts struck and devastated the orchards. Many Slovaks gave up and moved away. Those who stayed found another living: by 1952, somewhere between 15 and 25 families remained, and they carried on a more prosperous business in chicken farming. It did not last. Intense competition wore the poultry business down, and by 1965 even those families' farms had declined.

## A Map That Remembers

The founders' most lasting work was not a crop but a street plan. They named the roads running east to west after Slovak national and literary leaders. The Štúr name, for example, honors Ľudovít Štúr, a 19th-century leader of the Slovak national revival and the codifier of standard Slovak. Another road was named for Palacký, the celebrated Czech historian. The central boulevard honors Masaryk himself. The north-south streets pay tribute to American presidents, beginning with Wilson, who helped make Czecho-Slovak independence possible, and continuing with Jefferson, Madison and Lincoln. The effect is a small civic lesson laid out in asphalt: two national stories, one from Central Europe and one from the United States, meeting at right angles on every block. Few Slovaks live here today, yet anyone who asks for directions still has to say their heroes' names.

## A Crossroads on Route 41

Masaryktown sits on the southern edge of Hernando County, covering about 2.7 square kilometers, with Spring Hill to the north and west, Garden Grove to the northeast and Shady Hills, in Pasco County, to the south. U.S. Route 41 runs through its eastern side, leading 9 miles north to Brooksville, the county seat, and 17 miles south to Land o' Lakes. Tampa lies 36 miles to the south. The community remains unincorporated, a census-designated place that counted 920 residents in 2000 and 1,077 at the 2020 census.
