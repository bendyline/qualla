# Mount Dora, Florida

Once called Royellou, this Lake County plateau town has a freshwater lighthouse, a downtown Hollywood once painted pink, and a calendar so crowded with fairs that it calls itself the Festival City.

**Location:** 28.8064, -81.6431

**Categories:** Mount Dora, Florida, Cities in Lake County, Florida, Cities in the Greater Orlando, Cities in Florida, 1883 establishments in Florida, Populated places established in 1883

## Introduction

Ross C. Tremain named his village after his three children, folding Roy, Ella and Louis into a single invented word: Royellou. The name did not survive, but the village did. Today Mount Dora is a Lake County city of 16,341 people, counted in the 2020 census, with an antique-filled downtown looking out over Lake Dora, a lighthouse on a freshwater shore, and a calendar so packed with fairs and celebrations that it goes by the nickname "Festival City."

## A Mount a Hundred Feet High

Settlement began in 1874, when David Simpson arrived with his wife and two children. In 1880 Tremain became the first postmaster and went on to be a major real-estate developer for the area, and a post office called Mount Dora has operated here since 1883. The "mount" is honest, if modest. The town sits on a plateau rising to 184 feet above sea level, slightly more than 100 feet above the lakes that surround it, Dora, Gertrude and Beauclair. That modest height is enough to give the downtown its view over the water. U.S. Route 441 passes through, running five miles west to Tavares, the Lake County seat, and about 25 miles to Orlando, and Mount Dora marks the westernmost point of State Road 46. Water covers about an eighth of the city's area.

## A Lighthouse for a Lake

Lighthouses usually belong to seacoasts, which makes the one at Grantham Point Park, sometimes called "Lighthouse Park," a surprise. The 35-foot tower, built of brick covered with stucco, was dedicated on March 25, 1988, and stands sentry over the Port of Mount Dora on Lake Dora. After dusk, a 750-watt photocell powers a blue pulsator, a guiding light for boaters out on the water. Freshwater lighthouses are rare in Florida, and this is one of the few. Across Tremain Street lies Gilbert Park, named for its donor, Earl Gilbert, with pavilions, a barbecue area and an expanded "Wood Wonderland" playground. Downtown, Annie Donnelly Park hosts the annual tree lighting along with shuffleboard, tennis and pickleball courts, and serves as the stage for many of the city's events.

## When Downtown Turned Pink

Hollywood once repainted the town. For the feature film Honky Tonk Freeway, the buildings of downtown Mount Dora were painted pink. Being a movie location gave local merchants an economic boost, helped restore the downtown and brought the town national publicity. Literature found it too. The fictional Fort Repose in Alas, Babylon, the 1959 post-apocalyptic novel, is based on Mount Dora. The town keeps its own past close, too. The Mount Dora History Museum occupies the first fire station and city jail, opened in 1923, with exhibits covering life here from the 1880s to the 1930s. The Modernism Museum, opened in 2013, explores the Studio Arts Movement, where high art meets craftsmanship and visitors are invited to ask, "Is it sculpture, or is it furniture?"

## Festival City

Mount Dora earns its nickname month by month. The Mount Dora Arts Festival, a juried show of work by several hundred national artists, fills the first full weekend of February, and the Jane Austen Fest brings Regency dress, lawn bowling and English country dance lessons the same month. The Sailboat Regatta, hosted by the Mount Dora Yacht Club and billed as the oldest in the state, arrives in March, and the Blueberry Festival follows in April, celebrating a crop that is taking over from citrus in local farming. In early October the Chamber of Commerce holds what it bills as Florida's oldest bicycle festival, with rides from 8 to 100 miles. One of the biggest crowds gathers on the fourth weekend of October, when the Mount Dora Craft Fair draws more than 200,000 visitors, by the town's own figures, to browse the work of more than 350 crafters and artists, a festival Sunshine Artists magazine once ranked the best of its kind in America.

## Flight Context

Mount Dora lies at 28.8064 N, 81.6431 W in eastern Lake County, northwest of Orlando. From the air, look for the compact downtown on its rise above Lake Dora, with Lakes Gertrude and Beauclair nearby and the town of Tavares about five miles to the west along U.S. Route 441; the 35-foot lighthouse at Grantham Point marks the waterfront. A viewing altitude of 2,000 to 3,000 feet MSL shows the town and its chain of lakes well. Mid-Florida Airport in Eustis (FAA identifier X55) is about 2.3 nm to the north-northeast, Leesburg International (KLEE) about 9 nm to the west, Orlando Sanford International (KSFB) about 21 nm to the east, and Orlando Executive (KORL) about 23 nm to the southeast. Best seen in clear daytime conditions.
