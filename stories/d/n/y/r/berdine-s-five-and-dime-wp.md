# Berdine's Five and Dime

Opened in 1908 and in its purpose-built Court Street home since 1915, Berdine's in Harrisville has been called West Virginia's oldest five and dime, still stocked with tin toys, penny candy and oddities beneath its original tin ceilings.

## Introduction

Tin toys. Penny candy. Gag gifts and handmade glass Christmas ornaments from the Czech Republic. Step into Berdine's Five and Dime on North Court Street in Harrisville, West Virginia, and the shelves look the way small-town variety stores used to look, because in this case they are the same shelves. The oak counters, the tin ceilings and the antique confection counter are all original. Berdine's opened in 1908, celebrated its hundredth birthday in 2008, and has been called the oldest five and dime in West Virginia.

## Two Brothers on Court Street

The store began in 1908, when K.C. Berdine and his brother Lafayette opened Hunt and Berdines five and dime at the corner of Court and Main Streets in Harrisville, in Ritchie County. Business was good enough that by 1915 the store moved to a new building constructed specifically to house it, at 106 North Court Street. That two-story building, painted white and black, has been its home ever since. The store occupies the ground floor, and upstairs the Independent Order of Odd Fellows holds its meetings, an arrangement that pairs two very small-town institutions under one roof.

## Keepers of the Counter

Berdine's stayed in the family for decades. K.C.'s son Fred ran the store from 1954 until 1983, when he sold it to Dean Six, who was still its owner in a July 2025 local TV report and who bought it, the store's manager says, to keep it from closing. Along the way the fixtures that define it survived: the oak shelves and counters, the tin ceilings overhead and, at the heart of it all, the antique confection counter. The building is part of the Harrisville Historic District, and the store has become a tourist attraction in its own right.

## Everything Costs More Than a Dime

The name is a relic of an era when five- and ten-cent stores anchored main streets across the country, and prices have obviously moved on. What has not changed is the spirit of the inventory: novelty and old-fashioned items, tin toys, gag gifts, penny candies and all manner of oddities, alongside the handmade Czech glass ornaments. One description of the place sums it up as "full of the stuff you used to see at 5 and 10 cent stores... in every small town in America." In Harrisville, you can still see it, at the Court Street address where the Berdine brothers' store settled more than a century ago.
