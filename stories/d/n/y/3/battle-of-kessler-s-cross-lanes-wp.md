# Battle of Kessler's Cross Lanes

An August 1861 surprise attack scattered the 7th Ohio Infantry across the Nicholas County hills - and sent one Union major on an extraordinary 80-mile retreat through Confederate territory.

**Location:** 38.2350, -80.9350

**Categories:** civil-war, battles, appalachia, west-virginia, nicholas-county, western-virginia-campaign

## Introduction

The 7th Ohio Infantry was eating breakfast. It was August 26, 1861, the war was about four months old, and the Ohioans were camped at a four-way intersection of farm roads in Nicholas County called Kessler's Cross Lanes. They were green troops, recently mustered, unfamiliar with the mountains they had been sent to defend, and they had no idea that Confederate Brigadier General John B. Floyd had crossed the Gauley River the night before with the express purpose of attacking them at first light. When the firing started, Colonel Erastus Tyler's regiment scattered in two directions at once. Half of them, including Major Jack Casement, would spend the next several days hiking 80 miles through enemy country, ridge after ridge, to safety in Charleston - without losing a single man.

## Floyd Crosses the Gauley

John Buchanan Floyd had been Secretary of War in James Buchanan's cabinet just three years earlier. He had resigned, returned to his native Virginia, and accepted a brigadier's commission in the Confederate army. By August 1861, he commanded Confederate forces in the upper Kanawha Valley and had been ordered to recover western Virginia for the South. The Federal regiment at Kessler's Cross Lanes was a tempting target - small, isolated, and inexperienced. Floyd marched his men down to the south bank of the Gauley River by night, crossed at the ferry, and approached the Federal camp through the woods. The 7th Ohio had only minimal pickets out. The first warning most of the soldiers got was Confederate volleys at close range. Within minutes, the regiment had been broken into two pieces and was running for whatever cover the surrounding terrain offered.

## The Rout

Tyler's right wing fled north and west, eventually regrouping near the main Federal force at Summersville and then linking up with reinforcements arriving from the north under William S. Rosecrans. The left wing, under Major Jack Casement, was cut off from any direct retreat route. Casement made an instant decision that would define his Civil War service. Rather than try to fight through Confederate territory back to the main Union force a few miles north, he led his men south and west - directly into country held by Floyd's army - and began a forced march toward the Elk River. His soldiers traveled at night, hid by day, and survived on whatever they could carry or forage. They crossed ridge after ridge of central West Virginia mountains. They reached the Elk River at what is now Clendenin and followed it down to Charleston. Several days, more than 80 miles. Casement brought every man through. Not one was captured.

## Floyd, Wise, and Lee

Floyd's tactical victory at Cross Lanes did not translate into strategic gain. He withdrew toward the Gauley River and took up a defensive position at Carnifex Ferry, exactly where Rosecrans's three brigades would attack him two weeks later. The Confederate command structure in western Virginia was a mess of personal rivalries and competing authority. Robert E. Lee himself arrived in the region during August to attempt a coordination of Floyd, his fellow former-governor Henry A. Wise, Colonel Douglas Micklich, and Brigadier General William W. Loring. Lee's effort largely failed. The generals would not cooperate. The terrain was difficult. The Unionist sentiment of the local population deprived them of intelligence. By the end of 1861, Confederate forces had been pushed back out of most of the western counties of Virginia.

## Where the Battlefield Is

Kessler's Cross Lanes today is a small rural community at the four-way intersection of WV Route 39 and WV Route 129, in Nicholas County a few miles east of Summersville. The battlefield itself - the open fields where the 7th Ohio was attacked - is mostly private farmland, marked by interpretive signage from the American Battlefield Protection Program. The National Park Service has identified the core and study areas. The nearby Carnifex Ferry Battlefield State Park, where the second engagement took place, preserves a larger and more readily visited portion of the campaign's history. Major John S. Casement, who saved his battalion that August morning, went on to a long postwar career - he became a railroad construction contractor and, with his brother Dan, built the entire Union Pacific section of the transcontinental railroad - over 1,000 miles of track from Nebraska to Utah. Erastus Tyler eventually rose to brigadier general himself. The Confederacy never reclaimed western Virginia.

## Flight Context

Kessler's Cross Lanes sits at 38.23 N, 80.94 W, at the intersection of WV Route 39 and WV Route 129 in Nicholas County, West Virginia, a few miles east of Summersville. Recommended viewing altitude is 2,500 to 4,500 feet AGL. The crossroads and the surrounding open farmland are visible from the air; the Gauley River canyon runs to the south. Nearest airports are Summersville Lake Airport (KSXL) about 6 miles west and Yeager (KCRW) in Charleston about 45 miles west-southwest.
