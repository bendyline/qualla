# Jefferson Davis State Historic Site

At Fairview, Kentucky, a 351-foot obelisk built without reinforcing steel marks the birthplace of Confederate president Jefferson Davis, a monument whose long, halting construction was followed, shortly before the site opened as a public park, by the Klan being allowed to burn a cross atop it.

**Location:** 36.8419, -87.3006

**Categories:** Monuments and memorials to Jefferson Davis, Confederate States of America monuments and memorials in Kentucky, Kentucky State Historic Sites, Obelisks in the United States, Museums in Todd County, Kentucky

## Introduction

There is no steel inside the walls. As workers finished each pour of concrete for the obelisk at Fairview, Kentucky, they left large chunks of limestone jutting up to lock it to the next pour above, and no reinforcing steel went into the walls below the pyramidal top. The result, 351 feet tall, is described as the tallest unreinforced concrete structure in the world and the tallest concrete obelisk. It stands on the birthplace of Jefferson Davis, born here on June 3, 1808, who became the only president of the Confederate States of America.

## A Birthplace Soon Left Behind

Davis did not stay long in Kentucky. In 1811 his family moved to the Bayou Teche country of what is now Louisiana, and less than a year later to a farm near Woodville, Mississippi. Half a century on, from 1861 to 1865, he led the Confederacy through the American Civil War. The cause he led is not in doubt: historians widely agree that preserving the institution of slavery was the principal aim of the Southern states that declared their secession.

## Seventeen Years in the Making

Confederate general Simon Bolivar Buckner Sr. first proposed a monument to Davis in 1907, at a reunion of the Confederate Army's Orphan Brigade. Construction began in 1917 but stopped in 1918 at 175 feet, halted by building-material rationing during World War I. Work resumed in January 1922, and the obelisk was finished in 1924 at a cost of $200,000. Shortly before the site opened as a public park, the Ku Klux Klan was allowed to burn a large cross atop the monument. The builders set its base on limestone bedrock and quarried limestone on the site for use in construction. The concrete walls are 8.5 feet thick at the base and taper to 2.5 feet at the top. An elevator installed in 1929 carries visitors to an observation room at the top, with a window in each of its four walls.

## A Foot Short of Fourth

Among American monuments, the obelisk ranks fifth in height, behind the Gateway Arch at 630 feet, the San Jacinto Monument at 567 feet, the Washington Monument at 555 feet and Perry's Victory and International Peace Memorial at 352 feet, a single foot taller. The still-unfinished Crazy Horse Memorial has a planned height of 563 feet. In 1973 the Davis obelisk was believed to be the fourth-tallest monument in the United States and the tallest cast in concrete.

## The Site Today

The obelisk was closed from 1999 until May 2004 for renovations and the construction of a new visitor center. Today the 19-acre state historic site includes open and covered picnic areas and a playground. In the visitor center museum, a video covers Davis's life and the building of the monument, and guided elevator tours rise to the observation room daily during the season, which has been listed as running from May 1 through October 31, so check with the park before you visit. The center also sells books on Davis, the Civil War and the surrounding area, along with Kentucky handcrafts.

## Flight Context

The Jefferson Davis obelisk stands at 36.8419°N, 87.3006°W in Fairview, Kentucky, between Hopkinsville and Elkton. At 351 feet it is a genuine obstruction as well as a landmark: from 2,000–3,000 feet AGL the shaft stands out from well away, but keep well above and clear of it. Hopkinsville–Christian County Airport (FAA identifier HVC, no ICAO code listed) is about 7.5 nm west, and Clarksville–Montgomery County Regional Airport (KCKV) is about 14 nm south-southwest. Campbell Army Airfield (KHOP) at Fort Campbell lies about 14 nm southwest; it is a military field, so check for restricted airspace before routing that way. Summer haze can soften the view.
