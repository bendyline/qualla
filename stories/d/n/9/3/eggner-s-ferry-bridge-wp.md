# Eggner's Ferry Bridge

The 1932 truss bridge that carried US 68 across Kentucky Lake was raised for the new reservoir, knocked down by a ship carrying rocket stages in 2012, patched in time for Memorial Day, and finally replaced by a four-lane arch in 2016.

**Location:** 36.7722, -88.1205

**Categories:** Road bridges in Kentucky, Bridge disasters in the United States, Collisions between ships and bridges, U.S. Route 68, Transportation disasters in Kentucky

## Introduction

On January 26, 2012, the cargo ship Delta Mariner (since renamed RocketShip) was moving along Kentucky Lake carrying stages of Atlas and Centaur rockets. Operated for United Launch Alliance to haul rocket hardware, the vessel was about to pass beneath the old Eggner's Ferry Bridge on US 68. It headed for the wrong opening. Instead of the shipping channel it normally used, the Delta Mariner tried to pass through the lower recreational channel, and a span of the 1932 bridge came down across its bow. A pickup truck on the bridge stopped about five feet from the edge of the missing section. No one was hurt, and the rocket stages came through undamaged.

## Built Before the Lake

When the bridge opened to traffic on March 25, 1932, there was no Kentucky Lake. The two-lane bowstring truss was built to cross the Tennessee River, carrying US 68 and Kentucky Route 80 between Marshall and Trigg counties. Then came Kentucky Lake. On July 10, 1943, officials closed the bridge to build new pilings and raise the whole structure high enough to clear the coming reservoir, and a ferry carried traffic across the river in the meantime. Kentucky Governor Simeon S. Willis presided over the reopening in February 1944. Raised on its new pilings, the old truss went on carrying traffic across the lake for more than seven decades, with a sister bridge doing the same job over nearby Lake Barkley.

## The Wrong Channel

At least four vehicles were reported on the bridge near the time of the collapse. The driver of the pickup that stopped at the brink said two cars had halted directly behind him, and he could see another car on the far side of the gap. The Coast Guard reported the next day that the ship had tried the recreational channel, which has less clearance. Questions quickly turned to the bridge's lights. Some of its navigational lighting was reported to be out, and a state crew had been scheduled to repair lights the following day; a transportation cabinet spokesman said the lights facing the ship were working. The National Transportation Safety Board later blamed the bridge team's exclusive reliance on the contract pilot's incorrect navigational direction and their failure to use all available navigation tools, and cited the state's failure to maintain the bridge's navigational lighting as a contributing factor.

## An Hour Out of the Way

"Since that bridge carries 2,800 cars every day, we were very fortunate that no one was on the span at that time," Governor Steve Beshear said. The loss was felt quickly in Murray. About 270 Murray State University students lived in Trigg and Christian counties, where the bridge was the most direct route to campus, and the school's nursing students had a clinical placement in Hopkinsville. With the span gone, their drives grew by roughly an hour, along with their fuel bills. An underwater inspection found no significant damage to the piers, and on March 8, 2012, the state awarded an emergency repair contract to Hall Contracting of Kentucky, the same company that had completed emergency repairs to the Sherman Minton Bridge between New Albany, Indiana, and Louisville. The deadline was Memorial Day weekend. The repaired bridge reopened on May 25, 2012.

## Arch Over Truss

The old bridge had been living on borrowed time even before the ship arrived. The state had already planned to replace it and its Lake Barkley sister with arch bridges carrying four traffic lanes plus a sidewalk and bike path, with work first scheduled to begin in 2011 and finish in late 2017; the collision moved the timetable forward. The new tied-arch bridge opened to traffic in early April 2016, at first on two lanes with the rest of the four-lane span and path still to be finished, and the old truss was taken out of service (accounts date its permanent closure to April or May). On the morning of July 25, 2016, crews set off controlled explosives and the middle four spans of the 1932 Eggner's Ferry Bridge dropped into Kentucky Lake.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 36.7722°N, 88.1205°W, where US 68 and Kentucky Route 80 cross Kentucky Lake between Marshall and Trigg counties in western Kentucky. The arch of the new bridge is easy to spot spanning the broad lake; follow the highway east across the Land Between the Lakes to the sister crossing over Lake Barkley. A cruise around 2,000-3,000 feet MSL gives a good view of both bridges and the lakes. Nearby fields include Lake Barkley State Park Airport (FAA identifier 1M9, no ICAO code) about 11 nm east, Murray-Calloway County Airport (KCEY) about 14 nm southwest, and Kentucky Dam State Park Airport (FAA M34) about 17 nm north-northwest. Expect haze and summer thunderstorms, and fog over the lakes on cool mornings.
