# 38th parallel structures

A string of ancient circular scars strung 700 km along the 38th parallel from southern Illinois to eastern Kansas, with geologists still arguing over whether meteorites or volcanoes made them.

**Location:** 37.5000, -88.3000

**Categories:** Possible impact craters on Earth, Impact craters of the United States, Carboniferous impact craters, Landforms of Missouri, Landforms of Kansas, Landforms of Illinois, Hypothetical volcanoes

## Introduction

In 1994, astronomers watched comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 slam into Jupiter in a string of impacts, and interest in whether Earth might ever suffer the same kind of serial bombardment was suddenly piqued. Two years later, researchers Rampino and Volk pointed to a candidate in America's heartland. Running across southern Illinois and Missouri and into eastern Kansas, at a latitude of roughly 38 degrees north, lies a line of circular depressions and deformations (eight in Rampino and Volk's original count, seven in some later summaries) stretching some 700 kilometers. They are known as the 38th parallel structures, or the 38th parallel lineament, and their origin is still an open argument.

## A Row of Scars

What makes the structures so tantalizing is their arrangement. Circular features lined up along a single band of latitude invite an obvious question: did something arrive from the sky in pieces and strike them one after another? The structures are estimated to be about 300 million years old. Three are believed to be the scars of meteorite impacts. The others may be something entirely different, possibly the remnants of volcanoes. The line itself is real. The question is whether the line means anything.

## A Chain of Meteorites?

In 1996, Rampino and Volk proposed that the structures could be the remains of a serial meteorite strike during the late Mississippian or early Pennsylvanian periods, about 320 million years ago, give or take 10 million years. It is a dramatic picture, but not everyone accepts it. Many of the structures are difficult to date, and there are doubts about whether several of them came from outer space at all, which leaves some geologists skeptical. The hard evidence is thinner than the hypothesis. As of 2016, only two of the structures were listed as confirmed impact craters in the Earth Impact Database, both of them in Missouri: Crooked Creek, dated to about 320 million years ago with an uncertainty of 80 million years, and Decaturville, younger than 300 million years.

## Hicks Dome: Pressure from Below

In Hardin County, Illinois, where this story is pinned, the case for forces from below is strongest. There is evidence that at least some of the features, including Hicks Dome, are volcanic in origin. These structures are associated with faults and fractured rock, and they are accompanied by igneous rocks and mineral deposits. Hicks Dome is a structural dome whose central core of Devonian rock has been pushed upward some 4,000 feet relative to the surrounding strata, with small igneous dikes around its flanks. The dome is underlain by ultramafic igneous rocks and by diatremes, or breccia pipes, and most geologists accept that deep-seated igneous activity lifted the older rocks at its center. That same activity may also have supplied the fluorine in the region's fluorspar deposits. Fluorspar was mined in Hardin County until the 1990s, a local industry that may ultimately trace back to what happened deep underground.

## Why Earth Rarely Gets a Crater Chain

The Jupiter spectacle that revived interest in serial impacts also exposes the hypothesis's weakness. For a speeding comet or asteroid to break into a string of fragments, a planet's gravity must pull it close enough to be torn apart by tidal forces. Jupiter's enormous gravity can do that; Earth's weaker field is much less able to, and the likelihood of such an event occurring here has been estimated as vanishingly small. Yet the Moon, with gravity weaker still, bears chains of craters across its surface, evidence that serial impacts can happen even to a small world. So the 38th parallel structures remain suspended between two explanations. Two are confirmed impact craters, Hicks Dome points toward volcanism, and the idea that a single shattered visitor carved them all in one violent sequence is still waiting for proof.

## Flight Context

Reference point: 37.5N, 88.3W, in Hardin County, Illinois, about 4 nm east-southeast of Hicks Dome (centered near 37.53N, 88.37W). The lineament itself runs roughly east-west along the 38th parallel for about 700 km toward eastern Kansas, but it is not a continuous line you can follow from the air; its structures are best appreciated on a geological map. From 4,000 to 6,000 feet, fly over the Hicks Dome area and picture a core of rock pushed up some 4,000 feet beneath you. Nearby airports: Marion-Crittenden County James C. Johnson Regional in Kentucky (FAA: GDA), about 13 nm southeast; Sturgis Municipal in Kentucky (FAA: TWT), about 17 nm east; Harrisburg-Raleigh (KHSB), about 22 nm north-northwest; and Barkley Regional at Paducah (KPAH), about 35 nm southwest, with scheduled service.
