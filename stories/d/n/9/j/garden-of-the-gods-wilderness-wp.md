# Garden of the Gods Wilderness

In the Shawnee National Forest of southern Illinois, 300-million-year-old sandstone that the glaciers never reached has weathered into hoodoos and cliff-top overlooks, one of them now stamped on a U.S. quarter.

**Location:** 37.6069, -88.3947

**Categories:** Wilderness areas of Illinois, Shawnee National Forest, Rock formations, Hiking trails in Illinois, Protected areas of Saline County, Illinois

## Introduction

Look closely at the right 2016 quarter and you may find a piece of southern Illinois. That year the U.S. Mint struck some 300 million quarters bearing an image of a sandstone hoodoo from the Garden of the Gods Wilderness, with Camel Rock appearing on one set in the Mint's 56-image America the Beautiful series. The real rock stands in the Shawnee National Forest, within a wilderness of about 3,300 acres (the 1990 law that created it says approximately 3,293) that the Forest Service places in Hardin, Pope and Saline counties, where the land turns far steeper and rockier than in most of Illinois.

## Sandstone the Ice Never Touched

The story of these rocks begins in the Carboniferous period, around 300 million years ago, when local conditions laid down a thick bed of gray sandstone in what is now southern Illinois. Later that bed was lifted up, and the Garden of the Gods is part of the uplifted sandstone plateau that resulted. Its luck came much later. Unlike most of Illinois, this plateau was never buried by glaciers. During the Illinoian glaciation, the ice sheets made their farthest advance and stopped just to the north, leaving the old stone to weather in place.

## Anvils, Camels and Tables

Erosion has worked on the sandstone with comparatively dramatic results, carving hoodoos and other unusual formations. Several carry evocative names: Anvil Rock, Camel Rock, Table Rock and, according to the Forest Service, Devil's Smokestack. A quarter-mile Observation Trail, surfaced with natural sandstone and lined with interpretive signs on the area's geological history, leads visitors among them. The same forces opened scenic overlooks such as Buzzards Point, where raptors, scavenger birds and people alike can gaze out over the Shawnee National Forest. The trails and overlooks draw especially heavy use during the fall color season. For those who want to stay, Pharaoh Campground, open year round, offers 12 campsites.

## Farmland Gone Back to Forest

The woods here are younger than the rocks. Like other wilderness areas in the Shawnee, the Garden of the Gods is made up of second-growth forest on land that was farmed until federal land acquisitions in the 1930s. Shawnee National Forest was created in 1939. In 1990 Congressman Glenn Poshard sponsored, and Congress passed, the Illinois Wilderness Act, which set aside seven separate parcels within the forest as relatively small wilderness areas. Garden of the Gods is one of them, a roadless tract, but a tongue of non-wilderness land carries a paved road from Buzzards Point to a hiking, camping and public-use area in the southeastern corner of Saline County. From there, trails reach much of the wilderness, and that easy access has made Garden of the Gods the most visited of the Shawnee National Forest's wilderness areas. The long-distance River to River Trail passes through as well. The nearest town of any size is Equality.

## Flight Context

Garden of the Gods Wilderness centers near 37.6069°N, 88.3947°W in the Shawnee National Forest of southeastern Illinois, spanning parts of Hardin, Pope and Saline counties (some listings also name Gallatin); the nearest town of any size is Equality. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, look for exposed sandstone bluffs and rock outcrops breaking through the continuous forest canopy of the hills, a sharp contrast with the flatter farmland to the north. This is a designated wilderness, so fly high and quietly and avoid low passes over the overlooks, which are busy with hikers, especially in fall. Harrisburg-Raleigh Airport (KHSB) is about 14 nm north-northwest, and Veterans Airport of Southern Illinois at Marion (KMWA) is about 31 nm west-northwest. Morning fog can collect in the hollows on cool days.
