# Cave-In-Rock, Illinois

A 55-foot-wide cave in the Ohio River bluffs gave a tiny Illinois village its name and, for decades, a reputation as a haunt of river pirates, counterfeiters and horse thieves.

**Location:** 37.4700, -88.1664

**Categories:** Villages in Hardin County, Illinois, Illinois populated places on the Ohio River, Pirate dens and locations, 19th-century pirates, State parks of Illinois

## Introduction

For flatboat crews drifting down the Ohio River toward New Orleans, the cave was a landmark on a dangerous journey. Its mouth, 55 feet wide, opens in the limestone bluffs of the Illinois bank, carved by water long before any boatman saw it; the Illinois State Geological Survey attributes it to groundwater dissolving the rock, probably millions of years ago. A shelter like that was bound to attract company. From the 1790s to the 1870s, the country around it was plagued by what historians as early as the 1830s called the "Ancient Colony of Horse-Thieves, Counterfeiters and Robbers." Today the cave anchors a state park, and the village beside it, Cave-In-Rock, counted 228 residents in 2020.

## A Tavern in the Rock

The first European known to have found the cave was M. de Lery of France, in 1729. By 1790, the counterfeiters Philip Alston and John Duff were using it as some kind of rendezvous. Then, in 1797, Samuel Mason moved his base of operations from Diamond Island and Red Banks to the cave and made it a home for river pirates. Mason created a combination tavern, gambling den, brothel and criminal refuge, and his men lured in unwary river travelers, then robbed and killed them. The people who died there were ordinary voyagers trusting a riverside welcome. By one account, a sign over the entrance in the late 1790s read "Wilson's Liquor Vault and House for Entertainment." That spring the infamous Harpe brothers also arrived in the region, and grim stories cling to their time here; in one, they pushed a young couple off the cliff above the cave, and the couple survived. Mason eventually moved downriver and turned to highway robbery along the Natchez Trace.

## Outlaw Country

Mason's departure did not end the area's reputation. The Harpe brothers are tied to yet another story at the infamous Potts Spring area to the north of the cave. In the 1810s steamboats began working the Ohio, and travelers such as the artist Karl Bodmer bought passage up and down the river past the bluffs. Law officers led three raids on Sturdivant Fort, stronghold of the counterfeiter Sturdivant, in 1822 and 1823. The Ford's Ferry Gang broke up only after the mysterious deaths, or murders, of Ford's two sons, followed by his own assassination in 1833.

## From Rock and Cave to Cave-In-Rock

Ordinary settlers came too. The earliest known permanent white settlers arrived in 1816 and began building a town near the cave, first known as Rock and Cave, Illinois, with a post office under that name. On October 24, 1849, it was officially renamed Cave-In-Rock. The village sits in southeastern Hardin County, bordered on the south by the Ohio, and the Cave-In-Rock Ferry has long carried traffic across the river to Crittenden County, Kentucky, though its operator suspended service on July 1, 2026, in a dispute over state funding, with no date for its return announced as of mid-July, so check before relying on it. In 1929 the State of Illinois bought 64.5 acres around the cave for a park, which has since grown to 204 acres.

## Pirates on the Big Screen

The cave's outlaw legend made it irresistible to storytellers. Walt Disney Productions set the climax of its 1956 adventure Davy Crockett and the River Pirates at the cave, though the film itself is recorded as shot in Morganfield, Kentucky; in it Crockett and Mike Fink anachronistically battle Sam Mason and his pirates. The cave also appears in the 1962 epic How the West Was Won, with a scene filmed here and at Battery Rock. L. A. Meyer's novel Mississippi Jack sends its heroine on an equally anachronistic raid against river pirates as a homage to the Disney episode. In 2009 the artist Greg Stimac photographed the cave for a series on outlaw hideouts, and in 2013 his image, titled "Ancient Colony of Horse-Thieves, Counterfeiters and Robbers," was shown at the Kentucky Museum of Art and Craft. Modern crowds arrive for gentler reasons, including a village Frontier Days festival and a yearly heavy metal festival, Full Terror Assault, at the Hogrock Ranch and Campground.

## Flight Context

Cave-In-Rock sits at 37.4700°N, 88.1664°W on the Illinois bank of the Ohio River in southeastern Hardin County, with Crittenden County, Kentucky, directly across the water. From 1,500–2,500 feet AGL, follow the Ohio and look for the small village and the limestone bluffs of the state park just beside it; the ferry landing is a good marker. The cave faces the river, so it is best seen from over the water on the Kentucky side looking north. Marion-Crittenden County James C. Johnson Regional Airport in Kentucky (FAA identifier GDA, no ICAO code listed) is about 8.5 nm south-southeast, Sturgis Municipal Airport in Kentucky (FAA identifier TWT, no ICAO code listed) is about 11 nm east-northeast, and Harrisburg-Raleigh Airport (KHSB) in Illinois is about 27 nm northwest. River fog is common on calm, cool mornings.
