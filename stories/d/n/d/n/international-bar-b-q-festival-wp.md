# International Bar-B-Q Festival

Since 1979, Owensboro, Kentucky, has spent a May weekend each year celebrating its church-team barbecue, its mutton and its burgoo, in a festival now known as BBQ and Barrels.

**Location:** 37.7761, -87.1156

**Categories:** Food and drink festivals, Barbecue, Festivals in Kentucky, Owensboro, Kentucky, Bourbon

## Introduction

Ten tons of mutton. Five thousand chickens. Fifteen hundred gallons of burgoo. Those were the estimates for a single weekend in 2018, when an estimated 80,000 people were expected in Owensboro, Kentucky, for a festival that began in 1979 as the International Bar-B-Q Festival and is now called BBQ and Barrels. Held on the second weekend in May, it celebrates two staples of the Owensboro area, barbecue and bourbon, and it has taken place every year since it began.

## Church Teams and the Governor's Cup

The first festival, in 1979, was nothing more than a barbecue cooking competition. Teams cooked, and festival-goers tasted what they made. Each team was associated with a local church, and they competed for the Governor's Cup, with other titles awarded for best chicken, best mutton and best burgoo.

That church-team tradition is still at the heart of the event. The Backyard BBQ Cook-Off, the one activity that has been there since the beginning, brings together church teams and local vendors competing for overall first place and for best chicken, best pork and best beef. The overall grand champion has taken home $1,500, a first-place trophy, a traveling trophy, and a waiver of the next year's entry fee.

## Why Mutton?

Mutton is a particular delicacy in Owensboro, and its roots are traced to early Welsh settlements in the area. Those settlers relied heavily on sheep for their wool. Once the animals had matured past their wool-producing years, farmers harvested them for meat, and a regional taste was born.

Burgoo, a hearty stew, has its own community story: it is a fixture of Daviess County's Catholic parish picnics and fundraisers, in a county with a large German Catholic population. Put the two together, and the menu at an Owensboro barbecue carries the traces of the people who settled this stretch of the Ohio Valley, one pot and one pit at a time.

## A Drive-Thru and a New Name

In 2020 the pandemic made it impossible to hold the festival in its usual form. Rather than halt it entirely, organizers created a Drive-Thru Festival, handing out free barbecue on the weekend the event had been planned. When restrictions on gatherings were lifted in 2022, the festival returned as the Bar-B-Q Block Party, with no change to its activities. In 2023 it became BBQ and Barrels, a nod to the bourbon that now shares the spotlight with the smoke. The 2026 festival ran Friday and Saturday, May 8 and 9, and organizers say about 25,000 people attend in a typical year.

The list of things to do has grown well beyond the cook-off: bourbon tasting, food trucks, carnival rides, car shows, road races, live music, games, arts and crafts, pageants, eating competitions and raffles.

## A Barrel Lid for the Best Car

Even the car show has a bourbon twist. At the 2024 event, the car show and road race let attendees see a range of unique and classic vehicles. The show was invitation-only, with fewer than 100 vehicles allowed, and owners who hoped to take part had to email photos and descriptions of their cars. One winner went home with a custom bourbon barrel lid.

It is a long way from a single church cooking contest in 1979, but the core has not changed: neighbors, church teams and visitors gathered around mutton, chicken and burgoo on a spring weekend in Owensboro.
