# Red River Meeting House

In June 1800, a Presbyterian sacrament at a small church on Kentucky's Red River turned into what Kentucky's historical marker calls the first camp meeting and the spark of the Second Great Awakening.

**Location:** 36.7211, -86.8147

**Categories:** Camp meeting grounds, Presbyterianism in Kentucky, History of Christianity in the United States, Churches on the National Register of Historic Places in Kentucky, Logan County, Kentucky

## Introduction

"The cries of the distressed filled the whole house." That is how the Presbyterian minister James McGready remembered a Monday in June 1800 at the Red River Meeting House in Logan County, Kentucky. Over five days, June 13 to 17, frontier settlers gathered here for what is regarded as the first religious camp meeting in the United States, and it marked the start of the Second Great Awakening, a major religious movement in the first part of the nineteenth century.

## A Sacrament Becomes a Revival

It began as something ordinary. What became known as the Revival of 1800 started as a traditional Presbyterian sacramental occasion, organized by McGready, whose name is also spelled M'Gready, with several preachers taking part. McGready also led congregations at Muddy River and Gasper River, and as the revival spread to them, several hundred people attended meetings that ran from Friday through Tuesday. For settlers the gathering was a chance to end their relative isolation for several days and to meet new people. In a letter to a friend dated October 23, 1801, McGready called that June sacrament "the greatest time we had ever seen before."

## Struck Down Under Conviction

His letter describes a scene of raw emotion. On the Monday, he wrote, "multitudes were struck down under awful conviction." Profane swearers and sabbath breakers cried out, "what shall we do to be saved?" while "frolicers, and dancers" begged for mercy. He saw children of ten, eleven and twelve praying and crying "in agonies of distress." By the following Tuesday, McGready believed, ten people had been "savingly brought home to Christ." Barton W. Stone, another Presbyterian minister, left his own account of the Logan County revival, describing multitudes encamped for days and nights on the edge of a prairie. "The scene was new to me and passing strange," he wrote. "It baffled description. Many, very many, fell down as men slain in battle."

## Churches Lost and Rebuilt

The ground had a history before the revival. The first settlers of European descent, Ambrose Maulding and his family, arrived in 1780 and established what became known as Maulding's Station, or Maulding's Fort. A state historical marker says the church was organized in 1789 by "A Society of Presbyterians," and its first log building went up between the mid-1780s and 1792, depending on the source. That building was gone by the late 1850s (one account says it collapsed in 1856), and the cemetery later expanded onto its site. A white frame church built nearby, the Red River Cumberland Presbyterian Church, came down in 1929 by one account and 1935 by another, and a log cabin believed to resemble the original went up in 1959, only to burn from an unknown cause on May 2, 1992. The Red River Meeting House and Cemetery Association raised funds for a replacement pioneer church, built in May 1994.

## A Psalm in Gaelic

The cemetery beside the church holds the graves of numerous veterans of the Revolutionary War and the War of 1812. One stone, William McPherson's, is engraved with the Twenty-third Psalm in Gaelic. The site was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1976. It is privately owned but open to the public year-round from dawn to dusk, and admission is free; restrooms are available only by rental, and self-guided cemetery tour pamphlets are available on site. The Association holds its annual meeting on the grounds on the second Sunday in September, and during the local Tobacco & Heritage Festival, on the second weekend in October, a primitive camp meeting and rendezvous returns each year to the place where the first one began.

## Flight Context

The Red River Meeting House stands at 36.7211°N, 86.8147°W along Kentucky Route 663 and the Red River, about three miles east of U.S. Route 431 near Adairville in southern Logan County, a few miles north of the Tennessee line. From 2,000-2,500 feet AGL, follow the Red River through open farmland and look for the small church and cemetery near Route 663. Russellville-Logan County Airport (FAA identifier 4M7) is about 5 nm north, Bowling Green-Warren County Regional Airport (KBWG) is about 24 nm northeast, and Nashville International Airport (KBNA) is about 36 nm south, so stay clear of Nashville's airspace to the south. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
