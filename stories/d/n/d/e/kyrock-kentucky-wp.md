# Kyrock, Kentucky

Kyrock, a Kentucky company town that shipped rock asphalt as far as Rio de Janeiro and Havana, vanished after cheap petroleum asphalt closed its quarries, leaving a water tower, a footbridge foundation and a name that endures.

**Location:** 37.2633, -86.2567

**Categories:** Ghost towns in Kentucky, Company towns in the United States, Mining history, Edmonson County, Kentucky

## Introduction

Some of the streets of Rio de Janeiro and Havana were paved with rock from Edmonson County, Kentucky. It came from Kyrock, a company town on the Nolin River whose name was taken from its maker, the Kentucky Rock Asphalt Company. For forty years the company promoted, quarried and shipped natural rock asphalt, becoming the world's largest producer of it. Today almost nothing of the town survives beyond its original water tower and a concrete foundation for a footbridge over Pigeon Creek, yet the name has proved remarkably hard to erase.

## Rock That Paved the Roads

The story began on another river. From around 1900 the Wadsworth Stone and Pavement Company worked quarries along the Green River near the town of Asphalt, about six miles west of Brownsville. In 1917 a merger of two rock mining and paving firms created the Kentucky Rock Asphalt Company, and in 1918 the plant relocated to a new site, where eight new quarries and a processing facility opened. Company lore holds that its sand-based material was the first ever laid for the Indianapolis Motor Speedway, though the Speedway's own history describes the original 1909 surface as crushed stone and tar. In the company's 1920s heyday it was Edmonson County's largest local business, shipping hundreds of tons of rock asphalt by steamboat on the Nolin and Green rivers so that roads could reach places that had never had them.

## A Town Built to Order

Around the quarries the company built a town, incorporated in 1918. About 150 homes were spread across three sections, Kyrock itself plus Ridgedale and Woodside, and the company added an elementary and high school building, a Methodist church, two hotels, a commissary and a small baseball stadium. A post office opened in 1920. Besides the Nolin River itself, a ferry connected the town to Whistle Mountain and on toward what is now KY 728 in the northeastern part of the county. Kyrock High School was, at one point, the largest school in the county. For much of the first half of the twentieth century, Kyrock was known as a company town, built by and for the quarries.

## Headlines From Sand Cave

In 1925 Kyrock drew national attention for a very different reason. When cave explorer Floyd Collins was trapped by fallen boulders in Sand Cave, in what is now Mammoth Cave National Park, the company sent some of its asphalt mine workers to aid in the rescue effort, and the episode brought the company national media exposure. Attention was something the company understood. Heavy promotion and advertising in newspapers and trade publications are credited with keeping it open for a total of forty years, during which it became the state's most successful asphalt mine as well as the largest producer of natural rock asphalt anywhere in the world.

## When Oil Won

Natural rock asphalt could not outlast cheaper competition. Petroleum-based asphalt offered a lower-cost way to pave, and facing stiffer competition from other asphalt companies and higher shipping costs, the Kentucky Rock Asphalt Company shut down in 1957. The post office had already closed in 1955. In 1959 Kyrock High School merged with the county's other rural high schools and Brownsville High School to form Edmonson County High School, and the town was later disincorporated, in 1966 by one account and in the late 1950s by another. The homes, hotels and ballpark are gone. Aside from the original water tower, the only remnant is the concrete foundation of a swinging footbridge over Pigeon Creek.

## A Name That Stayed

Kyrock never quite left local life. After the high school moved away, the Kyrock School kept teaching the area's children, and since August 1981 its building has housed Kyrock Elementary, the only elementary school for all of northern Edmonson County since 1979. The school is set to move in 2027 to a new building across from the current one, which will be preserved. A volunteer fire department also carries the Kyrock name, a local diner that bore it closed in 2024, and the Kyrock Missionary Baptist Church still exists in a newer building. On May 23, 2014, the Kentucky Historical Society dedicated a historical marker at Kyrock Elementary, and Kyrock Road still runs from KY 259 in Sweeden to KY 728 just west of Nolin Lake.

## Flight Context

Kyrock lies at 37.2633°N, 86.2567°W in Edmonson County, Kentucky, about 2.4 miles east of Sweeden and 5 miles north-northeast of Brownsville, near the Nolin River and west of Nolin Lake. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, the long arms of Nolin Lake and the forested country of Mammoth Cave National Park to the south are the best references, since little of the town remains to see. Grayson County Airport at Leitchfield (FAA identifier M20) is about 8 nm north, Glasgow Municipal Airport (KGLW) is about 20 nm southeast, and Bowling Green-Warren County Regional Airport (KBWG) is about 20 nm south-southwest. River valley fog is common on autumn mornings.
