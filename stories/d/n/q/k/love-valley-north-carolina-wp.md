# Love Valley, North Carolina

A cowboy town built from scratch in the Brushy Mountains by a Charlotte contractor who never let go of his boyhood dream, where horses have the downtown streets to themselves.

**Location:** 35.9894, -80.9883

**Categories:** Towns in Iredell County, North Carolina, Towns in North Carolina, Populated places established in 1954

## Introduction

When Andy Barker was a boy in Charlotte, he wanted to be a cowboy. His teachers told him there were no cowboy towns in North Carolina. Barker grew up, went into the construction business with his father, served in the Army Corps of Engineers in World War II and built a successful contracting career. Then, in April 1954, at the age of 29, he left it behind, moved his wife and two small children to a one-room shack in the Brushy Mountains of northern Iredell County, and set out to prove his teachers wrong. The town he built is called Love Valley.

## A Cowboy and a Congregation

Barker had carried two dreams since his youth: he wanted to be a cowboy, and he wanted to build a Christian community. Love Valley combined them. The first structure in town was a church, and a small church still sits on a hill overlooking Love Valley, a reminder of that half of Barker's vision. Around it grew an Old West-style village modeled on the frontier towns of countless Hollywood films, complete with a saloon, a general store, hitching posts and rodeos. The remote valley setting helped the illusion. Love Valley was incorporated on April 2, 1963, and Barker's father, known as A.J. Barker, served as its first mayor. Andy Barker himself went on to hold the office for all but six of the town's first 48 years as an incorporated town.

## Horses Have the Right of Way

The most striking rule in Love Valley is the one about cars: automobiles are banned from the downtown streets. People get around on horseback and in horse-drawn vehicles, which puts Love Valley in unusual company alongside places like Mackinac Island. Miles of riding trails lead out into the surrounding Brushy Mountains, and the town has become a popular gathering spot for horse lovers and riders. In the late 1990s the Love Valley Horseman's Association and the Love Valley Mule Association were formed, and they have run many of the town's events. The town is still tiny, with 90 residents at the 2010 census and 154 by 2020.

## The Festival Years

Love Valley has not always been sleepy. In July 1970 the town hosted a three-day rock festival, with the Allman Brothers Band as the headliner, and the festival became one of the controversies the town weathered in the 1960s and 1970s, with some locals complaining that it drew drug users and other "troublemakers" to the area, though a state history account says the weekend passed without major incident. Barker, meanwhile, never lost his appetite for public life; he ran for governor of North Carolina in the Democratic primary in 1976 and 1984. He died on July 11, 2011, and he and his wife are buried in the cemetery of the church he built first. The boy who was told there were no cowboy towns in North Carolina had spent nearly six decades living in one of his own making.

## Flight Context

Love Valley is at 35.9894°N, 80.9883°W in the Brushy Mountains of northern Iredell County, North Carolina, north of Statesville. The town is tiny and tucked into a valley; look for its short Western-style main street and the small church on a hill above town, with the ridges of the Brushy Mountains around it. A viewing altitude of about 3,000-3,500 feet MSL keeps you clear of the surrounding ridges. Statesville Regional Airport (KSVH) is about 14 nm south, Wilkes County Airport in North Wilkesboro (KUKF) about 15 nm north-northwest, and Hickory Regional Airport (KHKY) about 25 nm southwest. Several small private airstrips dot the area, so watch for light-aircraft traffic. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms, and morning fog in the valleys.
