# Thirsty Beaver

A one-story Charlotte dive bar whose landlord refused to sell, leaving it wrapped in a horseshoe of new apartments and turning it into a local symbol of holding out against redevelopment.

**Location:** 35.2213, -80.8173

**Categories:** 2008 establishments in North Carolina, Buildings and structures in Charlotte, North Carolina, Culture of Charlotte, North Carolina, Dive bars in the United States, Drinking establishments in North Carolina, Economy of Charlotte, North Carolina, Saloons, Real estate holdout

## Introduction

The Associated Press called it "Charlotte's own version of the house from Up, the Pixar movie." In Charlotte's Plaza-Midwood neighborhood, about a mile northeast of Uptown, a one-story bar called the Thirsty Beaver sits in the middle of a 323-unit apartment complex that curls around it in a horseshoe. The apartments went up on every side they could reach. They could not reach the bar, because the land beneath it was not for sale.

## A Bar Among Empty Lots

Brothers Brian and Mark Wilson opened the Thirsty Beaver in 2008 in a small one-story building. At the time, the property was surrounded by vacant lots, and a low-slung neighborhood dive bar did not stand out much. That changed in 2015, when a development company bought all of the land around the bar and set out to build apartments. What happened next was less ordinary. The developer reached the one parcel in the middle of its new holdings and found that its owner would not budge.

## Two Offers, Two Refusals

The building's owner and the Thirsty Beaver's landlord, George Salem, has said the land had been in his family for many years and he did not want to sell. He turned down two offers from the developer. "We've made several attempts through the entire development process to acquire that property, very unsuccessfully," the developer said. So the company built around it, bending the apartments into a horseshoe that wraps the little bar. The bar's survival became what Mark Wilson called "a protest," and national writers took notice. The Washington Post described the small dive bar as a defiant gesture aimed at the development surrounding it, and the AP reached for the Pixar comparison.

## Beaver in a Cowboy Hat

The decor makes the attitude plain. On an orange side wall, a large mural shows a bloodshot-eyed beaver in a cowboy hat and boots. Inside, pictures of Waylon Jennings and Willie Nelson share wall space with a poster for Bartles & Jaymes and lunchboxes from Hee Haw and Gunsmoke. The place has been described as quirky, which may be an understatement. In 2021 it drew national attention when Mick Jagger of the Rolling Stones posted a picture of himself having a drink there. It is not a museum or an official landmark. It is a working bar with a beaver on the wall, kept in place by a landlord who did not want to sell, and that, as Mark Wilson's word "protest" suggests, is the point.
