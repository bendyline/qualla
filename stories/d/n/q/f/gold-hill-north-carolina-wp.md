# Gold Hill, North Carolina

A quiet Rowan County crossroads that was once a raucous gold-mining camp, now home to a few hundred people and a village of restored and relocated old buildings.

**Location:** 35.5208, -80.3383

**Categories:** Census-designated places in Rowan County, North Carolina, Census-designated places in North Carolina, Unincorporated communities in Rowan County, North Carolina, Unincorporated communities in North Carolina

## Introduction

In the 1840s, Gold Hill was "a raucous mining camp town with a considerable number of saloons and brothels." Today it is a quiet crossroads, an unincorporated community in southeastern Rowan County, near the Cabarrus County line, whose population the 2020 census counted at 372. Between those two versions of Gold Hill lies the story of the metal that gave the town its name, and of the people who later decided its old buildings were worth saving.

## Gold Country

The region had already learned what lay under its soil. Next door in Cabarrus County, the first documented gold find in the United States was made in 1799, when young Conrad Reed found gold in Little Meadow Creek, and it set off a gold rush in the area in the early 1800s. Gold turned up at Gold Hill in 1824. What followed was a genuine boom. Before the Civil War, about $6 million in gold was mined here, part of the regional output that led the federal government to open a mint in Charlotte in 1837. For a time the settlement had all the rough edges of a mining camp, saloons and brothels included.

## A Village Reassembled

Some of the boom-era town still stands. The E.H. Montgomery General Store, Mauney's Store, the old jail and a rock wall all date to the 1840s. Around them, Gold Hill has gathered other survivors from the surrounding countryside in what has been described as a "re-creation" of a historic village. A gas station built in 1928 was moved here from U.S. Highway 52 in 1998. The File Store, built in 1906 in Craven, near Salisbury, arrived in 2001. A miner's cabin was moved to Gold Hill in 2011 and became a bakery. The result is a village where buildings from different decades and different places stand side by side, many of them rescued from somewhere else.

## Small Town, Long Memory

Gold Hill has stayed small. It was first listed as a census-designated place only in 2020. It sits near the Yadkin River and is served by U.S. Highway 52 and Old Beatty Ford Road, and Gold Hill Township, one of the townships Rowan County created in 1868, shares its name. The community also produced at least one figure of regional consequence: Napoleon B. McCanless, born in Gold Hill in 1851, became a prominent regional entrepreneur, president of the Halifax Cotton Mill Co., with interests that included banking and mining. The gold is long gone as an industry, but the town's name still announces what first put it on the map.

## Flight Context

Gold Hill is at 35.5208°N, 80.3383°W in southeastern Rowan County, North Carolina, along U.S. Highway 52. The community is small, so use U.S. 52 as a reference; High Rock Lake, a reservoir on the nearby Yadkin River, lies about 8 nm to the north-northeast. A viewing altitude of about 2,000-2,500 feet MSL gives a good look at the village. Mid-Carolina Regional Airport in Salisbury (KRUQ) is about 12 nm northwest, Stanly County Airport near Albemarle (KVUJ, FAA identifier VUJ) about 11 nm southeast, and Concord-Padgett Regional Airport (KJQF) about 20 nm west-southwest. Several small private airstrips are scattered nearby, including one named for Gold Hill itself, so watch for light-aircraft traffic. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
