# Valdese, North Carolina

Founded in 1893 by Waldensian immigrants from the Cottian Alps of Italy, the Burke County town of Valdese has a green, white and red town flag - and has since become home to a Hmong-American community as well.

**Location:** 35.7433, -81.5586

**Categories:** Towns in Burke County, North Carolina, Waldensianism, Italian-American culture in North Carolina, Populated places established in 1893, North Carolina populated places on the Catawba River

## Introduction

Twenty-nine people came first. In 1893 a small party of Waldensians from the Cottian Alps of northern Italy arrived in Burke County, North Carolina, to prepare the way for more than two hundred others who followed before the year was out. They settled in the rolling country between Morganton and Hickory, and the town they built was named Valdese. More than 130 years later, the town of 4,689 people has a green, white and red town flag, still has the church its founders built, and still tells their story each summer on an outdoor stage.

## An Old Faith From the Mountains

The Waldensians were no ordinary group of emigrants. Their tradition began before the Reformation as an ascetic movement within Western Christianity, originally known as the Poor of Lyon. Its founder, Peter Waldo, was a wealthy merchant who gave away his property around 1173 to preach apostolic poverty. The movement spread into the Cottian Alps, in what is now France and Italy, where its followers were declared heretical by the Catholic Church. Over time they came to align themselves with Protestantism, and their congregations endured in the Piedmont region of northern Italy. It was from those mountain valleys that Valdese's founders came. The settlement they started in 1893 was incorporated as a town in 1920.

## A Church Like the Ones They Left

In 1897 the settlers began building a Romanesque-style church meant to resemble the churches they had left behind in Italy and France. It was completed in 1899. The congregation, now known as the Waldensian Presbyterian Church, is a congregation of the Presbyterian Church (USA) that keeps its ties to the Waldensian Church in Italy, and its building on East Main Street was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1984. Two other Valdese landmarks share that listing: the Jean-Pierre Auguste Dalmas House and Valdese Elementary School. The community's craftsmanship may have traveled, too. By one account, the stonework of Gimghoul Castle in Chapel Hill, finished in 1926, was the handiwork of Waldensian stonemasons from Valdese. The founders' story itself has become theater. The outdoor drama From This Day Forward has retold it in Burke County each summer since 1968, and its 2025 season was the 57th.

## Between the Lake and the Interstate

Valdese lies in eastern Burke County, with the town of Rutherford College as its neighbor to the east. Its limits stretch north to Rhodhiss Lake on the Catawba River, then follow the land along the lake's southern shore for about four miles to the west. U.S. Route 70 runs through town as Main Street, leading seven miles west to Morganton, the county seat, and fourteen miles east to Hickory. Interstate 40 traces the southern border, with exits 111 and 112 serving the town. It is a compact place, about 20 square kilometers, almost all of it land. From 1938 through 1950, it even had its own newspaper, The Valdese News, which served readers across Burke County.

## Bakeries, a Market and a Sister City

The Italian roots are easy to find. Italian restaurants and bakeries are abundant in the Valdese area, and the town's large Italian-American community now spans immigrants and their second- and third-generation descendants. Valdese keeps a formal link to the old country as well: its sister city, designated through Sister Cities International, is Torre Pellice, Italy. But the town's story did not stop with one wave of newcomers. Valdese is also home to a Hmong-American community, part of a Southeast Asian ethnic group with origins in Laos, Thailand, China and Vietnam. The Xieng Khouang Market Place, a Southeast Asian grocery on U.S. 70 West where local Hmong farmers sell their vegetables, operates in the Valdese area, and an ethnic Lao community lives in the surrounding region. A town built by immigrants has become home to new ones.

## Flight Context

Valdese sits at approximately 35.7433 N, 81.5586 W in the Catawba River valley of eastern Burke County, North Carolina, with the Blue Ridge escarpment rising to the west. From 3,000-4,000 ft MSL, look for Rhodhiss Lake along the town's northern edge, Interstate 40 along its southern edge and U.S. 70 running through the middle. Nearby airports: Foothills Regional Airport, Morganton (KMRN; about 5 nm north-northwest) and Hickory Regional Airport (KHKY; about 8 nm east). Charlotte Douglas International Airport (KCLT) is about 44 nm to the southeast. Morning fog can settle over the lake and river valley, and summer afternoons bring scattered thunderstorms.
