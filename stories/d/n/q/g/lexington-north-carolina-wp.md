# Lexington, North Carolina

A Piedmont county seat named for a Revolutionary battlefield has reinvented itself after textiles and furniture, but its real signature is slow-smoked pork, vinegar dip and red slaw.

**Location:** 35.8167, -80.2586

**Categories:** Cities in North Carolina, County seats in North Carolina, Cities in Davidson County, North Carolina, Barbecue, Populated places established in 1775

## Introduction

Here, the sauce has a local name: dip. It is thin and vinegar-based, cut with ketchup, water, salt, pepper and spices, and every restaurant guards its own recipe as a secret. Brushed over pork shoulder cooked slowly above a hardwood fire, usually hickory, it is the foundation of a claim Lexington makes without blushing. The city calls itself the "Barbecue Capital of the World." For a county seat of about 20,000 people in central North Carolina, 20 miles south of Winston-Salem, that is a big title, and Lexington has spent decades earning it.

## Red Slaw and a Secret Recipe

What sets a Lexington barbecue sandwich apart is the red slaw, sometimes called barbecue slaw. It is cabbage dressed with vinegar, ketchup and crushed black pepper, and it contains no mayonnaise, which is what separates it from ordinary coleslaw. Many locals, and plenty of visitors, consider it essential to a proper barbecue experience, and it turns up beside grilled poultry and as a relish on hot dogs. The taste of the dip varies widely from place to place, from tangy to slightly sweet or spicy. As of 2003, the city had more than twenty barbecue restaurants, more than one per thousand residents. In 2012, U.S. News & World Report ranked Lexington fourth on its list of the best cities for barbecue. Since 1984, the Lexington Barbecue Festival has filled the streets each October (it went virtual in 2020), growing into one of the largest street festivals in North Carolina.

## A Name from the First Skirmish

European settlers were living here, at least sparsely, by 1775. They named their community after Lexington, Massachusetts, site of the first skirmish of the American Revolutionary War. The town was incorporated in 1828, and a decade later, in 1838, silver was discovered at Silver Hill, about ten miles east of town; the mine that opened there the next year is described as the first silver mine in the United States. The oldest surviving house in town is The Homestead, built by Dr. William Rainey Holt, a physician who lived from 1798 to 1868. Its windows and sidelights carry the Palladian details of architect Asher Benjamin's pattern books. The Old Davidson County Courthouse, the Uptown Lexington Historic District and the Erlanger Mill Village Historic District are among more than a dozen local sites on the National Register of Historic Places. Among the area's notable figures is Benjamin Merrill, a Regulator captain from the Jersey Settlement south of town who rallied about 300 men to join the uprising against colonial officials that came before the Revolution. He arrived after the Battle of Alamance on May 16, 1771 had been decided, was captured, and was hanged near Hillsborough on June 19, 1771.

## When the Mills Went Quiet

For most of the twentieth century, Lexington worked in textiles and furniture. Then, from the late 1990s, most local manufacturers moved production to Asia and Mexico to cut costs and stay competitive. Most of the textile and furniture factories closed, a hard blow for a community that had depended on those two industries for its jobs. The city's industrial base has since diversified, with employers that have included PPG Industries, Jeld-Wen and Vitacost, and, more recently, a US Foods distribution center and Nucor Steel Lexington. In 2023, Siemens Mobility announced Lexington as the home of a new railcar manufacturing facility and broke ground that August; the plant, on a campus of about 200 acres, marked its opening in April 2026 and builds passenger coaches, with an East Coast service hub for overhauling coaches and locomotives. Meanwhile, the surrounding countryside is slowly becoming a bedroom community for Winston-Salem, Greensboro and High Point.

## Pigs on Parade

In 2003, pigs took over the streets. Uptown Lexington, Inc., a nonprofit created to revitalize downtown, launched Pigs in the City, a public art project in which sponsors paid artists to decorate life-sized pig sculptures and set them around town. Sponsoring one of the 20 pigs in that first exhibition cost $1,000, which paid for the whole initiative. It drew more than 40,000 visitors from across the state in its first year. The event ran from 2003 to 2005 and again in 2008 and 2009.

## Bass Water at High Rock

A few miles south of town spreads High Rock Lake, the second-largest lake in North Carolina, where the Yadkin and South Yadkin rivers come together. Its water covers 15,180 acres, edged by 365 miles of shoreline. Anglers have long counted it among the state's best fishing lakes. It hosted the Bassmaster Classic in 1994, 1995 and 1998, and it is best known for the quantity and quality of its largemouth bass, likely thanks to its relatively shallow water and good habitat. Catfish, crappie, bluegill, striper and white bass share the lake.

## Flight Context

Lexington lies at approximately 35.82°N, 80.26°W in the Piedmont Triad of central North Carolina. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL, look for the junction of I-85 and I-85 Business at the city's southwest edge and the web of US highways converging downtown; High Rock Lake's broad waters lie to the southwest and make an unmistakable landmark. Davidson County Executive Airport (FAA: EXX, KEXX) is about 3 nm southwest. Smith Reynolds Airport (KINT) in Winston-Salem is about 19 nm north, and Piedmont Triad International (KGSO) about 23 nm northeast. Spring and summer thunderstorms, some severe, are common in the area.
