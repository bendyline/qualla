# U.S. National Whitewater Center

Beside the Catawba River in Charlotte, seven giant pumps drive a man-made whitewater river through Class II to IV rapids, where beginners paddle alongside Olympic contenders.

**Location:** 35.2723, -81.0051

**Categories:** Artificial whitewater courses, Canoeing and kayaking venues in the United States, Sports venues in Charlotte, North Carolina, Sports venues completed in 2006, Outdoor recreation

## Introduction

This river has an electric bill. At the U.S. National Whitewater Center in Charlotte, North Carolina, the rapids exist because seven large submersible pumps, each rated at several hundred horsepower, push water uphill, and by one published estimate every one of them costs about $45 an hour in electricity to run. Switch them off and the whitewater stops. Switch them on and the result is described as the world's largest and most complex recirculating artificial whitewater river, the centerpiece of a not-for-profit outdoor recreation and athletic training center that opened to the public in 2006. Its operator, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, now brands itself simply Whitewater.

## Engineering a River

The river channels were designed by Scott Shipley, a three-time Olympian, and the system behind them is as much plumbing as landscape. Roughly twelve million gallons of water circulate through the course, which is reported to be cleaned every 24 hours by a filtration and ultraviolet system. Pumps made by Flygt fill the Upper Pool, and three of them feed each channel; with six running, both channels flow at once. When only one channel is in use, a low-pressure, air-bag-actuated gate seals off the other at the top so no water enters it. At the bottom, the Lower Pool connects back to the Upper Pool by a moving-belt conveyor that lifts boats, so paddlers can ride the loop again without ever carrying a hull up a hill.

## Two Channels, One Drop

The whitewater section runs about 3,750 feet in total by one count (the center describes each of its two channels as almost half a mile long), split between two channels with very different personalities. The competition channel is built to Olympic slalom standard. The wilderness channel is longer and splits around an island at its top. Both fall the same 6.4 meters, which means the wilderness route, stretching that drop over more distance, runs at a gentler slope. The rapids range from Class II to Class IV, navigable by canoe, by kayak or in a guided raft. Photographs of the course show features with names paddlers will appreciate, including a left turn known as Carnage Corner followed by a big drop. Plastic bollards line a left turn in the competition channel, slalom gates hang over the wilderness channel's right-hand split, and a bridge spans the competition channel at one of its drops.

## Rafters by the Hundred Thousand

The center is not only for elite athletes. In 2010 alone, 100,000 rafters paddled its Class II, III and IV rapids with trained raft guides. Kayakers of every level, from beginner to expert, can run the channels with or without instructors, sometimes alongside Olympic contenders, and periodic slalom races are open to all ages and skill levels. For those who prefer calmer water, flatwater kayaking takes place on the Catawba River itself, which runs beside the property. In 2011 the center added stand-up paddleboarding, in which participants balance on a board similar to a surfboard and steer with a long paddle along the Catawba.

## Rock, Trees and Trails

Away from the water, the center spreads across approximately 1,300 acres with roughly 40 to 50 miles of developed trail for hiking and mountain biking, depending on which published count you trust. The center bills its climbing as one of the largest outdoor climbing facilities in the US, with a 47-foot spire, 35 to 45 top-rope routes and nearly 50 bouldering problems. Ziplines, six of them up to 1,500 feet long and 100 feet high, and ropes courses take guests into the trees. Ice skating rounds out a list of activities that reads more like a mountain resort than a city park. Yet all of it sits within the city of Charlotte, organized around a river that only runs when someone turns on the pumps.

## Flight Context

The U.S. National Whitewater Center lies at approximately 35.2723 N, 81.0051 W on the east bank of the Catawba River on Charlotte's western edge. From 3,000-3,500 ft MSL the concrete whitewater channels, the pools and the wooded trail network stand out against the broad river. The site is only about 4.6 nm northwest of Charlotte Douglas International Airport (KCLT), inside busy Class B airspace, so coordinate with Charlotte Approach and expect traffic on the airport's approach paths. Other nearby airports: Gastonia Municipal Airport (KAKH, FAA identifier AKH; about 8 nm west-southwest) and Concord-Padgett Regional Airport (KJQF; about 16 nm east-northeast). Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common.
