# Mount Airy, North Carolina

Andy Griffith's hometown in the Blue Ridge foothills lives a double life as the inspiration for Mayberry, while its granite, mills and fiddle music tell a deeper story.

**Location:** 36.5017, -80.6058

**Categories:** Cities in North Carolina, Surry County, North Carolina, The Andy Griffith Show, Granite quarries, Old-time music, Blue Ridge Mountains

## Introduction

Mayberry never existed, yet you can still get a haircut at Floyd's. In Mount Airy, a city of about 10,700 people in the foothills of the Blue Ridge, the line between a real town and an imagined one has worn thin. This is where Andy Griffith was born, and it is believed to be the inspiration for the fictional Mayberry of The Andy Griffith Show. The city has embraced that connection with a museum, a festival and replica squad cars. Behind the television nostalgia, though, stands a working town built on granite, textiles and tobacco, with an old-time music tradition all its own.

## A Stagecoach Stop Below the Mountains

The Cheraw people lived in this country before European settlers arrived. In the 1750s, English, Scotch-Irish and German families began settling the region, and as westward expansion picked up after the French and Indian War, the community grew into a stagecoach stop on the road between what is now Winston-Salem and Galax, Virginia. The town's name is believed to have come from a nearby plantation. Mount Airy sits along the Ararat River, about 3 miles south of the Virginia line and, according to the local tourism office, about 21 miles from the Blue Ridge Parkway at Fancy Gap. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries it became a hub for textile and furniture manufacturing, with companies such as Spencer's Incorporated and Renfro Corporation running mills here. Global competition and automation brought hard times in the latter half of the 20th century.

## Stone for the Nation

Granite may be Mount Airy's most enduring export. The North Carolina Granite Corporation works what has been called the world's largest open-face granite quarry, and stone cut here found its way into some of the country's best-known monuments. The Wright Brothers National Memorial and the Arlington Memorial Bridge both use locally quarried granite, so a piece of these foothills stands in two of the country's best-known tributes. When the city adopted its official seal in 1977, granite took its place among the four industries pictured there, alongside furniture manufacturing, textiles and tobacco, the industries the seal was designed to honor as vital to the local economy.

## Welcome to Mayberry

Every year during the last weekend of September, the town becomes Mayberry for Mayberry Days. The celebration has been estimated at 30,000 people in 2009 and 90,000 in 2010, the show's 50th anniversary, and the University of North Carolina at Greensboro has estimated that it brings the town $5 million a year; more recent crowds have been put at up to about 50,000. Surviving cast members and relatives of others often return. Visitors have ridden in Ford Galaxie police cars painted to look like the ones on the show. The Andy Griffith Museum, founded in 2009 by Emmett Forrest, has been reported to draw around 200 visitors a day. Griffith was not the only well-known name tied to the town. Frank Beamer, the retired Virginia Tech head football coach, was born here. Chang and Eng Bunker, the conjoined twins show business billed as the "Siamese Twins," chose Mount Airy as their home when they left the stage behind.

## The Round Peak Sound

Just west of town lies the Round Peak community, the birthplace of the Round Peak style of old-time music. Mount Airy counts old-time musicians among its notable people, including fiddler, banjo player and singer Tommy Jarrell (1901–1985) and fiddler Benton Flippen (1920–2011). Country singer and songwriter Donna Fargo, best known for "The Happiest Girl in the Whole USA," is on that list too. Farther up the Blue Ridge Parkway, at Milepost 213 near Galax, Virginia, the Blue Ridge Music Center offers an amphitheater and a museum devoted to old-time music. Each October, on the second weekend, the Autumn Leaves Festival fills downtown with food, vendors and music on an open stage, drawing a crowd that has been estimated at more than 200,000 people. In 1994, Mount Airy was named an All-America City, in recognition of its community life and civic achievements. Even the public library has earned an American Institute of Architects Design Award.

## Flight Context

Mount Airy lies at approximately 36.50°N, 80.61°W along the Ararat River, about 3 miles south of the Virginia line where the Piedmont meets the Blue Ridge foothills. From 3,000 to 4,500 feet AGL, follow the Ararat River to town; on clear days the Blue Ridge rises to the north and west, and the open-face granite quarry is worth looking for nearby. Mount Airy/Surry County Airport (FAA: MWK, KMWK) is about 4 nm southeast. Smith Reynolds Airport (KINT) in Winston-Salem is about 29 nm southeast, and Piedmont Triad International (KGSO) about 40 nm southeast. Mind rising terrain to the north and west, and watch for mountain wave and fast-changing ridge weather in winter.
