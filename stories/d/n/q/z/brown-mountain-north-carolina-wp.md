# Brown Mountain, North Carolina

A rural Stokes County community with one of the county's few privately owned peaks, a stone church, and a school built by the Reynolds tobacco family on the spot where their mother was born.

**Location:** 36.4540, -80.3392

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Stokes County, North Carolina, Unincorporated communities in North Carolina

## Introduction

Many of the best-known heights in Stokes County sit inside the North Carolina state park system, but a few are privately owned, and one of those, Sauratown Mountain, has no public access because it serves as a major radio communications site. Another is Brown Mountain, a quiet unincorporated community north of the Sauratown range whose summit rises to roughly 1,550 to 1,580 feet. Vineyard Road leads to the top, and the view from there is described as unobstructed. From here, the neighboring heights are laid out on every side.

## The View from Vineyard Road

Turn slowly on the summit and the compass fills itself in. To the north, Virginia's Blue Ridge Mountains line the horizon. Swing south and the Sauratown Mountain range fills the foreground, with Hanging Rock State Park rising to the southeast. Off to the southwest, across the county line in Surry County, stands Pilot Mountain. Down below, the community keeps its own landmarks. Brown Mountain Baptist Church is built entirely of rock gathered in the area, which gives the building a literal kinship with the hills around it. The other landmark is a school, and its story leads straight to a famous name in American tobacco.

## Built Where Their Mother Was Born

Nancy Jane Cox Reynolds is said to have been born in the Brown Mountain community. Her sons included R.J. Reynolds, of the R.J. Reynolds Tobacco Company, and Will Reynolds. In 1923 the newly formed Reynolds Foundation fully financed the construction of a school on the site of her birthplace, and named it Nancy Reynolds Memorial School. The money came primarily from R.J. Reynolds' estate. It was not a one-time gift. In 1930 the foundation paid again, this time for two wings added to the original building. Today the school is a public elementary school serving kindergarten through fifth grade, standing on the spot said to be where the family's matriarch came into the world.

## A Family That Kept Paying

During the Depression, the school still lacked something it needed: accreditation. Will Reynolds covered the cost of an additional month of school so that Nancy Reynolds could earn it. The family's support continued into the 1950s, when the foundation financed and built the school's freestanding gymnasium and built and equipped a large agricultural building. The most lasting gift came after the death of Kate Bitting Reynolds, Will's wife. An endowment set up in her memory was designed to provide for the school, with its yearly dividends reserved strictly for the upkeep of the grounds, exterior beautification projects and playground equipment. As of 2008 the endowment was worth more than three-quarters of a million dollars, easily enough to cover its purpose. For a small elementary school on a country road, it is a remarkable inheritance, and a steady reminder of whose birthplace this was.

## Flight Context

Brown Mountain sits at 36.454°N, 80.339°W in northern Stokes County, North Carolina, with its summit at roughly 1,550 to 1,580 feet. From 3,500 to 5,000 feet MSL you can frame the whole neighborhood the summit looks out on: Pilot Mountain about 9 nm to the southwest, the Sauratown range and Hanging Rock State Park to the south and southeast, and the Blue Ridge across the Virginia line to the north. Keep clear of the communications towers on Sauratown Mountain to the south. Nearby airports: Mount Airy/Surry County Airport (KMWK) about 10 nm west, Smith Reynolds Airport in Winston-Salem (KINT) about 20 nm SSE, and Piedmont Triad International (KGSO) about 29 nm SE. Haze in the warmer months can flatten the distant ridgelines, so a crisp, clear day shows the panorama best.
