# South Carolina Petroglyph Site

At Hagood Mill in Pickens County, a barn-like museum shelters a low boulder carved with at least 32 figures, most believed prehistoric, including a boxy stick figure its discoverers nicknamed "refrigerator man."

**Location:** 34.9269, -82.7217

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Pickens County, South Carolina, Pickens, South Carolina, Museums in Pickens County, South Carolina, Petroglyphs in the United States, Rock art in North America

## Introduction

The boulder kept its secrets best in daylight. When Michael Bramlett, a volunteer with the South Carolina Petroglyph Survey, found carvings on a large, low-lying rock at Hagood Mill in January 2003, it was a rainy day. Later, archaeologist Tommy Charles and his team came back after dark and swept the stone with side-scanning lights, and figures that were invisible in the sun leapt out of the shadows. Today at least 32 carvings, including 17 or 18 human figures (counts vary), are protected right where they were made, inside the South Carolina Petroglyph Site, a small county-owned museum in Pickens County.

## Beneath the Old Road

The rock had more to show. A large part of the boulder lay buried under a dirt road built in the 1820s, so Charles, who headed the survey from the University of South Carolina, got permission from Pickens County to excavate the covered section. Underneath, he found more human representations. All were "sticklike figures" except one, which had a head and legs but no arms, and a torso shaped like a rectangle. The team dubbed him "refrigerator man." Charles believed the carvings were prehistoric, because they had been made by pecking, presumably with stone tools, rather than by scratching or incising with metal. Yet their exact age and purpose remain undetermined. Beyond motifs "universal to human experience, such as representations of animals or humans," nobody can say for certain what the carvers meant.

## Twelve Years to Open the Doors

Plans to put the rock art on display began almost immediately, with the hope of drawing visitors to the area. Opening day, though, was a long time coming. The project was slowed by political infighting, construction errors, the firing of a county administrator, fundraising that stalled during the Great Recession, and the sudden death of the Hagood Mill site manager. The museum finally opened in 2015, twelve years after Bramlett's discovery. Today it is owned by the county and managed by the non-profit Hagood Mill Foundation, as part of the Hagood Mill Historic Site.

## A Barn Built Around a Boulder

Rather than move the rock, the builders brought the museum to it. The South Carolina Petroglyph Site is "a two-room, barn-like structure" built directly over the petroglyphs, both to display them and to protect them for the long term. One room holds exhibits, including photographs and "portable" petroglyphs. The other is given over to the boulder itself, seen from an accessible viewing platform under lighting chosen to bring out the carvings. As visitors look down at the pecked figures, they can listen to a recorded description by Tommy Charles, the archaeologist whose nighttime lights first revealed how much the stone was hiding.

## Flight Context

The South Carolina Petroglyph Site lies at about 34.927 N, 82.722 W at the Hagood Mill Historic Site in Pickens County, South Carolina. The museum building is small, so fly at 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL and navigate by coordinates. Pickens County Airport (KLQK) is about 7 nm south, Oconee County Regional Airport (KCEU) near Clemson about 17 nm south-southwest, and Greenville Downtown Airport (KGMU) about 19 nm east-southeast. Greenville-Spartanburg International Airport (KGSP) lies about 25 nm east, so watch for airline traffic farther east. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
