# Falls Park on the Reedy

Reclaimed from old textile mill land in 1967, Greenville's Falls Park on the Reedy centers on a waterfall and the Liberty Bridge, a curving suspension footbridge held up by cables on only one side.

**Location:** 34.8448, -82.4019

## Introduction

The Liberty Bridge in Greenville, South Carolina, is a suspension bridge with a twist: its cables rise along only one side. The other side is left open, so that anyone walking its roughly 350-foot curve around the waterfall on the Reedy River gets an unobstructed view of the falls. The bridge is the showpiece of Falls Park on the Reedy, 32 acres beside downtown Greenville in the historic West End. The spot is considered the birthplace of the city, and for much of its history it was a place of mills rather than gardens and footpaths.

## Where Greenville Began

Traces of the old working river survive. Among the park's public gardens stands a wall from an early grist mill on the site; accounts date that mill anywhere from the 1770s to the early 1800s. Later, textile mills occupied the land along the river. The turn toward a park began in 1967, when the Carolina Foothills Garden Club reclaimed 26 acres of former mill land, and Falls Park was founded. Progress was gradual for decades. It accelerated in the late 1990s under Mayor Knox H. White, whose push to renovate the park led to the creation of the Falls Park Endowment, a private charity that supports its continuing development.

## A Bridge That Leans on Air

The Liberty Bridge at Falls Park on the Reedy was the renovation's boldest gesture. Designed by the Boston firm Rosales + Partners, with the German firm Schlaich Bergermann & Partner as structural engineers, it was completed in September 2004. Its single-sided cable support gives the deck a striking, asymmetrical profile as it curves around the waterfall, and in 2005 it was awarded the Arthur G. Hayden Medal for innovative design. For walkers, the effect is simple and memorable: the falls are right there, with nothing standing between them and the view.

## Campus, Mall and County Hall

The land on the park's southern edge has had many lives. Between 1851 and 1958, the men's campus of Furman University occupied the southern part of the park and the bluff above it; the university has since moved outside the nearby town of Travelers Rest. After feasibility studies in 1961, Bell Tower Associates announced plans in 1965 for a shopping center, and the Bell Tower Shopping Mall opened in July 1970. Its main anchor, Woolco, closed in 1982 and the mall went into decline. In 1984 the Furman Company, now Furman Realty, suggested the building be turned into government offices, and after additions and modifications Greenville County moved in in 1987. The county has since moved its offices to a new administrative building, and the old building was slated for demolition and redevelopment.

## Bronze, Gardens and Shakespeare

At the Main Street entrance near the bridge, visitors pass Bryan Hunt's 16-foot bronze sculpture Falls Lake Falls, and the Falls Park Center nearby has housed a restaurant and other amenities. The gardens spread out along the river, and every summer the park becomes a stage for the Upstate Shakespeare Festival. What was once reclaimed mill land now serves as Greenville's front porch on the Reedy, a place where the city's oldest story and a modern landmark share the same view of falling water.

## Flight Context

Falls Park on the Reedy lies at about 34.845 N, 82.402 W on the Reedy River at the western edge of downtown Greenville, South Carolina. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow the river through downtown and look for the falls and the curving Liberty Bridge beside the Main Street area. Greenville Downtown Airport (KGMU) is only about 2.6 nm east, Donaldson Field Airport (KGYH) about 5 nm south-southeast, and Greenville-Spartanburg International Airport (KGSP) about 9.5 nm east-northeast, so this is busy airspace; coordinate with air traffic control before any low sightseeing. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
