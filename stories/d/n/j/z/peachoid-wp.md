# Peachoid

Built in 1981 beside Interstate 85, Gaffney's 135-foot Peachoid water tower is a painted steel peach that doubles as a boast: this corner of South Carolina once out-grew Georgia in peaches.

**Location:** 35.0953, -81.6858

## Introduction

Georgia calls itself the Peach State. Gaffney, South Carolina, built a 135-foot peach to argue the point. The Peachoid rises beside Interstate 85 between exits 90 and 92, a water tower shaped and painted to look like the fruit, complete with stem, leaf and cleft. Locals call it "The Peach." Drivers passing through have named it "Mr. Peach" and "The Moon over Gaffney," and it can be seen for several miles around those exits. For travelers on the long run of I-85 between Charlotte and Atlanta, it is one of the most recognizable landmarks on the road.

## A Tank With a Message

The Peachoid began with a practical problem. The Gaffney Board of Public Works needed elevated water storage and wanted to build it with federal funding. The shape was a statement. Gaffney's economy then depended on peach orchards, and the town wanted everyone to know that South Carolina, and by the local boast even Cherokee County alone, produced more peaches in a typical year than the entire state of Georgia. A plain water tower would have held water. This one would make an argument, too. The finished tank holds one million gallons, and it stands off Peachoid Road near the Cherokee Foothills Scenic Highway.

## Building a Better Peach

The Chicago Bridge and Iron Company built the tower of steel and concrete in 1981, with the foundation and sub-contract work handled by Ford Construction Company of Selma, Alabama. Turning a tank into fruit took extra craft. An overlay stem and leaf were laid across the structure, and steel paneling formed an enormous cleft. Then Peter Freudenberg, an artist who worked in macro-art, painted the whole thing to look realistically like a peach. The result was so eye-catching that it introduced an element of tourism to the local economy, and the same company later built a smaller peachoid, holding 500,000 gallons, for Clanton, Alabama.

## Fame, Fiction and a Fence

Being a landmark has had its drawbacks. After years of continued vandalism, the Gaffney Board of Public Works installed a 6-foot chain-link security fence around the Peachoid in February 2018. By then the tower had also found a second life in fiction. It became a plot point in Chapter 3 of the television series House of Cards, in which Gaffney native Frank Underwood keeps a photo of it in his office and faces a political fight over the tower, including concern that its shape looked rather too much like part of the human anatomy. It also appears in Gregg Hurwitz's thriller Hellbent, part of the Orphan X series, where the hero spots it in a video feed and heads to Gaffney, unaware that Clanton has a smaller twin.

## Flight Context

The Peachoid stands at about 35.0953 N, 81.6858 W beside Interstate 85 on the edge of Gaffney, South Carolina, between exits 90 and 92. At 135 feet tall and painted to look like a peach, it is easy to spot from 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL; follow I-85 and look for the giant fruit beside the highway.
