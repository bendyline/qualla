# The Elephant Sanctuary in Tennessee

Near Hohenwald, Tennessee, more than 3,000 acres of woods and pasture give elephants retired from zoos and circuses something they never had in public life: the right not to be watched.

**Location:** 35.5941, -87.5851

**Categories:** Wildlife sanctuaries of the United States, Elephant conservation, Animal charities based in the United States, Buildings and structures in Lewis County, Tennessee

## Introduction

Ten elephants now live in the hills of Lewis County, Tennessee, and a visitor who comes hoping to see one in person will leave disappointed. That is the point. The Elephant Sanctuary in Tennessee, founded in 1995 near the small town of Hohenwald, has cared for 36 elephants retired from zoos and circuses over three decades, animals that once lived their lives in public view. Here the habitats are closed to the public, and the elephants get natural surroundings, individualized care for the rest of their lives and a safe haven dedicated to their well-being.

## A Barn Built for Tarra

It began with a single elephant. In late 1994 Carol Buckley bought about 112 acres in Lewis County and built a barn so her elephant, Tarra, could retire there; Tarra arrived on March 3, 1995. What started as one woman's plan for one elephant quickly became something larger: The Elephant Sanctuary was established shortly afterward and gained nonprofit status, and in 1999 the organization bought the property from Buckley. By then four rescued elephants were living alongside Tarra, and a second barn went up that year, with Buckley guaranteeing the loan. The sanctuary is licensed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture and the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency, and it is a member of both the Association of Zoos and Aquariums and the Global Federation of Animal Sanctuaries. Tarra herself left in November 2021, after a ten-year court case awarded legal custody of her to Buckley.

## Room Enough to Wander

The land kept growing. In 2001 the sanctuary added a 700-acre section that included a 25-acre lake, and in 2003 it bought another 1,840 acres, bringing the total to 2,700 acres, again with Buckley as the loan's guarantor. The first two African elephants arrived in 2004 for a new African habitat. A year later the resident Asian herd moved to a new, far larger habitat to make room for eight incoming elephants who needed to be quarantined. That Asian area was described as including a 22,000-square-foot barn and a 20,000-square-foot hay barn that can hold up to 35,000 bales. Today the sanctuary reports 3,060 acres in all, with separate protected habitats for Asian and African elephants and a quarantine habitat, with its own barns and staff, for elephants with known tuberculosis exposure.

## Seen Only on Screen

Keeping people away is part of the sanctuary's philosophy, but it has found other ways to share the elephants. Its Elephant Discovery Center, on East Main Street in Hohenwald, welcomes drop-in visitors who cannot see the animals in person but can talk with staff and watch them on live-streaming EleCams. The center also hosts programs on request for school classes, civic groups and the general public, and a web-based distance learning program links sanctuary staff with classrooms and other audiences online. For animals whose lives were once shaped by crowds, the quiet of these Tennessee woods may be the most important thing the sanctuary offers.

## Flight Context

The sanctuary is centered near 35.59°N, 87.59°W in Lewis County, Tennessee, just outside Hohenwald in the wooded hills southwest of Nashville. From the air, look for a large tract of woods and clearings with big barns and a lake; the habitats are closed to visitors, so please overfly at a considerate altitude, 3,000 feet or higher, to avoid disturbing the elephants. The nearest field is Paul Bridges Field (0M3) at Hohenwald, about 3 nautical miles to the south, and Maury County Regional Airport (KMRC) lies about 20 nautical miles to the east near Columbia and Mount Pleasant. Morning fog can settle in the valleys, and summer afternoons bring haze and scattered thunderstorms.
