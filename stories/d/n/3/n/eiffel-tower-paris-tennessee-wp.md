# Eiffel Tower (Paris, Tennessee)

Paris, Tennessee's roughly 60-foot Eiffel Tower began as a Douglas fir model built by Memphis engineering students for a 1990 festival salute to France, and now stands in steel in the town's Eiffel Tower Park.

**Location:** 36.2866, -88.3013

**Categories:** Towers in Tennessee, Replicas and derivatives of the Eiffel Tower, Towers completed in 1992, Buildings and structures in Henry County, Tennessee, Christian Brothers University, Tourist attractions in Henry County, Tennessee, Roadside attractions in Tennessee, Paris, Tennessee, 1992 establishments in Tennessee

## Introduction

Of course Paris has an Eiffel Tower. This one stands about 60 feet tall, by the city's account, in a city park in Paris, Tennessee, a replica billed as 1:20 scale of Gustave Eiffel's landmark in France. It did not start here, and it did not start in steel. The tower was born in Memphis as a college engineering project built from Douglas fir, traveled to Paris in pieces and found a permanent home in the town that shares its namesake's name.

## A Salute from Memphis in May

Each year the Memphis in May festival honors a different country, and in 1990 the country was France. Engineering students at Christian Brothers University marked the occasion by building an Eiffel Tower of their own. It went up in CBU's Buckman Quadrangle, and it became part of campus life. Brother Patrick O'Brien, a public relations official for the university at the time, later recalled that the tower "was the centerpiece of one of our most popular quads."

## Drawn from Eiffel's Plans

Three members of the CBU engineering faculty and staff designed the tower to scale: Tom Morrison, professor emeritus of civil engineering; Jim Jacobs, assistant professor of mechanical engineering; and Roland Raffanti, an engineering lab technician. According to Brother O'Brien, Morrison based the model on Gustave Eiffel's original drawings. The result was a tower about 60 feet high, billed as one-twentieth the size of the original. Building it took more than 10,000 hours of labor, all donated by CBU students, faculty, alumni and friends. Into it went 500 pieces of Douglas fir and 6,000 steel rods, a lattice of wood and metal tracing the famous silhouette.

## Moving to Paris

Eventually CBU dismantled the tower, and in February 1992 its parts were delivered to Paris, Tennessee. There they received two coats of paint, and the city's Public Works Department reassembled the tower in Memorial Park. On January 29, 1993, the city dedicated its new landmark with a joyful ceremony and placed a plaque at the base acknowledging the university's generous gift. A tower that had been built to celebrate France for a single festival season now belonged to a small Tennessee city with a French name.

## From Fir to Steel

Wood does not last forever outdoors, and in time the structure began to deteriorate. Keith Jackson, owner of Precision Grinding and Machine of Paris, took on the job of recreating the tower in steel. His shop built the new version in three sections and trailered each one to the park, where the tower was finally erected, and that steel tower still stands today. The park around it has since been renamed Eiffel Tower Park. Along with the tower, it offers playgrounds, an 18-hole disc golf course, a three-quarter-mile walking track, a splash pad, a swimming pool, sports fields, picnic areas, a trout pond and pavilions for rent.

## Flight Context

The tower stands at 36.2866°N, 88.3013°W in Eiffel Tower Park in Paris, Henry County, Tennessee. At roughly 60 feet (some sources say 70) it is small from the air, so plan to spot the park itself from 1,500–2,000 feet AGL and then look for the lattice spire among the sports fields and walking track. Henry County Airport (KPHT) is about 5 nm northwest of the park, and Paris Landing Airpark (FAA 50TN, private use, permission required) is about 6 nm southeast. The nearest larger fields are Jackson Regional (KMKL, formerly McKellar-Sipes Regional) at Jackson, about 51 nm southwest, and Barkley Regional (KPAH) at Paducah, Kentucky, about 52 nm north-northwest. Campbell Army Airfield (KHOP) at Fort Campbell is about 46 nm east-northeast. Summer haze and spring storms are typical.
