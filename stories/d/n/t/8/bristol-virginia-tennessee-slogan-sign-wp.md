# Bristol Virginia–Tennessee slogan sign

A 1910 electric sign that spans the state line on State Street, telling two cities in two states the same thing every night: Bristol is a good place to live.

**Location:** 36.5949, -82.1797

**Categories:** Buildings and structures completed in 1910, National Register of Historic Places in Bristol, Virginia, Bristol, Tennessee, Individual signs on the National Register of Historic Places, 1910 establishments in Tennessee, 1910 establishments in Virginia, Borders of Tennessee, Borders of Virginia, Boundary markers, National Register of Historic Places in Sullivan County, Tennessee

## Introduction

The first message was a command. When the big electric sign first lit up over Bristol in 1910, it told everyone who saw it to "PUSH!", then added, by way of explanation, "THAT'S BRISTOL." It was a boast about ambition, a bet on growth and future prosperity for a pair of young cities. Eleven years later a contest was held, and the winner was something gentler. Since 1921 the sign has carried the same message, spelled out in more than a thousand light bulbs, to two cities in two states at once: Bristol is "A GOOD PLACE TO LIVE."

## One Street, Two States

Bristol is really two cities, Bristol, Virginia, and Bristol, Tennessee, and the line between them runs down the main street. In 1901 the United States Congress officially designated the center of Main Street as the state line separating Tennessee and Virginia. The street has since been renamed, fittingly, State Street. Stand on one side and you are in Virginia; cross over and you are in Tennessee. The slogan sign hangs directly over that line, spanning the roadway, so it belongs to both states at once. The bureaucracy had to pick one, though. The National Register of Historic Places lists its location as Tennessee, even though the sign technically sits in both.

## From Push to a Good Place to Live

The sign was a gift from the power company. According to the Bristol Historical Association, the Bristol Gas and Electric Company donated and erected it in 1910 to advertise the city; Virginia's Department of Historic Resources credits the donation to Henry L. Dougherty, head of Dougherty and Company of New York, which owned Bristol's electric company. It made fitting advertising for an electric company, too. The sign first stood atop the Virginia-Tennessee Hardware Company building, and in 1915 it moved to its current spot over the street. It is no small ornament. Built of structural steel, it measures 60 by 35 feet and weighs two and a half tons. The 1921 slogan contest produced the words it still carries, and they have never been changed.

## Civic Pride in Lights

Virginia's historic preservation office calls the sign the most identifiable landmark of the twin cities, and it has been cared for accordingly. It was restored in 1982, according to that office, and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1988. Its fame has spread beyond the region, too: the sign reportedly appears at the beginning of a 2016 GEICO insurance commercial. Music history is close at hand as well. The site where the 1927 Bristol Sessions were recorded, often called the birthplace of country music, lies near the sign, on a spot that is now a parking lot. After dark, when the bulbs come on, the sign does the job it has done for more than a century, reminding residents on both sides of the line that they share a town, a street and a slogan.

## Flight Context

The slogan sign spans State Street in downtown Bristol at 36.595°N, 82.180°W, directly on the Tennessee–Virginia state line. The sign itself is small from the air, so use State Street as the reference line through downtown. A cruise of 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL keeps you clear of the surrounding ridges and gives a good view of the twin cities. Virginia Highlands Airport in Abingdon (KVJI) is about 9 nm northeast, and Tri-Cities Airport (KTRI) about 13 nm west-southwest, so watch for airline traffic. Mountain winds can be gusty over the ridges, and fog often settles in the valleys on cool mornings.
