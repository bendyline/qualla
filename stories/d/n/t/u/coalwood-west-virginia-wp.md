# Coalwood, West Virginia

A McDowell County company town founded on coal around 1905, made famous by the teenage rocket builders of Homer Hickam's memoir Rocket Boys and the film October Sky.

**Location:** 37.3872, -81.6525

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in McDowell County, West Virginia, Unincorporated communities in West Virginia, Mining communities in West Virginia, Company towns in West Virginia, Coal towns in West Virginia

## Introduction

In 1960, a group of West Virginia teenagers who called themselves the Big Creek Missile Agency went to the National Science Fair and came home with a gold and a silver medal in propulsion. One of them, Homer Hickam Jr., had grown up in Coalwood, a town built to pull coal out of the ground. He and his friends spent their high school years trying to send things the other way. Hickam later wrote it all down, and his memoir Rocket Boys made his McDowell County hometown the setting of a best-seller and a Hollywood film.

## The Town Carter Built

Coalwood began with one man and a mule. George Lafayette Carter arrived on muleback around 1905, found rich seams of coal and bought 20,000 acres. He opened a mine under the Carter Coal Company name and built the rest of the town around it: offices, houses, a schoolhouse, the Carter Coal Company Store, a church. He even hired a dentist and a doctor to care for his miners. In 1922 Carter sold the mine and the town's properties to the Consolidation Coal Company, which rebuilt the community and recruited new workers. When Consolidation defaulted on its payments in 1933, Carter took the company back. He died in 1936, and his son James ran the company until 1947, when he sold it to a group of industrialists who renamed it the Olga Coal Company. Coalwood was a company town in the fullest sense, owned and shaped from the top down.

## Coal Below, Rockets Above

At its height Coalwood had more than 2,000 residents. In 1956 the Coalwood mine was connected underground to the nearby Caretta mine, also owned by Olga, and in 1959 the company stopped bringing coal to the surface at Coalwood, prompting the Norfolk and Western Railway to begin pulling up the tracks to town. The combined Coalwood-Caretta mine kept producing, averaging about a million tons a year. Homer Hickam Jr., the second son of Homer Sr. and Elsie Hickam, was born and raised here and graduated from Big Creek High School in 1960. With five friends he built amateur rockets through generation after generation of designs, until the Big Creek Missile Agency qualified for the National Science Fair and won its medals. Hickam went on to become a NASA engineer who worked on spacecraft design and astronaut training, and he is credited with training the first Japanese astronauts.

## From Rocket Boys to October Sky

Hickam's story of growing up as a coal miner's son in Coalwood began as a magazine article about the Big Creek Missile Agency. Delacorte Press published the expanded version in 1998 as Rocket Boys, a New York Times best-seller, and in 1999 it became the film October Sky. That same year, West Virginia's governor declared an annual "Rocket Boys Day." Coalwood itself began celebrating each October with an October Sky Festival that drew scientists and astronauts, and Hickam always came. The 2007 festival also marked the 50th anniversary of Sputnik. On February 7, 2012, the Cape Coalwood Restoration Association announced that the 13th festival, held in October 2011, had been the last in Coalwood. Citizens of Raleigh County invited the event to Beckley, where the 2012 festival was held largely on the grounds of the Exhibition Coal Mine, and it remained an annual event there through 2019; the 2020 festival was canceled because of COVID-19. In May 2026 Hickam and his fellow Rocket Boys were inducted into the West Virginia Hall of Fame.

## After the Mine

The mine outlasted the rocket years by decades. In 1980 the LTV Corporation bought the Olga Coal Company, and on October 1, 1986, the Coalwood mine ceased production. By the 1990 census, the last time the town was counted separately, the population had fallen to 900. As of 2019, much of Coalwood sat abandoned, and many of its remaining buildings showed the toll of time and weather. The Carter Coal Company Store, built around 1912, was demolished in 2008 by its owner. The Rocket Boys' own alma mater, Big Creek High School, stood in the nearby town of War; it closed in 2010 and was destroyed by a fire in July 2015, days before its scheduled demolition. The Coalwood Community Church, which the coal company built in 1948, is described in a West Virginia Living visitor guide as still in use, a reminder of the town Carter built, and of the boys who looked up from it.

## Flight Context

Coalwood lies at 37.387°N, 81.653°W in McDowell County, southern West Virginia, deep in steep, forested coal country. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet above the ridgetops, look for the narrow valley settlement with its old company buildings; the terrain rises sharply on all sides, so stay well above the ridges. Kee Field in Pineville (FAA: I16) is about 14 nm north-northeast, Mercer County Airport in Bluefield (KBLF) about 22 nm east-southeast, and Raleigh County Memorial Airport in Beckley (KBKW), near the festival's later home, about 35 nm northeast. Morning fog often fills the hollows, and emergency landing options here are very limited.
