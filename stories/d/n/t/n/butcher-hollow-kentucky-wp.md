# Butcher Hollow, Kentucky

The eastern Kentucky coal hollow where Loretta Lynn was born, a place that is less a town than a road in Van Lear, and that one song made famous.

**Location:** 37.7780, -82.7170

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Johnson County, Kentucky, Unincorporated communities in Kentucky, Coal towns in Kentucky, Loretta Lynn

## Introduction

The maps say Butcher Hollow. Most people say it differently: Butcher Holler. Loretta Lynn was born in this narrow coal-country valley in Johnson County, Kentucky, and she sang about it in "Coal Miner's Daughter," the song that carried the name of this hollow far beyond eastern Kentucky. It is not even a town, strictly speaking. But try telling that to anyone who has heard the song.

## A Holler, Not a Town

Butcher Hollow took its name from a nearby valley, which was named for the local Butcher family. It is not an independent town or village. Butcher Hollow is part of the community of Van Lear, which the Consolidation Coal Company built in the early twentieth century and named for Van Lear Black, one of the company's directors. Most of the hollow lies outside the old Van Lear city limits, but ever since the Van Lear post office opened in 1909, the people who live here have had Van Lear mailing addresses. Although Butcher Hollow is often listed as a separate town, natives of eastern Kentucky think of it as a street or a neighborhood. Its proper address, then, is Butcher Hollow, Van Lear, Johnson County, Kentucky. Van Lear itself is now an unincorporated community.

## The Coal Miner's Daughter

Loretta Webb was born here on April 14, 1932, the oldest daughter and second child of Clara and Ted Webb. Her father was a coal miner and a subsistence farmer. The hollow was also the birthplace of her sister Peggy Sue, who became a singer too, and her brother Jay Lee Webb. The family eventually left. Ted Webb moved with Clara and the younger children to Wabash, Indiana, and four years later he died of a stroke at 52, while also battling black lung disease. By then his daughter had long since married Oliver Lynn. In 1970 her autobiographical song "Coal Miner's Daughter" went to No. 1 on the Billboard country chart and crossed onto the Billboard Hot 100 as well. The song pays tribute to Butcher Hollow and names Van Lear, the larger community around it.

## From the Holler to Hollywood

The story kept growing. Lynn's 1976 autobiography, also called Coal Miner's Daughter, became a best-seller and then a 1980 film starring Sissy Spacek as Lynn and Tommy Lee Jones as her husband. Spacek won the Academy Award for Best Actress for the role. Decades later, Lynn returned to her roots in name once again with Van Lear Rose, a 2004 album produced by the rock musician Jack White that earned five Grammy nominations and won two. She was inducted into the Country Music Hall of Fame in 1988 and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2013. She died on October 4, 2022, at the age of 90, and the hollow where she was born remains bound up in her story.

## Van Lear After the Mines

Coal built Van Lear, but deep mining in the town itself has ended, although some mines still operate nearby. Most residents now work elsewhere, in the nearby cities of Paintsville, Prestonsburg and Pikeville. Since the local mining ended, only a handful of businesses have carried on in the Van Lear area. Wikipedia lists a bookstore, the Mine Number 5 Store, the East Kentucky Museum of Mysteries, and Icky's 1950s Snack Shop, tucked inside the Coal Miners' Museum, so check ahead before visiting any of them. The Number Five Store is now Webb's General Store, a family-owned shop where tours of Loretta Lynn's childhood homeplace typically begin, generally from spring through mid-November, according to Paintsville Tourism. For visitors who come looking for Butcher Holler, the homeplace and the old company town around the hollow tell the rest of the story.

## Flight Context

Butcher Hollow lies at 37.778°N, 82.717°W near Van Lear in Johnson County, eastern Kentucky, a few miles from Paintsville. The terrain is a tangle of steep, forested ridges and narrow hollows, so fly at least 2,000 feet above the ridgetops and follow the valleys visually rather than descending into them. Big Sandy Regional Airport (KSJS) is about 4 nm east-southeast, Pike County Airport-Hatcher Field (KPBX) about 15 nm south-southeast, Julian Carroll Airport in Jackson (KJKL) about 31 nm west-southwest, and Tri-State Airport in Huntington (KHTS) about 36 nm north-northeast. Valley fog is common on cool mornings, and emergency landing options among these ridges are very limited.
