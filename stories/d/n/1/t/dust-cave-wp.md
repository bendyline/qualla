# Dust Cave

Layer by layer, a limestone cave above a Tennessee River backwater in north Alabama preserves some 7,000 years of seasonal visits by Paleoindian and Archaic hunter-gatherers, from Ice Age spear points to burials of people and dogs.

**Location:** 34.7781, -87.7239

**Categories:** Archaeological sites, Paleoindian period, Archaic period, Caves of Alabama, Lauderdale County, Alabama

## Introduction

Archaeologists labeled the layers of Dust Cave with the alphabet, from Zone A at the top to Zone Y at the bottom, and reading them downward is a journey backward in time. Zone A is the youngest. Zone Y, the oldest, dates to the Pleistocene, the epoch of the Ice Ages. In between, packed into the floor of a limestone cave in northern Alabama, lies the record of hunter-gatherers who returned to this shelter season after season for roughly 7,000 years.

## A Cave That Had to Empty First

Dust Cave sits in the limestone bluffs of the Highland Rim, overlooking Coffee Slough, a tributary of the Tennessee River. For a time it was not a shelter at all. Between about 17,000 and 15,000 years before present, river sediment filled and buried the cave. Only after water tables dropped and flushed that sediment out did the space become habitable. People then used it seasonally for some 7,000 years, spanning the end of the Pleistocene and the early Holocene. Five cultural components are recognized in its deposits: Quad, Beaver Lake and Dalton; Early Side-Notched; Kirk Stemmed; Eva and Morrow Mountain; and Benton.

## Digging to Bedrock

Dr. Richard Cobb discovered the cave in 1984, and excavation began in 1989 under Dr. Boyce Driskell of the University of Alabama. That first season, the team opened five small test units, each 30 centimeters square and a meter deep. One of them, Unit A, was enlarged by the end of the season and exposed a complex layering that included 20 burials of humans and dogs. In 1990 and 1991, seven test units, lettered A through G, were taken all the way down to bedrock. From 1992 to 1994 a trench two meters wide and twelve meters long revealed the full stratigraphy of the cave. Fieldwork continued from 1996 into the early 2000s.

## Ice Age Spear Points

The first analysis of the cave's stone tools examined 130 chipped-stone pieces from Unit F, chosen because it reached bedrock and contained all five cultural components. Zones T and U belong to the Paleoindian period. From those two layers came a reworked Cumberland point along with Quad, Beaver Lake, Hardaway Side-Notched and Dalton projectile points, all Paleoindian types dated between 12,650 and 11,200 calibrated years before present. Each was shaped by hand and carried into this cave by people living at the close of the Ice Age.

## People and Their Dogs

The burials in Dust Cave begin in Zone N and extend into Zone P, the stretch between the Kirk Stemmed occupation and the Eva/Morrow Mountain component, a phase of the Middle Archaic. The Eva/Morrow Mountain occupations, dated to roughly 6400 to 4000 cal B.C., mark the most intensive human activity in the cave's long history, following the Kirk Stemmed occupation of about 8200 to 5800 cal B.C. The last component, Benton, ran from about 4500 to 3600 cal B.C., after which the long record of seasonal visits in the cave's layers closes. The site has since fed a shelf of research, including Renee Beauchamp Walker's Foragers of the Terminal Pleistocene in North America (2007) and Kandace D. Hollenbach's Foraging in the Tennessee River Valley, 12,500 to 8,000 Years Ago (2009).

## Flight Context

Dust Cave lies at about 34.778N, 87.724W in Lauderdale County, northwest Alabama, in limestone bluffs above Coffee Slough on the north side of the Tennessee River west of the Florence-Muscle Shoals area. The cave itself is unlikely to be visible from the air; from 2,000-3,000 feet, use the broad Tennessee River and its sloughs as references, with Wilson Dam and the Shoals cities to the east. Northwest Alabama Regional Airport (KMSL) at Muscle Shoals is about 6 nm east-southeast. Haze and river fog are common on summer and autumn mornings.
