# Rosenbaum House

Frank Lloyd Wright's only building in Alabama, a cypress-and-brick Usonian house on the Tennessee River in Florence, leaked, ran over budget and still won its owners' devotion for nearly six decades.

**Location:** 34.7928, -87.6803

**Categories:** Frank Lloyd Wright buildings, Houses in Florence, Alabama, Houses on the National Register of Historic Places in Alabama, Historic house museums in Alabama, Usonian houses

## Introduction

The flat roof leaked almost immediately. The heating system failed. The first phase of construction cost $14,000, nearly double the budget. And yet Mildred Rosenbaum lived in the house Frank Lloyd Wright designed for her family in Florence, Alabama, for nearly six decades, and in her last five years there she personally guided nearly 5,000 visitors through it. Wright scholar John Sergeant called the Rosenbaum House "the purest example of the Usonian," and it remains the only Wright building in Alabama.

## A Lot, a Gift and a Magazine Cover

In 1938, Stanley Rosenbaum, a professor at Florence State Teachers' College (now the University of North Alabama), and his new wife, Mildred, received a building lot in Florence and money to build on it from his parents. Both newlyweds had read Wright's autobiography and a Time magazine cover story about the architect, and they commissioned him to design their home. The house was meant to be one of Wright's Usonian homes, which he intended as low-cost housing for middle-class American families. It did not stay low-cost. The couple's budget of under $8,000 swelled to $14,000 for the first phase, and the family seldom used the custom furniture built on site. Still, the Rosenbaums moved in in September 1940, and photographs of their house were reportedly shown at the Museum of Modern Art in New York City soon afterward; MoMA's big Wright retrospective opened that November.

## Usonia on the Tennessee

The house sits on a 2-acre plot on Riverview Drive, on the north bank of the Tennessee River. It is one of only 26 Usonian houses built before World War II, and it was the first of dozens of Usonian houses based on the prototype Wright had created in 1936, the Jacobs House in Madison, Wisconsin. Laid out in an L-shape and built largely of cypress and brick, it spreads beneath multilevel, low-rising steel-cantilevered roofs that shelter both the living spaces and an adjoining carport. Its most distinctive feature is its glass, which USA Today described as "blurring the distinction between indoors and outdoors." The original plan provided 1,540 square feet of living space. When the Rosenbaums' fourth child arrived, the house had grown cramped, and they asked Wright for more room. His addition, completed in 1948, added 1,084 square feet in a second L-shape.

## The Keeper of the House

The house raised a family, including the future film critic Jonathan Rosenbaum, who grew up there. It also turned Mildred Rosenbaum into a guide. In her last five years in the house, nearly 5,000 people received tours from her in person. The family kept the house until 1999, when it passed to the City of Florence, and Mildred died in 2006. The house joined the National Register of Historic Places in 1978, both individually and as a contributing building in the McFarland Heights historic district. After a restoration in the early 2000s, the City of Florence opened it in 2002 as a public museum, the Frank Lloyd Wright Rosenbaum House, where some of the original Wright-designed furniture is displayed. The restoration earned the 2004 Wright Spirit Award in the Public Domain from the Frank Lloyd Wright Building Conservancy. Out back, the garden is the Rosenbaums' own creation, not Wright's.

## Flight Context

Coordinates: 34.7928 N, 87.6803 W, on the north bank of the Tennessee River in Florence, Alabama. The low, horizontal house is small and tucked among trees in a residential neighborhood, so use the river as your guide; Wilson Dam lies a few miles upstream to the east, and Muscle Shoals and Sheffield sit across the water. The site is only about 4.5 nm northwest of Northwest Alabama Regional Airport (KMSL), so stay coordinated with local traffic and fly at 1,500-2,500 ft AGL. Other airports: Wilson Creek Airport in Florence (FAA identifier 0AL9; no ICAO code; private use, permission required) about 4 nm northeast; Huntsville International (KHSV) about 46 nm east. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the river valley.
