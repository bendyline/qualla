# George H. Carroll Lion Habitat

On the University of North Alabama campus, a $1.3 million habitat built entirely with donated money was home to Leo III and Una, once billed as the only live lion mascots in the United States.

**Location:** 34.8072, -87.6800

**Categories:** University of North Alabama buildings and structures, Tourist attractions in Lauderdale County, Alabama, Zoos in Alabama

## Introduction

In 1973 Dr. Robert Guillot, then president of the University of North Alabama, personally acquired a 12-pound lion cub from a zoo in Knoxville, Tennessee. That cub, now known as Leo I, started the school's live lion mascot tradition, and the tradition eventually needed a building of its own. The George H. Carroll Lion Habitat, dedicated on October 7, 2007, is a climate-controlled, 12,764-square-foot home built for Leo III and Una, who were at the time billed as the only live lion mascots in the United States.

## A Birthday Party for a Lion

Leo I left the university a lasting ritual. His birth date, April 14, has been celebrated ever since as the official lion mascot birthday, an annual event that draws kindergarten and elementary school children from throughout the Shoals region. It is a campus birthday party where the guest of honor is a lion. The Leo name carried on to the mascots that followed the first cub from Knoxville. Leo III and Una were born on November 18, 2002, at a USDA-sanctioned refuge in Greenville, New Hampshire, owned by Glen and Kathy Eldridge. That same year Dr. Dan Howard and his wife, Annemarie Howard, brought the two cubs back to the Shoals from their birthplace. The pair grew up to become what the university billed as the only live lion mascots in the country, and the habitat dedicated in 2007 became their home.

## Built Without a Tax Dollar

The habitat cost $1.3 million, and none of it came from federal or state funds or from student tuition. The facility carries the name of George H. Carroll, the late owner of the construction firm Pressure Concrete, which built it and donated labor, materials and money toward the project. Certified Alarm Company of Alabama supplied an alarm and surveillance system that is monitored at all times. The design exceeds the requirements of both the U.S. Department of Agriculture and the Association of Zoos and Aquariums, a professional accrediting agency. Keeping the lions was never cheap: their food and care ran about $35,000 a year, and charitable contributions covered all of it.

## In Loving Memory

The habitat's residents are gone now. Una died in 2020, and Leo III followed in 2024. The official website devoted to the pair has become a memorial, remembering the lions and the Howards as a significant part of the university's history of keeping live lions on campus. The climate-controlled enclosure tells its own story about how seriously one Alabama school took its mascot, from a single 12-pound cub carried home from Knoxville to a purpose-built habitat paid for entirely by donors. In August 2025 the university announced that live lions could return only if the habitat is roughly doubled in size to meet Association of Zoos and Aquariums standards, at an estimated cost of $5 million to $7 million, and said the old fencing would come down that fall so the site could serve as green space in the meantime.
