# Fancy Farm, Kentucky

A western Kentucky village of about 400 people hosts a Catholic church picnic so large and so political that it opens the state's fall campaign season every August.

**Location:** 36.7989, -88.7914

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Graves County, Kentucky, Census-designated places in Kentucky, Politics of Kentucky, Catholicism in Kentucky, Festivals

## Introduction

Fancy Farm counted 403 residents at the 2020 census, yet every August, Kentucky's politicians come to it. So many candidates make the trip to this corner of Graves County that it counts as news when a major state figure stays away, as U.S. Senator Jim Bunning did in 2007. The draw is a church picnic. The St. Jerome Catholic Church Picnic has been held since 1880 or 1881 (sources differ), and on the first Saturday of each August it marks the traditional start of Kentucky's fall campaign season, with speeches, barbecue and somewhere between 12,000 and 15,000 visitors crowding into a village of a few hundred people.

## A Catholic Outpost in the Purchase

Fancy Farm sits on Kentucky Route 80, ten miles northwest of the county seat of Mayfield, in the rural far-western part of the state called the Jackson Purchase. Roman Catholics began settling here in 1829, and the area is still known for its strong Catholic identity. The community grew up around St. Jerome Church, built in 1836. The first post office opened in 1843, and that is apparently when the fanciful name arrived, chosen by a man who was applying to be postmaster. The federal census was slow to notice. It first recorded Fancy Farm in 1870 as a post office in Boswell Precinct, listed it as a town in Magisterial District 5 in 1910, and finally counted it as a town with its own precinct in 1920.

## From Parish Supper to Campaign Stage

The picnic began in 1880 or 1881 as a purely local affair, and it is still held on the church grounds, which have grown partly thanks to the event's success. What turned it into a political institution was a governor with a nickname. A. B. 'Happy' Chandler, who twice served as governor of Kentucky and is best known as the second Commissioner of Major League Baseball, first appeared in 1931 while running successfully for lieutenant governor. After that, the picnic drew candidates from across the state. Its place on the calendar was fixed in 1956, when Kentucky moved its primary election from August to May; since then the picnic has fallen on the first Saturday in August, just as the general election campaign gets under way.

## Voices on the Picnic Stage

The stage has hosted national figures too. George Wallace spoke in 1975, Lloyd Bentsen in 1988 and Al Gore in 1992. Alben Barkley, the vice president who was born at nearby Wheel and lived in Paducah, spoke many times over the years, a local son returning to a home crowd. Kentucky's own officeholders and candidates attend in such numbers that a notable absence, like Bunning's, becomes a story in its own right. Outside vendors are not allowed on the grounds, although people representing political causes do sell their paraphernalia to the crowd. The result is an afternoon where church fundraiser and campaign rally share the same lawn, and the smell of barbecue drifts over the speeches.

## The "World's Largest Picnic"

Politics is only part of the day. The picnic offers the familiar pleasures of a country fair, including bingo games and raffle prizes. The Knights of Columbus hold a fish fry on Friday evening, and the main event brings barbecue and homemade baked goods. The cooks work on a remarkable scale: the 1985 Guinness Book of World Records is reported to have recognized the event as the 'World's Largest Picnic' after crowds at the 1982 picnic consumed 15,000 pounds of mutton, pork and chicken. All of it serves the parish. The proceeds pay for the Catholic elementary school and for local improvements, so every plate of barbecue sold during campaign season helps pay for a small community's school.

## Flight Context

Fancy Farm lies at approximately 36.7989 N, 88.7914 W, on Kentucky Route 80 in rural Graves County, about ten miles northwest of Mayfield. From 2,000-3,000 ft AGL, look for the small village and the church grounds of St. Jerome amid the rural farmland of the Jackson Purchase; the town of Mayfield to the southeast and the city of Paducah to the north-northeast help with orientation. Nearest airports: Mayfield Graves County Airport (FAA identifier M25; no ICAO code listed), about 10 nm east-southeast, and Barkley Regional Airport, Paducah (KPAH), about 16 nm north. On picnic weekends in early August expect heavy road traffic on the ground and possible afternoon thunderstorms and summer haze aloft.
