# Halfway, Illinois (Little Juarez)

For a decade a crossroads north of Marion, Illinois, known as Halfway or "Little Juarez" poured liquor, ran dice games and saw men shot dead, until raids and fires left nothing but weeds, charred wood and broken bottles.

**Location:** 37.7928, -88.9335

**Categories:** Ghost towns in Illinois, Populated places in Williamson County, Illinois, Prohibition in the United States, Organized crime in Illinois

## Introduction

By June 1925 there was almost nothing left to see. "Nothing now remains but weeds, charred wood and broken bottles," a reporter wrote of a crossroads north of Marion, Illinois. Only a few years earlier, saloons, dance floors, restaurants and sleeping rooms had crowded the spot. This was Halfway, or Half Way, a rough and very wet unincorporated community in Williamson County whose lawlessness, shootings, gambling and liquor earned it the nickname "Little Juarez." Its heyday ran from 1915 to 1925, and its best-known operator was the bootlegger and gangster Charlie Birger.

## Where Herrin Went to Drink

Halfway is believed to have stood at the crossroads of what are now Illinois Route 37 and Prosperity Road, between Marion and Johnston City. Its name came from sitting about halfway between Marion and Herrin, which lies a few miles west along what is now Stotlar Road. One of the earliest references to the place dates to September 1916, when circuit judge D. T. Hartwell issued an injunction forbidding 31 saloons and clubs in Herrin from "selling intoxicating liquors of any kind." The sheriff and his deputy, George Galligan, a future Prohibition-era sheriff, served the papers on the 9th. A few days later a Marion newspaper observed that "Herrin is again dry" and predicted that "now Energy and Half Way will become points of interest to Herrin tourists." It was right.

## Dice, Liquor and a Miner's Widow

When nationwide Prohibition arrived in 1920, Halfway only grew, with joints on just about every corner. On the southeast corner of the crossroads stood a roadhouse run by Ralph "Scrambler" Hill, Charlie Schafer, "Tick" Gallagher and Sue Riddle, where, according to testimony at the coroner's inquest, Hill ran a craps game. On June 3, 1923, Hill, Schafer and a man named Ezra Fowler were caught up in a gunfight. Hill was wounded in the thigh and leg. Fowler was killed. He was a coal miner who lived with his family in nearby Whiteash, and he was 34 years old. He left behind a widow and two children.

## Birger's Bloody November

Later that year the violence centered on Birger. On November 14, 1923, according to local histories, he killed his bartender, Cecil Knighton, on the road between two of the roadhouses. Days later, on November 18, the St. Louis gangster William F. "Whitey" Doering was killed on the porch of Birger's establishment, and Birger himself was wounded. No clear account ever settled who shot Doering, though most agree that Doering shot Birger. Birger claimed the fatal shot came from a large car full of gunmen passing down the highway; rumors the following year blamed his bodyguard and bartender that night, Jack "Hoghead" Davis. Birger's own end came years later and elsewhere. On April 19, 1928, he was hanged at the Franklin County Jail in Benton for the murder of Joe Adams, mayor of West City, the last man executed in a public hanging in Illinois.

## Raids, Fires and Broken Bottles

Halfway's undoing began with the Ku Klux Klan, which started organizing in Williamson County in the spring of 1923 with meetings drawing more than 5,000 people. Klan raids hit the joints in 1923 and early 1924, and two targeted fires eventually destroyed every building except Birger's. Then, on October 7, 1924, his burned too. It had been closed for a year under a government injunction; one room had been used to sell refreshments "contrary to the law," while the other had been fitted out as a dance hall that never opened. "The origin of the fire is unknown," one contemporary account noted, and the frame building and its contents were a total loss. The 1925 reporter who found only weeds and bottles claimed Little Juarez had really been part of Whiteash, though whether it lay inside that village or just beside it remains unclear. It is not to be confused with a second, quieter Halfway in the same county, a post office and country store between Marion and Corinth.

## Flight Context

The site of Halfway lies near 37.7928°N, 88.9335°W, about four miles north of Marion, Illinois, at what is believed to be the crossroads of Illinois Route 37 and Prosperity Road on the way to Johnston City. Nothing of the settlement survives, so navigate by the roads: from 2,000–2,500 feet AGL, follow Route 37 north out of Marion and look for the Prosperity Road crossing, with Herrin a few miles to the west. Veterans Airport of Southern Illinois at Marion (KMWA) is only about 4 nm west-southwest, and Benton Municipal Airport (FAA identifier H96, no ICAO code listed) is about 13 nm north. Summer haze can reduce visibility over the region.
