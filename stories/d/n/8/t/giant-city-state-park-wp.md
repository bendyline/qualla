# Giant City State Park

A 4,000-acre southern Illinois park where a Civilian Conservation Corps crew built a sandstone lodge and its furniture from local stone and timber, on ridges where people raised a stone wall more than a thousand years earlier.

**Location:** 37.6050, -89.1883

**Categories:** State parks of Illinois, Protected areas of Jackson County, Illinois, Protected areas of Union County, Illinois, Protected areas established in 1927, Park buildings and structures on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois, National Register of Historic Places in Union County, Illinois, 1927 establishments in Illinois, National Natural Landmarks in Illinois

## Introduction

The furniture was good enough, by one account, to earn the builders another job. When the 696th Company of the Civilian Conservation Corps built the lodge at Giant City State Park in southern Illinois, it also made the lodge's wooden furniture, and the park's published history says the quality of that work led Pere Marquette State Park to commission the same company to furnish its own lodge. That detail captures something about Giant City. This is a park where the stone, the timber and the labor all came from close at hand, and where, long before any lodge, people were stacking stone along its ridges more than a thousand years ago.

## Sandstone and White Oak

Between 1934 and 1935, the 696th Company raised the park's lodge and its original six cabins. The builders did not import their materials. They used locally quarried sandstone and locally harvested lumber, and the lodge's own description names multi-hued sandstone and white oak timber among the native materials, calling the building in perfect keeping with the spirit of the park. The furniture came from the same hands. The company built the lodge's wooden furnishings too, and the park's published history says they were well enough made that Pere Marquette, another Illinois state park, asked the 696th to furnish its lodge as well, though another account of Pere Marquette's lodge credits its furniture to Illinois inmates, so that claim is uncorroborated. Whatever the truth of that, a work crew's carpentry here became its calling card.

## Dedicated, Expanded, Rebuilt

In 1936, Illinois Governor Henry Horner dedicated the lodge and cabins. The CCC kept working afterward, adding a dining room and kitchen and six more cabins to the complex. Half a century later, in 1985, the lodge and cabins were added to the National Register of Historic Places. That same year the original cabins were demolished and replaced with replica cabins, and several new cabins were built alongside them. Today the cabins house overnight visitors, and the lodge holds a restaurant and gift shop, though the lodge and restaurant close for the winter, from mid-December until early February. Its Bald Knob dining room seats 200 and serves breakfast, lunch and dinner, and the lodge's signature is an all-you-can-eat, family-style chicken dinner, a hearty reward after a day on the trails.

## A Wall Older Than the Park

Within the park lies a far older structure. The Giant City Stone Fort Site, listed on the National Register of Historic Places on August 9, 2002, dates to the Late Woodland period, and was built and used between roughly AD 600 and 900. It covers about 1.4 acres atop a sloped ridge. Its original stone wall did not survive intact: European settlers in the region dismantled it and carried the stone away for their own building projects, leaving only the stone base. Professional archaeologists from Southern Illinois University first investigated the site in 1956, and the first in-depth survey took place in 2000 and 2001. Standing at the base of that wall, you are looking at the work of people who shaped this ridge centuries before the CCC arrived.

## Four Thousand Acres to Wander

Illinois acquired more than 1,100 acres here in 1927 and dedicated them as Giant City State Park. The park has since grown to some 4,000 acres spread across Jackson and Union counties, and it is designated a National Natural Landmark. It is a popular destination in Southern Illinois, drawing visitors for hiking, horseback riding, picnicking and rock climbing. Among the most popular hikes are the Giant City Nature Trail, the Red Cedar Trail and the Trillium Trail, and the long-distance River to River Trail passes through the park as well. A lodge and visitor center welcome guests at the start, but the park's real welcome waits out on the trails, in the same country that once supplied the lodge's stone and timber.
