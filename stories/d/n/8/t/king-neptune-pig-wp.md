# King Neptune (pig)

Spared from a pig roast, a crowned Hereford hog named King Neptune was auctioned again and again across Illinois during World War II, raising $19 million in war bonds before retiring to a farm near Anna.

**Location:** 37.4458, -89.1316

**Categories:** Individual pigs, Military animals of World War II, Pig mascots, Union County, Illinois, United States Navy in the 20th century

## Introduction

On at least one occasion, someone paid $25 for a pig's squeal. At another auction, a single bristle went for $500. The pig was King Neptune, a mostly red Hereford hog from southern Illinois, and during World War II he was sold over and over again, returned to his handlers after every auction so he could be sold once more. By the time his touring days ended, he had helped raise $19 million in war bonds for a battleship. He was buried with military honors in the countryside east of Anna, Illinois, where a memorial beside Interstate 57 has long told his story.

## Spared from the Roast

Neptune was born on May 16, 1942, one of a litter of 12 on the Sherman Boner farm near West Frankfort, Illinois. He was first called Parker Neptune, after his father, Parker Sensation, and Boner's daughter Patty raised him as a 4-H project. He was a Hereford swine, a breed that is mostly red with a white face, white ears and at least two white feet, much like Hereford cattle. On December 5, 1942, Sherman Boner donated him to be served at a fundraising pig roast. That might have been the end of the story, but the local Navy recruiter, Don C. Lingle, originally of Anna and working out of the office in Marion, had another idea: he would auction the pig to raise war bonds.

## A Crown, Earrings and a Navy Blanket

Lingle and auctioneer L. Oard Sitter took Neptune on the road across southern Illinois, auctioning him for bonds to build the battleship Illinois, then under construction. Bidders bought the whole pig, then his individual parts, then even his squeal, and each time Neptune came back to be auctioned again. Demand for appearances grew until the pair were traveling the rest of the state. Neptune played his part in costume, often displayed under a blue Navy blanket and wearing a crown and silver earrings. On March 6, 1943, Illinois Governor Dwight H. Green bought him for $1 million; the $500 bristle sold at that same auction. In early 1945, when someone wrote a check to King Neptune for his upkeep, the bank asked Lingle to obtain the pig's endorsement. Lingle's fundraising with Neptune helped earn him a promotion to Chief Petty Officer.

## The Battleship That Never Sailed

For all the bonds Neptune raised, the ship bearing his state's name never went to sea. The Iowa-class battleship Illinois was canceled on August 11, 1945, when she was about 22 percent complete, and she was broken up on her slipway in September 1958. Neptune very nearly met an ignominious end too. In 1946 he was slated to go to the Chicago Stockyards, but Lingle regained ownership and placed him on a local farm, where he lived out his life. He died of pneumonia on Ernest Goddard's farm near Anna on May 14, 1950, two days short of his eighth birthday. He had not been the only such fundraiser: in Oklahoma, a shorthorn steer and a Hereford bull called "General Grant" were also auctioned for around two million dollars each in war bonds.

## Military Honors for a Hog

Neptune was buried with military honors about six miles east of Anna, off Illinois Route 146, at a spot that became known locally as King Neptune Park. His original tombstone read: "King Neptune (1941–1950) Buried here — King Neptune, famous Navy mascot pig auctioned for $19,000,000.00 in war bonds 1942–1946 to help make a free world." The birth year was wrong. Lingle had bigger plans for the site, planning in 1956 to donate another 10 acres for a Naval Reserve National Park, and in 1963 a new location was selected less than a mile east of the Interstate 57 and Route 146 interchange. After the earlier monument was heavily vandalized, the state of Illinois placed a second one in the late 1980s at the nearby northbound I-57 rest area, where his birth year finally reads 1942. Illinois has since rebuilt that rest area, reopening it in late 2025 after almost five years of construction, so it is worth confirming that the monument is still there before you go.

## Flight Context

King Neptune's memorial area lies near 37.4458°N, 89.1316°W in Union County, Illinois, about six miles east of Anna, close to the Interstate 57 and Illinois Route 146 interchange; the state monument was placed at the northbound I-57 rest area nearby, which Illinois rebuilt and reopened in late 2025. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, follow I-57 south from Marion and look for the Route 146 interchange east of Anna. Veterans Airport of Southern Illinois at Marion (KMWA), the town where Lingle worked as a recruiter, is about 19 nm north-northeast, and Southern Illinois Airport at Murphysboro (KMDH) is about 21 nm north-northwest; Cape Girardeau Regional (KCGI) lies about 25 nm west-southwest across the Mississippi. Valley fog can linger in the hollows on cool mornings.
