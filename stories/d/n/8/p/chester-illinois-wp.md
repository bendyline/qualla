# Chester, Illinois

On a bluff above the Mississippi, the Randolph County seat of Chester grew on castor beans, flour and shoes, and is proudest of the hometown boy who created Popeye.

**Location:** 37.9139, -89.8231

**Categories:** Cities in Randolph County, Illinois, County seats in Illinois, Illinois populated places on the Mississippi River, Popeye, Populated places established in 1829

## Introduction

Before he drew a single comic strip, Elzie Segar worked in the dark at the Chester Opera House. He played musical accompaniment for films and vaudeville acts at the local theater, was eventually given the job of film projectionist, and did live performances there too. Segar was born in Chester, a river town on a bluff above the Mississippi, on December 8, 1894, and several of the characters he later drew grew out of his experiences with the people of his hometown. The most famous of them now stands beside the Chester Bridge: a six-foot, 900-pound bronze statue of Popeye the Sailor Man.

## Castor Beans and a Log-Cabin Wedding

Chester's first business was a general store that opened in 1830, along with a castor oil press run by R. B. Servant. He supplied local farmers with seed and growing methods, then bought back the beans they raised to press for oil, a flourishing trade until the petroleum industry made it obsolete. The town's first wedding took place on February 4, 1834, when Content Walker married Amzi Andrews in a log cabin sixteen feet square. Visitors of note soon passed through. Charles Dickens and his wife reportedly visited Chester on April 11, 1842. Mark Twain, who piloted on the Mississippi until the Civil War broke out in 1861, supposedly stayed on many trips at the Cliff House, a fine river hostelry. Revisiting the river years later, he wrote in Life on the Mississippi: "We found a railway intruding at Chester, Illinois; Chester has also a penitentiary now, and is otherwise marching on."

## Flour, Light and Shoes

In 1839 Nathan Cole founded what became the H.C. Cole Milling Company. It began as a small sawmill with a corn-grinding attachment, which encouraged townspeople to plant grain, and before long the first Cole flour mill was built. Cole also brought Chester its first electric generator and used surplus power from the mill to run street lights; that generator is now displayed at the Ford Museum in Dearborn, Michigan, and the mill itself still operates under Ardent Mills. In 1916, four men, among them Albert Gilster, developed a site in the center of town for the International Shoe Company. A second building followed in 1922, and the plant grew to more than 1,000 employees turning out thousands of pairs of children's shoes. During World War II, a workforce made up largely of women produced combat boots for the troops. By 1953 the payroll topped $2 million, but the company closed the factory in the early 1960s after 45 years. The Gilster-Mary Lee food company occupies the site today.

## The Sailor Man Comes Home

Segar's strip, Thimble Theatre, made its debut on December 19, 1919. For almost a decade it ran without its most famous character. Then, on January 17, 1929, when Castor Oyl needed a mariner to navigate his ship to Dice Island, he picked up a weatherbeaten sailor named Popeye. It is believed that Segar was remembering Frank "Rocky" Fiegel, a tough laborer who was always getting into fights but also gave out candy and treats to children, including a young Segar. In 1977 Chester named a park for him, the Elzie C. Segar Memorial Park, now home to the bronze Popeye beside the bridge. The town has kept building on it. Each year new statues of other Thimble Theatre characters join the Popeye & Friends Character Trail, and on the weekend after Labor Day, Popeye's Picnic brings rides, food stands, music, fireworks, a walk and run, and a Saturday morning parade of school bands, floats, fire departments, veterans, clowns and, bringing up the rear, horses.

## Bridge, Bluff and Walls

The Chester Bridge first opened on August 23, 1942, as a toll crossing to Perry County, Missouri. A storm destroyed sections of it in July 1944, and it did not reopen until August 1946; tolls finally ended on January 1, 1989. Chester's large employers include a different kind of institution: alongside Gilster-Mary Lee, a private-label food maker, the Chester area is home to the maximum-security Menard Correctional Center and the maximum-security Chester Mental Health Center. Hollywood has come calling too. Scenes from the 1967 film In the Heat of the Night were shot here, and the town earned a mention in 1993's The Fugitive. Along the river, part of the Middle Mississippi River National Wildlife Refuge lies at Chester's doorstep. The 2020 census counted 6,814 residents, and the bronze sailor still keeps watch by the bridge, above the river Twain once piloted.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 37.9139°N, 89.8231°W, in Chester, Illinois, on a bluff on the east bank of the Mississippi River. The Chester Bridge, crossing to Perry County, Missouri, is the best landmark; Segar Memorial Park and the Popeye statue sit beside its Illinois end, and the town spreads along the bluff above the river. A cruise around 2,000-3,000 feet MSL gives a good view of the bridge, bluff and river bottomlands. Perryville Regional Airport, Missouri (KPCD) is about 3 nm across the river to the southwest, and Sparta Community-Hunter Field (KSAR) about 15 nm northeast; Southern Illinois Airport near Murphysboro (KMDH) is about 28 nm east-southeast. River fog is common on cool mornings, and summer brings haze and thunderstorms.
