# Bald Knob Cross

Grown out of an Easter sunrise service first held in 1937, the 111-foot Bald Knob Cross of Peace watches over the Shawnee National Forest in southern Illinois.

**Location:** 37.5520, -89.3464

**Categories:** Monumental crosses in the United States, Monuments and memorials in Illinois, Buildings and structures in Union County, Illinois, Shawnee National Forest, 1963 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

On Easter morning in 1937, 250 people gathered on Bald Knob Mountain in southern Illinois for a sunrise service. The idea came from Wayman Presley and the Rev. William Lirely, who had proposed the mountain as an ideal spot after the Easter service of 1936, and the gathering has been held every year since. Out of that tradition rose the Bald Knob Cross of Peace, a white cross 111 feet tall near the village of Alto Pass. Completed in 1963, it looks out over the Shawnee National Forest, and it can be seen from a great distance across the surrounding hills.

## Raised One Fundraiser at a Time

Nothing about the cross came quickly. Once the site had been acquired, the structure went up in stages, paid for with money from a variety of fundraisers. The most famous of them was the fundraiser run by Myrta Clutts, whose campaign became part of the cross's legend. The Easter services continued through it all, and from 1984 to 1986 they were paired with a Passion Play. The cross that resulted is both a place of worship and a regional landmark, visible enough that flight students from Southern Illinois University often use it to find their way.

## Anatomy of a Landmark

The numbers explain why it can be seen so far away. The base of the cross sits 1,034 feet above sea level. From there it rises 111 feet, measuring 22 feet square at the bottom and 16 feet square at the top, while its arms stretch 63 feet from end to end. A steel framework weighing about 170 tons forms its skeleton, sheathed in four-inch insulated architectural metal panels, and the base is faced with granite roughly four inches thick. Below ground, 730 tons of reinforced concrete reach 20 feet down to bedrock. Each of the four sides bears a single word cut into the granite: Peace, Hope, Faith and Charity.

## A Truce on Christmas Eve

In 2006 the cross's own caretakers fell into conflict. A legal dispute arose among the members of its board, and a two-year court battle followed, during which a court order banned all members of the organization from the grounds. The cross, meanwhile, was falling into serious disrepair. Local residents formed a group called Friends of the Cross to raise money for its eventual restoration. The litigation ended on Christmas Eve 2008, when all sides signed an agreement to drop their charges and resign from the board if the court would hand leadership to a two-year Transitional Board of Directors, made up mostly of pastors from communities and counties across the region. That board held its first meeting on January 16, 2009, and elected officers.

## White Again Above the Forest

The transitional board put the organization back on firm legal and financial footing, and Friends of the Cross raised the money to restore the cross completely. The grounds have been improved as well, with a remodeled welcome center and gift shop, a new observation deck and other amenities. A planned interior lift, the Top of the Cross Experience, announced in April 2026 with a $200,000 state tourism matching grant, would carry visitors up to an enclosed deck about 100 feet high; the cross's official site says construction will soon begin. Below the summit, the Bald Knob Wilderness, a federally designated wilderness area, covers the mountain's western slope, so the cross stands at the edge of protected forest. Each spring, the sunrise service that started it all still greets Easter morning on the mountain.

## Flight Context

Bald Knob Cross stands at approximately 37.5520 N, 89.3464 W, near Alto Pass in Union County, Illinois; its base is 1,034 ft above sea level and the cross rises 111 ft above that. The tall white cross on its forested summit is a well-known visual landmark, used by Southern Illinois University flight students, and is conspicuous from the surrounding Shawnee National Forest hills. A comfortable viewing altitude is 2,500-3,500 ft MSL, which keeps you well clear of the summit and the top of the cross (about 1,145 ft MSL). Nearest airports: Southern Illinois Airport, Carbondale/Murphysboro (KMDH), about 14 nm north-northeast; Veterans Airport of Southern Illinois, Marion (KMWA), about 20 nm northeast; and Cape Girardeau Regional Airport (KCGI), about 22 nm south-southwest. Watch for training traffic from the Carbondale area, and expect summer haze and afternoon storms over the hills.
