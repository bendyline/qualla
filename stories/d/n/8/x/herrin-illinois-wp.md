# Herrin, Illinois

Built on a vein of coal struck at 185 feet, the Southern Illinois city of Herrin boomed with mines and big-band dances, bore the violence of the 1922 Herrin massacre, and now throws one of the region's biggest Italian festivals.

**Location:** 37.8025, -89.0281

**Categories:** Cities in Williamson County, Illinois, Coal towns in Illinois, Populated places established in 1816, Labor disputes in Illinois, Italian-American culture

## Introduction

Coal had already been found at nearby Carterville when, in 1892, three cousins went prospecting at the southwest corner of Mrs. Williams' farm on Herrin's Prairie in Williamson County: David Ruffin Harrison, Ephraim Snyder Herrin, and Mrs. Williams herself. As an account of the venture put it, "The men put up the cash, Mrs. Williams boarded the workers. A fine vein of coal was found at 185 feet." That seam made a town. Within eight years the scattered prairie settlement had a railroad, a new name, a town plat and a city charter, and Herrin was on its way to becoming one of the coal towns of Southern Illinois.

## Herring's Prairie

The name goes back further than the coal. The first permanent settler was Isaac Herring, a Baptist preacher, who entered the first land here on November 4, 1816, two years before Illinois became a state, paying $2 an acre for 160 acres. His son-in-law, David Herrin, arrived later, and the similarity of the two names eventually shortened Herring's Prairie to Herrin's Prairie. It sat at a crossroads of frontier trails: the route from Jordan's fort to Humphreys' ford on the Big Muddy River crossed the old trail from Lusk's ferry, at modern-day Golconda, to Kaskaskia, first settled by French colonists. In 1858 David Ruffin Harrison opened a store on the prairie, and during the Civil War he built a frame store building and secured a post office, which opened on May 26, 1864.

## A Railroad and a Plat

Next came the railroad. In 1895 the Chicago and Carbondale Railroad organized to link the Illinois Central at Carbondale with a new line running through the middle of the county, and Herrin persuaded the developers to route their tracks between Carbondale and Johnston City through town. On May 8, 1896, the post office became the Herrin post office. Cousins Harrison and Herrin, great-grandsons of Isaac Herring, platted a new 40-acre town and filed the plat on December 4, 1896. Herrin incorporated as a village on March 21, 1898, and as a city on April 17, 1900. Prosperity followed the coal. At one time Herrin had ten hotels, along with clothing, grocery and department stores.

## Two Days in June

The coal also brought violence. In June 1922, during a nationwide strike by the United Mine Workers of America, W. J. Lester, owner of the Southern Illinois Coal Company, at first agreed with the union to observe the strike, then, as coal prices rose and his start-up debts pressed, hired non-union workers to produce and ship coal. On June 21 and 22, 1922, the confrontation at Herrin turned deadly. Accounts put the dead at 21 to 23, among them three union miners, the mine's superintendent, and strikebreakers brought in to work the mine. No one was ever convicted. Prosecutors obtained hundreds of indictments by some counts (214 in one account), but only a handful of men were tried; the first trials ended in acquittals by local juries, and the remaining charges were dropped.

## Big Bands at White City

Good times came with the mines, too. White City Park opened on Memorial Day 1924 before a crowd of 5,000, with a saltwater swimming pool, rides and a theater. Touring big bands played there, and the Dorsey Brothers and Frank Sinatra performed at the park and played bocce ball. Presidents found their way to Herrin as well. Harry S. Truman came in September 1948, and Richard Nixon, John F. Kennedy and Ronald Reagan all campaigned here before winning the White House; Gerald Ford came to the Herrin-Marion airport in the 1970s. Among the many grocery stores the city has had over the years, not counting national chains, Louie's P&R is the only local one still open.

## Festa and Totality

Around Memorial Day each year, Herrin celebrates its Italian heritage with HerrinFesta Italiana, a week-long festival (in 2026 it ran May 18 to 25, with a five-day carnival midway) that is said to draw more than 60,000 people for live music, Italian food, a carnival, a bocce tournament, and pasta-eating and "Bigga Nose" contests. In 2015 Herrin also signed a twinning proclamation making Cuggiono, in Italy's Lombardy region, its sister city. The town has a rail story of its own: the Crab Orchard & Egyptian Railroad, founded in 1971 as a tourist line, now carries freight to local industries along about five miles of track in Herrin. And by pure coincidence, Herrin sat in the path of two total solar eclipses just seven years apart, on August 21, 2017, and April 8, 2024. The city counted 12,352 residents in 2020.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 37.8025°N, 89.0281°W, in Herrin in Williamson County, Southern Illinois, midway between Carbondale to the west and Marion to the southeast. From the air look for the city grid among farm fields and former mining ground, with Crab Orchard Lake spreading a few miles to the south. A cruise around 2,500-3,000 feet MSL gives a good view of the town. Veterans Airport of Southern Illinois at Marion (KMWA) is only about 3 nm away; Southern Illinois Airport at Murphysboro-Carbondale (KMDH) is about 11 nm west and Benton Municipal (FAA: H96, no ICAO code) about 13 nm north-northeast. Summers are hot and humid with afternoon thunderstorms.
