# Metropolis, Illinois

Founded in 1839 with a name far bigger than the town, Metropolis on the Ohio River grew up beside an old French fort and found its fame as the self-proclaimed hometown of Superman.

**Location:** 37.1531, -88.7253

**Categories:** Cities in Illinois, County seats in Illinois, Illinois populated places on the Ohio River, Superman, Populated places established in 1757

## Introduction

In one Superman comic, a villain named Massacre makes a wrong turn. Hunting the Man of Steel, he lands in Metropolis, Illinois, a small town on the Ohio River, and has to attack a security guard and threaten the townspeople just to get directions to the "other" Metropolis. The gag works because the real town has embraced the confusion for decades. A 15-foot painted bronze Superman stands in front of the Massac County courthouse, the local newspaper is The Metropolis Planet, and the water tower announces "Home of Superman."

## A Fort Above the River

Long before the Man of Steel, this bank of the Ohio was contested ground. For thousands of years Native American cultures lived in the region, and Mississippian regional centers rose along the Ohio and lower Mississippi valleys, linked by river trade routes. In 1757 a French expedition built Fort de L'Ascension, later called Fort Massac, for the French and Indian War; its garrison withstood a Cherokee attack. After the French defeat the fort was abandoned, and by the time the British arrived, the Chickasaw had already destroyed it. During the Revolution, George Rogers Clark led American troops into the Illinois country, landing near present-day Metropolis. In 1794 President George Washington ordered Fort Massac rebuilt high above the river, but the New Madrid earthquakes of 1811–12 severely damaged it, the Army left in 1814, and settlers carried off its timbers.

## A Grand Name for a River Town

In 1839 a merchant from Pittsburgh and a local landowner founded a town near the old fort. They expected the site on the Ohio to become a transport hub, and they chose a befittingly grand name. Massac County followed in 1843, with Metropolis as its seat, and the McCartney family became leaders in building the town. This part of Illinois was largely settled by migrants from the Upper South, many of whom brought enslaved people with them, and it was years after Illinois entered the Union as a free state that all of them gained their freedom. The area had many Southern sympathizers during the Civil War, though the state stayed with the Union. The town stayed small: the 2020 census counted 5,969 residents.

## Truth, Justice and a Legislative Resolution

What Metropolis lacked in size, its name eventually made up for. On April 25, 1972, the Illinois State Legislature passed Resolution 572, commending the city for its "Project Superman" community program. A few blocks from the Superman statue stands a statue of Noel Neill's Lois Lane from The Adventures of Superman. Since June 1979, when local civic clubs organized the first one, fans from around the world have gathered on the second weekend of June for the Superman Celebration. Now run by the Metropolis Chamber of Commerce, it has lasted more than four decades. Over a long June weekend there are celebrity guests from film, television and comics, vendors selling comics and homemade crafts, discussion panels, auctions and a costume contest. And in "Superman #92," the comics returned the favor, portraying the real Metropolis as a town whose citizens idolize their fictional hero.

## Bridges, Riverboats and Notable Neighbors

Everyday Metropolis runs on the river and the roads. Interstate 24 passes the city, U.S. Route 45 runs through its center, and the Paducah & Illinois Railroad crosses the Ohio on the Metropolis Bridge. Agriculture has long driven the regional economy, but when Illinois authorized riverboat gambling, the city became home to the Harrah's Metropolis casino, and tourism is now one of its largest industries. Metropolis is also the site of Metropolis Works, the uranium conversion plant that turns milled uranium into uranium hexafluoride for nuclear reactors; long run by Honeywell, it is now operated by Solstice Advanced Materials, the company spun off from Honeywell in October 2025. The town counts some remarkable people among its own, including the pioneering African-American filmmaker and author Oscar Micheaux, the businesswoman Annie Turnbo Malone, founder of Poro College, and the Weaver sisters, Betty, Jean and Joanne, who played in the All-American Girls Professional Baseball League.

## Flight Context

Metropolis sits at 37.1531°N, 88.7253°W on the north (Illinois) bank of the Ohio River, directly across from McCracken County, Kentucky, northwest of Paducah. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, follow the broad Ohio and look for the Metropolis Bridge carrying the railroad across the river; the Superman statue stands in front of the Massac County courthouse in town. Metropolis Municipal Airport (FAA identifier M30, no ICAO code listed) is about 2 nm northwest of town, and Barkley Regional Airport at Paducah (KPAH) is about 6 nm south-southwest across the river. River fog can form on calm mornings, and summer haze is common over the Ohio valley.
