# National Quilt Museum

Founded by two Paducah publishers in 1991 and named by Congress in 2008, this downtown museum hangs contemporary quilts as the art they are.

## Introduction

A quilt is usually something you sleep under, hand down to a grandchild, or pull out on a cold night. In downtown Paducah, Kentucky, quilts hang on gallery walls as art. The National Quilt Museum, which opened on April 25, 1991, exhibits fiber art and quilting from around the world, and USA Today has recognized it as one of the world's top quilt displays. Since 2008 it has also carried a national designation from the U.S. Congress: the National Quilt Museum of the United States. For a craft so often learned at a kitchen table, that is quite a promotion.

## A Publisher's Vision

The museum began with a Paducah couple. Bill and Meredith Schroeder were influential local publishers and the founders of the American Quilter's Society, and the museum was their vision. They built it around contemporary work: a museum dedicated to modern quilts and the people who make them. Victoria Faoro served as its founding executive director. The collection it has assembled reflects that focus: according to the museum, it features many of the best quilts made since 1980, and more than 95 percent of the collection was made by women. That figure is a quiet reminder of whose hands have carried this art, one careful stitch at a time.

## Seven Hundred Quilts, Always Moving

Walk into the main gallery and you see only a slice of what the museum holds. That gallery is devoted to a rotating selection from a collection of more than 700 quilts, so the walls change as pieces come and go. Two further galleries host rotating fiber art exhibits through the year, and a traveling exhibit of quilts and fiber art carries the collection beyond Paducah. The museum is also a place to learn. It supports local and expert quilters with workshops and other educational activities for adults and children at every skill level. Some of its programs have reached well beyond technique. In 1993 the museum hosted the first African American Quilters Forum, and in 1995 it developed "Gatherings," an exhibit and conference celebrating the quilt documentation projects completed across the country.

## An Act of Congress

In 2008, the U.S. Congress designated the Paducah institution the National Quilt Museum of the United States; the House passed the resolution, H. Con. Res. 209, on February 12, 2008. May Louise Zumwalt, a former executive director, was clear-eyed about what that meant: "Though it does not mean we will receive national funding, it does recognize that we are a quilt museum with national significance." The recognition brought more attention and helped draw more visitors, and the museum now averages 40,000 of them a year. Over its first 30 years, by the museum's own count, it attracted nearly one million visitors, curated more than 250 exhibitions and served 90,000 students. In July 2021, Matt Collinsworth became its chief executive officer, taking charge of a museum that describes itself as serving both its home community and the broader world of quilting.

## A River Town Stitched Together

The museum's home is a fitting one. Paducah sits at the confluence of the Tennessee and Ohio rivers, and it has built a reputation around handmade things. The American Quilter's Society hosts a week of quilt shows in the city, complete with classes, fabric shops and a crowd of vendors. On November 21, 2013, UNESCO designated Paducah a Creative City of Crafts and Folk Art. Downtown, the craft spills onto the streets themselves: since 1996, the Paducah Wall to Wall mural program, begun by Louisiana mural artist Robert Dafford and his team, has covered the city's floodwall with painted scenes. Between the murals, the rivers and the galleries, a visitor finds a town that treats needle and thread with the seriousness other cities reserve for oil paint.
