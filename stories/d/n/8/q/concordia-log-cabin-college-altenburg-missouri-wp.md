# Concordia Log Cabin College (Altenburg, Missouri)

In a 20-by-17-foot log cabin in Altenburg, Missouri, German Lutheran immigrants taught eleven children Latin, Greek and Hebrew in 1839, founding the first college of what became the Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod.

**Location:** 37.6286, -89.6025

**Categories:** Lutheran Church – Missouri Synod, Perry County, Missouri, Log buildings and structures on the National Register of Historic Places in Missouri, National Register of Historic Places in Perry County, Missouri, Tourist attractions in Perry County, Missouri

## Introduction

Latin, Greek, Hebrew, German, French and English. History, geography, mathematics, physics, natural history, philosophy, music and drawing. That was the program on offer when classes began on December 9, 1839, in a log cabin measuring 20 by 17 feet in the German Lutheran settlement of Perry County, Missouri. Eleven students reported that first day, seven boys and four girls. Their teachers were paid nothing, because the school had no money, but they had been trained in European universities and were deeply convinced that a sound education mattered. The cabin still stands in Altenburg, under a protective shelter in the maple grove of Trinity Lutheran Church.

## A Saxon Gymnasium in Missouri

The cabin was built in 1839 as the first school for a community founded during the German Lutheran migration of 1838-1839. It was patterned on the gymnasium, the Latin grammar school of Saxony, and its curriculum followed the program Christian humanists had advocated for centuries. Ambition outran resources almost at once. By 1841 enrollment had dropped to eight pupils, and two years later only five remained. From 1841 to 1843, Gotthold Heinrich Loeber and Theodor Brohm ran the school, until Brohm was called to New York and Loeber carried it alone for half a year, somehow keeping it alive. By 1843 it was obvious that the school needed a sound financial footing to survive.

## Firewood and Seven Dollars a Month

The solution was a partnership among congregations. Trinity congregation agreed to pay the bills, the Perry County congregations would supply food and firewood, and St. Louis provided seven dollars a month to sustain the college. Trinity also recommended John Goenner as a teacher to relieve Loeber. Bigger changes were coming. In late April 1847, at a meeting in Chicago, the Evangelical Lutheran Synod of Missouri, Ohio and Other States was organized, with C. F. W. Walther as its first president, and control of the school passed to the new synod on the understanding that it would move to St. Louis. Loeber and his congregation did not object to synod control, but they were reluctant to lose the college, and it took the synod's third convention, in 1849, to insist on the transfer. Loeber did not live to see the move.

## Five Pastors and a Synod

For its roughly ten years in Perry County, the college was small and always on the verge of collapse. Yet it persevered, producing five pastors, and it gave the ministers and congregations of St. Louis and Perry County a shared purpose that helped bring the Missouri Synod into being. It was the first college of what became the Lutheran Church-Missouri Synod and is described as the first Lutheran college west of the Mississippi River. The cabin's own later history is humbler. Loeber's heirs sold it at auction in 1855, a sale probably confirmed by an 1859 quit-claim deed that transferred the cabin and a 4.3-acre lot to Gottlieb Funke for $80. Funke lived there for 38 years. His will, written in 1893, left the cabin and lot to the church in thanks for the Altenburg congregation's care during his illness.

## Logs, Mortar and a Move

In 1912, at the suggestion of the Rev. H. Schmidt, the cabin was moved a few hundred yards to the church grove, where it now stands. The movers removed a chimney and placed two oak beams underneath, which stayed on as the bottom logs once the cabin was set on its new concrete foundation, and they attached 2-by-6-inch oak timbers upright against the walls to steady it. A fund drive across the synod paid for the shelter built over it in 1915. It may have moved once before, around 1840-1841, about a mile from Dresden to Altenburg to be nearer Pastor Loeber's parsonage, though that is not agreed upon. The building itself is plain: crudely shaped logs 6 to 10 inches across, ten logs high, with only slight notching and no dovetailing, chinked with mortar and brick under a shake-shingle roof. Inside, the log walls are left unplastered. The cabin was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1978.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 37.6286°N, 89.6025°W, in the small town of Altenburg in Perry County, Missouri, a few miles west of the Mississippi River between Perryville and Cape Girardeau. The cabin itself is small and sheltered in a church grove, so look instead for the town set among farm fields and wooded hills above the river bottoms. A cruise around 2,500-3,000 feet MSL gives a good view of the town and the river. Nearby airports include Southern Illinois Airport near Murphysboro (KMDH) about 19 nm east-northeast across the river, Perryville Regional (KPCD) about 19 nm northwest, and Cape Girardeau Regional (KCGI) about 24 nm south. River fog is common on cool mornings; summer brings haze and afternoon storms.
