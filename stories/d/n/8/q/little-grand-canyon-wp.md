# Little Grand Canyon

A sandstone box canyon in the Shawnee National Forest, once called both the Hanging Gardens of Egypt and the Rattlesnake Den, where a white-blazed trail drops 350 feet past seasonal waterfalls.

**Location:** 37.6864, -89.3984

**Categories:** Landforms of Jackson County, Illinois, Canyons and gorges of Illinois, Protected areas of Jackson County, Illinois, Shawnee National Forest, Hiking trails in Illinois, National Natural Landmarks in Illinois

## Introduction

In the 1930s, this place went by two names at once. Some called it the "Hanging Gardens of Egypt," for the wildflowers that crowded its walls. Others called it the "Rattlesnake Den," because it held one of the best rattlesnake dens in the United States. Both describe the same deep fold in the sandstone of southern Illinois: the Little Grand Canyon, officially named simply Grand Canyon, a box canyon in the Shawnee National Forest south of Murphysboro in Jackson County. Its sandstone walls hide waterfalls, unguarded cliff edges and a trail whose stone steps were laid by the Civilian Conservation Corps.

## Carved from the Shawnee Hills

Erosion did the work. Water cut this deep box canyon out of the sandstone of the Shawnee Hills until it opened onto the floodplain of the Mississippi River near Turkey Bayou. The canyon sits on the east bank of the Big Muddy River, with Turkey Bayou across the water to the west, Swallow Rock to the north and Chalk Bluff to the south. Visitors reach it from Hickory Ridge Road on the east side. Inside, several seasonal waterfalls and a few permanent ones spill down the rock, some of them descending through dramatic cascades of sculpted sandstone. In 1980 the area was designated a National Natural Landmark, a recognition of how much landscape is folded into this small corner of Jackson County.

## Following the White Diamonds

The loop trail runs roughly 3 to 3.6 miles, depending on the source, and is marked by white diamond blazes. It starts gently, in upland hardwood forest, then drops through a steep and sometimes slippery sandstone side canyon into a different world: the lusher, damper habitat of the canyon floor. From bottom to trailhead the elevation difference is roughly 350 feet, and the way back up demands a little climbing at the Three Sisters Waterfall. Along parts of the route, hikers tread on stone steps. Some believe Native Americans laid them, but they were in fact built by the Civilian Conservation Corps. Knowing that, each worn step becomes a small monument to the crews who shaped the route.

## Gardens and Serpents

The old nicknames tell a story of abundance and its limits. "Hanging Gardens of Egypt" nodded to the wildflowers that flourished in the canyon, and to the fact that southern Illinois has long been known as Little Egypt, a region dotted with towns bearing names such as Cairo and Thebes. "Rattlesnake Den" was just as literal. The canyon harbored one of the best rattlesnake dens in the country, and that reputation proved costly for the snakes. Excessive collection greatly reduced their populations. The first nickname celebrates what grows here; the second now reads partly as a memorial to what people took away.

## Respecting the Edges

The Little Grand Canyon rewards preparation. The trailhead offers a picnic area and a pit toilet, but no water, so hikers need to carry their own. The cliff edges along the way are unmarked and unguarded, and heavy rain brings the possibility of flash floods. None of that should keep anyone away; it simply means treating the canyon as the wild place it is, rather than a park path. Those who do will find waterfalls, the damp green world of the canyon floor and a scramble back out past the Three Sisters that leaves a lasting impression.

## Flight Context

Coordinates: 37.686N, 89.398W, in the Shawnee National Forest south of Murphysboro, Illinois. The canyon lies on the east bank of the Big Muddy River where the wooded Shawnee Hills break down to the flat Mississippi River floodplain; that sharp line between forested bluffs and open bottomland is the best visual cue. A viewing altitude of 2,500 to 4,000 feet shows the canyon notch and the river together. Nearby airports: Southern Illinois Airport (KMDH), about 9 nm northeast near Murphysboro; Veterans Airport of Southern Illinois at Marion (KMWA), about 19 nm east-northeast, with scheduled service; and Cape Girardeau Regional (KCGI), about 29 nm south-southwest across the Mississippi in Missouri. Dense forest canopy can hide the canyon in summer; late fall and winter, with the leaves down, give the clearest view of the sandstone.
