# Monkey's Eyebrow, Kentucky

A rural Ballard County crossroads whose name has outlived its stores, with at least three competing explanations and one very old grudge behind it.

**Location:** 37.1867, -88.9878

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Ballard County, Kentucky, Unincorporated communities in Kentucky, Paducah micropolitan area

## Introduction

Nobody agrees on how Monkey's Eyebrow got its name, and that may be the most charming thing about it. This rural unincorporated community in Ballard County, Kentucky, sits at the far edge of the Jackson Purchase; it is generally the northwesternmost community in that corner of western Kentucky to appear on the highway maps handed out by the Kentucky Transportation Cabinet. Its stores are gone. What remains is a name that has proved sturdier than any building raised beneath it, and a handful of rival stories, one of them involving a neighbor with a very long memory, about where that name came from.

## Old Monkey and New Monkey

There were once two of them. Locals called the pair Old Monkey and New Monkey: one sat at the top of a small hill, the other at the bottom, and each had a store of its own. Today there are no stores at either spot, and the two halves live on mostly in those nicknames. According to an article in the county newspaper, the Advance Yeoman, the area picked up its unusual label around the turn of the 20th century. Why it did is another matter entirely. Here the story splits as neatly as the community once did, into competing versions that no one has ever managed to settle, and that locals seem in no hurry to.

## A Map, a Hill, and a Grudge

The tidiest explanation is geographic. Looked at from the air, or on a map of Ballard County, the land is said to resemble a monkey's head, and the community sits right where the eyebrow would be. A variation holds that from a nearby hill, the settlement itself takes the shape of a monkey's eyebrow. The most entertaining version involves a grudge. Sometime before 1900, a local man who did not care for his neighbors refused to shop at the general store and blacksmith shop that John and Dodge Ray had built and owned, and went to nearby Needmore for supplies instead. He reportedly considered the Ray store "only fit for a bunch of monkeys" and its two owners monkey-like themselves, right down to eyebrows that resembled a monkey's. In another telling, it was the brush on Beeler Hill, above the store, that looked to him like a monkey's brow. Whichever version you prefer, a neighbor's insult may well have become a community's permanent address.

## Signing Off from the Eyebrow

For years, the name reached far beyond the county line through a television set. WPSD-TV in nearby Paducah frequently mentioned Monkey's Eyebrow in its sign-off message, because the station's broadcast transmitter, on a tower of roughly 1,600 feet, is located here. It was an odd kind of fame for a place without a single store, and it tied the community to the Paducah area, of which it is still counted a part. Daily life here follows the rhythms of the countryside. The state-controlled Ballard County Wildlife Management Area is a nearby attraction, and when winter arrives, goose hunting comes to Monkey's Eyebrow.

## Ten Generations Deep

The community's best-known modern voice grew up right here. Country singer Kelsey Waldon was born in Ballard County and raised in Monkey's Eyebrow, and her family's roots in Kentucky reach back more than ten generations, from tobacco farmers to cattle raisers. Some of her first jobs were in farming and planting tobacco. After self-releasing her early music, she was announced at the Grand Ole Opry in May 2019 as a new signing to John Prine's label, Oh Boy Records, the first artist the label had taken on in fifteen years. Her albums include White Noise/White Lines, No Regular Dog and Every Ghost. Musician Paul Watson was born in Monkey's Eyebrow too, a respectable musical showing for a community that no longer has a store to its name.
