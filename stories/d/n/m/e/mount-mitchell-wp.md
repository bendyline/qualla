# Mount Mitchell

The highest point in mainland North America east of the Mississippi, Mount Mitchell once topped the entire United States and still guards the grave of the professor who measured it.

**Location:** 35.7648, -82.2649

**Categories:** Mountains, Highest points of U.S. states, Blue Ridge Mountains, State parks, Natural history, North Carolina

## Introduction

Elisha Mitchell died checking his own numbers. The University of North Carolina professor explored the Black Mountain region and in 1835 announced that its tallest peak exceeded New Hampshire's Mount Washington by several hundred feet. In 1857 he returned to verify those measurements and fell to his death at nearby Mitchell Falls. His grave now lies at the base of the observation platform on the summit that carries his name, the highest point of the Appalachian Mountains and of mainland North America east of the Mississippi River.

## Alone on the Eastern Skyline

Mount Mitchell rises 6,684 feet above sea level in the Black Mountain subrange of the Appalachians, near Burnsville in Yancey County, North Carolina, about 19 miles northeast of Asheville. The Cherokee people, who long occupied this area as part of their homeland, called it Attakulla. Its height is lonelier than it sounds. Nothing in eastern North America south of the Arctic Cordillera stands taller; Barbeau Peak is higher, but it sits in the very remote Canadian Arctic. Look west and the nearest higher peaks are in the Black Hills of South Dakota and the highland foothills of Colorado. Measured to the nearest discernible higher point, Lone Butte in southeastern Colorado, Mount Mitchell's topographic isolation stretches 1,189 miles. It does have close company at nearly its own height. Mount Craig, the second highest point in eastern North America at 6,647 feet, stands roughly a mile to the north.

## A Crown Worn for Fourteen Years

For a while, Mount Mitchell was reportedly the top of the whole country. By one account, from 1789 it was the highest mountain in the United States, until the Louisiana Purchase of 1803 brought Mount Elbert under American claim. By the same account it held a second title far longer: the most isolated peak in the United States from 1789 until 1848, when the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo made Mount Whitney the new record holder. Neither of those western mountains grew an inch in the meantime. The border simply moved west, and the old champion found itself outranked by peaks that had been standing there all along. Its eastern crown, though, has never been taken.

## Stone From an Ancient Sea

The rock underfoot began as something entirely different. Mount Mitchell formed during the Precambrian, when marine deposits were metamorphosed into gneiss and schist. Those metasedimentary rocks were later uplifted during the Alleghenian orogeny. What has weathered from them now covers the summit: well-drained, dark brown, stony soils whose fine-earth material ranges in texture from sandy clay loam to loam or sandy loam, with the Burton and Craggey series the most common around the top. It is a strange inheritance to consider while catching your breath at the summit. Sediment first laid down in water became the highest ground in the East.

## Where the Firs Stand Gray

The summit wears a dense stand of Southern Appalachian spruce-fir forest, built mainly from two evergreens, the red spruce and the Fraser fir. It is not the forest it once was. In the latter half of the 20th century, a non-native pest, the balsam woolly adelgid, killed off most of the mature Fraser firs, and many of their dead trunks still stand among the summer green as stark reminders. The high elevation brings another threat. The summit is exposed to heavy pollution, including acid precipitation arriving as rain, snow and fog. The acids damage red spruce in part by releasing natural metals such as aluminum from the soil and by leaching away important minerals, though how much the pollution harms this high-altitude ecosystem remains debatable. The problem is hard to solve from here, because the pollutants often travel long distances on the air. Sources can be local or hundreds of miles away, and cleaner air on this peak requires cooperation from as far away as the Midwest.

## Blueberries at the Top of the East

For all its losses, the summit is far from barren. Wildflowers are abundant all summer long. Young fir and spruce trees do well in the subalpine climate, and their cones feed the birds, along with wild blueberry and blackberry shrubs. North Carolina recognized the place early. Established by the state legislature in 1915, Mount Mitchell State Park became the first state park in North Carolina, and it is surrounded today by the Pisgah National Forest. Reaching the top no longer takes a professor's endurance. A 4.6-mile road, NC 128, connects the Blue Ridge Parkway to a parking lot, and from there a steep paved trail of about 980 feet leads through conifer forest to the summit. The 40-foot stone observation tower that once stood there was torn down in late 2006, and a new observation deck opened to visitors in January 2009. Mitchell's grave rests at its base. Hurricane Helene closed the park for nearly a year, and it reopened on September 15, 2025, with the Blue Ridge Parkway, at the time, open to it only from the Asheville side.

## Flight Context

Mount Mitchell summit: 35.7648°N, 82.2649°W, elevation 6,684 ft MSL, the high point of the north-south crest of the Black Mountains; Mount Craig (6,647 ft) stands about a mile to the north. A viewing altitude of 9,000 ft MSL or higher gives safe clearance over the ridge while showing the summit observation deck, the gray trunks of dead Fraser firs among the spruce, and the Blue Ridge Parkway threading below. Burnsville lies to the north-northwest and Asheville about 19 miles to the southwest. Nearest airports: Asheville Regional (KAVL), about 24 nm southwest; Avery County Morrison Field at Spruce Pine (FAA identifier 7A8, no ICAO code), about 17 nm northeast; Foothills Regional at Morganton (KMRN), about 32 nm east. The Black Mountain crest is often wrapped in cloud, so fly it only in good VFR conditions with generous terrain clearance.
