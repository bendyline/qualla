# Cold Mountain (North Carolina)

A 6,030-foot peak that can only be reached on foot, Cold Mountain lent its name to a novel, a film shot mostly in Romania and an opera, while the community at its base kept writing down its own history.

**Location:** 35.4094, -82.8561

**Categories:** Mountains, Pisgah National Forest, Blue Ridge Mountains, Hiking, Literature and Film, North Carolina

## Introduction

When Hollywood came to film Cold Mountain, it went to Romania. The 2003 Miramax adaptation was shot mostly in Romania's Carpathian Mountains, with some scenes filmed in Virginia, South Carolina and North Carolina, and a scholarly study of the move concluded that economics trumped geographic realism. The real mountain never moved. It still stands in the Great Balsam Mountains of western North Carolina, a piece of the Blue Ridge and the long Appalachian spine, wrapped in the Shining Rock Wilderness of Pisgah National Forest.

## A Summit You Have to Earn

Cold Mountain rises to 6,030 feet above sea level, roughly 10 miles southeast of Waynesville. That puts it among the Southern Appalachians' 6,000-foot peaks, though near the bottom of that list: in one common tally of 53 such peaks it ranks 52nd by height. What sets it apart is the effort it demands. No road reaches the top. The peak is reached on foot, typically by a strenuous branch of the Art Loeb Trail, a round trip of about 10 miles with roughly 2,800 to 3,000 feet of elevation change, and nobody arrives at the summit by accident. The difficulty is part of the mountain's character. The vast majority of Cold Mountain lies on federal land within the Shining Rock Wilderness, and the wilderness asks to be met on foot, on its own terms. Anyone who stands on top has climbed every one of those feet.

## Mounds, a Mansion and Five-Dollar Acres

Long before anyone drew a national forest boundary, Indigenous peoples of the Southeastern Woodlands lived in this region. During the Woodland period and later under the South Appalachian Mississippian culture, they raised prehistoric platform mounds in the region, and they were ancestors of the Cherokee people. Mississippian culture became influential in the area beginning about 1000 CE. Centuries later, the land passed into very different hands. Much of the land that became Pisgah National Forest was once owned by George Washington Vanderbilt II, the man who built the Biltmore Estate. After his death, his widow, Edith, sold about 86,700 acres to the federal government, proposing a price of $5 an acre. That tract became the core of Pisgah National Forest, the first eastern national forest created from acquired lands. The ground around Cold Mountain itself had a different recent owner: according to Western Carolina University's local history, the Champion Fibre Company logged the region around the mountain until the 1920s. However it passed into public hands, a mountain once held by private owners is now largely public ground, and most of its slopes have remained so ever since.

## Neighbors on the Northwest Slope

Not every acre went to the government. About 800 acres on the northwestern side of Cold Mountain, in Panther Branch, remain privately owned. Roughly 15 residences sit on that northwest flank, and the property owners themselves pay to maintain the access roads. It is an unusual arrangement for a peak so closely tied to wilderness: a small cluster of households on the shoulder of a mountain that most visitors can only reach by a long, hard hike. Conservation work has continued to redraw the map. In 2016 the Southern Appalachian Highlands Conservancy bought the 162-acre Dix Creek tract from private owners. In late October of that year the land was transferred to the N.C. Wildlife Resources Commission, with plans for it to expand the adjoining Cold Mountain Game Lands to 3,500 acres in 2017. With that purchase, a little more of the mountain came under public care.

## One Mountain, Many Tellings

For most people, Cold Mountain is first a title. The mountain and the Bethel community near its base, outside Canton, served as the setting for Charles Frazier's 1997 novel Cold Mountain. The Miramax film followed in 2003, shot mostly an ocean away, and the story then reached the opera stage: Jennifer Higdon's Cold Mountain, with a libretto by Gene Scheer, received its world premiere at the Santa Fe Opera on August 1, 2015. The mountain's own community has kept its stories too. Legends, Tales, & History of Cold Mountain is a six-volume series in The Pigeon Valley Heritage Collection, written by Evelyn M. Coltman and distributed by the Bethel Rural Community Organization. In 2012 the North Carolina Society of Historians honored the six volumes with its Barringer Award of Excellence. The novel, the film and the opera borrowed the mountain's name. This series, distributed from the community at its foot, set down its legends, tales and history.

## Flight Context

Cold Mountain summit: 35.4094°N, 82.8561°W, elevation 6,030 ft MSL, in the Great Balsam Mountains of Pisgah National Forest's Shining Rock Wilderness. A viewing altitude of roughly 8,500-9,500 ft MSL keeps safe clearance over the surrounding high ridges while showing the peak's forested summit. Waynesville lies to the northwest and Canton to the north. Nearest airports: Asheville Regional (KAVL), about 15 nm east; Jackson County Airport at Sylva (FAA identifier 24A, no ICAO code), about 18 nm west-southwest; Hendersonville Airport (FAA identifier 0A7, no ICAO code), about 22 nm east-southeast. High terrain surrounds the peak in every direction and ridgetops are often in cloud, so plan this flight for clear VFR conditions.
