# Appalachian Ski Mountain

A small North Carolina ski hill known for relentless snowmaking, a 365-foot vertical drop, and one of only eleven Burton Progression Parks in North America.

**Location:** 36.1742, -81.6622

**Categories:** Ski resorts, Snowmaking, Terrain parks, Watauga County, Appalachian Mountains

## Introduction

The vertical drop is 365 feet. By Colorado standards, that's an elevator ride. But on a January night with floodlights blazing and the guns blasting white plumes across thirteen trails, Appalachian Ski Mountain stacks a snow base often deeper than what Aspen has on the ground. Tucked off US 321 between Blowing Rock and Boone, this is southern skiing as it actually works: small, lit, manufactured, and surprisingly serious.

## From Blowing Rock Lodge to App Ski

In 1962, around the same time Cataloochee was opening to launch commercial alpine skiing in North Carolina, a venture called Blowing Rock Ski Lodge cut its first trails on the same High Country ridge. The name changed to Appalachian Ski Mountain in 1968, but the mountain stayed faithful to the formula: short runs, friendly grades, and obsessive snow management. The base elevation sits at 3,635 feet, the summit at 4,000 - numbers a Vermont skier might wave off until they remember they're in the Carolinas, where 4,000 feet of mountain is most of what nature offers.

## The Snow Comes from a Pipe

Every slope is covered by snowmaking, and management runs the guns whenever closed slopes or off-hours allow. The result: snow gets pushed up to within a day of scheduled closing, and the resort has hit its planned final ski day for years on end. Peak-season base depths routinely top 100 inches, which would be respectable in the Rockies. The longest trail, Orchard Run, stretches a half mile - 800 meters of carved corduroy descending under cables strung with quad chairs, a double, a handle tow, and two conveyor lifts that ferry first-timers up the bunny slopes.

## A Park That Tests You First

Three terrain parks scatter across the mountain - AppalTop, Appaljack, and Appal Jam. The middle one is North Carolina's only Burton Progression Park, part of a network of just eleven in North America designed to teach riders how to read features safely. Park staff rebuild one of the parks each week after the lifts close. Since the 2010-2011 season, riders who want the upper parks need a pass: a short safety video, a ten-question quiz, and a $5 annual or $3 day fee. Anyone under 18 brings a parent. The program is policy with a purpose - keep beginners out of the big jumps until they understand what they're looking at.

## After the Last Run

When the lights finally come down, the base lodge keeps moving. The rental shop holds 2,125 pairs of skis, 450 snowboards, and 75 skiboards, along with bibs, gloves, jackets, goggles, and helmets - everything a first-timer needs and nothing they have to pack. An outdoor 6,000-square-foot skating rink sits beside the lodge, lit for night sessions, with rental skates available indoors. The annual calendar runs from Anniversary Weekend in early December through New Year's Eve fireworks and finishes with the Meltdown Games on closing weekend, when March slush meets costumes and pond-skim competitions.

## Flight Context

Appalachian Ski Mountain sits at 36.17N, 81.66W between Boone and Blowing Rock at roughly 3,600-4,000 ft MSL. Recommended viewing altitude 6,500-8,000 ft - terrain peaks beyond the resort at Grandfather Mountain (5,964 ft) eight miles south. Nearest GA airport is Watauga County Memorial (KGEV) at Boone Hickory, North Wilkesboro (KUKF). The ski runs read as long white scars on dark ridgelines from the air - distinctive against the surrounding hardwood forest. Mountain wave and rotor are common in winter; expect strong westerly flow over the ridge.
