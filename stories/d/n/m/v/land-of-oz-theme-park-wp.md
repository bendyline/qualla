# Land of Oz (theme park)

A yellow brick road laid across a North Carolina ski mountain in 1970 led to a short-lived Wizard of Oz theme park that burned, was looted, fell quiet, and came back as a beloved autumn festival.

**Location:** 36.1853, -81.8804

**Categories:** Amusement parks in North Carolina, Oz (franchise), Tourist attractions in Avery County, North Carolina, Abandoned amusement parks, 1970 establishments in North Carolina

## Introduction

It took more than 44,000 bricks, every one glazed yellow, to build a road to Oz in the mountains of North Carolina. Brothers Harry and Grover Robbins laid it at Beech Mountain, a ski resort they wanted to turn into a year-round destination, after finding a stretch of land that reminded them of the 1939 film The Wizard of Oz. The Land of Oz opened in 1970 and ran fully for only a decade, yet the road it left behind has proved remarkably hard to abandon.

## Ribbon-Cutting in the Clouds

The Robbins brothers began developing Beech Mountain in the 1960s, with construction of the park itself starting in 1969. The park, designed by Jack Pentes and opened by the Carolina Caribbean Corporation, was built around L. Frank Baum's Oz books and the famous film. Characters along the Yellow Brick Road and in the Emerald City lip-synced to songs composed by Alec Wilder and North Carolina native Loonis McGlohon, with one borrowed exception: the park obtained the rights to "Over the Rainbow" by E. Y. Harburg and Harold Arlen for the Emerald City show. The park opened on June 15, 1970, drawing 4,000 visitors that first day, and actress Debbie Reynolds cut the ribbon with her 13-year-old daughter, Carrie Fisher, looking on, at a ceremony the park's own history dates to July 3. That first year the Land of Oz drew more than 400,000 visitors and was hailed as a top tourist attraction.

## Fire in the Emerald City

The triumph was shadowed from the start. Grover Robbins died a few months before the park opened, and without his drive, the effort to keep the Land of Oz a special experience gave way to commercial pressures as real estate sales around the resort slumped. A failed investment in St. Croix left the Carolina Caribbean Corporation bankrupt in 1975. Then, on Sunday, December 28, 1975, fire broke out in the Emerald City Amphitheater and the gift shops around it. Two buildings burned, taking the park's offices, costumes, sound equipment and props with them. At the same time, thieves carried off many items from the park's museum, among them Judy Garland's Dorothy dress. The park hung on in full operation until 1980, and then the yellow road went quiet.

## Bricks That Wouldn't Stay Put

Oz kept surfacing anyway. Around 1990, Appalachian State University in Boone opened its Appalachian Cultural Museum with props and costumes from the park, and part of its floor was paved with surplus yellow bricks donated by Sanford Brick, bricks made for the park but never used. On July 4, 1991, the park reopened for a single day as part of Beech Mountain's Independence Day celebration. Visitors rode the ski lift up from Ski Beech and were met by students from Watauga High School, still in costume from their spring production of The Wizard of Oz. They walked on to Dorothy's house, by then the home of property owner Alex Hufty Hays, to see original costumes and props from the 1939 film. Not every visitor since has been so respectful. Urban explorers often slip in to take photos, and some have pried loose pieces of the yellow brick road itself.

## Autumn at Oz

The landowners began restoring parts of the park, and in 1993 a reunion for original employees grew into Autumn at Oz, an annual public festival. By 2009 it drew more than 8,500 people, with the film's characters, shows, a museum and an Emerald City, and the money raised went back into renovating and maintaining the park. In 2011 the International Wizard of Oz Club visited, and members of the original 1970 cast returned to share photographs and stories. Guided "Journey with Dorothy" tours followed, and for the film's 80th anniversary in 2019 the gates opened on extra summer days. By 2024 the park opened only for three September weekends. Then, on March 14, 2025, it canceled that year's festival, citing the need for "a comprehensive assessment of the event and the park itself" after the festival's rapid growth and Hurricane Helene. At the time, the park said it planned to reopen in 2026, and it scheduled Autumn at Oz for three September weekends that year (September 11-13, 18-20 and 25-27).

## Flight Context

The Land of Oz sits high on Beech Mountain in Avery County, North Carolina, at about 36.19°N, 81.88°W, beside the Ski Beech ski area. Beech Mountain is high terrain; stay at 7,000 feet MSL or higher for clearance and look for the ski slopes and resort roads near the summit. Nearby airports: Johnson County Airport at Mountain City, Tennessee (FAA identifier 6A4, about 14 nm), Avery County Airport-Morrison Field at Spruce Pine (FAA identifier 7A8, about 16 nm), William B. Greene Jr. Regional Airport in Elizabethton (formerly Elizabethton Municipal; FAA identifier 0A9, about 18 nm) and Tri-Cities Airport (KTRI, about 31 nm), the nearest with airline service. Mountaintop weather changes quickly; choose a clear day.
