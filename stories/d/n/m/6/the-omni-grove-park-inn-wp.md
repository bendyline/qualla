# The Omni Grove Park Inn

Built of granite boulders on the slope of Sunset Mountain in 1913, Asheville's Grove Park Inn has sheltered presidents, interned diplomats, a struggling F. Scott Fitzgerald and more than a century of mountain-bound travelers.

**Location:** 35.6206, -82.5422

**Categories:** Hotels in Asheville, North Carolina, American Craftsman architecture in North Carolina, Hotel buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in North Carolina, Resorts in North Carolina, Golf clubs and courses designed by Donald Ross, Hotels established in 1913

## Introduction

William Howard Taft, a former president of the United States, resigned from the Supreme Court in 1930 not in Washington but, by the inn's own account, in the Great Hall of a hotel in the North Carolina mountains, where he had been resting for a month. The Grove Park Inn was built for moments of that weight. It sits on the western-facing slope of Sunset Mountain above Asheville, a rough pile of granite advertised when new as having "walls five feet thick of granite boulders," and for more than a century it has drawn presidents, writers and anyone else who wanted the Blue Ridge laid out in front of them.

## Walls Five Feet Thick

In 1909, Edwin Wiley Grove and his son-in-law, Fred Seely, bought about 400 acres near Sunset Mountain to build a grand hotel. They built it of rough granite stones in the Arts and Crafts style and filled it with furnishings from the Roycrofters of East Aurora, New York. Construction was finished in 1913. The golf course was already there. Built in 1899, with imposing views of the Blue Ridge Mountains, it was redesigned in 1924 by the golf course architect Donald Ross. Helen Keller was among the inn's first guests, and the list that followed reads like a century of American fame: Henry Ford, Thomas Edison, Eleanor Roosevelt, John D. Rockefeller, Harry Houdini, Will Rogers, George Gershwin, Margaret Mitchell, Billy Graham, Michael Jordan and Jack Nicklaus.

## A Father-in-Law's Refusal

Behind the granite lay a family standoff. Grove had eagerly let Seely build the hotel, but he refused to sell it to him. Instead he leased it to his son-in-law to manage. Seely was ambitious in his own right: in 1917 he bought Biltmore Estate Industries from Edith Vanderbilt and ran it alongside the inn. The arrangement lasted until 1927, the year Grove died and the year Seely lost his legal bid to own the hotel. Grove left the inn to his second wife, Gertrude, his son, Eddie, and his daughter, Evelyn. Though Evelyn was Seely's wife, Grove made no concessions to him, and the hotel passed into what one advertisement cheerfully called "more liberal management."

## Presidents, Diplomats and a Novelist Adrift

Taft stayed in 1929 and 1930, and presidents and future presidents kept coming, among them Franklin D. Roosevelt, Dwight D. Eisenhower, Richard Nixon, and Barack Obama, who stayed as a candidate in 2008 and again in 2010. During World War II the inn took stranger guests, serving first as an internment center for Axis diplomats. In the mid-1930s, F. Scott Fitzgerald, author of The Great Gatsby, came to Asheville with his wife, Zelda, a painter of note who received formal care for her mental health there. Scott lived at the inn for about a year, contending with his own mental health struggles, heavy drinking and tuberculosis. The building later entered fiction. In Cormac McCarthy's 1979 novel Suttree, the title character spends four days in "a cool room high in the old rough pile of rocks." In 1956 the Supreme Court signed a letter of understanding naming the inn as its refuge in the event of a nuclear attack on Washington, and the inn says the agreement is still in force.

## A Dog in the Baby Carriage

In 1955 the inn became part of Sammons Enterprises. Charles A. Sammons put $100,000 into restoration and expanded the resort in 1958 and 1963, while his wife, Elaine, occasionally smuggled her dog into the hotel in a baby carriage. Between 1982 and 1988, $65 million went into a 50,000-square-foot sports center and the Sammons and Vanderbilt wings. KSL Resorts bought the property in 2012 for a reported $120 million, and Omni Hotels & Resorts acquired it in 2013 and began a $25 million restoration. Today the inn spreads across 140 acres, with 513 guest rooms, 10 restaurants and bars, and a subterranean spa. Since the early 1990s its halls have filled most holiday seasons with entries in the National Gingerbread House Competition. In 2024, Hurricane Helene's flooding across Asheville forced the resort to close for about a month and a half, and it welcomed guests back to the main inn on November 15; the gingerbread competition was canceled that year and returned in 2025.

## Flight Context

The Grove Park Inn sits at about 35.62°N, 82.54°W on the western-facing slope of Sunset Mountain on the north side of Asheville, North Carolina, overlooking the city and the surrounding Blue Ridge. From 4,500-5,500 feet MSL, look for the big granite building and its golf course on the mountainside, with downtown Asheville just to the south. Asheville Regional Airport (KAVL) is about 11 nm away and is the natural approach point; Hendersonville Airport (FAA identifier 0A7, about 20 nm) is the nearest smaller field. Mountain terrain surrounds the city, so watch for low cloud over the ridges.
