# Bat Cave, North Carolina

A small crossroads community in the Hickory Nut Gorge, named for a real and rarely seen granite fissure cave, whose road signs have proved almost too tempting for souvenir hunters.

**Location:** 35.4514, -82.2869

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Henderson County, North Carolina, Unincorporated communities in North Carolina, Asheville metropolitan area

## Introduction

In the early 1990s, with the Batman movies filling theaters, the road signs around one small North Carolina community kept disappearing. Bat Cave is a regular victim of sign theft, but the movies made it worse. The name shows up on lists of unusual place names, and it is easy to see why people want a piece of it. Yet this is no gimmick dreamed up for tourists. The community takes its name from a real cave on the mountain above it, one the public is not allowed to enter.

## The Cave in the Mountain

The cave lies on Bluerock Mountain, also known as Bat Cave Mountain, which rises to 2,835 feet on the line between Henderson and Rutherford counties. It is the largest known augen gneiss granite fissure cave in North America, and it has been a draw for a long time. Tourists were already coming to see the cave by carriage around 1900, and the visits continued until World War I. Today the 186-acre Bat Cave Preserve protects the endangered Indiana bat along with several other species of bats that live in the cave. The cave is a protected area, closed to the public, and the Rutherford County side of the mountain lies within Chimney Rock State Park. The bats get the cave. Visitors get the name.

## Turkeys on the Drovers' Road

Long before tourists came through, livestock did. The first connection to the area was a drovers' road, said to date to 1798, which gave herders a natural route into the interior of the Blue Ridge Mountains. It ran from Fairview, near Asheville, down through the gorge to Bat Cave, roughly along the line of today's US 74A. The traffic on it would surprise a modern driver: according to a local history, farmers drove cattle, hogs, mules and even turkeys down to the foothills along it from the early 1800s until 1885. The post office came later and took a winding path of its own. It reportedly first opened in 1843 as the Chimney Rock Post Office, at a spot where Lake Lure is today, and went through a series of closures before it was moved and reopened in 1879 under the name Bat Cave.

## Where Three Roads Meet

Bat Cave sits along the banks of the Broad River inside the Hickory Nut Gorge, and geography has made it a junction. Three highways merge here: US 64 coming in from Hendersonville, US 74A from Asheville and NC 9 from Black Mountain. Together they become the Lake Lure Highway, which runs on to the southeast toward Chimney Rock and Lake Lure. The community itself is small, known for its post office, shops and inns. Living beside a mountain river has its costs. The flood of 1916 destroyed the post office and much of the community, and Bat Cave had to rebuild. It faced significant damage again from Hurricane Helene in 2024.

## A Country Doctor

One notable 20th-century arrival was a physician. In 1948 George Bond established the Valley Clinic and Hospital here, and five years later the community recognized him as "Doctor of the Year." Bond is remembered among Bat Cave's notable people as a United States Navy physician in undersea and hyperbaric medicine, a field about as far from a mountain gorge as medicine gets. The community built up its own institutions too; in the 1970s residents organized the Bat Cave Volunteer Fire Department. It remains an unincorporated place, part of the Asheville metropolitan area on paper, but still very much a gorge community defined by its river, its roads and the mountain overhead.

## Flight Context

Bat Cave sits at 35.4514°N, 82.2869°W in the Hickory Nut Gorge of Henderson County, North Carolina, on the Broad River. From the air, follow the gorge and the highway that runs through it: US 64, US 74A and NC 9 merge here and continue southeast as the Lake Lure Highway toward Chimney Rock and Lake Lure. Bluerock Mountain, home of the protected cave, rises to 2,835 feet just south-southeast of the community. The surrounding Blue Ridge terrain climbs well above the gorge floor, so a viewing altitude of about 5,500-6,000 feet MSL is a sensible minimum. Asheville Regional Airport (KAVL) is about 12.5 nm west, and Hendersonville Airport (FAA identifier 0A7) is about 11 nm southwest. Expect gusty, turbulent air in the gorge on windy days and valley fog on calm mornings.
