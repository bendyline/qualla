# Linville Falls

At the head of the Linville Gorge on the Blue Ridge Parkway, the Linville River spirals through twin upper falls and a narrow canyon before thundering 45 feet into a deep plunge basin.

**Location:** 35.9496, -81.9268

**Categories:** Waterfalls of North Carolina, Blue Ridge Parkway, Protected areas of Burke County, North Carolina, Landforms of Burke County, North Carolina

## Introduction

Linville Falls may not always have looked the way it does now. By some accounts the main plunge was once split into two sections of equal height, until flooding brought the upper section down on top of the lower, turning the lower falls into a considerably longer drop. Today the Linville River comes down in several distinct steps: a twin set of upper falls, a run through a small gorge, and a final high-volume plunge of 45 feet. Linville Falls marks the beginning of the Linville Gorge.

## River of Many Cliffs

Below the falls, the Linville River carves the Linville Gorge, continuing on until it ends near Lake James. In the Cherokee language the river is called Ee-see-oh, often translated as "river of cliffs," and the gorge earns the name. The river runs roughly 1,400 feet below the ridge, and the surrounding Linville Gorge Wilderness, sometimes called the Grand Canyon of North Carolina, covers nearly 12,000 acres. When the Wilderness Act became law in 1964, it was among the first areas in the country formally designated as wilderness.

The falls themselves came into public hands through a gift. In 1952 John D. Rockefeller Jr. donated them to the National Park Service, having provided about $100,000 in 1951 to buy the land, including roughly 1,100 acres around the falls and part of the gorge. The seller was Giulia Luginbuhl of Des Moines, Iowa, whose father, F. W. Hossfeld of Morganton, North Carolina, had purchased the property around 1900.

## Four Ways to See the Water

The falls now belong to the Blue Ridge Parkway, whose trails, rated moderate to strenuous, lead to overlooks on both sides of the river. Hurricane Helene destroyed the Linville Falls visitor center in September 2024 and some trails remain closed, so check the Parkway's alerts before you go. The half-mile Upper Falls trail reaches the top of the falls, where visitors can watch the small twin upper falls and the water spiraling through a narrow canyon toward the main drop. The Erwin's View trail continues to more overlooks, including Chimney View at 0.7 miles and Erwin's View at 0.8 miles.

The half-mile Plunge Basin Trail leads to the Plunge Basin Overlook, which views the falls from the other side of the river. For the closest approach, the 0.7-mile Gorge Trail branches off it and descends to an area near the foot of the falls.

## Respecting the Plunge

The power that makes Linville Falls impressive also makes it dangerous. Swimming is unsafe at the upper falls and is prohibited throughout Blue Ridge Parkway waters, and boats and floating devices are banned on the Linville River. In August 2010 the professional kayaker Pat Keller made what Blue Ridge Outdoors reported as the first descent of the upper drop, a run that nearly ended in disaster; the falls are no place for anyone else to follow.

There are gentler waterfalls close by. Duggers Creek Falls, in the Linville Falls recreation area, has been reached by a short trail that starts at the back right corner of the visitor center's paved parking lot, with the small falls appearing on the right from a footbridge over the creek; check the Parkway for current trail status after the storm damage. Toms Creek Falls and Crabtree Falls are also in the area, for anyone who wants to make a day of chasing water through the Blue Ridge.

## Flight Context

Linville Falls is at 35.9496°N, 81.9268°W on the Linville River in Burke County, North Carolina, just off the Blue Ridge Parkway at the head of the Linville Gorge. From the air, follow the gorge's deep, cliff-lined cut upstream to where it begins at the falls; the gorge floor lies about 1,400 ft below the rims, and the terrain around it is rugged high country, so stay at least 2,000 ft above the rims and respect the wilderness area below. Nearby airports: Avery County Airport-Morrison Field, Spruce Pine (FAA identifier 7A8, no ICAO code), about 3.5 nm west; Foothills Regional Airport, Morganton (KMRN), about 17 nm east-southeast; and Hickory Regional Airport (KHKY), about 29 nm east-southeast. Mountain wave and turbulence are common over the gorge in strong westerly winds, and valley fog often fills it on cool mornings.
