# House of Cash

For a quarter century a former dinner theater in Hendersonville, Tennessee, served Johnny and June Carter Cash as museum, office and record-label home, and it held tapes nobody had catalogued.

**Location:** 36.3252, -86.5666

**Categories:** Johnny Cash, Museums in Sumner County, Tennessee, Country music museums, American independent record labels, Biographical museums in Tennessee

## Introduction

Among the things left at the House of Cash were tapes, many of them never catalogued. Gregg Geller went through them, in material that came to be called 'the Hendersonville tapes,' and in 2006 released Personal File, an album of solo recordings Johnny Cash had made between 1973 and 1982. It was a late echo from a building that had spent about a quarter century as Cash's museum, office and label headquarters. The House of Cash stood at 700 East Main Street in Hendersonville, Tennessee, a road now called Johnny Cash Parkway, and for those years it was where Cash's music and business came together.

## A Dinner Theater Changes Its Tune

The building did not start out with Cash in mind. It was built in 1960 as the Plantation Dinner Theatre, a two-story building of 15,000 square feet. In 1970 Johnny Cash and June Carter Cash bought it, and it became both the headquarters of House of Cash Inc. and a museum devoted to his life and work. About 30 people worked there. Around 1979 or 1980 the company expanded in an unusual way: it bought the Amqui Train Station in nearby Madison, Tennessee, and moved it to the property. For the next decade June Carter ran the old depot as an antique shop, the Amqui Antique Store.

## A Family on Record

The House of Cash was also the base of the family's record label, House of Cash Records, which released albums and many promotional singles. Its catalog reads like a family tree. Alongside Johnny Cash himself, it published June Carter Cash, Anita Carter, John Carter Cash, Carlene Carter, Rosanne Cash, Laura Weber Cash, the Carter Family, Tommy Cash and the Cash Crew Band. Friends of the family appeared on it too, among them Ernest Tubb, Hank Williams Jr., Carl Perkins, Porter Wagoner, Jean Shepard, Jerry Lee Lewis and the Statler Brothers. The label stayed headquartered in the building until it closed. The uncatalogued tapes later went to Geller, whose work turned uncatalogued reels into a record released three years after Cash's death.

## The House on Old Hickory Lake

Home was close by. From 1968 until Johnny Cash's death in 2003, he and June lived at 200 Caudill Drive in Hendersonville, in a 14,000-square-foot house overlooking Old Hickory Lake. His parents, Ray and Carrie, lived across the road. In 1979 Cash built a log cabin on the property as a place to rest and write songs, and later turned it into a recording space, Cash Cabin Studio. The lakeside house had a sad final chapter. Barry Gibb bought the estate in the mid-2000s, and the house burned down during renovations in 2007. Gibb and his wife chose to preserve what remained and build a new home rather than continue the restoration, and they sold the property in 2014. It has since changed hands again, and a later buying couple said they planned to build a home on the land.

## Offices Where the Exhibits Were

The museum had closed by 1995. In 2004 HALO Realty and HALO Builders bought the headquarters, museum and store, and the building was turned into 15,000 square feet of office space. HALO donated the Amqui Train Station back to Madison, and its founder, Danny Hale, used the former museum as a base for projects in Hendersonville that included land for the public library, the Indian Lake Village retail development and a new fire hall built on property Cash had once owned. A new and unrelated Johnny Cash Museum opened in Nashville in 2013. The old building's story lives on in print as well: in 2015, John Carter Cash published a biography of his father titled House of Cash: The Legacies of My Father, Johnny Cash.

## Flight Context

The former House of Cash is at approximately 36.3252 N, 86.5667 W, on Johnny Cash Parkway in Hendersonville, about 14 nm northeast of downtown Nashville. From 1,500-2,500 ft AGL, the broad arms of Old Hickory Lake just to the south are the unmistakable landmark; the Cash family home site on Caudill Drive overlooked the lake nearby. Nearest airports: Music City Executive Airport, Gallatin (KXNX), about 8 nm east-northeast, and Nashville International Airport (KBNA), about 13 nm south-southwest. Nashville's controlled airspace begins to the south, so coordinate with approach control when heading toward the city. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common; morning light on the lake is often best.
