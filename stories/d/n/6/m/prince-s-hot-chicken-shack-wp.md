# Prince's Hot Chicken Shack

Founded by Thornton Prince (dated to 1945 in most accounts, to the mid-1930s by the family and the James Beard Foundation) and run since 1980 by his great-niece André Prince Jeffries, Prince's Hot Chicken Shack is credited with popularizing Nashville hot chicken, a dish its family legend says began as revenge.

## Introduction

Nobody can prove it, but the story goes that Nashville hot chicken began as revenge. James Thornton Prince, the legend says, was something of a womanizer, and after one particularly late night a girlfriend served him a fried chicken breakfast loaded with hot pepper to teach him a lesson. The lesson backfired. Prince loved the taste so much that he and his brothers worked up their own recipe and opened a restaurant around it - in 1945 by most accounts, though Jeffries and the James Beard Foundation place its start in the mid-1930s. Prince's Hot Chicken Shack is now credited with popularizing the dish and inspiring other restaurants to serve their own versions.

## From Jefferson Street to Hell's Half Acre

The restaurant began as the Bar-B-Que Chicken Shack, at 28th Avenue and Jefferson Street. It later moved downtown into the district called Hell's Half Acre, close to the Ryman Auditorium, then the home of the Grand Ole Opry. In its heyday, Opry stars reportedly headed there after their performances. That popularity collided with the segregation of the era. With the Opry's stars among its fans, the restaurant ended up running segregation in reverse: Black customers ordered at the front, while white guests were served in a separate room at the back of the building, which by some accounts they reached by walking through the main dining room and the kitchen. In the late 1980s the shack moved again, to Ewing Drive.

## A Family Recipe Changes Hands

Prince's has stayed in the family. When Thornton Prince died, his brother Will took over, and after Will's death his wife, Maude, ran the business. In 1980 Maude chose Thornton's great-niece, André Prince Jeffries, to take over. Jeffries dropped the barbecue from the name, and Prince's Hot Chicken Shack carried on under her. The recognition piled up. Gourmet magazine named it one of Nashville's four "don't-miss dining experiences," and in 2013 the James Beard Foundation named Prince's an America's Classic.

## Heat on Television

Television spread the word further. The Travel Channel's Food Paradise featured Prince's in a 2013 episode, and the restaurant also appeared on Man v. Food Nation in 2011 and Bizarre Foods America in 2014. For a dish supposedly born out of a scorned lover's spite, it was a remarkable rise, with Prince's credited as the place that popularized hot chicken and inspired other restaurants to offer it too.

## Fire on Ewing Drive

In December 2018, a vehicle crashed into the strip mall that housed the Ewing Drive Prince's, setting a neighboring business on fire. The restaurant reportedly escaped the worst of the fire, but the building was shut and Jeffries at first expected to reopen within two weeks. Instead, repairs and the landlord's delays dragged on, and more than six months later she announced that the location would close permanently. The fire did not end the business. Today Prince's operates several locations in the Nashville area, among them one at 5814 Nolensville Pike, still serving the chicken that Thornton Prince's family has been making for generations.
