# Jubilee Hall (Fisk University)

Paid for by the Fisk Jubilee Singers' European tour of the 1870s, Fisk University's Gothic Revival Jubilee Hall was the school's first permanent building and still houses students today.

**Location:** 36.1689, -86.8047

**Categories:** National Historic Landmarks in Tennessee, Buildings and structures in Nashville, Tennessee, Fisk University, University and college buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in Tennessee, National Register of Historic Places in Nashville, Tennessee

## Introduction

Jubilee Hall was paid for with songs. In the 1870s, Fisk University, a young school for African-American students in Nashville, was in financial trouble, and it was the Fisk Jubilee Singers, touring Europe in 1873, who raised the money to build it. The Gothic Revival hall they funded was completed in 1876 and became Fisk's first permanent building. It is still one of the oldest buildings on campus, and inside hangs a floor-to-ceiling portrait of the original singers, a gift from Queen Victoria.

## A College With a Different Aim

Fisk was founded in 1865, in the immediate aftermath of the Civil War, through the efforts of the antislavery American Missionary Association, and it was named for Clinton B. Fisk. From the outset it set out to do something different from many other schools of its day: to give its African-American students a full liberal arts education. Fisk was one of the first historically Black colleges and universities established after the war, but ambition alone did not pay the bills. Within a few years the school was struggling financially, and it needed both money and a permanent home.

## Singing a Building Into Being

The answer came from the school's own singers. The Fisk Jubilee Singers took their music across the Atlantic on their first European tour in 1873, and the proceeds of their concert tours in those years paid for the new building. Construction ran from 1873 to 1876 to designs by New York architect Stephen D. Hatch. During that same 1873 tour, the singers received a remarkable gift: Queen Victoria gave the university a floor-to-ceiling portrait of the original Jubilee Singers, which still hangs in the hall that shares their name. In 2017 the Tennessee Historical Commission installed a marker honoring the Fisk Jubilee Singers.

## A Tower of Gothic Spires

Jubilee Hall stands on the northern side of the Fisk campus, on a loop drive at the northern end of 17th Avenue North at Meharry Boulevard. It is a large, L-shaped masonry building six stories tall, capped by a hip roof dotted with gabled dormers and projections. The main facade divides into a central section and slightly projecting end sections, and at the junction of the center and right sections a tower climbs to an open octagonal belfry and steeple, all adorned with Gothic spires. Windows sit singly or in pairs in segmented-arch openings under arched stone lintels, and decorative brick corbelling runs along the main roof cornice. It is a good local example of the Gothic Revival style.

## Still Home to Students

In 1974 Jubilee Hall was designated a National Historic Landmark, in recognition of Fisk's place as one of the first historically Black colleges and universities established after the Civil War. The building has never become a museum piece. Its exterior is little changed from the 1870s, but its interior has been completely modernized, and it serves today as an undergraduate residence hall. Students live under the same spires the Jubilee Singers' voices paid for, in a building that has anchored Fisk's campus since 1876.
