# Lane Motor Museum

In a former Nashville bread bakery, Lane Motor Museum shows off a collection of more than 500 mostly European cars, heavy on the odd, the tiny and the one-of-a-kind, including more than 20 Czech-built Tatras.

**Location:** 36.1403, -86.7342

**Categories:** Museums in Nashville, Tennessee, Culture of Nashville, Tennessee, Automobile museums in Tennessee, Museums established in 2002, 2002 establishments in Tennessee

## Introduction

For more than four decades, the big building on Murfreesboro Pike in Nashville made bread. Today its floors are lined with propeller-driven cars, amphibious vehicles, microcars and three-wheelers, and the former bakery holds an automobile collection of more than 500 vehicles, mostly European, with a strong taste for the unusual.

## From Loaves to Tatras

The building began life as a bakery. Construction started in 1951, and it served as the American Bread Company's bakery, known to the museum as the former Sunbeam Bakery, until 1994. In 2002 Jeff Lane established Lane Motor Museum there as a nonprofit, starting with his personal collection of 70 to 80 vehicles, and the museum unveiled its collection to the public in October 2003. The old bakery offers plenty of room: 132,000 square feet in all, with about 40,000 square feet of open space on the main floor.

## Odd Wheels

The collection is built around the unusual. European, American and Asian cars of distinctive design share space with propeller-driven vehicles, microcars, three-wheeled cars, amphibious vehicles, alternative-fuel vehicles, military vehicles, competition cars, one-off vehicles and prototypes. There is a particular soft spot for Tatra, with more than 20 examples of the Czech marque in the collection. Automobile art and memorabilia round out the exhibits. The effect is less a parade of polished status symbols than a tour of automotive experiments and roads not taken.

## Down in the Vault

Only a fraction of the collection is on the main floor at any given time. The rest waits in the basement of the old bakery, a storage area known as the Vault. On most weekends the museum offers tours down there for an extra fee, letting visitors walk among the cars that are not currently on display. It offers a glimpse of the collection's full scale, stored beneath the galleries.

## Behind the Wheel

Lane Motor Museum does not treat its cars purely as objects to be looked at. Since 2010 it has held an annual fundraiser, the Rally for The Lane, in which participants get to drive one of the museum's cars. The museum has also kept growing: in 2026 it opened what it calls the largest expansion in its history, converting its former delivery van parking garage into 25,000 square feet of new gallery space with room for about 60 more vehicles. What started with some 70 to 80 cars in a former bread bakery has become a collection of more than 500, still housed in the building on Murfreesboro Pike.
