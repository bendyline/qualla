# George Dickel

In a Tennessee hollow near Normandy, the Cascade Hollow Distillery makes George Dickel, a whisky spelled the Scottish way, chilled before charcoal mellowing, and revived in the 1950s as a small rival to the state's giant.

**Location:** 35.4386, -86.2447

**Categories:** Tennessee whiskey, Distilleries in Tennessee, Diageo brands, Cuisine of Tennessee, 1964 establishments in Tennessee

## Introduction

In 2013, Jack Daniel's sold 11.5 million cases of whiskey. The second-largest distillery in Tennessee sold 130,000. That smaller operation, tucked into Cascade Hollow near Normandy in Coffee County, makes George Dickel, and its modern life began with that very rivalry. In 1956 the liquor company Schenley tried to buy Jack Daniel's and was turned down. Schenley decided instead to return one of its own brands to its roots and compete with Jack Daniel's head to head. The brand it chose had a story that stretched back to a German immigrant, a Nashville liquor store and a distiller who cooled his mash at night.

## A Merchant From Germany

George A. Dickel was born in Germany in 1818 and emigrated to the United States in 1844. In the 1850s he was a Nashville merchant, and in 1861 he began selling liquor. After the Civil War he ran a liquor store on South College Street. Partners joined him over the years: Meier Saltzkotter, who had worked for him as a superintendent, and in 1881 his brother-in-law Victor Emmanuel Shwab, who had started with the company as a bookkeeper. By the late 1870s, George A. Dickel and Company was selling whiskey made at Cascade Hollow by John F. Brown and F.E. Cunningham. Dickel's labels still carry the Scottish spelling, whisky, without the e. According to the company, that is because Dickel believed his product was as smooth and high in quality as the best Scotch.

## Mellow as Moonlight

The partnership in the hollow kept changing. In 1879 Matthew Sims, a local businessman, bought Brown's share, and in 1883 another local businessman, McLin Davis, joined in. Davis became the operation's distiller and is credited with the whisky's recipe. By the early 1890s, Cascade Whisky was one of the more popular brands in the region, and its label promised that it was "Mellow as Moonlight," a phrase said to be rooted in Davis's habit of cooling the mash at night. Cold remains part of the process. Today's mash is 84 percent corn, 8 percent rye and 8 percent malted barley. The new spirit is chilled to 40 degrees Fahrenheit and mellowed for several days in vats of charcoal, the distillery's own take on the Lincoln County process, before it goes into barrels. Those barrels rest in warehouses only one story high, which keeps aging more even from barrel to barrel.

## Rebuilt to Take on a Giant

After its rebuff at Jack Daniel's, Schenley's comeback depended on a change in the law. In 1958, once a local referendum made it legal to produce liquor in Coffee County again, Schenley's Ralph Dupps reconstructed the Cascade Hollow distillery, and the modern George Dickel brand was introduced in 1964. The distillery's later years were bumpier. It reopened in 2003, almost too late to prevent a shortage of Old No. 8 by 2007, and Diageo, which now owns the brand, filled the gap with a younger, three-year-old whisky called Old-Fashioned Cascade Hollow Batch Recipe. Once aged stocks rebounded, it was discontinued in 2013. Around 2014 a new bottling line went in at the distillery, which offers public tours and is part of the American Whiskey Trail. In autumn 2025, however, Diageo said it would temporarily pause distilling and barrel-filling at Cascade Hollow through June 2026, though the visitor center stayed open for tours and tastings.

## Distillers and Double Golds

Master Distiller John Lunn oversaw the distillery from 2005 until 2015, when he left to become master distiller at the Popcorn Sutton microdistillery. In March 2018 Nicole Austin took over as general manager and distiller of Cascade Hollow Distilling Co., after two years as commissioning engineer at Tullamore D.E.W.'s grain distillery in Ireland. The lineup ranges from the black-labeled Old No. 8 at 80 proof to the beige-labeled Superior No. 12 at 90 proof. In 2014 came No. 1 Foundation Recipe, an unaged version of the standard high-corn mash sold as corn whisky. One product is made far from the hollow: George Dickel Rye, introduced in 2012, was distilled and aged under contract by MGP Ingredients in Lawrenceburg, Indiana, then charcoal-filtered and bottled in Plainfield, Illinois. At the 2015 San Francisco World Spirits Competition, No. 12 was named Best Tennessee Whiskey with a double gold medal, and the rye also took double gold.

## Flight Context

The Cascade Hollow Distillery lies at 35.4386°N, 86.2447°W near Normandy in Coffee County, Tennessee, a few miles north of Tullahoma. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet, look for Tullahoma's street grid and airport to the south and the wooded hollows of rolling farm country around the distillery. Tullahoma Regional Airport-William Northern Field (KTHA) is only about 3.5 nm to the south. Arnold Air Force Base (KAYX), a military field about 8 nm to the east-southeast, has its own airspace to respect, and Bomar Field-Shelbyville Municipal Airport (KSYI) is about 12 nm to the northwest. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common in Middle Tennessee.
