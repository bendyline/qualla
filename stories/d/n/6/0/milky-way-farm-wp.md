# Milky Way Farm

Candy maker Franklin C. Mars built a Tudor Revival estate in Giles County, Tennessee, named for his Milky Way bar; it bred a Kentucky Derby winner, survived decades of decline and a failed luxury subdivision, and is being restored today.

## Introduction

The workers who raised this estate out of the Giles County countryside in the early 1930s received free candy bars. Their employer could spare them. Franklin C. Mars had founded Mars Candies, and he named his Tennessee farm after the company's Milky Way bar. While it was being built it was the largest employer in the county, and about a decade after the purchase, a Milky Way Farm horse won the Kentucky Derby.

## Built on a Candy Bar

Franklin Mars and his second wife, Ethel V. Mars, bought the 2,800-acre property in 1930, shortly after the company opened a southern office in Nashville. They hired architect James F. Drake to design a Tudor Revival manor house along with facilities for breeding Thoroughbred horses and Hereford cattle. Construction peaked between 1931 and 1933, and with 800 workers on the job, Milky Way Farm became the largest employer in Giles County. Many of those workers lived on the farm with their families. Mars is reputed to have treated them generously. Beyond the candy bars, workers received small loans and other forms of assistance.

## Thirty-Five Rooms and a Forty-Foot Ceiling

The manor house was built for entertaining. Its 25,000 square feet hold 35 rooms, including 21 bedrooms and a dozen or more bathrooms, and many of the rooms are unusually large. The living room has exposed beams and a 40-foot ceiling, and the Mars family's dining table is reputed to have been the largest private dining table in Tennessee. Outside, landscaped grounds planted with magnolia trees surrounded the house, along with an outdoor swimming pool and tennis courts. Beyond the lawns spread a working estate of 70 cottages, 30 barns and a horse-racing track. Most of the farm buildings were raised from local limestone in a style that has been described as Spanish-inspired, and one of the surviving barns is octagonal.

## Herefords, Thoroughbreds and a Derby

Franklin Mars died in 1934, too soon to see the farming operations succeed. His widow, Ethel, oversaw the livestock program they had planned, and it flourished. The farm produced prize-winning Hereford cattle and Thoroughbreds that won on the racetrack from its first racing season in 1934. In 1936 the stable's winnings totaled $206,450, making Milky Way Farm the most successful owner of the season on the U.S. Thoroughbred circuit. The high point came in 1940, when the farm's Gallahadion won the Kentucky Derby. The Mars era ended soon after. In 1945, with Ethel Mars in declining health, the farm was sold; she died that December.

## Rescued From the Subdivision Plan

In the decades that followed, the estate fell into disrepair. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on September 27, 1984, but listing did not guarantee its future. In 2007 the property sold for a reported $10 million, and the buyer announced a $400 million luxury community of 750 to 900 homes, complete with an equestrian club, horse track, polo field, golf course and riding trails. Instead, the property went into foreclosure in May 2009. Beginning in 2010, a father and daughter bought the farm back piece by piece until more than 1,100 acres of the original estate were reunited. With help from the Tennessee Land Trust, the front acreage was placed in a permanent agricultural easement, and the manor house, barns, roofs, roads and stone walls have each received attention since.

## Mud Runs on a Candy Baron's Estate

In 2018, by the farm's own account, the daughter and her sister bought the farm and took over its care. Keeping a landmark this size standing takes money, and Milky Way Farm earns it in varied ways: events, tours, weddings, corporate meetings, trail runs, farming, guided hunts and seasonal festivals all help pay for its operation and restoration. The grounds Franklin Mars laid out for Thoroughbreds and Herefords have hosted the Warrior Dash, Tough Mudder, the Green Beret Challenge and the Spartan Race, with competitors slogging across the land of a farm that once won the Kentucky Derby. Work continues on the Show Barn, the manor house, the roads and the historic race track.
