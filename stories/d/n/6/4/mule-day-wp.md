# Mule Day

What began around 1840 as a one-day meeting of mule breeders in Columbia, Tennessee, has grown into a spring festival of parades, mule-driving contests and tall tales that organizers say draws more than 200,000 people.

## Introduction

Columbia, Tennessee, calls itself the "Mule Capital" of the world, and every spring it sets out to prove it. Mule Day began around 1840 as "Breeder's Day," a gathering of mule breeders. Today organizers say more than 200,000 people crowd into town for a festival held over several days in early April, where mules still take center stage but share it with bluegrass bands, craft vendors, line dancers and a contest devoted entirely to lying.

## A Monday for Breeders

The celebration started small and practical. Breeder's Day was a single-day livestock show and mule market, held on the first Monday in April, when breeders brought their animals to Columbia to show and to sell. The event took root, and Mule Day has been a Columbia tradition ever since the 1840s. The animals themselves never left the program. Contests still pick a "best of breed" and test a "working mule," echoes of the festival's beginnings as a showcase for breeders and their stock, and mule-driving contests put the animals through their paces in front of the crowds.

## Lost Years and a Revival

The tradition has not run without interruption. The celebration was called off in 1942, shortly after the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor brought the United States into the Second World War. According to the festival's own history, the celebration paused during World War II and later faded in the 1950s, until the Maury County Bridle & Saddle Club, a non-profit formed in 1973, brought it back in 1974 as a "Renaissance Year" with a Queen's Contest, a mule show and the return of the Saturday parade. More recently, Mule Day was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 because of the Covid-19 pandemic.

## Mule Pulls and Tall Tales

Today the program is crowded. Recent schedules have run from a pre-festival wagon train to riding, driving and gaited mule shows, jackpot mule races, barrel races, an old-fashioned log pull and mule pulling, with a parade on Saturday. Bluegrass and other live music, a line-dancing night and a daily craft fair run alongside the animal events, so a visitor can drift from the mule contests to the music and on to the crafts tables in a single afternoon. Then there is the Liar's Contest, a rural-themed storytelling competition, which may be the only event on the schedule where nobody expects the whole truth.

## Funnel Cakes and Imitators

Hungry visitors work their way through barbecue, roasted corn, home-made pies and funnel cakes, and organizers claim more than 200,000 of them come each year (a 2022 local news report quoted a Mule Day spokesperson at about 100,000), a long way from a one-day market on the first Monday in April. Columbia is not the only town to honor the mule. Other places have since started mule days of their own: Benson, North Carolina, in 1950; Bishop, California, in 1970; Calvary, Georgia, in 1973; Winfield, Alabama, in 1975; and Ider, Alabama, in 1987. Every one of them came more than a century after Columbia's breeders first gathered in the 1840s.
