# Stones River National Battlefield

Outside Murfreesboro, a park of more than 500 acres preserves the ground of one of the Civil War's costliest battles, along with the oldest Civil War monument still standing where it was built.

**Location:** 35.8808, -86.4350

**Categories:** Battlefields of the western theater of the American Civil War, National Park Service areas in Tennessee, Cemeteries on the National Register of Historic Places in Tennessee, American Civil War museums in Tennessee, Murfreesboro, Tennessee

## Introduction

In the summer of 1863, with the war far from over, men of Hazen's Brigade were sent back to the Round Forest outside Murfreesboro, Tennessee, to build a monument to their own dead. They raised a stone shaft in the middle of the brigade's cemetery and dedicated it to the soldiers who fell there on December 31, 1862: 'Their faces towards heaven, their feet to the foe.' It is the oldest surviving Civil War monument still standing in its original location. Around it now lies Stones River National Battlefield, a park of more than 500 acres along the Stones River three miles northwest of Murfreesboro and twenty-eight miles southeast of Nashville.

## Across the New Year

The Battle of Stones River was fought from December 31, 1862, to January 2, 1863, between Union Major General William S. Rosecrans's Army of the Cumberland and Confederate General Braxton Bragg's Army of Tennessee. It ended in a strategic Union victory, bought at a terrible price. Of the war's major battles, Stones River had the highest percentage of casualties on both sides. On January 2, massed Union artillery broke a Confederate assault on Union troops across the river, a moment later marked by a monument on the field. President Abraham Lincoln understood what the result meant. 'You gave us a hard-earned victory,' he wrote to Rosecrans, 'which had there been a defeat instead, the nation could scarcely have lived over.'

## Stones That Remember

The Hazen Brigade Monument, just outside the national cemetery proper, carries its distinction with a footnote. The 32nd Indiana Monument, now at Cave Hill National Cemetery in Louisville, Kentucky, is a year older, and an even earlier monument raised after the First Battle of Manassas in Virginia no longer survives. Hazen's shaft, still on the ground it honors, is the oldest standing where it was first placed. Veterans of the Army of the Cumberland added their own tribute, the U.S. Regular Brigade Civil War Monument. Decades later a railroad added another. In 1906 the Nashville, Chattanooga and St. Louis Railway erected a 31-foot obelisk at the position of the massed Union guns of January 2, 1863.

## Gathering the Fallen

Stones River National Cemetery was established on March 29, 1864, by order of Major General George H. Thomas. Under the supervision of Chaplain William Earnshaw, soldiers of the 111th Regiment United States Colored Troops took on the grim work of disinterring the dead from the battlefields of Stones River, Murfreesboro, Franklin, Shelbyville, Tullahoma and Cowan. Reburials began in October 1865 and took nearly a year. In November 1867, the bodies of 1,360 Union soldiers were moved here from Rose Hill Cemetery in Columbia, Tennessee, where a national cemetery had once been planned. Today the cemetery holds more than 7,000 interments, including about 6,100 Union soldiers, thousands of them unidentified. Most Confederate dead were taken to their hometowns or the nearest southern community; others went into a mass grave south of town and were later moved to another, Confederate Circle in Murfreesboro's Evergreen Cemetery.

## Tickets to the Battlefield

Preservation began with private citizens and a railroad. The Stones River National Battlefield and Memorial Association, led by Union and Confederate veterans and local leaders, was chartered on April 28, 1896, after the creation of Chickamauga and Chattanooga National Military Park stirred interest in saving Civil War battlefields. By June 1897 it had reportedly secured options on 3,400 acres, and its members put up wooden signs to mark key places. In 1912 the association asked Congress to 'establish an accurate system of markers,' but the measure failed, partly because former congressman Charles H. Grosvenor testified that the landmarks had been 'entirely obliterated.' Meanwhile the Nashville, Chattanooga and St. Louis Railway promoted the battlefield to boost passenger traffic, encouraging veterans' reunions and buying parts of the field. A 1927 act of Congress finally authorized a national military park under the War Department, and land acquisition ran from 1928 to 1934. The cemetery passed to the National Park Service on August 10, 1933.

## A Fraction of the Field

The battle sprawled across more than 3,000 acres, and the park preserves only a fraction of that ground. It has grown in pieces. In 1992 the City of Murfreesboro donated an intact segment of Fortress Rosecrans, which the National Park Service calls the largest earthen fortification of its kind built during the Civil War. The American Battlefield Trust and its members have saved more than 106 acres of the battlefield, some of which has been folded into the park. Walking the grounds today, visitors pass from open fields to the quiet rows of the national cemetery, where thousands of soldiers, many of them unknown, were gathered in part through the labor of the 111th U.S. Colored Troops.

## Flight Context

Stones River National Battlefield lies at approximately 35.8808 N, 86.4350 W, on the northwest edge of Murfreesboro and about 28 miles southeast of Nashville. From 2,000-2,500 ft AGL, follow the Stones River as it winds past the park's open fields and woods; the national cemetery's orderly rows and the city of Murfreesboro just to the southeast help fix the position. Murfreesboro Municipal Airport (KMBT) is only about 3 nm to the east, so watch for traffic in its pattern. Smyrna Airport (KMQY) lies about 9 nm north-northwest and Nashville International Airport (KBNA) about 19 nm northwest. Winter leaf-off gives the clearest view of the terrain; summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
