# Hundred Oaks Castle

A Winchester, Tennessee, plantation house turned into a castle by a governor's son after his travels in Europe, Hundred Oaks later sheltered Paulist Fathers for five decades, survived a 1990 fire, and was restored by a family in memory of their son.

**Location:** 35.1794, -86.1225

**Categories:** 1889 establishments in Tennessee, Buildings and structures in Franklin County, Tennessee, Former Christian monasteries, Houses completed in 1889, Winchester, Tennessee

## Introduction

Somebody had to count the trees. Legend says Arthur Marks did, reached one hundred oaks, and with his wife, Mary Hunt, gave the family estate in Winchester, Tennessee, the name it still carries. Arthur was not content with a name. After a diplomatic career that took him to Europe, he came home in 1889 with a head full of European castles and set about turning his family's house into one of its own.

## A Lawyer's Plantation

The story starts more modestly. Construction on the property began in 1830, when a two-story plantation house was built for Peter S. Decherd, a Virginian whose name the town of Decherd, Tennessee, carries. The house later passed to Albert S. Marks, who served as Governor of Tennessee from 1879 to 1881 and who lived on a plantation near Winchester. It was his son Arthur who would give the place both its name and its grandest ambitions. Arthur became a United States diplomat as a young man, a posting that let him travel in Europe, and it was there that his love of castles took hold.

## Castle Dreams, Cut Short

When Arthur returned home to Winchester in 1889, he wanted at once to transform the family house into a castle, and he began building on the plantation that year. The estate became Hundred Oaks Castle. The years that followed were hard ones for the family. Albert Marks died at the Maxwell House Hotel in Nashville on November 4, 1891. Arthur did not long survive him: he died of typhoid fever in 1892, only a few years after his return from Europe, leaving behind the castle he had imagined into being on the old Decherd property.

## Monks, a Restaurant and a Fire

Hundred Oaks then entered a long and varied second life. For roughly five decades, until the 1950s, it served as a monastery for the Paulist Fathers. After they left, the castle changed hands many times before the early 1990s. In the late 1970s it even became a museum and restaurant, and it later housed the Franklin County Adult Activity Center. Then, in 1990, fire damaged the building. For a house that had already been a plantation home, a governor's estate, a young diplomat's castle and a monastery, it looked as if the story might end there.

## Restored in Memory

It did not. In the mid-1990s (1996 by one account, 1997 by the castle's own history) a foundation of the Bramlett family bought Hundred Oaks Castle and restored it as a dedication to the family's late son. Over the years that followed, the Bramletts opened the castle for tours, letting visitors see what Arthur Marks's European dream had become in the Tennessee countryside. Tours were suspended during the COVID pandemic, and reports since conflict: one account says none have resumed, while at least one visitor listing describes guided tours organized by the family's foundation. Confirm with the castle before visiting; otherwise it is best appreciated from outside. Its name still recalls, by legend, Arthur Marks walking the grounds and counting his way to one hundred oaks.
