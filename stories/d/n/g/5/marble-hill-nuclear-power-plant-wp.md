# Marble Hill Nuclear Power Plant

Near Hanover, Indiana, the Marble Hill nuclear plant swallowed roughly $2.5 billion before it was abandoned half-finished in the 1980s, leaving high-voltage transmission towers, auctioned reactor parts and a long demolition.

**Location:** 38.6009, -85.4459

**Categories:** Cancelled nuclear power stations in the United States, Nuclear power plants in Indiana, Former nuclear power stations in the United States, Buildings and structures in Jefferson County, Indiana, Unfinished nuclear reactors, Nuclear power stations using pressurized water reactors, Former power stations in Indiana

## Introduction

For years, the towers stood in southern Indiana carrying nothing at all. They were guyed-V steel structures built for 765-kilovolt power lines, meant to carry electricity from a new nuclear plant near Hanover into the utility's grid. Construction on the lines began before the plant was finished and stopped before a single wire was strung. The plant they were built for, Marble Hill, never produced a watt. Construction stopped in January 1984, and that November the Public Service Company of Indiana's board formally voted to cancel the half-finished station after spending roughly $2.5 billion on it (accounts range from $2 billion to $2.8 billion), turning Marble Hill into one of the costliest unfinished projects in the history of American nuclear power.

## Two Domes on the Horizon

Marble Hill was designed around two signature containment buildings, each meant to house a 1,130-megawatt Westinghouse pressurized water reactor, with two cooling tower complexes just to the north. The station was a near architectural one-for-one copy of the Byron and Braidwood stations. Construction began in 1977 in Saluda Township, Jefferson County, and it got far enough that the lower portion of the reactor vessel for Unit 1 was already installed when work stopped. Behind the containment buildings stood a turbine building. It has since been demolished, but its multi-story concrete foundations were left standing, with what remains of the Unit 1 turbine still in place.

## A Plant Under Protest

Marble Hill was controversial long before it failed. In 1978 and 1979, the Ohio Valley environmental advocacy group the Paddlewheel Alliance staged two non-violent "occupations" of the company's property. The industry around it was shaken too. After the Three Mile Island accident near Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, in March 1979, more than 100 nuclear plants were cancelled across the country. As local support dwindled, Marble Hill's fate came to rest with Indiana's state government, and the utility sought state funding guarantees for its construction work in progress. In the end, the company announced it had to abandon the project because of an overwhelming increase in costs and a shortage of funds to finish it, and construction ended in 1984. When it was abandoned, it was described as the most expensive nuclear construction project ever given up, a devastating setback for a troubled industry.

## Selling the Pieces

What had been bought had to go somewhere. In October 1985, the utility, known as PSI and now Duke Energy, auctioned roughly $8 million worth of reactor hardware it had already purchased. In 1990 a company called Power Equipment Supply Co., or PESCO, was incorporated in Indiana to sell equipment and parts from the Marble Hill project, and it went on to broker and resell equipment for others too before PSI shut it down in early 1996. Even the empty towers eventually found work. American Electric Power bought them to replace structures on its Jefferson–Dumont line that had been destroyed in the early 1990s, when an ice storm caused a cascading line failure.

## Taking It Down

Demolition of the unfinished plant began on March 18, 2005. The site passed to an undisclosed Michigan-based company, with MCM Management Corp. contracted to do the work, and from 2008 the smaller Fuel Handling building between the two containment buildings was slowly torn away for scrap. In August 2010 one containment building was completely demolished, and the other had its reactor core removed. By May 2011 most structures were down or coming down, and as of December 2011 both containment domes were gone, with about two-thirds of the cooling stacks still standing and the control building under heavy demolition. Later conditions at the site are not documented in the sources we found. A monument to ambition had become a salvage yard.

## Flight Context

The Marble Hill site lies at 38.6009°N, 85.4459°W in Saluda Township, Jefferson County, near Hanover in southeastern Indiana. From 2,500 to 3,000 feet, look for the cleared industrial site and any remaining concrete foundations; reports from 2011 say demolition had removed the containment domes, so the plant no longer stands out as it once did; current conditions are unconfirmed.
