# Rose Island (amusement park)

Reached by steamboat or a swaying footbridge, Rose Island was an Ohio River pleasure ground of roller coasters, a hotel and a bear named Teddy Roosevelt until the 1937 flood buried it under ten feet of water.

**Location:** 38.4300, -85.6172

## Introduction

There was a black bear named Teddy Roosevelt. He lived at Rose Island, an amusement park on the Indiana shore of the Ohio River near Charlestown, where wolves lived in a pen and monkeys in a cage on the same grounds as a wooden roller coaster. Visitors arrived by steamboat or by crossing a wooden footbridge that swung 50 feet above the creek. For roughly fourteen years the park drew crowds from Louisville and beyond. Then the river rose in 1937, and Rose Island never reopened. Its ruins still sit in the woods on a rocky peninsula called the Devil's Backbone.

## Fern Grove

The peninsula was formed where Fourteen Mile Creek empties into the Ohio. In the 1880s it was a recreational area called Fern Grove, named for the many ferns that grew there, and it was used mostly as a church camp. The Louisville and Jeffersonville Ferry Company acquired the land and developed it with a practical goal: more people heading to the grove meant more people riding its ferries. As Fern Grove, the site thrived on church picnics and family outings, a quiet riverside retreat long before it had any rides.

## David Rose's Island

In 1923, David Rose bought the property, spent $250,000 transforming it and gave it his name. Rose Island covered 118 acres. He added an amusement park, a hotel and a swimming pool, along with a combined dance hall and roller skating rink. The rides included a wooden racing-derby coaster named the Devil's Backbone after the rock formation it stood on, and there was a Ferris wheel as well. Then there were the animals: wolves, monkeys and Teddy Roosevelt the bear. People reached the park either by driving to the footbridge, a wooden swinging span that swayed easily over the creek, or by steamboat. Getting there was part of the outing. Steamboats with names like the Steamer America, the City of Cincinnati and the Columbia carried passengers, and one of them, the Idlewild, would later become the Belle of Louisville. The boats moved at only 7 to 8 miles per hour, so the trip from Louisville took between 90 minutes and two hours, while a steamboat ticket from Madison cost 50 cents. For those in a hurry there were speedboats, such as the Vivianne III, that could quickly carry businessmen back to Louisville.

## Ten Feet of Water

The Great Depression hurt business, but it was the river that finished Rose Island. During the 1937 Flood, ten feet of water covered the park, and the damage was too great to rebuild in time for the 1937 season. The park never came back. Nature took over what was left. Fallen trees destroyed the old bridge, leaving only its supports, and most of the buildings vanished without a trace. The swimming pool, though draped in ivy, was still in excellent condition as late as 1980; it has since been filled in and covered with gravel. A few brick formations, the remains of a fountain and three pillars of the entryway still stand.

## Crossing Back Over

Today Rose Island is reached through Charlestown State Park, an Indiana state park that opened in 1996 on land that was once part of the Indiana Army Ammunition Plant. The park's trails overlook Fourteen Mile Creek, noted as one of the oldest unglaciated stream valleys in the state. In September 2011, the historic Portersville Bridge was relocated and rebuilt across the creek to carry visitors to the island, and park staff lead scheduled guided tours of the ruins during the season (the state's 2026 listings run into late October), where visitors can walk among the pillars and brickwork of a playground the river took back.

## Flight Context

Rose Island lies at 38.43°N, 85.6172°W on the Indiana shore of the Ohio River near Charlestown, where Fourteen Mile Creek meets the river. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet, look for the creek's mouth and the wooded peninsula beside it; the ruins themselves are small and hidden under trees, so the river and creek confluence are the best guide. Clark Regional Airport in Jeffersonville, Indiana (FAA code JVY) is about 7 nm southwest, and Bowman Field (KLOU) in Louisville about 12 nm south across the river.
