# Calumet Farm

Built on baking-powder money, Lexington's Calumet Farm has bred ten Kentucky Derby winners and two Triple Crown champions, survived scandal and near-liquidation, and still races under its famous name.

**Location:** 38.0479, -84.5771

## Introduction

The money came from baking powder. William Monroe Wright, founding owner of the Calumet Baking Powder Company, established Calumet Farm in Lexington, Kentucky, in 1924, and what began as a harness-racing venture became one of the most successful stables in the history of Thoroughbred racing. Calumet has bred ten Kentucky Derby winners, more than any other farm, along with two Triple Crown champions; it raced eight of those Derby winners itself. Today the farm spreads across 762 acres in the heart of the Bluegrass, a region well known for horse breeding, a short distance from Lexington's Blue Grass Airport.

## Trotters First

Calumet did not start with Thoroughbreds. Wright's Standardbred breeding operation was founded in Libertyville, Illinois, and he moved it to Kentucky for its more favorable climate. In those years, harness racing was the most popular kind of horse racing, and Calumet quickly made its mark. In 1931 the farm's trotter Calumet Butler won the Hambletonian, the most prestigious event of the day. Wright died the following year, and his son, Warren Wright Sr., took over. He began converting the farm to Thoroughbred breeding and training, and he invested in quality breeding stock. A key move was acquiring part ownership in Blenheim, a stallion imported from England, along with Bull Lea, who became Calumet's foundation sire.

## Derby After Derby

The results read like a roll call of racing history. The eight Kentucky Derby winners Calumet raced are Whirlaway in 1941, Pensive in 1944, Citation in 1948, Pensive's son Ponder in 1949, Hill Gail in 1952, Iron Liege in 1957, Tim Tam in 1958 and Forward Pass in 1968 after a disqualification. It bred two more that raced for other owners, Strike the Gold in 1991 and Rich Strike in 2022. Whirlaway and Citation both went on to win the U.S. Triple Crown, and the farm's fillies Real Delight (1952) and Davona Dale (1979) each won a fillies' Triple Crown. Calumet is also the leading owner of Preakness Stakes winners, with eight wins counting Oxbow in 2013, and the leading breeder, with seven. Eleven of its horses have been inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame, and its other well-known horses include Armed, Twilight Tear, Bewitch, Coaltown, Davona Dale and Alydar. Under Warren Wright Sr. and then his wife, Lucille Parker Wright, who inherited the farm when he died in 1950, Calumet is credited with having been racing's top money-earning farm for 12 years.

## A Name on a Railroad Car

Calumet's fame spread well beyond the racetrack. The Pennsylvania Railroad named its baggage car #5868 the "Calumet Farm." In 1969 the Keeneland Association honored the farm with its Keeneland Mark of Distinction for its contribution to Keeneland and to the Thoroughbred industry, and in 1991 Calumet Farm was added to the National Register of Historic Places. By then, though, the farm's fortunes had turned. Lucille Wright died in 1982, and under the terms of her first husband's will, Calumet passed to the heirs of the couple's only child, Warren Wright Jr., who had died in 1978. A family in-law took over as president and head of operations, and years of legal trouble followed. In 2000, after long proceedings, he and the farm's former attorney and chief financial officer were convicted of fraud and bribery and sent to prison.

## Rescue and New Colors

In 1992, a trust established by Henryk de Kwiatkowski, a Polish-born Canadian, bought Calumet Farm and saved it from possible liquidation. The farm has since been fully restored. In 2012, the Calumet Investment Group bought it from the de Kwiatkowski Trust for more than $36 million and leased it to Brad M. Kelley, who was reported to be a member of the investment group; some later accounts simply describe him as the owner. Kelley's horses race under the Calumet Farm name, but they wear his black and gold colors, because Calumet's original silks, the "Devil's Red and Blue," were sold to a Brazilian investment group in 1992. The name still wins. In 2013, Oxbow's victory in the Preakness Stakes brought Calumet back to the winner's circle of a Triple Crown race.

## Flight Context

Calumet Farm lies at 38.0479°N, 84.5771°W on the western edge of Lexington, Kentucky, only about 1.5 nm east-northeast of Blue Grass Airport (KLEX). From 2,000 to 2,500 feet, the farm's 762 acres spread across the Bluegrass countryside next to the airport, so the best view comes on arrival or departure at Lexington. Because the farm sits so close to the field, follow Blue Grass Airport's published procedures and stay clear of its approach and departure paths.
