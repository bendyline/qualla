# Falls of the Ohio State Park

On the Indiana bank across from Louisville, Falls of the Ohio State Park lays bare a Devonian sea floor of fossil corals roughly 390 million years old, beside the rapids that once blocked the entire river.

**Location:** 38.2756, -85.7636

## Introduction

Across the Ohio River from Louisville, on the Indiana bank at Clarksville, a sea floor lies open to the sky. Shelves of Jeffersonville Limestone spread along the shore, and in them corals sit in lifelike positions, just as they grew in a Devonian sea roughly 390 million years ago. No aquarium could show you this. The exposed fossil beds are the main feature of Falls of the Ohio State Park, which drew about 160,000 visitors a year in the state's 2018-19 count, many of them there to walk across the floor of a vanished sea.

## A Sea Floor Turned to Stone

The limestone at the falls formed during the Devonian period, roughly 390 million years ago by the state park's account, though published estimates differ by a few million years. What makes the beds remarkable is not only their age but how much of the original community survives. Large, diverse tabulate corals and rugose corals are exposed as they lived, joined by brachiopods, bryozoans and gastropods, the snails of that ancient sea. The state park belongs to the larger Falls of the Ohio National Wildlife Conservation Area, which received federal status in 1981.

## The River's One Barrier

The park takes its name from a hazard. Here the Ohio River once dropped 26 feet over a distance of two miles, and in earlier times the Falls of the Ohio were the only navigational barrier on the entire river. Boats needed a way around, and the Louisville and Portland Canal, completed in 1830, provided a bypass. Later the Army Corps of Engineers built the McAlpine Locks and Dam, which largely covered the falls. The river has not been tamed completely. On a staircase in the park, flood markers record how high the water climbed in 1937, 2018 and 2025, a reminder that the Ohio still rises when it wants to.

## Clark's Riverbank, Then and Now

The falls hold a famous meeting in American history. The Lewis and Clark Expedition came together here, when Lewis and Clark met at George Rogers Clark's cabin. George Rogers Clark had deep roots on this bank. He founded the town of Clarksville in 1783, and it is described as the oldest American town in the former Northwest Territory. The fossil shoreline, the cabin site and the old town all share the same stretch of riverbank, so a single visit covers both deep time and the early American frontier. Two centuries later, Indiana set out to tell the story of this shore. The state park was established in 1990, and that same year the Indiana state government hired Terry Chase, a well-established exhibit developer, to design the displays for a new interpretive center. Construction began in September 1992, and the finished building cost $4.9 million and covers 16,000 square feet. The center has its own admission terms. The annual entrance pass covers parking but not admission to the interpretive center, which charges its own fee (the Indiana DNR lists $9 for ages 12 and up and $7 for ages 5 to 11, with young children free). Its exhibits have since been renewed with newer interactive displays.

## Flight Context

Falls of the Ohio State Park lies at 38.2756°N, 85.7636°W on the Indiana shore of the Ohio River at Clarksville, directly across from downtown Louisville, Kentucky. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet the broad river, the dam structures and the exposed limestone shelves along the Indiana shore are the landmarks to find. Clark Regional Airport in Jeffersonville, Indiana (FAA code JVY) is about 5.5 nm north-northeast, Bowman Field (KLOU) about 5.5 nm east-southeast, and Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport (KSDF) about 6.5 nm south-southeast.
