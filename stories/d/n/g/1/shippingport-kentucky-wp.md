# Shippingport, Kentucky

Shippingport, a French-led river town at the Falls of the Ohio that once rivaled Louisville as a port, was stranded on an island by the canal built to bypass it, and its last families left in 1958 when the government took the remaining land.

**Location:** 38.2751, -85.7786

**Categories:** Former populated places in Kentucky, Geography of Louisville, Kentucky, History of Louisville, Kentucky, 1785 establishments in Virginia

## Introduction

The canal that made Shippingport an island also made it unnecessary. In the 1820s, construction of the Louisville and Portland Canal cut across the peninsula where the town stood, leaving it surrounded by water. Worse, the canal let ships slip past the Falls of the Ohio, and past Shippingport with them. For a few bright decades this settlement on the south bank of the Ohio had seriously challenged Louisville as Kentucky's most important port. Today it is a partly industrial site, described in 2018 as largely deserted and mostly owned by the Army Corps of Engineers and LG&E, inside the city it once rivaled, one of six formerly independent settlements at the Falls that Louisville eventually absorbed.

## A Town Without a Name

Shippingport began so quietly it did not even have a name. The settlement on the peninsula was incorporated without one on October 10, 1785, while Kentucky was still part of Virginia. It was later called Campbell Town, after John Campbell, a Revolutionary War soldier and settler who had been granted the land for his earlier service in the French and Indian War. In 1803 the town was sold to a Philadelphia-based partnership, which gave it the name that stuck: Shippingport.

## The Tarascon Brothers

Two French brothers named Tarascon shaped what came next. They became leaders of the French business community at the Falls, and by 1819 they had built a large warehouse, a rope walk 1,200 feet long, and a six-story, water-powered flour mill. The mill, finished in 1817, became the symbol of Shippingport's success. The streets carried the community's character. One was named Tarascon, after the brothers, and another, Bengal, was perhaps named for a French settler and schoolteacher who had come from Bengal by way of Calcutta and first settled in Paris, Kentucky. There was horse racing at the Elm Tree Garden, and a distillery called the Napoleon.

## Boom Years at the Falls

The town grew fast. Between 1810 and 1820 its population jumped from 98 to more than 500, and Shippingport reached its peak in the 1820s with about 600 residents. The river gave and the river took, though, and the town frequently flooded. Then came the canal. Once ships could bypass the Falls, the traditional business that had fed Shippingport drained away. In 1828, Louisville incorporated as a city and drew Shippingport inside its boundaries, and after a bad flood in 1832, most of the French community moved to Portland, then northwest of Louisville and now part of the city as well. Resentment of the canal lingered. The introduction to the Louisville Directory of 1844 called it "precisely one of those improvements for private interests, at the expense of the public good, which is obnoxious to the good of the whole community."

## The Last Families

What remained of Shippingport dwindled across the next century. The canal was gradually widened, and a hydroelectric plant rose on the island. The devastating Ohio River flood of 1937 swamped the area and forced most of the remaining families to leave. About 20 years later came the final blow. In 1958 the federal government acquired the last private property on the island, reportedly to widen the canal once more, and the families who had held on had to leave; accounts differ on whether this was a condemnation or a buyout. Some of them had roots there stretching back more than a century. After more than 170 years, the town that once challenged Louisville was gone, leaving only its name on the map of the Falls.

## Flight Context

Shippingport lies at 38.2751°N, 85.7786°W on the Kentucky side of the Ohio River at the Falls of the Ohio, just west of downtown Louisville. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet, look for the island between the river's main channel and the Louisville and Portland Canal, with Falls of the Ohio State Park close by on the Indiana shore. Clark Regional Airport in Jeffersonville, Indiana (FAA code JVY) is about 5.5 nm north-northeast, Bowman Field (KLOU) about 6 nm east-southeast, and Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport (KSDF) about 6.5 nm south-southeast. Check the airspace around Louisville Muhammad Ali International before descending over the river.
