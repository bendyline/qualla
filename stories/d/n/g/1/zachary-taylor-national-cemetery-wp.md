# Zachary Taylor National Cemetery

On the old Taylor family estate in Louisville, the 12th president rests in a 1926 mausoleum that sits inside a national cemetery yet is not owned by the federal government, surrounded by veterans of six wars.

**Location:** 38.2781, -85.6431

## Introduction

At the back of a national cemetery in Louisville stands a president's tomb that the United States does not own. Zachary Taylor, the 12th president, lies in a limestone mausoleum with a granite base and a marble interior, built by the federal government. The mausoleum and the Taylor family graves beside it sit within the boundaries of Zachary Taylor National Cemetery, yet they are not federal property. The National Cemetery Administration cares for them anyway, just as it tends the rows of veterans around them. The arrangement is a leftover of how the cemetery came to be: a family plot that grew into a national cemetery.

## Springfield

The story starts with a reward. Richard Taylor, a colonel in the American Revolutionary War, reportedly received a 400-acre estate called Springfield in gratitude for his service, and this land was part of it. His son Zachary had been born on November 24, 1784, in Orange County, Virginia, before the family joined the westward migration and settled near what would become Louisville, on the Ohio River. The boy grew up in a small woodland cabin until the family prospered enough to move into a brick house. The house where the Taylors lived for most of their time in Louisville still stands nearby and is known as the Zachary Taylor House. Richard Taylor and his wife, Sarah Strother Taylor, are buried in the old family cemetery, along with other members of the family.

## A President Comes Home

Zachary Taylor became a soldier first and a politician second. He earned the nickname "Old Rough and Ready" during the Second Seminole War, and in the Mexican–American War he won victories at Palo Alto and Resaca de la Palma in May 1846 and at Buena Vista in February 1847, where he defeated Santa Anna despite being severely outnumbered. His presidency was brief. He died suddenly of a stomach disease on July 9, 1850, after only 16 months in office, the third-shortest presidential term in U.S. history. His body was held temporarily at the Congressional Cemetery in Washington, D.C., from July 13 to October 25, 1850. On November 1, he was buried in his family's burial ground in Louisville. The vault that first received him reportedly still stands near the later mausoleum.

## From Half an Acre to Sixteen

In the 1920s, the Taylor family set out to turn their burial ground into a national cemetery. The Commonwealth of Kentucky donated two adjacent parcels of land, expanding the half-acre family cemetery to 16 acres. The Army judge advocate general, however, ruled against acquiring the Taylor cemetery itself, and the family graves remain outside federal ownership to this day. A new mausoleum was built in 1926, and Congress formally established the national cemetery in 1928. Taylor rests in the mausoleum with his wife, Margaret Mackall Smith Taylor. The Taylor family section lies at the rear of the grounds. The cemetery was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on November 3, 1983.

## Veterans of Six Wars

Around the president's tomb lie those who served in six wars: the Spanish–American War, World War I, World War II, the Korean War, the Vietnam War and the Persian Gulf War. As of 2014, the cemetery held more than 14,000 interments, and it is described as one of seven national cemeteries in Kentucky. Among them is U.S. Air Force Lieutenant General Roscoe Charles Wilson. The cemetery, administered by the National Cemetery Administration of the Department of Veterans Affairs, is now closed to new interments, though eligible family members may still be buried in an existing grave site. Each year on November 24, Taylor's birthday, soldiers from Fort Knox hold a wreath-laying ceremony in his honor.

## Flight Context

Zachary Taylor National Cemetery lies at 38.2781°N, 85.6431°W along Brownsboro Road (US-42) in eastern Louisville, Kentucky. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet, the orderly rows of headstones and the mausoleum at the rear of the grounds are the features to find, with the Ohio River to the north. Bowman Field (KLOU) is only about 3 nm south-southwest, so expect local traffic. Clark Regional Airport in Jeffersonville, Indiana (FAA code JVY) is about 7 nm northwest, and Louisville Muhammad Ali International Airport (KSDF) about 8 nm southwest.
