# Whitewater Shaker Settlement

A former Shaker community in southwestern Ohio, founded in 1823 and home to Shakers until 1916, whose brick meeting house, dwelling and workshops still stand on parkland along Oxford Road.

**Location:** 39.2944, -84.7428

## Introduction

In 1916, the last two Shakers left White Water. The religious community they walked away from had worked this corner of Crosby Township, in southwestern Ohio, for more than ninety years, raising brooms, seeds, fruit and livestock beside the Dry Fork of the White Water River. Their departure was prompted from far away: the Shakers' parent ministry in Canterbury, New Hampshire, had sold the property to private owners. Yet the buildings stayed. More than twenty Shaker-built structures still stand near New Haven, and the Whitewater Shaker Settlement is now a historic district slowly being coaxed back to life.

## Missionaries from Lebanon

The settlement's nucleus formed in 1823, when members of the Union Society of Shakers from Lebanon, Ohio, came to share their religion with a group of settlers here. The newcomers found rich soil, abundant timber and the flowing stream of the Dry Fork of the White Water River. Faith drove the building program. The first permanent brick structure was the meeting house, completed in 1827, followed in 1832 by a large dwelling. The village was organized into groups the Shakers called families, and the surviving buildings still carry those names, from the North Family's dwelling house, woodshed, smokehouse and seed house to the Centre Family's trustees' office, washhouse and broom shop.

## Broom Corn, Seeds and a Long Decline

Work at White Water began in the fields. Broom corn was the first crop the Shakers planted, and brooms became one of the community's products, alongside fruit products, wheat, sorghum and livestock. Seeds were grown for commerce as well, a trade that tied this rural village to buyers beyond its own fields. The Centre Family's broom shop on the east side of Oxford Road, photographed by the Historic American Buildings Survey in 1968 and 1974, is a reminder of that first harvest. After the Civil War, the tide turned against White Water. Strict Shaker religious practices lost their appeal. At the same time, industrial manufacturing reduced demand for handmade goods, and the Shaker seed market declined until it was discontinued altogether. The village that had once built so confidently in brick was left with fading markets and a shrinking future. The end came from outside. The parent ministry at Canterbury, New Hampshire, sold White Water to private owners, and in 1916 its last two Shakers departed. For much of the twentieth century, the buildings passed through private hands.

## A Village Held in Trust

Recognition came first on paper. In 1968 and again in 1974, the Historic American Buildings Survey photographed the meeting house and the North and Centre Family buildings along Oxford Road, and in 1974 the settlement was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as a historic district. Between 1989 and 1991, Great Parks of Hamilton County bought about 600 acres from private owners, taking in more than twenty Shaker-built structures. The Friends of White Water Shaker Village now work on restoration and preservation, with a mission to preserve, restore and open the buildings and grounds of the North Family. The village is open to the public only for scheduled events and by-appointment group tours, letting visitors step inside rooms the last Shakers left more than a century ago.

## Flight Context

The Whitewater Shaker Settlement lies at 39.2944°N, 84.7428°W along Oxford Road in Crosby Township, Hamilton County, Ohio, near New Haven. Look for a cluster of plain historic buildings set among open farm fields; about 1,500-2,000 feet AGL gives the best view of the layout. Cincinnati West Airport (FAA LID I67) in Harrison is only about 2.6 nm southwest, making the settlement an easy sightseeing target on departure or arrival. Butler County Regional Airport - Hogan Field (KHAO) is about 11 nm east-northeast, and Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International (KCVG) about 15 nm south-southeast.
