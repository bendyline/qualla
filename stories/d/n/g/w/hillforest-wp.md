# Hillforest

An 1855 mansion on a bluff above the Ohio River in Aurora, Indiana, built for a Scottish-born whiskey and steamboat magnate and designed to evoke the riverboats that made his fortune.

**Location:** 39.0539, -84.9017

## Introduction

From its bluff above the Ohio River, Hillforest looks almost ready to cast off. A rounded central section swells from the front of the house and climbs to a belvedere on the roof, porches wrap the facade like decks, and the whole design is evocative of the steamships that plied the Ohio and Mississippi Rivers in the nineteenth century. The resemblance suits the man who built it. Thomas Gaff's family kept a fleet of steamboats to carry its products, and in 1855 he gave himself a house in Aurora, Indiana, that seems to remember the river it overlooks.

## A Scotsman's Empire

Gaff was born near Edinburgh, Scotland, in 1808 and came to America at the age of three. He learned papermaking from his father and the distilling business from an uncle in Brooklyn, Charles Wilson, and he followed his brother James to Aurora in 1843; another brother, John, arrived in 1845 and would serve as the town's mayor. Together the Gaffs built a sprawling web of enterprises. Their T. & J. W. Gaff & Co. distillery on the banks of Hogan Creek produced bourbon, rye and Thistle Dew scotch whiskey, and their Crescent Brewing Company made Aurora Lager Beer. The family also had interests in railroads and farming, Nevada silver mines, a Cincinnati jewelry store, a foundry and machine works, turnpike and canal construction, and two Louisiana plantations.

## The Forest Queen Goes to War

To carry all those products, the Gaffs owned a fleet of steamboats, and one of them earned a place in Civil War history. The Forest Queen carried General William Tecumseh Sherman during the Vicksburg campaign, and the museum says she served as his headquarters. In April 1863 she ran the Confederate batteries at Vicksburg and was damaged, then towed to safety by a gunboat. The museum says her end was less glorious: Confederates burned the boat to the waterline in St. Louis, Missouri. Standing on Hillforest's porches, it is easy to see why a family so tied to the river would build a home that echoes the shape of its boats.

## Isaiah Rogers on the Bluff

The architect was Isaiah Rogers, and Hillforest is a rare, well-preserved example of his work, as well as one of the finest surviving examples of an Italian Renaissance estate house. It is a two-story frame house under a low-pitched hip roof. The main facade is finished in wide vertical boards, while the other sides wear shiplap siding with wooden quoin blocks at the corners. A single-story porch runs the full width of the front, and a second-story porch curves around the central projection. Full-length windows open directly onto the porches, removing the need for doors. Inside, the rooms are sumptuously finished, and original trompe-l'oeil artwork still decorates the walls, painted to fool the eye.

## After the Gaffs

Thomas Gaff died in 1884, but his family kept Hillforest until 1926, when it was sold to William Stark, a local furniture manufacturer. For a time the house served as a lodge for the Veterans of Foreign Wars, from the late 1940s to the mid-1950s, and in 1956 it opened as a museum. It is owned and operated by the Hillforest Historical Foundation, and it was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1992. The estate still covers 11 acres, with formally landscaped gardens, land historically used for farming and a carriage house that is a 1970s reconstruction rather than an original. Hillforest sits at 213 Fifth Street, within the Downtown Aurora Historic District, a few blocks from the river that shaped it.

## Flight Context

Hillforest stands at 39.0539°N, 84.9017°W on a bluff above the Ohio River in Aurora, Dearborn County, Indiana. Follow the Ohio River and look for Aurora's downtown on the Indiana bank; the mansion sits on the hillside just above it, marked by its rounded central bay and rooftop belvedere. About 1,500-2,000 feet AGL gives a good look at the house and its 11-acre grounds.
