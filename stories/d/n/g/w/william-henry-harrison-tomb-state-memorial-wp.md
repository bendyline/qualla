# William Henry Harrison Tomb State Memorial

The hilltop tomb in North Bend, Ohio, where the ninth U.S. president lies with his wife and with the only man to have been both the son and the father of a president.

**Location:** 39.1517, -84.7522

## Introduction

John Scott Harrison holds a distinction no other American can claim: he was both the son and the father of a U.S. president. He rests on a hilltop in North Bend, Ohio, beside his father, William Henry Harrison, the ninth president, and his mother, Anna. The William Henry Harrison Tomb State Memorial crowns the summit the family called Mt. Nebo, a spot the president wanted for its wide view of the Ohio River and of the corners of three states - Ohio, Indiana and Kentucky.

## One Month in Office

William Henry Harrison's time in the White House was brief. He died on April 4, 1841, one month after taking office, and was first buried in the Congressional Cemetery in Washington, D.C. He did not stay there long. In June his remains were removed for the journey to North Bend, where the family had chosen a site at the crest of Mt. Nebo on their own estate, and on July 7, 1841, he was laid in a simple family tomb on the summit. Below spread the Ohio River Valley, a region whose settlement he had helped to shape. His wife, Anna, who died in 1864, would eventually join him on the hill.

## Son of One President, Father of Another

Their son, John Scott Harrison, links the tomb to a second presidency. His own son, Benjamin, became the twenty-third president of the United States. John served in Congress himself, representing Ohio's 2nd district from 1853 to 1857. His death in 1878 was followed by a grim episode. Despite precautions that included a cemented brick vault and a watchman posted at night, grave robbers took his body, and one of his sons later found it at the Ohio Medical College. The outrage helped prompt passage of the Ohio Anatomy Law of 1881, which supplied medical schools with unclaimed bodies in an effort to discourage grave robbing. Today John Scott Harrison lies here among his family, the quiet link between two presidencies.

## Twenty-Four Vaults

The tomb holds 24 vaults. Besides the president, Anna and John Scott Harrison, other family members rest inside, and several sealed vaults carry no markings at all. The grounds around them have a history of their own. In 1871, John Scott Harrison sold all but six acres of the old estate and offered the remaining parcel, with the tomb and other burial sites, to the State of Ohio in exchange for a pledge of perpetual maintenance. The offer was not formally taken up for decades. Over several years of neglect, the tomb and grounds fell into disrepair, and it was not until 1919 that the Ohio General Assembly formally accepted the bequest and appropriated funds for its care.

## Keeping Watch on Mt. Nebo

Recognition followed rescue. The tomb was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on November 10, 1970. Today the Harrison-Symmes Memorial Foundation manages the site on behalf of the Ohio History Connection. In 2007, improvements included kiosks that teach visitors about Harrison, his role in settling the Ohio River Valley and his place in U.S. history. The Ohio History Connection has listed the grounds as open during daylight hours year-round, with private tours by appointment and the tomb opening for the weekend of Harrison's February 9 birthday. The gates to the tomb itself have at times been closed for maintenance, so it is worth checking ahead. Either way, the reason the family chose this summit is still on offer: the long view across the river valley toward three states.

## Flight Context

The William Henry Harrison Tomb State Memorial sits at 39.1517°N, 84.7522°W on a hilltop in North Bend, Hamilton County, Ohio, overlooking the Ohio River close to where Ohio, Indiana and Kentucky meet. The Cincinnati and Whitewater Canal Tunnel in Cleves lies less than half a mile to the north-northeast. From about 1,500-2,000 feet AGL, follow the Ohio River and look for the wooded summit above North Bend. Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International Airport (KCVG) is only about 7 nm south-southeast, so expect airline traffic and check current airspace before flying low here. Cincinnati West Airport (FAA LID I67) in Harrison is about 6.5 nm north, and Cincinnati Municipal Lunken Field (KLUK) about 16 nm east. River fog can linger in the valley on calm mornings.
