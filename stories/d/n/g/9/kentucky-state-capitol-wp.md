# Kentucky State Capitol

Kentucky's Beaux-Arts capitol in Frankfort, with staircases modeled on the Paris Opéra, has housed all three branches of state government since 1910, and is now closed for its first full-scale renovation in more than 70 years.

**Location:** 38.1867, -84.8754

**Categories:** State capitols in the United States, Government of Kentucky, Beaux-Arts architecture in Kentucky, National Register of Historic Places in Frankfort, Kentucky, Tourist attractions in Franklin County, Kentucky

## Introduction

For years, Frankfort residents took their exercise inside the seat of government. The Kentucky State Capitol used to be completely open during business hours, and locals walked the long marble hallways the way people elsewhere walk shopping malls. Those halls are quiet now. On August 20, 2025, the building closed to the public for its first full-scale renovation in more than 70 years, and the governor, the legislature and the Kentucky Supreme Court scattered to temporary quarters around town, not expected to return until 2029.

## Capitols Before This One

Kentucky's government has moved house several times. From 1792 to 1830, two buildings served as the capitol, and both burned completely. A new capitol built in 1830 stayed in use until 1910, and it was the scene of an assassination unique in American history. During the bitterly contested governor's election of 1899, with the results still in dispute, the Democratic claimant William Goebel walked toward that capitol on the morning of January 30, 1900, and was shot. He was sworn in as governor on his deathbed the next day and died on February 3, the only sitting state governor in United States history to die by assassination. The old building still stands, now a museum operated by the Kentucky Historical Society. It was replaced not because of that violence, but because a growing state government needed more room.

## A Paris Opera House in Frankfort

In 1904 the Kentucky General Assembly settled on Frankfort, rather than Lexington or Louisville, as the site for a permanent capitol and set aside $1 million to build it in the southern part of the city. Ground was broken on August 14, 1905, and the building was completed in 1909 at a cost of $1,180,434.80. The architect, Frank Mills Andrews, was distinguished and award-winning, and he worked in the Beaux-Arts style, bringing classical French design indoors. One example stands out: the capitol's marble staircases are replicas of those in the Opéra Garnier in Paris. The building houses all three branches of state government, executive, legislative and judicial, with chambers for the Senate, the House of Representatives and the Supreme Court, along with a state reception room and a law library.

## Who Stands in the Rotunda

Beginning in 1912, statues of historical figures took their places in the rotunda. The first was a bronze of Abraham Lincoln. Henry Clay and Ephraim McDowell followed in 1930, and both are the bronzed plaster models for the statues that represent Kentucky in National Statuary Hall in the U.S. Capitol. In 1936 a marble statue of Jefferson Davis, president of the Confederacy, was placed in the rotunda, paid for with donations and public funds under the auspices of the United Daughters of the Confederacy. It had stood there for 84 years when, on June 12, 2020, at Governor Andy Beshear's request, the Historic Properties Advisory Commission voted 11 to 1 to remove it, and crews took it down that weekend. The plan was to move it to the Jefferson Davis Monument State Historic Site in Fairview, his birthplace, but as of mid-2021 it remained in storage. In November 2022 a new figure arrived: a bronze of Nettie Depp by her great-great-niece Amanda Matthews, the first permanent large-scale monument to a woman inside the capitol, chosen as a representative of Kentucky women who achieved professional and personal success.

## Closed for Repairs, Until 2029

The renovation, which began with restoration work on the dome in 2022, is meant to tackle decades of deferred maintenance. The plans include revitalized public spaces, new terra cotta roof tiles, modern waterproofing, the removal of outdated electrical systems, better accessibility, and new heating and cooling. The 2025 General Assembly was the last session held in the building before it closed, and lawmakers now meet in a temporary structure built in the parking lot of the Capitol Annex. The offices of the governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general and secretary of state, along with the Supreme Court and the state law library, have all moved elsewhere in Frankfort. The grounds and monuments remain open, and the dome can still be admired from outside, whether from the US 60 lookout or from the Frankfort Cemetery, near the grave of Daniel Boone.

## Flight Context

The Kentucky State Capitol stands at 38.1867°N, 84.8754°W in southern Frankfort, near the Kentucky River, which turns through the center of town. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL the dome is an unmistakable landmark, and the river helps with orientation. Capital City Airport (KFFT) is only about 1.5 nm away, so traffic in the pattern deserves attention. Blue Grass Airport in Lexington (KLEX) is about 16 nm southeast. Best viewed in clear daytime conditions.
