# Kentucky Horse Park

On 1,224 acres north of Lexington, the Kentucky Horse Park is part working farm, part world-class arena and part retirement home for champions, with Man o' War resting beneath his statue by the entrance.

**Location:** 38.1520, -84.5170

**Categories:** Equestrian venues in the United States, Equine industry in Kentucky, Parks in Lexington, Kentucky, State parks of Kentucky, Eventing, 1978 establishments in Kentucky

## Introduction

The statue of a horse on its pedestal near the entrance is not just a monument. Man o' War lies beneath his own statue here, his grave moved from Faraway Farm when the Kentucky Horse Park was being built in the 1970s. It is a fitting welcome to a place that takes horses seriously enough to give them a home for life. Opened in 1978 on 1,224 acres in northern Fayette County, just off Interstate 75 at Exit 120, the park is dedicated to "man's relationship with the horse," and it pursues that idea as a working horse farm, an international competition venue and an educational park all at once.

## A Retirement Barn for Legends

The park's most famous residents came to it after the cheering stopped. Forego, one of the leading handicap horses of the 1970s, arrived in 1979, and others followed. John Henry, a two-time Horse of the Year and the world's richest Thoroughbred when he retired, lived here from 1985 until his death in 2007, apart from a few months in 1986. His neighbors included Bold Forbes, another 1970s champion, and later Cigar, whom the park bills as the Horse of the Decade for the 1990s, who died at the park in 2014. Da Hoss, the first horse to win Breeders' Cup races in non-consecutive years, lived here too. Alysheba and Funny Cide arrived in late 2008, though Alysheba died just months later, in March 2009, and 1994 Kentucky Derby winner Go For Gin moved in in 2011. The barns have also sheltered harness racing stars such as Rambling Willie, Cam Fella and the trotting champion Mr. Muscleman, along with five-gaited American Saddlebreds.

## Monuments in the Bluegrass

Sculpture turns the grounds into a kind of hall of fame. Beyond Man o' War's pedestal, a life-size statue by Jim Reno shows Secretariat, the 1973 Triple Crown winner, with jockey Ron Turcotte aboard and groom Eddie Sweat leading him, a monument that remembers the people around a champion as well as the horse himself. Harness racing has its own tribute, a statue of Bret Hanover. Indoors, the International Museum of the Horse, a Smithsonian Affiliate, keeps a permanent collection of horse history and memorabilia alongside rotating exhibitions built around a single theme. Past shows have included A Gift from the Desert, about Arabia; Imperial China; and All the Queen's Horses, about Britain.

## Horses of the World

In season, twice a day, the Horses of the World Show brings out common and rare breeds from around the globe, ridden by riders in authentic costume. It is the park's most direct lesson in how differently cultures have lived with horses. Behind the scenes, the park is also a business address for much of the American horse world. Its National Horse Center houses the headquarters of more than 30 national, regional and state equine organizations, from the United States Equestrian Federation and the United States Pony Clubs to the American Farrier's Association and the American Association of Equine Practitioners. Although the state owns the park, it is administered separately from Kentucky's state park system.

## The World Comes to Lexington

Competition has been part of the park from the beginning. The annual Kentucky Three-Day Event traces its run here to the 1978 World Three-Day Championships, held the year the park opened. The North American Junior and Young Rider Championships for riders aged 14 to 21 began here in 2009, the USEF Pony Finals have crowned national pony hunter and jumper champions since 2008, and the Southern Lights holiday festival, now in its thirty-third year, glows every winter. A defining moment came on December 6, 2005, when Lexington and the park were chosen to host the 2010 FEI World Equestrian Games, held that September and October. It was the first time the games had ever been staged outside Europe. The state prepared by approving the $36 million Alltech Arena, an indoor venue with a 135-by-300-foot floor and nine luxury suites, and the park's outdoor Rolex Stadium, with a 306-by-406-foot show ring of sand and fiber, is described as the largest outdoor concert venue in Central Kentucky.

## Flight Context

The park centers on 38.1520°N, 84.5170°W in northern Fayette County, where Iron Works Pike (KY 1973) meets Interstate 75 at Exit 120; the interstate corridor makes an easy line to follow north from Lexington. From 2,500-3,500 feet MSL, look for the large outdoor stadium, the indoor arena and the show rings near the I-75 interchange. Georgetown-Scott County Regional Airport (no ICAO code; FAA identifier 27K) is about 6 nm northeast, and Blue Grass Airport (KLEX) in Lexington, with an operating control tower, is about 8 nm south-southwest. Capital City Airport (KFFT) in Frankfort lies about 18 nm to the west. Summer haze can reduce visibility over the Bluegrass.
