# Old Friends Equine

On a farm outside Georgetown, Kentucky, Derby winners, Triple Crown rivals, a movie star and racing's most famous loser have shared the pastures of Old Friends, a retirement home for Thoroughbreds founded after the death of a champion.

**Location:** 38.1794, -84.5961

**Categories:** Horse welfare organizations, Horse racing in the United States, Animal charities based in the United States, Charities based in Kentucky, Eclipse Award winners

## Introduction

When Eldaafer, winner of the 2010 Breeders' Cup Marathon, retired to a farm outside Georgetown, Kentucky, in 2014, he did not come alone. He brought his goats, Yahoo and Google. Home for all three became Old Friends, a nonprofit retirement home where well over a hundred former racehorses spend their last years in pasture rather than in obscurity. It began with one leased paddock and only a horse or two. By one published count it now owns 136 acres, Dreamchase Farm, and leases more (the Associated Press has described the farm as 236 acres), and it describes itself as the only Thoroughbred retirement facility in the United States that accepts stallions on a regular basis.

## Because of Ferdinand

Old Friends was born from a loss. In 2002, Ferdinand, winner of the 1986 Kentucky Derby, died, and the news spurred Michael Blowen, a former movie critic for The Boston Globe, to create a place where retired racehorses could live out their lives. He founded Old Friends in 2003. The idea rests on a practical bargain: famous horses draw visitors and donations, and that money pays for the care of the many low-profile horses who never made headlines. In January 2015, the organization received a special Eclipse Award honoring extraordinary service to the Thoroughbred industry, and it is accredited by the Thoroughbred Aftercare Alliance.

## Rivals Grazing Side by Side

Some of the residents' stories read like racing history. Silver Charm won the 1997 Kentucky Derby and Preakness and the 1998 Dubai World Cup, stood at stud in Japan, and retired to Old Friends in 2014; his dam, Bonnie's Poker, had also lived at Old Friends before her death in 2010 at age 28. In 2015 Touch Gold arrived, the horse who won the 1997 Belmont Stakes and ended Silver Charm's bid for the Triple Crown. The old rivals ended up sharing a farm. That same year War Emblem, winner of the 2002 Derby and Preakness, came home from Japan and had to be gelded to comply with USDA regulations. Charismatic, the 1999 Derby and Preakness winner, died in his stall in February 2017 of a sudden, catastrophic pelvic fracture, just over two months after returning from Japan to retire at the farm. Old Friends has been bringing stallions back from overseas since 2004, when Creator, Ogygian and Sunshine Forever were among the first.

## Claimers, Losers and a Movie Star

Fame is not the admission ticket. I'm Charismatic, a son of the Derby winner, raced 92 times with limited success and sank to the lowest claiming ranks before he was bought for retirement in 2012. Tour of the Cat earned more than $1.1 million, slid down the claiming ladder, and was claimed in 2009 specifically so he could retire here. Popcorn Deelites, a low-level claimer, found a second career in film, playing the title role in the 2003 movie Seabiscuit before joining Old Friends in 2005. And Zippy Chippy, who holds the modern American record for the most losses, lived here too, remembered as "racing's most lovable loser." Even the miniature horse who served as the farm's official "spokeshorse," Little Silver Charm, received a book credit for A Charmed Life, the 2016 story of life at Old Friends, as told to Diane White.

## Racing Down the Fence Line

Daily life follows the horses' natures. They are kept turned out in pasture as much as possible, with run-in sheds for bad weather. Geldings usually share a paddock with one or two companions and form tight bonds. Stallions are more territorial and get pastures of their own, but they still interact, often by racing each other along their fences. Visitors can book guided tours to meet the retirees. The idea has spread beyond Georgetown. In 2009 a farm in Greenfield Center, New York, became Old Friends at Cabin Creek, named for Hall of Fame trainer Bobby Frankel. In 2015 a satellite opened at Kentucky Downs on the Kentucky–Tennessee border, and the track launched the Old Friends Stakes, whose winner is guaranteed a place at Old Friends upon retirement. The first running went to a 50-1 long shot, Kalamos.

## Headstones Brought Home

Old Friends also looks after horses who are already gone. When their original graves were threatened, several champions were re-interred on the farm: Hall of Famer Noor from the former Loma Rica Ranch, and 1998 Horse of the Year Skip Away from Hopewell Farm after it was sold at auction. Glacial Princess, a two-time Ohio Horse of the Year who broke down during the 1987 Ballerina Stakes, came from Beulah Park when that track was slated for development. For Springsteel, an "Iron Horse" gelding with 76 starts who was buried at Rockingham Park after breaking down there, photographer Barbara Livingston and Michael Blowen arranged for his headstone to be moved when the old track faced development.

## Flight Context

Old Friends' Dreamchase Farm lies at approximately 38.179°N, 84.596°W near Georgetown in Scott County, Kentucky, north of Lexington. From 1,500–2,500 ft AGL, look for fenced paddocks, barns and run-in sheds on the farm; please avoid low passes that could spook horses. Blue Grass Airport (KLEX) is about 16 km to the south, Georgetown-Scott County Regional Airport (FAA identifier 27K, no ICAO code) about 15 km to the east-northeast, and Capital City Airport in Frankfort (KFFT) about 27 km to the west. Spring and autumn usually bring the clearest skies; summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
