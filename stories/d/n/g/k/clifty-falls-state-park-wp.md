# Clifty Falls State Park

A canyon park above the Ohio River near Madison, Indiana, where four waterfalls, a fossil-strewn creek bed and an unfinished 1850s railroad tunnel reward hikers on some of the state's most rugged trails.

**Location:** 38.7482, -85.4152

**Categories:** Protected areas established in 1920, State parks of Indiana, Protected areas of Jefferson County, Indiana, Waterfalls of Indiana, Canyons and gorges of Indiana, Nature centers in Indiana, Landforms of Jefferson County, Indiana

## Introduction

Somewhere in Clifty Falls State Park is a canyon where the sun shines only during the middle of the day. The rest of the time it lies in shadow, while Clifty Creek and Little Clifty Creek work their way through the stone. This is southeastern Indiana, 46 miles northeast of Louisville, Kentucky, where the land falls toward the Ohio River through gorges and waterfalls. Spread across roughly 1,400 acres of Jefferson County, the park draws about 370,000 visitors a year. What they come to see is written in rock and water, from the height of the falls to the fossils in the creek bed.

## A Town's Gift

The park exists because a town wanted it to. In 1920, after a year's work, citizens of nearby Madison, Indiana, gave 570 acres to the state at the suggestion of Richard Lieber. Their gift became the core of a park that has since grown to more than twice that size. Clifty Falls soon took on a teaching role as well. When Indiana's state parks launched a naturalist program in 1927, it was one of the first four parks to put the idea into practice. Madison remains the park's closest neighbor. From Clifty Inn, in the southern part of the grounds, guests look out over the Ohio River, and the inn offers rooms for overnight visitors while a campground serves both RV and tent campers.

## Reading the Canyon

Clifty Creek Canyon runs the entire north-south length of the park. At the north end, the upper rim stands about 800 feet above sea level; to the south, the land drops to the valley of the Ohio River at about 500 feet. The creek itself descends to roughly 750 feet before pitching over one of the two Clifty Falls, then keeps running downhill until it meets the Ohio. Two more major canyons join from the east. Deans Branch carries Tunnel Falls, and Hoffman Branch carries Hoffman Falls. Look down at the creek bed and the rock tells a far older story. Clifty Creek's stony bed is scattered with the remains of a long-vanished marine ecosystem that included ancient corals, ancestral squids and brachiopods. Collecting them is prohibited, so each fossil stays where the water left it.

## Four Falls, Many Moods

The park takes its name from the waterfalls on Clifty Creek, yet the tallest drops are on the side streams. Big Clifty Falls and Little Clifty Falls each fall about 60 feet. Hoffman Falls reaches 78 feet, and Tunnel Falls, at 83 feet, is the highest of the four. The falls rarely look the same from one visit to the next. With the weather and the seasons, they can shift from roaring plunges to delicate bridal-veil mists, and in hard cold they freeze into gleaming towers of ice. One visit catches a single mood; a return trip in another season can feel like arriving at a different park.

## Brough's Folly

One of the park's strangest features is a railroad tunnel that was never finished. Known as Brough's Tunnel, the unfinished bore belongs to an 1852 railroad path nicknamed "Brough's Folly," and it was left abandoned in the hillside. The Tunnel Trail, like most of the park's paths, runs along the sides of the valley. These days the tunnel's most important occupants are bats. It closes to the public from November 1 to April 30 to protect hibernating bats from white-nose syndrome, and rockslides have closed it for longer stretches as well: the Indiana DNR currently lists it as closed indefinitely after an early-spring rockslide, with plans to reopen it in the future. Check before planning a walk through.

## The Toughest Trail in Indiana

The park's ten numbered trails range from easy to very rugged, and only one earns the top rating. Trail 2, about three miles long, is the park's only trail rated very rugged, and it is often billed as the most rugged trail in Indiana. Even so, it does not take hikers all the way to the bottom. Because of the danger of falling rocks, the end of the trail is blocked off, and a set of old steps that once led down to the falls is closed as well. The view comes instead from an overlook carved out of the side of the canyon, where the falls can be watched from a safer vantage. The rest of the network is gentler, and the park's nature trails, especially those that pass near Clifty Falls, are among its most acclaimed attractions.

## Flight Context

Clifty Falls State Park is centered near 38.7482°N, 85.4152°W, on the Indiana bank of the Ohio River beside Madison. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, look for the forested cleft of Clifty Creek Canyon running north from the Ohio River valley; the river itself is the dominant landmark to the south. Madison Regional Airport (KIMS, formerly Madison Municipal) lies about 2.4 nm west-northwest, close enough to make the park an easy sightseeing loop after departure. For larger fields, Louisville Muhammad Ali International (KSDF) is about 38 nm south-southwest and Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International (KCVG) about 39 nm east-northeast. The waterfalls run strongest after rain and are hard to pick out through leaf cover in summer; late fall and winter offer the clearest look into the canyon.
