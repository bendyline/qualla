# Florence Y'all Water Tower

A northern Kentucky water tower that once illegally advertised a mall that didn't exist yet, until a mayor's napkin sketch turned MALL into Y'ALL and gave a city its slogan.

**Location:** 38.9977, -84.6477

**Categories:** Culture of Appalachia, Buildings and structures in Boone County, Kentucky, Individual signs in the United States, Water towers in Kentucky, Towers completed in 1974, 1974 establishments in Kentucky, Florence, Kentucky

## Introduction

Millions of motorists a year on Interstates 75 and 71 in northern Kentucky look up at a red-and-white water tower and read two words: FLORENCE Y'ALL. It sounds like a greeting that was always meant to be there. It was not. The tower, owned by the city of Florence and built in 1974, originally said FLORENCE MALL, and the switch from M to Y began as a way to stay on the right side of the law. What started as a cheap legal patch became a regional landmark, a local motto and, in time, the name of a baseball team.

## Advertising a Mall That Wasn't There

The story starts with a deal. In the early 1970s, the developers of a mall that had not yet been built gave the city the land for the tower, on one condition: the words "Florence Mall" had to be painted on it where interstate motorists would see them. The Pittsburgh-Des Moines Steel Co. of Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, built the tower, which holds about a million gallons, and the Virginia Erection Co. painted it in 1974, spelling out FLORENCE MALL in giant letters. The giant advertisement raised legal concerns almost immediately. In July 1974, officials of the state Bureau of Highways told the city the sign was illegal because it advertised something that did not yet exist. The mall would not open until 1976, and Florence suddenly had an unlawful billboard towering over the interstate.

## Sketched on a Napkin

The city weighed its options, from repainting the tower to hiding the offending words under a large tarpaulin. With time running out to comply with the law, civic staff met for a brainstorming session at the Stringtown Restaurant with the mayor, C.M. "Hop" Ewing, who "sketched different ideas on a napkin." The idea Ewing settled on was almost absurdly simple. Remove the vertical strokes at the sides of the M in MALL, add a stem to turn it into a Y, then slip in an apostrophe. MALL became Y'ALL. Ewing called it a "corny solution, but cost-effective," and the numbers backed him up, since the small alteration cost about a third as much as a full repainting. A company from Cincinnati, Ohio, was brought in to make the change.

## Better Than a Billboard

The fix did more for the mall than the original sign could have. The publicity surrounding the Florence Y'all tower advertised the mall better than a passive sign alone, and when the mall opened in 1976, mall-goers created a traffic jam at the Kentucky Route 18 exit from I-75. Over the years, repaintings have hidden the original patchwork, so today's letters show no trace of their improvised origin. At some point the tower's red and white stripes were also extended onto its top, where they converge in a spoke pattern easily visible from aircraft. That matters here more than most places. The tower stands due south of Runway 18L/36R at Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International Airport, so its striped crown has an audience overhead as well as on the road.

## Y'all, Everywhere

Florence embraced the accidental slogan as its own. A town festival held around Labor Day was renamed the Florence Y'all fest, and the phrase turns up on lapel pins, bumper stickers, mousepads, postcards and T-shirts. The tower has been copied, too: replicas have been built at Florence's World of Golf, a local miniature golf course, and in the Soft Play area inside Florence Mall. Baseball joined in. The local independent-league team celebrated the tower throughout its 17 seasons as the Florence Freedom, marketing the Frontier League's 2016 all-star game as the "Y'All-Star Game" with the tower in the official logo, and in January 2020 it renamed itself the Florence Y'alls. Its large inflatable mascot is the "Y'all Star," and the team has given away water-tower bobbleheads. The tower has even found its way into literary fiction, earning a mention from the narrator of the novel Ducks, Newburyport.

## Flight Context

It lies due south of Runway 18L/36R at Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International Airport (KCVG), about 3.2 nm south-southeast of the airport's reference point, so it is best seen from an airliner window on approach to or departure from that runway rather than on a low sightseeing pass; check current airspace charts and coordinate with ATC before any flight nearby.
