# Daniel Carter Beard Boyhood Home

The 1821 brick house on the Licking River in Covington, Kentucky, where a boy who idolized Daniel Boone grew up to become a founder of the Boy Scouts of America.

**Location:** 39.0892, -84.5056

## Introduction

During the Civil War, a band of boys in Covington, Kentucky, made a game of slipping past the sentries of the Union Army camps in town. They called themselves the Boone Scouts, after the frontiersman whose stories they had grown up hearing, and they tried their best to live up to him. One of them was Daniel Carter Beard. Half a century later he would help found the Boy Scouts of America and serve as its National Scout Commissioner for more than thirty years. The brick house where he spent those boyhood years still stands above the Licking River, a National Historic Landmark in the Riverside Drive Historic District.

## The House on the Licking

The house was already four decades old when the Beards arrived. Built in 1821, the two-and-a-half-story brick home is one of the two oldest buildings in Kenton County, and it looks out over the Licking River, across the Ohio River from Cincinnati. Daniel Carter Beard was born in Cincinnati in 1850 and moved here with his family when he was eleven, which placed his boyhood in Covington squarely in the years of the Civil War. Union Army camps dotted the town, and the river neighborhood became the backdrop for the games that shaped him.

## The Boone Scouts

Young Dan grew up on stories of Daniel Boone, and he idolized the frontiersman so completely that he and his friends named themselves the Boone Scouts and set out to emulate him. Their adventures included sneaking past the sentries of the Union camps, a test of stealth that any frontier scout would have respected. Looking back, those years were a rehearsal. The camping, hand crafts and love of nature that filled Beard's adult life were already taking root along this stretch of river. He studied civil engineering at Worrall's Academy in Covington, graduating in 1869, and later made his way to New York City. In 1878 his family followed him there, closing the Covington chapter of the Beard story.

## Uncle Dan

Beard started out as an engineer and surveyor, then attended art school in New York City and turned to illustration, drawing pictures for a number of Mark Twain's books. He also wrote a series of articles for St. Nicholas Magazine that became The American Boy's Handy Book in 1882. The boyhood game never really ended. In 1905 he founded the Sons of Daniel Boone, an organization for young people rooted in American frontier traditions, and when the Boy Scouts of America was founded in 1910, Beard was among its founders. He served as National Scout Commissioner from that year until his death, known to many simply as "Uncle Dan." He died on June 11, 1941, at the age of 90, at his home in Suffern, New York.

## A Parade for the Boone Scout

Beard came back to Covington one last time in 1934, when the town held a parade in his honor and Scouts from Kentucky, Ohio and West Virginia turned out to show their appreciation. The house itself had moved on. By the time it was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1965, it belonged to William Booth Memorial Hospital, which used it as a nursing school and a dormitory for nursing students; the old hospital building, now converted to condominiums, still stands to the north. Today the old brick house is a private residence, so it is best admired from the street. Look at it from the riverbank and it is easy to picture a boy slipping out the door to go scouting.
