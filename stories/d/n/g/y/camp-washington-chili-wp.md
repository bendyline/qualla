# Camp Washington Chili

A round-the-clock Cincinnati chili parlor, founded in 1940, that won a James Beard award the same year a street-widening project forced it to move a few lots down the road.

**Location:** 39.1376, -84.5378

**Categories:** Restaurants in Cincinnati, Companies based in Cincinnati, Restaurants established in 1940, America's Classics winners, 1940 establishments in Ohio

## Introduction

The doors at Camp Washington Chili open at 8 a.m. on Monday and do not close again until 4 p.m. on Saturday. For six days straight, at any hour, somebody can walk into this Cincinnati chili parlor at the corner of Hopple Street and Colerain Avenue and order a plate. It has been feeding the Camp Washington neighborhood, just outside downtown Cincinnati, since 1940, and along the way it has collected the kind of praise usually reserved for far fancier dining rooms, including a James Beard Foundation award.

## A Corner Since 1940

Steve Andon and Fred Zarnbus founded the parlor in 1940 in Camp Washington, a neighborhood near downtown Cincinnati in the southwestern corner of Ohio. Over the decades it grew into a well-known Cincinnati landmark. For decades it was run by its Greek-born owner, Johnny Johnson, who died in October 2025 at age 90; the parlor carries on, and it keeps remarkable hours: open around the clock from Monday morning to Saturday afternoon, and closed only on Sundays. In 2000 the restaurant faced a forced change. The city told it to vacate its old location so that Hopple Street could be widened. Rather than leave the neighborhood, Camp Washington Chili moved to a site just a few lots away, now at 3005 Colerain Avenue, where its new home was modeled on a 1950s-style diner. The timing turned out to be memorable for another reason. That same year, its chili won an "American Regional Classic" award from the James Beard Foundation, an honor the restaurant credits to its "timeless appeal and its commitment to quality food." A year of upheaval had also brought national recognition.

## A Shelf Full of Praise

The Beard award was not the first national notice, or the last. As early as 1985, CBS News named it "the best chili in the nation." In 2009 the food writers Jane and Michael Stern wrote that "when we crave the best, there is just one place to go," and in a New York Times interview they declared it the best maker of Cincinnati five-way chili. Travel + Leisure listed it among "America's Best Chili(s)", Bon Appetit included it among "The Best Chili Spots," and Smithsonian Magazine named it one of "The 20 Most Iconic Food Destinations Across America." Television followed, with a 2011 appearance on the Travel Channel's Man v. Food Nation, along with features from HGTV, the Huffington Post and Every Day with Rachael Ray.

## Worth a Song

Few restaurants inspire music, but this one did. The blues musician Lonnie Mack recorded a song called "Camp Washington Chili," a tribute that fits a place so woven into its city's identity. The parlor also sits in good company. Just up the road stands the American Sign Museum, another Camp Washington institution devoted to the everyday Americana of neon and roadside lettering, and the two make an easy pairing for a visitor with an appetite for local character. Come for a bowl, stay for the hours, and remember that on Sunday you will find the doors locked.
