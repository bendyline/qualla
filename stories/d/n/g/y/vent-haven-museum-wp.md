# Vent Haven Museum

A Cincinnati tile executive's lifelong hobby became Vent Haven, a Fort Mitchell, Kentucky, museum that bills itself as the only one in the world devoted to ventriloquism, with more than 1,000 figures waiting for a voice.

**Location:** 39.0530, -84.5520

## Introduction

In 1908, a tugboat foundered in the Gulf of Mexico with a ventriloquist named William Wood and his daughter aboard. Wood died, under circumstances that remain unclear. Later, his performance figures washed ashore. Four of them are said to have ended up in Fort Mitchell, Kentucky, a few miles south of Cincinnati, at a museum that claims to be the only one of its kind in the world. Vent Haven Museum holds more than a thousand ventriloquist figures, a crowd of silent faces, each one built to speak in someone else's voice.

## The Man from the Mail Room

The collection began with a businessman, not a star. William Shakespeare Berger, a Cincinnati native known to friends as W.S., started in the mail room of the Cambridge Tile Company in 1895 at the age of 17, and retired as the company's president in 1947. He was an amateur ventriloquist, and in 1910 he bought his first figure, a dummy called Tommy Baloney. He kept on buying. The figures first lived in his home in Fort Mitchell, but the collection grew quickly through the 1930s and 1940s, and in 1947 Berger renovated his garage to house them, adding a second building in 1962. By his death in 1972 he had gathered roughly 500 dummies.

## A Voice for the Whole Community

Berger's passion reached far beyond his garage. From the late 1940s until 1960 he served as president of the International Brotherhood of Ventriloquists, published a monthly magazine called The Oracle, and corresponded with ventriloquists around the world, by the museum's account sometimes writing as many as 50 letters a week. Late in life he faced a problem. He had outlived his wife, his sons and his grandson, and he feared that after his death the collection would be split up and scattered. With his attorney, John R.S. Brooking, he set up a charitable foundation to hold his property and his figures. Berger died in 1972, and on June 30, 1973, Vent Haven opened as a public museum.

## A Thousand Silent Faces

Donations have kept arriving ever since, and the collection has doubled in size since 1973. Today it counts more than 1,000 ventriloquist figures from 20 countries, along with hundreds of photographs and other memorabilia. Famous names are well represented. There are replicas of figures used by Edgar Bergen, Paul Winchell and Shari Lewis, and genuine performance pieces that worked on stage with Jimmy Nelson, Willie Tyler, Jay Johnson, Terry Fator, Jeff Dunham and Darci Lynne. Seen together, they span generations of the craft. Vent Haven is a specialized museum rather than a walk-in attraction: it is open by advance appointment only, from May through September, at its home on West Maple Avenue. Its annual ConVENTion for ventriloquists, now held at a hotel in nearby Erlanger, marked its 50th anniversary in July 2026, a sign that Berger's community of performers outlived him too.

## The Figures from the Gulf

The most haunting objects may be the four performance figures that reportedly belonged to William Wood. What exactly happened when his tugboat went down in 1908 remains unclear, but the dummies were recovered after washing ashore and eventually found their way into the museum. Their story drew enough interest that a 2013 episode of the television series Mysteries at the Museum recounted the circumstances of Wood's death and the figures now kept at Vent Haven. They are a reminder that behind every wooden face was a real performer, with a life and a livelihood built around making an audience believe.
