# Heritage USA

Jim and Tammy Faye Bakker's 2,300-acre Christian resort in Fort Mill once rivaled the Disney parks for visitors, then collapsed amid scandal, fraud and a hurricane, leaving a half-finished tower and a landscape of second lives.

**Location:** 35.0606, -80.9118

**Categories:** Defunct amusement parks in the United States, Amusement parks in South Carolina, Fort Mill, South Carolina, Televangelism, Amusement parks opened in 1978

## Introduction

In 1987, the televangelist Jerry Falwell went down a 163-foot water slide at Heritage USA wearing a suit. Time magazine reported that he "plunged" down the "hellish" Typhoon to keep "a promise made during a fund-raising drive that netted $20 million for the debt-ridden PTL," and photographs of the plunge circulated worldwide. The fund drive was meant to help rescue an empire: a 2,300-acre Christian water park, theme park and residential complex in Fort Mill, South Carolina, built by Jim and Tammy Faye Bakker. Within two years it was closed.

## A Christian Disneyland

The Bakkers, founders of the television ministry The PTL Club, opened Heritage USA in 1978, and church builder Roe Messner built it. It grew into a world of its own. There was the 501-room Heritage Grand Hotel, an indoor shopping street called Main Street USA, the Heritage Village Church and the Upper Room, a 400-unit campground, the Jerusalem Amphitheater, a skating rink, television production studios, a Bible and evangelism school, prayer and counseling services, timeshares, and the Heritage Island water park. By 1986 it reportedly employed around 2,500 people and drew millions of visitors a year (nearly six million in 1986, according to The Charlotte Observer); by the park's own estimate, only Walt Disney World and Disneyland drew more. People called it a Christian version of Disneyland, and before the scandal that ended it, Jim Bakker was planning to expand.

## The Fall of PTL

The collapse began in 1987. According to Time, Bakker had arranged that March for Falwell to take over PTL, hoping to head off what he called a "hostile takeover" by people threatening to expose a sexual encounter he admitted having seven years earlier with a church secretary, who has said she was drugged and raped; Bakker later insisted it was consensual. Falwell's fund drive raised money, but the ministry's troubles deepened. At a peak when the park was earning $126 million a year, the IRS revoked its tax exemption, and attendance fell as Bakker's legal and personal troubles filled the headlines. Falwell argued that the park competed unfairly with tax-paying attractions and that the tax-exempt ministry should be separated from PTL's business interests. Under his leadership, Heritage USA sought Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection with debts estimated at $72 million. In 1988 Bakker was indicted on 24 counts of fraud and conspiracy, and in 1989 a jury convicted him on all of them. Sentenced to 45 years, he was paroled in 1994 after serving almost five.

## Six Dollars and Fifty-Four Cents

Among those left behind were the faithful who had paid in. In return for a promised four-day, three-night vacation stay every year for life, roughly 160,000 people had each given $1,000; the U.S. Postal Inspection Service counts 159,903 lifetime partners by the end of 1987, and Wikipedia puts the number at 165,000. According to prosecutors at his fraud trial, only one 500-room hotel was ever finished. By one account, each donor ended up with $6.54 in compensation. The land took its own blow in September 1989, when Hurricane Hugo severely damaged many of the buildings, and Heritage USA closed shortly afterward. The Christian Disneyland that had promised believers a lifetime of vacations lasted a little more than a decade.

## Second Lives

In 1991, San Diego evangelist Morris Cerullo and the Malaysian investment group MUI bought the property for $52 million and called it New Heritage USA. The partners fell out, MUI bought Cerullo's share and renamed the land Regent Park, and Laura Ashley moved its U.S. headquarters into the pyramid-shaped building where the Bakkers once had their offices. Golf fairways and subdivisions spread over much of the old park, and a Radisson resort in the hotel failed. The unfinished Heritage tower passed in 2004 to MorningStar Fellowship Church, which spent years in legal disputes with York County over whether to finish it or tear it down. A settlement in late 2024 required the church either to pursue construction permits or face demolition, and in 2026 reports described new momentum toward demolition and a senior-living project on the site, though no demolition date had been set. The King's Castle, once meant to hold the world's largest Wendy's, was demolished in 2013, and the Jerusalem Amphitheater came down in 2012; its campground lives on as Crown Cove RV Park. The Upper Room was bought in 2010 with plans to reopen it as a chapel, and in 2018 MorningStar's leader, Rick Joyner, spoke of rebuilding the theme park itself.

## Flight Context

The former Heritage USA site lies in Fort Mill, South Carolina, at about 35.06°N, 80.91°W, near Interstate 77, where a billboard once called for the old hotel tower's demolition. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, look for the unfinished Heritage tower (as of October 2026 still standing, with demolition under discussion), the pyramid-shaped Regent Building, and golf fairways and subdivisions spread across the former park grounds. The site sits beneath Charlotte's busy Class B airspace: Charlotte Douglas International Airport (KCLT) is only about 9 nm away, so talk to Charlotte Approach and follow ATC instructions. Rock Hill-York County Airport (KUZA, about 8 nm) is the closest general aviation field; Charlotte-Monroe Executive Airport (FAA identifier EQY, GPS code KEQY) and Gastonia Municipal Airport (FAA identifier AKH, GPS code KAKH) are each about 15 nm away.
