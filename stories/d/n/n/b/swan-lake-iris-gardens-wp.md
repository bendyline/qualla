# Swan Lake Iris Gardens

A Sumter businessman threw his failed irises into a fishing lake in disgust - and the flowers that bloomed there the next spring grew into a public garden that the city says is the only public park in the United States with all eight species of swans.

**Location:** 33.9189, -80.3697

**Categories:** Gardens in South Carolina, Sumter, South Carolina, Protected areas of Sumter County, South Carolina, Parks, Festivals

## Introduction

Hamilton Carr Bland gave up on his irises. The wealthy Sumter, South Carolina, businessman had begun landscaping the grounds of his private fishing lake with Japanese irises, and when they failed, he ordered his gardener to dig up every one and throw them into the water. The following spring, the discarded flowers bloomed. The blooms caught the attention of locals. Nearly a century later, that accidental bloom has grown into Swan Lake Iris Gardens, a public park that also claims a rare distinction: it is described as the only public park in the United States to have all eight species of swans.

## From Fishing Hole to Gift

The lake began in 1927 as Bland's private retreat, a place to fish rather than to host crowds. The irises changed its character, and in 1938 the gardens grew. A.T. Heath, Sr. deeded 30 acres on the north side of West Liberty Street to Bland, with the condition that the land be developed as part of the gardens. In 1949 the whole property passed to the City of Sumter, and the private lake officially became a public park. The city wasted little time: development of a Braille garden and a Butterfly Garden started in 1950. The two halves of the park, split by West Liberty Street, were finally stitched together in 1994 by the McDuffie overpass. The Heath family's generosity continued as well. Land behind the garden that the family donated to the city opened in 2002 as the Heath Pavilion.

## Eight Kinds of Swan

The swans give the lake its name and much of its fame. Royal white mutes, Black Necks and Coscorobas are here, and so are Whoopers, Black Australians, Whistlers, Bewicks and Trumpeters - the full set of eight, by the city's count, in one city park. The birds are central to the park's identity. When the main entrance closed for renovations on July 29, 2019, the new gateway that opened in February 2020 celebrates them. It showcases a 24-foot sculpture by Grainger McKoy titled Seven Swans. McKoy's work was already part of the park: in 2010 he created another sculpture, Recovery, which stands inside the grounds.

## A Garden That Smells Like Chocolate

Not every corner of Swan Lake is about irises and birds. The Chocolate Garden, established in 2009, is built around a single playful theme. Its edible plants include Chocolate Cherry Tomatoes, Chocolate Corn and Chocolate Mini Bell Peppers. Alongside them grow flowers chosen for a chocolate scent or color: Chocolate Daisy, Chocolate Mint and Chocolate Cosmos. It is a garden designed to be smelled as much as seen. Together with the Braille and Butterfly gardens, it shows a park that has kept finding new ways to invite people in, long after the first irises took root in the lake.

## Iris Season and a Million Lights

Two annual events bring the park big moments. Every May, the three-day Iris Festival fills the park (for years it ran over Memorial Day weekend, and in 2026 it was held May 15 to 17) with food, music, vendors, arts and crafts, car shows and children's activities. It is described as one of the largest and longest continuously running festivals in the Southeast. Most of the action sits inside the main entrance gate, with more vendors and activities across West Liberty Street on the north side of the garden. When winter comes, the park glows. The Fantasy of Lights spreads more than 1,000,000 Christmas lights through the main parking lot, along West Liberty Street and in another display on Garden Street. The city bills it as South Carolina's largest free Christmas light display, and it all began with a pile of irises that refused to die.

## Flight Context

Swan Lake Iris Gardens lies at approximately 33.9189 N, 80.3697 W on the west side of Sumter, South Carolina, straddling West Liberty Street. The lake surrounded by gardens is visible from 2,000-3,000 ft MSL over flat Midlands terrain. Nearby airports: Sumter Airport (KSMS; about 5 nm north) and Florence Regional Airport (KFLO; about 36 nm east-northeast, scheduled service). Shaw Air Force Base (KSSC) lies about 6 nm west-northwest - a military field, so expect military traffic and check airspace restrictions before overflying the area. Columbia Metropolitan Airport (KCAE) is about 38 nm west. Summer brings hazy skies and afternoon thunderstorms; the Iris Festival is held over a spring weekend in May (May 15 to 17 in 2026).
