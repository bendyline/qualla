# Burrows Cave

A cave of gold statues, mummies and inscribed stones supposedly lies near the Embarras River in southern Illinois, but no one has ever found it, and archaeologists regard Burrows Cave as a hoax.

**Location:** 38.7333, -88.0833

**Categories:** Hoaxes in the United States, Pseudoarchaeology, Archaeological forgeries, Pre-Columbian transoceanic contact theories, Richland County, Illinois

## Introduction

Somewhere near the Embarras River in Richland County, Illinois, there is supposed to be a cave filled with life-sized gold statues, gold coffins, mummies, diamonds, scrolls, swords and suits of armor. No one has ever located it. Since 1982, when Russell E. Burrows said he had stumbled into it, the cave has remained a story without a place, and because of the many inconsistencies and the lack of evidence behind his claims, archaeologists and anthropologists consider Burrows Cave a hoax. How the legend grew anyway is the real story.

## The Pit Beneath the Metal Detector

Burrows, born in 1935, is described as a Korean War veteran and former Illinois prison guard who lived in Olney, a short distance from the alleged site. He has claimed to have studied archaeology for two years at West Virginia University in the mid-1950s, a claim that has not been independently verified. By his account he was a pothunter, searching for artifacts near the river, when he found the cave. In one version his metal detector began to oscillate rapidly and he fell into a narrow vertical pit. Below, he said, lay long corridors, a series of sealed portals, statues, mummies, bronze weapons and portraits showing an apparent mix of Romans, Phoenicians, Hebrews, Christians, American Indians, and even Black Africans. He never revealed the precise location, saying he feared people would rob the cave of its treasures.

## A Guard and an Inmate

Much of what the world knows about the cave comes from Frank Collin, a former American Nazi Party activist who was an inmate at the Pontiac Correctional Center while Burrows worked there as a guard. After his release Collin reinvented himself as a writer under the name Frank Joseph, and he went on to publish articles and books about Burrows Cave with differing and conflicting details. Many of the treasures Burrows described have never been inspected by archaeologists or anthropologists because of his extreme secrecy. Perhaps the artifacts he is best known for are carved stone tablets bearing writing in unknown scripts, many with images said to represent Romans, Hebrews, Egyptians, Sumerians, Greeks and Phoenicians. Thousands of them have been sold to private collectors.

## An Oar Over the Bow

Until about 1993, the cave's promoters presented it as part of a mid-first-millennium colony trading Michigan copper to the Old World, and a tablet said to depict a Phoenician ship was central to the claim. Joseph reproduced it in his book The Lost Treasure of King Juba, placing it beside a genuine image of a Phoenician vessel. His reproduction cropped the Burrows stone, making the paddle end of one steering oar unidentifiable. The carving still showed steering oars at both ends of the ship, something impossible on a real vessel. Anthropologist and geographer George F. Carter, himself a supporter of trans-cultural diffusion, was critical. "The 'author' did not recognize the paired oars, and hung an 'impossible' oar over the bow," he commented.

## From Copper Traders to the Crucifixion

Then the story changed. Around 1993 Ancient American, a magazine that asserts the events of the Book of Mormon took place in North America and which Joseph himself edited, began publishing articles about the cave. Burrows now produced photographs of Judeo-Christian artifacts, explaining that he had held them back for fear of ridicule, and Joseph argued that the people who used the cave had witnessed the crucifixion of Jesus. The cave has since featured on America Unearthed and on the History Channel's Holy Grail in America. Thomas Emerson, Illinois's first state archaeologist (2013 until his retirement in 2018) and earlier chief archaeologist of the Illinois Historic Preservation Agency, warned that the claims of Burrows Cave proponents were sensational and not backed by evidence, and with no evidence that the cave exists, many archaeologists quickly dismissed it. More than four decades after its supposed discovery, it has still never been found.

## Flight Context

This story's map point, 38.7333°N, 88.0833°W, sits at Olney in Richland County, Illinois, the town where Burrows lived; the alleged cave has never been located, so there is no site to see. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, Olney's street grid stands out amid flat farmland, and the Embarras River, near which Burrows placed the cave, winds through the county. Olney-Noble Airport (KOLY) is about 4 nm west, Lawrenceville-Vincennes International Airport (KLWV) is about 22 nm east, and Evansville Regional Airport (KEVV) is about 49 nm south-southeast. Visibility is usually good over the prairie, with summer haze and thunderstorms the main concerns.
