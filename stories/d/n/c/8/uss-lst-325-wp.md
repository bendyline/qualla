# USS LST-325

A veteran of Sicily, Omaha Beach and, by her published history, Salerno that served two navies and crossed the Atlantic again in 2000–2001, LST-325 now rests in Evansville as a memorial to the ships the city built by the hundred.

**Location:** 37.9729, -87.5804

**Categories:** Museum ships, World War II, Amphibious warfare vessels, Military museums in Indiana, Evansville, Indiana, Ohio River

## Introduction

During World War II, Evansville's riverfront produced 167 tank landing ships, at a pace that reached roughly one every three or four days at the shipyard's peak. The LST now moored on that same riverfront is not one of them. USS LST-325 was launched in Philadelphia in 1942, took part in as many as three invasions, served in two navies, and crossed the Atlantic from Greece in the hands of retired servicemen before finding a home in the city that built so many of her sisters. Like many ships of her class, she never received a name. She is known only by her hull number, and she has made it count.

## Three Beaches

LST-325 was launched on October 27, 1942, and commissioned on February 1, 1943, under Lieutenant Ira Ehrensall of the Naval Reserve. She went to work in the North Africa area and took part in the invasion at Gela, Sicily, and, according to her published history, the landings at Salerno, Italy; the NavSource ship record credits her with battle stars only for Sicily and Normandy.

On June 6, 1944, she joined the largest armada in history for the Normandy landings at Omaha Beach. On that first trip she carried 59 vehicles, 30 officers and 396 enlisted men. On her first run back to England, her cargo was 38 casualties. Over the next nine months, Navy records show, she made more than 40 crossings of the English Channel, carrying thousands of men and the equipment they needed to complete the liberation of Europe. She kept running supply trips between England and France until she headed home to the United States in May 1945.

## From the Arctic to the Aegean

Peace brought a pause. LST-325 was decommissioned on July 2, 1946, at Green Cove Springs, Florida, and laid up in the Atlantic Reserve Fleet. In 1951 she returned to service with the Military Sea Transportation Service as USNS T-LST-325 and joined Operation SUNAC, the Support of North Atlantic Construction. Her work took her into the Labrador Sea, Davis Strait and Baffin Bay, helping build radar outposts along the eastern shore of Canada and western Greenland.

Struck from the Naval Vessel Register on September 1, 1961, she passed to the Maritime Administration for lay-up in the National Defense Reserve Fleet. In 1964 she was sent to Greece under the grant-in-aid program; sources give the date as May 1 or September 1. For more than three decades she served the Hellenic Navy as RHS Syros (L-144).

## The Veterans' Voyage

In 2000, a group of retired military men organized as USS LST Memorial, Inc. acquired Syros. They traveled to Greece, made the necessary repairs, and sailed her back across the Atlantic, arriving in Mobile Harbor on January 10, 2001.

She did not sit still for long. In 2003 she made a sentimental journey up the Mississippi and Ohio rivers, and during a 10-day stop in Evansville more than 35,000 people came aboard. In May and June 2005 she sailed up the East Coast under her own power on a 60-day tour, visiting Alexandria, Virginia, as well as Buzzards Bay, Boston and Gloucester in Massachusetts. Her memorial bills her as the only operational LST in World War II configuration afloat in U.S. waters, and by Wikipedia's count only one other navigable LST is in service in the United States: LST-510, now the ferry Cape Henlopen, which still carries passengers and cars between Orient, New York, and New London, Connecticut.

## Home to the Shipbuilders

Evansville was a natural home port. During the war its riverfront became a 45-acre shipyard that employed roughly 17,000 to 19,000 people, depending on the account, and became the nation's largest inland producer of LSTs. The yard eventually delivered 167 LSTs and 35 other vessels.

LST-325 made Evansville her home port on October 1, 2005, and serves as a memorial museum to the LSTs and the city's war effort, while still visiting other ports; her annual cruise takes her away for about a month (in 2026, September 15 to October 15, with Evansville tours resuming October 16), so check the tour schedule. She was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2009. On June 13, 2020, she moved from her Marina Pointe berth to a new port on Riverside Drive at Riverfront Park, across from Bally's Evansville, in a spot once occupied by the casino's own riverboat. Aboard, visitors can walk the tank deck, the troop compartments and the engine room, where thousands of men once rode toward the beaches.

## Flight Context

LST-325 is moored at approximately 37.97°N, 87.58°W on the Evansville, Indiana, riverfront along Riverside Drive, on the north bank of the Ohio River. Her long, low hull alongside the riverbank is best picked out at 1,500–2,500 feet AGL while following the river. Evansville Regional Airport (KEVV) is about 5 nm to the north-northeast, so expect nearby airport traffic. Owensboro-Daviess County Regional (KOWB) is about 24 nm to the southeast, and Henderson City-County Airport across the river in Kentucky (FAA identifier EHR) is about 11 nm to the south-southwest. Best in clear weather.
