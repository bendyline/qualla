# Bosse Field

Born of a mayor's political maneuver in 1915, Evansville's Bosse Field was billed as the nation's first municipally owned sports stadium and is still hosting professional baseball more than a century later.

**Location:** 37.9928, -87.5622

**Categories:** Baseball venues, Sports venues in Evansville, Indiana, Minor league baseball venues, Historic ballparks, Film locations

## Introduction

When the president of Evansville's school board opposed Mayor Benjamin Bosse's plan for a new ballpark, Bosse had him replaced with a friend. The reshaped board approved the project and, for good measure, voted to name the stadium after the mayor. Bosse Field opened on June 17, 1915, billed as the first municipally owned sports stadium in the United States, and it is still in regular use for professional baseball. Only two ballparks with that distinction are older: Fenway Park in Boston, from 1912, and Wrigley Field in Chicago, from 1914.

## A Mayor's Maneuver

Benjamin Bosse served as mayor of Evansville from 1914 to 1922, and he moved fast. Shortly after taking office in January 1914, he reached an agreement with Thomas Garvin's family to buy land on the north side of the city for a park. There was one problem: the city was running a deficit and could not afford the full $50,000 price.

Bosse's solution was to sell part of the land to the school board, which would then build a stadium serving both school functions and Evansville's professional baseball team. It was not a disinterested plan, since Bosse was a co-owner of that team. When the board's president objected, the mayor simply swapped him out. Construction was finished the following summer, and Evansville had a public ballpark that its taxpayers and its students could both call their own.

## Opening Day, 1915

The dedication drew a crowd of 8,082 fans, a Central League record, and the home team sent them away happy with a 4–0 win. The timing turned out to be lucky. Evansville was in third place when it moved into its new home in June 1915, and it went on to win the league championship.

That was the first of many titles celebrated here. The River Rats, who had also won the Central League in 1908, were followed over the decades by the Evas, Pocketeers and Hubs, the Braves and the Triplets. The Braves took Three-I League titles in 1946, 1948, 1956 and 1957, and the Triplets won the American Association in 1972, 1975 and 1979. In all, ten teams besides the current tenants have called the park home, and the Evansville Otters of the Frontier League added their own title in 2006.

## Legends Passing Through

Minor-league parks are where careers are made, and Bosse Field has seen some remarkable ones. Hall of Famers including Hank Greenberg, Chuck Klein and Warren Spahn played here during their careers. Bob Uecker, later honored by the Hall of Fame as a broadcaster, spent part of the 1957 season with the Evansville Braves. By one count, another 95 Hall of Fame members visited Bosse Field as members of opposing teams.

The park's résumé runs well beyond minor-league baseball. It has hosted spring training for the Detroit Tigers, college baseball, high school and college football, NFL football, and college soccer. It has also hosted concerts, among them an August 20, 1974 bill featuring the Allman Brothers Band, Elvin Bishop and REO Speedwagon.

## A Hollywood Close-Up

In 1991, Columbia Pictures brought its cameras to Bosse Field to film numerous game scenes for A League of Their Own, the 1992 comedy-drama. By then the park had been hosting baseball for 76 years, and it was about to reach a far larger audience than any Central League crowd.

Today the stadium seats roughly 5,100 to 5,200 people. The Evansville Otters, of the Frontier League (which announced in 2026 that it will become the National Association of Professional Baseball for the 2027 season), play their home games here, and so do high school and American Legion teams, so the same field serves professionals and teenagers alike. A ballpark that began with one mayor's political maneuvering has ended up belonging, in the most practical sense, to the whole city, though the field is owned by the Evansville Vanderburgh School Corporation, whose board approved $2.1 million in July 2026 to install artificial turf and new drainage after the Otters' season.

## Flight Context

Bosse Field sits at approximately 37.99°N, 87.56°W on the north side of Evansville, Indiana, with the Ohio River along the city's southern edge. The open diamond and grandstand are easiest to pick out at 1,500–2,500 feet AGL. Evansville Regional Airport (KEVV) is only about 3 nm to the north-northeast, so expect airport traffic nearby. Owensboro-Daviess County Regional (KOWB) is about 24 nm to the southeast. Best in clear weather.
