# Bluespring Caverns

When a farm pond near Bedford, Indiana, vanished overnight in 1940, it opened a way into Bluespring Caverns, a limestone cave system holding what is billed as the longest navigable underground river in the United States.

**Location:** 38.7962, -86.5460

**Categories:** Caves of Indiana, Show caves in the United States, Limestone caves, Tourist attractions in Lawrence County, Indiana, Karst

## Introduction

In 1940, after a severe storm passed through the area, a small pond on George Colglazier's farm vanished overnight. Where the water had been, there was now a sinkhole, and beneath it lay a river running through the dark. That hole became the present-day entrance to Bluespring Caverns, a cave system in Lawrence County, roughly 70 miles south of Indianapolis, with some 21 miles of surveyed passages and approximately three miles of navigable underground river, which the park bills as America's longest navigable underground river.

## The Spring That Went Silent

The pond was not the cave's first doorway. The system was known as early as the 19th century, and until 1913 people entered through the mouth of a spring that drained into the White River. That year a newly completed dam on the White River closed the entrance off. The new sinkhole on the Colglazier farm opened a second way in. The undeveloped cave quickly became the newest adventure spot on the family's 100-acre farm, and George Colglazier's sons Norman, James, Lee and Daniel were fascinated by it, though their sisters were less frequent visitors. Word spread about the new opening, and before long people from around the world were descending on a small dairy farm to see what lay underneath.

## Farmers, Not Showmen

George Colglazier and his wife, Eva, kept living on the farm and welcomed visitors to the caverns until George's death in the early 1980s. Neither of them ever profited from the cave. They made their living as hard-working dairy farmers, and they let cave explorers onto their property freely, asking them to obey a single rule: "not to destroy or remove from the caverns." It was the only thing they asked of their guests. Today visitation is controlled by Bluespring Caverns Park, a commercial corporation, and the cave has joined Marengo Cave, Wyandotte Caves, Indiana Caverns and Squire Boone Caverns on the list of Indiana's show caves.

## Limestone, Shale and Moving Water

Bluespring is a karst and river cave, and it drains a sinkhole plain of about 15 square miles. The passages formed in two kinds of limestone, Salem Limestone and St. Louis Limestone, separated by a thin layer of shale. Down here the seasons disappear. The cave holds a constant temperature all year long, somewhere between about 52°F and 54°F (11°C to 14°C), depending on the account. Crayfish, salamanders, crickets, spiders and beetles live in the passages, but the most remarkable resident is the rare, sightless Northern cavefish, which is abundant in this system. A small number of bats also shelter here, especially in winter, though the frequent flooding discourages them from staying year-round. Bluespring has been ranked the third-longest cave in Indiana.

## A Boat Ride Into the Dark

The featured way to meet the cave is on its river. In 2026 the park is open daily from mid-March through October 31, when visitors board a lighted electric boat carrying up to 17 people for an hour-long ride of about a mile and a quarter along the subterranean stream, and organized youth groups can book overnight adventures that include camping underground. The cave has inspired art as well as exploration. The artist Maya Lin explored the caverns, and her 2007 installation Above and Below, drawn from that experience, hangs at the Indianapolis Museum of Art (now part of Newfields). A pond that disappeared one stormy night has led, in the end, to a river tour, a family legacy and a work of art.

## Flight Context

Bluespring Caverns lies at 38.7962°N, 86.5460°W in Lawrence County, Indiana, west of Bedford and near the White River. The cave itself is underground, so from the air look for the White River valley and the farmland of the sinkhole plain above the cave; 2,000–3,000 feet AGL gives a good view of the karst country. The closest airport is Virgil I. Grissom Municipal at Bedford (KBFR), about 5 nm away. Others nearby: French Lick Municipal (KFRH), about 18 nm, and Monroe County Airport at Bloomington (KBMG), about 21 nm. Best in clear daytime weather.
