# Jug Rock

Outside Shoals, Indiana, a pillar of sandstone, 42 feet tall by an 1871 measurement, topped by a flat "stopper" stands apart from its cliff, billed as the largest free-standing table rock east of the Mississippi and the namesake of the local high school's Jug Rox.

**Location:** 38.6833, -86.8000

**Categories:** Rock formations of Indiana, Geology, Protected areas of Martin County, Indiana, Natural landmarks

## Introduction

It really does look like a jug. A tall mass of sandstone rises in the valley of the East Fork of the White River outside Shoals, Indiana, bulging in the middle and capped by a flat, projecting slab that has been called the "stopper" since at least 1871. Jug Rock is billed as the largest free-standing table rock formation, also called a "mushroom rock," in the United States east of the Mississippi River. It has been drawing admirers for at least 150 years.

## A Geologist's Postcard From 1871

Among its early chroniclers was Indiana's state geologist, E. T. Cox, in the Second Report of the Geological Survey of Indiana, published in 1871. Cox recommended the spot near Shoals, on the road to the Indian Sulphur Springs, as one of the most interesting places to see this kind of scenery. He described a high ridge of millstone grit ending within a few yards of the river, topped by a projecting mass of conglomerate called the "Pinnacle" that stood 170 feet above the stream, with "Cyclopean blocks" lying around its foot "in every conceivable position." On the north side of the ridge stood the jug itself, 42 feet high by his measurement (modern visitor guides give heights from 45 to 60 feet), named for its "fancied resemblance" to one. Cox even noted the details: irregular lines of false bedding just above the bulge, and a lower part thickly set with quartz pebbles.

## Cut Loose From the Cliff

The rock belongs to the Mansfield formation, sandstone laid down during the Pennsylvanian, some three hundred million years ago. It did not always stand alone. Erosion worked along fracture lines in the stone until the column separated from a nearby cliff, leaving a pillar standing on its own, still carrying its flat, projecting cap. Jug Rock has company. A companion feature, House Rock, stands opposite it, and together with the Pinnacle they make this stretch of the valley a gallery of sandstone shapes. In Cox's day, the frontispiece of his report showed Jug Rock, copied from a photograph taken by D. Allbright of Shoals. Cox thanked Allbright and B. F. Devol, also of Shoals, for this "faithful representative of a highly interesting geological scene."

## Mascot in Stone

Today Jug Rock is part of Jug Rock Nature Preserve, which is owned or managed by the Indiana Department of Natural Resources, and a short trail from a roadside pull-off leads to its base. It has also become part of the town's identity. Nearby Shoals High School named its teams after it, the Shoals Jug Rox, and the mascot is a jug rock called "Roxer Boxer." Few schools can claim a landmark quite like it: a pillar of ancient sandstone that has stood through the whole history of the town and long before it.

Jug Rock stands at about 38.683°N, 86.800°W just outside Shoals, in Martin County, Indiana, on the valley side of the East Fork White River.
