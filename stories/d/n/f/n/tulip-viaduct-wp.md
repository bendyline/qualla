# Tulip Viaduct

Striding some 2,300 feet across the Richland Creek valley on eighteen steel towers, the Tulip Viaduct was billed as the longest rail trestle in the United States when Italian immigrant crews finished it in 1906, and it still carries trains through the hills of Greene County, Indiana.

**Location:** 39.0753, -86.8552

**Categories:** Railroad bridges in Indiana, Trestle bridges in the United States, Illinois Central Railroad, Greene County, Indiana, Bridges completed in 1906

## Introduction

The graffiti reportedly stays. Over the years, layer upon layer of spray paint has built up on the Tulip Viaduct, especially at the bases of the towers nearest the road, and the Indiana Rail Road is said to have left it there. By one account the thickness of the paint keeps rain and snow from rusting the steel, which would make the vandalism an unlikely form of preservation. Above those painted feet, the trestle runs about 2,300 feet (2,295 by the most commonly cited figure) across the valley of Richland Creek between Solsberry and Tulip in Greene County, Indiana, rising 157 feet above the ground at its highest point. The authors of Railroads of Indiana called it "easily the state's most spectacular railroad bridge."

## Coal and a Silent Partner

The bridge was built for coal. Its purpose was to carry trains loaded at Greene County's mines to large cities such as Chicago, and it was built in 1905 and 1906 by the Indianapolis Southern Railway and its successor, the Indianapolis Southern Railroad. Behind them stood a larger player. The work was secretly financed by the Illinois Central Railroad, and the Indianapolis Southern became part of the Illinois Central, in 1911 by most accounts. A groundbreaking ceremony led by Joe Moss opened construction on May 22, 1905. Who actually raised the steel is a matter of some interest: a placard that once hung beneath the tracks on a large I-beam on the western side of the bridge credited the New York Bridge Company, though other sources name the American Bridge Company. By 2004 that placard had been removed.

## Thirty Cents an Hour

The trestle was built mostly by Italian immigrant laborers, men who were paid up to 30 cents an hour, considered an excellent wage in 1906. What they assembled was enormous: 2,700 short tons of steel, arranged as individual spans of 40 and 74 feet resting on eighteen towers. The original cost came to $246,504, estimated at about $20 million in 2020 dollars. When the viaduct was finished in December 1906, it was described as the longest rail trestle in the United States (a longer wooden trestle across Great Salt Lake had opened in 1904) and the third-longest bridge of its kind in the world. Others have since surpassed it, among them the Hi-Line Railroad Bridge in Valley City, North Dakota, at 3,860 feet.

## Still Carrying Trains

Remarkably little has changed. Two 45-foot sections were added in 1916, and otherwise the bridge stands as it was originally constructed. Passenger trains once crossed the valley here too, until passenger service was discontinued in 1948. Today the viaduct is part of the Indiana Rail Road's line between Indianapolis and Newton, Illinois. It goes by several names, the Greene County Viaduct and the Tulip Trestle among them, and officially it is Bridge X76-6. Whatever it is called, it remains a working piece of early twentieth-century engineering, its steel legs planted in a creek valley and its deck still part of a working rail line more than a century after the Italian crews finished it.

## Flight Context

The Tulip Viaduct crosses the Richland Creek valley at about 39.075°N, 86.855°W in Greene County, Indiana, between the hamlets of Solsberry and Tulip, west of Bloomington. From 1,500–2,500 feet AGL the long line of steel towers striding across the valley is unmistakable in good light; follow the Indiana Rail Road line to find it. Nearest airports: Monroe County Airport at Bloomington (KBMG), about 12 nm east; Virgil I. Grissom Municipal at Bedford (KBFR), about 24 nm southeast. Check current charts before flying the area; the viaduct is easiest to spot in clear daytime conditions.
