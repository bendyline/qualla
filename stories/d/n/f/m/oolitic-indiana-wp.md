# Oolitic, Indiana

A small quarry town named for the stone beneath it, Oolitic, Indiana, sent more than 35 million pounds of limestone to build the Empire State Building and is home to a limestone statue of a comic-strip boxer.

**Location:** 38.9009, -86.5253

**Categories:** Towns in Lawrence County, Indiana, Limestone quarrying, Geology, Empire State Building, Populated places established in 1896

## Introduction

For the Empire State Building, which opened in 1931, workers cut roughly 35 million pounds of limestone block (35,017,500 by one count) out of a quarry that Wikipedia places about half a mile north of a small Indiana town, and shipped it to Manhattan, where it became part of the 102-story skyscraper. The pit they left behind is known as the Empire Hole, and it sits on private quarry land. The town beside it is Oolitic, population 1,137 at the 2020 census, and it takes its unusual name from the very rock that left for New York.

## A Name the Post Office Refused

The town was not supposed to be called Oolitic. The site was first platted as "Limestone" on September 26, 1888. When the name was sent to Washington, D.C., in 1896 so that a post office could be established, the request was refused, because Indiana already had a town named Limestone. Doctor R. B. Short suggested "Oolitic" instead. The Bedford Quarries Company platted the town on March 23, 1896, and it was incorporated by the end of 1901. Its purpose was never in doubt. Oolitic was founded to quarry the stone it sits on, for building, and it grew with the trade: its population was 1,079 in 1910 and had risen to about 2,000 by 1914. Today the town covers less than a square mile, in Indian Creek and Shawswick townships of Lawrence County.

## Stone Made of Eggs

Oolite is a type of limestone found in Indiana, and the town is built directly upon it. The word "oolitic" describes that kind of limestone, and it comes from a Greek root meaning "egg stone." The name fits what the rock is made of. Oolite forms from ooids, spherical grains built up in concentric layers, most between a quarter of a millimeter and two millimeters across. The limestone beneath Oolitic is a sedimentary rock that includes small shells left behind when this area was covered by an inland sea. That ancient seabed is the reason the town exists, and the reason it has the name it does.

## Hauling Out the Skyline

Just north of town lie some of the largest limestone quarries in the world, many of them worked since the nineteenth century. Many famous buildings have been built from local oolitic limestone, and the most famous of all is the Empire State Building. The Empire Hole supplied the stone for its construction around 1930, and, according to Wikipedia, stone for the skyscraper's 2001 renovations came from the same hole. If so, there is something fitting in that: when the tower needed repair some seventy years later, it went back to the quarry where it began. For a town of little more than a thousand people, it is an outsized claim on the American skyline.

## Palooka and the Hoosiers

Oolitic's stone also speaks in a lighter voice. The town is home to a limestone statue of Joe Palooka, the comic-strip boxer, which the Indiana limestone industry carved for a 1948 dedication in neighboring Bedford and which was given to Oolitic in 1984. The town has a small place in film history, too: according to Wikipedia, in Hoosiers, the movie about small-town Indiana basketball, Oolitic is the opposing team in the season opener. The climate here is humid subtropical, with hot, humid summers and generally mild to cool winters, but it is the stone underfoot, not the weather, that has always defined the place. It gave the town its reason to exist, its name and its most famous export.

## Flight Context

Oolitic lies at 38.9009°N, 86.5253°W in Lawrence County, Indiana, just north of Bedford. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL the limestone quarries north of town are the best landmark; Wikipedia places the Empire Hole about half a mile north of town, on private quarry land. The closest airport is Virgil I. Grissom Municipal at Bedford (KBFR), about 5 nm. Monroe County Airport at Bloomington (KBMG) is about 15 nm north. Best in clear daytime conditions.
