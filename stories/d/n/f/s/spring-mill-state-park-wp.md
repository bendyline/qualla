# Spring Mill State Park

In the sinkhole country east of Mitchell, Indiana, Spring Mill State Park joins a cave-fed pioneer mill village reportedly bought for a single dollar, stands of virgin timber and boat tours into Twin Caves with a memorial to hometown astronaut Gus Grissom and his Gemini 3 capsule, the Molly Brown.

**Location:** 38.7333, -86.4200

**Categories:** State parks of Indiana, Caves of Indiana, Living museums in Indiana, Civilian Conservation Corps in Indiana, Spaceflight history

## Introduction

The water that drove the mill starts underground. A spring rises inside Hamer Cave, and the stream that flows from it powered the mill around which a whole community grew, the original Spring Mill Village. Today that mill and 19 other structures form a reconstructed pioneer village inside Spring Mill State Park, a 1,358-acre park about three miles east of Mitchell, Indiana, on State Road 60. It is a place where caves, virgin forest, 1860s crafts and a spacecraft all share the same patch of limestone country.

## A Village for a Dollar

The heart of the park came remarkably cheap. According to Wikipedia, the 295-acre village area was purchased from a cement company for a single dollar in 1923 and the park was established in 1927, though the Indiana State Library and other histories date the restoration to 1928 and the park's official opening to 1930. Restoring the village fell to Richard Lieber and E. Y. Guernsey of Indiana's Department of Conservation, who led the work in the late 1920s and early 1930s. They kept some original structures and brought in other buildings from elsewhere in the state. Heritage interpreters now demonstrate the crafts and skills of the 1860s in the village, which includes a leather shop and gardens alongside the water mill. South of the village lies the Hamer Cemetery. Much of what surrounds the village is the work of the Civilian Conservation Corps, which built the Spring Mill Inn, Spring Mill Lake and most of the park's trails in the 1930s.

## A Thousand Sinkholes to the Mile

Spring Mill sits on the Mitchell Karst Plain, where water has dissolved the limestone into caves and sinkholes. In the Mitchell Karst Plain Nature Preserve, the sinkholes number more than 1,000 per square mile. Below ground lie Bronson Cave, Twin Caves, Hamer Cave and Shawnee Cave, also known as Donaldson Cave, among others. In summer, visitors can ride a boat about 500 feet into Twin Caves on a tour run by the Indiana Department of Natural Resources. The caves can be unforgiving: in 1989 the television show Rescue 911 aired a segment about a man who became trapped in Donaldson Cave and was later rescued. Above ground, part of the park is old-growth forest described as virgin timber, protected in the Donaldson Woods Nature Preserve.

## The Unsinkable Capsule

The park also honors a hometown astronaut. Gus Grissom, a Mitchell native and one of the original Mercury Seven astronauts, is remembered in a memorial that displays the spacecraft he flew on Gemini 3. On March 23, 1965, Grissom and John Young flew three orbits of the Earth on the first crewed mission of Project Gemini. Grissom had a reason to be wary of splashdowns. His Mercury capsule, Liberty Bell 7, had sunk after landing, so he nicknamed the new spacecraft Molly Brown, a playful nod to the Broadway musical The Unsinkable Molly Brown. The memorial also shows a short film about his life, along with a spacesuit, a helmet and many personal effects. Grissom later died in the Apollo 1 accident. The Molly Brown now rests about two miles east of his hometown.

## Campfires and Cave Light

Spring Mill drew about 675,000 visitors in 2018-19, and it is built for them to stay, with the historic Spring Mill Inn, roughly 70 rooms, which reopened in late 2024 after a two-year renovation. The Lakeview Activity Center overlooking Spring Mill Lake has live native snakes and turtles, natural and cultural history exhibits and a children's area. The campground offers some 220 sites, according to the Department of Natural Resources 178 with electric service and 45 with full hookups, plus youth tent camping. It is an easy place to spend a weekend moving between eras, from a mill driven by cave water to a spacecraft that circled the Earth three times in less than five hours.

## Flight Context

Spring Mill State Park lies at about 38.733°N, 86.420°W, roughly three miles east of Mitchell, Indiana, along State Road 60. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, look for Spring Mill Lake and the wooded park grounds just east of town, set in karst country pocked with sinkholes. Nearby airports: Virgil I. Grissom Municipal at Bedford (KBFR), about 7 nm north and named for the astronaut honored in the park; Orleans Airport (FAA identifier 7I4; no ICAO code listed), about 5 nm; French Lick Municipal (KFRH), about 17 nm. Best in clear daytime weather.
