# Saint Meinrad Archabbey

Founded by Swiss monks in 1854 to serve southern Indiana's German-speaking Catholics, Saint Meinrad grew into one of only two archabbeys in the United States, a seminary, and a mother house for monasteries from Arkansas to California.

**Categories:** Benedictine monasteries in the United States, Religious organizations established in 1854, Buildings and structures in Spencer County, Indiana, Swiss-American history, Seminaries

## Introduction

On a church map of Indiana, one township of Spencer County does not match its neighbors. The rest of the county answers to the Diocese of Evansville, but Harrison Township belongs to the Archdiocese of Indianapolis, and the reason is the monastery inside it. Saint Meinrad Archabbey, founded by Swiss monks on March 21, 1854, is described as one of only two archabbeys in the United States and one of 11 in the world. More than 60 monks live here, and five times a day they gather to pray together.

## From Einsiedeln to Indiana

The monks came from Einsiedeln Abbey in Switzerland, and they named their new home for St. Meinrad of Einsiedeln, a monk who died in 861. They crossed the ocean because a local priest, the Reverend Joseph Kundek, asked for help. Southern Indiana's German-speaking Catholic population was growing, and it needed both pastoral care and local men trained as priests. The Benedictines began with high school courses for local youths, and in 1861 they added undergraduate studies in philosophy and theology. That work set the mission Saint Meinrad still pursues: preparing men to serve the Church as priests. The Pope named Saint Meinrad an abbey in 1870, with Martin Marty as its first abbot and Fintan Mundwiler as prior, and in 1954 the Holy See raised it to the rank of archabbey.

## Five Times a Day

Life here follows the Rule of St. Benedict, the sixth-century instructions for living in community. The monks gather for morning prayer, Mass, noon prayer, evening prayer and compline, praying for the Church and the world, and guests often join them in the Archabbey Church. Gregorian chant carries much of the monastic Office. Short, simple antiphons frame the singing of the Psalms, while the Great Responsories of Matins unfold in far more complex lines. Between the hours of prayer come work and private reading of spiritual texts. More than a score of the monks serve outside the walls, in parishes, chaplaincies and diocesan assignments.

## A Mother House

Saint Meinrad did not stay a single community for long. In 1877, Abbot Martin Marty negotiated with an agent of the Little Rock and Fort Smith Railroad, which controlled thousands of acres and hoped a monastery and school would draw settlers; the railroad granted 640 acres. Three monk-missionaries founded the new house in Logan County, Arkansas, on March 15, 1878. It became independent in 1886, was named Subiaco Abbey by Pope Leo XIII in 1891, and later donated 80 acres for a townsite, where the first train reached the new town of Subiaco in 1909. In 1889, monks answered an invitation from the Archbishop of New Orleans and founded what became St. Joseph Abbey near Covington, Louisiana. Marmion Abbey in Aurora, Illinois, followed in 1933, and Prince of Peace Abbey in Oceanside, California, in 1958. Blue Cloud Abbey, founded in 1950 near Marvin, South Dakota, to serve Native American communities of the region, closed in 2012 as its community aged and dwindled.

## Classrooms for Priests and Laity

Teaching has always been central here. The undergraduate program, St. Meinrad College, opened its doors to men not seeking the priesthood and even considered admitting women, but enrollment fell too low and it closed in 1998. The graduate Seminary and School of Theology carries on. Priesthood candidates work toward a Master of Divinity, and lay students can also earn graduate degrees, a Master of Arts in Theology or in Pastoral Theology. The School of Theology also forms permanent deacons and supports priests as they move from seminary into parish life.

## Presses and Poplar

Prayer and work go together here, and the work has long helped pay for the community's ministries. The monks' printing operation, begun in 1867, grew into Abbey Press, an international business selling cards, books and gifts across the United States and abroad. After nearly 150 years the archabbey announced its closing in December 2016, and by mid-2017 some 70 people had lost their jobs. A newer craft continues. Since 1999, Abbey Caskets has sold handmade caskets and urns of poplar, cherry, walnut and oak, including a Monastic Abbey Casket modeled on the caskets used by the monks of Saint Meinrad themselves, with all proceeds supporting the archabbey and its school.

## Flight Context

Saint Meinrad Archabbey sits at approximately 38.166°N, 86.811°W in Harrison Township, Spencer County, southern Indiana, roughly a 15-minute drive from the Benedictine sisters' Monastery Immaculate Conception in Ferdinand. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL, look for the cluster of monastery, church and seminary buildings in the countryside about 11 km east-northeast of the town of Santa Claus. Nearby airports include Huntingburg Airport (KHNB), about 16 km to the northwest, and Perry County Municipal Airport in Tell City (KTEL), about 20 km to the south-southeast; Owensboro-Daviess County Airport (KOWB) lies about 57 km to the south-southwest across the Ohio River. Expect summer haze and thunderstorms; spring and autumn offer the clearest views.
