# Christ of the Ohio

A cement-and-pink-stone statue of Christ on a hill above the Ohio River at Troy, Indiana, sculpted by a former German prisoner of war whose talent was first noticed by a camp chaplain.

**Location:** 37.9914, -86.7963

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Perry County, Indiana, Outdoor sculptures in Indiana, Tourist attractions in Perry County, Indiana, Statues of Jesus, 1956 sculptures

## Introduction

In 1943, Herbert Jogerst was a German prisoner of war, held at Camp Breckinridge near Morganfield, Kentucky. Today one of his sculptures stands on Fulton Hill in Troy, Indiana, overlooking the Ohio River, a figure of Christ with arms stretched up toward the sky. The pedestal reads simply "Christ of the Ohio, AD 1956." Near a bend in the river, the statue has served boats as a beacon since its dedication. The path that led a captive sculptor to this riverbank ran through a camp chaplain, an Indiana archabbey and a meeting with a doctor who wanted something remarkable for his summer home.

## A Sculptor Behind the Wire

Camp Breckinridge was both a World War II infantry division camp and a prisoner-of-war camp, and it held about 3,000 German prisoners of war; it was deactivated in 1949 and reused during the Korean War. Jogerst arrived there in 1943. Even as a prisoner he kept working on sculptures, and his skill impressed the camp chaplain, Peter Behrman, a priest from St. Meinrad Archabbey. That connection outlasted the war. Jogerst returned to Germany in 1948 and struggled to find work as an artist. With few prospects at home, he reached out to Behrman for help, and St. Meinrad Archabbey responded by commissioning sculptures from him. A former enemy soldier found his artistic footing again through a priest who had come to know him in captivity.

## A Commission for Grandma's House

Jogerst's work for St. Meinrad led to the commission that would put his art on the banks of the Ohio. While he was working on pieces for the Archabbey, he met Dr. Nicholas A. James, who was visiting. James asked him to create a statue for his summer home, a place known as "Grandma's House," on the banks of the Ohio River. The finished work was dedicated on May 1, 1957, though its pedestal carries the date 1956. In time Grandma's House was sold, and the town of Troy purchased the statue, keeping Christ of the Ohio as a public landmark above the river.

## Cement, Pink Stone and Open Arms

Up close, the statue shows Christ standing barefoot and bearded, dressed in a me'il, a long outer robe, his arms raised toward the sky. Jogerst made it from white Portland cement mixed with crushed pink stone, and it is said to weigh 6,300 pounds. It rests on a pedestal of cement and stone from Saint Meinrad, the same community that gave the sculptor his second start, with the words CHRIST OF THE OHIO AD 1956 engraved on its face. The statue stands on the east side of Troy on the riverfront, where it has long doubled as a navigational marker for river traffic. In 1993 the Smithsonian Institution's Save Outdoor Sculpture! survey found it "well maintained." That same year, Herbert Jogerst died, his hilltop Christ still keeping watch over the Ohio.
