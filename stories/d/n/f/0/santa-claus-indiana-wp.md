# Santa Claus, Indiana

A rejected post office application in 1856 left a small southern Indiana town with the name Santa Claus, and everything since, from sacks of holiday mail to a feud between rival Santas to one of the country's earliest theme parks, has followed from it.

**Location:** 38.1189, -86.9214

**Categories:** Santa Claus, Towns in Spencer County, Indiana, Christmas traditions, Roadside attractions, Theme parks

## Introduction

"Choose some name other than Santa Fe." That was the whole reply when a young settlement in Spencer County, Indiana, applied for a post office in the mid-1850s. How the townspeople got from that rejection to their answer has dissolved into legend, and there are many versions of the story, along with other names they considered and dropped. What is certain is that in 1856 the Post Office Department accepted the name Santa Claus; the post office was established on May 21, 1856. The town has been living up to it ever since.

## Letters Addressed to Santa

For decades, the name drew little notice. On June 25, 1895, a nationwide push to standardize place names squeezed the post office's title into a single word, Santaclaus. Then, in the late 1920s, Postmaster James Martin began promoting the Santa Claus postmark, and the town's fortunes changed. The two-word name was restored on February 17, 1928, and holiday mail began pouring in. The flood created such staffing and logistical headaches that the Post Office Department decided there would never be another Santa Claus Post Office in the United States. In 1929 the volume of mail caught the eye of Robert Ripley, who featured the little post office in his syndicated Ripley's Believe It or Not! cartoon. The tradition endures: every December the post office creates a hand-cancelled pictorial Christmas postmark, and it still draws mail from all over the world.

## Too Many Santas

The name was an invitation to entrepreneurs. Milt Harris of Vincennes answered first with Santa's Candy Castle, a red-brick confection sponsored by the Curtiss Candy Company and dedicated on December 22, 1935. It was the town's first tourist attraction and is purported to be the first themed attraction in the United States. Around it grew Santa Claus Town, with a Toy Village of miniature fairytale buildings sponsored by national toy makers, the town's first newspaper and a chamber of commerce. Carl Barrett, the Chicago head of the Illinois Auto Club, disliked what he called Harris's materialism and built a rival, Santa Claus Park. Three days after the Candy Castle opened, on Christmas Day 1935, Barrett dedicated a 22-foot Santa statue on the highest hill in town. It was billed as solid granite, a claim that crumbled years later when cracks exposed it as concrete. The two men fought for years in lawsuits over land ownership that reached the Indiana Supreme Court, and newspapers covered the saga under a perfect headline: "Too Many Santas."

## A Retired Industrialist's Playground

The feud proved a costly distraction for both men, and over the years their attractions slipped into neglect as the town's growth drifted west onto land developed by the Koch family. On August 3, 1946, retired industrialist Louis J. Koch opened Santa Claus Land, which is claimed to be the world's first theme park. Where the rival Santas had squabbled, Koch's venture simply kept growing. It became Holiday World in 1984, and in 1993 a water park called Splashin' Safari was added. Still owned and run by the Koch family, the park draws roughly a million visitors a year. Its wooden roller coaster, The Voyage, has repeatedly been voted the best wooden coaster in the world by coaster enthusiasts.

## Christmas All Year Round

The holiday theme runs right through daily life. The main road, Indiana 162, is known as Christmas Boulevard. Most residents live in Christmas Lake Village, a gated community developed in the late 1960s on 2,500 acres around three lakes named Christmas Lake, Lake Holly and Lake Noel, where every street takes its name from the season. The growth of that community has helped the town swell from fewer than a thousand residents in 1990 to 2,586 in 2020. The old attractions have found new life, too. A local development company bought Santa's Candy Castle and the rest of Santa Claus Town in 2005 and reopened the Candy Castle on July 1, 2006. The 40-ton concrete Santa was restored in 2011, and in 2012 a historic church and the town's original post office were moved to stand beside him. Forbes named Santa Claus one of the world's top Christmas destinations in 2011, and since 1936 the town has been the "First Day of Issue" site for the initial release of Christmas seals in the United States, the holiday stamps sold to raise money to fight tuberculosis.

## Flight Context

Santa Claus lies at approximately 38.119°N, 86.921°W in Spencer County, southwestern Indiana, a short distance north of the Ohio River and the Kentucky line. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL, the best visual cues are the three lakes of Christmas Lake Village, the roller coaster structures of the Holiday World & Splashin' Safari theme park, and Indiana 162 (Christmas Boulevard) running through town. Nearby airports include Huntingburg Airport (KHNB), about 15 km to the north-northwest; Perry County Municipal Airport in Tell City (KTEL), about 22 km to the east-southeast; and Evansville Regional Airport (KEVV), about 55 km to the west. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms; the lakes make the easiest reference point in any season.
