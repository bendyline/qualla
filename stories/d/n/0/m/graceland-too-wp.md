# Graceland Too

For twenty-five years, one man's round-the-clock Elvis shrine in Holly Springs, Mississippi, drew curious visitors and songwriters, until a fatal shooting at the front door ended it in 2014.

**Location:** 34.7666, -89.4450

**Categories:** Elvis Presley, Roadside attractions, Defunct museums, Holly Springs, Mississippi, Popular culture

## Introduction

Elvis Presley's Graceland is a 13.8-acre estate in Memphis, opened as a museum in 1982 and reported as drawing roughly 600,000 visitors a year, said to be second among American homes only to the White House. About forty miles southeast, in Holly Springs, Mississippi, stood its unofficial echo: a two-story house on East Gholson Avenue where, for $5 and at any hour of the day or night, a man named Paul MacLeod would lead you through his own shrine to the King. It had no affiliation with the original. It was called Graceland Too.

## A Lifelong Fixation

MacLeod was born in Detroit in 1942. He worked in prisons and at automobile assembly plants, but was unemployed for most of his life. His obsession with Presley began young, and by the mid-1970s he had moved to Holly Springs, just forty miles from Graceland. Presley died in 1977, and while Graceland became a museum, MacLeod kept amassing hundreds of pieces of Elvis memorabilia of his own. He married twice, and both wives divorced him over his fixation on Elvis. Around 1989 or 1990 he opened his home to the public as Graceland Too, and it became a local tourist attraction.

## Open Twenty-Four Hours

Graceland Too never closed. MacLeod lived on the top floor and gave tours personally, around the clock and year-round, through rooms so packed with Elvis paraphernalia that they became a fire hazard. He claimed the collection was worth millions of dollars. He also became famous for his eccentricity, including his claim to drink at least two dozen cans of soda a day. The house itself kept changing its skin, repainted in gaudy colors: first pink, then white, then blue. Over twenty-five years the strangeness of it all made Graceland Too a local landmark, and it left a mark on musicians. The indie rock band Parquet Courts wrote "Uncast Shadow of a Southern Myth" after a visit, and Phoebe Bridgers named a song for the house on her 2020 album Punisher.

## A Fatal Night in July

On the night of July 15, 2014, MacLeod shot and killed David Taylor (Dwight David Taylor Jr.), a 28-year-old Black local handyman, just inside the front door of the house. Accounts of what led to it differ. MacLeod's attorney said Taylor broke a window, forced his way in and demanded money, while other reports describe a dispute over an unpaid ten dollars. MacLeod cooperated with police and no charges were filed. About a day and a half later, he suffered a heart attack and died on the front porch of Graceland Too. Within two days, two men were dead at the same address, and the attraction that had never closed was finished. MacLeod's death made national headlines and drew mourners who decorated the property with soda cans after his funeral.

## What Became of the Shrine

The collection went to auction in January 2015 and reportedly sold as a single lot for $54,500 to an anonymous buyer from Georgia, but a problem with the online bidding company forced a second auction that May. Most of the property, including hundreds of pieces of Presley memorabilia, sold for an undisclosed sum. Later that year the house and everything left in it were bought for $5,500 by a couple, Marie and Geoffry Underwood, after the Bank of Holly Springs auctioned it. In August 2024 Marie Underwood reopened it for a single weekend of tours and events, its first public opening in a decade, and said she hoped to make it "rock again"; we found no confirmation of regular visiting hours since. The story did not end with the house. Documentary filmmakers had already spent five years on a film about MacLeod, a photography book called Graceland Too Revisited appeared the week of the auction, and in 2022 a play about MacLeod, Taylor and the museum, Graceland Too: The Building Elvis Never Left, premiered at the New York Theater Festival.

## Flight Context

Graceland Too stood at 200 E. Gholson Avenue in Holly Springs, Mississippi, at about 34.767N, 89.445W, near downtown Holly Springs. From 1,500-2,000 feet the individual house is hard to single out among the tree-lined residential streets; use the downtown grid as a reference. Holly Springs-Marshall County Airport (FAA identifier M41, no ICAO code) lies about 4.4 nm west-northwest, University-Oxford Airport (KUOX) about 23 nm south, and Frederick W. Smith International Airport (KMEM), formerly Memphis International, about 31 nm west-northwest, so keep busy Memphis airspace in mind to the north and west.
