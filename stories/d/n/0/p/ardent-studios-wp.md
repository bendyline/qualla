# Ardent Studios

Born in a Memphis family garage in 1959, Ardent Studios became the city's first four-track studio, launched Big Star, and recorded or mixed everyone from ZZ Top and R.E.M. to Led Zeppelin and the White Stripes.

**Location:** 35.1375, -89.9944

**Categories:** Recording studios, Music of Memphis, Rock music, Memphis, Tennessee, Music history

## Introduction

In 1959, three childhood friends set up a recording studio in John Fry's family garage in Memphis. Fry recorded his first 45s there for the Ardent Records label. One of his partners, Fred Smith, co-founded the studio as a teenager and would later build FedEx. For more than six decades Ardent kept recording on Madison Avenue, and the list of artists who have passed through it reads like a who's who of rock, soul and blues. In 2026, however, the studio went dark: it has been closed since January amid dueling lawsuits between its owner and a former employee, and as of June 2026 reports no reopening date had been announced.

## Tubes in the Garage

The garage studio that Fry, John King and Smith built ran an Altec tube mixing console, an Ampex two-track tape recorder, a Pultec equalizer and Neumann microphones. In 1966 the operation moved into a commercial space it shared with a bookshop. At the time, Tom Dowd was consulting with the manufacturer Auditronics on an early multitrack console for nearby Stax Records, and Fry ordered the same input modules for his own second mixing board. When Ardent upgraded to a Scully four-track tape recorder, it became the first four-track studio in Memphis. It was also the first studio in the area to use EMT plate reverbs.

## Big Star on Madison Avenue

On October 25, 1968, Fry acquired a parcel of land at 2000 Madison Avenue and broke ground on a purpose-built home for the studio. Ardent finished moving in over Thanksgiving weekend in 1971, with two rooms, Studio A and Studio B. Studio C followed in 1980. The building became home to a generation of young producers and engineers, among them Jim Dickinson, Terry Manning, Joe Hardy and John Hampton, and later Skidd Mills, Jeff Powell, Brad Blackwood and others. One of the studio's earliest groups was Big Star, whose first two albums appeared on the Ardent Records label in the early 1970s. All three Big Star albums were later included in Rolling Stone's list of the 500 Greatest Albums of All Time, and their song "In the Street," from the first album, was reworked, in a version by Cheap Trick, as the theme for That '70s Show. The link long endured: Jody Stephens, Big Star's drummer, went on to work at the studio for years.

## From Tres Hombres to Green

The roster reads like a record store. Ardent recorded Sam & Dave, Isaac Hayes, Leon Russell and the Staple Singers, and handled the mixing of Led Zeppelin III. James Taylor's Mud Slide Slim and the Blue Horizon is among the studio's notable albums, and ZZ Top's catalog runs through Ardent, from Rio Grande Mud and Tres Hombres to Eliminator and Afterburner. The Box Tops' Cry Like a Baby, the Replacements' Pleased to Meet Me, R.E.M.'s Green, the Afghan Whigs' Gentlemen and Stevie Ray Vaughan's Live at Carnegie Hall are all associated with the studio as well. In the 2000s a younger wave arrived, including the White Stripes, the Raconteurs, Cat Power and North Mississippi Allstars, and the soundtracks for Hustle and Flow and Black Snake Moan were produced here too.

## A Studio Gone Quiet

In the years before the closure, Ardent renovated its control rooms: Studio A's was unveiled in 2024 and Studio B's followed in 2025, according to the studio's own site, which is not consistent about the year. The complex has four studios, lettered A through D, each with different gear and mixing and recording capacities, with Studio D the newest room, plus a mastering suite. Studio A has a 72-channel Neve 88RS console and a Studer A827 multitrack, and Studio B was fitted with an SSL Duality Fuse console. The gear has changed a great deal since the Altec tube board in the Fry garage.

## Flight Context

Ardent Studios, closed since January 2026, is at 2000 Madison Avenue in midtown Memphis, Tennessee, at about 35.138N, 89.994W. From 2,000-2,500 feet, follow Madison Avenue east from downtown and the Mississippi River; the studio itself is hard to single out along the corridor, so use downtown's skyline to the west as the main reference. The airspace is busy: Frederick W. Smith International Airport (KMEM), formerly Memphis International, the city's main airline and cargo hub, lies about 5.8 nm south, and General DeWitt Spain Airport (FAA identifier M01, no ICAO code) is about 4.8 nm northwest near the river. Contact Memphis approach before overflying midtown.
