# Craighead Caverns

Beneath the foothills between Sweetwater and Madisonville, Tennessee, Craighead Caverns holds the Lost Sea, the largest underground lake in the United States, along with the tracks of an Ice Age jaguar and the remains of a nightclub.

**Location:** 35.5356, -84.4311

**Categories:** Caves of Tennessee, Underground lakes, Show caves in the United States, National Natural Landmarks in Tennessee, Tourist attractions in Monroe County, Tennessee

## Introduction

Ben Sands measured the room with his ears. In 1905 the thirteen-year-old, who was said to play often in the cave, squeezed through a small opening into a space so large his lantern could not find its ends. So he threw balls of mud into the dark in every direction and listened. He heard splashes. He had found the lake now called the Lost Sea, deep in Craighead Caverns between Sweetwater and Madisonville, Tennessee. It is the largest underground lake in the United States, and it is generally ranked as the world's second-largest non-subglacial one.

## The Lake That Hid Itself

Nobody believed him at first. By the time Ben persuaded his father to go back down and explore, the water had risen and hidden the entrance, and the lake stayed lost until local explorers found it again several years later. At normal "full" capacity, its visible surface runs 800 feet long and 220 feet wide, about 4.5 acres. That is only what can be seen. In the 1970s, cave divers pushed through several rooms completely filled with water without ever reaching the end of the cave. For many years the Lost Sea was considered the largest underground lake in the world; today that ranking belongs to Dragon's Breath Cave in Namibia, leaving Craighead Caverns in second place among lakes that do not lie beneath glaciers.

## Councils and Saltpeter

Long before the lake had a name, the caverns had people. The system sits in the foothills of the Great Smoky Mountains, and it is said to be named for a former owner, Chief Craighead, a Cherokee. The Cherokee used the caves as a meeting place for their councils. War brought a harder use. During the American Civil War, Confederate soldiers mined the caves for saltpeter, a commodity necessary to the manufacture of gunpowder. The cave also holds more delicate treasures than minerals for munitions: stalactites, stalagmites, a waterfall and an abundance of the crystal clusters called anthodites.

## The Jaguar in the Mud

In 1939 two cave guides, Jack Kyker and Clarence Hicks, were exploring on their own time when they came across fossilized bones. They reported the find to the cave's owners, Dr. W. J. Cameron and W. E. Michael of Sweetwater, who sent the bones to the American Museum of Natural History in New York. There they were identified as the bones of a very large Pleistocene jaguar and of an elk fawn. In May 1940 George Gaylord Simpson, a vertebrate paleontologist at the museum, came to see for himself. He recovered more jaguar bones and made casts of several jaguar footprints pressed into the cave's mud floor, publishing the work, with photographs, in American Museum Novitates in 1941. Simpson assigned the fossils to the large Panthera onca augusta, and for a time Craighead's remains were among the best known of that subspecies.

## Mushrooms, Then a Dance Floor

The same years brought a stranger enterprise. From 1939 to 1940 a mushroom farm operated in the Big Room, a few hundred feet northeast of the historic entrance, fed with manure hauled from Fort Oglethorpe, where many horses were stabled. In 1947 the space changed character entirely. A wooden dance floor went down where the mushroom beds had been, and a nightclub called the "Cavern Tavern" opened underground. In 1974 the National Park Service added Craighead Caverns to its list of National Natural Landmarks.

## When the Sea Falls

Boat tours still glide across the lake, and for many visitors they are the highlight of the trip underground. The Lost Sea does not hold still, though. In times of extreme drought it recedes significantly, and during the drought of 2007–08 its managers said the water dropped 28 feet below its normal level. To keep the boats running, they had to extend the walkway and the boat dock. At such times visitors see a much larger cavern opening above the water, a reminder that the room Ben Sands once sounded with mud balls is bigger than it lets on. The lake has also been featured on CBS's America Wonders series.

## Flight Context

Craighead Caverns lies at 35.5356°N, 84.4311°W in Monroe County, Tennessee, between Sweetwater and Madisonville in the foothills of the Great Smoky Mountains. Everything of interest is underground, so the surface landmark is the attraction's visitor complex between the two towns; from 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, look for the rolling farmland of the valley with the high Smokies rising to the east on clear days. Monroe County Airport at Madisonville (KMNV) is only about 2.5 nm east, making the caverns an easy fly-in; McMinn County Airport at Athens (KMMI) is about 10.5 nm southwest. Morning valley fog and summer haze are common in East Tennessee's valleys and can hide the mountains.
