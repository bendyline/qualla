# Fall Creek Falls State Park

Where Cane Creek cuts a 15-mile gorge into the Cumberland Plateau, a park restored from badly eroded land by New Deal crews protects waterfalls, caves and the 256-foot plunge of Fall Creek Falls, billed as the highest free-fall waterfall east of the Mississippi.

**Location:** 35.6621, -85.3498

**Categories:** State parks of Tennessee, Waterfalls of Tennessee, Civilian Conservation Corps in Tennessee, Protected areas of Van Buren County, Tennessee, Caves of Tennessee

## Introduction

Lawson Fisher woke to the roar of rising water. It was the night of the Good Friday Flood of 1929, and Fisher ran the grist mill perched at the head of Cane Creek Falls, so he raced inside to save the account books. "I had taken perhaps four or five steps when I felt that old mill building quiver," he later testified. He ran for the door, stepped out on solid ground, and turned to see what was going to happen, "but folks, it had already happened. The mill wasn't there. I could just see pieces of planking and timbers going over the falls." The creek that took Fisher's mill is the same one that carved the gorge at the heart of Fall Creek Falls State Park.

## A Gash in the Plateau

Cane Creek rises atop Little Mountain, on the plateau's eastern edge above the Sequatchie Valley, and winds north across the Cumberland Plateau, gathering Meadow Creek and smaller streams. Then the land falls out from under it. Entering the gorge, the creek drops several hundred feet in less than a mile, tumbling 45 feet over Cane Creek Cascades and 85 feet over Cane Creek Falls. Nearby, Rockhouse Creek spills 125 feet into the same plunge pool, and Fall Creek and Piney Creek arrive from smaller gorges to the west. Along the way part of the creek vanishes into limestone sinks and resurfaces at a spring known as "Crusher Hole." The Cane Creek Gorge runs some 15 miles, a great gash in the western edge of the plateau, before the creek reaches the Highland Rim and empties into the Caney Fork River. The park wrapped around it covers more than 30,000 acres of Van Buren and Bledsoe counties.

## Two Hundred Fifty-Six Feet of Air

The park's namesake drops 256 feet in a single plunge, and Fall Creek Falls is billed as the highest free-fall waterfall east of the Mississippi River. A short trail descends from the parking lot on the plateau to the base of the gorge and the waterfall's plunge pool, and an overlook beside the lot looks straight down into Fall Creek Gorge. Almost beside it, Coon Creek Falls plunges 250 feet into the same gorge, overshadowed by its neighbor. Piney Creek Falls drops 95 feet, and at Lost Creek Falls the water emerges from a cave, falls 60 feet, and disappears from the pool at the bottom. The flow over the main falls is no longer left entirely to chance: an earthen dam holds Fall Creek Falls Lake, built in 1966 with its valves closed that October 18, to keep water moving over the edge.

## Floodwater and Worn-Out Land

The few residents along Cane Creek were often at its mercy, because the creek, like most of the Upper Caney Fork watershed, was prone to flash floods. The 1929 Good Friday flood was the worst, swelling the Caney Fork and its tributaries to record volumes and wiping out dozens of mills, houses and bridges. One resident woke to a cabin floor under several inches of water and spent the night in the loft, watching helplessly as it kept rising. Several small farms in the lower valley were destroyed outright, and the Cane Creek Mill, which had stood above the falls since 1831, was never rebuilt. The land itself was badly eroded when the federal government began buying it in 1937. The next year the Works Progress Administration and the Civilian Conservation Corps set to work restoring the forest and building park facilities, and in 1944 the National Park Service handed the park to the State of Tennessee.

## A Fall at Buzzard's Roost

Not every story here is geological. Millikan's Overlook, in the Piney Creek section, is named for Glenn Millikan, head of physiology at Vanderbilt University School of Medicine and son of Nobel laureate Robert A. Millikan. On May 25, 1947, while he was climbing Buzzard's Roost, the cliff beneath the overlook, a falling rock killed him. Two decades later the park became a resort. An $8 million modernization begun in 1968 added a hotel, cabins, pools, a golf course, a nature center and trails, and Governor Winfield Dunn dedicated it on July 8, 1972. In 2006 the state bought 12,500 acres along the White-Van Buren County line to help build an unbroken corridor of public land between the park and Scott's Gulf to the north.

## Caverns and Cameras

Beneath the trails lies another park. Fall Creek Falls is reported to have the second-most caves of any park in the eastern United States, behind only Mammoth Cave National Park. Rumbling Falls Cave, in the Dry Fork section near Spencer, is reported to hold the second-largest cave chamber in the country, and Lost Creek Cave has five entrances, a waterfall and 7 miles of passageways. Above ground, the scenery has drawn filmmakers: scenes for the 1986 film King Kong Lives, starring Linda Hamilton, were shot around Cane Creek Cascades and Cane Creek Falls, and the park was one of the primary filming locations for Disney's live-action adaptation of The Jungle Book. The artist Gilbert Gaul, acclaimed for his Civil War illustrations, worked from a studio south of Spencer on land the park now owns.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 35.6621°N, 85.3498°W, on the western Cumberland Plateau in Van Buren and Bledsoe counties, near the town of Spencer. From the air the Cane Creek Gorge shows as a deep wooded cleft cutting north across the flat plateau, with Fall Creek Falls Lake spread across the park's southern section as the easiest landmark. A pass at about 3,500-4,500 feet MSL gives a view into the gorge while staying well above the rim. Nearby fields include Crossville Memorial-Whitson Field (KCSV) about 22 nm northeast, Warren County Memorial at McMinnville (KRNC) about 24 nm west and Upper Cumberland Regional at Sparta (KSRB) about 25 nm north-northwest; Chattanooga (KCHA) is about 38 nm south. Watch for plateau fog in the mornings and for rapidly building summer thunderstorms.
