# Horace Burgess's Treehouse

Built over twelve years by a Crossville, Tennessee, minister who said God told him to build it, this treehouse church was often called the largest in the world until it burned to the ground in about fifteen minutes in 2019.

**Location:** 35.9855, -84.9944

**Categories:** Trees and humans, Tourist attractions in Cumberland County, Tennessee, Houses in Cumberland County, Tennessee, Landmarks in Tennessee

## Introduction

In 1993, Horace Burgess says, God made him an offer. The local minister was praying when, as he tells it, he heard the words: "If you build a tree house, I'll see that you never run out of material." Burgess took the promise literally. Over the next twelve years he built a treehouse and church in Crossville, Tennessee, that grew into a popular attraction and was often called, unofficially, the largest treehouse in the world.

## Never Running Out

Burgess did most of the construction himself, and he worked on the treehouse for twelve years after his vision. The numbers give a sense of the labor. He drove 258,000 nails with a nail gun, and about 500 pounds of penny nails by hand. What rose among the trees was also known as the Minister's Treehouse, and it doubled as a church. Burgess officiated 23 weddings in the treehouse church, so for some couples it was not a curiosity at all but the place where their marriages began.

## Largest in the World, Unofficially

Over time the treehouse became a popular tourist attraction, until the state closed it to the public in 2012 for failing to meet building and fire safety codes. Guinness World Records has no category for the largest treehouse, so no official title was ever at stake, but it was often described as the world's largest anyway. One of the people who made that claim knew the subject well. Pete Nelson, who hosted Animal Planet's Treehouse Masters from 2013 to 2018, author of six books about treehouses and owner of a business that builds them, said it was "by far" the largest in the world. Not everyone saw only the charm. One official called it a "deathtrap," adding that "it was very cool, but also very dangerous."

## Fifteen Minutes

On October 22, 2019, the treehouse and the trees that held it up burned to the ground. The fire lasted about fifteen minutes. Cumberland County Fire Rescue responded late that Tuesday night, with reports putting the call at about 10:30 or 11:30 p.m., and no cause has been established. Twelve years of building and 258,000 machine-driven nails were gone in a quarter of an hour. Burgess himself was not sorry to see it go. He said he was relieved after the fire, and that the treehouse had "always been a pain." The man who had spent twelve years building it was, in the end, glad to be free of it.
