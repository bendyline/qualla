# Nameless, Tennessee

A Jackson County, Tennessee, community whose post office came back from Washington stamped "Nameless," or so one of several competing stories goes, and which still celebrates its ordinary rural life each summer.

**Location:** 36.2544, -85.7111

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Jackson County, Tennessee, Unincorporated communities in Tennessee

## Introduction

The residents asked for a post office. What they got, at least in the best-known version of the story, was a name nobody had chosen. Nameless is a small unincorporated community in Jackson County, Tennessee, and its unusual name has attracted writers' attention for decades. No one agrees on how it happened. There are at least three explanations, each with its own charm, and none has ever fully displaced the others.

## A Blank on the Form

The most familiar story is a bureaucratic accident. When residents applied for a post office, the tale goes, they left blank the space on the application where the community's name belonged. The U.S. Post Office Department sent the form back with "Nameless" stamped on it, and the stamp became the name. It is the kind of story that is easy to repeat and impossible to confirm, which may be why it has proven so durable. Whatever the truth, a post office called Nameless opened in 1866 and stayed in operation until 1909, more than four decades of letters addressed to a place that officially claimed to have no name.

## "Leave It Be"

William Least Heat-Moon reported a different account. In his 1982 book Blue Highways: A Journey Into America, he reported that the residents themselves decided the community should go without a name, after one of them declared, "This here's a nameless place if I ever seen one, so leave it be." In this version, Nameless was not a clerk's mistake but a local verdict, delivered with a shrug. Put into print, it added another layer to the mystery of how the community came to be called what it is.

## The Name That Was Rejected

A third version appeared in 1933, in an article in the Jackson County Sentinel. According to that account, a local official first tried to name the post office "Morgan," after county attorney general George Morgan. The Post Office Department turned the name down, possibly because "Morgan" still called up associations with the Confederacy, including the Confederate general John Hunt Morgan. Rebuffed, the official wrote back that if his original request could not be used, he would prefer the post office to be nameless. The federal authorities, in this telling, took him at his word.

## Moon Pies at the General Store

At its peak Nameless had about 250 people. Nameless School operated until 1965, and the former J.T. Watts General Merchandise Store, restored in 2004, now serves as a museum that opens only a few days a year. The community has traditionally held Nameless Heritage Day each summer, a celebration of ordinary rural life, with Nameless T-shirts, giveaways from the Nameless Fire Department and bake sales for Nameless veterans, and visitors buying RC Cola, Moon Pies and boloney and cheese sandwiches from the old general store; check locally for current dates. The name has even made it into music: "Nameless, TN" is the eleventh track on Old Crow Medicine Show's 2023 album, Jubilee.
