# Barkley Marathons

Born from a failed prison escape and run on secret dates through the thickets of Frozen Head State Park, the Barkley Marathons has been finished only 26 times by 20 runners since 1995.

**Location:** 36.1260, -84.5010

**Categories:** Ultramarathons in the United States, Morgan County, Tennessee, Sports competitions in Tennessee, Recurring sporting events established in 1986, Multiday races

## Introduction

Somewhere in Frozen Head State Park, on a morning nobody outside the race knows in advance, a conch shell sounds. The runners now have one hour. The start signal itself is quieter: race founder Gary Cantrell, known here as Lazarus Lake, lights a cigarette, and the Barkley Marathons begins. Over the next 60 hours, 35 to 40 invited runners will try to complete five unmarked loops through the steep woods of Morgan County, Tennessee. Almost all of them will fail. Nicknamed "The Race That Eats Its Young," the Barkley had been completed only 26 times, by 20 runners, from its first finish in 1995 through the 2026 event, which, like 2025, produced no full finisher.

## Eight Miles in Fifty-Four Hours

The idea came from a prison break. In 1977, James Earl Ray, the man convicted of assassinating Martin Luther King Jr., escaped from the nearby Brushy Mountain State Penitentiary. In 54 hours on the run he covered only about 8 miles. Cantrell and his friend Karl "Raw Dog" Henn, who often hiked Frozen Head together, turned the idea into a race. Cantrell named the race for his longtime neighbor and running companion, Barry Barkley, who died in 2019 at 70. The first event started on March 1, 1986. In its early years the course ran about 50 to 55 miles with some 25,000 to 27,000 feet of climbing, and the loop has only grown longer since. It took until 1995 for anyone to finish the full long version, when Mark Williams of the United Kingdom came in at 59 hours, 28 minutes and 48 seconds, with barely half an hour to spare.

## A Letter of Condolence

Getting in is its own ordeal. Hopefuls must email the right address at the right time and pay a $1.60 application fee, a penny per mile for the 60-mile and 100-mile races. Then comes an essay: "Why I Should be Allowed to Run in the Barkley." The accepted receive a "letter of condolence" from Lazarus Lake warning of a "very bad thing waiting." First-timers, called virgins, must bring a license plate from their state or country as part of the entry fee. Cantrell also picks at least one "sacrificial virgin," a runner he believes, from the essay and test answers, has no chance of finishing. The field is capped at 35 to 40 runners, largely because of limits set by Tennessee park officials, and the start date, usually between mid-March and early April (the 2026 race began on February 14, the earliest ever), stays a closely held secret.

## Rat Jaw and the Meatgrinder

The course is an unmarked loop, officially 20 miles though now estimated at around 25, run five times, with the direction alternating. Its landmarks carry names like Checkmate Hill, Little Hell, Meatgrinder, Rat Jaw and Testicle Spectacle. Before the start, runners copy the route from a master map and printed directions onto their own maps; after that, they navigate by their notes alone. Along each loop they must find between 9 and 15 books and tear out the page matching their race number, which changes every lap. The titles tend toward the grim, among them Death Walks the Woods, Heart of Darkness and A Time to Die. Lose a page and you are disqualified. Runners do get lost. In 2006 a 75-year-old runner wandered for 32 hours after covering just two miles and ended up in a different county, and more than 30 competitors have failed even to reach the first book, two miles out. Those who press on face sleep deprivation and hallucinations, and when a runner quits, a bugler plays "Taps" when they return to the start.

## Ninety-Nine Seconds

The race has even taunted women directly: the 2013 application claimed they were "too soft" to finish. Several women completed the three-loop "fun run," including Suzi Thibeault, Nancy Hamilton, Sue Johnston and Beverley Anderson-Abbs, each of them twice, and Johnston's 66 miles in 2001 stood as the best women's performance for more than two decades. In 2023, Jasmin Paris reached the ninth book on the fourth lap. She came back in 2024 and became the first woman to complete all five loops within the time limit, finishing 99 seconds before the 60-hour cutoff. The Barkley has also traveled: in Berkeley, Gloucestershire, a tribute event called the Berkeley Marathons, suggested as a pun by Lazarus Lake himself, sends runners looking for paperback books on five loops of about 26 miles under a 24-hour limit. Back in Tennessee, both 2025 and 2026 ended with no finishers at all.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 36.126°N, 84.501°W, in Frozen Head State Park in Morgan County, Tennessee, northwest of Oak Ridge and Knoxville. From the air the park is a block of steep, densely forested mountains with few clearings; the town of Wartburg lies to the west and the former Brushy Mountain State Penitentiary at Petros sits at the foot of the ridges nearby. Cruise at 5,000-5,500 feet MSL to stay comfortably above the terrain. Nearby fields include Rockwood Municipal (KRKW) about 15 nm southwest, Scott Municipal at Oneida (KSCX) about 20 nm north, and Campbell County Airport at Jacksboro (KJAU, also listed as Colonel Tommy C. Stiner Airfield) about 21 nm northeast; McGhee Tyson (KTYS) serving Knoxville is about 31 nm southeast. Late winter and early spring, when the race is held, bring low cloud, rain and mountain fog.
