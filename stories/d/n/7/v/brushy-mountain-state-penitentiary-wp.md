# Brushy Mountain State Penitentiary

Born of the Coal Creek War and hemmed in by the wild Cumberland Plateau, Tennessee's Brushy Mountain prison operated from 1896 to 2009 and held James Earl Ray before reopening as a tourist destination.

**Location:** 36.1052, -84.4529

**Categories:** Defunct prisons in Tennessee, Buildings and structures in Morgan County, Tennessee, Labor history of Tennessee, Tourist attractions in Tennessee

## Introduction

On June 10, 1977, seven inmates went over a fence at Brushy Mountain State Penitentiary, and one of them was James Earl Ray, the convicted assassin of Martin Luther King Jr. Getting out of the prison yard turned out to be the easy part. Brushy sits in the remote community of Petros in Morgan County, Tennessee, nearly encircled by rugged, wooded mountains on the Cumberland Plateau. Less than 58 hours later, Ray was captured in that terrain about eight and a half miles from the prison. Escape attempts here were infrequent and almost always unsuccessful, with the rugged terrain working against runaways.

## Born of the Coal Creek War

The prison exists because of a labor revolt. Tennessee had leased its convicts to mining companies and other businesses, a system that had the effect of suppressing wages for free workers across the state. In 1891, after miners in Coal Creek and Briceville protested the use of this unpaid convict labor in their mines, the companies locked them out, and the conflict became known as the Coal Creek War. At one stockade the guards surrendered without a fight, and the convicts held there were marched to Coal Creek, loaded onto a train and sent to Knoxville. The upheaval led the state legislature to pass a bill abolishing the convict lease system, and all of Tennessee's lease arrangements ended on January 1, 1896.

## A Prison That Was Also a Mine

Tennessee's answer was to put convicts to work for itself. With the help of consulting geologists, the state chose a mountainous, secure site and built the Brushy Mountain Mine and Prison. Some 210 prisoners released from the old leases became its first inmates in 1896. They and those who followed built a railroad spur, worked the coal mines on the property, operated coke ovens and farmed the land. Even the first prison, a wooden structure, was raised by prisoner labor. In the 1920s it was replaced by a castle-like building of stone that inmates quarried on the property itself, and that stone building still stands against the forested slopes. Next door lies the rugged country of Frozen Head State Park and Natural Area.

## Strikes, Closures and a Final Lockdown

Brushy's long run was not continuous. In 1972 the prison closed after its guards went on strike to protest unsafe working conditions, and it did not reopen until 1976, the year before Ray's escape. Over the decades it reportedly held some of Tennessee's most notorious prisoners, among them Byron Looper, convicted in 2000 of murdering State Senator Tommy Burks, and Paul Dennis Reid, the serial killer known as the "Fast Food Killer." Its grim reputation is also said to have reached fiction: Thomas Harris's novel The Silence of the Lambs reportedly has Hannibal Lecter offered a transfer to Brushy Mountain in exchange for information about a killer. The prison finally closed on June 11, 2009, after 113 years, and its functions moved to the Morgan County Correctional Complex.

## Doing Time by Choice

Beginning in 2018, the old penitentiary found a new life as a tourist destination. Visitors now come of their own free will, mostly in a season that runs from spring to late autumn, for self-guided tours, events, concerts, car shows and foot races through the surrounding hills (the site is closed for the winter, and one cell block is closed for structural reasons). A distillery on the grounds produces moonshine, vodka, whiskey and liqueurs, and the Warden's Table Restaurant serves Southern and American staples such as barbecue and cheeseburgers in a cafeteria-style setting. The stone walls that inmates cut from this mountain still stand, now framing a place people choose to visit that others spent years trying to leave.

## Flight Context

Brushy Mountain State Penitentiary sits at 36.1052°N, 84.4529°W in Petros, Morgan County, Tennessee, in a remote section of the Cumberland Plateau beside Frozen Head State Park and Natural Area. From 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, look for the stone prison complex tucked against the base of steep, heavily forested mountains, with Frozen Head's wild ridges rising behind it; the terrain climbs quickly here, so keep a comfortable margin over the ridgelines. Rockwood Municipal Airport (KRKW) is about 16 nm southwest, Scott Municipal Airport at Oneida (KSCX) is about 22 nm north, and McGhee Tyson Airport serving Knoxville (KTYS) is about 28 nm southeast. Clouds can hang on the plateau ridges and morning fog fills the valleys, so check ceilings before flying low.
