# Auraria, Georgia

Born in the Georgia Gold Rush of 1832 and named for the Latin word for gold, Auraria boomed, lost the county seat to Dahlonega and faded to a ghost town, but its people carried its name and know-how west to California and Colorado.

**Location:** 34.4750, -84.0233

## Introduction

When gold turned up in California in 1848, someone had to prove it was real. The first person to do so was Jennie Wimmer, a cook in rural California and a former resident of Auraria, Georgia, who was the only one on the scene who knew how to perform the proper tests. The discovery set off the California Gold Rush of 1849, and in doing so helped empty the Georgia town Wimmer had once lived in. Today Auraria is a ghost town a few miles southwest of Dahlonega, where an old store, a red house long identified as a bank and a scatter of foundations are about all that remain of one of Georgia's first gold rush boomtowns.

## Scuffle Town

The rush began on former Cherokee lands. Thousands of settlers poured into the area during the Georgia Gold Rush and after the Gold Lottery of 1832, and in June of that year William Dean built a cabin between the Chestatee and Etowah rivers. A town grew around it with startling speed, collecting names as it went: Dean, Deans, Nuckollsville and, tellingly, Scuffle Town. Nathaniel Nuckolls built a tavern, a hotel and several buildings to house the miners, and the settlement briefly served as the temporary seat of Lumpkin County. Within six months of the lottery, the town counted "one hundred family dwellings, eighteen or twenty stores, twelve or fifteen law offices, and four or five taverns." By May 1833 it had 1,000 residents, with 10,000 in the county.

## A Golden Name, a Disputed Lot

Big names took an interest. Vice President John C. Calhoun bought the land east of town and established the Calhoun Mine. In November 1832, a traveling companion of his, Dr. Croft, suggested renaming the settlement Aureola. The townspeople preferred another suggestion, from John Powell: Auraria, from aurum, the Latin word for gold. Mines lined the banks of the Etowah River, Camp Creek and Cane Creek, with names like Battle Branch, Whim Hill, Hedwig-Chicago, Gold Hill, Barlow, Ralston and Etowah. Then came a legal dispute. The 40-acre gold lot on which most of Auraria stood had been won by John R. Plummer, but his right to take part in the lottery was questioned. Faced with that challenge, the lower court judges chose a different site for the county's business, north of Auraria near the Cane Creek mining area. Businesses and county offices followed, and Auraria went into a sharp decline. The first session of the Lumpkin County Superior Court met on August 22, 1833, in the place that became known as Dahlonega. Thanks to its location and political influence, Dahlonega went on to receive a federal mint for gold coins, and its star rose as Auraria's fell.

## The Name Goes West

The final blow came from the other side of the continent. After gold was found in California, and soon after in Colorado, Georgia's mining all but ceased as miners headed west in search of new ground. Auraria's population dwindled and the town deteriorated. Yet its name did not die. In 1858, William Greeneberry Russell and a party of men from Auraria set out for Kansas Territory and founded a settlement they also called Auraria, in what is now Colorado. That town later merged with Denver, so a piece of this Georgia gold town lives on in the history of Denver.

## What Remains

Finding Auraria takes a little effort. From the square in Dahlonega, the road west toward Dawsonville passes the college, whose gold-domed building stands on the foundation of the old mint, and a left turn at the bottom of that hill leads about 3 miles to the ghost town. The old red house on the left is often identified as the bank, though accounts differ. A dilapidated building just past it, once the hotel, has been reported cleared away in the early 2010s, although some accounts still describe it standing. Beyond that stands Woody's store at Castleberry Bridge Road, which stayed open until the early 1980s. From there, Castleberry Bridge Road leads down to the Etowah River, the same river that bounded William Dean's first cabin site.

## Flight Context

Auraria lies at about 34.475 N, 84.023 W in Lumpkin County, Georgia, a few miles southwest of Dahlonega, near the Etowah River. Little of the old town is visible from the air, so use Dahlonega and the Etowah River as references and fly at 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL. Lumpkin County-Wimpys Airport (FAA code 9A0) is about 6.5 nm north, and Lee Gilmer Memorial Airport (KGVL) in Gainesville is about 15.5 nm southeast. Watch for valley fog on cool mornings and afternoon thunderstorms in summer.
