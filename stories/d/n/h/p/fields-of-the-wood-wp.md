# Fields of the Wood

In the far western corner of North Carolina, the Church of God of Prophecy has spelled out the Ten Commandments across a mountainside in letters five feet tall, at a 200-acre park founded where A. J. Tomlinson believed his church was revealed.

**Location:** 35.1206, -84.2523

**Categories:** Church of God of Prophecy, Pentecostalism, Cherokee County, North Carolina, Tourist attractions in Cherokee County, North Carolina

## Introduction

Each letter is five feet tall and four feet wide, and the whole text stretches 300 feet across the face of a mountain. On Burger Mountain in Cherokee County, North Carolina, the Ten Commandments are laid out in what is described as the largest representation of them in the world, framed by a border of concrete. This is Fields of the Wood, a Christian religious park of roughly 200 acres owned by the Church of God of Prophecy, a Holiness Pentecostal denomination, in the state's far southwestern corner.

## A Revelation and a First Flight

The park was the creation of A. J. Tomlinson, who lived from 1865 to 1943 and was the ultimate founder of the several Holiness Pentecostal denominations known as the Church of God, five of which are headquartered in Cleveland, Tennessee. Tomlinson said that God revealed the true church to him here in 1903. He later realized that in the same year he founded his church in the extreme southwestern county of North Carolina, the Wright Brothers made their first successful flight at the far eastern end of the state. Tomlinson believed the same chapter of the Book of Isaiah had foretold both events. In 1940, as head of the Church of God of Prophecy, he returned to memorialize the place, naming it after a line in Psalm 132 of the King James Version: "We found it in the fields of the wood."

## Tracts From the Sky

Flight featured again at the dedication in 1941. As Tomlinson preached a sermon at the site, airplanes leased by the church dropped gospel tracts, an act meant to fulfill a verse in Deuteronomy comparing teaching to falling rain. A few temporary markers went up in 1940, but most of the permanent construction came after Tomlinson's death in 1943, under the direction of his son, M. A. Tomlinson, and other church leaders. Aviation remains part of the landscape: a small airplane retired from the "White Angel Fleet" sits near the monuments. The surrounding countryside holds other places the Church of God treasures, including the house where its first General Assembly met in 1906, the site of a school where religious meetings in 1896 included speaking in tongues, known as glossolalia, and a marker for the Holiness Church at Camp Creek, a predecessor of the Church of God.

## Monuments on Two Mountains

Some of the park's markers speak to Christians generally, and others matter chiefly to members of the Church of God of Prophecy. Visitors find representations of the three crosses of Golgotha and the Garden Tomb, along with a baptismal pool. "Prayer Mountain" holds 29 monuments bearing Bible quotations and four markers summarizing the church's history, and at its summit stands a "Prayed and Prevailed" marker where visitors often leave written prayers. Across on Burger Mountain, above the Ten Commandments, a concrete "All Nations Cross" is outlined on the ground, surrounded by 86 flagpoles flying the flags of countries where the church has ministries. When the park was built, that cross was billed as the largest Christian cross in the world. Three short trails lead back down to picnic tables and a gift shop. There is no campground and no large preaching venue, and local newspaper readers voted the park Cherokee County's best attraction in 2023 and again in 2024.

## Flight Context

Fields of the Wood lies at 35.1206°N, 84.2523°W in the mountains of Cherokee County in far southwestern North Carolina, near the Tennessee and Georgia lines. The Ten Commandments, 300 feet across on the slope of Burger Mountain, and the cross and flagpoles at its summit are large enough to make a distinctive target on a clear day. Mountainous terrain surrounds the site, so fly no lower than 5,000-6,000 feet MSL and stay well above the ridgelines. Martin Campbell Field in Copperhill, Tennessee (no ICAO code listed; FAA identifier 1A3) is about 8 nm to the southwest, and Western Carolina Regional Airport in Andrews (KRHP) about 20 nm to the east-northeast. Chattanooga Metropolitan (KCHA) is about 47 nm west and McGhee Tyson (KTYS) near Knoxville about 43 nm north-northeast. Valley fog and low cloud are common in the mornings, and summer afternoons bring thunderstorms.
