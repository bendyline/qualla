# Whiteside Mountain

Between Cashiers and Highlands in North Carolina, Whiteside Mountain carries some of the highest cliffs in eastern North America, a Cherokee monster legend, and a mysterious Spanish carving that someone once claimed for Hernando de Soto.

**Location:** 35.0809, -83.1382

**Categories:** Mountains of North Carolina, Landforms of Jackson County, North Carolina, Landmarks in North Carolina

## Introduction

Someone carved a message into the overhanging rock near the top of Whiteside Mountain, and the question of who has never quite been settled. The carving itself was read as a claim that Hernando de Soto had been there, and that his men had thrown their sick, crippled horses over the edge. Another explanation blamed a few boys in 1926. A third, and stranger, came from a minister's confession. The riddle suits the mountain. Whiteside, known to the Cherokee as Sanigilâ'gĭ, rises in Jackson County, North Carolina, between Cashiers, Highlands and the Georgia border, and it holds some of the highest cliffs in eastern North America.

## Spearfinger's Bridge

Long before anyone scratched letters into its stone, the mountain had a place in Cherokee storytelling. According to Cherokee myth, Spearfinger, the powerful woman monster, tried to build a rock bridge through the air from the Hiwassee River to Whiteside Mountain, until the Higher Beings struck it with lightning. She is one of the more fearsome figures of Cherokee legend, a witch whose name, U'tlun'ta', means "the one with the pointed spear," for a right forefinger shaped like a spear or a blade. She was also called Stone-dress, for skin as hard as rock. It is a fitting legend for a mountain of sheer stone.

## The Courthouse on the Cliffs

Among Whiteside's cliffs is a feature called Devil's Courthouse, not to be confused with the Devil's Courthouse about 20 miles away in Transylvania County. Getting there has become harder by design. Since the 1980s the Forest Service has tried to restrict access to it and has let the foot trail leading there grow over, because of dangers such as strong winds, and it has also sought to prevent accidents from paragliding and similar activities launched from the summit or the Courthouse. There is also an overhang, a small platform less than 7 feet across, which juts out from the mountain like a cantilever. Standing on it, with nothing but air below, is not for the faint of heart.

## A Memorial to a Future Saint

The carving on the overhang long passed as evidence that Hernando de Soto came this way. But the terrain is too rough for men and horses to cross, especially in an age with no roads or paths, and the inscription may well have been a hoax cut by some boys around 1926, when more of the overhang probably survived than does today. Then a different account emerged. The inscription actually reads "'T.T.' Un luego Santa a la memoria," and by one account the Rev. Herman Alley confessed in 1946 to carving it. He loved the Spanish language, and he is said to have made the carving during a sad period in his life, meaning to say "a memorial to a future saint." Which story is true remains uncertain, though its quotation marks, at least, would have been out of place in De Soto's day.

## Flight Context

Whiteside Mountain rises to about 4,930 feet at roughly 35.081 N, 83.138 W in Jackson County, North Carolina, between the towns of Cashiers and Highlands and a short distance north of the Georgia border. Its cliffs make it a distinctive target; fly at least 2,000 feet above the summit, around 7,000 feet MSL, and give the cliffs a wide berth, as strong winds around the mountain can produce turbulence and downdrafts. Jackson County Airport (FAA code 24A) near Sylva is about 15 nm north-northwest, and Macon County Airport (FAA code 1A5) near Franklin about 16 nm west-northwest. Pickens County Airport (KLQK) in South Carolina is about 27 nm southeast. Mountain weather changes quickly, and low cloud can hide the summit.
