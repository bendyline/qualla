# Clermont Lounge

In the basement of a 1924 building on Ponce de Leon Avenue, Atlanta's oldest strip club has outlasted city closure attempts, a shuttered hotel and six decades of change with its cash-only, jukebox-driven charm intact.

**Location:** 33.7735, -84.3613

**Categories:** Culture of Atlanta, Nightclubs in the United States, Dive bars in the United States, National Register of Historic Places in Fulton County, Georgia

## Introduction

Upstairs, the Hotel Clermont is a restored boutique hotel, its historic details polished and its building listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Downstairs, and entirely separate, is something Atlanta has never managed to polish at all. The Clermont Lounge does not serve food or draft beer, and it does not take credit cards. The dancers pick their own songs on a jukebox and pay for them out of their own pockets. Since 1965 this basement bar at 789 Ponce de Leon Avenue has been the city's oldest strip club, and against considerable odds it is still here.

## A Basement With Many Lives

The building above came first. Constructed in 1924, the Clermont began as an apartment building before it was converted into a hotel. Its basement cycled through a series of nightclubs, including the Gypsy Club, the Continental Room and the Anchorage. For a time the space was the "Atlanta Playboy Club," an unofficial attempt to cash in on the popularity of Hugh Hefner's magazine, until a lawsuit shut it down in the early 1960s. In 1965 the Clermont Lounge opened its doors, and it has stayed put ever since.

## Two Businesses, One Address

Though they share a building and half a name, the lounge and the hotel are completely separate businesses. There is no way into the lounge from inside the hotel; guests of the bar use their own entrance at the back of the building, near the hotel's valet parking area. That separation proved vital. In December 2009 the health department ordered the hotel closed over a long list of violations, from dirty linen and mold on the walls to black water spilling from faucets and broken toilets. The lounge kept operating. The property went on the market in 2010, and Clermont Hotel Partners LLC bought it in January 2013 with plans for a boutique hotel. The lounge itself went dark for months in 2017 while the building was redeveloped, its reopening delayed more than once, and in 2018 the hotel reopened, brought up to code with its iconic details restored. The building joined the National Register in 2021.

## The Stage in the Middle of the Bar

Inside, a single stage sits in the middle of a circular bar, and there is usually no DJ at all, just the jukebox. The club has a completely female ownership, and it has built a nationwide reputation for its kitschy atmosphere and its unconventional dancers. The crowd tends to swing between a few handfuls of regulars and waves of college students, newcomers to town and tourists. Among its dancers is Anita Rae Strange, known on stage as Blondie, who has danced at the Clermont since 1979 and was the subject of the 2012 documentary AKA Blondie, directed by Jon Watts. The lounge has also survived multiple attempts by Atlanta's city government to close it.

## Late-Night Fame

The Clermont's unpolished reputation has drawn television cameras more than once. Insomniac with Dave Attell featured it in an episode that aired in February 2002, and in January 2013 the Travel Channel's The Layover showed Anthony Bourdain and Alton Brown visiting during an Atlanta episode. Music found it too. The shock rocker GG Allin wrote a song about the hotel while living in its Room 216, only months before he died of a heroin overdose, and it came out in 1993. Bubba Sparxxx released a track called "Claremont Lounge" in 2006, spelled differently but likely a nod to the basement bar that Atlanta could never quite close.

## Flight Context

The Clermont sits at 33.7735°N, 84.3613°W on Ponce de Leon Avenue in Atlanta's Poncey-Highland neighborhood, a little over 1 nm east of the Midtown skyline. The Jimmy Carter Presidential Library and Museum lies about half a nautical mile to the south-southeast. The lounge itself cannot be picked out from the air; use Ponce de Leon Avenue and the Midtown towers as references from 3,000-4,000 feet MSL. The area lies beneath Atlanta's Class B airspace, so any sightseeing requires ATC coordination. DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) is about 7 nm north-northeast, Fulton County Airport-Brown Field (KFTY) about 8 nm west, and Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) about 9 nm south-southwest. Summer afternoons bring haze and thunderstorms.
