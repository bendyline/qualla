# Jimmy Carter Library and Museum

On land once meant for a freeway interchange and once part of Sherman's headquarters, Atlanta's Jimmy Carter Library and Museum holds about 27 million pages of a presidency, a replica Oval Office and the Nobel Peace Prize.

**Location:** 33.7663, -84.3563

**Categories:** Presidential libraries, Presidential museums in Georgia (U.S. state), Museums in Atlanta, Jimmy Carter, Libraries in Georgia (U.S. state), 1986 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state)

## Introduction

The ground beneath the Jimmy Carter Library and Museum was supposed to carry traffic. The Georgia Department of Transportation had acquired this land in Atlanta's Poncey-Highland neighborhood for an interchange between two highways, but those roads were cancelled amid the city's freeway revolts, by Jimmy Carter himself when he was governor of Georgia. Years later, the land became the home of his presidential library. Its past runs deeper still: during the Civil War's Battle of Atlanta, this ground was part of General Sherman's headquarters.

## Georgia, Chosen Early

Early in his presidency, Carter signaled that he wanted his library in Georgia. The Atlanta firm Jova/Daniels/Busby designed it in cooperation with Lawton/Umemura/Yamamoto of Hawaii, and while the building went up, Carter's papers waited in the former post office building downtown. The library's first director, Dr. Donald Schewe, had helped transfer and process the administration's records when Carter's term ended in early 1981. The library opened to the public on October 1, 1986, Carter's 62nd birthday. Beside it runs John Lewis Freedom Parkway, a road that during planning was called Presidential Parkway and, at one point, Jimmy Carter Parkway. The complex is a hybrid: the library and museum are run by the National Archives and Records Administration as part of the federal presidential library system, while privately owned wings house Carter's offices and the Carter Center, a nonprofit human rights organization.

## Twenty-Seven Million Pages

The building covers 69,750 square feet, with 15,269 square feet devoted to exhibits and nearly 20,000 to archives and storage. Its stacks hold about 27 million pages of documents, more than half a million photographs and more than 40,000 objects, along with films, videos and audiotapes, covering everything from foreign and domestic policy to the private lives of Jimmy and Rosalynn Carter. Among its exhibits are two showpieces. One is a full-scale replica of the Oval Office as it looked during the Carter administration, complete with a reproduction of the Resolute desk. The other is Carter's Nobel Peace Prize, awarded in 2002 for the work that grew out of the Carter Center after he left office. The museum also opens its doors widely to young people: students 16 and under get in free, and funding from the library and The Coca-Cola Foundation helps Georgia schools cover transportation when they bring students for educational visits.

## Home to Plains

Most presidents since Herbert Hoover have been buried at their presidential libraries, but the Carters chose differently. They arranged to be laid to rest on the grounds of their home in Plains, Georgia, a house that is part of the Jimmy Carter National Historical Park and is slated to become a museum under the National Park Service. The Atlanta library still had a final role to play. After Rosalynn Carter's death, she lay in repose here on November 27, 2023. Jimmy Carter died on December 29, 2024, at 100, the longest-lived president in American history, and from January 4 to 7 he too lay in repose at the library before being flown to Washington, D.C., for his state funeral. The building that began as a highway site ended up as the place where Atlanta said goodbye.

## Flight Context

The library and museum sit at 33.7663°N, 84.3563°W in Atlanta's Poncey-Highland neighborhood, beside John Lewis Freedom Parkway, about 2 nm east-northeast of downtown's towers; the parkway is the best line to follow toward it. The area lies beneath Atlanta's Class B airspace, so any sightseeing at 3,000-4,000 feet MSL needs ATC coordination. Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) is about 8.5 nm to the south-southwest, DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) about 7 nm to the north-northeast, and Fulton County Airport-Brown Field (KFTY) about 8 nm to the west. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
