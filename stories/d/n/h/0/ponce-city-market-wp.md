# Ponce City Market

A vast 1926 Sears, Roebuck building on Atlanta's Ponce de Leon Avenue, later City Hall East and then empty, was reborn in 2014 as Ponce City Market, a food hall, shops, offices and homes beside the BeltLine.

**Location:** 33.7729, -84.3657

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Atlanta, Food markets in the United States, Mixed-use developments in Georgia (U.S. state), Sears Holdings buildings and structures, National Register of Historic Places in Fulton County, Georgia

## Introduction

At noon, three times a week, old-time musicians and string bands went out over WSB radio from the tower of the Sears, Roebuck building on Atlanta's Ponce de Leon Avenue. The show was called Dinner Bell R.F.D., for a club called the Radio Farmers' Democracy, and from August 1926 until October 1928 it was part of Sears' push to win over Southern farmers. The building that housed it still stands, all 2.1 million square feet of it, one of the largest by volume in the Southeast. Today it is Ponce City Market.

## Springs, Rides and a Retail Giant

The site had a life before Sears. It was once Ponce de Leon Springs, and later the Ponce de Leon amusement park. In 1926 Sears, Roebuck and Co. opened a combined retail store, warehouse and regional office here, on a lot that covers 16 acres, and Atlanta's public broadcaster would later recall the opening under the headline "Largest Building in the South Opens on Ponce de Leon Avenue." The Atlanta headquarters was closely tied to the Sears Agricultural Foundation and its courtship of farm families. Beyond the radio show, Sears ran a farmers' market at the back of the property from May 1930 through New Year's Day 1947. The retail store closed in 1979, and the building continued as a Sears regional office until 1987.

## From Catalog Warehouse to City Hall East

In May 1990 the City of Atlanta bought the building for $12 million, planning to house 2,000 police and fire employees and rent space to county, state and federal agencies. The central offices of the police and fire departments moved in, a city-funded art gallery opened on the first floor, and from 1995 to 1999 the Southeastern Flower Show filled its halls. The building became known as City Hall East, and it served the city for two decades before closing to the public on March 29, 2010. The following year, on July 11, 2011, the city sold the property for $27 million to Jamestown, the investment firm behind New York's Chelsea Market, which financed a $180 million plan by developer Green Street Properties to turn the old catalog giant into a mixed-use complex.

## A Market Under One Roof

Jamestown's managing director said the goal was to create the country's fourth nationally relevant food hall, alongside Seattle's Pike Place, San Francisco's Ferry Building and Chelsea Market. Ponce City Market officially opened on August 25, 2014, its Central Food Hall followed in 2015, and the building was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2016. Inside are apartments, shops, educational facilities and offices for tenants including FanDuel, Capital One and Airbus, along with a food hall whose original lineup included restaurants from James Beard Award-winning chefs Anne Quatrano and Linton Hopkins. The roof holds an amusement park, a curious echo of the rides that once stood on this ground. Planners hoped the market, together with the neighboring BeltLine trail and Historic Fourth Ward Park, would help stitch together the four neighborhoods that meet here and revive the Ponce de Leon corridor.

## Flight Context

Ponce City Market stands at approximately 33.773°N, 84.366°W on Ponce de Leon Avenue in Atlanta's Old Fourth Ward, where the BeltLine trail crosses the avenue, between Midtown and the Virginia-Highland and Poncey-Highland neighborhoods. From 2,500–3,500 ft AGL, the massive building with its central tower is easy to spot east of the Midtown skyline, beside the BeltLine trail; Atlanta's Class B airspace covers this area at some altitudes, so check the current terminal area chart for the floors and coordinate with ATC. DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) is about 13 km to the north-northeast, Fulton County Airport–Brown Field (KFTY) about 14 km to the west, and Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) about 16 km to the south-southwest. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms; spring and autumn offer the clearest views.
