# Fernbank Museum of Natural History

Built beside a rare stretch of old-growth Piedmont forest that one Atlanta woman loved and protected, the Fernbank Museum pairs a 123-foot dinosaur in its atrium with 65 acres of living woods out back.

**Location:** 33.7739, -84.3280

**Categories:** Museums in Atlanta, Natural history museums in Georgia (U.S. state), Dinosaur museums in the United States, Druid Hills, Georgia, Museums in DeKalb County, Georgia

## Introduction

Emily Harrison grew up in the late 1800s in a patch of woods east of Atlanta, and she had a name for it: Fernbank. The name stuck, and so did the woods. Today a glass-walled atrium looks out over that same forest, and inside stands a dinosaur so enormous it seems to belong to another world, a 123-foot Argentinosaurus. The Fernbank Museum of Natural History exists because one environmentalist and her allies decided a forest was worth keeping, and the trees outside remain as much a part of the visit as anything in the galleries.

## A Forest With a Charter

Harrison did not keep Fernbank to herself. Along with others, she created a charter for Fernbank in 1938, and the group secured the woodland on which the museum now stands. In 1964 the Fernbank trustees joined with the DeKalb County School System to create the Fernbank Science Center, and that partnership sparked a wider wish to share Fernbank's resources with the general public. The science center, run by the DeKalb County Board of Education, remains a separate organization, not affiliated with the museum. The museum itself took longer. After master planning by the Cambridge, Massachusetts, firm Graham Gund Architects, ground was broken in 1989, and the Fernbank Museum of Natural History opened on October 5, 1992, tucked behind a row of historic houses.

## Giants in the Atrium

Visitors meet the headliners first. Giants of the Mesozoic fills the atrium with a 123-foot Argentinosaurus, one of the largest dinosaurs ever classified, alongside a Giganotosaurus. Beyond them stood A Walk Through Time in Georgia, which told two stories at once, the natural history of the state and the development of the planet itself; the gallery was scheduled to close in April 2026, with a new exhibit, Changing Earth, due to open in its place in 2027 as part of a multi-year renovation. Children head for Fernbank NatureQuest, an immersive, interactive exhibition designed and produced by Thinkwell Group, which won the 2012 Thea Award for Outstanding Achievement for a Museum Exhibit. Other galleries explore shells, clay, culture and the sky, and special exhibitions rotate through every two to four months. The Rankin M. Smith, Sr. Giant Screen Theater, once an IMAX theater, was upgraded in 2017 with a 4K 3D laser projection system.

## Old Trees in a New City

The museum's greatest exhibit may be the one outside. It stands on 65 acres that the museum describes as the largest old-growth urban Piedmont forest in the country, a mature mixed woodland with relatively old trees for the region, which has made it a subject of extensive scientific study. Along one slope grow large white oaks and tulip poplars that can reach 156 feet tall, with beech, oaks, hickories, winged elm and red maple filling out the canopy. In 2016 the museum opened WildWoods, an accessible 10-acre area of trails and interactive exhibits directly behind the building, and at the same time opened access to the newly restored Fernbank Forest. Once a month, on the second Friday, Fernbank After Dark brings adults back for hands-on activities, food, drinks and live music, and teenagers aged 13 to 17 can volunteer year-round as Fernbank Youth Interpreters.

## Flight Context

The museum sits at 33.7739°N, 84.3280°W in the Druid Hills area east of Atlanta, and the dense tree canopy of Fernbank Forest behind it is the best visual cue. Downtown Atlanta's towers lie about 3.5 nm to the west, and the Jimmy Carter Presidential Library and Museum is about 1.4 nm west. The area lies beneath Atlanta's Class B airspace, so sightseeing at 3,000-4,000 feet MSL requires ATC coordination. DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) is about 6 nm to the north, Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL) about 10 nm to the southwest, and Fulton County Airport-Brown Field (KFTY) about 10 nm to the west. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
