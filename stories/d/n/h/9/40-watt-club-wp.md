# 40 Watt Club

Named for the single bare bulb in a College Avenue loft, Athens' 40 Watt Club moved five times and became home base for Pylon, R.E.M. and generations of bands after them.

**Location:** 33.9583, -83.3799

## Introduction

One bulb. That was the entire lighting scheme in Curtis Crowe's loft at 171 College Avenue in Athens, Georgia, back in the late 1970s: a single 40-watt bulb hanging from the ceiling. Crowe and his friend Bill Tabor joked that the place was a "40 Watt Club," and the joke stuck harder than either could have imagined. The name has since moved through five addresses and more than four decades of music. From that dim room grew a venue that served as the main stage for Pylon, R.E.M. and a whole generation of what people came to call "Athens bands."

## Halloween in the Loft, a Riff Through the Floor

The first party in Crowe's space took place on Halloween, October 31. Onstage, such as it was, stood his band Strictly American, a group of friends from Marietta, Georgia, that included members of the future Guadalcanal Diary. It was a loft, a crowd and a bulb, and the informality was the whole point. That do-it-yourself ethos, more than any single show, is what would later make the 40 Watt so important to punk rock and to the "scene" that took shape in Athens. Meanwhile, the building had music coming up through the floor. Crowe and Tabor would hang out upstairs and listen as Michael Lachowski and Randy Bewley practiced the same riff over and over again in the space directly below. Eventually Crowe went downstairs, knocked on their door and asked whether they could use a drummer. Pylon was born. When the new band toured the Northeast, Crowe began to think that opening a real club on a shoestring budget was possible. He did exactly that, officially opening the club in May 1980, and the joke name from the loft became the name on the door.

## Five Addresses, One Name

The club has rarely stayed put. After the loft at 171 College Avenue came a second College Avenue address, 101 College Avenue, known as "40 Watt East," where the club officially opened in May 1980. It then moved to 256 West Clayton Street, spent part of the mid-1980s as "40 Watt Uptown" on East Broad Street, and returned to West Clayton Street in 1987. In 1991 it moved to 285 West Washington Street, where it has stayed ever since. Accounts of the early dates and street numbers differ. Through much of that journey one person has kept the lights on: Barrie Buck, the club's owner since 1987.

## Home Base for Athens

Over the years the 40 Watt served as the primary performance space for a long roll call of local bands, among them Pylon, R.E.M., Love Tractor, Dreams So Real, Guadalcanal Diary, the Primates and Modern Skirts. Later generations found the same welcome. Of Montreal, Reptar, Drive-By Truckers and the Whigs, all nationally known bands with Athens roots, have treated the club as home base. Touring acts came too, and the list of those who have played the room is striking in its range: Nirvana, Iggy Pop, Run-DMC, Sonic Youth, Wilco, Cat Power, Interpol, Built to Spill, My Morning Jacket, Belle and Sebastian, Morphine and Ween. Along with CBGB, the Whisky a Go Go and a select few others, the 40 Watt is credited as instrumental in launching American punk rock and new wave music.

## Legendary, Officially

Recognition followed. VH1 ranked the 40 Watt as the second most legendary rock club in the country. Musicians from abroad have felt its pull as well. Playing the club in October 2018, former Smiths guitarist Johnny Marr told the crowd: "It's an interesting thing for me, as a British musician, and all those guys as British musicians, to come to this place and play for you guys." It is a long way from a bare bulb in a College Avenue loft, but every show still happens under a name that began as a joke about the lighting.
