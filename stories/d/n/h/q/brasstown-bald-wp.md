# Brasstown Bald

Georgia's highest summit, the mountain the Cherokee call Enotah, carries a flood legend, a name born of a mistranslation, and a view that on a clear day reaches Atlanta's skyline 85 miles away.

**Location:** 34.8742, -83.8107

**Categories:** Mountains of Georgia (U.S. state), Highest points of U.S. states, Appalachian balds, Chattahoochee-Oconee National Forest, Cherokee history

## Introduction

In the Cherokee telling, a great flood swept over the land and everyone died except a few families who took refuge in a giant canoe. When the canoe finally ran aground, it came to rest on the summit of a forested mountain. There was no game to hunt and no place to plant, so the Great Spirit killed all the trees on the mountaintop, and the survivors grew their crops there until the water subsided. That mountain is Enotah. On modern maps it is Brasstown Bald, and at 4,784 feet it is the highest point in Georgia.

## A Name Lost in Translation

The English name is the product of a mistake. According to two Georgia historical markers, the country around the mountain was settled by the Cherokee, who called their village here Itse'yĭ, meaning "New Green Place" or "Place of Fresh Green." English-speaking settlers confused that word with Ûňtsaiyĭ, the Cherokee word for brass, and the settlement became Brasstown. The Cherokee gave the same locative name to several distinct places in their territory, including one just across the state line in present-day North Carolina, where a community called Brasstown still sits in Clay County.

The mountain's own Cherokee name has been transliterated in several ways: Echia, Echoee, Etchowee and Enotah. The word "bald" is southern Appalachian shorthand for a mountaintop with an unobstructed 360-degree view, which fits a summit whose trees, in the legend, were cleared on purpose.

## A Summit Split Down the Middle

Brasstown Bald rises in the Blue Ridge Mountains of northeastern Georgia, south of the city of Hiawassee, within the Blue Ridge Ranger District of the Chattahoochee National Forest. The line between Towns and Union counties runs straight through the peak. Underfoot, the summit is ringed by ultramafic rock such as soapstone and dunite.

Height changes everything here. The summit has an oceanic climate, cooler than most of the Southeastern United States and noticeably wetter than the lower cities of the Upland South. It is also home to a creature known from nowhere else: Brasstown Bald is the only known location of the isopod Ligidium enotahensis, whose scientific name carries the Cherokee name of the mountain. On a clear day the view from the top reaches the tallest buildings of Atlanta, some 85 miles away.

## Roads That Punish

Getting there is its own test. From the northeast, starting where Owl Creek Road meets the Unicoi Turnpike near Mountain Scene, the climb runs 8.5 miles and gains 2,820 feet at an average grade of 6.3 percent. The route from the west, beginning near Choestoe at the Richard B. Russell Scenic Highway, is 9.3 miles and gains 2,940 feet. The cruelest stretch is the last: from Jacks Gap, where Route 180 meets the Route 180 Spur, the road gains 1,720 feet in 2.9 miles at an average gradient of 11.2 percent.

Those numbers made the mountain a natural stage for professional cycling. From 2005 through 2008, the Tour de Georgia finished a King of the Mountains stage on Brasstown Bald, rated hors catégorie — French for "beyond category."

## The Last Half Mile

Most visitors take the easy way. Georgia State Route 180 Spur leads to a parking lot at the foot of a steep, 0.6-mile paved trail to the top, and from there a shuttle bus or that hike finishes the ascent to the summit observation deck. Up top, U.S. Forest Service webcams watch the horizon, and NOAA Weather Radio station KXI22 broadcasts from the summit.

A stone monument honors Thomas S. Candler, a former judge of the Georgia Supreme Court, who worked to bring more visitors to the mountain and to establish a visitor center for them. It was erected in 1971, three months before he died.

The mountain also marks a rite of passage. The Wagon Train Trail climbs to the summit from Young Harris College, and graduating seniors traditionally hike it in the days before commencement, ending with a picnic dinner and a vespers service at the top — a gathering on the same summit where, in the old story, the survivors of the flood began again.

## Flight Context

Brasstown Bald's summit is at 34.8742°N, 83.8107°W, elevation 4,784 ft MSL, the highest terrain in Georgia. Look for the summit tower and visitor center complex on the ridge south of Hiawassee; Brasstown and the North Carolina line lie just to the north. Keep at least 2,000 ft above the summit (6,800 ft MSL or higher) when crossing the ridge, and expect mechanical turbulence and downdrafts on the lee side in any wind. Nearby airports: Blairsville Airport (KDZJ), about 9 nm west; Western Carolina Regional Airport at Andrews, NC (KRHP), about 19 nm north; and Habersham County Airport (KAJR), about 26 nm south-southeast. The summit sits in its own weather — cooler and wetter than the surrounding valleys — and can be cloud-capped when the lowlands are clear; check ceilings before planning a close look.
