# Helen, Georgia

A fading logging town on the Chattahoochee River remade itself as a Bavarian alpine village in the Georgia mountains — and wrote the look into its zoning code.

**Location:** 34.7025, -83.7275

**Categories:** Cities in White County, Georgia, German-American culture in Georgia (U.S. state), Georgia populated places on the Chattahoochee River, Oktoberfest, Tourist attractions in White County, Georgia

## Introduction

In Helen, Georgia, even the national chains have to look Bavarian. Since 1969 the town's zoning has required the classic southern German style on every building, franchises included, so the main street along the Chattahoochee River reads like a village transplanted from the Alps to the Appalachians. It is a costume Helen chose for itself. Before the makeover, this was a logging town in decline, and the idea of remaking it as an old-world village came from John Kollock, an Atlanta artist. Only 531 people lived here at the 2020 census, yet on a peak autumn weekend the streets can be crowded.

## A Daughter's Name and a Very Old Road

Long before the lumbermen, this ground was traveled and settled. Helen grew up along the former route of the Unicoi Turnpike, a Native American trail some 1,000 years old that linked what are now Tennessee, North Carolina and Georgia, and the Nacoochee Indian Mound, one of the area's landmarks, was occupied from 1350 to 1600.

The town itself was laid out by John E. Mitchell of St. Louis in 1912 and 1913. When the small settlement needed a name, a lumber official suggested his daughter's, and there was little opposition. Helen, nicknamed the "Star of the North," was incorporated on August 18, 1913. It was a logging town, and in time a logging town in decline.

## Reinventing a Mill Town

Kollock's suggestion was audacious: turn Helen into a replica of a Bavarian alpine town. The community took it seriously enough to write it into law. Zoning first adopted in 1969 mandates the style on every building, down to the small number of national franchisees that have set up shop.

The transformation was recorded as it happened. In 1975 a Documerica photographer, Al Stephenson, documented the life, recreation and economy of the Helen area before and during the reconstruction. His images include the office of The Helen Mountain Eagle, a monthly newspaper, photographed in July 1975, and dancers at Helen's Oktoberfest that same year — a small mountain community rehearsing, in real time, the identity that would define it.

## Oktoberfest, Balloons and Volkswagens

Tourism is now the heart of Helen's economy. Most visitors come for the weekend from the Atlanta area, joined by motorcyclists who enjoy the roads in and around town. Late October, when the autumn leaves typically peak, draws big crowds, and Helen's own Oktoberfest stretches across September, October and November.

The calendar is full the rest of the year too. An annual hot-air balloon race lifts off on the first weekend in June, and a "Southern Worthersee" meet has gathered Volkswagen and Audi enthusiasts in a stateside tribute to the Worthersee Tour in Austria. All of that traffic funnels onto Georgia 75, the main north-south road through town, which crosses the Chattahoochee in the middle of downtown on a bridge carrying the Helen stream gauge. A parallel route to the west, Georgia 75 Alternate, exists largely to bypass the jams that form in autumn and on some weekends.

## Lake, Falls and Forest

Immediately northeast of town on Georgia 356, Unicoi State Park and Lodge wraps around Unicoi Lake, a freshwater lake with a swimming beach, trout streams, wheelchair-accessible fishing docks and seasonal canoe, kayak and paddleboat rentals. The park holds 7.5 miles of hiking trails, with more in the adjoining Chattahoochee National Forest, and 8 miles of mountain biking trails. Access to Anna Ruby Falls, part of the national forest, runs through the park.

Hardman Farm State Historic Site lies near Helen as well. North of town, Georgia 17 and 75 run together for 21 miles to Hiawassee, climbing into the high country crowned by Brasstown Bald, the highest peak in Georgia.

## Helen on Screen

A town built to look like somewhere else has proved irresistible to filmmakers. Smokey and the Bandit (1977) includes a scene shot on Chimney Mountain Road just outside Helen. In the second season of the television series Atlanta, the episode titled "Helen," which aired on March 22, 2018, sends the characters Vanessa and Earn to a Fastnacht celebration here; Vanessa, played by Zazie Beetz, is fluent in German. The Lifetime movie Christmas Love Letter was filmed in Helen and aired on December 21, 2019.

Each production borrows the same illusion that Kollock imagined and the 1969 zoning made permanent: an alpine village in the Georgia mountains, a logging town that decided it would rather be a storybook.

## Flight Context

Helen lies at 34.7025°N, 83.7275°W in the Chattahoochee River valley of White County, Georgia, where the Blue Ridge begins to climb toward the North Carolina line. From the air, look for the compact downtown strung along the river and Georgia 17/75, with Unicoi Lake immediately to the northeast. Brasstown Bald (4,784 ft), Georgia's highest point, rises roughly 11 nm to the north-northwest, so plan at least 3,000 ft AGL over town and more to the north. Nearby airports: Habersham County Airport (KAJR), about 15 nm southeast; Blairsville Airport (KDZJ), about 16 nm northwest across the mountains; and Lumpkin County-Wimpys Airport near Dahlonega (FAA identifier 9A0, no ICAO code), about 16 nm west-southwest. Mountain weather changes quickly; low clouds and fog can fill the valleys on autumn mornings, the same season the foliage draws the crowds.
