# Horse Pens 40

Named for a 40-acre tract in an old deed, Horse Pens 40 on Alabama's Chandler Mountain is a boulder field prized by climbers, an ancient rock shelter, and a bluegrass park the state legislature calls the home of the South's bluegrass music.

**Location:** 33.9231, -86.3032

**Categories:** Protected areas of St. Clair County, Alabama, Parks in Alabama, Climbing areas of Alabama

## Introduction

The name comes straight out of an old land deed. When the plot on top of Chandler Mountain was divided, the document laid it out in three parts: "the home 40, the farming 40, and the horse pens 40," each tract holding 40 acres. The last of those phrases stuck, and today Horse Pens 40 is a privately owned outdoor nature park in St. Clair County, near Steele, Alabama, in the foothills of the Appalachian Mountains. Climbers know it as a premier bouldering site in the American Southeast. Bluegrass fans know it as something else entirely.

## Elephant Rock and the Stone Fort

The park is a landscape of stone. Vertical rock faces and unusual formations crowd the mountaintop, and many have names of their own: Lookout Point, Elephant Rock, Mushroom Rock, the Stone Fort and the Horse Pens themselves. By the park's account, its natural rock shelters have seen more than 15,000 years of human habitation. Its official website describes the place as "a natural stone fortress atop a fortress-like mountain," and says Native Americans used it as a protected village and ceremonial area for thousands of years. According to that account, living, working and burial areas across the park date from the Paleo and Archaic periods up to more recent times. The site also claims what may be the only remaining example in the United States of an ancient leaching pit that has seen actual historical use.

## Slider, Genesis and a Pope in a Cowboy Hat

For climbers, Horse Pens 40 is a destination. Its boulders carry problems graded from roughly V0 to V11, and a roster of notable routes records the favorites. Genesis, graded V3, was the first route established at the park. At the harder end of that roster sits the Slider family: Slider at V9, with Super Slider and Slider Sit both at V10. In between are routes with names that show climbers' sense of humor, including Crisifix, Brawn, Wasp, Uniball, Thumbalina, Man With The Slow Hand, Stretch Armstrong, Millipede, Mortal Combat, Red Arrow, Spirit, Bumboy and the memorable Pope In A Cowboy Hat. The park hosts annual bouldering competitions, including a leg of the "Triple Crown" series, drawing climbers to test themselves on the same stone.

## The Home of the South's Bluegrass Music

Horse Pens 40 has a second identity. The site is home to a Bluegrass Park that hosts festivals showcasing the genre several times a year, and the biggest of them has traditionally fallen on the Fourth of July. The Alabama State Legislature has formally recognized the park, in House/Senate Joint Resolution 177, as "The Home of the South's Bluegrass Music." Visitors who come for the music or the rock can stay overnight, since the park offers both RV sites and primitive camping. On the Fourth of July, the boulders that draw climbers have long been the backdrop for a bluegrass party on a mountaintop in the Alabama foothills.

## Flight Context

Horse Pens 40 is at 33.9231°N, 86.3032°W atop Chandler Mountain in St. Clair County, Alabama, near Steele, in the foothills of the Appalachians. The mountain itself is the landmark; the boulder field itself is best appreciated from about 2,000–3,000 feet AGL. Northeast Alabama Regional Airport (KGAD) at Gadsden is about 11 nm east-northeast, Robbins Field at Oneonta (FAA 20A) is about 5 nm northwest, and Peterson Field (FAA 7AL2, private use, permission required) is about 3.5 nm south. Birmingham-Shuttlesworth International (KBHM) lies about 31 nm southwest, and Anniston Regional (KANB) about 30 nm southeast. Ridgetop terrain can generate turbulence on windy days, and summer thunderstorms build quickly over the hills.
