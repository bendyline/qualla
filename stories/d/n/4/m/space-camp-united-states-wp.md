# Space Camp (United States)

Born from Wernher von Braun's observation of note-taking schoolchildren, Huntsville's Space Camp has spent four decades spinning, launching and moonwalking young people toward science.

**Location:** 34.7111, -86.6517

**Categories:** Space camps, U.S. Space & Rocket Center, Summer camps in Alabama, 1982 establishments in Alabama, Wernher von Braun

## Introduction

In 1977, Wernher von Braun was touring the U.S. Space & Rocket Center in Huntsville, Alabama, when he noticed a group of students taking notes on what they were learning. The sight led the rocket pioneer to propose an idea: a camp. Five years later, in 1982, the center's executive director, Edward O. Buckbee, founded Space Camp to promote mathematics and science to young people, using America's journey into space as the lesson plan.

## A Classroom Next to the Rockets

Space Camp sits on the grounds of the U.S. Space & Rocket Center museum, near NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center, and offers programs for children and adults on space exploration, aviation and robotics. Both the museum and the camp are run by the Alabama Space Science Exhibit Commission, a state agency whose members are appointed by the Governor of Alabama. A separate nonprofit, the U.S. Space & Rocket Center Foundation, handles scholarships, fundraising and the licensing of camps outside the United States. Space Camp was the first of the programs offered here, and its name now serves as the umbrella for all of them. Its signature session runs from Sunday to Friday for children between 9 and 11.

## Growing Up Through the Ranks

Campers can climb a ladder of programs as they age. Space Academy is intended for 12- to 14-year-olds, and Advanced Space Academy serves those from 15 to 18. That program began in the fall of 1987 as Space Academy Level II and in the same year became college accredited for one credit hour through the University of Alabama in Huntsville. Graduates can go on to Advanced Space Academy Elite. Family Camp lets parents or guardians attend alongside their children over a long weekend, and the camp once offered a twelve-day Advanced Space Academy that included multiple missions, scuba diving and a twenty-four-hour extended-duration mission. For aspiring pilots, the Aviation Challenge runs programs for ages 9 to 18, using computer-based flight simulators to train attendees to fly, act and think like fighter pilots.

## Spinning, Floating and Walking on the Moon

Then there are the simulators. The Multi-Axis Trainer simulates disorientation, much like the Multiple Axis Space Test Inertia Facility developed for Project Mercury. The 1/6 Chair simulates walking on the Moon, while the 5DF Chair mimics the frictionless environment of outer space in five degrees of freedom. The Manned Maneuvering Unit simulates working untethered, as astronauts do during a spacewalk. Rides on the museum grounds double as teaching tools: the Space Shot simulates liftoff, and the G-Force Accelerator reproduces the forces astronauts feel during launch or re-entry. Since February 2019, the Intuitive Planetarium, an 8K dome theater lit by laser projectors, has replaced the Spacedome IMAX theater that had operated at the center since 1982.

## Jedi, Alumni and a Hall of Their Own

Not every session is for kids. The center also runs corporate programs, programs for adults and educators, field trips for school groups and X-Camp, an outdoor leadership camp. Themed camps have occasionally appeared alongside museum exhibits, and in the summer of 2010 a Jedi Experience camp ran in connection with the traveling exhibit Star Wars: Where Science Meets Imagination. The camp has also tried to grow beyond Alabama. Space Camp California operated in Mountain View from 1996 to 2002, run by the now-defunct U.S. Space Camp Foundation, before financial difficulties closed it. Back in Huntsville, the 25th-anniversary celebrations in 2007 brought special alumni sessions and the inauguration of the Space Camp Hall of Fame, created to honor alumni and former employees for their contributions and the time they spent at Space Camp.

## Flight Context

Space Camp and the U.S. Space & Rocket Center sit at approximately 34.711°N, 86.652°W, in Huntsville, Alabama, near NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center. From 3,000–5,000 feet MSL, look for the museum grounds near the NASA center. Redstone Army Air Field (KHUA) is about 3 miles away and Huntsville International Airport (KHSV) about 9 miles; Huntsville Executive Airport–Tom Sharp Jr. Field (KMDQ, formerly Madison County Executive) lies about 11 miles away. With a military airfield so close, check current charts and NOTAMs before maneuvering nearby. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
