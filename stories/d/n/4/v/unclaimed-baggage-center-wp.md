# Unclaimed Baggage Center

Lost airline luggage ends up for sale in Scottsboro, Alabama, where a store founded in 1970 with a borrowed pickup truck now fills a city block, draws over a million shoppers a year and keeps a museum of the strangest things travelers have left behind, including a Jim Henson puppet.

**Location:** 34.6732, -86.0444

**Categories:** Companies based in Alabama, Tourist attractions in Jackson County, Alabama, Buildings and structures in Jackson County, Alabama

## Introduction

Somewhere in this building there might be something you once packed. Unclaimed Baggage in Scottsboro, Alabama, sells the contents of lost and unclaimed airline luggage, and it has been in business since 1970. It bills itself as the nation's only retailer of lost luggage. More than a million customers a year wander a store that stretches the length of a city block, browsing through thousands of newly added items. Most sell at discounts of 20 to 80 percent, and every one of them once belonged to a traveler who never got it back.

## A Tip Over Ham Radio

The business began with a conversation on the airwaves. In 1970 Doyle Owens, a Scottsboro insurance salesman, heard from a bus driver friend over ham radio that Trailways buses had more unclaimed luggage than they could deal with. Owens saw an opportunity. He borrowed a pickup truck, drove to Washington, D.C., collected the bags and brought them home to sell. Airline bags came later, in 1978, when the business landed its first airline contract, with Eastern Airlines. From that first load grew a business built on other people's lost belongings. In 1995 Doyle sold the store to his son, Bryan Owens, and Sharon Owens, who expanded the storefront to its present size and added a museum and a cafe.

## Half a Percent

Very little luggage actually goes permanently missing. Less than one half of one percent of bags in the United States end up unclaimed, and of what reaches Scottsboro, more than half is donated or thrown away because it is not suitable for sale. What survives is sorted and laid out by category across the sales floor. Some traditions have lasted decades. Since 1981 the store has held an annual ski sale on the first Saturday in November, an unlikely fixture on the calendar of a small Alabama town.

## Hoggle and the Medieval Armor

The strangest finds go on display. The Museum of Found Treasures, inside the store, features unusual items discovered in luggage. One display tells a story that began in 1997, when lost bags turned out to contain props from the 1986 film Labyrinth. The Jim Henson Company allowed the store to keep them, and among them was the puppet Hoggle. In 2024 the store published its first "Found Report," covering 2023 and tracking trends in what travelers pack along with the oddities that turn up. That year's list included a 13.3-foot women's pole vault and two live snakes, a reminder that anything people can carry onto a trip, they can also lose on one.

## Famous for Lost Things

A store selling strangers' luggage has proven irresistible to television. Unclaimed Baggage has drawn national attention on The Oprah Winfrey Show, Late Night with David Letterman, Fox News and the Today show. In 2024 Leanne Morgan visited and shared her experience on TikTok. The appeal is easy to understand. Every rack holds items with a story nobody here will ever fully know, from the airport they were checked in to the reason they never reached their owner.
