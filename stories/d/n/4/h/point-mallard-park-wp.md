# Point Mallard Park

A Decatur mayor's glimpse of German wave pools became one of America's earliest wave pools, a 500-acre park on Flint Creek where a president once drew a crowd larger than the town.

**Location:** 34.5735, -86.9380

**Categories:** Parks in Decatur, Alabama, Water parks in Alabama, Landmarks in Alabama, 1970 establishments in Alabama

## Introduction

Gilmer Blackburn, mayor of Decatur, Alabama, from 1962 to 1968, found his big idea in Germany, where he saw enclosed swimming pools that made their own waves. He became convinced that one could be built in the United States as a tourist attraction for his city. Working with J. Austin Smith, an Ohio pool manufacturer, Decatur designed its own, and on August 8, 1970, Point Mallard Park opened with a wave pool at its heart. More than half a century later, the pool still anchors a 500-acre park stretched along the shoreline of Flint Creek, a tributary of the nearby Tennessee River.

## Waves Without a Tax Dollar

The plan came with a condition. In 1967 the Point Mallard Authority was formed to create a new park around the aquatic attraction without the use of city tax revenue. Its site, in Decatur's far southeastern corner, had previously been occupied by the Tennessee Valley Authority for industrial use. The city issued $3.8 million in bonds for the initial cost, construction began in 1969 by most accounts, and when the gates opened the following August, visitors found the wave pool, an Olympic-sized swimming pool and a public golf course. For years Decatur's promotional materials boasted that it had built the nation's first wave pool. The claim was generous. Big Surf in Tempe, Arizona, opened its wave pool in the summer of 1969 and is the older of the two, and an outdoor wave pool had opened at Oceana Park in Newbury, Ohio, as early as Memorial Day 1961.

## An Olympian in Training

Point Mallard's pool drew more than tourists. From 1976 to 1979, Greg Louganis trained there, years before he won Olympic gold at the 1984 and 1988 Summer Games. In 1987, the wave pool took the name of the man who had imagined it, becoming the J. Gilmer Blackburn Aquatic Center. The facility kept growing with the decades. In 2013 the park finished a lazy river and two new speed slides, and in 2016 the Aquatic Center welcomed more than 165,000 visitors. The name of the park itself comes from the wetlands next door. Point Mallard borders the Wheeler National Wildlife Refuge and is named for a frequent avian visitor to the refuge, the mallard duck.

## The Day the Crowd Outnumbered the Town

In 1976, after nine years at Ogle Stadium and Delano Park beside Decatur High School, the city's Fourth of July celebration, the Spirit of America Festival, moved to Point Mallard. It brings musical performances, civic awards ceremonies and a closing fireworks display; a beauty pageant was part of the program until declining interest ended it in the 2010s. The festival's most famous moment came on July 4, 1984, when President Ronald Reagan, then campaigning for reelection, spoke to a crowd reported at some 60,000 people. Decatur at the time had only 40,000 to 50,000 residents, so by that estimate the audience was larger than the city itself. That visit is remembered as the festival's high-water mark for attendance, and the park's Ronald Reagan Field, a soccer field, is named in its honor.

## Balloons in May, Skirmishes in September

Since 1978, a second celebration has filled the park's calendar. The Alabama Jubilee Hot Air Balloon Classic takes place each year on Memorial Day and the days before it, and it began partly to debut a then-new municipal balloon meant to promote the city's tourism. Balloon racing is the main event, drawing enthusiasts mostly from the Southeast along with spectators, while car and tractor shows, arts and crafts and some live music fill out the weekend. Since around the 1990s, Civil War reenactors have staged mock battles here each September. Between festivals, the park stays busy with an 18-hole golf course on 200 acres, a campground of more than 200 campsites, a tennis center with clay and hard courts, a soccer park and trails for hiking, jogging and biking. The open-air ice complex that opened in 1973 closed in 2019, and the city planned to convert the building into an event center.

## Flight Context

Point Mallard Park lies at approximately 34.574°N, 86.938°W, in the far southeastern corner of Decatur, Alabama, along the shoreline of Flint Creek near the Tennessee River. From 2,000–3,500 feet MSL, look for the broad Tennessee River and the wetlands of the Wheeler National Wildlife Refuge that border the park; the golf course and pool complex stand out in clear weather. Pryor Field Regional Airport (KDCU) is about 5.5 miles away, Huntsville International Airport (KHSV) about 10 miles, and Hartselle-Morgan County Regional Airport (FAA identifier 5M0) about 11 miles. Around Memorial Day weekend and July 4, watch for hot-air balloons and fireworks-related activity. Summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
