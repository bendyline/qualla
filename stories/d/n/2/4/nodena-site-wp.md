# Nodena site

On a horseshoe bend of the Mississippi in northeast Arkansas, a carefully planned Mississippian town thrived from about 1400 to 1650 CE, and many archaeologists link its people to a province Hernando de Soto's expedition encountered.

**Location:** 35.5543, -89.9517

**Categories:** Mississippian culture, Archaeological sites, Native American history, National Historic Landmarks, Mississippi County, Arkansas, Hernando de Soto

## Introduction

Did Hernando de Soto's men walk here? In 1541 the Spanish expedition encountered a province known as Pacaha. Many archaeologists believe that province corresponds to the Nodena phase, a culture named for this site east of Wilson, Arkansas. Between about 1400 and 1650 CE, a town long described as palisaded stood here on a meander bend of the Mississippi River, its mounds, plazas and houses laid out with a precision that shows the whole place was planned.

## Neighbors, Traders and Rivals

Nodena was not alone. It is the type site for the Nodena phase, and archaeologists consider the nearby Parkin site a candidate for Casqui, another province of the period. The Nodena people were also part of the Southeastern Ceremonial Complex, an extensive religious and trade network. Through it, chert, whelk shells and other exotic goods made their way to this bend in the river. Because the site's artifacts date from about 1400 to 1650 CE, the town's life spans the years of the first Spanish expedition through the region, whatever the precise identity of the provinces the Spanish recorded.

## A Town Laid Out by Design

The village covered 15 acres on a horseshoe bend of the Mississippi about 5 miles east of Wilson, and early excavators described it as enclosed by a palisade, though more recent trenching has not confirmed one. It had three to eight mounds, two of them large platform mounds built to hold structures on their summits. The largest, Mound A, was 111 feet wide, 120 feet long and 15.5 feet high, with two levels: a top 40 by 60 feet and a terrace 30 feet wide. Excavators found the remains of three buildings on it, one on top and two on the terrace. Mound B, 117 by 111 feet and 4 feet tall, was reported by Hampson to have held the remains of a round structure 60 feet across. The site also had several large plazas, and directly behind Mound A lay what Hampson described as a "chunkey field." The houses were laid out in an orderly fashion on the same axis as the mounds, evidence that the town was designed as a whole. At the far end of the chunkey field stood Mound C, a low burial mound about 3 feet high and, depending on the account, 75 to 93 feet across. By one count, 316 graves lay beneath it, and nearly all of the people buried there were men.

## The Plantation Owner's Museum

The site lies on land that belonged to the Hampson Plantation, and its owner, Dr. James K. Hampson (1877-1956), was among the first to excavate it, and he preserved its artifacts and documented their discovery. In 1900 he also recorded the skeleton of a mastodon found on Island No. 35 in the Mississippi River, 2 miles south of the site. A museum for the collection was built in 1946, and on March 30, 1957, the Arkansas General Assembly formally accepted the artifacts from the Hampson family. The Hampson Museum State Park opened in Wilson in 1961; now called the Hampson Archeological Museum State Park, it moved into a new building on Wilson's town square in 2018. Its exhibits explore the crops, hunting, social life, religion and politics of the Nodena people, and include a human head effigy pot. The Nodena Site was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1964.

## Flight Context

The Nodena Site lies at about 35.554N, 89.952W in Mississippi County, Arkansas, on a meander bend of the Mississippi River roughly 5 miles east of Wilson and northeast of Reverie, Tennessee. From 2,000-3,000 feet the site itself is unlikely to stand out; orient on the river's big horseshoe bend and the town of Wilson to the west. Osceola Municipal Airport (FAA identifier 7M4) is about 9 nm north-northwest, and Millington-Memphis Airport (KNQA) about 12.5 nm south-southeast across the river. Delta haze is common in summer, and river fog can form on cool mornings.
