# Henning, Tennessee

Henning, a West Tennessee town of 871 people, holds Alex Haley's boyhood home and grave, sits near the site of the Fort Pillow massacre, and counts the founder of Halliburton and a string of blues musicians among its own.

**Location:** 35.6736, -89.5775

**Categories:** Towns in Lauderdale County, Tennessee, Towns in Tennessee, Populated places established in 1873, 1873 establishments in Tennessee, Majority-minority cities and towns in Tennessee, Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians

## Introduction

Alex Haley came back to Henning for good in 1992. The author of Roots died of a heart attack in Seattle that February, and he was buried beside his childhood home in this small West Tennessee town, where a historical marker stands out front. Henning counted just 871 residents at the 2020 census and covers only about 2.5 square miles of Lauderdale County. Yet the ground in and around it holds a remarkable weight of American history: one of the Civil War's most notorious atrocities, a lynching, one of America's best-known family sagas, and a list of notable people that runs from Chicago blues to the oil business.

## A Railroad Man's Town

Henning was founded in the late 1800s and named for William H. Henning, a prominent businessman and railway official. It has stayed small. The 2020 census found 871 people. Beneath that quiet sits a less visible hazard. Henning lies on the southeastern edge of the New Madrid Seismic Zone, an area with a high risk of earthquakes. Near town, the state operates the West Tennessee State Penitentiary in unincorporated Lauderdale County, and the Cold Creek Correctional Facility once stood in the area as well. Henning is also home to a community of the Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians. The tribal land there, about 88 acres by one 2020 account, has been described as the first and only federally recognized tribal land in Tennessee.

## Hard Ground at Fort Pillow

On April 12, 1864, Confederate forces under General Nathan Bedford Forrest attacked Fort Pillow, a Union post on the Mississippi River near Henning. The battle was a Confederate victory, but it is remembered for what came after. Of the roughly 600 Union troops present, some 277 to 297 were reported dead, and a congressional investigation concluded that the Confederates shot most of the garrison after it had surrendered. The disparity in who was taken prisoner tells the story: only 58 Black soldiers, around 20 percent, were taken prisoner, while about 60 percent of the white defenders were. Survivors' accounts described a massacre, and a federal investigation later supported them. The Confederate refusal to treat Black troops as ordinary prisoners of war infuriated the North, and the Union insisted on equal treatment for Black prisoners in any exchange. The site is preserved today as Fort Pillow State Historic Park, designated a National Historic Landmark in 1974. Violence against Black residents did not end with the war. In 1900 a mob lynched Anderson Gause, a local Black man. Accounts of the town give his death little space, but his name belongs in any honest account of this place.

## Where Roots Took Root

Henning was Alex Haley's boyhood home. His best-known book, Roots, grew out of genealogical research that traced his maternal ancestry to Jufureh, in The Gambia, and it opens with the story of Kunta Kinte, who was kidnapped in The Gambia in 1767. The book won Haley a special Pulitzer Prize in 1977 and became an acclaimed television miniseries. Haley's life ended far from West Tennessee, in Seattle, but his story ends here. He was buried beside the Henning house where he had spent part of his childhood, and a historical marker stands in front of it.

## From Jug Bands to Oil Fields

Henning's list of notable people is long for a town its size, and wildly varied. Noah Lewis was a jug band performer, and John Henry Barbee was a blues guitarist known for his connection to Chicago's blues scene. Erle P. Halliburton, who founded the Halliburton Company, was born in Henning. Jim Hickman played outfield in the major leagues from 1962 to 1974, and Ramon Foster played offensive line for the Pittsburgh Steelers. Music, oil, baseball and football all trace a line back to this one small town in West Tennessee.

## Flight Context

Henning is at 35.6736°N, 89.5775°W in Lauderdale County, West Tennessee. The town is small, so fly it at 2,000–3,000 feet AGL and look for its compact street grid. Fort Pillow State Historic Park lies to the west, on the Mississippi River. Covington Municipal Airport (FAA M04) is about 5.4 nm south, and Millington-Memphis Airport (KNQA) is roughly 24 nm southwest. Memphis's Frederick W. Smith International Airport (KMEM) is about 43 nm south-southwest, and Jackson Regional (KMKL, formerly McKellar-Sipes Regional) is about 33 nm east. Summer haze and spring thunderstorms are the main visibility concerns in this part of Tennessee.
