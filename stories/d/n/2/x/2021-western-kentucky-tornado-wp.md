# 2021 Western Kentucky tornado

On a December night in 2021, a high-end EF4 tornado touched down near Woodland Mills, Tennessee, and carved a path of about 165 miles across Western Kentucky, killing 57 people and leaving towns like Mayfield and Dawson Springs to rebuild.

**Location:** 36.4830, -89.1350

**Categories:** Tornadoes in Kentucky, Tornadoes of 2021, 2021 natural disasters in the United States, 2021 in Kentucky, F4, EF4 and IF4 tornadoes

## Introduction

A photograph from a destroyed house in Dawson Springs, Kentucky, turned up in New Albany, Indiana, almost 130 miles away. It had been carried aloft by the updrafts of a tornado that stayed on the ground for nearly three hours. On the night of Friday, December 10, 2021, that tornado, sometimes called the Mayfield tornado or simply The Beast, traveled about 165 miles, crossed eleven Kentucky counties, killed 57 people and injured 519. It began on a rural road in Obion County, Tennessee, just south of the Kentucky line.

## A Storm Forecasters Saw Coming

The warnings started days ahead. On December 8 the Storm Prediction Center flagged a slight risk of severe weather across the Mississippi Valley, raised it to an enhanced risk the next day, and on the morning of the 10th added a moderate risk from northeastern Arkansas into southern Illinois, noting that conditions favored nighttime supercells capable of long-tracked, strong tornadoes. One storm that formed near Arkadelphia, Arkansas, matured into exactly that. It would persist for more than 550 miles, producing eleven tornadoes, and later became known as the Quad-State supercell. Radar showed it holding a nearly continuous, high-end rotation for about four hours and twenty minutes, a rarity among supercells. At 8:54 p.m. Central time, along Woodland Mills Road in Obion County, Tennessee, it spawned the tornado that would cross into Kentucky.

## Cayce and the Scoured Fields

The tornado grew violent quickly. In the small community of Cayce, just over the Kentucky line, it struck at low-end EF4 intensity, leveling homes and small businesses and destroying the volunteer fire department building, whose metal beams twisted as heavy machinery was flung about, and a music venue in a former school building was badly damaged. One person died there, and several others were hurt. In Hickman County the tornado appeared to intensify dramatically as it crossed US 51, where it scoured trenches into farm fields, stripping away the grass and several inches of topsoil. From there it tracked into Graves County, paralleling the Purchase Parkway and US 45, headed straight for Mayfield.

## Mayfield's Longest Night

The National Weather Service issued a tornado emergency for Mayfield at 9:26 p.m. as a massive wedge tornado approached from the southwest. Downtown was largely destroyed. Multi-story brick buildings collapsed into piles of brick and lumber, the Graves County Courthouse lost much of its roof and its clock tower, and the town's water tower toppled. The metal-framed Mayfield Consumer Products candle factory collapsed on the workers inside, and eight of them died. Some employees later alleged supervisors had warned that leaving early could cost them their jobs; the company denied it, and lawsuits followed on behalf of workers, including three who were killed. Twenty-two people were killed in and around Mayfield. Some National Weather Service analysts considered the damage there to border on EF5 intensity, and a 2025 research paper called it an "EF5 candidate."

## One Hundred Sixty-Five Miles

The tornado did not stop. Just southeast of Princeton it struck a Kentucky Mesonet weather station and brought down the top of its tower, but a low-level anemometer that survived recorded a 120.1 mph gust, which a federal climate-extremes committee accepted as the highest wind gust measured in Kentucky. Four people died in Princeton. Fourteen died in the Dawson Springs area, where a mother and her two children survived, with major injuries, by clinging to a mattress as they were thrown 250 feet into a field. Eleven more were killed in and around Bremen, among them District Judge Brian Crick. At its widest the tornado spanned 2,600 yards, and it finally lifted near Rough River Dam State Resort Park. Its toll made it the deadliest December tornado ever recorded in the United States and the deadliest American tornado since Joplin, Missouri, in 2011.

## Clearing the Wreckage

President Joe Biden toured Mayfield and Dawson Springs on December 15, calling the damage "almost beyond belief." The Weather Service office in Paducah asked for mental-health officers to accompany meteorologists surveying the path, and the Mayfield Messenger printed 2,000 extra copies per run to hand out free. By April 2022 the Army Corps of Engineers had removed 433,408 cubic yards of debris from Graves County, and crews with the Tennessee Valley Authority and Living Lands and Waters hauled 1.2 million pounds of tornado debris, including docks, cars and kayaks, out of Kentucky Lake. A state relief fund drew about 150,000 donations totaling $52 million. In December 2022 a temporary memorial went up on Mayfield's court square, and in 2024 the city won $31.5 million to rebuild its downtown streets.

## Flight Context

This story's coordinates, about 36.48°N, 89.14°W, mark the tornado's starting point near Woodland Mills in Obion County, Tennessee, just south of the Kentucky line and east of the Mississippi River and Reelfoot Lake. The damage path runs northeast for about 165 miles through Cayce, Mayfield, Princeton, Dawson Springs and Bremen to Rough River Dam State Resort Park; from 5,000 to 7,000 feet, follow the Purchase Parkway and US 45 northeast to Mayfield, where the courthouse square marks the center of town. Nearby airports include Everett-Stewart Regional Airport (KUCY) at Union City, Tennessee, Mayfield Graves County Airport (FAA identifier M25), and Barkley Regional Airport (KPAH) at Paducah. Winter fronts here bring low ceilings and fast-moving storms, and as this night showed, severe weather is not confined to spring.
