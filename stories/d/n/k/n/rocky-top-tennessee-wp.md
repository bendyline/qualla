# Rocky Top, Tennessee

Coal Creek, then Lake City, then Rocky Top: a small East Tennessee town whose three names trace a miners' rebellion, a New Deal lake and a fight over a famous song.

**Location:** 36.2242, -84.1558

**Categories:** Cities in Tennessee, Cities in Anderson County, Tennessee, Coal towns in Tennessee, Knoxville metropolitan area, Music

## Introduction

The song came first. In 1967, in Room 388 of The Gatlinburg Inn, Boudleaux and Felice Bryant wrote "Rocky Top," a tune that became so tied to the University of Tennessee that many fans treat it as the school's fight song, although the official one is "Down the Field." There is a real Rocky Top, a peak on the North Carolina border in Great Smoky Mountains National Park about 14 miles from Gatlinburg, but no record shows the couple knew of it when they wrote. Nearly half a century later, a small city northwest of Knoxville took the song's name for itself. On June 26, 2014, after a federal judge turned away a last-minute legal challenge from the song's copyright owners, Lake City, Tennessee, officially became Rocky Top. It was the town's third name.

## Coal Creek and Its War

The first name came from the land. The town was founded in the early 19th century, after the 1798 Treaty of Tellico opened the area to settlement, and it took the name of the stream that runs through it: Coal Creek. Most of the community lies in northern Anderson County, with a small portion in Campbell County. Coal Creek was the birthplace, in 1879, of Catherine Wiley, an artist of the early 20th century. In 1891 the town and nearby Briceville became the scene of a major lockout of coal miners. The conflict centered on convict leasing: mine owners were replacing paid miners with convicts leased from the state, and the miners tried to force an end to the practice. The state answered with its militia, which was stationed in Coal Creek through much of 1891 and 1892. The struggle, remembered as the Coal Creek War, ended in 1892 with hundreds of miners arrested, but the outcry it caused helped push Tennessee to end convict leasing in 1896. The town's mining story did not end there. In 1902 the Fraterville Mine disaster struck nearby, in the village of Fraterville.

## Lake City

The second name came from the Tennessee Valley Authority. When TVA completed nearby Norris Dam in 1936 and its new reservoir, Norris Lake, took shape, the town adopted the name Lake City. Not everyone embraced the change. Over the years some residents, including the nonprofit Coal Creek Watershed Foundation, urged a return to Coal Creek as a way of honoring the community's heritage. Norris Dam State Park remains a close neighbor, and the city sits at the northern terminus of U.S. Route 441, the road that crosses the top of the dam.

## A Name Worth a Lawsuit

The third name came from a business pitch. In 2013 a business group proposed to build and operate a water park in the city if it would rename itself Rocky Top, hoping to capitalize on the song. On November 7, 2013, the city council voted to ask the Tennessee General Assembly to amend the city charter. The House of Bryant, which holds the song's copyright and related trademarks, sued in federal court, alleging trademark infringement, false advertising and unfair competition. There, District Judge Thomas Varlan denied the company's last-minute attempt to block the council's final vote, and on June 26, 2014, the new name took effect. The water park, a $100 million plan, was still awaiting state approval of the land use in 2018; a 2023 news report says its developer never secured financing and the plans were scrapped.

## Rocky Top Today

The renamed city remains small. It counted 1,628 residents in the 2020 census, and since 2014 it has seen minimal growth, mostly in the form of shops and restaurants near the Interstate 75 exits within city limits. The interstate leads 24 miles south to Knoxville and 145 miles north to Lexington, Kentucky, and U.S. Route 25W passes through as well. The town has its own musical claim to fame beyond the borrowed name. It is the hometown of songwriter Dean Dillon, whose songs became hits for George Strait, Toby Keith, Keith Whitley, George Jones and Kenny Chesney.

## Flight Context

Rocky Top lies at about 36.224°N, 84.156°W, northwest of Knoxville in northern Anderson County, Tennessee. Interstate 75 runs through the town, and U.S. 441 leads southeast to Norris Dam, only about 3-4 miles away, with the long arms of Norris Lake beyond. A viewing altitude of 3,000-4,000 feet MSL shows the town, the interstate and the dam together. Nearest airports: Colonel Tommy C. Stiner Airfield in Jacksboro (KJAU), about 7 nm north; Knoxville Downtown Island Airport (KDKX), about 21 nm southeast; and McGhee Tyson Airport (KTYS), about 26 nm south-southeast. Morning valley fog is common.
