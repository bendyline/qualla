# Dollywood

A small Civil War-themed train ride opened in Pigeon Forge in 1961 went through four names and several owners before Dolly Parton put hers on it, turning it into one of Tennessee's biggest ticketed tourist attractions.

**Location:** 35.7951, -83.5312

**Categories:** Amusement parks in Tennessee, Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, Dolly Parton, Music of East Tennessee, Tourist attractions in Sevier County, Tennessee, Amusement parks opened in 1961

## Introduction

Rebel Railroad. Goldrush Junction. Goldrush. Silver Dollar City Tennessee. This patch of Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, went through four names in twenty-five years before a country singer-songwriter lent it hers. Since 1986 it has been Dollywood, jointly owned by Herschend and Dolly Parton through her company, Dolly Parton Productions, and it has grown into one of the biggest ticketed tourist attractions in Tennessee, drawing close to 4 million guests in 2025. Alongside its roller coasters, the park has long traded on something older: a steam train, traditional crafts, food and the music of the Smoky Mountains that rise just beyond it.

## A Steam Train Under Siege

The story begins in 1961, when the Robbins brothers of Blowing Rock, North Carolina, opened a small tourist attraction called Rebel Railroad. It was modeled on their first successful park, Tweetsie Railroad, and offered a steam train, a general store, a blacksmith shop and a saloon. Its theme came from the Civil War centennial, and the entertainment was a staged fantasy very much of its era: as the train rolled, riders faced mock "attacks" by Union soldiers, train robbers and Native Americans, all fought off by Confederates guarding the cars. Accounts differ on the date, from 1964 to 1970, but the park became Goldrush Junction, trading the war for a wild west theme like its sister park's. In 1970 it changed hands, bought by Art Modell, who also owned the Cleveland Browns football team. He kept the railroad and added an outdoor theater and the Robert F. Thomas Chapel.

## Enter Dolly

By the mid-1970s the name had been shortened to Goldrush, and in 1976 (some accounts say 1977) Jack and Pete Herschend, owners of Silver Dollar City in Branson, Missouri, bought the park. They renamed it Silver Dollar City Tennessee and spent about $1 million upgrading it. The decisive change came on May 3, 1986, when it reopened as Dollywood. A new Rivertown Junction area brought a whitewater rafting ride, the Back Porch Theater, Aunt Granny's Dixie Fixins' Restaurant and Dolly's Tennessee Mountain Home, a replica of the cabin that was Parton's childhood home. A museum, "Rags to Riches: The Dolly Parton Story," displayed articles and mementos from her life and career. The formula worked at once. In its first season as Dollywood, attendance doubled to more than a million guests.

## Crafts, Gospel and Banana Pudding

Dollywood is organized into eleven themed areas, most of them reflecting the historical eras and culture of East Tennessee, and many attractions focus on Southern Appalachian history. In Craftsman's Valley, a grist mill, the Calico Falls Schoolhouse and the Robert F. Thomas Chapel sit among craft exhibits and the Eagle Mountain Sanctuary. Music runs through all of it. The 1,739-seat Celebrity Theater went up beside the entrance in 1988, the Southern Gospel Museum and Hall of Fame arrived in 1999, and every year the park hosts concerts that include appearances by Parton and her family along with national and local acts. The food carries the same accent. More than 25 vendors have served everything from barbecue ribs and cinnamon bread to banana pudding made with Dolly's recipe, while Aunt Granny's, open since the first Dollywood season, lays out chicken and dumplings buffet-style.

## Wood, Steel and a Tree Full of Butterflies

The thrill rides kept pace. The Tennessee Tornado, a steel looping coaster, opened in 1999, and Wild Eagle arrived as America's first winged coaster. On June 13, 2016, Lightning Rod opened, billed as the world's first launched wooden coaster and, at 73 mph, the fastest wooden coaster in the world; it has since been reworked, with part of its track converted to steel and its launch replaced by a chain lift when it reopened in March 2024. Then, on May 10, 2019, came Wildwood Grove, a $37 million, five-acre area described as "a land built from Dolly Parton's dreams," with a suspended family coaster and a 50-foot tree whose canopy is covered in butterflies. It was part of a $300 million, six-year expansion. Big Bear Mountain followed on May 12, 2023, the longest coaster in the park's history. Through all the new steel, the narrow-gauge Dollywood Express steam train keeps running, a reminder that this park started with a locomotive.

## Five Million Lights

Dollywood's season stretches from mid-March to the Christmas holidays, stitched together by festivals, and the grandest finale is Smoky Mountain Christmas, with 5 million lights, holiday shows and a nightly parade. By 2021 it had reportedly won the Golden Ticket Award for Best Christmas Event for 13 straight years. The wider industry has taken notice as well. In 2010, the Liseberg amusement park in Gothenburg, Sweden, gave Dollywood its biennial Applause Award, which honors a park "whose management, operations, and creative accomplishments have inspired the industry with their foresight, originality, and sound business development." Parton accepted it herself at the IAAPA Attractions Expo in Orlando. In June 2022, Tripadvisor named Dollywood the number one theme park in the United States - not bad for a place that began as a small train ride with a general store and a saloon.

## Flight Context

Dollywood sits at about 35.80°N, 83.53°W in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, in the foothills at the gateway to Great Smoky Mountains National Park. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, look for the dense tourist strip of Pigeon Forge and the park's coaster tracks and large parking complex, with the main Smokies ridges rising to the south. Gatlinburg-Pigeon Forge Airport (KGKT) at Sevierville is only about 4 nm away. Knoxville Downtown Island Airport (KDKX, about 20 nm), McGhee Tyson Airport (KTYS, about 23 nm) and Morristown Regional Airport (KMOR, about 24 nm) are also nearby. Terrain climbs quickly toward the national park, so keep clear of the high ridges and watch for valley haze and low cloud over the mountains.
