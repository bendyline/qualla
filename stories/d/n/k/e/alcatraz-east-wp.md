# Alcatraz East

A crime museum built to look like a 19th-century prison, which moved from Washington, D.C. to the resort town of Pigeon Forge and now displays evidence from some of America's best-known criminal cases.

**Location:** 35.8016, -83.5715

**Categories:** Museums in Sevier County, Tennessee, 2016 establishments in Tennessee, Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, Law enforcement museums in the United States, History museums in Tennessee, Museums established in 2016

## Introduction

Pigeon Forge is a town of theme parks, dinner shows and outlet malls, and in the middle of it stands a prison. It is not a real one. Alcatraz East is a two-story building of roughly 25,000 square feet dressed up as a 19th-century penitentiary, its look inspired by the famous island prison of Alcatraz in San Francisco. Inside is a privately owned, for-profit museum devoted to the history of crime in America: how crimes were committed, how cases were solved, and what happened to the people caught up in them, on both sides of the law.

## A Move South

The museum did not start in the Smokies. It opened in May 2008 in Washington, D.C., as the National Museum of Crime and Punishment, built by Orlando attorney and businessman John Morgan in partnership with John Walsh, the host of America's Most Wanted, with Janine Vaccarello as its chief operating officer. After its Washington lease forced it out in September 2015, it reopened in 2016 at its present site in Pigeon Forge under a new name and with a prison-block facade. The shift put it among the tourist attractions of a mountain resort town rather than the monuments of the capital, and the aim is still a behind-the-scenes look at crime history, from the criminal's intent to the jury's verdict.

## Twenty Rooms, Five Themes

Visitors move through 20 exhibit areas organized around five themes: the history of American crime, the consequences of crime, crime scene investigation, crime fighting, and pop culture. One historical area walks through how crimes were carried out, how investigators solved them and how juries reached agreement on final sentences, with anecdotes about the backgrounds and behavior of infamous prisoners along the way. Just as importantly, the museum sets aside space for victims' stories, a reminder that every case file started with someone who was harmed. Much of the building is meant to be touched as well as looked at. Visitors can work through a CSI lab, try their hand at safe-cracking, step into a simulated shooting range and take their own digital fingerprints, while an interactive DUI safety training exhibit turns the subject toward prevention.

## Evidence Under Glass

What draws many visitors are the objects themselves, authentic pieces once used as evidence in well-known cases. John Dillinger's Essex-Terraplane is here, and so is Al Capone's rosary. So is the Volkswagen Beetle that belonged to Ted Bundy, and the Ford Bronco from the O. J. Simpson murder case. Other pieces carry a different weight: an FBI polygraph machine, and items related to the 2012 Benghazi attack. Pop culture gets its corner too, with the sub-machine gun Al Pacino carried in the movie Scarface. The collection also reaches into the present with displays of the latest law enforcement technology. Seen together, the artifacts span old gangland legends and modern tragedies, and several are tied to cases in which real people lost their lives. It makes for an unusual stop in a vacation town: part history lesson, part forensic puzzle and part sober look at what crime costs.

## Flight Context

Alcatraz East is in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, at 35.8016°N, 83.5715°W. Pigeon Forge lies about 5 miles north of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, strung along US-441 and the Little Pigeon River, with the forested ridges of the Smokies rising to the south. The museum's prison-style building is one of many attractions lining the town's main corridor. A viewing altitude of about 3,000-3,500 feet MSL keeps you above the valley floor with room to pick out the attractions; stay well clear of the higher terrain to the south. Gatlinburg-Pigeon Forge Airport (KGKT) is only about 4 nm north-northeast in Sevierville, and McGhee Tyson Airport (KTYS) in Knoxville/Maryville is about 21 nm west. Haze and low cloud are common along the edge of the Smokies, and mountain winds can make the approach from the south bumpy.
