# Sunsphere

The gold glass ball that crowned Knoxville's 1982 World's Fair has outlasted a gunshot, failed restaurant schemes, a Simpsons joke and a pandemic closure to become the city's enduring symbol.

## Introduction

"What better place for basketball offices," team officials said in 1991, "than a giant gold basketball in the sky." The Pensacola Tornados of the Continental Basketball Association were scouting Knoxville at the time, and the giant ball they had in mind was the Sunsphere: a 75-foot sphere of gold-colored glass perched atop a hexagonal steel truss, 266 feet tall in all, in World's Fair Park. It had been built as the symbol of the 1982 World's Fair. Nine years later, Knoxville was still trying to decide what to do with it.

## A Sun Scaled Down

The Knoxville firm Community Tectonics designed the tower, and engineering publications took note of its unusual form. The symbolism was built into the numbers. The sphere was originally meant to be 86.5 feet across, a scale reference to the Sun's disc, roughly 865,000 miles in diameter. The finished ball came in smaller, at 75 feet, but the idea held: a sun raised over the fairgrounds. Not everyone treated it kindly. In the early morning hours of May 12, 1982, a shot fired from outside the fair site shattered one of the sphere's windows. No one was arrested. Today the Sunsphere and the Tennessee Amphitheater, which faces it across a man-made pond, are the only structures that remain from the fair.

## Years of Big Ideas

When the fair ended, the question became what a golden ball in the sky is for. The basketball offices never happened. In March 1994 the World's Fair Park Development Committee tried to reopen the Sunsphere as a restaurant along the lines of Seattle's Space Needle. One company proposed a casual spot called the World's Fare Restaurant, another a fine-dining room, and both proposals failed. The tower was also floated as part of the new Knoxville Convention Center. It was never physically joined to the building, but the convention center's north edge was designed with an open curve to allow access to it. During construction the city closed the observation deck, which had briefly reopened with its original World's Fair-era displays still in place, and the Knoxville Public Building Authority moved in, using the tower as offices for, quite literally, overseeing the work.

## Lounges, Views and a Long Pause

The fourth-level observation deck reopened on July 5, 2007, with room for 86 people. The fifth floor became a cafe with concessions and early evening drinks, and on August 27, 2008, it opened as the SkyBox bar and lounge. When that closed, real estate investor Tony Capiello put $450,000 into Icon Ultra Lounge in its place. By October 2013 the seventh and eighth floors were available for commercial rent, and Visit Knoxville announced a renovation of the observation deck. The COVID-19 pandemic shut the deck in 2020, and Visit Knoxville reopened it on February 22, 2022. From up there the 360-degree view runs from downtown to the Great Smoky Mountains, taking in World's Fair Park, the Tennessee River and the University of Tennessee campus, while a 1982 World's Fair timeline, gallery and memorabilia fill in the backstory.

## The Wigsphere and the Lord's Golf Tee

Not many landmarks can say The Simpsons knocked them over. In the March 1996 episode "Bart on the Road", Bart, Nelson, Martin and Milhouse drive to Knoxville for the World's Fair, only to discover they are more than a decade late. The Sunsphere has become a dilapidated storage warehouse for a wig store called the Wigsphere, and when Nelson throws a rock at it, the whole thing topples onto their rental car, stranding them in town. Off screen, the tower stands firm as a symbol of Knoxville, turning up on postcards and logos: from 1993 to 1999 it appeared in the logo of the Knoxville Smokies minor league baseball club, and the mascot of the 2002 AAU Junior Olympics, Spherit, had red hair and a Sunsphere-shaped body. It has also been called, affectionately or otherwise, "The Lord's Golf Tee."

## Flight Context

The Sunsphere stands at 35.9617°N, 83.9233°W in World's Fair Park in downtown Knoxville, Tennessee. Its 266-foot-tall tower, topped by the gold glass ball, catches the light and is easy to pick out against the city, with the Tennessee River and the University of Tennessee campus close by. A viewing altitude of about 2,500-3,000 feet MSL gives a good look while staying clear of the downtown buildings. Knoxville Downtown Island Airport (KDKX) is only about 2.4 nm east, and McGhee Tyson Airport (KTYS), the region's airline airport, is about 10 nm south-southwest, so expect nearby traffic and check the sectional for its airspace before maneuvering over the city. On clear days the Great Smoky Mountains are visible on the southeastern horizon; summer haze can shorten that view considerably.
