# Forbidden Caverns

A cave on the flank of English Mountain near Sevierville where Eastern Woodland toolmakers once gathered flint and moonshiners later made liquor, open to visitors since 1967.

**Location:** 35.9037, -83.3461

**Categories:** Landforms of Sevier County, Tennessee, Tourist attractions in Sevier County, Tennessee, Caves of Tennessee

## Introduction

The name promises secrets, and the cave has kept a few. In the early twentieth century, the caverns on the northwestern face of English Mountain, near Sevierville, Tennessee, were used to make moonshine. Underground and out of sight, the place suited a trade that preferred not to be found. Yet the moonshiners were late arrivals. Long before them, the cave was a source of stone.

## Flint Before Firewater

Native peoples of the Eastern Woodlands used these caverns long before. Accounts of the cave say they took flint from it and shaped it into arrowheads, knives and scrapers, and that the cave also gave them shelter. Their use gives the cave a working history far older than any tourist trail, one measured in tools rather than tickets. The distillers who followed in the twentieth century used the same darkness for a very different craft, turning the cave from a place people took things out of into a place where they hid what they were making. Neither group left the cave as a showpiece; for them it was simply useful ground beneath the mountain.

## Opening the Dark

By the 1960s a new idea had taken hold: that the cave itself was worth seeing. Plans began to excavate it as a natural attraction for the public, and after three years of planning and development, Forbidden Caverns opened in 1967. The experience today is a guided one. The trails are well lit, with handrails where visitors need them, and tour guides lead the way while special lighting effects pick out the formations. It is a long way from a moonshiner's lantern.

## Fifty-Eight Degrees Below

The cave descends several hundred feet below the surface, into a world of grottos, natural chimneys and an underground river. That clear stream is fed by a large underground lake beneath English Mountain, the 3,629-foot ridge in the foothills of the Great Smokies that the caverns are tucked into. One of the cave's showpieces is a large wall of rare cave onyx. Down here, the seasons stop mattering. The temperature holds near a constant 58 degrees all year, cool in July and mild in January.

## Flight Context

Forbidden Caverns is at 35.9037°N, 83.3461°W on the northwestern side of English Mountain, east of Sevierville, Tennessee. The cave itself is invisible from the air, so navigate by English Mountain, a 3,629-foot ridge rising about 2.5 nm to the east of the site in the northeastern foothills of the Great Smoky Mountains. A viewing altitude of 5,000-5,500 feet MSL gives comfortable clearance over the ridge. Gatlinburg-Pigeon Forge Airport (KGKT) in Sevierville is about 9 nm west-southwest, Moore-Murrell Airport (KMOR) at Morristown about 17 nm north, and McGhee Tyson Airport (KTYS) about 32 nm west. Expect haze and low cloud around the Smokies foothills, and watch for mechanical turbulence downwind of the ridge on windy days.
