# Mount Le Conte (Tennessee)

Rising more than 5,000 feet from its base near Gatlinburg, Mount Le Conte carries the highest lodge in the eastern United States, reachable only on foot and supplied by helicopter and llama.

**Location:** 35.6542, -83.4367

**Categories:** Mountains of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, Mountains of Tennessee, Southern Sixers, Mountain huts in the United States, Protected areas of Sevier County, Tennessee

## Introduction

At the highest inn in the eastern United States, the supplies come in by helicopter or on the backs of llamas. There is no road to LeConte Lodge. Every guest arrives on foot, on one of five trails that climb Mount Le Conte in the heart of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, and the llama trains make the trip along the Trillium Gap Trail. The effort matches the mountain. Le Conte tops out at 6,593 feet, the third-highest peak in the park after Kuwohi and Mount Guyot and the highest peak that lies entirely within Tennessee. Measured from its immediate base near Gatlinburg to its summit, it rises 5,301 feet, often cited as the greatest relief of any mountain east of the Rocky Mountains.

## A Massif, Not a Peak

Le Conte is less a single summit than a long ridge, a massif running east to west with four peaks above 6,000 feet: West Point, High Top, Cliff Tops and Myrtle Point, with Balsam Point forming the massif's dramatic western end. High Top is the true summit. Cliff Tops and Myrtle Point both open onto nearly 360-degree views, while the other peaks are completely forested. For a sense of scale, the rise from Gatlinburg's elevation of 1,292 feet to High Top outdoes pre-1980 Mount St. Helens in Washington, which stood roughly 5,000 feet above its base. The mountain sits about seven miles north-northeast of Kuwohi and three miles from the North Carolina line. It is built of Late Proterozoic rock, mostly metamorphosed sandstone, siltstone, shale and conglomerate, worn over millions of years into the gentle profile typical of the region. A dense stand of Southern Appalachian spruce-fir forest, a remnant of the Last Glacial Period, cloaks the peaks and upper slopes.

## Which Le Conte?

Even the name is contested. The United States Geological Survey lists the geologist Joseph Le Conte as the mountain's namesake, supposedly honored by the Swiss explorer Arnold Guyot. In recent years local authorities, including the authors of A Natural History of Mount Le Conte, have challenged that account. They believe the mountain was named for Joseph's older brother, John Le Conte, a physicist at the University of South Carolina. Their version holds that Samuel Buckley chose the name in thanks for John's help in moving Buckley's barometer to Waynesville, North Carolina. Both brothers remain in contention, and the mountain keeps its secret.

## A Night in a Tent

The mountain was measured in the 1850s, but little happened on it until the 1920s, when Paul Adams moved to Knoxville. An enthusiastic hiker and explorer, he spent much of his free time on adventures in the mountains, and in 1924 he joined the Great Smoky Mountain Conservation Association, a group working to turn the region into a national park. Soon afterward he led an expedition up Le Conte with dignitaries from Washington, D.C., to show them the rugged beauty the Smokies held. The party spent the night in a large tent, and on that site LeConte Lodge was eventually built. The trip was a great success. About a decade later, Mount Le Conte and the surrounding region were protected as part of Great Smoky Mountains National Park.

## Cabins Above the Clouds

LeConte Lodge dates its beginnings to a tent camp that Paul Adams put up in 1925, and Jack Huff began building its cabins in 1926. It grew into a cluster of small log cabins with a central lodge and dining hall near the summit, run under a lease with the National Park Service. It holds a few dozen guests and is generally open from late March to late November. Water comes from a spring on top of the mountain. The weather up here resembles southern Canada's, with cool summers and cold, snowy winters. Snowfall averages about 40 inches a year near the base and about 80 inches on the peaks, and the highest temperature on record there is a modest 82°F, as of 2023, reached on July 1, 2012, and again on August 7, 2023.

## Five Ways Up

Each trail to the top has its own character. The Alum Cave Trail, about 5 miles one way, is the shortest route and among the most scenic and most used, passing Arch Rock and Alum Cave Bluff with views into Huggins Hell. The Boulevard leaves the Appalachian Trail east of Newfound Gap and never dips below 5,500 feet. The Bullhead Trail, about 6.8 miles, climbs a heath-covered bald with steady views, and the Park Service has been closing it Monday through Thursday for rehabilitation. The Rainbow Falls Trail follows LeConte Creek to Rainbow Falls, widely described as the park's highest single drop of water, while the roughly 6.7-mile Trillium Gap Trail passes behind Grotto Falls, often called the only waterfall in the park you can walk behind. The Park Service warns of extreme crowding and limited parking at the trailheads, and together the five routes make Le Conte one of the park's most heavily traversed mountains. The area was affected by the 2016 Great Smoky Mountains wildfires, which led to closures and evacuations in and around the park.

## Flight Context

Mount Le Conte's summit, High Top, is at about 35.6542°N, 83.4367°W, 6,593 feet, in the center of Great Smoky Mountains National Park in Sevier County, Tennessee, rising steeply above Gatlinburg to the north. Look for the long east-west massif with its open, rocky viewpoints at Cliff Tops and Myrtle Point; Kuwohi lies about 7 miles to the south-southwest and Newfound Gap along the main crest to the south. Because of the mountain's great relief and nearby 6,000-foot-plus terrain, a viewing altitude of 8,500-9,500 feet MSL is prudent. Nearest airports: Gatlinburg-Pigeon Forge Airport (KGKT), about 13 nm north-northwest; McGhee Tyson Airport in Knoxville/Maryville (KTYS), about 29 nm west-northwest; and Asheville Regional Airport (KAVL), about 46 nm east-southeast. Expect mountain weather, with the upper slopes often in cloud and conditions much cooler than in the valleys.
