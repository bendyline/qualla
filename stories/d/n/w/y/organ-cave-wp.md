# Organ Cave

Beneath Greenbrier County, West Virginia, Organ Cave holds Ice Age bones, what is billed as the nation's largest collection of Civil War-era saltpeter hoppers, and more than 38 miles of surveyed passage.

**Location:** 37.7181, -80.4369

**Categories:** Caves of West Virginia, Show caves in the United States, National Natural Landmarks in West Virginia, National Register of Historic Places in Greenbrier County, West Virginia, Landforms of Greenbrier County, West Virginia

## Introduction

A giant ground sloth, a grizzly bear, a sabre-tooth cat, a reindeer, a nine-banded armadillo and an Ice Age porcupine: the fossils found in Organ Cave read like the roster of a vanished world. This large and historic cave lies beneath Greenbrier County, West Virginia, so central to its surroundings that the community above takes its name from it. Its passages hold not only deep natural history but a human record stretching from Native American arrowheads to the gunpowder works of the Civil War.

## The Sloth That Got Away

Organ Cave's most famous fossil may not have come from Organ Cave at all. For a long time, Thomas Jefferson's giant ground sloth, Megalonyx jeffersonii, was assumed to have been found here. In 1995, Smithsonian paleontologist Frederick Grady proposed that the true source was Haynes Cave in nearby Monroe County, basing his case on historical land-ownership records. The old story proved hard to shake. In 2008, the governor of West Virginia held a ceremony at Organ Cave to present its owner with a certificate naming M. jeffersonii the official state fossil, and repeated the claim that it had been found there, though without producing any new evidence.

## Arrowheads and a Doubtful Date

People came underground here long before the Europeans. Flint arrowheads left by early Native Americans attest to the cave's human history. Later visitors left marks of their own, including the date "1704" scratched into a wall. That inscription is probably not what it seems. The first white settlers reached Greenbrier County only in the 1740s, so the date may well be a later addition.

## Gunpowder From the Dirt

The cave's floor turned out to be valuable. Its dirt is high in calcium nitrate, and since before 1835 miners dug it as a source of nitre, or saltpeter, for making gunpowder. In hoppers, they turned the calcium nitrate into potassium nitrate. During the American Civil War, Confederate forces returned to mine the cave for nitre again. Their equipment remains: Organ Cave is widely said to hold the nation's largest collection of Civil War-era saltpeter hoppers, and the National Natural Landmark listing singles out its saltpeter troughs and vats.

## Miles Beneath the Plateau

Members of the National Speleological Society first surveyed Organ Cave in July 1948, and the mapping has continued ever since. In 1973 the Organ Cave System, also called the Organ-Hedricks Cave System, was registered as a National Natural Landmark as "the largest cave system in the State, containing many caves, one of which is Organ Cave." Explorers have since found longer systems. As of March 2022 the Organ Cave System had 38.452 miles of surveyed passage. Newer surveys have pushed other West Virginia systems past it: the Great Savannah Cave System, reported in January 2024 as the state's longest at 53.47 miles, the Friars Hole Cave System, and the Hellhole System in Pendleton County all outrank it, and Wikipedia's current list of the longest U.S. caves puts Organ Cave 18th. Organ Cave has also been listed on the National Register of Historic Places since 2005.

## Flight Context

Organ Cave is at 37.7181 N, 80.4369 W in Greenbrier County, West Virginia, on the limestone plateau south of the Lewisburg area. The cave itself is underground, so from 3,000-4,000 ft AGL use the small community of Organ Cave, the farmland of the Greenbrier Valley and the surrounding forested ridges as references. Greenbrier Valley Airport near Lewisburg (KLWB) is about 9 nm north-northeast and is the natural base for a visit; Virginia Tech Montgomery Executive Airport in Blacksburg (KBCB) is about 31 nm south. Mountain terrain surrounds the valley, and fog can linger in low areas on cool mornings.
