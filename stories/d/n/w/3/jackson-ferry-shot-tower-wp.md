# Jackson Ferry Shot Tower

Above the New River in Wythe County, Virginia, a limestone tower completed in 1807 turned molten lead into ammunition by dropping it 150 feet, half through the tower and half down a shaft cut into the cliff.

**Location:** 36.8700, -80.8706

**Categories:** Shot towers, Towers completed in 1807, National Register of Historic Places in Wythe County, Virginia, Tourist attractions in Wythe County, Virginia, Industrial buildings completed in 1807

## Introduction

At the top of a stone tower above the New River, molten lead was poured through a sieve, and 150 feet later it was ammunition. That was the whole trick of the Jackson Ferry Shot Tower in Wythe County, Virginia: let gravity and air shape falling metal into spheres. Completed in 1807 by Thomas Jackson, the 75-foot limestone tower is one of the few shot towers left in the United States, and it achieves its full 150-foot fall with a clever piece of engineering hidden underground.

## Rain Made of Lead

Shot towers existed for one purpose, making lead shot for firearms. Lead from the nearby Austinville mines went into a kettle at the top of the tower, where a firewood furnace melted it together with arsenic. Workers poured the molten metal through a sieve, and it broke into drops matching the size of the holes. As the droplets fell 150 feet, they pulled into spheres and cooled enough to become rigid. At the bottom waited a large kettle of water, which finished the cooling and gave the shot a soft enough landing to keep it from deforming. The finished shot was sold to hunters, traders and merchants.

## Half Tower, Half Shaft

What sets this tower apart is how it was built. Most shot towers were brick, but this one is limestone, with solid stone walls two and a half feet thick. Those walls made the structure stronger and kept the interior temperature cooler and more consistent, which improved the quality of the shot. The builders also borrowed height from the land itself. Rather than raise a tower 150 feet tall, they set a 75-foot tower on the edge of a cliff and dug a 75-foot vertical shaft beneath it, doubling the drop. To reach the bottom of that shaft, workers used a horizontal tunnel, an adit, that opened near the shore of the New River.

## A Question of Who Came First

Some have claimed the tower is older than 1807, and that Moses Austin and his brother Stephen Austin, not to be confused with Moses's son Stephen F. Austin, the "Father of Texas," were already making drop shot here before 1800. Supporters point to newspapers of the era. Those papers, however, identify shot factories in Lynchburg and Richmond, both on the James River in the Chesapeake Bay watershed, not on the New River, whose waters flow toward the Ohio. Letters from the time explicitly name Richmond as the factories' location. The mix-up likely stems from the fact that Moses Austin & Co. owned the Austinville lead mines, near where the Jackson Ferry shot factory would later rise.

## Climbing the Tower Today

The tower was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on October 1, 1969, and it is the centerpiece of Shot Tower Historical State Park. It stands beside New River Trail State Park, a long rail-trail park linking the historic towns of Pulaski and Galax. Virginia State Parks says visitors may climb the tower on scheduled days in summer, with free admission to the tower (the daily parking fee applies), and the grounds are open from dawn until dusk. Tours can be unavailable, though: the park asks visitors to call about availability, and the New River Trail State Park page says tours are unavailable due to renovations, so check before you go.

## Flight Context

The Jackson Ferry Shot Tower stands at 36.87 N, 80.8706 W on a bluff above the New River in Wythe County, southwestern Virginia. From 2,500-3,500 ft AGL, follow the New River as it winds through rolling, forested hills; the New River Trail runs along the water, and the small stone tower sits at the cliff edge above it. Nearby airports: Twin County Airport (KHLX) near Hillsville, about 12 km south-southeast; New River Valley Airport (KPSK) near Dublin, about 34 km north-northeast; and Mountain Empire Airport (KMKJ), serving Marion and Wytheville, about 43 km west. Hills rise well above the river, and valley fog is common on cool mornings.
