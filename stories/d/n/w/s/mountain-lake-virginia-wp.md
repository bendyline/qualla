# Mountain Lake (Virginia)

High on a Giles County mountain, one of Virginia's only two natural lakes drains and refills on its own mysterious cycle, beside a stone lodge that became Kellerman's in Dirty Dancing.

**Location:** 37.3600, -80.5339

**Categories:** Lakes of Virginia, Bodies of water of Giles County, Virginia, Tourist attractions in Giles County, Virginia, Protected areas of Giles County, Virginia, Film locations

## Introduction

From 2008 to 2020, one of Virginia's only two natural lakes was little more than a reddish-brown pit. Mountain Lake, which usually covers about 50 acres at 3,875 feet in Giles County, had drained through its own floor and shrunk to little more than a pond. Then, in the wet spring of 2020, resort employees noticed the water coming back. By July 14 the lake was about a third full. Researchers who studied it during the dry years had reached a remarkable conclusion: the lake rises and falls on a natural cycle, and it may bottom out roughly every 400 years.

## A Lake With a Drain

Natural lakes are common far to the north, where glaciers shaped the Appalachians in geologically recent times. Mountain Lake is the only natural one in the southern Appalachians, and why it exists at all has long been debated. Recent studies point to an unusual combination of processes. A fissure in the lake bottom acts as an outlet for both water and sediment, which keeps the basin from filling in and becoming a bog. Refilling depends on rainfall, and during dry periods accumulated sediment is believed to move through a natural plumbing system of openings in the bedrock. The first known written record of the lake came from Christopher Gist of the Ohio Land Surveying Company in 1751. Its old name, Salt Pond, recalls a humbler use: it was where cattle were given salt. The long dry spell also revealed something more somber: human remains in the lakebed that, according to Wikipedia citing a 2010 Roanoke Times report, were those of Samuel Ira Felder, said to have drowned in July 1921 after falling overboard while canoeing with friends.

## Stagecoaches to Salt Pond

Visitors came to Salt Pond through the early nineteenth century and before, and in March 1856 Henley Chapman incorporated the Mountain Lake Company to house and entertain them. A wooden hotel and a sawmill soon followed. Stagecoach travelers from Christiansburg, Virginia, and Union, West Virginia, were its main customers, before the Virginia and Tennessee Railroad arrived nearby in the late 1850s. War passed over the mountain too: after the Battle of Cloyd's Mountain in 1864, Union General George Crook marched his troops over Salt Pond Mountain toward Union. In 1869 the property went to General Herman Haupt, who had headed the United States Military Railroad during the war, and his wife. When guests began paying for their stay, Giles County required a hotel license, and the Haupts reopened the hotel to the public. Railroad passengers later stepped off at Pembroke or Eggleston and rode horse-drawn carriages up the dirt Doe Creek Road.

## A Stone Lodge and a Texas Fortune

Under manager Gilbert Porterfield, who ran the resort for 25 years, Mountain Lake earned the reputation for fine cuisine it still carries. Guests could build their own cottages on 15-year leases, after which the cottages reverted to the hotel, and most of the names those first builders gave them more than a century ago are still in use. In the 1930s, William Lewis Moody Jr. of Galveston, Texas, bought the hotel, and in 1938 he replaced the wooden building with the present one, built of stone from the surrounding land. His daughter, Mary Moody Northen, owned it until her death in 1986. Following her wishes, her estate's trustees created an endowment to keep the hotel and its land as she had known them, without overbuilding and without upsetting the surrounding ecology.

## Kellerman's in Virginia

The stone lodge also has a second identity, familiar to movie fans. Much of the on-location filming for the 1987 hit Dirty Dancing, starring Patrick Swayze and Jennifer Grey, took place at Mountain Lake Hotel, where the Stone Lodge stood in for Kellerman's Resort, a fictional retreat in the Catskill Mountains of upstate New York. The film's other location was the 1927 Lake Lure Inn in North Carolina. Like its fictional counterpart, the real resort is built around the outdoors: the lodge's own site promotes some 22 miles of trails. Once open only from late spring to early fall, the lodge now says it is open year-round, with winter hikes, sledding and an ice skating rink among its seasonal offerings.

## Spruce Bogs and a Record Cold

The land around the lake is as unusual as the water. Nearby stands one of Virginia's few virgin forests, including Mann's Bog, a rare virgin spruce bog with an unusual array of northern species far from their usual range. The lake drains into Little Stoney Creek, which tumbles over a spectacular waterfall called The Cascades on its way to the New River. Winters can be brutal: during a severe cold wave in January 1985, Mountain Lake set Virginia's statewide record low of −30°F. Since 1930, the University of Virginia has run the Mountain Lake Biological Station nearby, where an annual survey of Silene plants along the surrounding roadsides has continued since 1988. The nonprofit Mountain Lake Conservancy helps protect some 2,600 acres around the hotel and offers environmental and cultural education to the public.

## Flight Context

Mountain Lake lies at 37.36°N, 80.5339°W high in the mountains of Giles County, Virginia, at about 3,875 ft above sea level, near Newport and not far from the West Virginia line. Given the surface elevation, fly no lower than about 5,500–6,000 ft MSL; when the lake is full, look for the patch of open water with the stone lodge nearby and forest around it. Virginia Tech Montgomery Executive Airport (KBCB) at Blacksburg is about 11 nm south-southeast, New River Valley Airport (KPSK) at Dublin is about 15 nm south-southwest, and Roanoke–Blacksburg Regional Airport (KROA) is about 27 nm east. Mountaintop weather changes quickly, and cloud can cap the high ground even when the valleys are clear.
