# Peters Cartridge Company

On the Little Miami River, a powder works and cartridge factory armed nations through two world wars, survived deadly explosions, pressed phonograph records, stored a distiller's stock, and finally became a brewery.

**Location:** 39.3503, -84.2425

**Categories:** Industrial history, Ammunition manufacturers, Shot towers, National Register of Historic Places, Ohio

## Introduction

In Kings Mills, Ohio, the explosions eventually became part of the background. As one writer put it about the powder and cartridge works along the Little Miami River, "Later they became of such frequency as to cause little comment, unless they were especially violent." Some of them were. The Peters Cartridge Company, founded here in 1887 by Gershom Moore Peters, supplied military ammunition to various countries during both world wars, and its surviving brick buildings and shot tower in the river gorge are a record of an industry that lived with the constant risk of explosion.

## Powder on the Little Miami

The story starts before Peters. In 1855 Joseph Warren King bought the Austin & Carleton powder mill on the Little Miami River and expanded it into the Miami Powder Company, complete with a company town called Goes Station. In 1877 he sold that business to build something bigger, the Great Western Powder Works, at a more favorable hydropower site downstream. Its company town took his name: Kings Mills. A wooden dam diverted the river into a power canal running through the gorge, in a narrow valley between steep hills. Along the 2-mile canal, King spread the manufacturing buildings far apart on purpose. Explosions were infrequent in those years, but when one came, distance was meant to keep a single blast from taking everything with it.

## The Son-in-Law's Company

Gershom Moore Peters was King's son-in-law. He began working at the powder mill in 1881, and when King died in 1885, Peters became president of the powder company. Two years later, in 1887, he formed the Peters Cartridge Company at Kings Mills, adding cartridge making to the powder business on the river. The operation grew fast. By 1889 its machinery was manufacturing four thousand cartridges an hour. The works had its own rail traffic, with loaded cars shunted from place to place, and a railroad station, freight house, warehouses and employee homes stood close by the plant. That closeness would matter terribly the following summer.

## The Fifteenth of July, 1890

On 15 July 1890, loaded railway cars were being shunted at the powder works when they collided. The collision set off an explosion that killed twelve people by the most common count; some accounts say eleven. Fires spread from the blast and destroyed the railroad station, the freight house, two Peters Cartridge Company office buildings, the shell factory, the cartridge loading plant, a large warehouse and six employee residences. Behind that list of buildings were the people who never came home, and employee households who lost theirs. The company rebuilt on the opposite side of the river from the old powder works, at 1415 Grandin Road. The new wood-frame complex included a large shot tower, completed in 1895. The last wood-frame buildings, machine shop R-3 and shotgun shell loading building R-21, went up in 1907.

## War Orders in Brick and Concrete

As World War I approached, large ammunition orders arrived from the Russian Empire and from the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland. Bullet manufacturing building R-6 was built of brick, and money from the wartime contracts replaced most of the old wooden buildings with brick and reinforced concrete. The main R-1 building rose in 1916, the R-17 power house in 1917, metallic cartridge loading building R-2 in 1918, and the R-23 indoor shooting range and ballistics laboratory in 1919, along with primer assembly building R-9. A taller brick shot tower, painted with a large "P," became a local landmark. In 1934 Remington Arms bought the company. During World War II, facilities expanded to include the federally owned Kings Mills Ordnance Plant on an adjacent hill south of the factory. In 1942 an explosion at the neighboring King Powder Company reportedly shattered windows as far as 30 miles away. Production of military ammunition ended in March 1944.

## Records, Warehouses and a Brewery

The site's second life was quieter. Remington sold the Kings Mills factory to Columbia Records, which made 78 rpm phonograph records there until 1949. As 45 rpm records grew more popular, the buildings became warehouse space for Seagram distillers until 1968, when the site closed for good and began falling into disrepair. Many structures survived anyway, including R-1 and the brick shot tower, and the historic buildings were added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1985. The ground carried a harder legacy. After an EPA investigation from 1994 to 1999, the agency formally declared in 2009 that the soil was contaminated with copper, lead, mercury, boiler ash and slag from cartridge manufacturing, and it ordered DuPont, as the former owner of Remington Arms, to remove the hazardous waste. The $5 million cleanup began in April 2015 and reportedly took nearly a month. Then came renewal. Redevelopment then brought a brewery and an apartment complex themed to the old company to the site starting in 2020, restoring many of its structures and putting its history on display for guests. The brewery, Cartridge Brewing, opened to the public on October 31, 2020.

## Flight Context

Peters Cartridge Company site: 39.3503°N, 84.2425°W, on the Little Miami River at Kings Mills, Warren County, Ohio, northeast of Cincinnati. A viewing altitude of about 2,000-2,500 ft AGL (roughly 2,800-3,300 ft MSL) shows the river gorge, the brick factory buildings and the tall brick shot tower beside the water. Nearest airports: Warren County Airport/John Lane Field at Lebanon (FAA identifier I68, no ICAO code), about 7 nm north; Cincinnati Municipal Lunken Field (KLUK), about 17 nm south-southwest; Butler County Regional-Hogan Field at Hamilton (KHAO), about 13 nm west. Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International (KCVG) airspace lies to the southwest; check the Class B boundaries before flying toward the city.
