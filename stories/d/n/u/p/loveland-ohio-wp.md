# Loveland, Ohio

Named for a storekeeper rather than romance, the Little Miami River town of Loveland turned its name into an identity, stamping valentines by hand every February while its old railroad corridor became a popular bike trail.

**Location:** 39.2689, -84.2706

## Introduction

Every February, a volunteer known as the Valentine Lady helps hand-stamp thousands of envelopes, by one count as many as 20,000 in Loveland, Ohio, each marked with a cancellation that reads "There is nothing in this world so sweet as Love." The Loveland Area Chamber of Commerce has run the program since the early 1970s, and the city's water towers carry its nickname, "Sweetheart of Ohio." The romance is borrowed, though. The name came from James Loveland, who ran a general store and post office near the railroad tracks downtown. Loveland simply decided to make the most of it.

## Before It Was Loveland

The first settler arrived in 1795. Colonel Thomas Paxton, who had served in General Anthony Wayne's army and fallen for the Miami country, sold his Kentucky farm over a faulty title and bought 1,200 acres where Loveland now stands. He was sixty when he came, and he named the settlement after himself. It kept his name until 1850, when it was renamed for the storekeeper. Loveland incorporated as a village in 1876 and reincorporated as a chartered city on July 25, 1961, absorbing small neighboring settlements including Paxton, Obanionsville and Symmestown. Paxton's own homestead, White Pillars, remained unincorporated despite being the first settlement in the area, until Loveland bought it in 1996 and moved its border east. Today the city of 13,307 people, counted at the 2020 census, spreads across three counties: Hamilton, Clermont and Warren.

## Little Switzerland of the Miami Valley

In its early days Loveland was a resort, with summer homes for the wealthy that earned it the nickname "Little Switzerland of the Miami Valley." Salmon P. Chase, the future Chief Justice, kept a country home nearby, and the Cincinnati YWCA maintained a summer cottage. The surrounding Clermont County countryside was known for peaches and strawberries, and in 1810 John Smith built what is described as Ohio's first paper mill here, remembered today in the name of Kugler Mill Road. Railroads did the rest. Loveland grew at the junction of the Little Miami Railroad and the Marietta and Cincinnati Railroad, with 40 passenger trains a day running between Loveland and Cincinnati. Crossing the river took longer to solve. After years of pressure, a $75,000 suspension bridge was dedicated on July 4, 1871, anchored by four 7,000-pound wrought iron columns, reportedly the heaviest ever made in the United States at the time.

## Mastodons, Floodwater and a Castle by Subscription

The ground here holds older stories. In 1886, workers found a mastodon skeleton and prehistoric stone tools in a Loveland gravel pit. The town had a temperance streak as well: in 1903 it voted to go dry, banning alcohol sales 17 years before national Prohibition, and it became a center of Ohio's temperance movement. Being so close to the Little Miami River has also left downtown vulnerable to flooding. The Ohio Flood of March 1913 destroyed a corn mill and washed out the Loveland Bridge, and after another major flood in 1959 a dike went up along the river in 1962 and 1963. In the 1920s, a Cincinnati newspaper offered a free riverside plot in Loveland with a subscription. The Loveland Castle, also known as Chateau Laroche, was built on several plots obtained through that promotion.

## Hearts, Frogs and a Flag

Loveland leans into its accidental name with real affection. The first Valentine Lady was Doris Pfiester, and since 1989 the town has also held an annual greeting card design contest. On January 27, 1994, the Loveland Post Office hosted the issue of the 29-cent "Love Sunrise" stamp, the first self-adhesive Love stamp. The city flag was designed by Howard Miller, according to City Hall staff quoted by the Flags of the World website. It is divided diagonally, white over dark blue, with a heart bearing the date 1796 at the center, five green leaves above it and a red ribbon reading LOVELAND below. A replacement chosen in a city contest, "The Heart Land," was slated to be unveiled during the city's 2026 sesquicentennial. The city even has an unlikely mascot. The Loveland frog, a legendary humanoid frog from the area, serves as the city's mascot.

## The Trail Through Town

The old Little Miami Railroad corridor, long abandoned, was converted into a bike trail in the 1980s and joined the Little Miami Scenic Trail in 1984. Downtown sits in a small valley on both banks of the river, and in summer the trail is busy with cyclists while canoeists paddle down the river from the local livery. The city built a race around it, the Tour de Loveland, and hosted USA Cycling Elite National Championship criteriums in June 1998. Loveland's modern history has harder chapters too. In 1972 and 1973 the town was the site of multiple Ku Klux Klan rallies that drew members from several states, and a nativity scene left City Hall's lawn in 1993 after a Klan representative asked to erect a cross beside it. On April 9, 1999, a tornado that briefly reached F4 strength killed four people in northeastern Hamilton County before the storms reached the city, which the National Weather Service listed among the hardest-hit areas that day; Wikipedia reports that one of the four was a Loveland resident.

## Flight Context

Loveland sits at 39.2689°N, 84.2706°W, about 597 ft above sea level, where the Little Miami River winds through a small valley northeast of Cincinnati. From 2,500–3,500 ft MSL, follow the river: downtown straddles its banks, the Little Miami Scenic Trail corridor traces the old railroad line along the water, and the city's residential neighborhoods climb the hills on either side. Kings Island lies about 4 nm to the north. Cincinnati Municipal Lunken Field (KLUK) is about 12 nm southwest, Clermont County Airport (FAA identifier I69) at Batavia is about 12 nm south, and Warren County Airport/John Lane Field (FAA identifier I68) is about 12 nm north. Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International Airport (KCVG) is about 23 nm southwest, so check Cincinnati-area airspace before descending. River fog can linger in the valley on calm mornings.
