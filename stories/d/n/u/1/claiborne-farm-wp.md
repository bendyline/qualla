# Claiborne Farm

Run by one family since 1910, Claiborne Farm near Paris, Kentucky, stood Secretariat at stud, shaped American bloodlines for a century, and keeps a cemetery that reads like a roll call of racing history.

**Location:** 38.1904, -84.2451

**Categories:** Horse racing, Thoroughbred breeding, Bluegrass region, Hancock family, Kentucky

## Introduction

Walk among the graves at Claiborne Farm and the headstones read like a history of American racing. Secretariat is here. So are Gallant Fox, Bold Ruler, Nasrullah, Mr. Prospector, Round Table and Riva Ridge, and in a second cemetery on the farm's Marchmont division lie Danzig, Damascus, Easy Goer and Sir Ivor. The roster runs on from there. This thoroughbred breeding operation near Paris, Kentucky, has been run by the same family since 1910, and the horses resting in its ground are the evidence of what that family built.

## A Virginian in the Bluegrass

Claiborne Farm was established in 1910 by Arthur B. Hancock, who already owned Ellerslie Stud in Albemarle County, Virginia. Hancock looked across the Atlantic for his foundation. He imported breeding stock from Europe, and those horses made Claiborne an international leader in breeding, sales and racing. Success came early. Hancock bred Vigil, winner of the 1923 Preakness Stakes. Then came Sir Gallahad, purchased from France, who became the leading sire in 1930, 1933, 1934 and 1940. In 1930 his son Gallant Fox won the U.S. Triple Crown, and a stallion brought over from France had produced one of the greatest prizes in American racing.

## The Hancock Line

The farm has stayed in Hancock hands ever since. Arthur B. Hancock (1875–1957) was followed as owner by Arthur B. "Bull" Hancock Jr. (1910–1972), who was born the same year Claiborne was founded, and then by Seth W. Hancock, born in 1949, who handed the reins in 2015 to his son Seth Walker "Walker" Hancock Jr., the fourth generation of Hancock men to lead the farm. Arthur B. Hancock III (1943–2026), who died in September 2026, built Stone Farm, a separate breeding operation nearby. Through those years the imports continued. In 1936 Claiborne joined a consortium that brought Blenheim over from England, and in 1944 the farm bought Princequillo, who became the leading sire in the United States in 1957 and 1958. Each acquisition added another strand to the farm's bloodlines, and Claiborne's name became tied to the pedigrees of horses far beyond its own fences.

## Secretariat Comes Home to Stud

At the end of 1973, Claiborne received a resident whose name needed no introduction. Seth Hancock syndicated Secretariat for breeding, and the horse stood at stud at Claiborne from the close of his racing career until his death in 1989. The farm also became home to all the major horses owned by the Phipps family, among them Orb, winner of the 2013 Kentucky Derby. Recognition followed. The Keeneland Association honored Claiborne in 1973 with its Mark of Distinction for its contribution to Keeneland and the thoroughbred industry, and the farm won the Eclipse Award for Outstanding Breeder in 1979 and again in 1984. It has even welcomed royalty. Queen Elizabeth II of the United Kingdom, who owned racehorses herself, dined at Claiborne during a visit to Kentucky; Wikipedia counts two visits.

## Where the Champions Rest

The cemeteries hold more than names; they hold the farm's record of influence. Bold Ruler, buried here, was the leading sire in 1963, 1964, 1965, 1966, 1967, 1968, 1969 and 1973. Nasrullah led the list five times, Mr. Prospector twice, and on the Marchmont side Danzig led it three years running, from 1991 to 1993. Sir Gallahad III lies there too. Not every story is one of long life. Swale's dates read 1981 to 1984, and Ruffian's 1972 to 1975. Ruffian was originally buried at Belmont Park, but in 2023 the remains were relocated to Claiborne, bringing one more celebrated name to rest in the farm's Kentucky ground. Among the earliest dates on the list are War Cloud's, 1915 to 1923.

## A Legacy Still Breeding

Racing historian Edward L. Bowen considers Claiborne Farm one of the most influential American breeding operations, because of the many breeders who benefited from its horses and the length of time that influence has lasted. The work continues. War Front, a son of Danzig once described as the world's top sire of turf juveniles, is now pensioned. The stallion roster on the farm's website in October 2026 includes Blame, who won the 2010 Breeders' Cup Classic over Zenyatta, along with Mindframe, Prince of Monaco, Annapolis, Johannes and Bright Future. More than a century after Arthur Hancock founded the farm, its bloodlines are still being written on the same land.

## Flight Context

Claiborne Farm: 38.1904°N, 84.2451°W, in the rolling Bluegrass horse country just outside Paris, Bourbon County, Kentucky. A viewing altitude of about 2,000-2,500 ft AGL (roughly 3,000-3,500 ft MSL) shows the farm's pastures, barns and fence lines against the surrounding countryside. Nearest airports: Blue Grass Airport at Lexington (KLEX), about 20 nm southwest; Georgetown-Scott County Regional (FAA identifier 27K, no ICAO code), about 9 nm west; Cynthiana-Harrison County (FAA identifier 0I8, no ICAO code), about 11 nm north. Keep a respectful altitude over the paddocks, since low-flying aircraft can startle horses.
