# Grant Birthplace

The small frame cottage on the Ohio River where Ulysses S. Grant was born in 1822, a house that spent half a century touring Ohio before coming home to its original foundation.

## Introduction

"Grant was himself the supreme example of American opportunity," President Warren G. Harding said in Point Pleasant, Ohio, on the hundredth anniversary of Ulysses S. Grant's birth there. The house where Grant was born was not there to hear him. The little frame cottage where the Union general and future president was born had been taken apart decades earlier and carried off on tour, first to Cincinnati and then to Columbus. It would take another fourteen years, and a crew of workers with six trucks, to bring it home.

## Two Dollars a Month

The cottage was built in 1817 near a tannery in Point Pleasant, a small settlement on the banks of the Ohio River in what is now Monroe Township, Clermont County. In 1821 Jesse Root Grant, who was overseeing the local tannery, married Hannah Simpson, and the couple moved into the one-story house, paying $2 a month in rent. Their first child was born there on April 27, 1822. His name was Hiram Ulysses Grant. He became Ulysses S. Grant only years later, when a clerical error on his 1839 nomination to West Point swapped his given names and added an initial for his mother's maiden name, Simpson. The family did not stay long in Point Pleasant. In 1823, the year after his birth, they moved to Georgetown, Ohio. He would go on to command the Union Army in the Civil War and serve as the 18th President of the United States.

## The House That Went on Tour

After Grant died in 1885, admirers had the cottage dismantled and shipped by barge down the river to Cincinnati, where it went on display at the Ohio Valley Centennial Exposition. From there it traveled to Goodale Park in Columbus, where it became a focal point of the Grand Army of the Republic's national encampment in 1888, a gathering of Union veterans. Later it moved to the Ohio State Fairgrounds. Years of open-air display and crowds of visitors took a visible toll on the little house, and in 1896 a protective enclosure of glass and limestone was built around it. Back in Point Pleasant, meanwhile, a different house had gone up on the original site. The spot was not forgotten, though. In 1907 preservation-minded enthusiasts placed a Civil War-era cannon there beside a plaque honoring Grant.

## Coming Home

Grant's birth centennial in 1922 gave the effort to bring the cottage home real momentum. Ohio created a U.S. Grant State Memorial Association and a Grant Centenary Commission, and that year Congress authorized commemorative Grant gold dollars and silver half-dollars, with part of the proceeds going to improve the road to the site. The Grant Memorial Bridge across Indian Creek opened in 1927. The return itself came in 1936, with help from the Works Progress Administration. Crews took the fairgrounds structure apart piece by piece, loaded it onto six trucks and drove it to Point Pleasant. The newer house on the original lot was moved next door, and the birthplace cottage was rebuilt on its original stone foundation. It was rededicated on October 4, 1936.

## A Small House, Carefully Kept

Today the Ohio History Connection operates the Grant Birthplace as a historic house museum, with day-to-day management by Historic New Richmond, Inc. The cottage is furnished with items that once belonged to Grant along with a few period pieces. In 1998 the birthplace and several associated commemorative sites were listed on the National Register of Historic Places as a historic district. The ground itself has stories too. In 1984, replacing a nearby bridge disturbed a small refuse heap holding early nineteenth-century pottery, and a 2005 survey of the open grounds turned up a couple of stone flakes from an unidentified prehistoric period. The site is open seasonally, from April through September; check the Ohio History Connection's page for current hours and admission. It is a modest beginning for a man who would lead armies and a nation.

## Flight Context

The Grant Birthplace stands at 38.894°N, 84.233°W in Point Pleasant, Ohio, on the north bank of the Ohio River in Clermont County, near the junction of U.S. Route 52 and State Route 232. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, follow the Ohio River upstream from Cincinnati and look for the small riverside settlement of Point Pleasant. Clermont County Airport (FAA: I69) is about 11 nm north, Cincinnati Municipal Airport-Lunken Field (KLUK) about 15 nm northwest, and Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International (KCVG) about 22 nm west-northwest, so expect busy Cincinnati-area traffic. River valley fog is common on calm mornings, especially in spring and fall.
