# World's Largest Horseshoe Crab (Hillsboro, Ohio)

A hollow fiberglass horseshoe crab, roughly 67 feet long, built for a Baltimore harbor display in the 1990s and passed from Kentucky to an Ohio church to a Hillsboro roadside before reportedly heading to Alabama.

**Location:** 39.1977, -83.5945

**Categories:** roadside-attraction, sculpture, fiberglass, ohio, highland-county

## Introduction

Crabbie has lived in as many as four states and never once walked anywhere. The nickname belongs to a fiberglass and foam horseshoe crab roughly 67 to 68 feet long, 28 to 33 feet wide and 10 to 12 feet high, a hollow shell that began life in the 1990s as part of a maritime display in Baltimore's Inner Harbor. From there it went to the Creation Museum in Kentucky, then to a church in Blanchester, Ohio, then to private property near Hillsboro, where it became a roadside attraction with a very large claim to fame. Its address keeps changing, but the title it carries has stayed the same.

## Built for the Harbor

The crab was made for the Columbus Center, in Baltimore's Inner Harbor, which opened in May 1997 as an interactive Hall of Exploration. The idea was to entertain visitors while teaching them about marine life and the Chesapeake Bay. A multimedia presentation on sharks was shown inside the crab's shell, a theater inside the body of an animal that is not a shark at all.

The sculpture itself is built of fiberglass and foam, and it is hollow. That emptiness turned out to matter. A solid crab would have been a statue; a hollow one was a room.

The animal it imitates has an old lineage. Horseshoe crabs are not true crabs; they are chelicerates, more closely related to spiders and scorpions, and similar-looking relatives appear in the fossil record as far back as around 445 million years ago.

## Too Big for a Museum

In the mid-2000s the crab was moved to the Creation Museum in Petersburg, Kentucky. Because of its size, the museum donated it in 2006 to Freedom Worship Baptist Church in Blanchester, Ohio.

There, the crab arrived without an instruction manual. Pastor Jim remembered the horseshoe crab shells he had collected as a boy in Florida, brought them to the church, and used them as a blueprint for assembly. The church called the finished crab a "community pavilion" and claimed that 60 people could sit within its shell. Weddings were held inside, and movies were screened there as well.

## Eleven Pieces and a Chainsaw

Ben and Darlene Sexton, who own a sportsmen's lodge on the property where the crab came to rest, bought it in 2015. Moving it was a project. The crab had originally been made in nine sections, but it had to be cut with a chainsaw into eleven before it could be transported. It ended up on State Route 124, at the eastern edge of Hillsboro, in Highland County. The local Times-Gazette, writing in September 2015, gave its size as 67 feet long, 28 feet wide and 12 feet high, not counting a tail that extends higher.

"I wanted to save it as the bit of Americana that it is," Sexton said. He planned to close in the open areas under the crab, install a floor, and decorate the inside. In the meantime the hollow structure was open to visitors as a roadside attraction, periodically repainted, and it was used informally for small gatherings and photo opportunities.

## Southbound Again

The crab did not stay in Hillsboro. A visitor tip posted to Roadside America in August 2025 says it was sold and taken away, said to be going to Dinosaur Adventure Land, a creationist ministry and amusement park in Lenox, Alabama. Wikipedia says it was donated to the park and now sits near Lenox, which would make Alabama the fourth state on its list, but neither account is confirmed by the park or by news coverage, so its current whereabouts are uncertain.

That is a long way for a sculpture that began as part of a harbor exhibit. Each stop gave it a new job: a theater for a shark presentation in Baltimore, a pavilion for weddings and movies in Blanchester, an Americana landmark in Hillsboro. The coordinates on this page still mark the Ohio roadside where it stood, but the crab itself has reportedly moved on.

## Flight Context

The coordinates, 39.20°N, 83.59°W, mark the sculpture's roadside site on State Route 124 at the eastern edge of Hillsboro, Ohio, in Highland County. The crab was reported sold and taken away in August 2025, said to be bound for Alabama, so it may no longer be at this site; the spot is simply the edge of a small Ohio town. Highland County Airport (KHOC) serves Hillsboro, and Cincinnati/Northern Kentucky International (KCVG) is a major commercial airport in the region. Around 1,500 feet above ground level gives a good view of Hillsboro and the countryside around it.
