# Old East

Begun in 1793, with enslaved labor, Old East at UNC Chapel Hill is the first state university building in the United States - and every October 12, the university still celebrates the day its cornerstone was laid.

**Location:** 35.9125, -79.0508

**Categories:** National Historic Landmarks in North Carolina, University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill landmarks, School buildings completed in 1793, University and college residential buildings, Slavery in the United States

## Introduction

Every October 12, a building gets a birthday party. The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill marks it as University Day. In some years students have been released from classes for part of the morning, and many have drifted to the Old East Birthday Bash, where past celebrations have featured cake, athletic signings, puppies, appearances by the chancellor, dance routines and a cappella performances. The guest of honor is a brick residence hall on the north side of campus. Old East is the first state university building in the United States. Its story includes the enslaved people who labored on it.

## Bricks, Land and Forced Labor

In December 1792, Colonel John Hogan signed a contract to make 150,000 bricks on the site where the building would rise, and he also donated 200 acres toward the university's grounds. The construction contractor was James Patterson of Chatham County. The labor that raised the walls, however, very likely included enslaved people: UNC's library notes that enslaved labor was likely used for the construction, and Patterson wrote in 1795 that several of the people he enslaved painted the roof. The contracts and ceremonies preserved the names of the men in charge; the enslaved people whose labor helped raise Old East deserve to be remembered alongside them. On October 12, 1793, William Richardson Davie ceremoniously laid the cornerstone; he would go on to serve as governor of North Carolina from 1798 to 1799. The building began as two stories with sixteen rooms. Its design reflected the trustees' vision of a quadrangle running north toward the town of Chapel Hill. UNC's library says trustees hired Patterson to build it for about $5,000, but exactly what it cost is unknown; records from 1799 show the university spent a total of $12,180 on Steward's Hall, Old East, Person Hall and the President's House.

## Older Than It Looks, Younger Than Some

Old East's claim to be the first state university building in the country comes with a careful distinction. The Wren Building at the College of William & Mary in Williamsburg, Virginia, is older, begun in 1695. But William & Mary did not become a public university until 1906, so its famous building was not, at its founding, a state university building. Old East was. Over the centuries it has served both as a classroom building and as a residence hall; today it is purely a residence hall. The Old Well and Old East, as the earliest buildings at UNC, have long served as symbols of the university, and collections of art depicting them are held at the Blowing Rock Art and History Museum and in UNC's own Wilson Library.

## Growing Up and Out

The building needed work early. By 1804 it required significant repairs, and it received new doors, window sashes and ladders, along with a repainted roof. A third story went on in 1823. In 1844 the university hired Alexander Jackson Davis to lengthen Old East by a third and give it a new north entrance. Davis added large north-facing windows framed by brick panels and two brick porches on the building's east side. The new rooms became the home of the Philanthropic Society and also served as a library. Their interior woodwork was designed and built by Thomas Day, a skilled and respected African-American cabinetmaker. The Philanthropic Society stayed in Old East until New East was completed in 1860.

## University Day

In 1877, Zebulon Baird Vance, then Governor of North Carolina and chair of the Board of Trustees, declared that October 12 - the anniversary of Old East's cornerstone - would be University Day, to "be observed with appropriate ceremonies under the direction of the faculty." The tradition has drawn presidents. John F. Kennedy spoke at University Day in 1961, and Bill Clinton did so in 1993. On December 21, 1965, the National Park Service designated Old East a National Historic Landmark, and it stands within the Chapel Hill Historic District. More than two centuries after its cornerstone was laid, it still houses students, which may be the best measure of all: the country's oldest state university building is not a museum piece but somebody's home.

## Flight Context

Old East stands at approximately 35.9125 N, 79.0508 W on the northern part of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill campus, beside the historic quadrangle and a short distance from the dome of Morehead Planetarium. From 2,500-3,000 ft MSL, the wooded campus and its brick quads are easy to pick out in the town of Chapel Hill. Raleigh-Durham International Airport (KRDU) lies about 13 nm to the east-southeast; check the sectional chart for its controlled airspace and expect to work with Raleigh-Durham Approach. Burlington-Alamance Regional Airport (KBUY) is about 22 nm west-northwest. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common.
