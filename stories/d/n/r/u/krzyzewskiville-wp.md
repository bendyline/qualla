# Krzyzewskiville

Every winter, Duke undergraduates turn the lawn outside Cameron Indoor Stadium into a rule-bound tent city, camping for weeks for a place in the student section against North Carolina.

**Location:** 35.9973, -78.9405

**Categories:** Duke Blue Devils men's basketball, College traditions, Duke University, Durham, North Carolina, Carolina–Duke rivalry

## Introduction

In 1986, a Duke senior named Kimberly Reed decided that sleeping in line the night before the North Carolina game was not ambitious enough. She and a group of friends from the Mirecourt living group, fifteen or so in all, showed up on a Thursday for a Sunday tip-off and pitched four or five tents outside Cameron Indoor Stadium. The rest of campus noticed. By game time, 70 to 75 tents stood in line, NBC put the campers on the evening news, and they made the front page of USA Today. Duke won, 82-74. A tradition was born, and it would take the name of the coach whose teams made the wait worthwhile: Krzyzewskiville, or K-ville for short.

## Not a Ticket Line

K-ville is the line undergraduates join for the right to attend Duke's designated tenting games, and it is often mistaken for a ticket line. There are no student tickets. Students are admitted straight from the line an hour and a half before tip-off. The village honors Mike Krzyzewski, "Coach K," who led Duke from 1980 to 2022 and helped build one of the best programs in the nation. He was known to buy pizza for the tenters from time to time, and before games against the arch-rival Tar Heels he held open-forum "team meetings" with the Cameron Crazies. By 2000 the site had been landscaped with a uniform lawn for tents and was getting wireless internet, although students reported the connection was unreliable.

## Law and Order on the Lawn

In recent seasons tenting has run three to eight weeks before the home game against UNC, and the Duke Student Government regulates it closely. Up to twelve students share a tent, and student line monitors run regular, periodic checks. In the first third of the season, a tent of twelve must keep two people inside by day and ten at night. The requirements ease to one by day and six at night, then one and two. On the final weekend come personal checks, when each member must answer three of five roll calls spread over two nights. Miss two tent checks and your group drops to the back of the line, or out entirely if K-ville is full at its 100 standard tents with a waitlist. Flex tents sit on top of that cap, and in 2025 K-ville had 130 tents in all. Heaters are forbidden. Severe weather brings "grace": temperatures below freezing, more than two inches of snow or winds of 35 miles per hour.

## The Race to the Secret Spots

Getting in is its own contest. Groups registering early, as black or blue tents, commit to the longest and hardest stretch. For the last 30 places, white registration demands the Race to the Secret Spots. Line monitors post locations online at a set time, written as riddles, and hundreds of tent representatives sprint across campus to reach them. Most groups station members all over campus in advance, and the spots fill in minutes. The next 30 finishers get flex spots, following white-tent rules without a guaranteed seat. Flex tenting replaced the old walk-up line, which formed 48 hours before tip-off and left some students who had waited more than a day outside the gym. The frustration boiled over into a near-riot before the 2018 UNC game, and flex tenting began the following year.

## Spies in Blue

The rivalry has seeped into the rulebook itself. Robertson Scholars from UNC spend a semester at Duke, and their program promises full student privileges at both schools, including sporting events. In the 2006 tenting season, some Robertsons tented alongside Duke students and used their Duke ID cards to get into the game. Once inside the student section, they peeled off their Duke clothing to reveal UNC gear. Duke Student Government banned visiting Robertson Scholars from K-ville tenting starting in 2006, a decision some consider a violation of the program's promise.

## Trivia, P-Checks and Jabari Bars

Demand keeps rising. In January 2017, more than 150 groups registered for the 70 early spots, the first time the cap was exceeded, so line monitors set a trivia test on the current season. In 2021-22, almost a third of Duke's undergraduates sat for a trivia exam covering team statistics and players' social media. Personal-check nights have become parties, with live music, food trucks and games; in 2024, one tent even hosted a mariachi band. The best-known tents are handed down between student groups, and the oldest, nicknamed "Das Boot," has been one of K-ville's most prominent tents since 2013. Players have joined in too. Jabari Parker baked homemade "Jabari Bars" for tenters, and Marshall Plumlee gave tours of the most notable tents for a video series called "K-Ville Kribs."

## Flight Context

Krzyzewskiville occupies the lawn beside Cameron Indoor Stadium on Duke University's campus in Durham, North Carolina, at approximately 36.00°N, 78.94°W. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the athletic complex on the university campus; in January and February the rows of tents may be visible on the grass beside the arena. Raleigh-Durham International Airport (KRDU) is about 10 nm southeast, so expect busy airline traffic, check the sectional chart for the controlled airspace, and plan to work with Raleigh approach. Winter brings cold fronts and occasional snow or ice, the same weather that sets off K-ville's grace rules.
