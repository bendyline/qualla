# Dinosaur Kingdom II

In the woods at Natural Bridge, Virginia, artist Mark Cline's Dinosaur Kingdom II stages an alternate Civil War in fiberglass, with some thirty dinosaurs turning on the Union soldiers who tried to use them as weapons.

**Location:** 37.6309, -79.5408

**Categories:** Tourist attractions in Rockbridge County, Virginia, Roadside attractions in Virginia, 2005 establishments in Virginia, Dinosaurs in popular culture, Sculptures of dinosaurs, American Civil War alternate histories

## Introduction

"It's 1864. Them wacky Yankees is at it again!" So begins the pitch for Dinosaur Kingdom II, a roadside attraction in Natural Bridge, Virginia, where history takes a very sharp turn. In this version of the Civil War, living dinosaurs have been found in Virginia, the Union Army has tried to turn them into weapons, and the dinosaurs have turned on the Union instead. About thirty fiberglass dinosaurs fill the park, along with a smaller number of Union soldiers, Abraham Lincoln, and, for reasons all its own, a gorilla in a cowboy hat. It is the work of local artist Mark Cline, the man behind Foamhenge.

## Lincoln and the Pteranodon

One account of the premise has paleontologists discovering dinosaurs in Virginia in 1863, while the park's own pitch sets its scene in 1864; either way, the plan to weaponize them goes badly for the Union. Visitors follow a gravel walking path through the woods past a series of set pieces. The park's own descriptions promise Stonewall Jackson battling a spinosaurus, a stegosaurus being milked, and Abe Lincoln having lassoed a pteranodon that is chewing up the Gettysburg Address. Everything is played for laughs, and the joke is in the collision of solemn history with monster-movie spectacle. Accounts differ on the park's age: one dates it to 2005, but RVA Magazine reports that Dinosaur Kingdom II reopened in 2016 after the original park was lost to a fire, and today the attraction bills itself as Dinosaur Kingdom II, "Back from Extinction."

## Why the Yankees Got Eaten

The Union Army was not Cline's first choice of victim. By one account, he originally planned to have his dinosaurs besiege Pancho Villa's army, but he switched to Union troops to win local favor, explaining that "people still fight the Civil War down here." The choice is a knowing wink at the region's long memory of the war, delivered in the broad dialect of the park's own advertising, which casts the dinosaurs as Dixie's last trick. According to Cline, Northerners have never complained about playing the villain. He has also said that a man in Gettysburg once showed interest in a version there, and that in a park in Pennsylvania he would have made the Confederacy the enemy.

## A Boy at Dinosaur Land

Cline's dinosaurs began with a childhood stop. He had wanted to build dinosaur statues ever since stopping at the Dinosaur Land park in White Post, Virginia, as a boy. The theme came from the movies: The Valley of Gwangi, in which cowboys discover living dinosaurs in a Mexican valley, gave him the idea of dropping prehistoric monsters into the middle of American history. The result is part sculpture garden, part B-movie, and wholly the product of one imagination. Cline may stop by the park, though the park says visitors are not guaranteed to meet him; it suggests his Haunting Tales ghost tour in Lexington as the best way to do so.

## A Summer Kingdom

Dinosaur Kingdom II is a seasonal attraction, open from May through October, on weekends early and late in the season and daily through the heart of summer. Dogs are welcome as long as their owners clean up after them, and the gravel trail has ramps for wheelchairs, though a few small hills make some stretches harder going. Besides the dinosaur scenes, the park offers a dino dig for fossil hunters, a maze, and a gift shop. Less than a mile away stood Foamhenge, Cline's full-scale styrofoam replica of Stonehenge, until the Natural Bridge became a state park in 2016; Foamhenge was dismantled that year and now stands at Cox Farms near Centreville. The dinosaurs stayed, keeping this corner of Rockbridge County stocked with one artist's sense of humor.
