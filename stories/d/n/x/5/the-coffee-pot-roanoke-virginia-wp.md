# The Coffee Pot (Roanoke, Virginia)

Built in 1936 as a filling station and tea room with a giant steaming coffee pot on its roof, Roanoke's log-walled Coffee Pot soon became a roadhouse and has been drawing crowds, and touring musicians, ever since.

**Location:** 37.2536, -79.9869

**Categories:** Commercial buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in Virginia, Novelty buildings in Virginia, Roadside attractions in Virginia, Commercial buildings completed in 1936, Buildings and structures in Roanoke, Virginia, National Register of Historic Places in Roanoke, Virginia, 1936 establishments in Virginia

## Introduction

The giant pot on the roof says coffee. The building underneath it was designed to serve tea, and gasoline. When Clifton and Irene Kefauver opened The Coffee Pot on the southern approach to Roanoke, Virginia, in 1936, they built it as a filling station and tea room, then crowned it with a big red coffee pot that once puffed real steam from its spout. The tea room didn't last long. By 1937 it had become a roadhouse, and the Coffee Pot has been pouring for a thirsty, music-loving public ever since.

## Built to Stop Traffic

The Coffee Pot rose beside what later became U.S. Route 221, Brambleton Avenue, in what was then Roanoke County. When it was built, it was the first commercial building a traveler passed on the highway into Roanoke from the south, and it was designed with that traveler in mind. This kind of building is sometimes called mimetic, or novelty, architecture: a structure shaped like the thing it advertises, a three-dimensional sign meant to catch a passing motorist's eye and stick in the memory. Here the joke carried a twist from the start. The Kefauvers put a coffee pot on a place built to serve tea. The city eventually came to the Coffee Pot rather than the other way around; the area was annexed into the city of Roanoke in 1943, and the building now sits in the Grandin Court neighborhood.

## Logs, Stucco and Steam

Look past the pot and the building is surprisingly rustic. Its four facades are made of unhewn vertical logs, in the rustic style popularized by National Park Service facilities, so that the whole thing resembles a log home. The coffee pot itself, about 15 feet tall, is a masonry form finished in stucco, perched on the southern end of the roof and painted red with a white spout and top. In its early days, steam rose from that spout, fed by a furnace in the storeroom below, so that the pot appeared to be brewing in full view of the highway. It was a simple bit of showmanship, and it made the building hard to forget.

## From Tea Room to Roadhouse

In 1937 the Coffee Pot became a roadhouse, and, Wikipedia says, it has operated continuously since. Locals and travelers alike came for dancing and beer, a long way from the tea service the Kefauvers had planned. The name has changed only once: from 1960 to 1963 the place was called Joe's Ranchhouse Restaurant before going back to the Coffee Pot for good. The building's value as a piece of roadside history was formally recognized in 1996, when it was added to the Virginia Landmarks Register in March and the National Register of Historic Places in May. It has been described as the only active roadhouse left in the Roanoke Valley.

## The Biggest Small Stage in the South

What really made the Coffee Pot's reputation was the music. According to Wikipedia, over the decades its small stage has hosted a remarkable run of performers, including Willie Nelson, Richie Havens, Dickey Betts, Rick Derringer, Ritchie Valens, Danny Gatton and Root Boy Slim. Regional bands made it a regular stop on their tours too, among them SNUFF and the Skip Castro Band. All those big names playing a log-walled roadhouse under a giant coffee pot earned the place a nickname that some have used: "the biggest small stage in the South." Generations after the Kefauvers opened their tea room, the pot is still on the roof. The Virginia Department of Historic Resources lists the Coffee Pot as continuing as a Roanoke entertainment venue, and a concert listing shows it hosting a show as recently as November 2025, though its longtime owner said in 2016 that he hoped a buyer would take it over, so check current hours before you visit.

## Flight Context

The Coffee Pot stands at 37.2536°N, 79.9869°W on Brambleton Avenue (U.S. Route 221) in the Grandin Court neighborhood of Roanoke, Virginia. The building is small, so it is only identifiable at low altitude; from about 1,500 feet AGL, follow Brambleton Avenue and look for a log-walled building with a red coffee pot on its roof. Roanoke-Blacksburg Regional Airport (KROA) is only about 4 nm to the north, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with approach control. Other nearby airports: Lynchburg Regional Airport (KLYH), about 38 nm east; Greenbrier Valley Airport (KLWB) at Lewisburg, West Virginia, about 41 nm north-northwest; and Danville Regional Airport (KDAN), about 51 nm southeast. Best viewed in clear daytime conditions.
