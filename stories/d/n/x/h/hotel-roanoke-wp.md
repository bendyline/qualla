# Hotel Roanoke

Built by the Norfolk and Western Railway in 1882 and rebuilt again and again, Roanoke's Tudor Revival grand hotel hosted future presidents and famous travelers, closed in 1989, and was brought back to life by a city-wide fundraising campaign.

## Introduction

In the weeks before Christmas 1992, Roanoke raced a deadline. Virginia Tech, which now owned the city's shuttered grand hotel, had given the "Renew Roanoke" campaign until December 31 to raise the money to reopen it. In a Christmastime push, the campaign raised $5.006 million, and Norfolk Southern, the railroad that had sold the hotel to the university for $65,000, added $2 million more. In April 1995 the Hotel Roanoke reopened. A Tudor Revival landmark that a railroad first opened on Christmas Day, 1882, had been rescued by a Christmas campaign.

## A Railroad's Parlor

The first Hotel Roanoke was built in 1882 by the Norfolk and Western Railway, which had recently built its administrative offices in the city, bringing in more than a thousand railroad workers. The Philadelphia architect George T. Pearson designed the hotel; the hotel itself says its distinctive Tudor facade came with the 1937-38 alterations. The hotel officially opened on Christmas Day, 1882. It did not stay the same for long. In 1890 a new main wing on the western side replaced the center of the original building, the first of many transformations that make the hotel's history read like a sequence of rebuilds.

## Fire, Wings and a Tower

In July 1898 a fire that began in the kitchen burned out the second and third stories of the 1890 main wing and closed the hotel for several months; it reopened in January 1899. In 1916 the last part of the 1882 building was moved to the rear to make room for a new east wing by the Roanoke firm Frye and Chesterman, and in 1931 that surviving original wing was demolished and replaced. The small 1931 wing at the back is now the oldest surviving part of the hotel. The great change came in 1938, when the main wing was torn down and replaced by the central wing that stands today, with its tower, lobby and public rooms, designed by Knut W. Lind of the New York firm George B. Post and Sons. A larger east wing followed in 1946, and a small 1954 addition gave the hotel its present form.

## Who Came, and Who Waited

The guest list is long and famous. In 1918 Henry Ford, Thomas Edison, Harvey Firestone and John Burroughs stopped here on one of their camping trips through the South. Dwight Eisenhower stayed as a general. Richard Nixon, Gerald Ford and George H. W. Bush all stayed while they were vice presidents, as did Nelson Rockefeller and Spiro Agnew. Ronald Reagan came as governor of California, and Jimmy Carter made a campaign stop, bringing the total to six future presidents. Entertainers who have stayed include Jerry Seinfeld, Kevin Hart, Aerosmith and Wayne Newton. The list also records a long exclusion. By one account, the hotel's first Black guest was the gospel singer Mahalia Jackson, in 1964, eighty-two years after the doors first opened.

## Closing Time

The hotel's later years under the railroad were not always smooth. In 1983 its employees went on strike for six months after union negotiations broke down, and when the strike ended the hotel declined to rehire 36 of the strikers until the National Labor Relations Board ordered them reinstated. In 1989 Norfolk Southern deeded the hotel to Virginia Tech for $65,000. After a flag-lowering ceremony on November 30, the doors closed, and a sale of the contents ran for 17 days. For the next few years, the grand hotel beside the tracks stood closed. The 1992 campaign gave the answer, and Norfolk Southern's $2 million gift came to about thirty times what it had been paid.

## The Second Opening

With the money in hand, the hotel was completely remodeled and restored at a cost of about $28 million, and a new $13 million conference center was built right next to it. A pedestrian bridge now carries guests over Norfolk Southern's tracks to downtown Roanoke. The Hotel Roanoke took its first guests on April 3, 1995, managed by DoubleTree, and was added to the National Register of Historic Places the following year. Since February 2016 it has operated as part of Hilton's Curio Collection, and Virginia Tech's real estate foundation has owned it since 1989. Walking in beneath the 1938 tower, guests step into a building that has burned, been rebuilt, been emptied and been rescued, and still does the job the railroad built it for.

## Flight Context

Hotel Roanoke stands at 37.2745°N, 79.9394°W in Roanoke, Virginia, on the hill above the Norfolk Southern tracks, with a pedestrian bridge linking it to downtown across the rails. From about 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for the large Tudor Revival building with its central tower beside the rail corridor. The O. Winston Link Museum lies about 0.1 miles away. Roanoke-Blacksburg Regional Airport (KROA) is only about 3.5 nm north-northwest, so expect airport traffic, check the sectional chart, and expect to work with approach control. Other nearby airports: Lynchburg Regional Airport (KLYH), about 35 nm east; Greenbrier Valley Airport (KLWB) at Lewisburg, West Virginia, about 41 nm north-northwest; and Danville Regional Airport (KDAN), about 51 nm southeast. Best viewed in clear daytime conditions.
