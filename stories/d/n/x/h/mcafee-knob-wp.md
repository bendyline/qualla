# McAfee Knob

A ledge of rock jutting from Catawba Mountain near Roanoke, McAfee Knob offers a 270-degree view of the Catawba Valley and is billed as the most photographed spot along the Appalachian Trail.

**Location:** 37.3928, -80.0367

**Categories:** Appalachian Trail, Mountains of Roanoke County, Virginia, Mountains of Virginia, Hiking destinations, Scenic viewpoints

## Introduction

At the end of the climb, the rock simply runs out. McAfee Knob ends in a ledge that sticks out from the top of Catawba Mountain like a diving platform, with nothing in front of it but air and, 1,600 feet below, the Catawba Valley. Hikers come to stand on that ledge and photograph the view, and so many do it that the Appalachian Trail Conservancy calls McAfee Knob the most photographed location along the trail. At 3,197 feet, it sits about 10 miles northwest of downtown Roanoke, Virginia, as the crow flies.

## A Settler's Name on the Summit

A knob is a rounded hill or mountain, the kind usually found in older mountain ranges, and this one carries the name of an early settler. James McAfee, pronounced "Mac-a-fee," was a Scottish-Irish immigrant who settled in the Catawba Valley in the late 1730s. His knob rises to nearly 3,200 feet, making it reportedly the tallest mountain around the community of Catawba. The slopes have not always been wild. In the mid-1900s, much of Catawba Mountain was open farmland. Today the summit lies on the Appalachian Trail, the 2,190-mile footpath that follows the Appalachian Mountains between Georgia and Maine and is a unit of the National Park Service.

## The Walk Up

The shorter approach starts where the trail crosses Virginia Route 311 at the top of Catawba Mountain and heads north; the alternative is a 16-mile hike south from U.S. 220 in Troutville. The round trip is just under eight miles, with a climb the Roanoke Appalachian Trail Club puts at about 1,400 feet, most of it in the final 1.5 miles (other guides say 1,700), and most people take four to six hours. Near the start, hikers pass an information kiosk and cross four wooden walkways before reaching the Johns Spring Shelter, and just beyond it a spring where a white pipe pokes out of the rocks with a stream of water. Farther on, the path follows an old fire road near a high-voltage power line clearing, passes an overlook with the first view of the Catawba Valley, and meets the McAfee Knob Spur Trail. Less than 30 yards later, the world opens up. Because it is colder at the summit, hikers are advised to carry an extra layer, along with plenty of water and snacks.

## Two Hundred Seventy Degrees

From the ledge, the panorama sweeps across 270 degrees. The Catawba Valley spreads out below, North Mountain stands to the west, Tinker Cliffs lies to the north, and the Roanoke Valley opens to the east. The view has traveled far beyond Virginia. McAfee Knob was prominently featured in the 2015 film A Walk in the Woods, starring Robert Redford and Nick Nolte and based on Bill Bryson's 1998 book about hiking the trail, and the knob appears on the movie's poster. Sharp-eyed hikers have pointed out that the scene does not fit the order of a traditional northbound hike, given where the characters said they were beforehand. That same year, McAfee Knob appeared in the October 2015 issue of the UK travel magazine Wanderlust.

## Loved, and Looked After

Popularity has its price, and the knob has people guarding it. The National Park Service manages the trail with the Appalachian Trail Conservancy, other agencies and volunteer clubs, and volunteers of the Roanoke Appalachian Trail Club maintain more than 120 miles of the Appalachian Trail. In 2015, the McAfee Knob Task Force was founded, 18 members who help the conservancy's ridge runners patrol the area. In a single year the group logged more than 500 volunteer hours, counted over 9,000 visitors, spoke with more than 5,000 of them and hauled out 54 gallons of trash. The Roanoke Appalachian Trail Club's hiking guide says there is no camping or campfire on McAfee Knob or Tinker Cliffs, and camping elsewhere in the area is limited to designated sites such as the Johns Spring and Campbell shelters. It also says no alcohol, dogs on a leash no longer than six feet, and no parking along Route 311. Roanoke County says parking at the trailhead is extremely limited and strictly enforced, and it runs a weekend shuttle in season.

## Flight Context

McAfee Knob is at 37.3928°N, 80.0367°W on Catawba Mountain in Roanoke County, Virginia, at about 3,197 ft, about 10 miles northwest of downtown Roanoke as the crow flies. From the air, look for the long ridge of Catawba Mountain rising about 1,600 ft above the Catawba Valley, with the Roanoke Valley opening to the east; the bare rock ledge of the knob faces out over the valley. Stay at or above about 5,000 ft MSL to keep a comfortable margin over the ridge. Roanoke–Blacksburg Regional Airport (KROA) is only about 5 nm southeast, so check the sectional chart and expect to work with approach control before maneuvering nearby. Virginia Tech/Montgomery Executive Airport (KBCB) at Blacksburg is about 21 nm southwest. Ridge winds can produce turbulence on breezy days, and summer haze often softens the long views.
