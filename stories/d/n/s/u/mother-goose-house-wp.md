# Mother Goose House

A stone house in Hazard, Kentucky, built in the shape of a goose after a Thanksgiving dinner in the 1930s, which lost its head to the weather in 2021 and got it back that summer.

**Location:** 37.2728, -83.1930

**Categories:** Bed and breakfasts in Kentucky, Novelty buildings in Kentucky, Buildings and structures in Perry County, Kentucky, 1940 establishments in Kentucky, Buildings and structures completed in 1940, Tourist attractions in Perry County, Kentucky, Birds in popular culture, Hazard, Kentucky

## Introduction

It started with a Thanksgiving goose. In the 1930s, a Hazard, Kentucky, man named George Stacy brought one home for the holiday, and after his wife had skinned it, something about the bird's body gave him an idea: he would build a house in the shape of a goose. Construction began in 1935, and by 1940 the idea was standing in stone. The Mother Goose House has been watching over Hazard ever since, and when it briefly lost its head in 2021, the whole town noticed.

## A Bird Built of Stone

Construction began in 1935 and was completed in 1940. The result is a circular building whose top half rises into the upper body of a goose, neck and head included, with oval-shaped windows set into the walls. The building is made of rocks gathered from many American states and from Canada, which makes this odd bird something of a geological patchwork as well as a piece of folk architecture. It does not try to hide what it is. Anyone driving past sees a goose, and that has always been the point. The house is both a landmark and a home, a monument to one man's imagination that people could also live in.

## Market, Filling Station, Inn

Over the decades the goose has served many purposes. Before it became a bed and breakfast, it served as a filling station and, for many years, as a store called the Mother Goose Market. Its oddity made it famous well beyond eastern Kentucky. The house was featured on The Oprah Winfrey Show and on a Home and Garden Television program (local reports call it Outrageous Homes), and it reportedly made the pages of the New York Times. Closer to home, it earned a spot on the cover of Hazard-opoly, a board game based on the town. In recent years the Mother Goose House has operated as a bed and breakfast, which means visitors can do more than photograph it. They can spend the night inside the goose; check with the inn for current availability.

## The Day the Goose Lost Her Head

A wooden head on a stone body takes a beating from mountain weather, and since the late 1980s the goose's head has gone through many renovations, often because of leaking water. On March 24, 2021, the head fell off. The city of Hazard blamed "age and the stress added by the recent ice storms and flooding." The owner was undaunted, telling residents that "it wouldn't be Hazard without the Goose." The city of Hazard's official social media account called the house "beloved" and promised, "She will be back." The rebuild ran from mid-March into late August. The neck got new planks, the beak was replaced with a stronger plastic, and the eyes, which once glowed yellow, were changed to a much lighter orange. Inside the attic, workers repaired the flooring, the rafters and the main beam that holds up the head. On August 27, 2021, the goose got her head back.

## Flight Context

The Mother Goose House is in Hazard, Kentucky, at 37.273°N, 83.193°W, in the rugged hills of eastern Kentucky. The building is small, so plan a slow pass around 1,500 to 2,000 feet above the ridges, and look along the roads through town for the round stone building with the goose's neck and head on top. Wendell H.
