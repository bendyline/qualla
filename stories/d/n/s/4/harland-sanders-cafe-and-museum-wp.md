# Harland Sanders Café and Museum

The North Corbin roadside café where Harland Sanders perfected his fried chicken recipe, lost his traffic to a moved highway, and set off to build KFC.

**Location:** 36.9597, -84.0942

**Categories:** Commercial buildings completed in 1940, National Register of Historic Places in Laurel County, Kentucky, Museums established in 1990, Restaurants in Kentucky, KFC, Museums in Laurel County, Kentucky, Commercial buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in Kentucky, Restaurants established in 1940, Food museums in the United States, Biographical museums in Kentucky

## Introduction

"A very good place to stop en route to Cumberland Falls and the Great Smokies," the food critic Duncan Hines advised travelers in 1939. "Continuous 24-hour service. Sizzling steaks, fried chicken, country ham, hot biscuits." The listing in his guide, Adventures in Good Eating, pointed diners to a roadside restaurant in North Corbin, Kentucky, run by a man named Harland Sanders. That same year, the place burned down. What rose in its place is the building that still stands on the old highway: the café where Sanders developed the recipe that would make his face famous around the world.

## Supper at the Filling Station

Sanders came to North Corbin in 1930, when the Shell Oil Company offered him a service station there rent-free in exchange for a percentage of its sales. He began serving meals on the side, chicken dishes along with country ham and steaks, and at first he fed customers in his own living quarters next to the station. His local popularity grew. In 1935 Kentucky Governor Ruby Laffoon commissioned him a Kentucky Colonel, an honorary title rather than a military rank, and by 1937 Sanders's skill in the kitchen was well enough known that he built a proper restaurant, the Sanders Café, with seating for 142 people. Then, in 1939, came the Hines listing and, that November, the fire that destroyed the restaurant and motel.

## Built Again on U.S. 25

Construction on the present Sanders Café began soon after the fire, along with a motel, and Sanders ran the restaurant from 1940 to 1956. Its location was its fortune. The café sat on U.S. 25, the main north–south route through central Kentucky, and business boomed as travelers streamed past. It was here, in the café's kitchen during the 1940s, that Sanders developed the secret recipe that would become the foundation of Kentucky Fried Chicken. The building even held a model motel room. Then the road itself moved.

## When the Highway Left

The point where U.S. 25 divided into 25E and 25W sat in the literal middle of Sanders's properties. To give better access to the planned Interstate 75, that split was moved to a new 25E roadbed a mile to the north. Traffic bound for the Carolinas and the Georgia coast now bypassed the restaurant and the town entirely, and when I-75 was finished through the area in the early 1970s, it did the same for travelers heading to Atlanta and Florida. Sanders sold the café in 1956, at age 65. Left with his savings and a Social Security check of $105 a month, he threw himself into franchising his chicken. He had already sold his first franchise in 1952, to Pete Harman of South Salt Lake, Utah. Now he took to the road, visiting restaurants, cooking his chicken for the staff and often sleeping in the back of his car. The highway that took his customers away had sent him out to find new ones.

## Chicken and History Under One Roof

At some point after franchising began, a KFC franchise moved into the old café, using the original kitchen and converting the model motel room into larger restrooms. It kept frying chicken until 1988, when it closed for renovation. In the fall of 1990 the building reopened as a museum, the same year it was added to the National Register of Historic Places, on August 7. A modern KFC kitchen was built next door on part of the old motel land, and an entrance lobby filled with smaller museum displays joins the two. The model motel room was restored to its original state, and new restrooms were added at the back. The café closed in 2019 and reopened after a renovation completed in 2022. Visitors can step into Harland Sanders's office, see the kitchen display tied to his secret formula, browse the KFC memorabilia, and then order the chicken that started here.

## Flight Context

The Harland Sanders Café sits at 36.960°N, 84.094°W in North Corbin, Kentucky, on the old U.S. 25 corridor just north of Corbin. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL, follow the highway corridors through town; Interstate 75 runs nearby, the road whose arrival helped end the café's heyday. London-Corbin Airport/Magee Field (KLOZ) is about 7 nm north. Williamsburg-Whitley County Airport (KBYL) is about 11 nm south-southwest, and Lake Cumberland Regional (KSME) about 26 nm west-northwest.
