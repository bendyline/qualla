# Blue Grass Chemical Agent-Destruction Pilot Plant

Near Richmond, Kentucky, a purpose-built plant spent years dismantling rockets and shells filled with sarin, VX and mustard agent - and on July 7, 2023, destroyed the last chemical weapon in the United States stockpile.

**Location:** 37.7171, -84.2077

**Categories:** Chemical weapons destruction facilities, United States chemical weapons depots, Military installations in Kentucky, Buildings and structures in Madison County, Kentucky, 2010 establishments in Kentucky

## Introduction

The last one was a rocket. On July 7, 2023, at a plant near Richmond, Kentucky, workers destroyed an M55 rocket filled with GB nerve agent, better known as sarin. It was the final munition in the stockpile at the Blue Grass Army Depot, and with it went the last chemical weapon in the United States stockpile. The Blue Grass Chemical Agent-Destruction Pilot Plant, known as BGCAPP, had been built for exactly this job. It took two decades of design, construction, testing and patience to get there, and the plant spent the years that followed taking itself apart.

## Decades in Storage

Since 1944, the Army had stored 523 short tons of chemical agents at the depot: the nerve agents sarin and VX, plus mustard agent. They were loaded into 155mm projectiles, 8-inch projectiles and M55 rockets. The Blue Grass stockpile amounted to about 2 percent of the nation's original chemical weapons stockpile. Destroying it was a requirement of the Chemical Weapons Convention, an international treaty to which the United States is a party, and the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons monitored the country's progress. The Program Executive Office, Assembled Chemical Weapons Alternatives - PEO ACWA - oversaw the work. In June 2003, a joint venture of Bechtel National and Parsons Corporation, the Bechtel Parsons Blue Grass team, won the contract to design, build, systemize, operate and eventually close the plant.

## Missed Deadlines, Kept Promises

The timeline stretched. In March 2005 the schedule was extended to make the program more affordable year to year, and final designs for the remaining facilities were not completed until 2010. The treaty's deadline of April 29, 2012, came and went before Blue Grass could finish. Congress had set its own targets, too: legislation in 2007 required destruction of the remaining national stockpile by the April 2012 date and in no circumstances later than December 31, 2017, a deadline later pushed to December 31, 2023, by the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2016. The United States eventually committed to the OPCW to finish chemical weapons destruction by September 30, 2023. With that July 2023 rocket, it did.

## Taking Weapons Apart

The Department of Defense weighed four approaches: incineration, and three non-incineration methods built around chemical neutralization or electrochemical oxidation. It chose neutralization. Munitions were disassembled by modified reverse assembly, and the agent drained out and mixed with caustic and water, destroying it through hydrolysis. The resulting liquid, called hydrolysate, was held and tested to confirm the agent was gone. The original plan paired neutralization with supercritical water oxidation, but in 2020 officials decided instead to ship the nerve agent hydrolysate to Veolia North America near Port Arthur, Texas, which completed its destruction on August 28, 2023. Metal parts were heated above 1,000 degrees Fahrenheit for at least 15 minutes and then recycled, and exhaust gases passed through HEPA and carbon filters before release.

## Chambers From Sweden

Not every munition suited the main plant. X-rays showed that the mustard agent had significantly solidified inside its rounds, so the team turned to explosive destruction technology and chose a Static Detonation Chamber, which uses an electrically heated vessel to deflagrate a munition and destroy both agent and explosives. The chamber passed factory acceptance testing at the Dynasafe workshop in Kristinehamn, Sweden, in June 2015 and was installed in Kentucky in 2016. On June 7, 2019, it destroyed its first mustard-filled munition; on September 4, 2021, it destroyed the last of Kentucky's mustard 155mm projectiles. A second, larger chamber, the SDC 2000, broke ground on January 22, 2020, and began work on January 27, 2023, on rocket warheads containing residual sarin. The original chamber, renamed the SDC 1200, later took on warheads with residual VX, destroying the last of them on September 11, 2025.

## Closing the Plant

With the weapons gone, the plant turned to erasing itself. State environmental regulators approved the closure permit modification on September 27, 2023. Closure means disposing of contaminated and non-contaminated waste, decontaminating and demolishing facilities, returning real property to the Blue Grass Army Depot, closing environmental permits and closing out the contract. Both detonation chambers finished their work in 2025, and the team began preparing the main plant for demolition that year. By mid-2026 the CDC described the plant as undergoing demolition, and the plant's operator says demolition of the facilities will be complete by the end of 2026; the wider closure work, including closing out permits and the contract, is expected to run longer. The community's link to the project has narrowed as well. The Blue Grass Chemical Stockpile Outreach Office, long the public's main information source on chemical weapons destruction in Kentucky, closed its doors in April 2024, though its staff continued to answer questions and work with the governor-appointed Kentucky Chemical Demilitarization Citizens' Advisory Commission until the outreach program itself closed.

## Flight Context

The Blue Grass Chemical Agent-Destruction Pilot Plant is at approximately 37.7171 N, 84.2077 W within the Blue Grass Army Depot, southeast of Richmond in Madison County, Kentucky. The depot is an active military installation: check NOTAMs and charts for any restricted airspace or flight restrictions, and do not overfly at low altitude. From a respectful 4,000-5,000 ft MSL, the depot's large reservation stands out among the surrounding farmland. Nearby airports: Central Kentucky Regional Airport near Richmond (KRGA; about 8 nm southwest) and Blue Grass Airport, Lexington (KLEX; about 27 nm northwest, with scheduled service). London-Corbin Airport/Magee Field (KLOZ) is about 39 nm south. Expect haze in summer and scattered afternoon thunderstorms.
