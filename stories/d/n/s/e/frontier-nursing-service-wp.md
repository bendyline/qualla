# Frontier Nursing Service

Founded in 1925 by a mother who had lost both her children, the Frontier Nursing Service sent nurse-midwives on horseback into the mountains of eastern Kentucky and started one of the country's first graduate midwifery schools.

**Location:** 37.1653, -83.3792

**Categories:** History of nursing, Midwifery in the United States, Healthcare in Kentucky, Appalachia, National Register of Historic Places in Leslie County, Kentucky

## Introduction

Mary Breckinridge knew grief before she knew Leslie County. Her daughter, Polly, lived only a few hours. Her son, Breckie, died at the age of four. Moved by those losses to care for children in places where help was hardest to find, she came to the mountains of eastern Kentucky in 1925 and founded what became the Frontier Nursing Service. It was the first group in the United States to employ nurses who were also qualified midwives. A century later, the school it started, now Frontier Nursing University, still trains nurse-midwives and nurse practitioners, though it no longer does so in the mountains.

## Choosing the Hardest Place

Breckinridge, a 1910 graduate of St. Luke's Hospital nursing school in New York, had just seen the Highlands and Islands Medical Service at work in Scotland. Before choosing a site, she traveled through Leslie, Knott and Owsley counties, interviewing local midwives and recording each one's age, birthplace, literacy, years in practice, cleanliness and number of children raised. She settled on Leslie County for three reasons. It was remote and hard to reach, so success there would suggest success anywhere. The region lacked licensed physicians. And her well-known Kentucky family gave her a network of supporters across the state. The need was real: in the early 1920s, the national maternal death rate stood at roughly six to seven per 1,000 live births, and physicians, officials and reformers argued over whether doctors or midwives should oversee childbirth. The effort began under another name, the Kentucky Committee for Mothers and Babies.

## Nurses on Horseback

With two nurses, Edna Rockstroh and Freda Caffin, Breckinridge opened the first clinic in Hyden in 1925. In its first month, she and two nurse-midwives served 233 patients. By December she had raised a log house at Wendover, the Big House, as her home and headquarters. The Hyden Hospital and Health Center opened in 1928, followed by nine outpost nursing centers in Leslie County and Clay County's Red Bird River section. Breckinridge ignored county lines and placed the outposts nine to twelve miles apart along the waterways and natural routes of travel and trade. The nurses rode horseback, many to homes without running water or electricity, accompanied by couriers who knew the trails, helped set up supplies and made sure no nurse rode alone, even late at night. Because the country had no midwifery schools, early nurses came from Great Britain or were sent there on scholarship, promising two years of service in return. A 1931 documentary, The Forgotten Frontier, captured the work.

## A School Born of War

World War II closed the route to British training, so the service opened its own Frontier Graduate School of Midwifery. The first class began in November 1939, and the school has operated ever since. The six-month course followed the curriculum of the Central Midwives Board of England and Scotland, and every student had to attend 20 midwifery cases. Graduates spread far beyond Kentucky: by 1952, 122 were working across the U.S. and in countries including the Philippines, Japan, India and Thailand. By 1959, the service's nurse-midwives had attended more than 10,000 births. In the late 1960s, the school created what its history describes as the first certificate program to prepare family nurse practitioners, and in 1970 it became the Frontier School of Midwifery and Family Nursing. When births in Leslie County declined in the 1980s, it adopted a community-based distance model that let nurses train without leaving home, graduating more than 1,000 nurse-midwives.

## Leaving the Mountains

In 1972, the service opened the forty-bed Mary Breckinridge Hospital and Health Center in Leslie County, and in 1991 the Frontier Nursing Service complex was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The school earned accreditation to grant a Master of Science in Nursing in 2004 and became Frontier Nursing University in 2011. That same year, after years of operating in the red, the service sold the hospital to Appalachian Regional Healthcare. Then the university left. After buying a 67-acre campus in Versailles in 2017, it began moving its operations out of Leslie County in 2018. The university said the new site would keep it in a rural county near Lexington's airport, though the USDA classifies Woodford County as metropolitan, not rural. In August 2020 the university took a stained glass window from St. Christopher's Chapel in Hyden to Versailles, which drew a petition and a rebuke from Congressman Hal Rogers, who said the board's action, along with moving educational operations out of Leslie County, did not align with "Mary Breckinridge's legendary heart of service."

## Flight Context

The Frontier Nursing Service heartland lies around Hyden and Wendover in Leslie County, Kentucky, at approximately 37.17°N, 83.38°W, in remote Appalachian mountain country. From 3,500 to 5,000 feet AGL, follow the river valleys to the small town of Hyden, and maintain safe clearance over the surrounding ridges. Wendell H. Ford Airport (KCPF) near Hazard is about 14 nm northeast, Julian Carroll Airport (KJKL) at Jackson about 26 nm north, and London-Corbin/Magee Field (KLOZ) about 34 nm west. Morning valley fog and low ceilings are common in the mountains.
