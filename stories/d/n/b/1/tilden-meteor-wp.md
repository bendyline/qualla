# Tilden Meteor

On a July day in 1927, a humming bolide exploded over the village of Tilden, Illinois, and scattered cold stone fragments across fields in Randolph, St. Clair and Washington counties.

**Location:** 38.2000, -89.6833

**Categories:** Meteorite falls, Meteorites found in the United States, 20th-century astronomical events

## Introduction

Allan Raney was sitting on his front porch after dinner when the sky began to hum. It started as a rushing sound from somewhere high above, and it grew until it seemed to hang directly overhead, like "the humming sound made by the propeller of an airplane." Raney got up to investigate. Then came "a terrific explosion high overhead in the sky, then another and another." It was July 13, 1927, and a meteor was bursting apart in the air above Tilden, Illinois, in what the Illinois State Museum calls the first and largest meteorite to strike the state, at least in recorded history.

## A Hum, Then Thunder

Raney was far from the only witness. The Tilden Meteor was a bolide, a meteor that exploded in the air, and it was seen and heard by a large number of people. It approached from the southeast, humming like an airplane, before erupting in three distinctive sets of explosions that shook houses in the area. What followed was just as eerie. Fragments were seen plunging toward the ground, resembling, as one description put it, "a dark streak, like smoke, for an instant." People heard the pieces "shrieking" on their way down, and then heard the dull thuds of stone striking earth. The air burst scattered meteorite fragments across a radius of about 2 miles, turning an ordinary summer day in a small Randolph County village into a shower of rock from space.

## Cold to the Touch

Raney's account captures the moment of discovery in plain, vivid detail. As he stepped off the porch, he heard "a distinct shriek overhead" and saw something like a streak descend toward the earth, followed by a puff on the ground about 100 yards north of his neighbor John Stone's house. He walked over to look. There, to his astonishment, was a wedge-shaped piece of what appeared to be rock, buried about a foot deep in the soil. He studied it for a while before cautiously touching it with a finger. It was not hot. As he put it, he "found it was cold, much cooler to the touch than any ordinary material of its kind found lying on the ground. In fact it felt as if it had just come out of a refrigerator."

## Pieces of the Sky

Three large fragments of the meteorite, weighing about 110, 46 and 9 pounds, were recovered along with a smaller fourth piece, immediately after the fall, each of the big three in a different county. That speed matters: these were stones seen falling and collected at once, not rocks turned up by chance years later. The event earned the Tilden Meteor its place as the first and largest meteorite known to have struck Illinois, at least in recorded history, and as a meteorite fall witnessed by a crowd rather than a lone observer. Tilden itself remains a small place, a village in Randolph County with about 750 residents at the 2020 census. On a quiet day there, it is not hard to imagine the porches and yards where people once looked up at a humming sky and watched pieces of it come down.
