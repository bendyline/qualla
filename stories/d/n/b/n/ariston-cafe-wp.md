# Ariston Café

A 1935 brick cafe on Old Route 66 in Litchfield, Illinois, founded by a Greek immigrant restaurateur and run by his family for decades, now on the National Register of Historic Places.

**Location:** 39.1772, -89.6681

**Categories:** National Register of Historic Places in Montgomery County, Illinois, Litchfield, Illinois, Buildings and structures on U.S. Route 66, U.S. Route 66 in Illinois, Commercial buildings completed in 1935, Restaurants in Illinois, Restaurants established in 1935, Retail buildings in Illinois, Commercial buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in Illinois, 1935 establishments in Illinois

## Introduction

Work began on April 4, 1935, and by July 5 the building was finished: a modest brick restaurant in Litchfield, Illinois, with a curved parapet across its front and a pair of fuel pumps out by the road. Contractor Henry A. Vasel built it for two experienced restaurateurs, Pete Adam and Tom Cokinos. More than ninety years later, the Ariston Café still stands along Old U.S. Route 66, listed on the National Register of Historic Places and, by its own reckoning, one of the oldest, if not the oldest, Route 66 restaurants anywhere.

## Following the Highway

The Ariston's story starts a town away. Pete Adam, a Greek immigrant, founded the Ariston Cafe in Carlinville, Illinois, in 1924, and he had been running a restaurant for more than a decade by the time the Litchfield building went up. According to the café's own history, it relocated to Litchfield in 1929, although the National Park Service ties the move to the post-1930 realignment that bypassed Carlinville. The road itself was shifting too: after 1930, a realignment moved U.S. Route 66 in Illinois farther east. In 1935, Adam and his partner Tom Cokinos committed to a purpose-built home on the highway in Litchfield, the building that still serves customers today. For a business that depended on travelers, being on the right road mattered, and the Ariston has stayed at this location on Route 66 ever since.

## Brick, Booths and a Curved Parapet

Nobody built the Ariston to be an architectural showpiece. It does not follow any particular style; it was built in a utilitarian manner common to small commercial buildings of its day. Look closer, though, and the craftsmanship shows. Outside, the most distinctive features are the curved parapet wall on the front façade and the fine, varied brickwork. Inside, the booths carry echoes of Art Deco, the popular style of the time. Like many Route 66 businesses, the café originally had two fuel pumps out front, serving cars as well as the people in them. Over the years there have been a few changes, including the addition of a banquet room, but the building still holds the character of the era in which it was built, a representative of the kind of business that once thrived along the old highway.

## A Family at the Counter

For decades, the Ariston was a family affair. Beginning in 1966, Pete's son Nick and his wife, Demi, carried on the traditional service their family had built. Recognition followed. In 1992, the Ariston Café and the Adam family were inducted into the Illinois Route 66 Hall of Fame, and on May 5, 2006, the building was added to the National Register of Historic Places. The Adam era ended in July 2018, when the café was purchased by new owners who took over a business with nearly a century of history behind it. The booths, the brick and the parapet remain, and so does the café's place on Old Route 66, where it has fed travelers since 1935.
