# Cloudland Canyon State Park

On the western edge of Lookout Mountain, a gorge more than 1,000 feet deep drops through sandstone and shale to two waterfalls, in a Georgia state park the Civilian Conservation Corps helped build in a corner of the state once reachable only through Tennessee or Alabama.

**Location:** 34.8403, -85.4829

**Categories:** State parks of Georgia (U.S. state), Lookout Mountain, Canyons and gorges of Georgia (U.S. state), Civilian Conservation Corps in Georgia (U.S. state), State parks of the Appalachians, Waterfalls

## Introduction

Until 1939, the only way into this corner of Georgia ran through another state. To reach the area, and much of Dade County around it, travelers had to come in through Tennessee or Alabama. In that remote corner lies one of Georgia's most dramatic landscapes: a gorge more than 1,000 feet deep, gouged into the western edge of Lookout Mountain by Sitton Gulch Creek. It was once known as Sitton Gulch, or Sitton Gulf, or Trenton Gulf. Since 1939 it has been Cloudland Canyon State Park, and it ranks among the largest and most scenic parks in Georgia.

## Built by the CCC

Georgia began buying the land in the late 1930s, piece by piece from private owners, among them the Mathews, McCauley and McKaig families. During the Great Depression, under President Franklin D. Roosevelt, the Civilian Conservation Corps did much of the early work, building the first facilities, signs and access roads, and the park opened in 1939. It has never really stopped growing. Expansion continues sporadically whenever new land can be bought, and the park that began with 1,924 acres now covers about 3,500 (3,538 by the state's current count). The park lists 18 cottages, 10 yurts and a group lodge for overnight stays, along with 75 tent, trailer and RV sites, 30 walk-in campsites and 13 backcountry sites for backpackers. An interpretive center near the main parking lot is open on weekends, and the park has noted a new visitor center under construction. Past calendars have featured a Wildflower Program and Adventure Weekend in April and a Kids' Catfish Rodeo in May.

## Where Two Creeks Carve a Canyon

The park sits on the Cumberland Plateau, atop Lookout Mountain. Up on the summit, Daniel Creek and Bear Creek each cut their own gorges through the rock before converging to form Sitton Gulch Creek. The result is a canyon ranging in elevation from 800 feet to more than 1,800. Its walls are mostly sandstone, and the shale layers beneath are marked by pine trees. At the bottom, a talus slope of broken sandstone and shale spills toward a valley floor rich in fossil-bearing limestone. Geologically, Lookout Mountain is a transition zone. The flat-lying sedimentary beds of central Tennessee lie to one side, and to the east, beyond Chickamauga Valley, rise the narrow Armuchee ridges, which show more intensive folding and faulting than the flat-topped plateau.

## Six Hundred Steps Down

The most popular way into the gorge is the Waterfalls Trail. It starts on pavement at the main overlook, then drops into the canyon along a 40-degree slope, mostly on gravel and a staircase of some 600 steps. At the bottom, two waterfalls pour across layers of sandstone and shale into small pools, and the water continues down a boulder-strewn stream bed. How much water falls depends on the season, since the creek's flow varies considerably through the year. From the foot of the stairs, the Sitton's Gulch Trail follows the creek all the way out to the canyon mouth in Trenton, a strenuous 6-mile round trip by the park's count. Those who prefer to stay high can find canyon views from the picnic area parking lot and all along the rim.

## Rims, Hemlocks and Wet Crossings

The West Rim Loop, about 5 miles round trip, is rated moderately strenuous by the park, with a few short, steep and rocky stretches. Starting from the Daniels Creek Bridge, it opens panoramic views before climbing out of the canyon onto the plateau, taking in the gorge itself, the town of Trenton below and neighboring Sand Mountain. Rhododendron and mountain laurel thickets mix with sour gum and dogwood, while large oaks, hickories, hemlocks and maples shade the path. Longer still is the Bear Creek Trail, which Wikipedia describes as roughly nine miles and the longest in the park. It reopened in the fall of 2011 after nearly a decade closed, and its crossing of Bear Creek has no bridge, which can make it difficult in wet seasons. A longer link now runs beyond the park: the roughly 34-mile River to Clouds Route, unveiled in November 2023, connects it to the Riverwalk in St. Elmo in Chattanooga.

## Flight Context

Cloudland Canyon lies at 34.8403°N, 85.4829°W on the western edge of Lookout Mountain in northwest Georgia. The canyon rim tops out above 1,800 feet MSL, so plan to overfly at 3,500 to 4,500 feet MSL; from there the gorge appears as a deep, forested notch cut into the flat plateau, opening west toward the town of Trenton in the valley below, with Sand Mountain across the valley. Chattanooga Metropolitan Airport-Lovell Field (KCHA) is about 18 nm to the northeast. Closer in, Barwick LaFayette Airport (FAA identifier 9A5, no ICAO code) is about 13 nm southeast, and Marion County Airport-Brown Field (KAPT) near Jasper, Tennessee, is about 14 nm north-northwest. Ridge-top winds can be gusty and low cloud or fog can settle in the valleys, especially on cool mornings.
