# Ellison's Cave

Beneath Pigeon Mountain in northwest Georgia, Ellison's Cave holds more than 12 miles of passage and two of the deepest pits in the contiguous United States, a vertical world that rewards preparation and punishes its absence.

**Location:** 34.6649, -85.3637

**Categories:** Caves of Georgia (U.S. state), Landforms of Walker County, Georgia

## Introduction

At the lip of Fantastic Pit, the floor is 586 feet below. That single drop, inside a mountain in northwest Georgia, is one of the deepest pits in the contiguous United States, and it is only one of the vertical spaces hidden in Ellison's Cave. The system winds through more than 12 miles of known passage beneath Pigeon Mountain in Walker County, and it reaches a vertical extent of 1,063 feet, enough to rank it among the 20 deepest caves in the country (12th by one count, 19th by another). Cavers from across the United States know it well and visit often, and what they find is a world built on the vertical.

## Fantastic and Incredible

Two giants sit on opposite sides of the cave. Fantastic Pit drops 586 feet and Incredible Pit 440, and both rank among the deepest pits in the contiguous United States. Fantastic is technically one of several rappel points into a massive canyon called TAG Hall. It marks the canyon's upstream end, while All-in-One, at 374 feet, is the farthest downstream, and the Smokey I rig point, at 500 feet, sits midway between them. Elsewhere in the cave are New Pit at 256 feet, Smokey II Pit at 262 feet, Snowball Dome at 265 feet and Broken Dome at 281 feet. Rankings differ by source: one list puts Fantastic second in the United States and Incredible sixth, while the New Georgia Encyclopedia has called Fantastic the deepest straight cave drop in the continental United States. By the first list, no U.S. cave makes the global top 50 for deep pits, a list dominated by caves in eastern Europe, China, Mexico and Iran.

## A Mountain Split Along a Fault

Ellison's is a solutional cave, and it lies within a bedrock fault in Pigeon Mountain. The story of that fault reaches back to the Ordovician Period, when the tectonic subduction that built the Appalachians left a chain of seismically active fault lines stretching from northern Alabama to eastern Tennessee. Continued mountain building fractured the bedrock into a large fault zone across the southern Appalachians and northern Georgia. Those fractures, combined with thick, uniform beds of limestone, contribute to the cave's notable depth. Water still moves through the system: after its journey through the cave, it resurges at a natural spring at the foot of the mountain called the Blue Hole.

## When the Rope Becomes a Trap

The same waterfalls and long drops that draw cavers can turn deadly. On March 10, 1999, a caver climbing the 440-foot Incredible Pit became tangled in a second, parallel rope and was stranded in the torrent of the waterfall about 140 feet above the floor. Unable to free himself or call effectively for help, he died of hypothermia and suspension trauma. On February 12, 2011, two University of Florida students with no caving experience died of hypothermia after becoming stranded on rope in the frigid waterfall of the 125-foot Warm-Up Pit. They had come with a group of eight others from a university church club that carried only one harness and one set of ascenders among them, and a post-accident review found that the tragedy had been avoidable with proper equipment and basic training. Rescues have tested the cave's volunteers too. In 2013, an injured caver received a blood transfusion from the attending medic in the low crawlway stream that meanders through the lower cave, one of only a few known in-cave transfusions at the time, before rescuers hauled him up Fantastic Pit and Warm-Up Pit.

## Permits and Preparation

The Georgia Department of Natural Resources owns and manages the cave and the land around it, part of the Crockford-Pigeon Mountain Wildlife Management Area. The DNR says caving trails close during firearm deer seasons, cavers must sign in and out at the cave kiosk, and visitors to the area need a hunting or fishing license or a Lands Pass, so check the agency's current rules before any trip. The more important requirements are not on paper. In 2016 a 22-year-old caver with improper ascending gear became so exhausted that rescuers had to haul him up both Fantastic and Warm-Up pits; he and his group, reports found, lacked basic knowledge of rope rigging. He walked down the mountain unharmed. Ellison's rewards those who arrive trained and equipped, and it offers little margin to anyone who does not.

## Flight Context

Ellison's Cave lies at 34.6649°N, 85.3637°W on Pigeon Mountain in Walker County, northwest Georgia; the cave itself is invisible from the air, so use the mountain as your reference. The Blue Hole spring sits at the mountain's foot. A viewing altitude of 4,000 feet MSL or higher keeps a comfortable margin over the ridges. Barwick LaFayette Airport (FAA identifier 9A5) is the closest field, about 4 nm east-northeast. Chattanooga Metropolitan Airport (KCHA) lies about 24 nm north-northeast, and Richard B. Russell Airport (KRMG) at Rome about 21 nm south-southeast. Clear days give the best view of the ridgeline.
