# Berry College

Founded on 83 inherited acres where students worked in exchange for schooling, Berry College grew into a vast campus of forest and mountain outside Rome, Georgia, where even bald eagles get a say in where the stadium goes.

**Location:** 34.3075, -85.2136

**Categories:** Berry College, Liberal arts colleges in Georgia (U.S. state), Universities and colleges established in 1902, Historic districts on the National Register of Historic Places in Georgia (U.S. state), Protected areas of Floyd County, Georgia

## Introduction

In 2012, Berry College had a site picked out for its new football stadium near the Cage Center. Then a pair of bald eagles built a nest nearby. The birds came back and successfully raised chicks in 2013 and again in 2014, and the college responded by moving the stadium to a new location well removed from the nest. The eagles have since become a symbol of the school. The decision fits a place where the land itself has always been part of the lesson, a college that began with one determined woman, a few inherited acres and an unusual bargain.

## Eighty-Three Acres and a Bargain

In 1902, Martha Berry, daughter of a prominent local business owner, founded the Boys Industrial School on 83 acres of land she had inherited from her father, near Rome in northwestern Georgia. Her terms were simple. Students would receive an education, and in exchange they would work to help build, run and maintain the school itself. Their labor kept construction and operating costs low. In 1904 the school became an approved school of the Daughters of the American Revolution, and in 1909 the Martha Berry School for Girls joined it; together they became known as the Berry Schools. The institution kept climbing. It became a junior college in 1926, then a senior college, and graduated its first class in 1932.

## From Free Labor to LifeWorks

The 1930s brought dramatic growth. Helped by a large donation from Henry Ford, the campus expanded to nearly 30,000 acres during that decade. Berry College earned accreditation from the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools in 1957, and in the 1960s it made two significant changes: it began paying student workers instead of crediting their accounts, and it ended mandatory religious services. Martha Berry's founding idea still shapes daily life. Through the LifeWorks program, every student who wants a campus job is guaranteed one, and the work continues to help offset the cost of tuition. Today the college offers bachelor's, master's and education specialist degrees through four schools, and the college now enrolls more than 2,200 students.

## A Campus with a Mountain in It

Visitors enter through the Gate of Opportunity, and what lies beyond is more wilderness than quad. The campus spans roughly 27,000 acres of fields, forests and Lavender Mountain, which Berry bills as the world's largest college campus. The campus is home to a large deer population, with estimates ranging from 1,500 to 2,500 animals. The Georgia Department of Natural Resources oversees about 16,000 acres, conducting managed hunts and offering recreation, while the land around the academic buildings and public spaces is a wildlife refuge where no hunting is allowed. More than 80 miles of trails for hiking, biking and horseback riding, along with two disc golf courses, are open to the public. In September 2011, Travel+Leisure ranked Berry among the most beautiful college campuses in the United States, singling out its many fountains and pools set among English Gothic-style buildings.

## Hollywood's Gothic Backlot

Those buildings have had long second careers on screen. Parts of Remember the Titans and Sweet Home Alabama were filmed here, and Disney's Perfect Harmony (1991) was shot almost entirely on campus, at Oak Hill, Frost Chapel, the Old Mill and the Ford Buildings. A short scene from Dutch was filmed at Berry, as were scenes for the Fox series The Following, starring Kevin Bacon. The Constantine television series used the Ford Buildings, the Old Mill and the main campus near Victory Lake as locations. More recently, the Netflix series Stranger Things filmed parts of its fourth season at the Ford Complex, carrying a campus first built by working students onto screens around the world.

## Vikings, Valhalla and the Eagles' Veto

Berry's teams are the Vikings, competing in NCAA Division III as a founding member of the Southern Athletic Association since 2012–13. They have collected national titles, including three NAIA women's soccer championships and three IHSA equestrian championships. Football arrived only in the fall of 2013, after a Board of Trustees vote that proved controversial; because of the expense and the school's traditions, a significant portion of students, faculty and alumni opposed it. The team needed a home, which is where the eagles came in. With the original site near the Cage Center ruled out, the relocation was announced in May 2014, construction at the new location began that fall, and the stadium, fittingly named Valhalla, opened for the 2015 season. It now serves football, track and lacrosse.

## Flight Context

Berry College lies at approximately 34.308°N, 85.214°W, in the Mount Berry community adjacent to Rome, Georgia, about 60 miles northwest of Atlanta and 51 miles south of Chattanooga. From 3,000–4,500 feet MSL, the campus reads as a huge block of forest and open fields rising onto Lavender Mountain beside the city. Richard B. Russell Regional Airport (KRMG) at Rome is about 4 miles away, and Tom B. David Field at Calhoun (KCZL) is about 19 miles. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms, and mind the ridgelines of Lavender Mountain in low visibility.
