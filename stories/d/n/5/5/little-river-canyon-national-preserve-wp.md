# Little River Canyon National Preserve

On top of Alabama's Lookout Mountain, a river that runs along the mountain rather than down it has cut one of the Southeast's deepest canyons, with 600-foot sandstone cliffs, Alabama's tallest above-ground waterfall and the rusting remnants of a failed canyon ski resort.

## Introduction

Rivers usually carve their way down off mountains. The Little River does something stranger: for almost its entire length it flows along the middle of Lookout Mountain in northeast Alabama, high on the mountain's back, and over eons it has cut one of the Southeast's deepest canyons into the rock. It is sometimes said to be the nation's longest mountaintop river. Since 1992, when an Act of Congress created Little River Canyon National Preserve, 15,288 acres of that landscape near Fort Payne have been protected by the National Park Service.

## May's Gulf

Long before it was a national preserve, the canyon was known as May's Gulf. "Gulf" is a common word throughout the Cumberland Plateau for this kind of deep, steep-sided gorge. The river that made it begins in Georgia. Its East Fork runs 17 miles and its West Fork 25 before they join to form the main stem, which then flows another 23 miles through the canyon and off the mountain to empty into the Coosa River at Weiss Lake, near Leesburg, Alabama. Local legend holds that a minor Civil War skirmish took place on the canyon rim. Before the Park Service took charge, the canyon formed the southernmost unit of Alabama's DeSoto State Park.

## Six Hundred Feet of Sandstone

Sandstone cliffs rise as much as 600 feet above the narrow canyon floor, and many of the best views come from the Little River Canyon Rim Parkway, a scenic drive along the western rim that follows Alabama State Route 176; the Park Service describes the drive through the preserve as about eleven miles, running from Little River Falls to Eberhart Point, where the paved rim road was dedicated in June 1954. Below, the Little River is said to be among the cleanest and wildest waterways in the South. It is undammed except for a small, derelict hydroelectric project at DeSoto Falls on the West Fork near Mentone. That is one of the river's three major waterfalls. Little River Falls marks the beginning of the canyon, and the seasonal Grace's High Falls pours 133 feet into the side canyon cut by Bear Creek, which the Park Service calls the tallest above-ground waterfall in Alabama.

## The Ski Lift That Wasn't

The canyon bears the marks of some unlikely ambitions. In the late 1960s the State of Alabama permitted a private consortium of Fort Payne businessmen to build a ski lift and amusement park project here. It was abandoned after a couple of years, but not before crews cut a vehicle access road down into the gorge. That road, now a rough, eroded path from Eberhart Point above the confluence of Bear Creek and the Little River, is the most convenient way to reach the canyon floor, and debris from the project still lies in Pine Tree Hole at the bottom. A second venture followed. From the 1970s into the early 1980s, a commercial mini-theme park called Canyonland Park operated in the area, complete with a zoo and a lift. Some of the lift pedestals still stand.

## Down to Pine Tree Hole

Getting into the canyon takes effort. From the bottom of the Eberhart Point path, an unmaintained but fairly well-defined trail runs about eight miles downstream to the canyon mouth. Heading upstream is far harder and suits only hikers used to bushwhacking. Practiced whitewater paddlers carry their boats down to Pine Tree Hole to run the river. Camping is not permitted in the canyon, and ATVs are no longer allowed. Hunters need a license from Alabama or any other state, and anglers need an Alabama fishing license, with net and seine fishing prohibited. At the canyon mouth, a day-use area where a campground once operated offers picnic spots and is a popular place to swim.

## A Preserve Under Pressure

The National Park Service runs few visitor facilities within the preserve. DeSoto State Park, operated by the Alabama State Park System, lies inside the preserve boundaries and provides a lodge, a restaurant and several campgrounds. Jacksonville State University's Little River Canyon Center, a 23,000-square-foot LEED-certified building, serves as a visitor center. Protection has limits, though. The land around the canyon lies outside the preserve, and vacation houses and subdivision development have been reported pressing in on its edges.

## Flight Context

The preserve centers on 34.4406°N, 85.5956°W atop Lookout Mountain in northeast Alabama, east of Fort Payne. From 3,000–4,000 feet AGL the canyon is a dramatic dark slot in the mountaintop, and the Little River Canyon Rim Parkway traces its western rim; follow the gorge downstream to where the river leaves the mountain for Weiss Lake near Leesburg. Isbell Field at Fort Payne (FAA 4A9) is about 6.5 nm west-northwest, and Cloudmont Airpark (FAA AL09) near Mentone, a private-use field, is about 6 nm north. Richard B. Russell Regional Airport (KRMG) at Rome, Georgia, is about 22 nm east-southeast, and Chattanooga's Lovell Field (KCHA) about 41 nm north-northeast. Mountaintop winds can bring turbulence over the canyon, and morning fog may fill it in cool months.
