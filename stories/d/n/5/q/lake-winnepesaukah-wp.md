# Lake Winnepesaukah

Opened by Carl and Minette Dixon in 1925 on a small lake in Rossville, Georgia, Lake Winnie has grown from a place to swim and boat into a classic midway park, home to what is considered the oldest operating mill chute in the United States.

## Introduction

Carl Dixon designed the Boat Chute himself. When the water ride opened in 1927 it became a fixture of the little amusement park he and his wife, Minette, had built around a lake in Rossville, Georgia, just south of Chattanooga. Nearly a century later, the National Amusement Park Historical Association considers it the oldest operating mill chute in the United States. The park around it, Lake Winnepesaukah, or simply Lake Winnie to generations of visitors, has changed a great deal since then. Remarkably, the family that founded it still owns it.

## A Lake and a Borrowed-Sounding Name

In 1924 the Dixons bought about 100 acres surrounding a 9-acre lake in Rossville. They opened their park on June 1, 1925, and more than 5,000 visitors reportedly came when it opened for boating, fishing and picnicking. The Dixons chose the name Winnepesaukah, which supposedly came from a Cherokee word meaning "beautiful lake of the highlands." The name closely resembles that of New Hampshire's Lake Winnipesaukee, which has an Abenaki origin and was at one time believed to carry the same meaning, so the story of the name is best taken with a grain of salt. What is certain is that the park has been in Dixon hands since the day it opened.

## Swimmers First

In its early years, Lake Winnie was mostly about the water. In 1926 the Dixons opened a 22,000-square-foot swimming pool, described as the largest in the southeastern United States at the time. The Boat Chute followed in 1927. Over the 1940s and 1950s, a number of flat rides joined the midway. Then, in the 1960s, came the roller coasters: first the Mad Mouse in 1960, and then, in 1967, the Cannon Ball, a wooden coaster from designer John Allen. Built by the Philadelphia Toboggan Company, it reaches a top speed of 50 miles per hour over 2,272 feet of track and has become the park's staple attraction.

## Sixty-Eight Painted Steeds

The oldest ride at the park is not the Boat Chute but a carousel that is older than the park itself. Philadelphia Toboggan Company carousel number 39 was manufactured in 1916, and the park marks 2026 as the 110th anniversary of the antique carousel, which carries 68 hand-painted steeds. Some rides arrived with their own history. Several rides from the original Miracle Strip Amusement Park in Panama City Beach, Florida, including its Big Eli Ferris wheel, have found a new home at Lake Winnie, and when the revived Miracle Strip at Pier Park closed in 2015, many of its rides moved to Lake Winnie permanently. For years the park also ran an Eyerly Fly-O-Plane, which was taken out of service in July 2016 after two children were ejected from it and injured, and it no longer operates.

## Midway Traditions and Modern Thrills

Lake Winnie is laid out like a classic American fair, with a midway of food, games and rides. In autumn the park runs Spookah nights and Family By Day weekends. The 21st century brought new thrills, including the drop tower OH-Zone! and an inverting ride called Fire Ball, and in 2013 the park added SoakYa, a 5-acre water park. The old Pipeline Plunge slides, refurbished before the 2016 season as the Zoom Flume, now count as part of it. Today the park advertises more than 40 rides, shows and attractions, including the water park. Beginning with the 2026 season, IB Parks & Entertainment operates the park under a long-term agreement, while the Dixon family, the founders' descendants, still owns the place Carl and Minette opened more than a hundred years ago.

## Flight Context

Lake Winnepesaukah sits at 34.9764°N, 85.2472°W in Rossville, Georgia, just south of Chattanooga, Tennessee. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet MSL, look for the small 9-acre lake with the park's rides clustered around it. Chattanooga Metropolitan Airport (KCHA) is only about 4 nm north-northeast, so expect airline traffic nearby. Collegedale Municipal Airport (KFGU) lies about 12 nm east-northeast. Clear mornings give the best view of the lake.
