# Rock City (attraction)

Atop Lookout Mountain in Georgia, Rock City turned a maze of giant boulders into a gnome-studded garden trail in 1932, then made itself famous with "See Rock City" painted on hundreds of barns across 19 states.

## Introduction

For decades, drivers across the Southeast and Midwest kept meeting the same three words painted on barn roofs and walls: See Rock City. The owners, Garnet and Frieda Carter, hired a painter named Clark Byers in 1935 to spread the message, and by 1969 he had painted more than 900 barns in 19 states. The destination at the end of all those miles was a garden trail threaded through giant boulders on top of Lookout Mountain, in Lookout Mountain, Georgia. It had opened in May 1932, and it grew out of a real estate plan and a trail one woman marked out with string.

## A Citadel of Rocks

Long before anyone charged admission, people knew about these rocks. Cherokee people lived on and around Lookout Mountain, and in 1823 two missionaries, Daniel S. Butrick and William Chamberlain, came to minister to them. On August 28 of that year, Butrick wrote in his journal of "a citadel of rocks" on top of the mountain, noting the immense size of the boulders and how they were arranged "as to afford streets and lanes." By the time the Civil War reached the mountain's slopes, the place was already being called Rock City. During the Civil War, a Union diarist and a Confederate diarist each recorded in a journal the claim that seven states could be seen from the summit. Through the late 19th century, hikers and geologists knew Rock City well.

## Fairyland and a Ball of String

Garnet Carter's plan was a residential neighborhood on the mountaintop. He named it Fairyland, after his wife Frieda's interest in European folklore. The development was meant to have a golf course, but building one took too long, so Garnet built a miniature golf course instead, then franchised the idea as Tom Thumb Golf, which Rock City bills as the nation's first miniature golf course, though earlier mini-golf courses are documented. Fairyland covered 700 acres, and Rock City lay within it. Frieda set out to turn the rocks into one great garden. Taking string, she marked a trail that wound among the giant formations and ended at Lover's Leap, planting wildflowers along the way and placing imported German gnome statues and other fairytale characters at spots along the path. Garnet realized that Frieda had created something people would pay to see, and in 1932 the couple opened Rock City to the public. The original clubhouse and ten cottages survive as part of the Lookout Mountain Fairyland Club.

## Seven States from Lover's Leap

The barn roofs made Rock City famous, and the view gave it a boast. Since its earliest days, the attraction has claimed that seven states are visible from Lover's Leap. When a University of Tennessee scientist was asked to weigh in on the claim in 2007, he noted that it refers to seeing mountains and other high points in those states, and that it was made long before the air pollution that came with the spread of automobiles and coal-fired power plants. His verdict was mild: "I never thought it significant." The claim, like the barns, had already done its work.

## Squeezes, Bridges and Blacklight

A walking trail of about 4,100 feet winds through gardens with hundreds of labeled local trees and plants and past rock formations with names to match their oddity. Among them are Balanced Rock, said to weigh roughly 1,000 tons, and Fat Man's Squeeze. Along the way are Standing Stone Gardens, Rainbow Hall, the Swing-a-long Bridge, Stone Bridge, Cave of the Winds, Sky Bridge, Needle's Eye and Goblin's Underpass. Two caves, Fairyland Caverns and Mother Goose Village, glow with blacklight-responsive sculptures, and the High Falls, a man-made waterfall, reportedly drops about 140 feet into a small pool. The calendar stays busy, with seasonal birds-of-prey shows that began in 2006, the Enchanted Pumpkin Patch each fall and the Enchanted Garden of Lights from mid-November through December. Rock City has found its way into fiction, too, from Neil Gaiman's novel American Gods to the Drama Desk Award-winning musical See Rock City & Other Destinations, which premiered Off-Broadway in 2010.

## Flight Context

Rock City sits at 34.9739°N, 85.3486°W on top of Lookout Mountain in Lookout Mountain, Georgia, near Chattanooga. From 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL, follow the mountain's long ridgeline; the gardens and boulders are hard to pick out among the trees, but the mountaintop is unmistakable. Chattanooga Metropolitan Airport (KCHA) lies about 8 nm east-northeast, and Marion County Airport-Brown Field (KAPT) about 13 nm west-northwest. Clear days are essential if you want to test the seven-state claim; haze quickly shortens the view.
