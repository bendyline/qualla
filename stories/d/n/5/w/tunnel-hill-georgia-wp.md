# Tunnel Hill, Georgia

A northwest Georgia town that renamed itself twice after the railroad tunnel bored through its ridge, Tunnel Hill later hosted William T. Sherman's headquarters as he planned the opening battles of the Atlanta campaign.

**Location:** 34.8458, -85.0431

**Categories:** Cities in Georgia (U.S. state), Cities in Whitfield County, Georgia, Dalton metropolitan area, Georgia, Populated places established in 1848, 1848 establishments in Georgia (U.S. state)

## Introduction

Before the railroad came, this corner of northwest Georgia was called Doe Run. Then the state-owned Western & Atlantic Railroad set out to run its line through Chetoogeta Mountain, and the community renamed itself for the hole in the hill: first as Tunnelsville, when it incorporated on March 4, 1848, and then, from 1856, as Tunnel Hill. The name has outlasted the tunnel's working life. At the 2020 census 963 people lived here, and the original bore, long since replaced by a newer one, is open to the public as a historic site.

## A Quarter-Mile Through the Ridge

The Chetoogeta Mountain Tunnel was the reason for everything. Built in the late 1840s, with its two headings driven through on October 31, 1849, and completed in 1850, it carried the Western & Atlantic's tracks through the ridge rather than over it. Mark A. Cooper, an industrialist from Etowah, performed the dedication on the railroad's behalf. Sources differ on its exact length, giving figures from 1,447 feet (on the state historical marker) to 1,497 feet, with the museum's own site saying 1,477, but either way it ran more than a quarter-mile through the mountain, and it was the first major railroad tunnel in the South. The mountain appears on USGS maps as "Tunnel Hill Ridge," and the town grew up in rugged country along its western base.

## Where the Waters Part

The ridge is more than an obstacle for trains. The Tennessee Valley Divide runs along its top, splitting the land between two watersheds, one draining to the Tennessee River and the other to the Conasauga River. Below it, the town covers only about two square miles. Tunnel Hill straddles two counties, lying in northwest Whitfield County and reaching into southern Catoosa County, and it counts as part of the Dalton metropolitan area. U.S. Route 41 runs through on its way between Ringgold to the northwest and Dalton to the southeast, while Georgia State Route 201 heads northeast to Varnell. The Georgia General Assembly incorporated Tunnel Hill as a town in 1856, the same year it traded Tunnelsville for its present name.

## Sherman at the Austin House

War came to Tunnel Hill again and again. Throughout the Civil War, homes around the town served as part of a major hospital system, and the area hosted many engagements and camps. The fighting here fell on September 11, 1863; on February 23 and 24–25, 1864; on May 5–7, 1864; and finally on March 3, 1865. The town's most famous wartime resident was a temporary one. Union General William T. Sherman made the Clisby Austin House his headquarters while he planned attacks against nearby Dalton and Resaca. Those clashes, beginning with the Battle of Rocky Face Ridge in May 1864, opened what would later be known as the Atlanta campaign, so the campaign's first moves were directed from a house in a small railroad town.

## Two Tunnels, One Name

Between 1926 and 1928 a second tunnel, 1,557 feet long, was built, and it remains in active use by CSX Transportation. The original was renovated between 1998 and 2000 and is now open to the public as a historic site. Tunnel Hill keeps its past close in other ways too. The town has a Heritage Center and Museum, and an annual battle reenactment, held in September, has recalled the engagements and camps of the war years. The population has shifted over the decades, from 1,209 in 2000 to 856 in 2010 and 963 in 2020, but the name chosen for a railroad tunnel in the 1840s has held.
