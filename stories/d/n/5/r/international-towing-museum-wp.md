# International Towing Museum

A few miles from where Ernest Holmes built what is credited as the first tow truck in 1916, Chattanooga's International Towing & Recovery Hall of Fame & Museum celebrates the wrecker, the people who drive it and those who died doing the job.

**Location:** 35.0169, -85.3231

**Categories:** Automobile museums in Tennessee, Museums in Chattanooga, Tennessee, 1995 establishments in Tennessee, Museums established in 1995

## Introduction

The tow truck began with a favor. In one telling, when a friend's Model T flipped over into a ditch, Chattanooga businessman Ernest Holmes Sr. helped get it out, and the experience set him thinking. He rigged his 1913 Cadillac with an iron chain, a pulley and several poles so it could pull disabled cars and haul them to his garage. By 1916 his Ernest Holmes Company in Chattanooga had built what is widely credited as the first tow truck, about 3.5 miles from where the International Towing & Recovery Hall of Fame & Museum stands today. Chattanooga is considered the birthplace of the U.S. towing industry, and the museum makes the case in steel.

## The Split Boom

Holmes's key idea was the split-boom wrecker. It could anchor itself on one side while it retrieved a vehicle on the other without tilting, which made it especially useful when a wreck had gone down a steep embankment. That principle became the basis of his patents. The museum honors the invention with a replica of the very first tow truck, built, like the original, from a 1913 Cadillac. Around it, the collection traces the wrecker from the early days of the automobile onward, with restored antique tow trucks and equipment alongside tools, toys, memorabilia and pictorial histories of the trade.

## A Museum That Started on the Road

For its first years, the museum had no building at all. In the mid-1980s a group called the Friends of Towing founded the Towing Hall of Fame and Museum to recognize distinguished professionals in the industry and to start collecting its memorabilia and artifacts. With no permanent home, the collection lived in a trailer and traveled to towing shows around the country, a fitting arrangement for an industry built on hauling things from place to place. In 1995 it finally settled in Chattanooga as the International Towing and Recovery Hall of Fame and Museum, dedicated that October as a non-profit organization. The industry still marks its milestones here. In 2026, the company Landoll donated its 40,000th trailer, to be auctioned on October 16, 2026 for the museum's benefit, to celebrate the Hall of Fame's 40th anniversary.

## The Wall of the Fallen

Alongside the gleaming wreckers, the museum keeps a more sober record. It is home to the Towing Hall of Fame, honoring leaders of the trade, and in September 2006 it dedicated the Wall of the Fallen, a memorial dedicated to those who lost their lives working in towing. It is a reminder that the job Ernest Holmes invented, pulling other people out of trouble, carries real risk. The restored trucks tell the story of the machine. The wall tells the story of the people who drove them.
