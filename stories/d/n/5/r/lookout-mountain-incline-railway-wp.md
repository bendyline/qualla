# Lookout Mountain Incline Railway

Since 1895, cable-hauled cars have climbed about a mile up the face of Lookout Mountain from Chattanooga's St. Elmo neighborhood on grades as steep as 72.7 percent, surviving two fires, a change of owners and a 2024 rockslide.

## Introduction

At its steepest, the track climbs at a grade of 72.7 percent, rising nearly 73 feet for every 100 feet it covers horizontally. That makes the Lookout Mountain Incline Railway one of the steepest passenger railways in the world. Its cars leave St. Elmo's Station in the historic St. Elmo neighborhood of Chattanooga, Tennessee, and are hauled about a mile up the face of Lookout Mountain toward Point Park, which overlooks the city and the Tennessee River. The ride has been running since 1895, and it has outlasted two fires, a change of owners and, most recently, a rockslide and the wildfire it is believed to have sparked.

## Incline No. 2

Lookout Mountain has had more than one incline. The first, the Chattanooga and Lookout Mountain Railway, known as Incline No. 1, ran from 1886 to 1895 and was dismantled in 1900. The railway that survives is Incline No. 2, opened on November 16, 1895, by the Chattanooga Incline and Lula Lake Railway. Its cars were hauled up the slope by steel cables, which makes it essentially an elevator laid against a mountainside. The line serves as a major way to reach the top of the mountain.

## One Track, Two Cars

The railway is approximately a mile long and almost entirely single-track. At the midway point, a short two-track passing loop allows two cars to operate at the same time. The line is built to standard gauge. At the top, the upper station has an observation deck and a gift shop, and Ruby Falls and Rock City are both a short drive away. In normal years the railway has drawn more than 100,000 visits, making it one of the Chattanooga area's main tourist attractions. It has appeared in numerous regional and national publications and on television, most prominently in a February 2011 episode of Only in America with Larry the Cable Guy. In 1991 it was designated a Historic Mechanical Engineering Landmark, and it is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

## Fire at the Summit

The early years were hard on the upper station. On December 13, 1896, barely a year after opening, fire destroyed the powerhouse, the upper station and the cars stored there overnight. In March 1919 it happened again, and the railway's own history says that fire closed it for two years. Both times the railway was knocked out of service for a while, and passengers rode the Chattanooga Railway and Light Company's Lookout Mountain route instead. In the 1940s the incline was sold to a private operating company, Southern Coach Lines in some accounts, and in 1973 it became part of the Chattanooga Area Regional Transportation Authority, the region's public transit agency, which still operates it and makes it one of the more unusual rides on any city's transit system.

## Rockslide and Return

The most recent crisis came from the mountain itself. On December 7, 2024, a rockslide on the east face of Lookout Mountain, just south of the incline, is believed to have sparked a wildfire, which the National Park Service put at about 10 acres. All funicular traffic stopped. The fire damaged the rails, cables and cross timbers, and the railway stayed out of service for months while repairs were made. On July 14, 2025, the incline reopened, and the cars resumed their steep climb between St. Elmo and the summit, as they have for most of the time since 1895.

## Flight Context

The Lookout Mountain Incline Railway runs up the mountain's face from St. Elmo near 35.0078°N, 85.3356°W, in Chattanooga, Tennessee. From 4,000 to 5,000 feet MSL, look for the line of track running up the mountain's face toward Point Park at the top, with the Tennessee River and the city spread out below. Rock City lies about 2 nm to the south-southwest on the mountaintop. Chattanooga Metropolitan Airport (KCHA) is about 6.7 nm east-northeast, and Marion County Airport-Brown Field (KAPT) about 13 nm west-northwest. Clear mornings offer the best view of the mountain and the river.
