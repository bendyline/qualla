# Chattanooga: Where the Choo Choo Ran and the Moon Pie Was Born

Tennessee's fourth-largest city where Lookout Mountain rises above the Tennessee River, where the 'Chattanooga Choo Choo' made the railroad famous, where the Moon Pie was invented, and where the city built America's fastest municipal internet.

**Location:** 35.0455, -85.3095

**Categories:** tennessee, chattanooga, city, civil-war, mountains, revival

## Introduction

In 1941, Glenn Miller's orchestra recorded 'Chattanooga Choo Choo,' and a railroad depot in southeastern Tennessee became famous worldwide. The song captured something true about Chattanooga: this was a railroad town, a junction where lines from Atlanta and Nashville and Louisville converged, where the Confederacy built one of its most industrialized cities and the Union fought pitched battles to capture it. The Battles of Chattanooga in 1863 - Lookout Mountain, Missionary Ridge - broke Confederate control of the vital rail junction. A century later, the railroad declined and Chattanooga struggled, its downtown dilapidated, its river polluted. Then something remarkable happened: the city rebuilt itself. The old terminal became the Chattanooga Choo Choo Hotel; the Walnut Street Bridge became the world's longest pedestrian bridge; the riverfront became a Tennessee Aquarium; and the city built the fastest municipal internet in America. Chattanooga became a model for post-industrial revival, its story as unlikely as the Moon Pie - invented here in 1917 - becoming a Southern icon.

## Lookout Mountain

Lookout Mountain rises 2,100 feet above the Tennessee River at Chattanooga's southern edge, offering views that stretch into seven states on clear days. The Battle Above the Clouds was fought here on November 24, 1863, Union forces capturing Confederate positions in fog so thick that soldiers below could only hear the fighting. Point Park preserves the battlefield; the Incline Railway, operating since 1895, climbs the mountain at a 72.7% grade - one of the steepest passenger railways in the world. Rock City, on the mountaintop since 1932, offers rock formations and gardens with a view of seven states (weather permitting); 'See Rock City' barn roofs dotted the South for decades. Ruby Falls, inside the mountain, features a 145-foot underground waterfall discovered in 1928. Lookout Mountain is Chattanooga's most famous landmark - battlefield, tourist attraction, and reminder that this city sits where the Cumberland Plateau meets the Tennessee Valley.

## Civil War Battleground

Chattanooga was the Confederacy's most important railroad junction after Atlanta, the connection linking eastern and western theaters. Union capture of the city would split the Confederacy; Confederate defense was desperate. The Battles of Chattanooga in November 1863 - Lookout Mountain on the 24th, Missionary Ridge on the 25th - broke Confederate lines and opened the path to Atlanta. Chickamauga, fought in September 1863 just south of the city, was one of the war's bloodiest battles and the Confederacy's last major victory in the western theater. The Chickamauga and Chattanooga National Military Park, America's first national military park (established 1890), preserves both battlefields. Monuments dot the ridgelines; cannons mark positions; the visitor center explains campaigns that shaped the war's outcome. Chattanooga's Civil War history is unavoidable - the mountains themselves were contested ground.

## Riverfront Renaissance

By the 1980s, Chattanooga was a poster child for post-industrial decline - polluted air, abandoned factories, a downtown people avoided. The city's transformation began with the Tennessee Aquarium, opened in 1992 as one of the world's largest freshwater aquariums, an anchor that drew visitors back downtown. The Walnut Street Bridge, built in 1891 and closed to vehicles in 1978, was restored as a pedestrian bridge - now the world's longest, connecting downtown to the revived North Shore district. Coolidge Park, on the north bank, features a restored carousel and interactive fountains. The Tennessee Riverwalk extends ten miles along the river. The city also built America's fastest municipal internet - gigabit fiber to every address - attracting tech workers and startups. Chattanooga's renaissance is real, visible in the restaurants and galleries and businesses that fill downtown, in the families walking the riverfront, in a city that remade itself.

## Moon Pie Country

The Moon Pie was invented in Chattanooga in 1917 by the Chattanooga Bakery, supposedly when a traveling salesman asked Kentucky coal miners what snack they'd like - 'something with graham crackers and marshmallow, as big as the moon.' The combination of graham crackers, marshmallow, and chocolate coating became a Southern icon, sold at every gas station and grocery store from Texas to Virginia. The bakery still operates in Chattanooga, producing hundreds of thousands of Moon Pies daily. The city is also birthplace of the tow truck - the Holmes wrecker, invented here in 1916, revolutionized the business of rescuing broken-down vehicles. Miller Industries, the world's largest tow truck manufacturer, is headquartered here. The Tow Truck Museum commemorates the invention. Chattanooga's industries are smaller now but distinctive - the Moon Pie, the tow truck, the Krystal hamburger chain that started here in 1932.

## Scenic City Position

Chattanooga Metropolitan Airport (CHA) offers connections to major hubs. I-75 and I-24 intersect here; Atlanta is ninety minutes south, Nashville two and a half hours northwest, Knoxville ninety minutes northeast. The Tennessee River runs through downtown, navigable from the Gulf of Mexico via TVA dams. From altitude, Chattanooga appears at a bend in the Tennessee River where Lookout Mountain rises abruptly from the valley floor - the distinctive ridge visible from miles away, the river curving around Moccasin Bend, downtown clustered on the eastern bank. What appears from the air as a mid-sized Southern city nestled against mountains is where the Civil War's western campaigns were decided, where the Choo Choo became famous, where the Moon Pie was born, and where a declining industrial city became a model for 21st-century urban revival.
