# Big Chicken

A 56-foot steel chicken, designed with a moving beak and eyes, has towered over a Marietta, Georgia, fast-food restaurant since 1963, surviving storm damage, a near-miss tornado and a public outcry that kept it from being torn down.

**Location:** 33.9514, -84.5204

## Introduction

Ask for directions in Marietta, Georgia, and there is a good chance a bird will come into it. Locals routinely tell visitors to "make a turn at the Big Chicken," or that a destination is "about so many miles past the Big Chicken." The instruction makes sense the moment you see it: a 56-foot steel-sided chicken rising from the roof of a KFC at the corner of Cobb Parkway and Roswell Road, its beak and eyes designed to move as if it is keeping an eye on traffic. Built in 1963 as a roadside advertisement, it has become one of the best-known landmarks in the area.

## Tubby Davis Makes a Statement

In 1963, U.S. 41 had a new stretch through Cobb County, the county's first divided highway, and it offered travelers a quicker route. S. R. "Tubby" Davis saw an opportunity. He owned a restaurant called Johnny Reb's Chick, Chuck and Shake at 12 Cobb Parkway, right on the new road, and he wanted drivers to notice it. So he erected a 56-foot chicken over the building. The design came from Hubert Puckett, a Georgia Tech architecture student, and the structure was fabricated by Atlantic Steel in nearby Atlanta. The original bird had a flaw: its moving parts set up vibrations strong enough to break windows. Davis later sold the restaurant to his brother, and it eventually became a KFC franchise, with the chicken still perched on top.

## The Storm and the Outcry

In January 1993, storm winds damaged the structure. Tearing it down would have been the simple answer, but the public would not allow it, and the outcry forced KFC to put the chicken back up. Among those who objected were pilots, who used the giant bird as a reference point when approaching Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport and Dobbins Air Reserve Base. The rebuilt Big Chicken kept the original design, moving beak and eyes included, and Wikipedia says the window-rattling vibrations were eliminated. Accounts differ on when the beak and eyes actually resumed moving: one roadside guide says the bird stood still for decades and had its eyes rolling again only by its 60th birthday in 2023. Pieces of the old one were sold to collectors as souvenirs. The chicken's luck held in early April 2006, when a small nighttime tornado moving east narrowly missed it, overturning a tractor-trailer at a Kmart across the street and damaging another building nearby.

## Twelve Weeks and Two Million Dollars

By 2017 the landmark was due for a refresh. KFC announced a twelve-week, $2 million renovation (reported as $2.2 million when it finished) that would bring the restaurant up to the chain's newest design and add a gift shop. Work began in early 2017, and on May 11 the restaurant reopened with a ribbon-cutting. The gift shop's souvenirs emblazoned with the Big Chicken have been cheerfully ranked alongside world landmarks such as Big Ben and the Eiffel Tower. It is a joke, but only partly. In Marietta, the chicken really does hold that kind of place.

## Turn Left at the Big Chicken

The bird has worked its way deep into local culture. A board game promoting the city of Marietta, produced in 1985, featured the Big Chicken among other local landmarks. A barbershop-style singing group, the Big Chicken Chorus, formed in 1986 and still performs concerts, and a Marietta band called The Big Chicken Beatles Band often plays at town events. The fame has spread farther, too. The Big Chicken appeared on a promotional card in Sim City: The Card Game, and cartoonist Bill Griffith, fascinated by roadside icons, put it in his comic strip Zippy the Pinhead. The video game Rock Band 3 even warns players on the road not to be alarmed if the locals tell them to "turn left at the Big Chicken."
