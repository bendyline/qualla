# High Museum of Art

Born in a family home on Peachtree Street and reshaped by the 1962 plane crash that killed 106 of Atlanta's arts patrons, the High Museum of Art grew into the largest visual art museum in the Southeast and a national leader in folk art and photography.

**Location:** 33.7900, -84.3856

**Categories:** Museums in Atlanta, Midtown Atlanta, Museums of American art, Folk art museums and galleries in Georgia (U.S. state), Art museums and galleries established in 1905

## Introduction

In the fall of 1962, Whistler's Mother crossed the Atlantic. Only months earlier, a group of Atlanta arts patrons had seen the painting at the Louvre during a museum-sponsored trip to Paris, and on June 3, 106 of them died in a plane crash at Orly Airport. As a gesture of goodwill to the people of Atlanta, the Louvre sent the painting to be shown at the Atlanta Art Association's museum on Peachtree Street. That museum, known today as the High Museum of Art, would be rebuilt around the memory of the people Atlanta lost.

## A House on Peachtree

The High began in 1905 as the Atlanta Art Association. Its name comes from the High family, who in 1926 donated their home on Peachtree Street to house the collection, following a series of exhibitions organized by Atlanta collector J. J. Haverty with the Grand Central Art Galleries. Many pieces from Haverty's collection still hang in the museum. In 1955 a separate building went up beside the family home. It was a modest beginning. When Gudmund Vigtel became director in 1963, the High was a regional institution in a simple brick building, with a staff of four and an operating budget of $60,000.

## The Loss at Orly

The crash at Orly on June 3, 1962, tore through Atlanta's cultural life. The 106 arts patrons who died came from some of the city's most prominent families, among them members of the Berry family, which founded Berry College. With crew and other passengers, 130 people were killed, at the time the worst single-plane disaster in aviation history. The grief reached across the ocean. Besides the Louvre's loan of Whistler's Mother, the French government gave the High Auguste Rodin's sculpture The Shade in memory of the victims. At home, Atlanta built the Atlanta Memorial Arts Center to honor them, giving the museum a new home rooted in remembrance.

## Meier's Galleries, Piano's Additions

Vigtel led the High for 28 years and oversaw its transformation into one of the nation's most successful art museums. During his tenure the permanent collection tripled, the staff grew from four to 150, and the budget rose to $9 million. His crowning achievement, as a later director put it, was a 135,000-square-foot building by architect Richard Meier, which opened in 1983, funded by a $7.9 million challenge grant from former Coca-Cola president Robert W. Woodruff and $20 million raised by the museum. Meier won the Pritzker Prize the following year. In 2005, three new buildings by Renzo Piano, built for $124 million, more than doubled the museum's size to 312,000 square feet. Today the High, part of the Woodruff Arts Center, is described as the largest museum for visual art in the Southeastern United States.

## Art of the People

The High's most distinctive strengths lie outside the usual canon. It began collecting the work of living self-taught artists in 1975, and in 1994, the High says, it became the first general-interest museum to create a dedicated department for folk and self-taught art. The collection is especially rich in work by Southern and African American artists, with holdings the museum describes as the largest public collections of work by Bill Traylor, Nellie Mae Rowe and Thornton Dial, alongside a major group by Howard Finster. Photography is its largest collection, some 7,500 prints gathered since the early 1970s, including one of the largest collections of civil rights movement photographs. Since 1996 its "Picturing the South" initiative has commissioned photographers such as Sally Mann, Dawoud Bey and Alec Soth to respond to the region's landscape and culture.

## From Bellini to Kusama

The collection of roughly 20,000 works reaches back seven centuries. A 1958 gift from the Samuel H. Kress Foundation, including Giovanni Bellini's Madonna and Child, became the core of the European collection, and a 2019 gift from Atlanta collectors Doris and Shouky Shaheen enriched its French Impressionist holdings, among them Claude Monet's Autumn on the Seine, Argenteuil. From 2006 to 2009 the museum ran Louvre Atlanta, a three-year partnership of long-term loans from Paris with an estimated $18 million budget, closing a circle that began in 1962. The High has since hosted the popular Yayoi Kusama: Infinity Mirrors, and in 2025 the High was billed as the first art museum in the United States to hold an exhibition on South Korean painter Kim Chong Hak. Film crews come too: the High has stood in for the Museum of Great Britain in Black Panther.

## Flight Context

The High Museum of Art stands at about 33.79°N, 84.39°W on Peachtree Street in Midtown Atlanta, the city's arts district; the museum is a division of the Woodruff Arts Center. From 3,000 to 4,000 feet, locate the Midtown towers along the Peachtree corridor north of downtown, with the green expanse of Piedmont Park just to the east. Atlanta lies beneath busy Class B airspace around Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (KATL), about 11 miles south, so coordinate with ATC; DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK) is about 8 miles northeast and Fulton County Executive Airport-Charlie Brown Field (KFTY) about 8 miles west. Expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the city.
