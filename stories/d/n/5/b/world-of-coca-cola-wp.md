# World of Coca-Cola

A few blocks from where pharmacist John Pemberton created his syrup in 1886, the World of Coca-Cola tells the company's story in its hometown, with a tasting room of drinks from around the world and a first home that ended as a Capitol parking lot.

## Introduction

At the World of Coca-Cola, the tasting room is a trip around the world. The museum invites visitors to sample more than 100 beverages from the Coca-Cola family, and the effect is part history lesson, part sugar rush. It suits a museum that sits in the company's hometown, just blocks from where an Atlanta pharmacist created a new soft drink in 1886. The current building opened on May 24, 2007, on Baker Street downtown. It replaced a first museum that had drawn millions of visitors, and whose old building has since vanished entirely.

## A Pharmacist and a Bookkeeper

The museum was conceived as a continuation of a story that began in 1886. That year Dr. John S. Pemberton, a pharmacist from Atlanta, created a soft drink built on a distinctive flavored syrup, and it became highly popular. His partner and bookkeeper, Frank M. Robinson, gave it the name Coca-Cola and was responsible for its well-known script lettering. The Coca-Cola Company is headquartered in Atlanta, and when the museum needed a new home, it landed at 121 Baker Street, just a few blocks from where Pemberton created the original formula. Even the complex carries his name: the museum stands on Pemberton Place.

## The Neon Pavilion

The first World of Coca-Cola opened in 1990 at 55 Martin Luther King Jr. Drive, between the Georgia State Capitol and the Underground Atlanta entertainment district. Its three-story pavilion announced itself with a huge neon Coca-Cola sign, 30 feet high and 26 feet wide, built by Metals Manufacturing of West Valley, Utah. Tours began on the top floor and worked downward through about 1,000 artifacts arranged in chronological order, a replica 1930s soda fountain, decades of advertising on video and a ten-minute film about Coke around the world. At the Spectacular Fountain, visitors sampled Coke products, and at Tastes of the States in the same room they could try 22 soft drink brands, some sold only regionally. A Tastes of the World exhibit filled the International Lounge. Over its seventeen years the original museum welcomed more than 13 million visitors, by the company's count.

## Pemberton Place

The replacement cost $97 million and covers 92,000 square feet. It anchors the 20-acre Pemberton Place complex, across Baker Street from Centennial Olympic Park, which it shares with the Georgia Aquarium and the Center for Civil and Human Rights. Inside, the museum has featured exhibits about the secret formula, along with a 3D movie in which an intrepid scientist and his assistant set out to uncover it for themselves. The building also included a fully functional bottling line that once filled 8-ounce bottles of Coca-Cola to hand out to guests. Citing operating costs, the museum discontinued that practice, and from 2013 the line ran in simulation, a factory performing a factory for an audience.

## Coke Around the World

Atlanta was never the only World of Coca-Cola. A second one opened in July 1997 in the Showcase Mall on the Las Vegas Strip. The attraction closed in 2000 amid restructuring at the company, though the Coca-Cola store there kept going. Also in 2000 a third location opened on the sixth floor of Mediage in Odaiba, Tokyo, and it closed in 2007. A 2007 travel guide also listed a Coca-Cola Museum in Taoyuan City, Taiwan. At Walt Disney World, Epcot has hosted a Coca-Cola shop and tasting attraction since 1998, first themed as a polar expedition called Ice Station Cool, complete with a snowcat, and later known as Club Cool, which reopened in a new spot in 2021 after a two-year closure. In 2016 a new Coca-Cola store at Disney Springs opened, modeled on the Atlanta attraction and topped with a rooftop bar.

## The Old Pavilion's Last Chapter

The state of Georgia bought Coca-Cola's original museum building for about $1 million (reports differ on whether the sale came in 2005 or 2007), and it sat vacant after the company moved out in 2007. The state later sought the surrounding land as well, in a property exchange with the city of Atlanta. Legislators proposed turning the pavilion into a state history museum, but the cost of refurbishing it, and the lack of money to pay for that, meant the idea never moved forward. In June 2024 the building that had welcomed more than 13 million visitors was demolished. Its site became parking for the State Capitol complex, making room for construction of a new legislative office building and renovations to the Capitol itself.

## Flight Context

The World of Coca-Cola stands at 33.7628°N, 84.3928°W on Pemberton Place in downtown Atlanta, across Baker Street from Centennial Olympic Park and beside the Georgia Aquarium. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet MSL, the park's open green space next to the downtown towers is the best reference for finding the complex. Nearby airports: Fulton County Airport-Brown Field (KFTY), about 6.5 nm west; Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL), about 8 nm south-southwest; and DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK), about 8 nm north-northeast. Clear mornings usually give the best view of the city.
