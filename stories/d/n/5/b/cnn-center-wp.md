# CNN Center

Built as a mixed-use complex whose indoor theme park folded within six months, downtown Atlanta's CNN Center became the global face of 24-hour cable news for nearly four decades before the network moved out and the building became simply "The Center."

**Location:** 33.7567, -84.3966

**Categories:** CNN, Mass media in Atlanta, Office buildings in Atlanta, Mass media company headquarters in the United States, Office buildings completed in 1976

## Introduction

The escalator was built for a theme park that barely lasted a season. Rising roughly 193 to 196 feet through the atrium of a downtown Atlanta complex, it was once listed by Guinness World Records as the longest freestanding escalator in the world, supported only at its ends. It first carried visitors up to The World of Sid and Marty Krofft, and later up to tours of CNN's newsroom. For nearly four decades, the building around it was CNN Center, the international headquarters of the cable news network, where several of its channels made their broadcasts.

## Ice Rinks and Puppet Rides

The building opened in 1976 as the Omni International Complex, a development by Cousins Properties. Next door stood the Omni Coliseum, an NBA and NHL arena that had opened on October 14, 1972. The new complex was built for spectacle. Its main floor held an indoor ice rink, a handful of restaurants, a Gold Mine video arcade and a multi-screen movie theater. Sid and Marty Krofft, the producers behind a string of popular children's television shows, opened an indoor amusement park inspired by their creations. The World of Sid and Marty Krofft, described as the first indoor theme park in the United States, opened in May 1976 and closed within six months. Above all the activity, the Omni International office tower sat largely vacant, a grand address still waiting for a reason to exist.

## Ted Turner Moves the News Downtown

That reason arrived with Ted Turner. The building sold for $42 million in 1985, and in 1987 CNN moved its headquarters there from its Midtown site, the old Progressive Club at 1050 Techwood Drive that was also home to Turner Broadcasting System. The building now housed the main newsrooms and studios for several CNN channels, while other Turner units filled the commercial office space alongside business tenants and foreign consulates. Turner's personal tastes showed up in unexpected places. For years the complex's movie theater offered daily showings of the 1939 film Gone with the Wind, which Turner called "The greatest movie ever made." Visitors rode the long escalator up to the CNN tour to see where the broadcasts were made.

## Implosions, Storms and Sorrow

The neighborhood kept changing around the newsroom. The Omni Coliseum closed on May 11, 1997, and was imploded that July 26, close enough that CNN Center suffered minor exterior window damage. Its replacement, Philips Arena, now State Farm Arena, opened on September 18, 1999. Some days brought real grief. On April 3, 2007, a woman named Clara Riddles was shot inside the building by a former boyfriend; the nearby CNN.com newsroom was evacuated, and she died of her injuries. On March 14, 2008, an EF-2 tornado tore through downtown Atlanta, leaving water and dust in the upper floors. Rain poured through the damaged atrium ceiling and partially flooded the food court, and CNN's library was damaged. The attached Omni Hotel was evacuated as a precaution, and more than 400 of its rooms stayed empty for two weeks.

## When the Building Became the Story

On May 29, 2020, four days after the murder of George Floyd in Minneapolis, CNN Center became the scene of rioting. The building also housed a Zone 5 precinct of the Atlanta Police Department. Rioters spray-painted the CNN logo out front and damaged the entrance's glass curtain wall and police vehicles. The front lobby was damaged, though no one broke through into the building's core common area, and the logo sign was soon repainted. The unrest came as the company was already looking elsewhere. In 2016 Turner had said it would divest its 50 percent stake in the Omni Hotel to focus on redeveloping its Techwood campus in Midtown. On June 29, 2020, exactly a month after the riot, WarnerMedia announced plans to sell CNN Center and move CNN's Atlanta operations back to the campus it had left in 1987.

## Signing Off From Downtown

CP Group acquired the office building in 2021. CNN's control room activities moved back to the original Midtown campus on October 30, 2023, and several weekend programs lingered downtown a little longer. On February 25, 2024, CNN Newsroom and CNN This Morning Weekend aired their final broadcasts from CNN Center before moving to Techwood the following week. That April 4, CP Group announced a renovation and a new name: "The Center." The Krofft-era escalator, so woven into the original structure that it was very hard to remove, came out during the week of February 24, 2025, along with the CNN Studio Tour. By 2026 the redeveloped complex was being styled "The CTR": its renovated atrium opened on May 20, 2026, with the CTR Food Works food hall slated to follow in June, in time for Atlanta's World Cup matches, and longer-term plans to convert former hotel rooms into apartments. The building still sits beside Centennial Olympic Park, served by MARTA's SEC District station, and it is still commonly called by its old name.

## Flight Context

The CTR (formerly The Center, and before that CNN Center) sits at 33.7567°N, 84.3966°W on the western edge of downtown Atlanta, beside Centennial Olympic Park and State Farm Arena. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet, look for the open green space of the park and the arena's broad roof beside the building, with the downtown skyline rising to the east. The area lies beneath Atlanta's Class B airspace, so coordinate with approach control. Nearest airports: Fulton County Executive Airport-Charlie Brown Field (KFTY), about 6 nm west-northwest; Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International (KATL), about 7 nm south; and DeKalb-Peachtree Airport (KPDK), about 9 nm northeast. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common; winter mornings often give the sharpest views.
