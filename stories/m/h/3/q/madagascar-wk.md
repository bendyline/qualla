# Madagascar

The world's fourth-largest island, separated from India 88 million years ago, where over 90% of wildlife exists nowhere else on Earth and Austronesian settlers built a culture unlike anything on the African mainland.

**Location:** -20.0000, 47.0000

**Categories:** country, biodiversity, island, culture

## Introduction

Some ecologists call it the eighth continent. Madagascar split from India approximately 88 million years ago, and that long isolation produced a natural experiment in evolution found nowhere else: over 90 percent of its wildlife and 80 percent of its plant species are endemic. But the strangeness extends beyond biology. Despite lying just 400 kilometers off the coast of Mozambique, Madagascar's people, language, and culture trace their deepest roots not to Africa but to Southeast Asia. The Austronesian settlers who arrived in the mid-first millennium CE brought rice cultivation, outrigger canoes, and a language more closely related to those spoken in Borneo than to any tongue on the African mainland.

## An Island of Ancestors

DNA and linguistic studies confirm what the language itself suggests: Madagascar's people are predominantly Austronesian in origin, with their ancestors arriving between 350 BCE and 550 CE. East African migrants crossed the Mozambique Channel around 1000 CE, followed over the centuries by Arabs, Indians, and Chinese immigrants. The result is a population of 18 ethnic groups whose appearance, customs, and way of thinking blend these diverse origins. The largest group, the Merina of the central highlands, developed a sophisticated political kingdom that began to dominate by the 15th century. The entire island speaks one language, Malagasy, in various regional dialects. French became the second official language under colonial rule, and English is increasingly common in tourist areas. The Malagasy people appreciate foreigners who attempt even a few words of their language, a warmth that travelers encounter across the island.

## The Forests That Remain

Madagascar is home to nearly 15,000 plant species, including massive ancient baobab trees, over 800 species of orchids, and the strange spiny forests of the south where plants resemble coral more than vegetation. The island supports more than 100 species of lemurs, nearly all rare or threatened, along with over 300 bird species, approximately 260 reptile species, and a vast number of amphibians and insects. The eastern windward slopes catch moisture from the Indian Ocean, producing tropical rainforests, while the western and southern sides lie in a rain shadow that creates dry forests, thorn scrub, and desert. Since the arrival of humans, approximately 90 percent of the original forest has disappeared, burned primarily for agricultural purposes. Six national parks along the eastern coast form the Rainforests of the Atsinanana, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and dozens of other parks and private reserves protect what remains.

## Cyclones, Trade Winds, and the Rainy Season

Madagascar has two seasons: a hot, rainy stretch from November to April and a cooler, dry period from May to October. The east coast, most exposed to the Indian Ocean trade winds, receives as much as 3,500 millimeters of annual rainfall, along with destructive cyclones that sweep in from the direction of the Mascarene Islands. The central highlands are drier and cooler owing to altitude, though thunderstorms and lightning are serious hazards during the rainy season. Antananarivo receives practically all of its 1,400 millimeters of rain between November and April. The dry season brings pleasant, sunny weather, though mornings can be chilly. In the south, conditions become arid, completing a gradient from equatorial deluge to near-desert within a single island. This climatic variety is what allows Madagascar to support its extraordinary range of ecosystems.

## Roads, Taxi-Brousses, and Getting There

International flights arrive at Antananarivo or the resort island of Nosy Be, served by airlines from Paris, Johannesburg, Nairobi, Istanbul, and Dubai. Once on the ground, Madagascar's roads test patience and vehicle suspension in equal measure. Three major paved routes radiate from the capital, but beyond those, roads dissolve into potholed tracks that become impassable in the rainy season. The taxi-brousse, an interurban shared taxi, is how most Malagasy travel. Drivers wait until their 15-seat minibuses are full before departing, and delays of several hours are standard. The ride is cramped, dusty in the dry season, and guaranteed to introduce you to your fellow passengers. Madagascar Airlines serves 11 domestic destinations, offering a faster but less reliable alternative. A single railway line still operates between Moramanga and Ambila Lemaitso on the east coast, a colonial-era route where breakdowns are frequent.

## Vanilla, Diving, and the Weight of History

Most visitors come for the wildlife. Andasibe-Mantadia National Park, a few hours from the capital on paved road, is the most accessible, while remote parks require days of driving and trekking. Scuba diving and snorkeling are exceptional around Nosy Be, where visibility can exceed 30 meters, though the nearest hyperbaric chamber lies across the Mozambique Channel. In the far north, constant 30-knot winds between April and November make it one of the best kitesurfing spots in the southern hemisphere. Madagascar produces some of the world's finest vanilla, which is cheaper and better here than almost anywhere else. The country gained independence from France on 26 June 1960, but political instability has marked the decades since, including a military coup in 2009 that ousted President Marc Ravalomanana. The situation has stabilized, though Madagascar remains one of the poorest countries in the world, a place where extraordinary natural wealth and deep human poverty exist side by side.

## Flight Context

Located at approximately 20S, 47E in the Indian Ocean, 400 km east of Mozambique. Madagascar is the world's fourth-largest island, roughly 1,600 km long and 570 km wide. The capital Antananarivo (FMMI/Ivato International Airport) sits in the central highlands. Other major airports include Nosy Be (FMNN/Fascene Airport), Toamasina (FMMT), Toliara (FMST), and Mahajanga (FMNM). The island's central spine of highlands is visible from cruising altitude, with the lush green eastern rainforests contrasting sharply with the drier western lowlands. Recommended viewing: the Avenue of the Baobabs near Morondava, the limestone tsingy formations, and the eastern rainforest canopy.
