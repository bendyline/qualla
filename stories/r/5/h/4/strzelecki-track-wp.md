# Strzelecki Track

A historic outback stock route in northeastern South Australia, first driven by a cattle thief in 1870 and now the access road to the Cooper Basin gas fields.

**Location:** -27.7333, 140.7330

**Categories:** Australian outback tracks, Roads in South Australia, Far North (South Australia)

## Introduction

The road was opened by a crime. In 1870, a stockman named Harry Readford quietly gathered around a thousand head of cattle from Bowen Downs station in central Queensland and pointed them south, away from the brands and the law, into country no drover had crossed before. The route he improvised through the Channel Country and the Strzelecki Desert became the Strzelecki Track, today a 472-kilometre line of mostly unsealed road linking Lyndhurst to Innamincka in the far northeast of South Australia. It is named, like the desert it crosses, for the Polish explorer Sir Paul Edmund de Strzelecki.

## The Cattle Thief Who Made a Highway

Readford knew his stolen mob would be recognised the moment he tried to sell it in Queensland, so he gambled on the unknown. Over roughly three months he pushed the cattle some 1,300 kilometres down the Cooper and across the dunes, watering them at native wells, before selling the survivors for 5,000 pounds at Blanchewater station, east of Marree. His audacity made him a folk hero. The bushman character Captain Starlight in Rolf Boldrewood's novel Robbery Under Arms was drawn partly from Readford's exploits, though Readford himself was never actually called Starlight, a name that belonged to a different bushranger entirely. When Readford was finally tried for the theft in 1873, the jury famously acquitted him despite overwhelming evidence, apparently in admiration of the feat. The judge was reportedly appalled. The track he blazed outlived the scandal and became a working stock route, moving cattle from the Queensland channels to the South Australian railhead for generations.

## The Route and Its Country

The track threads through the gibber plains and dune fields of the Strzelecki Desert, prone to flooding after heavy rain and, the rest of the time, to corrugations, loose stone and dust. A shorter alternative runs via a public access road between Moomba and Innamincka, trimming the distance to 458 kilometres. The land is sparsely dotted with the relics of pastoral struggle: the heritage-listed ruins of Tinga Tingana homestead, the old wells and whims at Coochilara waterhole and Old Mulga Bore on Merty Merty station, and the lonely Gray's Tree, thought to mark a grave linked to the Burke and Wills expedition that passed through this region a decade before Readford. These scattered sites trace a single hard story: the slow, costly European attempt to graze stock in one of the driest corners of the continent.

## From Stock Route to Gas Corridor

The Strzelecki's modern purpose lies beneath it. Midway along the track sits Moomba, a Santos-operated processing settlement at the centre of the Cooper and Eromanga basins. The first commercial gas was found at the Gidgealpa field in 1963; oil followed at Tirrawarra in 1970 and at the Strzelecki field itself in 1978. The plant now consolidates output from more than a hundred fields and feeds pipelines running south to Adelaide and east toward the seaboard cities, making this remote road a piece of national infrastructure. In recent years Moomba has also become a site for carbon capture and storage, with carbon dioxide injected back into depleted reservoirs. The track that began as an escape route for stolen cattle now serves as the surface artery of one of Australia's most important energy provinces.

## Endurance and Caution

For all its history, the Strzelecki remains demanding country. It is passable to conventional vehicles in the dry season, but only with caution and proper preparation, and heavy rain can close it entirely. People have died of exposure within sight of the inland's watercourses. That tension, between the apparent emptiness and the very real danger, is the through-line of the track's whole story, from Readford's gamble to the Burke and Wills tragedy that unfolded just north of its terminus at Innamincka on Cooper Creek. The Strzelecki is short by Australian standards, but it asks more of a traveller than its length suggests.

## Flight Context

The Strzelecki Track runs through far northeastern South Australia, centred near 27.73 degrees S, 140.73 degrees E, linking Lyndhurst in the south to Innamincka on Cooper Creek in the north. From altitude it reads as a faint pale scar across red gibber and parallel dunes of the Strzelecki Desert, with the Moomba gas plant and its flares roughly midway and the green channels of Cooper Creek defining the northern end. Nearest airfields are Moomba (YOOM) and Innamincka (YINN), with Leigh Creek (YLEC) to the south. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000 to 6,000 feet AGL; the flat, reflective gibber and frequent heat haze make midday visibility variable, and dust storms can obscure the route completely.
