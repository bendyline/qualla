# Papua

The Indonesian half of New Guinea - six provinces stretched across the world's second-largest island, where tree-kangaroos share rainforest with the world's largest reef system.

**Location:** -4.3167, 138.7500

**Categories:** regions, indonesia, new-guinea, travel, biodiversity

## Introduction

A travel permit called a surat jalan is required to go almost anywhere in Papua beyond the main coastal towns. Two passport photos, a nominal fee of about five thousand rupiah, a list of every place you plan to visit - no changes allowed without getting a new permit - and a stamp at the police station in every new town. Make copies, because hotels will want them too. The bureaucracy hints at what Papua is: Indonesia's most remote province, the hardest to reach, and among the most closely watched. It is also the place in Indonesia where a traveler can walk the Baliem Valley alongside Dani farmers, dive among the richest reefs on Earth, and pass through ecosystems unchanged for millennia, all within a single fortnight.

## Six Provinces, Not One

Until 2003, the whole Indonesian half of New Guinea was a single province. In that year, the Bird's Head Peninsula and surrounding islands were split off to form West Papua. In 2022 the central government carved four more provinces out of the original Papua: Central Papua, Highland Papua, South Papua, and Southwest Papua. The traditional regional names - Tabi Saireri, Mee Pago, La Pago, Anim Ha, Bomberai, Domberai - still describe the cultural zones that colonial borders cut through. Roughly five million people live across all six provinces combined. Most are Melanesian, culturally and linguistically closer to Papua New Guinea than to Java, though migration from other parts of Indonesia has been reshaping the coastal cities for decades.

## The Baliem Valley and the Dani

High in the central range, the Baliem Valley was unknown to the outside world until 1938, when an American-led Archbold Expedition flew over it and saw cultivated fields stretching to the horizon. Thousands of feet below the surrounding ridges lived the Dani people, farmers who had been working the valley's rich volcanic soils for perhaps 9,000 years. Cannibalism and ritual warfare were practiced in some highland areas into the 1970s. Today the valley is reached by a flight to Wamena Airport, and the Dani economy now includes selling souvenirs and performing at the annual Festival Lembah Baliem. Some men still wear the traditional koteka, a penis gourd, though younger generations often adopt Western dress. The honai, round thatched houses with conical roofs, still cluster in compounds the way they always have.

## Raja Ampat and the Richest Reefs

Off the western tip of the Bird's Head Peninsula lies an archipelago of limestone islands rising from jewel-colored water. Raja Ampat - the name means Four Kings, for the four main islands - contains what Conservation International has documented as the highest marine biodiversity anywhere on the planet. Over 550 species of coral grow here, out of roughly 800 known worldwide. The reefs sit at the geographic center of the Coral Triangle, where the Pacific and Indian Oceans exchange species and evolutionary experiments. Manta rays patrol cleaning stations. Pygmy seahorses smaller than a fingernail cling to sea fans. The islands are hard to reach and more expensive to visit than nearby destinations, which has protected them. Visitors stay at small resorts on Misool, Sorido, or Arborek, or aboard liveaboard dive boats that move between sites.

## Getting There and Around

Almost all travel into Papua is by air. Sentani Airport near Jayapura is the main gateway, with flights from Jakarta and Makassar and onward connections across the region. Biak, Manokwari, Sorong, and Timika also receive long-haul service. Within Papua, road networks remain limited - the Trans-Papua Highway is still being built, with the Sorong-Manokwari-Bintuni section of nearly 600 km completed. For most internal trips, flying is the practical choice. Big carriers like Garuda and Lion Air's subsidiary Wings Air connect the cities. Smaller operators - Susi Air, Trigana, Aviastar - hop between regional airstrips. More than 300 remote village strips are served by charter operators and mission aviation organizations like Associated Mission Aviation in Jayapura. For those with time, PELNI ferries run from Surabaya up through Makassar to Sorong, Manokwari, Biak, and Jayapura. The voyage takes about two weeks.

## The Wildlife of Another Continent

Biogeographically, New Guinea is part of the Australian continent, not Asia. The wildlife reflects that. Marsupials fill ecological niches that placental mammals occupy elsewhere - tree kangaroos climb forest canopies, wallabies graze grasslands, cuscus hunt at night. The endangered long-beaked echidna, a monotreme that lays eggs, survives in a few highland strongholds. Cassowaries stalk the rainforest understory. Birds of paradise perform their courtship displays in specific forest clearings where males return year after year. The Papua monitor, the world's longest lizard, slips through riverbank vegetation. In Teluk Cendrawasih National Park on the north coast, divers can swim with whale sharks that gather near fishing platforms. Protected areas include Lorentz National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and Wasur National Park, a Ramsar wetland of international importance. The equatorial glaciers of the central range are still melting back, but for now they remain - ice fields on a tropical island, a combination found almost nowhere else.

## Flight Context

Papua (Indonesian New Guinea) spans roughly 1.5 to 9 degrees south latitude and 131 to 141 degrees east longitude, the western half of the island of New Guinea. Major airports include Sentani (WAJJ) at Jayapura, Biak (WABB), Sorong (WASS), Timika (WABP), and Wamena (WAVV) in the Baliem Valley. Terrain includes the central range above 4,000 meters with equatorial glaciers on Puncak Jaya. Expect tropical convective weather, heavy afternoon cloud buildup, and limited navigation infrastructure outside major hubs. Surat jalan permits are required for most interior travel.
