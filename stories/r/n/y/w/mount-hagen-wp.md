# Mount Hagen

The third-largest city in Papua New Guinea at 1,677 meters elevation in the Wahgi Valley, named for an extinct volcano named for a German colonial officer, and founded at an airstrip that arrived in April 1934.

**Location:** -5.8667, 144.2167

**Categories:** cities, papua-new-guinea, highlands, colonial-history, coffee

## Introduction

Bob Gurney landed at the Mogei drome on 1 April 1934 with the first flight ever to reach the Wahgi Valley. The first airmail left the next day. Gurney was flying into country that had been unknown to the outside world until the Leahy brothers flew over it the previous year - a valley of somewhere over a hundred thousand people, practicing intensive agriculture on volcanic soil at 1,677 meters of elevation. Ninety years later, Mount Hagen is the third-largest city in Papua New Guinea, capital of Western Highlands Province, and the commercial hub of an entire region. The Mogei strip became the city's main street. The new airport sits 15 minutes out. The coffee plantations still run up the valley sides.

## The Leahy Expedition

Mick Leahy was an Australian gold prospector. His brother Dan was his constant partner. Government officer Jim Taylor joined them for the 1933 overflight that first revealed the Wahgi Valley to any outsider. Flying over in a biplane called Canberra, they saw exactly what everyone had said did not exist: a densely populated agricultural country, gardens terraced up the hillsides, smoke from cooking fires. A short time later the Leahys and Taylor walked in with a well-supplied patrol. They became the first Europeans ever to make contact with the tribes that would become Mount Hagen's neighbors. The first patrol built an airstrip at Kelua, near the future town. A year later, the Mogei drome replaced it. By the late 1930s the mine workers, missionaries, and government officials who arrived next were landing on a strip that would eventually become a downtown street.

## A Volcano Named for a Man Who Never Came

The city takes its name from Mount Hagen, an extinct, eroded volcano about 24 kilometers to the northwest, rising to 3,765 meters. The volcano was named in 1889 by German colonial officials for Curt von Hagen, an administrator for the German New Guinea Company who served in what was then German New Guinea. Von Hagen never visited the mountain. He was murdered by local people near Astrolabe Bay in August 1897, having apparently underestimated either the ground situation or the people he was attempting to govern. His name stuck to a peak he never saw, and through the peak, to a city founded decades after his death. Curt von Hagen was born in 1859 and died young, but the map he never drew still carries his signature.

## The Wahgi Valley Speaks Many Languages

Western Highlands Province has a population of roughly 950,000 across four electorates: Mul Baiyer Lumusa, Dei Council, Hagen Central, and Tambul Nebliyer. The city itself held an estimated 46,250 people as of 2013. The dominant language around Mount Hagen is Melpa, one of the Mount Hagen languages of the Trans-New Guinea family, spoken by several hundred thousand people. Nearby communities speak different Mount Hagen languages - Jagai, Kakoli, and others - with dialect chains running across the valley floor. Traditional culture remains strong in Mount Hagen and its surrounds, though it coexists now with smartphones, supermarkets, and the Nazarene hospital at Kudjip. Sing-sing dances at the Mount Hagen Cultural Show each year bring tribes from across the country into one ground.

## Sorcery Accusations and Their Cost

The maintenance of traditional belief systems in the Highlands has not been uniformly benign. In 2009, and again in 2013, local women in the Mount Hagen area were reportedly burned alive after being accused of sorcery - hideous deaths that drew international condemnation and prompted legal reforms in PNG. Academic analysis has linked the rise in sorcery accusations to poor development outcomes and the erosion of social trust that has accompanied rapid economic change. Traditional communities once handled suspicions through mediation and compensation; modern pressures have sometimes tipped accusations into violence. Papua New Guinea repealed the 1971 Sorcery Act in 2013 and strengthened penalties for sorcery-related killings. The problem has not disappeared. It is part of the uneasy transition the Highlands is making between one framework of belief and another.

## Kuk Swamp and Ancient Agriculture

Twelve to thirteen kilometers northeast of Mount Hagen, in the Wahgi Valley at about 1,550 meters, lies Kuk Swamp. Archaeology has established that people here began systematic agricultural drainage around 9,000 years ago. They cultivated taro, then added bananas and sugar cane to the system by roughly 6,900 years before present. Kuk Swamp was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008 as one of the handful of places on Earth where agriculture was independently invented. It remains the only UNESCO World Heritage Site in Papua New Guinea. The modern city's supermarkets and stoplights sit a short drive from a landscape where the distant ancestors of modern Highlanders were already reshaping wetland into cropland while Europe was still fully in the Mesolithic.

## Getting There, Getting Out

Mount Hagen Airport at Kagamuga is an international airport, though regular international flights go only through Port Moresby's Jacksons. Air Niugini operates Fokker 100 and Dash 8 aircraft to Port Moresby, Moro, and Cairns. PNG Air runs regular services to Port Moresby, Tabubil, Kiunga, and Wewak. Hevilift flies ATR-42 and Twin Otter charters from Cairns for Porgera mine workers. Asia Pacific Airlines supports the Ok Tedi mine with Dash 8 service to Tabubil. The Highlands Highway reaches Mount Hagen from Lae on the Huon Gulf and continues to Madang, Goroka, Wabag, Mendi, and Kundiawa. The road is theoretically sealed throughout, but landslides and deterioration leave sections rough or unpaved. Four-wheel drive is recommended beyond Mount Hagen. The city has a sister relationship with Orange, New South Wales, in place since 1985 - a pairing born in the Cold War era of town-twinning diplomacy and still quietly observed.

## Flight Context

Mount Hagen sits at 5.87 degrees S, 144.22 degrees E, 1,677 meters elevation in the Wahgi Valley. Kagamuga Airport (AYMH / HGU) is Papua New Guinea's second-busiest airport. Runway length combined with altitude and midday heat can restrict takeoff weights; departures before noon strongly preferred. Mount Hagen (volcano) stands 24 km northwest at 3,765 meters. Mount Giluwe (4,368 m) lies south, Mount Wilhelm (4,509 m) east in Chimbu Province. Recommended viewing altitude 12,000-18,000 feet for Wahgi Valley overview. Nearest alternates: Wapenamanda (AYPM / WBM) and Goroka (AYGA / GKA). Weather: afternoon thunderstorms typical; lightning within airport vicinity grounds all traffic. Kuk Swamp World Heritage Site visible 12-13 km northeast of the city.
