# Fonda Milagros Nightclub Fire

A 2023 fire in a cluster of Murcia nightclubs killed thirteen people, most of them young migrants, in a building that had been ordered closed a year earlier.

**Location:** 37.9939, -1.1158

**Categories:** Disasters, Fires, 21st century, Modern history, Region of Murcia, Spanish history

## Introduction

It was just after six in the morning on the first Sunday of October, and inside a cluster of Murcia nightclubs the night was winding down. Then a fire started on the first floor, and within a short time thirteen people were dead. Many of them were young, and many had come from far away, nationals of Colombia, Ecuador, and Nicaragua who had made lives in this Spanish city. It was the deadliest nightclub fire Spain had seen in more than three decades, and much of what made it deadly had been flagged, in writing, long before that morning.

## A Sunday Morning in October

The fire broke out around 6 a.m. on 1 October 2023, in a building that housed at least three nightclubs. It began inside the Fonda Milagros club and spread to the adjoining Teatre and Golden. Authorities later pointed to the air conditioning system as a path for the flames, which moved fast enough that people had little time to get out. The Fonda Milagros suffered such severe structural damage that it collapsed, and it was in the wreckage that the first victims were found. These were people who had gone out to dance and celebrate, and who did not come home. Emergency crews arrived to a building already failing, and the search for the missing stretched into the day as families gathered outside, waiting for news they dreaded to hear.

## Walls of Plasterboard

The building's own layout worked against everyone inside it. In 2019, the owners had asked the city for permission to split the venue in two, creating the Fonda Milagros, also known as La Fonda, alongside the existing club. The two were divided by walls made only of plasterboard, offering almost no resistance to fire. When flames reached that flimsy partition, the separate clubs effectively became one burning space. A building meant to hold hundreds of people had been subdivided in a way that made a single ignition point a threat to all of them at once.

## Warnings on Paper

The danger was not a secret. This same address had a history: the Teatre, the first of the clubs, opened in 2008 and was evacuated in June 2009 after a short circuit set its facade alight. Years later, in 2022, authorities ordered both the Fonda Milagros and its neighbor closed, finding that they needed separate licenses and lacked basic requirements, including an approved limit on how many people they could hold. The clubs kept operating anyway. The closure order that might have prevented the tragedy existed, but it was never enforced into reality, and Spanish prosecutors opened an investigation into the deaths. Officials would have to reckon with how a venue under a standing closure order had stayed open on a crowded Saturday night, and whether the tragedy was less an accident than a failure long in the making.

## A City in Mourning

Murcia grieved in the open. Mayor Jose Ballesta declared three days of official mourning, and King Felipe VI joined the nation in expressing condolences. The Palacio de Deportes, the city's sports arena, was turned into a counseling center for the families and friends left behind. Behind the headline number were real households, many of them immigrant families who had crossed an ocean to build a future here, now planning funerals instead. The fire became a case study in how permits ignored and corners cut can end in loss, but for those who loved the thirteen, it was simply the morning the music stopped. In the days that followed, the flowers and candles left outside the ruined club spoke for a community that included people from across Latin America who had chosen to make Murcia their home.
