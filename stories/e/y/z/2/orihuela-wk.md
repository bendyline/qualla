# Orihuela

A historic cathedral city cradled between a mountain and the Segura River, ringed by one of Europe's great palm groves, birthplace of the poet Miguel Hernandez, and now split between an ancient inland town and a booming stretch of coast.

**Location:** 38.0856, -0.9469

**Categories:** Cities and towns, Costa Blanca, Valencian Community, History of the Province of Alicante, Literary landmarks, Travel destinations

## Introduction

A shepherd boy once herded goats on the slopes above Orihuela, memorizing poems he could not yet imagine publishing. His name was Miguel Hernandez, born here in 1910, and he would become one of the great voices of 20th-century Spanish poetry before dying in a prison cell in 1942, barely thirty-one, caught in the wreckage of the Civil War. His humble birthplace in the old town is a museum now. It is the truest introduction to Orihuela: a city that has produced kings' capitals and cathedrals but keeps its deepest pride for a poet who grew up watching this landscape from a hillside.

## Between the Sierra and the River

Orihuela sits in the Vega Baja del Segura, the low fertile plain of the Segura River, with a rocky sierra rising sharply behind the town and the ruins of a mountaintop Arab castle crowning the ridge. The land around it is generous. For centuries its farms have yielded lemons and oranges, almonds and olives, pomegranates and vegetables, along with the silk and wool that once fed local industry. Wrapping the historic center is the Palmeral de Orihuela, a vast palm grove of Islamic origin that ranks as the second largest in Europe, a green sea of fronds that gives the inland city an unexpectedly African, oasis-like edge.

## The Shepherd Poet

Miguel Hernandez belongs to Orihuela the way few writers belong to any single place. The son of a goat dealer, largely self-taught, he rose from these streets and hillsides to stand among the poets of Spain's great early-century flowering, his verse fierce with love, grief, and the countryside he came from. His life ended in tragedy. Imprisoned after the Spanish Civil War, he died young of tuberculosis in 1942, his talent cut brutally short. Orihuela has never let him go. The house where he was born survives as a memorial, and the poet remains the human heart of a town otherwise defined by stone monuments and old titles.

## A City of Churches and Crowns

Orihuela wears its history in its rank. It has been the capital of both a province and a kingdom, was named the first city of the province of Alicante in 1437, and once held the standing of a university center and an episcopal see. That pedigree left an extraordinary concentration of monuments in a small place: the medieval core alone holds five National Monuments. Beyond the great cathedral rise the church of Santa Justa y Rufina and the soaring Baroque church of Santo Domingo. The town's Holy Week processions are famous, and in the Diocesan collection hangs Velazquez's Temptation of Saint Thomas, a masterpiece few cities this size could dream of owning.

## Old Town, New Coast

There are really two Orihuelas. One is the ancient inland city of churches and palms, walkable and dense, where every corner seems to carry a plaque. The other is Orihuela Costa, a stretch of Mediterranean beaches and resort development that has become the engine of the modern economy, home to La Zenia Boulevard, the largest shopping mall on the Costa Blanca and Costa Calida. The mix has drawn a strikingly international population; some 40 percent of residents are foreign, roughly half of them British. Getting here is easy: Alicante-Elche airport is close, and high-speed AVE trains link the city to Madrid in about three and a quarter hours. Between mountain and sea, Orihuela holds its old soul and its new crowds in the same municipal embrace. It is a strange and very Spanish balance. Retirees from northern Europe sun themselves on beaches a short drive from a medieval cathedral, and a shepherd's poems are taught in schools within sight of the hill where he once herded goats. The town has always absorbed newcomers, from Romans and Muslims to Castilians and, now, the British; it simply keeps folding them into a story that began long before any of them arrived.

## Flight Context

Orihuela lies at about 38.086 N, 0.947 W, roughly 25 km inland from the Mediterranean in the Vega Baja del Segura. The unmistakable landmark from the air is the ruined Arab castle on the bare sierra directly above the old town, with the Segura River curling past and the dark mass of the Palmeral palm grove spreading beside the city. A viewing altitude of 2,500 to 5,000 feet above ground captures the town, the mountain, and the coast beyond. Nearest airport is Alicante-Elche (LEAL), roughly 35 km northeast; Region de Murcia International (LEMI) lies about 30 km southwest, with the resort coast of Orihuela Costa a short hop east. Clear skies and long visibility are the norm along this coast.
