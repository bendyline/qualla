# Gran Hotel Bali

For five years, the tallest building in all of Spain was not in Madrid or Barcelona but a beachfront hotel in the package-holiday capital of Benidorm.

**Location:** 38.5319, -0.1640

**Categories:** Skyscrapers, Hotels, Benidorm, Costa Blanca, Modern architecture, Spain

## Introduction

For a little over five years, the tallest building in all of Spain stood not in Madrid, not in Barcelona, but in a package-holiday town on the Costa Blanca. The Gran Hotel Bali rose 186 meters above Benidorm's beaches, a slab of pale concrete looming over a skyline already crowded with high-rises. When it opened its doors on 17 May 2002, it edged past Madrid's Torre Picasso to claim the national record. It held the title until November 2007, when the capital's Torre Espacio finally overtook it. In the meantime, the honor of Spain's highest roof belonged to a place better known for sangria buckets and sunburn than for architectural firsts.

## Taller Than the Capital

The tower is not one building but four, locked together into a single soaring mass by the architect Antonio Escario. It stands in La Cala de Benidorm, roughly 350 meters back from the sands of Playa Poniente, close enough to the Mediterranean that guests on the upper floors watch the horizon curve. The finished height of 186 meters was itself a compromise. The original construction plan had called for a tower of 210 meters, an ambition trimmed as the project dragged on. Even shortened, it dwarfed everything around it, and Benidorm's improbable forest of towers suddenly had a clear king.

## Fourteen Years of Concrete

The Bali did not go up quickly. Work began in 1988 and ground on for fourteen years before the hotel finally opened in 2002, the pace of construction almost matching the unhurried rhythm of the resort below. The final bill came to 90 million euros. What that money bought was vast: 776 rooms with space for as many as 2,000 guests, eighteen lifts to carry them skyward, gardens, and swimming pools. The internal layout is a puzzle of technical floors, machine rooms, mezzanines, a duplex crowning the two topmost suites, and a viewing platform where the whole sweep of the bay opens out below.

## The Vertical Race

Every year, athletes gather to run straight up the building. The stair climb to the viewpoint has become a fixture, and the times are brutal. In the first edition in 2003, the Australian Paul Crake hauled himself to the top in 4 minutes and 35 seconds. His record stood for eleven years, until 2014, when Ángel Llorens, a 43-year-old firefighter, shaved it to 4 minutes and 33 seconds. Christian Riedl went faster still in 2016, reaching the summit in 4 minutes and 20 seconds. The tower fills with other contests too: a Grand International Chess Festival has run here since the hotel opened, and a national foosball championship comes every May.

## A Star of the Skyline

The Bali's most audacious moment came in October 2010, when the Moroccan tightrope walker Mustafa "Danger" rode a motorbike across a steel cable strung more than 500 meters from the Tossal de La Cala to the hotel, balanced an average of 150 meters above the ground. The stunt earned a Guinness World Record. Long before that, the half-finished tower had its screen debut: in 1993, when the structure had climbed only to around its thirtieth floor, it appeared in Bigas Luna's film Golden Balls, starring Javier Bardem. It surfaced again in 2008 in the science-fiction film adaptation of Michel Houellebecq's The Possibility of an Island.

## Benidorm From Above

Seen from the air, the Bali reads as a marker for the whole city, a single pale finger among dozens of towers pressed against a curving coastline. This dense vertical skyline was a deliberate bargain: Benidorm chose to build up rather than out, packing hundreds of thousands of summer visitors into a compact strip and leaving the surrounding hills comparatively untouched. The Gran Hotel Bali sits at the western end of that skyline, watching over Playa Poniente, the fishing headland of the Tossal, and the wide blue plate of the Mediterranean beyond.

## Flight Context

The Gran Hotel Bali stands at 38.5319°N, 0.16399°W, on the western edge of Benidorm at the back of Playa Poniente. At 186 meters it is one of the most conspicuous man-made objects on the Costa Blanca and is visible from high cruising altitude in clear weather; a viewing altitude of 3,000 to 6,000 feet gives the best sense of the tower against Benidorm's clustered skyline, the Tossal de La Cala headland, and the long arc of the bay. Nearest airport is Alicante-Elche (LEAL), roughly 45 km southwest; Valencia (LEVC) lies about 130 km north and Murcia-Corvera (LEMI) further south. Coastal haze can soften the view on hot summer afternoons; mornings are clearest.
