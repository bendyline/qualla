# Puig Campana

A limestone giant above Benidorm carries a clean notch in its ridge that legend blames on the sword of Charlemagne's paladin Roland.

**Location:** 38.5972, -0.1958

**Categories:** Mountains, Province of Alicante, Folklore and Legends, Geology, Hiking, Natural Landmarks

## Introduction

From the beaches of Benidorm, one mountain refuses to look natural. Puig Campana rises 1,406 meters just ten kilometers inland, and near its western summit a clean gap has been cut from the ridgeline, as if something enormous took a bite from the skyline. Valencians call the notch El Portell, the Gate. The name that stuck, though, belongs to a knight who died fighting in the Pyrenees more than a thousand years ago and, by every serious account, never set foot on the coast of Alicante at all.

## Bell Peak and Its Hidden Twin

Puig Campana translates literally as Bell Peak, and it forms part of the Prebaetic System that ripples across the southeast of Spain. From the town of Finestrat at its foot, the mountain reads as a single wall of pale rock. Climb higher and the trick reveals itself: there are actually two summits, and the taller one hides behind the ridge, invisible from below. Only Aitana, at 1,559 meters, stands higher in the whole province of Alicante. What sets Puig Campana apart is not its height but that severed western crown, the missing wedge of stone that draws every eye from the sea and demands an explanation.

## Roland's Blade

The explanation the locals reached for is a story imported from the Pyrenees, where a real cleft called the Breche de Roland splits the peaks. Here the tale was transplanted whole. Roland, commander of Charlemagne's army, fought the leader of the Moorish host in single combat, and the duel ended on this summit. With his enemy flung to the ground, Roland raised his famous sword Durendal for the killing blow. He missed. The stroke carried through the mountain instead, shearing off a chunk of rock that flew out to sea and landed as the Benidorm islet offshore. A gentler version survives too: Roland's beloved Alda was fated to die at the last ray of sunset, so he cut the mountain to delay the sun and buy her a few more minutes of life.

## Stone Older Than the Story

It is a magnificent legend, and it is geologically impossible. Professor B. Granier of the Universite de Bretagne Occidentale, who did his doctoral fieldwork here in the 1980s, put the two rocks side by side. The core of Puig Campana is Late Jurassic limestone. The rock cropping out on the Benidorm islet is younger, Early Cretaceous of the Albian age, laid down millions of years apart in different seas. The islet could not have been sliced from the mountain because it was never part of it. The notch, in other words, is honest weathering and structure, not a sword-cut. Knowing this changes nothing about how the gap looks at dusk, when the setting sun sometimes threads straight through it.

## The Fire of 2009

The mountain's most recent wound needs no folklore. On 24 January 2009, a fierce wind toppled a high-voltage electricity pylon, and the sparks caught the dry winter scrub of the northeastern slopes. Roughly 1,000 hectares burned to ash. Around 15,000 people were pulled from their homes across 25 hillside urbanizations as the flames advanced through the tourist country below the peak. Years afterward, much of the scarred ground still lay abandoned, the question of how best to coax the pines and rockrose back into it left unresolved. On a mountain shaped by legends of destruction, this was destruction of the ordinary, modern kind.

## The Climb

Puig Campana rewards walkers and punishes the unprepared in equal measure. Several paths converge toward the top, most beginning at Font del Moli near Finestrat and grinding up a long, loose scree slope on the south face. The trail is barely a footpath in places, marked only by faded red symbols dabbed on rock, and in mist the crisscrossing routes near the summit dissolve into guesswork. Locals report a steady stream of tourists who set off casually and end up needing rescue. The tourist office in Finestrat hands out maps and asks to know your plans before you go. Reckon on five to eight hours round trip, carry your own water and food, and treat the Gate not as a landmark you can lean on but as a reminder of how easily this mountain confounds those who underestimate it.

## Flight Context

Puig Campana stands at 38.597 N, 0.196 W in the municipality of Finestrat, a substantial 1,406 m terrain obstacle only about 10 km inland from the Benidorm coast. The distinctive twin summit with its cleft western crown is unmistakable against the Mediterranean skyline; the offshore Benidorm islet lies just seaward as a paired landmark. Recommended viewing altitude is 6,500 to 9,000 ft to clear surrounding ridgelines while keeping the notch in profile. Alicante-Elche (LEAL) lies roughly 40 km southwest; Valencia (LEVC) is farther north. Expect afternoon mountain turbulence and rapidly forming orographic cloud that can hide the higher of the two peaks; morning offers the clearest views inland from the coast.
