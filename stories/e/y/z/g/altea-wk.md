# Altea

A few miles from Benidorm's skyscrapers, Altea kept its whitewashed old town, its blue-domed church and its artists, a Costa Blanca town that chose character over concrete.

**Location:** 38.6000, -0.0489

**Categories:** Coastal towns, Costa Blanca, Valencian Community, Province of Alicante, Historic towns, Arts and culture

## Introduction

A few miles up the coast from Benidorm's wall of skyscrapers, Altea made the opposite bet. Where its neighbor grew upward in towers of glass and concrete, this seaside town of about 24,600 people held onto its whitewashed alleys and its blue-domed church on the hill. Both towns share the same warm Costa Blanca light and the same stony beaches. Only one of them still feels like the old Mediterranean, a labyrinth of cobblestones and shuttered windows climbing toward a bell tower, and that difference is the whole point of Altea.

## From Althaia to Altea

People have lived on this sheltered stretch of coast for a very long time. Iberian tribes settled here first, and Greek colonists later founded a market town they called Althaia, a name that still echoes in the modern one. Rome came next, then the Visigoths, then centuries of Moorish rule, each culture leaving its mark on the hillside above the sea. When King James I of Aragon swept south during the reconquista of the 13th century, the town was fortified, and the remains of those old defensive walls can still be traced through the streets of the old town today, stone thresholds worn smooth by eight hundred years of feet.

## The White Town on the Hill

The heart of Altea is the casco antiguo, known locally as the Poble Antic or El Fornet. It is a place best explored on foot and preferably without a map, a tangle of steep, narrow lanes lined with typical white houses and broken constantly by flights of steps. Everything climbs toward the parish church of Nuestra Señora del Consuelo, whose blue-and-white tiled domes rise over the rooftops and have become the emblem of the entire Costa Blanca. Cars are banned from the core, parking left to the edges, so the old town stays quiet, the loudest sound often just footsteps on stone and the clink of cutlery from a terrace restaurant.

## The Town That Kept Its Soul

Tourism reached Altea in the 1950s, the same wave that would soon transform this coast beyond recognition. Yet Altea resisted the fate of much of the overdeveloped Costa Blanca, and Benidorm in particular. Rather than bulldoze its past for high-rise hotels, the town leaned into its character, and the choice paid off. Today it draws visitors precisely because it feels Spanish, an antidote to the package-holiday strip a short bus ride away. The two districts sit side by side, the flat, commercial beachfront below and the picturesque old town above, and it is the climb between them that most people remember.

## Artists, Markets, and Coca

That preserved character has made Altea a magnet for artists and artisans, who fill the studios and galleries along Carrer Sant Miquel and Carrer Major, near the main church. On summer nights an open-air handcrafts market spills across the Plaça de l'Església beside the church, its stalls glowing with jewelry, pottery, and painting. The kitchen keeps its own traditions too. The town bakes an Altean version of coca, the Catalan and Valencian flatbread cousin of pizza, called coca a la llumà, fired in a special wood oven and served, by custom, on Thursdays. Down on the beaches to the north, seasonal chiringuitos open through the summer for a plate and a drink at the water's edge.

## Flight Context

Altea sits on the coast at 38.600°N, 0.049°W, between Benidorm and Calpe. It is a low-lying coastal town, so a viewing altitude of 1,500-3,000 ft gives the best perspective on the old quarter climbing its hillside. The unmistakable landmark is the blue-and-white tiled twin domes of the church of Nuestra Señora del Consuelo, crowning the whitewashed old town above the marina and the shingle beaches. Inland, the pale ridges of the Serra de Bernia rise behind the town. Nearest airport is Alicante-Elche (LEAL), about 55 km southwest; Valencia (LEVC) lies roughly 100 km north. Coastal sea breezes and afternoon haze are common; mornings usually offer the clearest air.
