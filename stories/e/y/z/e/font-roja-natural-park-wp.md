# Font Roja Natural Park

On a shaded mountain flank above Alcoy, one of eastern Spain's last great holm-oak forests survives where the surrounding hills were long ago stripped bare.

**Location:** 38.6611, -0.5098

**Categories:** Natural parks, Valencian Community, Mountains, Province of Alicante, Mediterranean forest

## Introduction

Climb the shaded northern flank of the Serra del Menejador on a blazing August afternoon, and the thermometer falls with every switchback. Down on the plains around Alcoy the light hammers white off bare rock, and the cicadas roar. Up here, beneath a dense canopy of holm oak, the air turns cool, green, and faintly damp. This is the Font Roja, the "red spring" that gives the mountain its name, and it is one of the best-preserved natural areas in the whole Valencian Community, a pocket of old Mediterranean woodland that the axe and the grazing goat somehow never finished off.

## A Forest the Lowlands Lost

Most of eastern Spain's mountains were long ago cleared for firewood, charcoal, and pasture, their slopes left to scrub. The Font Roja is the exception. Its ridges even carry names that read like a field guide: El Carrascar de la Font Roja for its carrascas, the holm oaks that shade the trail, and La Teixereta for its teixos, the yews that cling on in cooler corners. The secret is the mountain's orientation. These north-facing slopes stay damp and sheltered from the fiercest sun, holding moisture long enough for a shady, layered forest to thrive where the sun-blasted southern hills manage only rosemary and thyme. Walk in from the plain and the change is physical, the temperature dropping as the canopy closes overhead.

## Protected by Proclamation

The Generalitat Valenciana, the regional government, declared the Font Roja a natural park on 13 April 1987, drawing a legal line around 2,298 hectares of ridge and forest. The park sits in the comarca of Alcoià, in the north of Alicante province, and by the standards of a coastline better known for high-rise resorts, it is a startling contrast. A short drive separates the crowded beaches of the Costa Blanca from a mountain where the loudest sound is wind in the oaks. Conservation here was not about restoring something lost. It was about protecting something that had, remarkably, never quite gone away.

## The Roof of the Alcoià

The land rises in a long, aligned spine of three massifs: L'Alt de Sant Antoni, El Carrascar de la Font Roja, and La Teixereta. Their high point is the summit of the Serra del Menejador, which tops out at 1,356 metres, the loftiest ground in the park. From up there the whole geography clicks into place. The forested folds fall away toward Alcoy and Ibi below, and on a clear day the eye can carry all the way to the glitter of the Mediterranean. It is a viewpoint that rewards the climb, a rare vantage where woodland, valley, and distant sea line up in a single sweep.

## Between Two Working Towns

The Font Roja is no remote wilderness. It rises directly between the busy towns of Alcoy and Ibi, close enough that generations of local families have treated its cool heights as a summer refuge from the heat below. The park also anchors a wider green belt: just to the northwest lies the Serra Mariola Natural Park, another swathe of protected Valencian mountain, while the medieval Barxell Castle stands guard in the country between them. Together they form a corridor of forest and stone that has outlasted the mills, roads, and resorts crowding in from every side, a reminder that even in one of Spain's most developed regions, the old mountain endures.

## Flight Context

Font Roja Natural Park centers on 38.661°N, 0.510°W, with its high point at the Serra del Menejador summit (1,356 m / about 4,450 ft). Plan a cruising altitude of roughly 6,500-7,500 ft to clear the ridgeline comfortably. From the air the park reads as a dark, densely forested spine standing out against the paler, drier hills around it, wedged between the towns of Alcoy and Ibi, with the larger Serra Mariola massif to the northwest. Nearest airport is Alicante-Elche (LEAL), about 55 km southeast; Valencia (LEVC) lies roughly 90 km north. Expect mountain turbulence and rapidly building cloud on the windward slopes; best visibility comes on calm, clear mornings before afternoon convection develops.
