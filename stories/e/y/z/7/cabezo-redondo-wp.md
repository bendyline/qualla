# Cabezo Redondo

A Bronze Age regional capital on a round hill near Villena, where archaeologists found a gold treasure, the weights of a 3,000-year-old loom, and the outline of a real town.

**Location:** 38.6433, -0.8956

**Categories:** Archaeological sites in the Valencian Community, Bronze Age sites in Spain, Argaric culture, Archaeology, Ancient history, Villena

## Introduction

In 2008, diggers on a rounded hill near Villena lifted forty-eight clay weights out of the ground, along with the fragments of the loom they once hung from. Together they told a quiet, remarkable story: that some three thousand years ago, someone stood at a tall vertical frame here and wove cloth, the weights pulling the warp threads taut exactly as the loom's designers intended. It is easy to picture the gold and the grandeur of the Bronze Age and forget the ordinary hours of a working life. Cabezo Redondo preserves both, and the humble loom weights may be the more moving find.

## A Capital Before Capitals

This was no scattering of huts. Cabezo Redondo was a genuine regional center, inhabited between 1500 and 1100 BC and most likely belonging to the Argaric culture that dominated southeastern Iberia in the Bronze Age. The evidence points to something close to a capital: a central location, considerable size, deliberate urban planning, a dense population, and busy workshops turning out agricultural goods, livestock products, metalwork, and textiles all at once. Archaeologists believe that people from smaller settlements scattered around the ancient Villena Lagoon drew together here, consolidating into a single dominant town. Long before Romans, Moors, or Christian kings drew their borders across this land, a sophisticated community was already organizing life on this hill.

## Gold in the Spring of 1963

The hill had teased investigators for generations. The first probes may date back to Juan Vilanova i Piera around 1870, but it was the archaeologist Jose Maria Soler who began systematic study in 1959, prompted by the discovery of scattered objects of gold, silver, and copper. His patience was rewarded in the spring of 1963, when he unearthed the Cabezo Redondo treasure: thirty-five personal adornments, a diadem, rings, bracelets, pendants, beads, spirals, bands, and a small gold ingot, together weighing 150 grams. These were the ornaments of people who valued beauty and status, worn on real bodies in this real town. The treasure is now held in the Archaeological Museum of Villena, where it can still be seen.

## What the Quarries Took

The site that survives is only a fragment of what once existed. Cabezo Redondo originally spread across the entire hill, but by the time Soler made his great find, local gypsum quarries had already eaten away much of the ancient settlement, grinding centuries of archaeology into building material. It is a familiar and painful pattern, the past consumed for the needs of the present before anyone recognized what was being lost. From 1963 onward, at least, the remaining ground was protected and studied, and in 1968 it was folded into Villena's Historic-Artistic Complex and declared public land, safe from the quarries that had claimed the rest.

## The Dig That Never Ends

The story of Cabezo Redondo is still being written, one summer at a time. In recent years the University of Alicante, joined by the universities of Valencia and Granada, has run annual excavation campaigns on the hill, led by Mauro S. Hernandez Perez with Gabriel Garcia Atienzar and Virginia Barciela. Each season peels back a little more of the Bronze Age town, and finds like the 2008 loom show how much ordinary detail still waits in the earth. What began as a treasure hunt has matured into a long, careful conversation with a vanished community, coaxing out not just its gold but the texture of how its people actually lived.

## Flight Context

Cabezo Redondo lies at 38.64 degrees N, 0.90 degrees W, on a low rounded hill, the cabezo redondo that gives it its name, about 2 km from Villena in the northwest of Alicante province. From the air the site itself is subtle, a modest hillock marked by excavation terraces and the pale scars of old gypsum quarrying, best located by reference to the nearby town of Villena and its prominent hilltop castle just to the east. A viewing altitude of 2,000 to 3,500 feet above the terrain frames the hill and the surrounding plain, once the basin of the ancient Villena Lagoon. The nearest major airport is Alicante-Elche (LEAL) to the southeast; Valencia (LEVC) lies to the north. The dry interior air usually delivers clear skies and excellent visibility.
