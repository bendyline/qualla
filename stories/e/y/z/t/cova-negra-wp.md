# Cova Negra

A dark riverside cave near Xativa where Neanderthals sheltered for over a hundred thousand years, hunting doves and leaving behind the bones of their children.

**Location:** 38.9669, -0.4752

**Categories:** Archaeology, Prehistoric sites, Neanderthal sites, Caves, Natural sites, Valencian Community

## Introduction

The name means Black Cave, and its darkness has kept an unlikely record. Above the Albaida river near Xativa, this shadowed mouth in the rock was a shelter that Neanderthals returned to across an almost unimaginable span of time, long before our own species reached this coast. They left stone tools, the bones of the birds they ate, and, more soberly, the bones of their own dead, including several children.

## The Black Cave

Cova Negra opens onto a valley beside the Albaida river, a short distance from the town of Xativa in the interior of Valencia province. It is not a grand cavern but a practical one, the kind of dry, sheltered overhang that made sense to anyone moving through this landscape on foot. The setting has been protected as a Cultural Heritage Site across some 57 hectares, and since 2006 it has also been designated a Municipal Natural Site. Its scientific life began in 1933, when a local priest named G. Vines identified the cave as an archaeological treasure. Nearly a century of digging has followed, turning up not only bones but the flaked flint tools of the Mousterian, the stone technology that Neanderthals carried and made across Europe. Those chipped edges are the everyday hardware of the cave, the knives and scrapers that turned a carcass into a meal.

## Deep Time

How old is the human story here? For years the estimates were uncertain, until a 2019 study produced the first proper chronology using ESR and uranium-thorium dating. The results pushed the occupation back further than anyone had assumed: Neanderthals used Cova Negra between roughly 273,000 and 146,000 years ago, in the late Middle Pleistocene. These were not long, settled stays. The evidence points to sporadic, short-term visits, a group pausing here for a while before moving on, then another group generations or millennia later doing the same. The cave was less a home than a way station, used and abandoned and used again across a hundred thousand years.

## Doves on the Menu

One of the cave's surprises lies in its garbage. Excavators recovered 247 bird remains from at least 18 species, doves and choughs foremost among them. That matters, because catching small, fast birds is not the crude work the old cartoon of the club-swinging caveman suggests. It takes patience, cunning, and skill. Cova Negra is not alone in this: similar bird bones at caves like Bolomor, Gorham's Cave, and Ibex Cave show that Neanderthals across the northwestern Mediterranean routinely hunted quick, elusive prey. The picture that emerges is of resourceful people reading their environment closely and taking whatever it offered, from large game down to the doves flushed from the cliffs above the river.

## Those Who Stayed

The most affecting finds are the human ones. Over the years the cave has yielded 25 fragments of Neanderthal remains, belonging to seven individuals. In 2013 researchers added a piece of an adult skull, a fragment from a child's cranium, and a child's premolar tooth. What lingers is how young many of them were: among the seven, a teenager and four children. These were families, not specimens, people who carried their young through this valley, who sheltered here when the weather turned, and who buried or lost their children in the same dark cave that kept them warm. Standing in that entrance now, it is hard not to feel the weight of it, a hundred thousand years of ordinary life and grief pressed into a single hollow in the rock.

## Flight Context

Cova Negra lies at 38.967 degrees north, 0.475 degrees west, in the valley of the Albaida river just outside Xativa in Valencia's interior. From 2,000 to 4,000 feet the river valley, the limestone ridges around it, and the compact town of Xativa with its long hilltop castle nearby make useful visual references. The nearest major airport is Valencia (LEVC), roughly 55 km to the north; Alicante-Elche (LEAL) lies to the south and Murcia (LEMI) farther on. Inland visibility is generally clear, with morning mist possible in the river valley before it lifts.
