# Collegiate Basilica of Gandia

Gandia's great Gothic church, La Seu, owes its rank to a Borgia pope and its patron saint to a Borgia duke, and it survived a Civil War fire that gutted its interior.

**Location:** 38.9670, -0.1802

**Categories:** Churches, Gandia, Gothic Architecture, House of Borgia, Province of Valencia, Bien de Interes Cultural

## Introduction

The Borgia name usually calls up poison, intrigue, and a scandalous Renaissance papacy. In Gandia it means something quieter and closer to home. Here the Borgias, or Borja in their native Valencian, were the dukes of the town, and their fingerprints are all over its principal church. A Borgia pope granted it its rank. A Borgia duke became its saint. And the great Gothic pile they favored, La Seu, still stands at the center of Gandia, having outlived not only the family's notoriety but a twentieth-century fire that nearly gutted it.

## Two Centuries of Gothic

The Collegiate Basilica of Santa Maria, La Seu to everyone in Gandia, went up slowly. Construction began in the fourteenth century and was not finished until roughly two centuries later, an unhurried span that produced a fine example of Valencian Gothic in the Catalan-Aragonese manner. The plan is disciplined and plain: a single central nave with chapels tucked into the thickness of the walls on either side. Its architecture is austere by nature, the drama meant to come not from the stonework but from what once filled it, and for centuries that austerity was cloaked in altarpieces, paintings, and sculpture.

## A Borgia Pope's Favor

The church owes its elevated status to the most infamous member of the family. Rodrigo Borgia, the Valencian who ruled the Catholic Church as Pope Alexander VI, granted Santa Maria the rank of collegiate church by papal bull in 1499. It was no small favor. A collegiate church was served by a chapter of canons, a mark of importance that lifted Gandia's parish toward the prestige of a cathedral without quite becoming one. That a reigning pope reached down to distinguish this particular church says everything about how tightly the Borgias were bound to the town they held as dukes.

## The Duke Who Became a Saint

One Borgia turned the family's reputation on its head. Francis Borgia, the fourth Duke of Gandia and a great-grandson of Pope Alexander VI, renounced worldly power to become a Jesuit and was later canonized, ending up the patron saint of the very ducal city his family had ruled. It is a striking arc for a dynasty better known for ambition than sanctity: from a pope whose name is a byword for corruption to a duke venerated as a saint, all within a few generations and all attached to the same small Valencian town and its collegiate church.

## The Doors of Damian Forment

For all its austerity, La Seu holds one artistic treasure worth crossing a town for. Among its several portals, the southern doorway known as the Puerta de Santa Maria and the Puerta de los Apostoles count among the earliest works of Damian Forment, the sculptor regarded as a precursor of the Spanish Renaissance. To find early Forment here is to catch a great artist at the beginning, before the reputation, working in a moment when Gothic habits were just starting to loosen into Renaissance ideas. In the carved figures around these doors you can watch that transition happening in stone, the stiff verticality of the Gothic giving way to something rounder and more human. The doors are a hinge between two eras, set into the fabric of the church itself, and among the few original works to come through the twentieth century intact.

## What the War Took

The church was declared a National Historic Monument in 1931, and then very nearly destroyed. During the Spanish Civil War, on August 2, 1936, the church was set ablaze. The high altar burned along with the rich interior of altarpieces, paintings, and sculptures that had accumulated over centuries, an act of destruction that stripped the church of nearly everything movable inside it. La Seu survived as a shell. Restoration began in the 1940s, and the lost square presbytery was rebuilt in a polygonal Neo-Gothic form. The building standing today is a survivor twice over, of the family that made it and of the war that tried to unmake it.

## Flight Context

La Seu, the Collegiate Basilica of Gandia, stands at 38.967 N, 0.180 W in the heart of the city of Gandia, on the coastal plain of the Valencian Community about 65 km south of Valencia city and just inland of the Gandia beach and marina. From the air, look for the compact Gothic church and its tower rising from the dense old town, set between the Mediterranean shore to the east and the sierra behind Gandia to the west. Recommended viewing altitude 2,500 to 4,500 ft AGL over the coastal plain. Valencia (LEVC) is the nearest major field to the north; Alicante-Elche (LEAL) lies farther south. Coastal visibility is usually excellent, with possible sea breeze haze and cloud building against the inland hills by afternoon.
