# Archaeological Museum of Gandia

Housed in a medieval hospital at the edge of Gandia's old walls, this museum guards the treasure of the Parpalló cave, one of the greatest hoards of Ice Age portable art ever found.

**Location:** 38.9670, -0.1779

**Categories:** Archaeological museums in the Valencian Community, Museums in Valencia, Route of the Borgias, Archaeology, Prehistory, Valencia

## Introduction

Long before anyone built a museum, Ice Age hunters were already making art and stacking it up. In a cave in the hills above Gandia, over thousands of years, they left behind more than five thousand small stone plaquettes, each one scratched or painted with horses, deer, mountain goats and mysterious signs. It is one of the richest collections of portable art the Old Stone Age ever produced, and much of it now rests a few kilometres away, inside a former hospital at the edge of the town's medieval walls.

## An Ice Age Sketchbook

The Parpallo cave is the reason this museum matters beyond its region. Its artists worked across an astonishing span of time, roughly 26,000 to 11,000 years ago, filling flat stones with finely engraved and painted figures in black and shades of red and yellow. Horses gallop, deer turn their heads, goats stand alert, and among them run geometric marks whose meaning is lost to us. Taken together, the plaquettes form something close to a stratified diary of the imagination, layer upon layer of image-making by generations who never met. Few Palaeolithic sites anywhere on the Mediterranean have yielded so much, and to stand before a case of these stones is to look directly into the mind of the deep human past.

## A Museum in a Hospital

The building itself has a long memory. The Archaeological Museum of Gandia, known by the acronym MAGa, opened in 1972 with the backing of the Diputacion de Valencia inside the old Sant Marc Hospital, a medieval institution set at the southeastern corner of Gandia's town walls, on the left bank of the Serpis river. Its story since has not been smooth. From 1987 to 2003 the museum stood closed to the public, but the years were not idle: behind locked doors, its collections were researched, documented and painstakingly restored. When the doors reopened, the museum returned with its holdings better understood than ever before.

## From the First Fires to Iron

The permanent exhibition sweeps across the whole prehistory of La Safor, from the earliest inhabitants of the Paleolithic to the communities of the Iron Age. Alongside Parpallo, the museum draws on other key sites of the region: the very ancient cave of Bolomor, where some of the area's oldest human traces survive, and the cave known as Les Meravelles, or the Marvels, on Gandia's own doorstep. Walking the galleries is a lesson in scale. The tools, bones and images gathered here compress tens of thousands of years of human presence into a few rooms, reminding visitors that people have been living, hunting and making things in these hills for longer than written history can reach.

## The Town Above the Caves

Modern Gandia wears a different, more famous identity. This was the seat of the Dukes of Gandia, the branch of the Borgia family that gave the town a splendid ducal palace and a place on the Route of the Borgias, that trail of Renaissance power and intrigue. The museum quietly widens the frame. It sets the glittering dukes of the 15th and 16th centuries against a backdrop of hunters who worked stone by firelight, and in doing so it makes the point that Gandia's history did not begin with its nobles. Beneath the palaces and the beach resorts lies a far older town, one whose first residents left their mark not in ledgers but in carved rock.

## Why the Stones Still Speak

There is something disarming about the Parpallo plaquettes. They are small, portable, personal, made not for a temple wall but perhaps to be held in the hand. Across an unimaginable distance of time, they carry an impulse anyone can recognise: to look at an animal and want to draw it, to make a mark that outlasts the moment. That the museum protecting them sits in a repurposed hospital, itself centuries old, only deepens the sense of continuity. In this modest building on the Serpis, the whole arc of human making is on display, from the first artists of the Valencian coast to the hands that carefully cleaned and catalogued their work.

## Flight Context

The Archaeological Museum of Gandia lies at 38.97 degrees N, 0.18 degrees W, in the historic core of Gandia in the coastal plain of La Safor, southern Valencia. From the air, orient on the old town beside the Serpis river, marked by the ducal palace and the tower of the Collegiate Church, with the wide sweep of Gandia's beach and port a few kilometres to the east and the Mondúver hills, home of the Parpalló cave, rising to the west. Best viewed from 2,000 to 4,500 feet in clear conditions. Valencia (LEVC) is roughly 65 km to the north; Alicante-Elche (LEAL) about 110 km to the south. Sea breezes keep the coastal air clear on many mornings, though summer humidity can haze the plain by afternoon.
