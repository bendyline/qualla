# Ducal Palace of Gandia

The palace where the Borja family lived produced a Catholic saint from a dynasty better known for poison and papal scandal.

**Location:** 38.9658, -0.1801

**Categories:** Palaces, History, Religion, Route of the Borgias, Architecture, Gandia

## Introduction

Say the name Borgia and people think of poison, papal intrigue, and Renaissance ambition without limits. Yet in this palace pressed against the old walls of Gandia, that same family produced something the popular legend never expects: a saint. Francis Borgia was born here in 1510, fourth Duke of Gandia and great-grandson of Pope Alexander VI. He would trade the dukedom for a monk's habit. The Ducal Palace holds both stories at once, the worldly and the holy, in the same stone rooms.

## A Castle That Learned to Be a Home

The oldest bones of the palace date to the 15th century, built in the Valencian Gothic style favored across the medieval Kingdom of Valencia. It began as something closer to a fortress than a residence, an urban castle in civil Gothic dress, hard-edged and defensive. Attached to the wall that once ringed the city, it guarded as much as it housed. Over generations the fortress softened into a comfortable fortified palace, its grim purpose giving way to grandeur. Two gates survive from that long transformation, one Gothic and one Renaissance, standing side by side like a timeline you can walk through. The change tells you something about the family inside: they no longer needed to fear the town they ruled.

## Through the Rounded Arch

You enter through a robust door crowned by a rounded arch, the oldest element of the Gothic palace along with the hallways just beyond it. The passage opens without warning onto an imposing central parade ground, a courtyard scaled for horses and ceremony rather than intimacy. From here an impressive exterior staircase climbs toward the great halls above, a stone theater built for arrivals and processions. The Dukes of Gandia understood that architecture is a language of power, and every visitor who crossed this courtyard read the message before a single word was spoken.

## Gold and Crowns

Upstairs, the palace abandons Gothic restraint for Baroque exuberance. The Salon de Coronas, the Crown Hall, and the Galeria Dorada, the Golden Gallery, are the showpieces of the interior, dense with ornament and gilding that catch what little light the deep rooms allow. These were the rooms where a ducal family displayed its taste and its wealth to anyone worth impressing. The azulejo tilework woven through the building, a hallmark of grand houses across the Valencian Community, adds color at the level of the feet and hands, a craft tradition as much a signature of this coast as the citrus groves outside.

## The Duke Who Became a Saint

Francis Borgia bent the family legend in an unexpected direction. Born in this palace on the 28th of October, 1510, he was a Grandee of Spain and a great-grandson of Pope Alexander VI, the most notorious of all the Borgia figures. As a boy he was drawn to the religious life, but his family sent him instead to the court of Emperor Charles V, where he was welcomed as a kinsman. In 1529 he married Leonor de Castro, a Portuguese noblewoman and lady-in-waiting to the Empress Isabella, and together they raised a family while he served the crown and held his dukedom. Then, in 1546, Leonor died. Her death turned him decisively toward faith. He renounced his titles, entered the newly founded Society of Jesus, and rose to become its third Superior General. The Church canonized him in 1670.

## Holy Ground on the Borgia Trail

After his canonization, the Jesuits made his birthplace their own, restoring the Gothic palace and turning it into a sanctuary to the saint who had left it behind. That devotion is why the building survives so intact: it was tended not as a relic behind glass but as holy ground, a living shrine. Today the Palau Ducal is protected as a Bien de Interes Cultural and stands among the great stops on the Route of the Borgias, the trail that threads together the Valencian sites where this improbable dynasty rose, ruled, and reached for something beyond power. Visitors cross from Gothic hallway to Baroque gallery in a single afternoon, tracing not only an evolution of style but the strange arc of a family remembered for both its scandals and its one saint.

## Flight Context

The Ducal Palace of Gandia sits at 38.9658 N, 0.1801 W in the compact old town of Gandia, roughly four kilometers inland from the long beaches of the Gulf of Valencia. From the air the palace reads as a dense block of pale stone and tiled roofs against the tighter medieval street grid, with the Collegiate Basilica nearby as a taller companion landmark. Best appreciated at 1,500 to 2,500 feet in clear coastal light. The nearest major field is Valencia (LEVC), about 65 km north; Alicante-Elche (LEAL) lies to the south. Coastal haze and summer heat shimmer can soften detail; morning offers the sharpest view.
