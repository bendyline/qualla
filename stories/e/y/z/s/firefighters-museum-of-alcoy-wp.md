# Firefighters Museum of Alcoy

In a mill town that once manufactured matches, an old textile factory by the Serpis now guards the tools and engines of the people who fought its fires.

**Location:** 38.7030, -0.4719

**Categories:** Museums, Firefighting, Alcoy, Province of Alicante, Industrial Heritage, Bien de Interes Cultural

## Introduction

For two centuries, Alcoy made a business of fire. The mills crowded along the Serpis turned out paper and textiles, and among the town's many factories were ones that manufactured matches, boxes of small flames sold by the millions. It is a fitting irony, then, that one of those old industrial buildings now houses a monument to the opposite trade. The Firefighters Museum of Alcoy, known locally as MUBOMA, keeps the engines, hoses, and helmets of the people whose job was to put out what a factory town so easily started.

## A City Built on Water and Fire

Alcoy sits in rugged foothills north of Alicante city, where two headstreams gather to form the Serpis river. That falling water made the town. From the eighteenth century it grew into one of the leading paper producers of the Kingdom of Valencia, its hydraulic mills feeding a demand for specialized papers. A royal textile factory arrived in 1800, and Alcoy went on to host one of the first industrial schools in Spain, a place proud of its machines and the skills to run them. It became a city of looms and matchworks, of deep ravines and the fine wrought-iron bridges spanning them that earned it the nickname the city of bridges. Where there are factories, boilers, and dense stone streets packed tight against one another, there is always the risk of flame, and a town this industrial needed people trained to answer it.

## The Factory Turned Museum

The museum occupies a building known locally as the Factory of Plugs, an industrial hall raised in cut stone in the mid-nineteenth century to house a textile business, standing close by the Serpis. Its bones are honest and plain: a long, linear structure of a ground floor and two upper storeys, framed almost entirely in pine timber, with a front that borrows a few restrained neoclassical touches. A later brick chimney tapers upward in a conical shaft, the kind of stack that once marked every working mill in the valley. Choosing a former factory was fitting: the museum honors a working trade, and it does so inside a working building, its timber and stone still bearing the character of the industry that raised it. Converted for cultural use, the old workspace now holds a very different cargo than cloth.

## The First Engines

Inside is the accumulated inheritance of the fire stations across the province of Alicante, gathered over decades and set out for visitors. The collection runs to the earliest vehicles pressed into the work of extinguishing fires, alongside the smaller instruments of the trade: pumps and nozzles, ladders and lamps, the equipment and uniforms that firefighters wore into danger. Each object is a piece of practical history, evidence of how a dangerous profession slowly professionalized, from hand-worked apparatus and horse-drawn tanks to motorized engines. A brass pump or a scorched helmet carries the marks of real emergencies, and standing among them you begin to sense the weight of the gear these crews hauled and the heat they walked into. Seen together, the machines trace the long effort to give people a fighting chance against the flames that industrial towns like Alcoy knew all too well.

## Keeping the Profession's Memory

MUBOMA opened to the public in July 2011 as the first firefighters museum in the Valencian Community, run under the province's fire consortium. It found an audience quickly, passing 9,000 visitors within two years of opening its doors. The appeal is easy to understand. Firefighting is one of the few jobs whose entire purpose is to walk toward what everyone else flees, and a museum devoted to it is less about hardware than about the people who carried it. Behind every restored engine stands a shift that answered a bell, a crew that climbed into a burning mill or a family's home, sometimes at real cost. By preserving their engines and their working gear, Alcoy honors generations of firefighters whose names rarely made the record but whose service protected the mills, the bridges, and the neighbors of a town that lived so close to fire.

## Flight Context

The Firefighters Museum of Alcoy stands at 38.703 N, 0.472 W in the town of Alcoy (Alcoi), set in the interior foothills north of Alicante city near the headwaters of the Serpis river. From the air, look for the old mill building with its tapering brick chimney close to the river, within a dense town crossed by its distinctive wrought-iron bridges over deep ravines. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000 to 5,000 ft AGL, mindful of the surrounding sierra terrain that rises sharply around the valley. Alicante-Elche (LEAL) lies roughly 45 km south-southeast; Valencia (LEVC) is farther north. The mountain-ringed setting can trap morning haze and generate afternoon thermals, so clear midmorning light gives the best view of the town's bridges and rooftops.
