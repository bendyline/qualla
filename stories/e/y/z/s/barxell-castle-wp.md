# Barxell Castle

A small 13th-century frontier tower on a rocky knuckle in the pine forests above Alcoy, one link in a chain of medieval strongholds guarding the Serpis valley.

**Location:** 38.6908, -0.5301

**Categories:** castles, history, medieval, mountains, archaeology, landmarks

## Introduction

Drive the CV-795 between Alcoy and Banyeres de Mariola, and if you are not watching for it, you will miss it entirely. Barxell Castle does not loom. It sits low and compact on a rocky mound in the middle of a pine forest, 800 meters up, a knuckle of stone among the trees. There is no town clustered at its foot, no ticket booth, no crowd. Just a medieval building that has kept its lonely watch over this mountain pass for some seven hundred years, close enough to the road to touch and quiet enough to overlook.

## A Tower in the Pines

The setting is the first thing you notice, and the most memorable. Barxell rises from its rocky outcrop where the pines close in on every side, a stubby fortress of the 13th century that seems to have grown out of the rock rather than been set upon it. At 800 meters, the air is cooler and the light sharper than on the coast an hour away. Nearby lies the place the old records call Solanetes, a spot that held a small population back in the Muslim era. The castle and that vanished hamlet belong to the same story: people making a life in high, hard country where flat ground is scarce and defensible ground is everything.

## The Valley of Polop

Barxell stands in the Valley of Polop, a natural enclave prized for its scenery and pinned between two protected mountain landscapes, the Font Roja and the Serra Mariola. This is limestone country, folded and forested, where springs surface and rivers begin their run toward the Mediterranean. The valley's beauty is not incidental to the castle; it is the whole point of its placement. Whoever built here wanted the high ground and the long view, and got both. Today that same commanding position, once chosen for control, offers walkers and drivers one of the more quietly striking sights in the Alicante highlands.

## Living on the Frontier

To understand a building like Barxell, you have to picture the border it once guarded. These mountains sat along the shifting seam between Christian and Muslim Iberia in the 1200s, a frontier where control of a pass or a spring could decide who farmed the valley below. A tower here was not decoration but insurance: a place to watch the road, shelter livestock, and signal trouble. The scattering of fortified farmhouses and small strongholds through these hills speaks to a landscape where ordinary life and military necessity were braided together, and where a modest stone tower could mean the difference between a harvest kept and a harvest lost.

## One Link in a Chain of Stone

Barxell was never meant to stand alone. It belongs to a network of medieval towers and castles threaded through the central Serpis valley, structures that historians of the region have studied together as a single defensive system. Its neighbors are famous cousins: the Cocentaina Castle to the north and the Almohad fortress at Banyeres de Mariola, itself a stop on the Route of the Castles of Vinalopó, the trail that strings such strongholds together elsewhere in the province. Read that way, Barxell stops being an isolated curiosity beside a country road and becomes what it always was, one carefully placed piece in a landscape organized, centuries ago, around who could see whom coming.

## Flight Context

Barxell Castle sits at 38.691 N, 0.530 W, at 800 meters elevation on a rocky mound in pine forest beside the CV-795, roughly midway between Alcoy and Banyeres de Mariola in the Valley of Polop. From the air, look for a small isolated stone structure crowning a wooded knoll, framed by the surrounding ridges of the Font Roja and Serra Mariola parks. It is compact, so it rewards low, slow passes rather than high cruise. Nearest major airport is Alicante-Elche (LEAL), about 55 km southeast; Valencia (LEVC) is roughly 90 km north. Best viewing altitude 2,000-3,000 ft AGL in clear morning light, before mountain haze thickens.
