# Cartagena Naval Museum

A museum on Cartagena's seafront tells the story of Spanish naval construction and guards its greatest relic, the original Peral, the world's first electric battery-powered submarine.

**Location:** 37.5979, -0.9872

**Categories:** Museums, Maritime, Naval, History, Science and technology, Cartagena

## Introduction

In a long stone building on Cartagena's seafront rests a cigar-shaped hull of riveted steel, barely twenty-two meters of it, that once frightened the navies of the world. This is the original Peral, and around it the Cartagena Naval Museum has gathered the tools, charts, uniforms, and torpedoes of a port that has built and sailed warships for centuries. It is a small museum with an outsized story to tell.

## A Building With a Hard Past

The museum opened in 1986, first in a 1926 building that had trained apprentices for the state shipbuilder, and later moved to grander and grimmer quarters on the waterfront. Its current home is the former Maritime Instruction Headquarters, raised in 1786 by the military engineer Mateo Vodopich and facing the Botes Basin. That handsome facade hides a heavy history: since its construction the building has served as a state penitentiary, a presidio, and, after the Civil War, a barracks for training sailors. Since a 2005 agreement, it shares its rooms with the Polytechnic University of Cartagena, the museum occupying the southern half of the ground floor, learning and remembrance under one old roof.

## Rooms Full of the Sea

The collection wanders through the whole craft of naval life. In the Arsenal Room lie the carpenter's planes and rigging tools of a working dockyard, models of sailing ships, and relics from famous wrecks, the treasure galleons Nuestra Senora de Atocha and Santa Margarita. A cartography room holds copies of old maps and letters, sextants, telegraphs, and the rudder of a nineteenth-century steam warship. Other halls trace the marine infantry through its battles, review the history of navy diving, and display flags, uniforms, and the fine polychrome saints once carried aboard. Among the treasures are copies of a Catalan atlas from 1450 and of Christopher Columbus's 1492 letter announcing a new world across the ocean.

## The Inventor and His Boat

One man dominates the museum, and he was born just up the hill. Isaac Peral y Caballero, born in Cartagena in 1851, was a naval officer and engineer who built something the world had not yet managed: a genuinely practical submarine. Launched on 8 September 1888, his Peral ran on banks of electric batteries driving twin motors, carried a torpedo tube, and could navigate reliably beneath the surface, the first vessel that truly could. A room here holds his personal papers and portrait; the museum has kept the boat itself since 2012. Peral's life did not match his invention's promise. Rejected and worn down by the navy's politics, he left the service and died in 1895, at only forty-three, his craft mothballed while other nations raced to copy the idea.

## Echoes of 1873

The museum does not shy from Cartagena's more turbulent chapters. One hall is given over to the agitation of the nineteenth century, the political upheavals, the military campaigns to Cuba, and, tellingly, remains from the bombardment the city suffered in 1873. Among the objects is a bullet from that shelling, a small, blunt piece of iron that carries the memory of a city that has more than once been fought over by its own countrymen. Alongside models of nearly every submarine Spain has operated, and the officers' quarters lifted from a mid-century boat, the collection insists on a simple truth. In Cartagena, the sea and its ships are not decoration. They are the thread the whole city hangs on.

## Flight Context

Centered near 37.60 N, 0.99 W, on Cartagena's seafront beside the harbor. From low level, 1,500-3,000 ft, the long eighteenth-century museum building fronts the water near the marina and cruise dock, with the historic Arsenal walls just to the west and the old town rising to Concepcion hill behind. The preserved Peral submarine sits near the waterfront. Nearest airports are Region de Murcia (Corvera) LEMI about 30 km northwest, Murcia-San Javier LELC to the northeast, and Alicante-Elche LEAL up the coast. Coastal light is best in the morning before the summer haze builds.
