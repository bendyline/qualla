# Terjit

In a gorge on the western edge of Mauritania's Adrar plateau, warm and cold springs rise beneath a curtain of date palms, creating one of the Sahara's most improbable green places.

**Location:** 20.2571, -13.0942

**Categories:** Adrar region, Populated places in Mauritania, Oases of Africa

## Introduction

Water should not be here, and yet it is. In a narrow gorge cut into the western edge of the Adrar plateau, a spring surfaces from bare rock and feeds a stream that runs for a few hundred meters beneath a dense canopy of date palms. The Sahara presses in on every side, but inside this cleft the air is cool and the ground is green. Terjit is an oasis in the truest sense of the word, a desert water source, and the contrast between the parched plateau above and the shaded stream below is what has drawn people here for generations.

## A Spring in the Rock

Terjit's water is its whole reason for being. The oasis is fed by springs that emerge from the rock at the base of the gorge, and unusually for the region, both warm and cold sources surface here, the cooler ones tempering the warm. The spring is among the only naturally flowing water in the Adrar, which is part of why this particular fold in the plateau became a settlement rather than just another dry canyon. The resulting stream nourishes a palm grove that stretches a few hundred meters down the valley floor, its shade dropping the temperature noticeably below the heat radiating off the surrounding desert.

## Geography of the Adrar

The oasis lies about 45 km by road south of Atar, the regional hub, and forms part of the commune of Maaden in the Aoujeft Department. The Adrar plateau itself is a sandstone massif rising out of the western Sahara, its escarpments and gorges hiding pockets of life that the open desert cannot sustain. Terjit nestles against the plateau's western edge, where the rock has been cut deeply enough to reach water. This is a landscape of sharp contrasts: bare ochre stone above, a ribbon of palm green below, and the dividing line between them as crisp as a coastline.

## Ceremony and Tradition

Terjit's significance has never been only practical. Historically the oasis served as a place of ceremony, used especially for weddings and, on rarer occasions, for the coronation of African princes. The reasons are not hard to imagine. In a region where shade and fresh water are scarce and precious, a cool green gorge with a flowing spring is the natural setting for the moments a community most wants to mark. Today the oasis draws visitors for the same qualities that once made it ceremonial ground: its charm, its fresh water, and its shade. A modest fee admits travelers, who can stay in tents pitched among the palms.

## Flight Context

Terjit lies at 20.26°N, 13.09°W on the western edge of the Adrar plateau, about 45 km south of Atar. The nearest airport is Atar International (GQPA); Nouakchott (GQNN) is the larger gateway to the southwest. From altitude, the oasis appears as a slender green line inside a canyon, sharply set against the surrounding sandstone. A viewing altitude of 2,000-4,000 ft AGL best reveals the grove against the plateau. Skies are usually clear in the dry season; harmattan dust can cut visibility considerably.
