# Basilica of Our Lady of the Forsaken

Valencia's beloved patron - affectionately called 'la Geperudeta' - watches over her city from a Baroque basilica built on the old Roman forum, veiled each spring in a mantle of flowers.

**Location:** 39.4762, -0.3748

**Categories:** religious sites, Valencia, Baroque architecture, history, Spanish culture, landmarks

## Introduction

There is a small barred window in one of the basilica's doors, and it is there for a reason. Even when the church is locked and dark, anyone passing through the passage that separates it from Valencia's cathedral can peer through that window and see the illuminated statue of the Virgin glowing inside. The Lady of the Forsaken - patron saint of Valencia and of the whole old Kingdom of Valencia - is never entirely hidden from her people. Valencians call her, with deep affection, 'la Geperudeta,' the little hunchback, for the gentle forward stoop of her image. It is the nickname you give not to a distant deity but to someone you love.

## The Window That Never Closes

That barred window captures something essential about this place: the relationship between a city and its Virgin is intimate, constant, and physical. The image kept here is the one Valencians turn to in grief and celebration alike, the figure they have carried in their hearts for centuries. She is 'Our Lady of the Forsaken' because her devotion began as care for the abandoned - the destitute, the orphaned, the condemned. Pope Pius XII granted the sanctuary the formal dignity of a basilica in 1948, but the title only ratified what the people of Valencia had always felt: that this was the spiritual heart of their city.

## Built on the Roman Forum

The basilica stands at the highest point of old Valencia, on the Plaza de la Virgen, facing a fountain that represents the Turia River and the irrigation channels that made this a fertile land. Together with the neighbouring cathedral - to which it is joined by an upper arch across the lane between them - it forms one of the most significant corners of the historic centre. Dig here and you strike Rome. Archaeological work has shown that this square sits atop the Roman forum of ancient Valentia, and the connection is literally built into the walls: several of the ashlars on the basilica's main façade are Roman tombstones and inscriptions, reused stones carrying words cut two thousand years ago.

## A Baroque Jewel

The basilica went up between 1652 and 1667, designed by Diego Martínez Ponce de Urrana, and it holds a special distinction: it is the only church in old Valencia raised entirely new from its foundations rather than built over an earlier parish or convent. Its architects reached for the grandest models. The spatial plan echoes the 'double cube' of Bramante's San Pietro in Montorio and of St Peter's in Rome, while the dome invites comparison with the ancient Treasury of Atreus and with Brunelleschi's dome in Florence. Inside, the ceiling blazes with the frescoes of Antonio Palomino, whose swirling Baroque vision crowns the space above the faithful.

## A Mantle of Flowers

Once a year the Virgin steps outside, in a sense. During the Fallas festival each March, Valencia holds the Ofrenda de Flores, the flower offering, and it is one of the most moving spectacles in Spain. More than a hundred thousand Valencians in traditional dress parade through the streets, each carrying a bouquet, and the flowers are worked into an enormous wooden framework shaped like the Virgin and Child that rises in the square. Over two days the bare frame is clothed, petal by petal, in a fresh mantle of flowers - a different design every year - until the patron of the city stands robed in colour before her own basilica. It is devotion made visible on a civic scale.

## The Heart of Valencia

Strip away the architecture and the art, and what remains is a bond. For Valencians the Basilica of Our Lady of the Forsaken is not primarily a monument to admire but a place to return to - the fixed point their city has revolved around through plague, war, celebration and grief. The little hunchbacked Virgin behind the barred window has watched over the Plaza de la Virgen while empires rose and fell around her, while Roman stones were buried and rediscovered beneath her feet. To stand in that square, cathedral on one side and basilica on the other, is to stand at the very centre of what Valencia understands itself to be.

## Flight Context

The basilica sits at 39.476°N, 0.375°W, in the heart of Valencia's historic centre on the Plaza de la Virgen, immediately beside Valencia Cathedral and its landmark octagonal bell tower, the Miguelete. From the air, look for the tight medieval street grid of the old town near the curving green park of the former Turia riverbed; the basilica's dome and the cathedral tower mark the spot. Valencia's airport (LEVC) at Manises lies just west of the city; Alicante–Elche (LEAL) and Murcia (LEMI) are well to the south. This is dense inner-city Valencia near the Mediterranean coast, best appreciated at low altitude, where the cathedral-and-basilica ensemble stands out among the packed rooftops of the old quarter.
