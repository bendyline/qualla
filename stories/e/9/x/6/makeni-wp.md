# Makeni

The Temne capital of Sierra Leone's north, a bauxite-and-bombali heartland that endured rebel occupation and rebuilt itself into a city of universities and hospitals.

**Location:** 8.8817, -12.0442

**Categories:** Makeni, Populated places in Sierra Leone, Bombali District, Northern Province, Sierra Leone

## Introduction

During Sierra Leone's civil war, Makeni was not a battlefield the country could afford to ignore. The Revolutionary United Front held it as a stronghold, and for a time this northern city was the beating heart of the rebellion rather than the state. Two decades later, Makeni is the fifth-largest city in Sierra Leone, the economic engine of the Northern Province, and the proud home of the country's largest private university. The distance between those two Makenis is the real story.

## Capital of the North

Makeni is the largest city in the Northern Province and the capital of Bombali District, sitting roughly 110 miles east of Freetown across good roads that climb inland toward the interior. The 2021 census counted 85,116 residents, making it the fifth-largest city in the country. This is Temne country, the heartland of one of Sierra Leone's two largest ethnic groups, and Makeni has long served as the place where the north does its business, trades its goods, and sends its children to school. The surrounding region holds some of the nation's mineral wealth, and Makeni's recent growth has tracked the fortunes of nearby mining and biofuel ventures. It is a city built on the land around it, and on the people who have always made that land yield.

## The Years of the War

The civil war that tore through Sierra Leone from 1991 to 2002 reached deep into Makeni. The Revolutionary United Front occupied the city and made it one of their principal bases, and the people who lived here endured the occupation rather than choosing it. These were ordinary residents, families and traders and farmers, caught inside a conflict notorious for its brutality toward civilians. When the fighting finally ended in 2002, the country faced the enormous task of rebuilding not just roads and roofs but institutions and trust. Makeni's recovery is woven into that national effort, and the scale of what was lost is the measure of how far the city has since come.

## Rebuilding, Brick and Body

After the war, Sierra Leone's Ministry of Health and Sanitation shifted toward a decentralized system meant to widen care, and Makeni became a center for it. The Holy Spirit Hospital, the largest private hospital in the Northern Province, works alongside a government regional hospital, private clinics, and traditional healers who remain part of everyday medicine. In 2019, five organizations founded the First Responder Coalition of Sierra Leone here. They trained 1,000 residents as first responders, equipped each with a first aid kit, and by year's end had trained 4,529 lay responders in total, who went on to treat 1,850 patients in the following six months. In a country where a road accident can be a death sentence, these were neighbors taught to save neighbors.

## A City That Learns

Makeni is home to the University of Makeni, the largest private university in Sierra Leone, a remarkable thing for a city that not long ago was a rebel headquarters. It is also the hometown of Ernest Bai Koroma, born and raised here before he became the country's president. Sierra Leone's most-told story of survival from the war years may belong to Michaela DePrince, the ballerina who grew up an orphan in the civil war and rose to dance on the international stage — though she was born in Kenema, in the east, not Makeni. The city's politics run hot, too; in the June 2023 mayoral election, Abubakarr Kamara, nicknamed Lamtales, of the All People's Congress won 93.38 percent of the vote. Makeni today is loud, growing, and stubbornly forward-facing, a place that knows exactly what it endured and refuses to be defined by it.

## Flight Context

Makeni sits at 8.88°N, 12.04°W, in the interior of northern Sierra Leone about 110 miles east of the coast. The city's clock-tower center and the spread of newer development around the mining and university districts make it the dominant settlement in an otherwise green, low-relief landscape. The nearest major airport is Freetown-Lungi International (GFLL), roughly 175 km west by road. Best viewed from medium altitude in the dry season (December to April), when haze is minimal; the rainy season brings dense afternoon buildups over the inland hills.
