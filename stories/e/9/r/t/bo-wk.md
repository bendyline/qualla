# Bo

A traveler's waypoint in southern Sierra Leone - more crossroads than destination, where Star beer is cold and the heat is relentless.

**Location:** 7.9564, -11.7400

**Categories:** Travel, Sierra Leone, Cities, West Africa

## Introduction

Most people who pass through Bo are on their way somewhere else. Sierra Leone's second city is, by the honest reckoning of those who know it, less a destination than a hinge - a place to spend a night, find a meal, and push on toward the coast or the diamond country. But it is an easy hinge to reach. The paved road from Freetown is genuinely excellent, and the four-hour drive south is one of the smoothest journeys in the country. From Kenema to the east, it is barely an hour. Bo is where the south's roads meet, and travelers meet there too.

## Getting There and Around

The good news for any traveler is the road. The route from Freetown is paved and well maintained, and the four-hour drive is comfortable by regional standards. From Kenema, the run takes about an hour over solid road. Within Bo, the town is compact enough to navigate on foot or by short hops, and it functions best as a base for day trips into the surrounding Southern Province. Small-scale diamond mining operates in the area - a glimpse of the trade that has shaped this corner of Sierra Leone - and can be worth seeking out with a local guide who knows where it is welcome to look.

## Eating and Drinking

Bo sits a long way from the sea, and the menu reflects it. Seafood is scarce; what you will find instead is beef, chicken, and river fish, prepared simply and served in modest places around town. The drinking is more dependable than the dining. Bars are plentiful, and most of them pour Star beer at reasonable prices - the local lager that lubricates evenings across Sierra Leone. Pull up a plastic chair, order a cold one if the generator is cooperating, and you have found the heart of a Bo night out. Expect conviviality rather than refinement, and you will not be disappointed.

## Where to Sleep

Set your expectations honestly: hotels in Bo are basic compared to Freetown, and electricity is often available only at night, if at all. The Countryside Hotel, on the Bo-Kenema road south of town, is the upper end of the local range, with night-time electricity, air-conditioned rooms, a swimming pool, and a tennis court - though it has worn with the years, and music can run loud into the small hours. In the center, Madame Woki Hotel offers very basic rooms, some with simple air conditioners that work when the power does. The Imperial Hotel also sits on the Bo-Kenema road. Town-center options are convenient but noisy; the road-edge hotels trade walkability for a quieter night.

## Coping with the Climate

The thing no guidebook should soften is the heat. Bo's combination of high temperature and heavy humidity can be genuinely punishing for anyone not acclimatized, and the difference between a tolerable visit and a miserable one often comes down to a single amenity: an air-conditioned room to sleep in. Treat it as essential rather than a luxury, and plan your days around the cooler hours. When you want a tether to the wider world, the BBC World Service comes through on 94.5 MHz FM, and the Western-style Capital Radio broadcasts on 102.3 MHz - a familiar voice in the heat, while the fan or the generator hums.

## Flight Context

Bo lies inland in southern Sierra Leone at 7.96°N, 11.74°W, about 230 km southeast of Freetown by the well-paved highway. The local Bo Airport is no longer in regular service; arrivals to the country route through Freetown International Airport (Lungi), ICAO GFLL. For overflight, recommended viewing altitude 6,000-9,000 ft reveals the town set in rolling green country threaded by red-earth roads. Visibility drops sharply in the dusty Harmattan months (December-April).
