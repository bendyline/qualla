# Tiwai Island

The 'Big Island' of the Moa River: 12 square kilometers of Upper Guinea rainforest that holds eleven primate species, pygmy hippos, and Sierra Leone's first World Heritage status.

**Location:** 7.5425, -11.3458

**Categories:** Islands of Sierra Leone, Protected areas of Sierra Leone, River islands of Africa, Pujehun District, World Heritage Sites

## Introduction

Its name in Mende means simply "Big Island," and on the Moa River that is exactly what it is: at roughly 12 square kilometers, Tiwai is one of the largest inland islands in Sierra Leone. But size is the least remarkable thing about it. This single river island, rising barely 80 to 100 meters above sea level, packs in one of the highest concentrations of primates found anywhere on the planet. In 2025 it earned international recognition to match its biological wealth, becoming part of the Gola-Tiwai complex, the country's first UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## Eleven Kinds of Primate

Tiwai's biological headline is its primates. The island supports what researchers describe as one of the highest concentrations and greatest diversity of primates in the world, with eleven distinct species sharing its forest. Alongside them lives a population of pygmy hippopotamus, the small, secretive cousin of the common hippo and a species of real conservation concern across West Africa. The bird life is equally rich, with more than 135 species recorded. This density of life is no accident: Tiwai is a surviving fragment of the Upper Guinea Rainforest, an ecosystem that once stretched across West Africa and now persists only in scattered remnants like this one.

## A Gift Between Two Chiefs

The island's modern history begins with an act of generosity. Tiwai belonged to the Barri people until the late 19th century, when the Barri chief, Queen Nyarroh, gave half of it to the Koya chief whose lands lay across the Moa River. From that moment the two peoples shared ownership of the island, a joint stewardship that still underpins its protection. In the late 1970s scientists recognized Tiwai as a special biosphere, and through the 1970s and 1980s a steady stream of researchers came to study its flora and fauna. Their work, supported by the Barri and Koya communities, led to the island's designation as a game reserve in 1987.

## Interrupted by War

For a few years Tiwai flourished as a model of community-based conservation, hosting ecological research, wildlife management, tourism, and forestry training. Then the Sierra Leone Civil War erupted in 1991. Funding dried up, and researchers and visitors could no longer reach the island; for years its programs stood frozen. Recovery came through the Environmental Foundation for Africa, a Sierra Leonean NGO that rebuilt both the tourism and research facilities once peace returned. That restoration set the stage for the island's eventual global recognition, proof that a conservation project can survive even a country's collapse and emerge stronger.

## A Tropical Climate on a Tropical Island

Tiwai's climate is classic tropical rainforest. The year splits into a rainy season from May to October and a dry season from December to March, with an average temperature around 27 degrees Celsius and a remarkable 3,000 millimeters of rainfall annually. That abundant water, fed continuously by the surrounding Moa River, is the engine behind the island's astonishing fertility. Sitting 60 kilometers from the Atlantic and 15 kilometers from the town of Potoru in the Southern Province, Tiwai occupies a sweet spot of warmth, moisture, and isolation, the exact conditions a rainforest needs to support this much life on so small a stage.

## Flight Context

Tiwai Island lies at 7.54°N, 11.35°W on the Moa River in Sierra Leone's Southern Province, 60 km from the Atlantic coast and 15 km from Potoru. Recommended viewing altitude 3,000 to 6,000 feet to resolve the forested island, 80 to 100 meters above sea level, set within the river's slow bends. It appears as a distinct dark-green landmass surrounded by water, a reliable waypoint for inland navigation. Nearest international airport is Freetown–Lungi (GFLL), roughly 220 km to the northwest. Visibility is best before the harmattan haze of late December; the May to October rainy season brings dense cloud.
