# Tashirojima

Japan's famous 'Cat Island' where a 1602 decree freed all domestic cats to protect silkworms, creating a feral colony that now vastly outnumbers the island's dwindling human population.

**Location:** 38.3000, 141.4167

**Categories:** Islands of Miyagi Prefecture, Ishinomaki, Cat islands in Japan, wildlife, cultural-curiosity

## Introduction

In 1602, the Tokugawa shogunate issued a decree: free every pet cat in Japan. The silkworm industry was under siege from rodents, and cats were the answer. On most of the archipelago, the freed cats eventually blended back into domestic life. On Tashirojima, a small island off the Oshika Peninsula in Miyagi Prefecture, they never did. Four centuries later, the descendants of those liberated cats rule an island whose human population has dwindled from 1,000 in the 1950s to roughly 80 people, most of them elderly. Dogs are not allowed. The cats do not appear to have voted on this policy, but they would approve.

## The Decree That Made an Island

Tashirojima's transformation from fishing village to feline sanctuary traces back to the economics of silk. During the late Edo period, the island's residents raised silkworms, and cats were the essential pest control keeping mice away from the cocoons. The 1602 decree freeing all domestic cats accelerated what was already a working relationship. After the silk trade waned, fixed-net fishing drew mainland fishermen to the island, and the cats adapted again, haunting the inns where crews stayed overnight and begging scraps. The fishermen began interpreting feline behavior as weather forecasts and fishing omens -- a cat washing its face meant rain, certain postures predicted good catches. When a falling rock accidentally killed one of the cats, the fishermen built a small shrine on the spot, elevating the island's cats from useful animals to spiritual beings.

## A Shrine Between Two Villages

The Neko Jinja -- cat shrine -- sits in the forest between Tashirojima's two settlements, Oodomari and Nitoda. It is modest in scale, as cat shrines tend to be, but serious in intent. In Japanese culture, cats are considered bearers of good fortune, attracting wealth and luck to those who treat them well. The shrine is draped with small offerings and surrounded by cat figurines left by visitors. The path between the villages takes about two hours on foot, winding through forest and past abandoned buildings that speak to the island's demographic collapse. Some islanders even credit the cats with protecting Tashirojima during the devastating 2011 Tohoku earthquake and tsunami, which destroyed the port at Oodomari and caused catastrophic damage across the Sanriku coast.

## Famous Cats and Their Audiences

Media discovery turned Tashirojima from a curiosity into a destination. In 2006, a Terebi Asahi television crew filmed on the island, introducing Japan's mainland audience to the cat colony. Fuji Television followed with a program featuring a cat named Jack, which spawned the Nyanko film series -- four installments, each including an update on Jack's island life. Package tours advertising the chance to "look for Jack" became a niche travel product. In 2012, the BBC featured Tashirojima in Pets: Wild at Heart. In 2015, filmmaker Landon Donoho crowd-funded Cat Heaven Island, a documentary following both human and feline residents. Cat photo contests and exhibitions are held regularly. The island also produces oysters and abalone, but nobody visits for the shellfish.

## Extinction and Endurance

Tashirojima carries an official designation that translates to something like "endangered village" -- more than half the population is over 65, and the mathematics of attrition are unforgiving. The island merged administratively with the city of Ishinomaki in 2005, but governance from the mainland cannot reverse the demographic tide. The manga resort, open from April through October, rents cat-shaped cottages and campsites to visitors, and a small cafe near the old school sells food and souvenirs. Three daily ferries make the 40-minute crossing from Ishinomaki. It is a thin economic thread for a community that once supported a thousand souls. Yet the cats are thriving, indifferent to the census data, and each arriving ferry brings new humans bearing the one thing that keeps an island alive: attention.

## Flight Context

Located at 38.30N, 141.42E in the Pacific Ocean off the Oshika Peninsula, Miyagi Prefecture, Japan. Tashirojima is a small forested island visible from altitude, lying north of neighboring Aji Island. The two port areas of Oodomari and Nitoda are on opposite coasts. Nearest airports: Sendai Airport (RJSS) approximately 45nm southwest, JASDF Matsushima Air Base (RJST) approximately 20nm west-northwest. The island is approximately 15km from downtown Ishinomaki. The Sanriku coastline's dramatic rias bays provide visual navigation landmarks. Expect maritime weather with potential for fog and low visibility, especially in spring and early summer.
