# Donner Pass

A mountain notch in the northern Sierra Nevada became California's gateway, witness to pioneer tragedy, railroad triumph, and some of the heaviest snowfall in the contiguous United States.

**Location:** 39.3144, -120.3269

**Categories:** Donner Party, Mountain passes, Railroad history, Lincoln Highway, Sierra Nevada

## Introduction

Of the 81 settlers who made camp near this Sierra Nevada notch in November 1846, only 45 would live to see California. The Donner Party's desperate winter gave the pass its name and its legend, but the story of Donner Pass is far larger than a single tragedy. This gap in the mountains became California's front door, channeling wagon trains, the first transcontinental railroad, the Lincoln Highway, and Interstate 80. Each generation found a way through, leaving behind tunnels bored through granite, snowsheds stretching for miles, and roads carved into cliffs.

## The Winter of 1846

The Stephens-Townsend-Murphy Party found the pass first, in 1844, following the Truckee River to a low notch in the mountains. Two years later, the Donner Party arrived too late in the season. Early November snow blocked the route, and the emigrants were forced to make camp on the eastern side of the pass. What followed became one of the most infamous episodes in American frontier history. Trapped until spring rescue parties could reach them, some survivors resorted to cannibalism to stay alive. The pass, previously unnamed, would forever carry the memory of that desperate winter. Donner Lake below and Donner Memorial State Park preserve the story for those who want to understand what the emigrants endured.

## Blasting Through Granite

The Central Pacific Railroad faced the pass in 1865 and spent nearly three years conquering it. Hundreds of Chinese workers drilled and blasted through solid granite, constructing four tunnels including the 1,659-foot Summit Tunnel. They built miles of wooden snowsheds to protect tracks from avalanches and constructed a hand-crafted stone retaining wall known as the Chinese Wall in recognition of its builders. On June 18, 1868, the first passenger train rolled through the Summit Tunnel, opening commercial transportation across the Sierra for the first time. The original track remained in daily use until 1993, when modern trains began routing through the newer Tunnel 41 beneath Mount Judah.

## Snow Country

Donner Pass receives an average of 411 inches of snow annually, making it one of the snowiest locations in the contiguous United States. Four times since 1880, total snowfall has exceeded 700 inches, topping 800 inches in both 1938 and 1952. Wind gusts exceeding 100 miles per hour tear through during winter storms. The winter of 1846-47 that trapped the Donner Party was especially severe, but the pass has continued collecting tragedy. In February 1936, a blizzard trapped more than 750 motorists, killing seven. In January 1952, the passenger train City of San Francisco became stranded west of the pass for three days when snow buried the tracks. California's all-time record low temperature was recorded at Boca, east of Truckee, in January 1937.

## Gateway to California

The Lincoln Highway, America's first transcontinental road, crossed Donner Pass in the early twentieth century. The Victory Highway followed, and both eventually became U.S. Route 40. Interstate 80, built in the early 1960s, crosses the Sierra at nearby Euer Saddle, slightly higher but with gentler grades suitable for modern highway standards. The 1920s highway has been preserved as scenic Donner Pass Road, offering travelers a taste of what earlier generations experienced. Today, ski resorts including Donner Ski Ranch, Boreal, and Sugar Bowl take advantage of the heavy snowfall that once threatened pioneers. The pass that claimed so many lives now draws thousands seeking powder and alpine adventure.

## Flight Context

Located at 39.314N, 120.327W in the northern Sierra Nevada, approximately 10 miles west of Truckee, California. The pass sits at approximately 7,056 feet elevation with the surrounding peaks rising higher. Interstate 80 crosses the nearby Euer Saddle at 7,239 feet. Donner Lake is visible below the pass to the east. Nearest airports: Truckee Tahoe Airport (KTRK) approximately 8nm east. Exercise extreme caution: winter weather can be severe with heavy snow, reduced visibility, and high winds. Mountain wave turbulence common. Check weather carefully before crossing the Sierra.
