# The Cabazon Dinosaurs

The two giant dinosaur statues in the California desert built as a roadside attraction, immortalized in Pee-wee's Big Adventure, then purchased by creationists who filled them with exhibits arguing that humans and dinosaurs lived together.

**Location:** 33.9297, -116.7720

**Categories:** california, roadside-attraction, dinosaurs, kitsch, desert

## Introduction

Driving Interstate 10 through the California desert west of Palm Springs, you suddenly see them: a 150-foot-long Apatosaurus and a 65-foot-tall Tyrannosaurus rex, looming beside the highway like refugees from a fever dream. Claude Bell, a retired Knott's Berry Farm sculptor, built them starting in 1964 as a roadside attraction for his truck stop. He spent 11 years on Dinny the Apatosaurus; Mr. Rex took another decade. They became iconic - featured in films including 'Pee-wee's Big Adventure.' Bell completed both dinosaurs by 1986 and died in 1988. Then creationists bought the property. Today, visitors can enter the dinosaurs, shop for souvenirs, and view exhibits explaining that evolution is a lie and humans walked with dinosaurs 6,000 years ago. The roadside kitsch acquired a theological mission.

## The Builder

Claude Bell was a sculptor and portrait artist who spent 24 years designing characters and attractions for Knott's Berry Farm, the Southern California theme park. In retirement, he bought land beside Interstate 10 and opened a restaurant called the Wheel Inn. Then he started building dinosaurs. Working with steel, concrete, and mesh, Bell spent over a decade on Dinny, the Apatosaurus, which he completed in 1975. Inside Dinny's belly, he built a gift shop. He then started Mr. Rex, the T. rex, completing it in 1986. Bell died in 1988 at age 91; his family couldn't sustain the business.

## The Fame

The Cabazon Dinosaurs became an icon of California kitsch. Millions of travelers spotted them from the freeway; many stopped for photos. They appeared in films and TV shows - most famously in 'Pee-wee's Big Adventure' (1985), where Pee-wee Herman hides in Dinny's mouth while fleeing a biker gang. The image of Pee-wee dancing on the dinosaur's teeth became a pop culture moment. Travel guides listed them as essential Americana. Artists photographed them against desert sunsets. Bell's vision had succeeded beyond what he probably imagined.

## The Purchase

After Bell's death, his family struggled to maintain the property. In 2005, a group with creationist beliefs purchased the dinosaurs. The new owners transformed the attraction into a venue for their religious viewpoint: the Cabazon Dinosaurs became part of a larger 'Creation and Earth History Museum.' Inside the dinosaurs and in adjacent buildings, exhibits argue that the Earth is 6,000 years old, that dinosaurs and humans coexisted, and that evolution is scientifically false. The gift shop now sells creationist literature alongside rubber dinosaurs. Bell's secular roadside attraction became an instrument of religious education.

## The Irony

The transformation is rich with irony. Bell built his dinosaurs as entertainment, monuments to prehistoric creatures separated from humans by 65 million years. The new owners use them to argue the opposite - that the very concept of 'millions of years' is a deception. Visitors expecting kitschy Americana find theological arguments. Some are confused; some are offended; some are delighted. The dinosaurs themselves remain silent on the matter. They continue to loom over the desert, drawing travelers off the freeway, their meaning entirely dependent on who owns them and what story they choose to tell.

## Visiting the Cabazon Dinosaurs

The Cabazon Dinosaurs are located at 50770 Seminole Drive in Cabazon, California, just off Interstate 10 at the Main Street/Cabazon exit. Visitors can photograph the exterior freely. Entering the dinosaurs and viewing the creationist exhibits requires paid admission. The gift shop is inside Dinny. Be aware of the religious content if visiting with children expecting a secular dinosaur experience. The Wheel Inn restaurant Bell built is now closed. Palm Springs is 20 miles east; Los Angeles is 90 miles west. The nearby Morongo Casino is visible from the dinosaurs. Ontario International Airport is 60 miles west; Palm Springs International is 25 miles east.

## Flight Context

Located at 33.93°N, 116.77°W in the San Gorgonio Pass between the San Bernardino and San Jacinto Mountains. From altitude, the two dinosaur sculptures are visible beside Interstate 10 - the Apatosaurus a long gray form, the T. rex standing upright. The Morongo Casino lies nearby. The pass is a wind corridor; windmills cover the ridges. Palm Springs spreads to the east; the pass funnels traffic between the LA basin and the desert. The dinosaurs are surprisingly visible from altitude despite their modest size - they're painted to stand out against the desert landscape.
