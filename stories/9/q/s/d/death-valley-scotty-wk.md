# Scotty's Castle

The con man's castle he didn't build or own - where Death Valley Scotty claimed he had a gold mine and convinced a Chicago millionaire to build a mansion in the desert, then lived there as a beloved fraud for 50 years.

**Location:** 37.0314, -117.3419

**Categories:** california, death-valley, con-man, architecture, desert

## Introduction

Scotty's Castle isn't Scotty's, and it isn't a castle. The Spanish-Mediterranean mansion in Death Valley's Grapevine Canyon was built by Chicago insurance magnate Albert Johnson in the 1920s, but everyone knows it by the name of Walter Scott - 'Death Valley Scotty' - the con man, showman, and professional liar who somehow became Johnson's friend and the estate's most famous resident. Scotty claimed he had a secret gold mine in Death Valley and spent decades convincing investors (including Johnson) to fund his prospecting. There was no mine. Johnson eventually discovered this, but instead of prosecuting Scotty, he became his patron and friend. The two men lived together at the desert mansion for decades, Scotty spinning tales for tourists while Johnson financed the fantasy. It's the strangest friendship in American con-man history, and the castle stands as monument to a lie that became more valuable than truth.

## The Con Man

Walter Scott was a genuine Western character - he'd worked as a trick rider in Buffalo Bill's Wild West show - but his talents lay in talking, not mining. In 1902, he convinced New York investor Julian Gerard that he had found gold in Death Valley. Gerard funded expeditions; Scotty returned with stories but no gold. The scam required constant energy: Scotty staged a famous 'record-breaking' train ride from Los Angeles to Chicago (funded by Gerard) purely for publicity. He feuded publicly with other supposed miners. He created a legend while finding nothing but stories.

## The Millionaire

Albert Johnson was Scotty's perfect mark - and ultimately his salvation. A Chicago life insurance executive, Johnson met Scotty around 1904 and began funding expeditions. Unlike other investors, Johnson kept coming back despite the lack of gold. The desert seemed to help his chronic health problems. Eventually, Johnson stopped caring about gold and started valuing Scotty's company and the desert itself. In the 1920s, Johnson began building a vacation home in Grapevine Canyon. Scotty had no ownership; Johnson paid for everything. But Scotty told everyone it was his castle, built with gold-mine wealth, and Johnson played along.

## The Castle

Johnson spent an estimated $2.5 million (perhaps $50 million today) building his Death Valley mansion. The Spanish Colonial Revival complex includes the main house with its famous clock tower, a guest house, stables, and various outbuildings. An ingenious system brought water from a spring miles away. A pipe organ was installed. The 1929 crash and Johnson's failing health stopped construction before completion. But what was built is spectacular: hand-crafted tiles, wrought iron, carved beams, all in the middle of one of Earth's most hostile environments. The castle became a tourist attraction even before Johnson's death in 1948.

## The Legend

Scotty lived at the castle until his death in 1954, spinning tales for visitors and maintaining the fiction that he owned the place. Johnson, who died in 1948, understood the value of the legend and never corrected it publicly. Mrs. Johnson, who outlived both men, let Scotty stay. The Gospel Foundation inherited the property and opened it to tours. When the Park Service took over, they preserved the Scotty legend as part of the story. Scotty was a con man, but he was a good con man, one who made people happy. His grave is on a hill above the castle, next to his dog.

## Visiting Scotty's Castle

Scotty's Castle is located in the northern part of Death Valley National Park, about 50 miles north of Furnace Creek. A flash flood in 2015 caused major damage, and the castle has been closed for restoration ever since; check current status before visiting. When open, ranger-led tours explore the main house and grounds, emphasizing both Johnson's construction and Scotty's storytelling. The setting is spectacular - a desert canyon with cottonwoods and springs, an oasis in the wasteland. Death Valley's other attractions (Badwater, the dunes, Zabriskie Point) are to the south. Las Vegas (LAS) is 150 miles southeast. Visiting Death Valley requires preparation: bring water, fuel, and sun protection.
