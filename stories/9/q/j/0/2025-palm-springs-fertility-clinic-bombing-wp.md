# 2025 Palm Springs fertility clinic bombing

On May 17, 2025, a car bomb exploded in the parking lot of a Palm Springs fertility clinic — an act of terrorism rooted in antinatalist ideology that the FBI described as one of the largest bombing investigations in Southern California in years.

**Location:** 33.8392, -116.5458

**Categories:** Palm Springs California, Terrorism, 2025 events, Extremism

## Introduction

The explosion happened early on a Saturday morning, May 17, 2025, in the parking lot behind the American Reproductive Centers clinic at 1199 North Indian Canyon Drive in Palm Springs. The clinic, the only full-service fertility facility in the Coachella Valley, was closed for the weekend. No staff were inside. The blast left a large hole in one of the building's walls and damaged the structure. One person died: the man who had brought the bomb. Four others were injured. Within hours, the FBI had declared the explosion an act of terrorism and launched what they would describe as one of the largest bombing investigations in Southern California since a deadly package bombing in Aliso Viejo in 2018.

## The Perpetrator

Guy Edward Bartkus was 25 years old, from Twentynine Palms, and had grown up partly in Waterbury, Connecticut. He had worked as a computer technician and as a monitor for special-needs children on school buses. His father described him as a good-hearted young man and a computer prodigy who had built his own computers from age nine. The FBI described him as holding antinatalist and nihilistic views. Bartkus left behind a video in which he called himself a pro-mortalist — someone who believed that human existence itself was a harm — and a 30-minute audio recording in which he explained his reasoning. 'Basically, it just comes down to I'm angry that I exist,' he said. He died in the explosion he had set. The FBI had not been tracking him before the attack.

## The Co-Conspirator

The investigation quickly identified a second person: Daniel Park, a man from Kent, Washington, who shared Bartkus's ideology and had been in contact with him for months. Park had shipped approximately 270 pounds of ammonium nitrate to Bartkus in multiple purchases between October 2022 and May 2025 — 180 pounds in January, then another 90 pounds days before the bombing. Investigators found an explosive recipe in Park's Kent home that they described as similar to the one used in the 1995 Oklahoma City bombing. Park had fled to Warsaw four days after the bombing, on May 21. He was extradited back to the United States on May 30. On June 24, 2025, Park died at the Metropolitan Detention Center in Los Angeles, having jumped from an upper level of the facility.

## The Ideology Behind the Attack

Antinatalism — the philosophical position that procreation causes harm and that human beings have no right to bring new people into existence without their consent — has a long intellectual history, but the version Bartkus and Park subscribed to had migrated into darker territory, sometimes called efilism or promortalism. Online communities organized around these ideas had been active on platforms including Reddit, which banned one such subreddit in the wake of the bombing. Bartkus's manifesto framed the attack on the fertility clinic as a logical extension of the belief that creating new human life is an act of harm: a clinic that helped people have children was, in his framework, a legitimate target. Brian Levin, founder of the Center for the Study of Hate and Extremism, called Bartkus 'part of a growing group of radicalized lone wolves influenced by dark websites.'

## What Survived

The clinic's essential medical operations — which for a fertility clinic means embryos in storage, ongoing IVF cycles, biological material that represents for many patients years of hope and significant expense — were not disrupted. The bombing occurred outside business hours, as Bartkus apparently intended it to, though the manifesto made clear that ideology rather than minimizing casualties was the primary motivation. The building was damaged; the operations continued. The desert community of Palm Springs, which has absorbed historical weight from different directions over the decades, absorbed this one too.
