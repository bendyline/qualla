# Eisenhower Medical Center

Eisenhower Medical Center in Rancho Mirage grew from a 1966 Bob Hope charity golf tournament into a 437-bed teaching hospital — a place where two American presidents received care, Betty Ford died, and the Coachella Valley's healthcare infrastructure took its defining institutional shape.

**Location:** 33.7633, -116.4060

**Categories:** California hospitals, Rancho Mirage California, Coachella Valley, Bob Hope

## Introduction

The hospital that bears the name of a president began with a comedian at a charity golf tournament. In 1966, Bob Hope was asked to lend his name to a golf event and to serve on the board of the hospital that the tournament's proceeds would help build. He said yes. He and his wife Dolores donated 80 acres of land in Rancho Mirage. They raised private funds. The hospital that opened in 1971 was named for Dwight D. Eisenhower, who had spent considerable time in the Coachella Valley and whose name gave the institution the presidential authority its founders felt appropriate for a major desert medical center. More than half a century later, Eisenhower Health is a 437-bed nonprofit teaching hospital, named one of the top 100 hospitals in the United States in 2024.

## The Golf Tournament That Built a Hospital

The Bob Hope Chrysler Classic, as it became known, was one of the most prominent charity golf tournaments in the country for decades, drawing professional golfers and celebrities to the Coachella Valley every winter. The hospital it helped create was the anchor institution. The original three medical buildings were named for local philanthropists: the Probst family, Peter Kiewit, and Hazel Wright. In 2010, a $212.5 million four-story addition opened — the Walter and Leonore Annenberg Pavilion. Lee Annenberg alone donated over $100 million to the campaign for that expansion, a figure that reflected both the wealth concentrated in the valley's resort communities and the importance of the hospital to those communities. Other campus institutions include the Barbara Sinatra Children's Center and the Dolores Hope Outpatient Care Center, connecting the names of the valley's entertainment community to the institution they helped create.

## Presidents and Their Deaths

Gerald Ford came to the Coachella Valley in his retirement, living in Rancho Mirage in the same resort community where the hospital was situated. In January 2006, he was admitted to Eisenhower Medical Center for sixteen days for treatment of pneumonia. It was not his last visit. When Ford died on December 26, 2006, at the age of 93, his body was taken to Eisenhower Medical Center before the state funeral that followed. Betty Ford, his wife and the founder of the Betty Ford Center addiction treatment program, died at Eisenhower Medical Center in 2011. The hospital had become not just the healthcare institution of the Coachella Valley but the place where the desert community's most prominent residents, including former heads of state, faced their final illnesses.

## The Teaching Hospital

Eisenhower Health's educational mission developed significantly after the hospital's founding. In 2010, the institution established an ACGME-accredited Graduate Medical Education program with residencies in Family Medicine, Internal Medicine, and Emergency Medicine, along with fellowship programs in Sports Medicine, Pulmonary Medicine, Addiction Medicine, Infectious Disease, and Geriatric Medicine. The Coachella Valley's desert location, with its aging resort population and seasonal demographics, created specific healthcare demands that shaped the hospital's specializations. The annual figures — approximately 25,000 inpatient visits, 100,000 emergency department visits, and one million outpatient clinic visits — reflect a regional hospital serving a geographically isolated community where it is the primary point of care.

## Dolores Hope and the Institution

Dolores Hope served as president, chairman of the board, and chairman emeritus of Eisenhower Medical Center from 1968 until her death in 2011. She participated in every major decision about the hospital for more than four decades. Her involvement was not ceremonial — she was, by all accounts, deeply engaged with the institution's development and direction. The hospital she helped shape outlasted both her and her husband Bob, who died in 2003. The Dolores Hope Outpatient Care Center carries her name. The campus she and Bob Hope helped establish on that original 80 acres has grown considerably from what they first donated; the institution it anchors serves a region that has grown with it.

## Flight Context

Located at 33.76°N, 116.41°W, in Rancho Mirage, California, in the heart of the Coachella Valley resort corridor. From the air, the hospital campus is visible among the golf courses and resort development of Rancho Mirage, with the Santa Rosa Mountains to the south and the San Jacinto range to the west. Highway 111 and Bob Hope Drive are visible landmarks. Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) is approximately 7 miles to the west-northwest.
