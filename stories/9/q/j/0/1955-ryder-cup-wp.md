# 1955 Ryder Cup

In November 1955, the Ryder Cup came to the desert for the first time — to Thunderbird Country Club in Rancho Mirage, California — and America's golfers won their seventh consecutive match against the British in the warm autumn sun.

**Location:** 33.7560, -116.4300

**Categories:** Golf history, Ryder Cup, Rancho Mirage California, Palm Springs area sports

## Introduction

By 1955, the Coachella Valley already had the courses, the country clubs, the celebrity members, and the desert light that made the game feel like something other than what it was in the gray Midwestern cities where most Americans encountered it. Thunderbird Country Club in Rancho Mirage was, by any measure, a desirable venue for the 11th Ryder Cup Matches — two days in November when the best American and British golfers played match play across 36-hole rounds on fairways edged by mountain views. The United States won 8 to 4. It was their seventh consecutive victory. The British team went home without the cup, as they had every time since 1933.

## The Format and the Stakes

From 1927 through 1959, the Ryder Cup operated on a fixed format: four foursome matches on the first day, eight singles matches on the second, for a maximum of twelve points. Six points won the cup. All matches were played to a maximum of 36 holes. The format rewarded consistency over brilliance — a player could fall behind early and still recover over 36 holes. It was a format that suited the American team, which in 1955 included Sam Snead, Cary Middlecoff, Doug Ford, Tommy Bolt, and Chick Harbert, among others. These were not unfamiliar names to the British side. They were names that, across seven consecutive Ryder Cups, had proven very difficult to beat.

## Two Days in the Desert

Saturday's foursome matches produced three American wins — Ford and Kroll by 5 and 4, Burke and Bolt by one hole, Snead and Middlecoff by 3 and 2 over Bradshaw and Rees — and one British win, John Fallon and John Jacobs edging Harper and Barber by one hole. After the first day, the Americans led 3 to 1. Sunday's singles confirmed what the foursomes had suggested: Bolt, Harbert, Snead, Burke, and Ford all won their matches. Eric Brown, Arthur Lees, and John Jacobs — who beat Middlecoff and finished the week unbeaten — won for Great Britain, a few bright results in an otherwise frustrating week for the visitors. The final score, 8 to 4, was a comfortable American margin. Ken Bousfield, selected for the British team, did not play in any matches.

## The Venue and the Era

Thunderbird Country Club had opened in 1951 with the Coachella Valley's first 18-hole golf course. Bing Crosby and Bob Hope were among its celebrity regulars — the sort of membership that reflected both the club's location near the celebrity resort culture of Palm Springs and its moment in American social history, when golf was becoming the game of presidents and entertainers as much as old money. Hosting the Ryder Cup in 1955 placed Thunderbird — and by extension the desert resort region around it — on the map of serious golf venues. It was also the first time the Ryder Cup was played in the California desert; nearby Eldorado Country Club in Indian Wells hosted it again in 1959.

## A Long British Drought

For the British team, 1955 was one more year in a drought that had lasted since 1933 — the last time Great Britain had won the Ryder Cup outright. Great Britain broke the streak just two years later, at Lindrick in 1957, but the Americans then dominated for decades. Not until 1985 would Europe, now competing as a unified team rather than Great Britain alone, win the cup again. The 1955 defeat in Rancho Mirage was just another year in a run of losses that, in retrospect, marked the era when American professional golf had no serious international peer. The desert sun, whatever advantage it conferred, was not enough to change that.
