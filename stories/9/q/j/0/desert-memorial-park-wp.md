# Desert Memorial Park

Desert Memorial Park in Cathedral City holds Frank Sinatra, Frederick Loewe, Mac McDonald of McDonald's, and dozens of other figures from American entertainment, business, and public life — plus California's official LGBTQ Veterans Memorial, designated by the state in 2018.

**Location:** 33.8198, -116.4423

**Categories:** Cemeteries, Cathedral City California, Palm Springs area history, LGBTQ history

## Introduction

The cemetery opened in 1956 in what is now Cathedral City (incorporated in 1981), a few miles east of Palm Springs, and received its first interment in 1957. The Coachella Valley's resort culture, which had attracted celebrities, entertainers, and wealthy retirees since at least the 1930s, meant that when those residents died, they were often buried nearby. Desert Memorial Park became the resting place of a remarkable assembly of people whose names are woven into the fabric of 20th-century American culture: Frank Sinatra, Frederick Loewe, William Powell, Betty Hutton, Suzanne Somers, Mac McDonald who with his brother Dick invented the original McDonald's restaurant. It also became the site of California's official memorial to LGBTQ veterans — a distinction that reflects the Coachella Valley's long history as a place where LGBTQ communities found a degree of freedom and acceptance unusual for their time.

## The LGBTQ Veterans Memorial

In 2001, American Veterans Post 66 dedicated a memorial at Desert Memorial Park honoring all LGBTQ veterans — an act that reflected both the organization's advocacy for equal rights and the particular character of Palm Springs and its surrounding communities as a place where LGBTQ residents had long lived openly. The memorial is an obelisk of South Dakotan mahogany granite bearing the logo of American Veterans for Equal Rights. In 2018, the California state legislature passed Assembly Bill 2439 designating this memorial as California's official LGBTQ Veterans Memorial — the first state in the United States to create such a designation. A second plaque was affixed to the monument in recognition. The formal dedication ceremony for the state designation took place on April 27, 2019.

## The Voice in the Ground

Frank Sinatra died on May 14, 1998, and was buried at Desert Memorial Park alongside his mother Dolly Sinatra (1894–1977), his father Anthony Martin Sinatra (1892–1969), and his wife Barbara Sinatra (1926–2017). The Sinatra family presence at Desert Memorial Park is a measure of how thoroughly Frank Sinatra's life was intertwined with the Coachella Valley — he had lived in Rancho Mirage, performed at local venues, been part of the resort community for decades. Frederick Loewe, who with Alan Jay Lerner wrote My Fair Lady and Camelot, is buried here. So is Jimmy Van Heusen, the songwriter who wrote 'High Hopes,' 'Call Me Irresponsible,' and 'Come Fly with Me' — all associated with Sinatra's most popular period.

## A Cross Section of American Life

The burial records at Desert Memorial Park read like an annotated history of mid-20th-century American popular culture. William Powell (1892–1984), the debonair actor of the Thin Man films, spent his final decades in Palm Springs and is buried here. Betty Hutton (1921–2007), the force-of-nature actress and singer who starred in Annie Get Your Gun, is here. Maurice 'Mac' McDonald (1902–1971), who with his brother Dick created the original McDonald's drive-in in San Bernardino, California, is buried here — a connection between the world's most recognizable fast food operation and a desert cemetery in Cathedral City that most people would not anticipate. Suzanne Somers, the actress and health advocate who died in 2023, was buried here as well.

## The Landscape of Endings

Desert Memorial Park is maintained by the Palm Springs Cemetery District, which also operates the older Welwood Murray Cemetery in Palm Springs. The cemetery sits in Cathedral City, across Ramon Road from Forest Lawn Cemetery — a small geography of final resting places in a desert city that has always served as a place where people came to be comfortable in their later years, attracted by the climate, the culture, and the sense that the desert offered a kind of clarity that busier places did not. The Santa Rosa Mountains are visible from the cemetery, as they are from almost everywhere in the valley. The light, in the late afternoon, is what it always was.

## Flight Context

Located at 33.82°N, 116.44°W, in Cathedral City, California, just east of Palm Springs. From the air, the cemetery's green landscape contrasts with the surrounding desert. Cathedral City's urban grid is visible extending east from Palm Springs along Highway 111. Palm Springs International Airport (PSP) is approximately 4 miles to the west.
