# Agua Caliente Cultural Museum

The Agua Caliente Cultural Museum in downtown Palm Springs tells the story of the Cahuilla people who have called the Coachella Valley home for centuries — and as the first Native American institution accepted into the Smithsonian Affiliations Program, it occupies a nationally significant position.

**Location:** 33.8210, -116.5470

**Categories:** Native American history, Palm Springs California, Cahuilla people, California museums

## Introduction

The city of Palm Springs exists on land that the Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla Indians has occupied for more than a millennium. The resort hotels, the golf courses, the mid-century modern houses that draw architecture tourists from around the world — much of it sits on what is, in legal and cultural terms, Cahuilla land. The museum at 140 North Indian Canyon Drive in downtown Palm Springs was established in 1991 to make that history visible and to tell the story of a people whose presence in the Coachella Valley predates the arrival of Spanish missionaries, American settlers, and the entire infrastructure of Southern California resort culture. That it became the first Native American museum accepted into the Smithsonian Affiliations Program is a measure of the seriousness with which it pursues that work.

## Who the Cahuilla Are

The Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla Indians takes its name — agua caliente, warm water — from the hot springs that the Cahuilla people had long used in the area now occupied by downtown Palm Springs. Those springs, sacred and practical at once, gave the band's homeland a distinctive character. The Cahuilla were not nomadic: they have deep roots in specific territories of the Coachella Valley and surrounding mountains, with complex social organization, sophisticated knowledge of desert ecology, and ceremonial traditions that continue today. The checkerboard land ownership pattern that characterizes Palm Springs today — where alternating square-mile sections were granted to the Southern Pacific Railroad or set aside, in the even-numbered sections, for the Agua Caliente Reservation created by executive order in 1876 — is a legacy of how railroad land grants and reservation boundaries were drawn in the 19th century, a pattern that shaped the development of the entire city and remains a defining feature of the urban landscape.

## The Museum and Its Collections

The museum's exhibitions cover Cahuilla culture and history, and have included the Florence Patencio Collection, which documents a significant cultural leader of the Agua Caliente community. Over the years, off-site exhibitions have extended its reach: into the lobby of the former Spa Resort Casino Hotel, where an installation addressed the spiritual significance of the Agua Caliente hot spring; to California State University San Bernardino's Palm Desert campus, which featured Native American athletes; and to Palm Springs City Hall, with milestones in Cahuilla history. A 1,200-volume reference library has supported the museum's research function.

## The Smithsonian Affiliation

The Smithsonian Institution Affiliations Program connects museums and educational organizations across the United States to the resources, expertise, and collections of the Smithsonian. When the Agua Caliente Cultural Museum became the first Native American institution accepted into the program, it signaled both the museum's quality and the Smithsonian's recognition of the importance of Indigenous-led institutions in telling American history. The museum also participates in the American Alliance of Museums' Assessment Program and has received recognition for its publications — the 2009-2010 museum brochure and program announcement cards won a first-place award in the AAM Publications Design Competition.

## Growth and the Future

The museum began in the Village Green Heritage Center on South Palm Canyon Drive, and early expansion plans called for a 100,000 square-foot facility designed by architects Jones and Jones. The museum that finally opened on November 3, 2023, as the heart of the Agua Caliente Cultural Plaza, is a roughly 48,000-square-foot building designed by JCJ Architecture, sharing the downtown site with The Spa at Séc-he and an Oasis Trail — giving the institution room to display more of its collections, expand its educational programs, and become more fully a center for Cahuilla cultural life rather than simply a display space. The long build-out reflects the Agua Caliente Band's investment in ensuring that the story of the Coachella Valley's first people is told on a scale commensurate with that history's depth.
