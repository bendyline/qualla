# Murder of Lana Clarkson

On February 3, 2003, actress Lana Clarkson was shot and killed inside the Pyrenees Castle mansion in Alhambra, California. The man whose home it was — music producer Phil Spector — was convicted of second-degree murder six years later, after one of the longest trials in Los Angeles history.

## Introduction

The mansion was called the Pyrenees Castle. It sat behind gates in Alhambra, its turrets an eccentric monument to one of pop music's most brilliant and troubled minds. In the early hours of February 3, 2003, a limousine driver waited outside. When Phil Spector finally emerged, he told the driver: 'I think I killed somebody.' Inside, Lana Clarkson — actress, club hostess, a woman who had worked hard for decades at the edges of Hollywood — lay dead.

## A Life on the Margins of Fame

Lana Clarkson was 40 years old when she died. She had appeared in Roger Corman's cult film Barbarian Queen and worked steadily in the kind of roles Hollywood offered women who were not quite famous enough to be protected by their prominence. In early 2003, she was working as a VIP hostess at the House of Blues on the Sunset Strip — a job that put her in proximity to celebrities and record industry figures who cycled through the club.

Phil Spector was one of those figures. The producer who had invented the 'Wall of Sound,' who had worked with the Beatles and the Ramones and transformed pop music, came to the club on the night of February 2. He invited Clarkson back to his home. The two had met only that evening.

What happened inside the Pyrenees Castle in the hours before dawn will never be fully known. Spector would later claim that Clarkson had shot herself, that she had kissed the gun. The physical evidence, and eventually two juries, told a different story.

## Years in Court

The first trial of Phil Spector began in 2007. For months, the world heard testimony about blood spatter patterns, gunshot residue, and the behavior of a man whose eccentricities — the wigs, the guns he allegedly brandished at other women over the years, the isolation inside his castle — were laid out in vivid detail. On September 26, 2007, the jury deadlocked: 10 voted for conviction, 2 for acquittal. A mistrial was declared.

The second trial began in 2008. This time, five women testified that Spector had pointed guns at them on previous occasions — a pattern that prosecutors argued made his account of an accidental suicide implausible. On April 13, 2009, after deliberating for 30 hours over eight days, the jury returned a verdict of guilty on second-degree murder. It was more than six years after Clarkson's death.

Spector was sentenced on May 29, 2009, to 19 years to life in state prison. His attorney vowed to appeal. The appeals went nowhere. Spector died on January 16, 2021, in prison, from complications of COVID-19. He had served twelve years.

## What the Case Left Behind

The murder of Lana Clarkson became, in the way that Los Angeles tragedies sometimes do, a referendum on power and fame. Spector's legal team spent enormous resources suggesting that Clarkson had taken her own life — a strategy that required portraying a woman with no history of suicidal behavior as spontaneously choosing death in a stranger's castle.

For Clarkson's family and friends, the years of legal proceedings meant years of watching her death picked apart in service of a defense. Her mother, Donna Clarkson, sat through both trials. The conviction, when it finally came, was described not as closure but as a measure of justice — imperfect, delayed, but real.

The Pyrenees Castle still stands in Alhambra, the turrets visible from the street. It is someone else's property now, the site of a crime that took years to be named as one.
