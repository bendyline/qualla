# Kent Island (California)

A small Marin County island where a 1960s hotel and marina development was defeated by conservationists.

**Location:** 37.9122, -122.6790

**Categories:** Islands of Marin County, California, Environmental conservation

## Introduction

In 1964, developers proposed building a hotel and marina on Kent Island, a small undeveloped island in Bolinas Lagoon in Marin County. The plan would have transformed a quiet tidal island into a commercial resort. Conservationists organized, fought, and in 1967 defeated the proposal -- one of the early victories in the Bay Area's environmental movement.

## An Island at Stake

Kent Island sits in Bolinas Lagoon, surrounded by some of the most ecologically sensitive coastal habitat in Marin County. The lagoon supports eelgrass beds, harbor seals, migratory shorebirds, and the quiet tidal rhythms that make estuarine environments among the most productive ecosystems on earth. The development proposal envisioned a hotel, marina, and associated infrastructure that would have fundamentally altered the island and the surrounding habitat.

## The Fight to Save It

The campaign against the Kent Island development drew together local residents, environmental organizations, and conservation-minded politicians. The opposition argued that the lagoon's ecological value far exceeded whatever economic benefit a hotel might generate, and that once the island was developed, the damage would be irreversible. In 1967, the development proposal was defeated, establishing a precedent for the land-use battles that would define Marin County's future.

## A Precedent Preserved

Kent Island remains undeveloped today. Its story is part of the larger narrative of Marin County's transformation from a county that could have been covered in subdivisions to one of the most protected landscapes in the Bay Area. The defeat of the Kent Island development helped build political momentum that would eventually create the Golden Gate National Recreation Area and protect tens of thousands of acres of coastal Marin from development.
