# Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary

America's most infamous prison sits on an island where no inmate officially escaped, though five men vanished without a trace.

**Location:** 37.8267, -122.4231

**Categories:** Alcatraz Island, 1912 establishments in California, 1934 establishments in California, 1963 disestablishments in California, Defunct prisons in California, Golden Gate National Recreation Area

## Introduction

The Ohlone people called it the island of evil spirits. Later, Spanish sailors named it La Isla de los Alcatraces, the Island of the Gannets, for the pelicans that nested on its rocky shores. By 1934, it had earned a new name: The Rock. Sitting 1.25 miles off the San Francisco coast, surrounded by frigid currents and bone-chilling water, Alcatraz Federal Penitentiary held America's most notorious criminals for 29 years. During that time, the prison claimed no successful escapes. But five men are still listed as missing and presumed drowned, their fates unknown to this day.

## From Fortress to Penitentiary

The island's transformation into America's most secure prison began long before the first federal inmate arrived. Fort Alcatraz was built in the 1850s, and during the Civil War, the citadel held Confederate sympathizers, including C. L. Weller, brother of California's governor. The main cellhouse, constructed between 1910 and 1912 for $250,000, was reportedly the longest concrete building in the world at completion. When the Federal Bureau of Prisons took over in 1934, they invested $260,000 to modernize the facility. Workers cut holes in concrete, installed 269 tool-proof steel cell fronts using four carloads of steel from Stewart Iron Works, and added electromagnetic metal detectors and remote-controlled tear gas canisters in the dining hall ceiling.

## Life on the Rock

The three-story cellhouse contained four main cell blocks, A through D. Cells measured just 5 by 9 feet with 7-foot ceilings, furnished with a bed, desk, washbasin, and toilet. Privacy did not exist. D-Block housed the worst inmates, with six cells at its end designated as The Hole for brutal punishment. Prison corridors carried names like Broadway and Michigan Avenue, echoes of the cities the inmates had terrorized. The library held 10,000 to 15,000 books, but all crime-related content was torn from magazines, and newspapers were forbidden entirely. New inmates faced 90 days of quarantine before they could receive visitors. Working in the Model Industries Building or New Industries Building was considered a privilege, earned through good behavior.

## The Rogues' Gallery

Alcatraz held 1,576 prisoners during its federal penitentiary years. Alvin Creepy Karpis, nicknamed for his sinister smile, served the longest sentence at 26 years, having been personally captured by J. Edgar Hoover. Machine Gun Kelly arrived on September 4, 1934, and spent his time boasting about crimes he never committed, though Warden Johnston considered him a model inmate. Robert Stroud, the self-taught ornithologist known as the Birdman of Alcatraz, wrote his classic Digest on the Diseases of Birds while confined mostly to solitary in D-Block. Bumpy Johnson, the Godfather of Harlem, reportedly played chess in the recreation yard with fellow Puerto Rican prisoner Rafael Cancel Miranda, who served as an altar boy at Catholic services.

## Vanished into the Bay

During 29 years of operation, 36 prisoners made 14 escape attempts. Twenty-three were caught. Six were shot and killed. Two drowned. Five disappeared. The most famous attempt came on June 11, 1962, when Frank Morris and brothers John and Clarence Anglin chiseled through salt-damaged concrete using tools including a metal spoon soldered with silver from a dime and a drill made from a stolen vacuum cleaner motor. They concealed their progress behind false walls and masked the noise with accordions during music hour. The official FBI report concluded they drowned in the cold currents. But evidence uncovered in the 2010s suggested otherwise: a raft found on nearby Angel Island with footprints leading away, a 1955 blue Chevrolet stolen that night by three men, and a 1975 photograph showing two men who resembled the Anglin brothers in Brazil.

## The Rock Today

The prison closed in 1963. From 1969 to 1971, Native American activists occupied the island in protest. Today, Alcatraz operates as part of the Golden Gate National Recreation Area, drawing 1.5 million visitors annually who arrive by boat to tour the decaying cellhouse. The atmosphere remains what visitors often describe as eerie, ghostly, and chilling. The salt-damaged buildings are slowly being restored by the National Park Service. In May 2025, President Trump proposed reopening Alcatraz as a functioning prison, though experts noted the renovation costs would be enormous. For now, The Rock remains a monument to an era when America believed geography alone could contain its most dangerous criminals.
