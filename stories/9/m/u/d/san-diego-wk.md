# San Diego: America's Finest City

The Southern California city with near-perfect weather, where the Navy dominates the harbor, where the Zoo became world-famous, and where California began when Junipero Serra founded the first mission in 1769.

**Location:** 32.7157, -117.1611

**Categories:** california, san-diego, city, navy, zoo

## Introduction

San Diego calls itself 'America's Finest City' - a boast justified by weather that averages 70°F and 266 sunny days per year. But perfection can breed blandness, and San Diego works to be more than a pleasant backdrop. This is where California began: Father Junipero Serra established the first of California's 21 missions here in 1769, the same year the Spanish built the presidio overlooking the bay. The Navy arrived during World War I and never left; aircraft carriers still dominate the harbor. The Zoo, Balboa Park, the Gaslamp Quarter, the beaches from La Jolla to Coronado - San Diego assembled the components of California dreaming before the phrase existed. The result is a city that can feel too comfortable, too content, until you remember that contentment is underrated.

## Where California Began

On July 16, 1769, Father Junipero Serra founded Mission San Diego de Alcala, the first of the 21 Spanish missions that would stretch to Sonoma. The presidio (military fort) was established the same day on the hill now called Presidio Park. From this starting point, Spanish colonization extended north along El Camino Real, the Royal Road that connected the missions. The original mission was relocated six miles inland in 1774; the whitewashed church that stands there today is a 1931 reconstruction. Old Town San Diego preserves the Mexican-era settlement that grew around the presidio - adobe buildings, period furnishings, a reminder that this was Mexican territory until 1848. California's story began here, 250 years ago, on a hill overlooking a perfect natural harbor.

## Navy Town

San Diego is the largest naval base on the West Coast - home to aircraft carriers, submarines, destroyers, and over 50 ships. Naval Base San Diego and Naval Air Station North Island dominate the bay. The USS Midway, a decommissioned aircraft carrier, is now a museum ship at the Embarcadero, where visitors can walk the flight deck and explore the ship that served from World War II through the Gulf War. The Navy's presence shapes the city: 34,000 active-duty sailors, thousands more civilian employees, an economy dependent on defense spending. Top Gun was filmed here; the Miramar air show draws hundreds of thousands annually. San Diego's relationship with the military is not complicated - the Navy is family.

## The World-Famous Zoo

The San Diego Zoo opened in 1916 with animals left over from the Panama-California Exposition, eventually growing into one of the largest and most famous zoos in the world. The 100-acre park in Balboa Park houses over 12,000 animals from more than 650 species, including giant pandas (on loan from China until 2019), koalas, and African elephants. The Safari Park, a 1,800-acre preserve 30 miles north, offers open-range animal encounters. The zoo pioneered naturalistic habitats over cages, conservation breeding programs, and veterinary care standards. It's been called the best zoo in the world - a claim that's difficult to dispute. What began as leftover exposition animals became a global leader in wildlife conservation.

## Balboa Park

Balboa Park contains 1,200 acres of gardens, walking trails, museums, theaters, and the famous Zoo. Most of the park's Spanish Colonial Revival buildings were constructed for the Panama-California Exposition of 1915-1916, temporary structures that proved too beautiful to demolish. The California Building's tower has become San Diego's most recognizable landmark; El Prado, the central promenade, connects museums including the San Diego Museum of Art, the Air & Space Museum, and the Museum of Man. The Japanese Friendship Garden and Spanish Village Art Center add variety. Urban parks don't come more impressive - Balboa Park rivals New York's Central Park in ambition and exceeds it in architectural interest. It's San Diego's cultural heart.

## Beaches and Border

San Diego International Airport (SAN) lies remarkably close to downtown - the approach over Balboa Park and landing beside the harbor are memorable. The beaches extend from La Jolla (upscale cliffs and coves) through Pacific Beach (college town energy) to Ocean Beach (laid-back, sometimes scruffy). Coronado, across the bay, offers the Victorian grandeur of the Hotel del Coronado and wide sandy beaches. The border with Mexico is 20 miles south; Tijuana's urban sprawl visible from nearby hilltops. From altitude, San Diego appears as coastal development between desert mountains and Pacific Ocean, the harbor crowded with Navy vessels, the distinctive shape of Point Loma reaching toward the open sea - America's Finest City, where California began and the weather never disappoints.

## Flight Context

Located at 32.72°N, 117.16°W along San Diego Bay on the Southern California coast, 20 miles north of the Mexican border. SAN airport sits right on the bayfront. Coronado's distinctive shape curves into the harbor. What appears from the air as a Southern California coastal city with exceptional harbor is America's Finest City - where Serra founded California, where the Navy made its Pacific home, and where the weather does its best to prove the nickname right.
