# Montgomery Memorial

A 93-foot stainless steel wing panel from a World War II bomber stands upright on Otay Mesa as a monument to John J. Montgomery, who flew experimental gliders from these hills in the 1880s — decades before Kitty Hawk.

**Location:** 32.5786, -117.0690

**Categories:** History of San Diego, Landmarks in San Diego, California Historical Landmarks, Aviation history of the United States

## Introduction

The monument visible for miles across Otay Mesa is not a conventional sculpture. It is a wing — specifically, a stainless steel static test wing panel from the Consolidated B-32 Dominator, a World War II bomber, mounted upright 93 feet in the air. Gleaming in the Southern California sun, a piece of a real airplane makes a fitting emblem for a memorial celebrating flight. The man it honors flew far more modest craft on these same hills in the 1880s, long before the Wright brothers made their first flight.

## The Glider Pioneer of Otay Mesa

John J. Montgomery experimented with glider aircraft on the hills of Otay Mesa, San Diego, in the 1880s — a period when controlled flight was an aspiration rather than an achieved technology. Montgomery's experiments were among the earliest systematic investigations of wing design and aerodynamic control in North America. He built and flew gliders, studied their behavior, modified their design based on observation, and recorded his findings.

The extent to which Montgomery's work influenced subsequent aviation developments has been debated by historians. What is established is that he was conducting methodical flight experiments on these hills years before the Wright brothers' 1903 flight at Kitty Hawk, and that his investigations of wing design contributed to the body of knowledge from which powered aviation eventually developed. The Otay Mesa hills where he flew are the landscape that the Montgomery Memorial was placed to honor.

## Building the Memorial

The San Diego Junior Chamber of Commerce took up the cause of a memorial to Montgomery's pioneering work. In 1945, San Diego County purchased 7 acres of land for the monument site, and by March 1946 the acquisition had been expanded to 18 adjacent acres for Montgomery Park. The land acquisition was led by Paul Mannen and Gordon Wiggins, with Bob Wilson — who would later serve in Congress — as research chairman.

Montgomery's widow, Regina Cleary, visited San Diego on April 1, 1946, to review the chosen site and the plans for the monument. The design was entrusted to Lloyd Ruocco, a pioneering modernist architect whose work in San Diego included several significant mid-century buildings. Ruocco's design employed the wing panel as its central element — an unorthodox choice that gave the memorial its distinctive, instantly recognizable character.

## Dedication Day, 1950

The Montgomery Memorial was formally dedicated on Sunday, May 21, 1950. John Montgomery's brother James and sister Jane were present. Robert Wilson chaired the Montgomery Memorial Committee; Jerome Rudrauff presented the memorial on behalf of the San Diego Junior Chamber of Commerce. James A. Robbins, Chairman of the San Diego County Board of Supervisors, formally accepted it.

Around the monument's base, a terrazzo walkway was set with the names of aviators who followed in Montgomery's footsteps — a literal inscription of the lineage from the 1880s glider experiments to the aviation achievements of the mid-twentieth century. A tablet nearby carried an inscription commemorating Montgomery's winged flight. The memorial is recognized as California Historical Landmark #711 and as Historic Landmark #13 by the San Diego Historical Resources Board.

## Silver Wing and Its Recreation Centers

Locals sometimes call the memorial by a nickname: Silver Wing, for the gleaming stainless steel panel that is visible for miles. The name was affectionate and apt. In the 1970s, a recreation center near the monument was formally named the Silver Wing Recreation Center, institutionalizing what had been an informal local name into city infrastructure.

The Montgomery Park land originally acquired by the Junior Chamber of Commerce became the Montgomery-Waller Recreation Center in 1964, following a donation by the family of Luckie Waller of an adjacent parcel named for her son. The monument and the recreation facilities around it have given Otay Mesa a gathering place whose name links a neighborhood park to the aviation history that was made on these hills more than a century ago.

## Flight Context

Located at 32.579°N, 117.069°W on a hilltop at Coronado Avenue and Beyer Boulevard in the Otay Mesa area of southern San Diego, less than 3 miles north of the Mexican border. The 93-foot stainless steel wing panel is a distinctive landmark visible for miles. It stands next to the Montgomery-Waller Recreation Center. Brown Field Municipal Airport (KSDM) is about 5 miles east. San Diego International Airport (KSAN) is about 13 miles north-northwest.
