# Silver Strand Training Complex

The narrow Silver Strand sandbar has been in military use since 1920, and the facility that operates here today trains the Navy SEALs and other special operations forces who undertake some of the most demanding missions in the American military.

**Location:** 32.6000, -117.1300

**Categories:** Military installations in California, United States Navy SEALs, Coronado, California

## Introduction

The Silver Strand is a thin strip of sand connecting Coronado to Imperial Beach, barely wide enough in places for the road that runs its length. It is the sort of geography that seems defined by what it lacks — a sliver of land between two bodies of water, exposed to wind and spray, with nowhere to shelter from either. This exposure is exactly why the Navy chose it. The military has used this stretch of the Silver Strand since 1920, and today its beaches are a training ground for one of the most demanding military preparation programs in the world.

## A Century of Military Use

Military activity at this site began in 1920, when the Navy established a radio compass station on the narrow sandbar, a presence that would expand and intensify over the following century. During World War II, the Army established Fort Emory here, operating the facility from 1942 to 1947 before transferring it to the Navy. The transition marked the beginning of the site's evolution into the specialized training complex it is today.

The wartime history of the site includes an unexpected chapter: by 1945, 112 women serving in the WAVES (Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service) worked at the Navy's direction-finding station here conducting signals intelligence — skilled, demanding, classified work of a kind that went largely unacknowledged for decades after the war ended. It was a significant contribution to the war effort, conducted in a facility that is now known primarily for training combat swimmers.

## The Elephant Cage

For decades, the Silver Strand Training Complex was home to one of the most distinctive pieces of Cold War infrastructure in California: a massive Wullenweber antenna array, installed in 1965 and nicknamed the 'elephant cage' for its appearance. The array was a huge circular installation designed for high-frequency direction finding — tracking radio signals, locating transmitters, serving intelligence purposes that were never fully disclosed publicly.

The elephant cage stopped operating in 1999, and the Navy, calling it obsolete equipment, had it demolished by early 2015, ending its long run as a local landmark. Its removal came just as a major expansion of the training complex was getting under way, with investment in the facility increasing substantially in the years that followed.

## What SEALs Endure Here

The Silver Strand's beaches are best known as the proving ground of Basic Underwater Demolition/SEAL training — BUD/S — the grueling course, run by the Naval Special Warfare Center at nearby Naval Amphibious Base Coronado, that shapes Naval Special Warfare operators. Candidates training here face surf zone workouts in the Pacific, endurance runs in soft beach sand, and cold-water immersion exercises conducted regardless of season. The Pacific off the Silver Strand runs cold, and the exposed position of the strand means wind and spray are near-constant factors.

The training course deliberately uses the environment as an adversary. The cold Pacific water, which falls into the 50s Fahrenheit in winter, tests physiological limits in ways that cannot be replicated in controlled settings. The soft sand demands more energy from every step. The open exposure means no respite from conditions. The program's completion rates reflect this intensity — the majority of candidates do not finish. Those who do have demonstrated they can function effectively when conditions are at their most punishing.

## Expansion and Investment

In recent years, the Silver Strand Training Complex has undergone substantial expansion, with investment exceeding $700 million committed to upgrading facilities, adding training infrastructure, and increasing the complex's capacity. The investment reflects the Navy's continued emphasis on special operations capabilities and the institutional understanding that the Silver Strand environment — with its combination of ocean access, beach terrain, and proximity to Naval Base Coronado — is uniquely suited to preparing personnel for maritime special warfare missions.

The expansion, a campus meant to bring Naval Special Warfare commands together in one place, is turning what was once a relatively modest facility into one of the most significant installations in the Naval Special Warfare enterprise. The same narrow strip of sand where the Navy set up a radio compass station in 1920 is becoming a major component of the infrastructure that develops the Navy's most specialized operators.

## Flight Context

Located at 32.60°N, 117.13°W on the Silver Strand, the narrow sandbar connecting Coronado to the south. The strand is clearly visible from altitude as a thin strip between San Diego Bay to the east and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The training complex occupies a significant portion of the strand's length. NAS North Island (KNZY) is at the northern end of the Coronado peninsula. San Diego International Airport (KSAN) is approximately 10 miles north-northwest. At lower altitudes, beach training areas are sometimes visible.
