# Silver Strand Training Complex

The narrow Silver Strand sandbar has been a site of military training since 1920, and the facility that operates here today shapes the Navy SEALs who undertake some of the most demanding missions in the American military.

**Location:** 32.6000, -117.1300

**Categories:** Military installations in California, United States Navy SEALs, Coronado, California

## Introduction

The Silver Strand is a thin strip of sand connecting Coronado to the Imperial Beach peninsula, barely wide enough in places for the road that runs its length. It is the sort of geography that seems defined by what it lacks — a sliver of land between two bodies of water, exposed to wind and spray, with nowhere to shelter from either. This exposure is exactly why the Navy chose it. Since 1920, the Silver Strand has been a training ground, and today it hosts one of the most demanding military preparation programs in the world.

## A Century of Military Use

Military activity on the Silver Strand began in 1920, establishing a training presence on the narrow sandbar that would expand and intensify over the following century. During World War II, the Army established Fort Emory here, operating the facility from 1942 to 1947 before transferring it to the Navy. The transition marked the beginning of the site's evolution into the specialized training complex it is today.

The wartime history of the site includes an unexpected chapter from 1945: 112 women serving in the WAVES (Women Accepted for Volunteer Emergency Service) worked here conducting signals intelligence operations. Their work intercepted and analyzed Japanese communications — skilled, demanding, classified work that was largely unacknowledged for decades after the war ended. It was a significant contribution to the war effort, conducted in a facility that is now known primarily for training combat swimmers.

## The Elephant Cage

For decades, the Silver Strand Training Complex was home to one of the most distinctive pieces of Cold War infrastructure in California: a massive Wullenweber antenna array, nicknamed the 'elephant cage' for its appearance. The array was a huge circular installation designed for high-frequency direction finding — tracking radio signals, locating transmitters, serving intelligence purposes that were never fully disclosed publicly.

The elephant cage at Silver Strand was dismantled in 2015, ending its role as both a functional piece of signals intelligence infrastructure and a local landmark. Its removal was part of a broader modernization and expansion of the training complex, as investment in the facility increased substantially in the years following. The site where the array stood has been incorporated into the expanded training area.

## What SEALs Endure Here

The Silver Strand Training Complex is most widely known as the home of Basic Underwater Demolition/SEAL training — BUD/S — the grueling course that shapes Naval Special Warfare operators. Candidates training here face surf zone workouts in the Pacific, endurance runs in soft beach sand, and cold-water immersion exercises conducted regardless of season. The Pacific off the Silver Strand runs cold, and the exposed position of the strand means wind and spray are near-constant factors.

The training course deliberately uses the environment as an adversary. The near-freezing Pacific water temperatures test physiological limits in ways that cannot be replicated in controlled settings. The soft sand demands more energy from every step. The open exposure means no respite from conditions. The program's completion rates reflect this intensity — the majority of candidates do not finish. Those who do have demonstrated they can function effectively when conditions are at their most punishing.

## Expansion and Investment

In recent years, the Silver Strand Training Complex has undergone substantial expansion, with investment exceeding $700 million committed to upgrading facilities, adding training infrastructure, and increasing the complex's capacity. The investment reflects the Navy's continued emphasis on special operations capabilities and the institutional understanding that the Silver Strand environment — with its combination of ocean access, beach terrain, and proximity to Naval Base Coronado — is uniquely suited to preparing personnel for maritime special warfare missions.

The expansion has transformed what was once a relatively modest facility into one of the most significant training installations in the Naval Special Warfare enterprise. The same narrow strip of sand that the Army established as a minor facility in 1920 is now a major component of the infrastructure that develops the Navy's most specialized operators.

## Flight Context

Located at 32.60°N, 117.13°W on the Silver Strand, the narrow sandbar connecting Coronado to the south. The strand is clearly visible from altitude as a thin strip between San Diego Bay to the east and the Pacific Ocean to the west. The training complex occupies a significant portion of the strand's length. NAS North Island (KNZY) is at the northern end of the Coronado peninsula. San Diego International Airport (KSAN) is approximately 10 miles north. At lower altitudes, beach training areas are sometimes visible.
