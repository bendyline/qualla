# Ironwood State Prison

Ironwood State Prison in the California desert south of Blythe has become an unlikely venue for rehabilitation innovation — from The Last Mile's coding program to a TEDx conference that drew Richard Branson into a room with incarcerated men.

**Location:** 33.6309, -114.6700

**Categories:** Prisons in California, Prison rehabilitation, California corrections, Blythe California

## Introduction

The desert south of Interstate 10 near Blythe is not where most people would expect innovation to happen. But Ironwood State Prison — a Level III facility in eastern Riverside County, adjacent to the now-closed Chuckawalla Valley State Prison — has developed a reputation that sits uneasily alongside the grim statistics of California's overcrowded corrections system. In 2014, a TEDx conference took place inside Ironwood's walls. In 2015, the prison became one of the first in the country to host The Last Mile, a program that teaches computer programming to people in prison. Richard Branson attended one event. The desert, it turns out, is full of surprises.

## The Physical Reality

Ironwood holds roughly 2,464 people — 112 percent of its design capacity as of mid-2022. It consists of four Level III facilities plus an outside Level I area, with portions converted to Sensitive Needs Yard housing for people who require separation from the general population. The warden since 2014 has been Neil McDowell. The facility sits in a landscape of extraordinary flatness, the Chuckwalla Mountains visible to the west, the Chocolate Mountains to the south, and Interstate 10 cutting across the desert to the north. Nothing about the physical setting suggests a place where someone might learn to code or hear a TED talk.

## The Programs List

California's prison system has long offered rehabilitation programming; the range at Ironwood is notable. Academic offerings run from basic literacy through GED preparation and independent study. Vocational training covers auto mechanics, carpentry, plumbing, welding, electronics, and masonry. The rehabilitative programming list is longer still: Actors' Gang (the theater program founded by actor Tim Robbins), Anti-Recidivism Coalition, Inside Out Writers, Father2Child Literacy Program, Veterans Group Ironwood, and Victim Offender Education Group, among many others. In 2015, The Last Mile — a nonprofit that has expanded coding education into multiple prisons — chose Ironwood as its second launch site.

## A TEDx in the Desert

In 2014, the Anti-Recidivism Coalition partnered with the Ford Foundation to stage a TEDx conference inside Ironwood State Prison. Hundreds attended, including Richard Branson, whose interest in prison reform had led him to participate in events aimed at changing how the public and policymakers thought about incarceration. ARC documented the event in a short film accessible online. The juxtaposition — billionaire entrepreneur in a desert prison, giving a platform to men who had lost theirs — was deliberate and pointed. Whether such moments translate into lasting systemic change is a separate question, but as evidence that the ideas inside Ironwood were not limited to what the facility's architecture suggested, the TEDx event served its purpose.

## The Long View

Ironwood's history of rehabilitation programming exists alongside the other reality: a prison holding more people than it was built for, in a remote desert location chosen partly because remoteness itself is a form of control. The men incarcerated there — including, at one point, Robert John Bardo, who murdered actress Rebecca Schaeffer in 1989 — serve sentences that measure years in a place where days are measured by meals, counts, and the movement of desert light across concrete yards. The coding classes and the TED talks take place within that context, not outside of it.

## Flight Context

Located at 33.63°N, 114.67°W, south of Interstate 10 near Blythe, California. The prison complex is visible from the air as a structured cluster of buildings on flat desert terrain. The adjacent Chuckawalla Valley State Prison (now closed) was immediately to the west. Blythe Airport (BLH) is approximately 10 miles to the northeast.
