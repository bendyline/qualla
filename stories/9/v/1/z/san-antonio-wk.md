# San Antonio: Remember the Alamo

The Texas city where 200 defenders died at the Alamo in 1836, where the River Walk transformed urban renewal into tourism gold, and where Mexican and Texan identities blend in the oldest major city in the state.

**Location:** 29.4241, -98.4936

**Categories:** texas, san-antonio, city, alamo, history

## Introduction

On March 6, 1836, Mexican forces under General Santa Anna overran the Alamo mission after a 13-day siege. Nearly 200 Texan defenders died - Jim Bowie, William B. Travis, Davy Crockett among them. 'Remember the Alamo!' became the rallying cry that led to Texas independence. The mission chapel still stands in downtown San Antonio, surrounded now by hotels and office buildings, a small fortress engulfed by the city it helped create. San Antonio is the oldest major city in Texas, founded as a Spanish mission in 1718, still more Mexican than other Texas cities, still proud of its distinct identity. The River Walk, below street level, has become the city's other defining image - a linear park along the San Antonio River that turned a flood control project into Texas's most-visited attraction.

## Remember the Alamo

The Alamo began as Mission San Antonio de Valero, founded in 1718, secularized by the Spanish and later converted to a military garrison. When Texan revolutionaries occupied it in December 1835, they knew they were outmatched. Santa Anna's army of thousands surrounded fewer than 200 defenders. The siege lasted 13 days; the final assault came before dawn on March 6, 1836. All the defenders died - or were executed after capture. But the battle delayed Santa Anna long enough for Sam Houston to organize the army that defeated him at San Jacinto six weeks later. The Alamo became the foundational myth of Texas - sacrifice for liberty, standing against overwhelming odds. The chapel and its mission grounds are now a shrine; 2.5 million visitors come annually.

## The River Walk

The San Antonio River flooded regularly, catastrophically in 1921 when 50 people died. The city considered paving over the downtown stretch; architect Robert Hugman proposed instead a linear park below street level, with shops, restaurants, and hotels opening onto the river. Construction began in 1939 as a WPA project; the River Walk has expanded ever since, now stretching 15 miles. The downtown section - Paseo del Rio - curves below street level, lined with restaurants and cafes, connected to hotels by bridges and staircases. It's artificial but successful, transforming urban infrastructure into Texas's most-visited attraction (after the Alamo). The River Walk proved that urban rivers could be assets rather than problems.

## The Missions

The Alamo was one of five missions established along the San Antonio River in the 18th century. The others - Mission Concepcion, Mission San Jose, Mission San Juan, and Mission Espada - survive south of downtown, forming San Antonio Missions National Historical Park (and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2015). Mission San Jose, the 'Queen of the Missions,' is the largest and most fully restored, its carved limestone rose window famous. These missions converted and incorporated local indigenous people into Spanish colonial society - a process that was often coercive but that created the mestizo culture that defines San Antonio today. The missions still hold Catholic services; they're living history, not museum pieces.

## Tejano San Antonio

San Antonio is the most Mexican of major American cities - 64% Hispanic, predominantly Mexican-American. The culture shows everywhere: the breakfast tacos (chorizo and egg, barbacoa, bean and cheese), the Market Square (El Mercado, claiming to be the largest Mexican market outside Mexico), the conjunto and Tejano music in west side clubs. Fiesta San Antonio, 11 days in April, celebrates the Battle of San Jacinto with parades, food, and general excess. The cultural heritage isn't nostalgic - it's contemporary, a living tradition that makes San Antonio feel like the border city it geographically isn't. Austin may be quirky and Houston global, but San Antonio is deeply, authentically Tejano.

## Texas Military City

San Antonio International Airport (SAT) lies north of downtown. The city's military presence is enormous: Joint Base San Antonio combines Fort Sam Houston (Army), Lackland Air Force Base, and Randolph Air Force Base; the city calls itself 'Military City, USA.' The Alamo city also has the San Antonio Spurs (five NBA championships), SeaWorld, and Six Flags Fiesta Texas. The Pearl District, a former brewery complex, has become the upscale dining and shopping destination. From altitude, San Antonio appears as sprawling development across flat South Texas terrain - the downtown river visible as a dark ribbon, the Alamo lost among larger buildings, the missions strung along the river to the south - the city that remembers, the oldest in Texas.
