# Reno: The Biggest Little City Where the Sierra Meets the Desert

Nevada's second gambling city at the base of the Sierra Nevada, where Marilyn Monroe filmed her last movie, where the Truckee River flows through downtown, and where Burning Man pilgrims gather before heading to the desert.

**Location:** 39.5275, -119.8135

## Introduction

The famous arch across Virginia Street proclaims Reno 'The Biggest Little City in the World' - a nickname earned in 1929 and maintained ever since. Reno existed before Las Vegas, the original Nevada gambling town where divorce seekers waited out residency requirements and casinos thrived when California outlawed games of chance. The city sits where the Sierra Nevada mountains meet the Great Basin desert, the Truckee River cutting through downtown on its way from Lake Tahoe to its terminus at Pyramid Lake. This is where Marilyn Monroe and Clark Gable filmed 'The Misfits' in 1961 - the last completed film for both. This is where Burning Man pilgrims gather supplies before heading to the Black Rock Desert. This is Nevada's other gambling city, older and grittier than Las Vegas, a high desert town that combines mountain recreation with casino neon.

## The Divorce Trade

Reno became famous for divorce before it became famous for gambling. In the early 20th century, when most states required years of residency and proof of fault to dissolve a marriage, Nevada required only six weeks. Unhappy spouses - typically wealthy women from the East Coast - traveled to Reno, established residency at dude ranches or hotels, and emerged six weeks later legally single. The divorce trade shaped Reno's economy and reputation; hotels catered to women serving their 'Reno-vation'; lawyers specialized in quick separations. 'Getting a Reno' became slang for divorce nationwide. The trade declined as other states liberalized their divorce laws, but it established Reno's identity as a place where rules were different.

## The Misfits

In 1960, director John Huston brought Marilyn Monroe and Clark Gable to Reno and the surrounding Nevada desert to film 'The Misfits,' written by Monroe's husband Arthur Miller. The production was troubled - Monroe's pill dependency caused delays, Gable's heart condition worried everyone, the desert heat baked cast and crew. Gable died of a heart attack two weeks after filming wrapped; Monroe died eighteen months later. The film became a eulogy for both and for the Old West it depicted. Reno scenes included downtown casinos and Virginia Street; desert sequences filmed near Pyramid Lake and the Mustang Ranch area. The Misfits remains Reno's most famous film, its melancholy perfectly matching the high desert landscape.

## Gateway to Burning Man

Each August, Reno transforms as 80,000 people pass through on their way to Burning Man in the Black Rock Desert, 100 miles north. The week before the festival, Walmart and hardware stores fill with burners buying last-minute supplies - water, shade structures, costumes, bicycle parts. Hotels charge premium rates for those arriving early or recovering late. The Great Reno Balloon Race coincides with Burning Man timing, launching hundreds of hot air balloons over the Truckee Meadows. Reno embraced its role as gateway to Black Rock City, understanding that the same libertarian streak that built Nevada gambling also attracts people heading to a temporary city where normal rules don't apply.

## Lake Tahoe's Casino Side

Lake Tahoe lies 45 minutes southwest, its Nevada shore lined with casinos that California wouldn't allow. Reno serves as the urban center for the entire region - Lake Tahoe's ski resorts, Virginia City's Comstock Lode history, Pyramid Lake's sacred waters, Carson City's state government. The California Zephyr train stops daily, one of the most scenic rail routes in America crossing the Sierra Nevada from the Bay Area. Skiers discovered that Reno hotels offered cheaper rooms than Tahoe's slopes; the combination of gambling and skiing created a winter economy. Squaw Valley (host of the 1960 Winter Olympics), Heavenly, and Alpine Meadows are all within easy driving distance.

## Truckee Meadows

Reno-Tahoe International Airport (RNO) serves the region with connections to major Western hubs. I-80 crosses the Sierra Nevada via Donner Pass (yes, that Donner) to connect Reno with the Bay Area; the route closes occasionally during winter storms. The Truckee River flows through downtown, its kayak park providing whitewater in the middle of the city. Virginia Street runs north-south through the casino core; the famous arch spans the street at the heart of downtown. From altitude, Reno appears as development filling the Truckee Meadows - the Sierra Nevada rising dramatically to the west, the Great Basin desert stretching east, the river visible as a thread of green through the high desert. The Biggest Little City, older than Las Vegas and proud of it.
