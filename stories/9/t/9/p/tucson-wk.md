# Tucson: The Old Pueblo in the Sonoran Desert

The desert city surrounded by saguaro cacti, where the Mission San Xavier del Bac has stood since 1797, and where Pima and Spanish and Mexican and American layers create the authentic Old West.

**Location:** 32.2226, -110.9747

**Categories:** arizona, tucson, city, desert, mission

## Introduction

Tucson is the real Arizona - the Old Pueblo, continuously inhabited for over 4,000 years, older than Phoenix will ever be. The Hohokam farmed here; the Tohono O'odham people remain. The Spanish established a presidio in 1775; the Mission San Xavier del Bac, its white walls gleaming against the desert, has served worshippers since 1797. Arizona was Mexican territory until 1854, and Tucson feels it - the food, the architecture, the attitude owe more to Sonora than to the American Southwest that came later. Where Phoenix sprawls across the desert floor, Tucson nestles against mountains: the Santa Catalinas to the north, the Rincons to the east, the Tucsons to the west. Saguaro cacti, those iconic symbols of the American Southwest, grow wild here in the only place they're found - the Sonoran Desert that Tucson calls home.

## The White Dove of the Desert

Mission San Xavier del Bac rises from the desert nine miles south of downtown, its white stucco walls and ornate Spanish Colonial facade earning the nickname 'White Dove of the Desert.' Father Eusebio Kino founded the mission in 1692; the current church, completed in 1797, is the oldest intact European structure in Arizona. The interior contains original frescoes, carved wooden saints, and a reclining figure of Saint Francis Xavier that pilgrims visit seeking healing. The Tohono O'odham reservation surrounds the mission; the tribe maintains it as an active church. Mass is still celebrated; visitors are welcome when services aren't in session. The mission represents Tucson's oldest layer - Spanish Catholicism brought to the desert frontier.

## Saguaro Country

The saguaro cactus grows only in the Sonoran Desert, primarily in southern Arizona. These giant cacti can reach 40 feet tall, live 200 years, and don't grow their first arm until age 70. Saguaro National Park, divided into two districts on either side of Tucson, protects thousands of these iconic plants. The western Tucson Mountain District offers sunset views among dense saguaro forests; the eastern Rincon Mountain District climbs from desert floor to pine forest. The cactus forests look alien - towering green columns with arms reaching skyward, each plant a water storage system adapted to survive extreme drought. No image says 'American Southwest' quite like a saguaro silhouette against an orange desert sunset.

## University Town

The University of Arizona was established in 1885, Tucson's consolation prize after Phoenix got the state capitol (Tucsonans were reportedly furious). Today UA dominates the city - 45,000 students, major research programs, and the Wildcat sports teams that unite the community. The campus features the Arizona State Museum (anthropology), the Center for Creative Photography, and the Flandrau Science Center. Fourth Avenue, adjacent to campus, provides the college-town strip of bars, vintage shops, and music venues. UA's optical sciences program helped build telescopes on nearby Kitt Peak; the astronomy department takes advantage of dark desert skies. What seemed like a disappointment in 1885 became Tucson's anchor.

## Sonoran Food

Tucson was designated a UNESCO City of Gastronomy in 2015 - the first American city to receive the honor. The designation recognized not fine dining but heritage cuisine: the Sonoran hot dog (bacon-wrapped, topped with pinto beans, onions, tomatoes, mayonnaise, mustard, and jalapeno sauce), the cheese crisp (basically a Mexican pizza), the chimichangas that may have been invented here. The food draws from indigenous ingredients (tepary beans, chiles, nopales), Spanish colonial traditions, and the ongoing Mexican influence that keeps Tucson connected to Sonora. El Charro Cafe, operating since 1922, claims to have invented the chimichanga. Mi Nidito served presidents Clinton and Obama. The food is the culture; Tucson eats its history.

## The Old Pueblo

Tucson International Airport (TUS) lies south of downtown. The Sun Link streetcar connects the university to downtown and Fourth Avenue. The Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum, west of town, combines zoo, botanical garden, and natural history museum in an outdoor desert setting. Old Tucson, a movie studio and theme park, recreates the Old West film set where hundreds of Westerns were shot. Tombstone, site of the OK Corral gunfight, lies 70 miles southeast. From altitude, Tucson appears as development surrounded by desert and mountain ranges - the Santa Catalinas rising dramatically to the north, saguaro-studded foothills in every direction, the white mission visible to the south. The Old Pueblo, where layers of history coexist, where the desert defines daily life.
