# El Paso: The Sun City at the Pass of the North

The Texas border city where the Rio Grande divides two nations, where Spanish explorers named the 'Pass of the North' in 1581, and where Fort Bliss guards the largest military installation in the United States.

**Location:** 31.7591, -106.4876

**Categories:** texas, el-paso, city, border, desert

## Introduction

Spanish travelers named this place 'El Paso del Norte' - the Pass of the North - in 1581, recognizing its importance as the gateway between Mexico's interior and the northern frontier. The Rio Grande cuts through here, squeezing between the Franklin Mountains and the Sierra de Juarez, creating a natural corridor that humans have used for millennia. El Paso grew on the American side; Ciudad Juarez grew on the Mexican side; together they form a binational metropolis of 2.4 million people. The river that divides them is sometimes a trickle, sometimes dry, always symbolic. El Paso is Texas but not Texas - closer to San Diego than to Houston, Spanish-speaking majority, Chihuahuan Desert culture, the Sun City that gets over 300 sunny days a year in a landscape that looks more like Breaking Bad than Dallas.

## The Border

Four international bridges connect El Paso to Ciudad Juarez - pedestrians and vehicles crossing daily for work, shopping, family visits, dentist appointments. The Paso del Norte bridge downtown provides the most dramatic crossing, connecting El Paso's Santa Fe Street to Juarez's Avenida Juarez. Before the Mexican-American War, this was all Mexico; after 1848, the Rio Grande became the boundary. The border is porous by necessity: Mexican workers commute to American jobs; American patients cross for cheaper medical care; families span both countries. The violence in Juarez during the cartel wars (2008-2012) made El Paso nervous but didn't spill over - El Paso consistently ranked among America's safest cities even as its sister city experienced horrific violence.

## Fort Bliss

Fort Bliss is among the largest military installations in the world - 1.12 million acres spreading from El Paso into New Mexico. The Army has stationed troops here since 1848, initially to protect the border after the Mexican-American War. Today Fort Bliss serves as the Army's primary air defense artillery training center and hosts tens of thousands of soldiers and their families. The military presence shapes El Paso's economy and culture; military surplus stores cluster near the base; restaurants cater to soldiers' tastes. White Sands Missile Range and Holloman Air Force Base lie north in New Mexico, part of a continuous military landscape that dominates the region.

## The Margarita Was Born Here

The original margarita was invented in the El Paso-Juarez region on July 4, 1942, by Francisco 'Pancho' Morales at Tommy's Place bar. When a customer ordered a Magnolia - a drink Morales didn't know how to make except that it contained Cointreau - he improvised with tequila, lime, and triple sec. The drink spread through El Paso's bartender school where Morales taught. The name 'margarita' means 'daisy' in Spanish, possibly a nod to the Magnolia he was trying to approximate. Other cities claim margarita invention, but El Paso's claim has documentation. The drink perfectly suits the border climate - something cool and strong for 300 sunny days.

## UTEP and the Bhutanese Architecture

The University of Texas at El Paso campus looks like nowhere else in America. In 1917, the wife of the university's first dean noticed a National Geographic article about Bhutan and decided the Himalayan kingdom's architecture suited the desert climate. UTEP's buildings have been constructed in Bhutanese style ever since - ornate rooflines, white and orange facades, a visual echo of a mountain kingdom 8,000 miles away. The Sun Bowl stadium hosts college football; the Don Haskins Center (named for the basketball coach who won the 1966 NCAA championship with an all-Black starting five, a civil rights milestone) hosts basketball and concerts. The Cincinnati Entertainment District near campus provides nightlife.

## Sun City

El Paso International Airport (ELP) serves the city with connections to major hubs; Las Cruces, New Mexico lies 45 miles north. I-10 runs through town, connecting El Paso to points east (Houston is 745 miles) and west (Tucson is 265 miles). El Paso is actually closer to San Diego than to Dallas. The Chihuahuan Desert landscape surrounds the city - the Franklin Mountains running north-south through the middle of town, the only mountain range contained entirely within a single American city limits. From altitude, El Paso appears as development filling the pass between mountains - the Rio Grande visible as a thin line dividing nations, Ciudad Juarez spreading south, the Sun City that looks nothing like the rest of Texas.
