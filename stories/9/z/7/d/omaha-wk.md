# Omaha: Warren Buffett's Town and the College World Series

Nebraska's largest city where Warren Buffett still lives in his original house, where the College World Series has crowned baseball champions since 1950, and where the Henry Doorly Zoo ranks among the world's best.

**Location:** 41.2500, -96.0000

## Introduction

Warren Buffett, one of the world's richest people, still lives in the house he bought in Omaha's Dundee neighborhood in 1958 for $31,500. He could live anywhere; he stays in Omaha. That says something about a city that outsiders often overlook. Omaha sits on the Missouri River bluffs, the largest city in Nebraska and a Fortune 500 headquarters town despite a metro population under a million. Each June, the College World Series brings baseball's best collegiate teams to Charles Schwab Field, a tradition since 1950 that makes Omaha the center of college baseball for two weeks. The Henry Doorly Zoo - often ranked the world's best - spreads across 160 acres with the largest indoor desert and nocturnal exhibit anywhere. Omaha isn't flashy; it's quietly excellent.

## Warren Buffett's Omaha

Warren Buffett was born in Omaha in 1930, built Berkshire Hathaway into one of the world's most valuable companies, and never left. His gray stucco house on Farnam Street in Dundee would be unremarkable except for the security detail. Berkshire Hathaway's annual shareholders meeting brings 40,000 investors to Omaha each May - the 'Woodstock of Capitalism' - where Buffett holds court answering questions. Omaha hosts four Fortune 500 headquarters despite its modest size: Berkshire Hathaway, Union Pacific, Kiewit, and Mutual of Omaha. The city has more millionaires per capita than anywhere in America. Buffett could have moved to New York decades ago; his staying made Omaha believe in itself.

## The College World Series

Since 1950, the NCAA Division I Baseball Championship has concluded in Omaha - first at Rosenblatt Stadium, now at Charles Schwab Field downtown. For two weeks each June, eight teams compete in the double-elimination tournament while 24,000 fans pack the stadium nightly. The College World Series is Omaha's defining annual event, filling hotels and restaurants, turning downtown into a baseball festival. The tournament has remained in Omaha for over 70 years when other cities would have poached it, the locals' genuine enthusiasm keeping it home. Omaha claims baseball the way other cities claim football; the CWS is how Nebraska summers begin.

## The Zoo That Defies Expectations

The Henry Doorly Zoo consistently ranks among the world's best - a surprise in a mid-sized Midwestern city. The Lied Jungle recreates an indoor rainforest; the Desert Dome houses the world's largest indoor desert under a geodesic dome; the Kingdoms of the Night exhibit is the world's largest nocturnal animal display. The Scott Aquarium, Gorilla Valley, African Grasslands - 160 acres of world-class exhibits that draw visitors from across the region. The zoo succeeds through community support; Omaha votes for bond issues, donates to capital campaigns, and treats the zoo as civic infrastructure. Henry Doorly didn't happen by accident; it happened because Omaha decided it mattered.

## Old Market and Saddle Creek

The Old Market preserves Omaha's 19th-century warehouse district - brick streets, covered sidewalks, galleries and restaurants filling buildings that once stored grain. Horse-drawn carriages offer rides through narrow streets where street performers play. It's Omaha's answer to a European quarter, an unexpected urban village in a city known for sprawl. The Saddle Creek record label put Omaha on the indie music map in the early 2000s, signing Bright Eyes, The Faint, and Cursive - the 'Omaha Sound' that proved alternative culture could emerge from the Plains. The Slowdown and Waiting Room venues continue hosting national acts in a city that punches above its cultural weight.

## Missouri River City

Eppley Airfield (OMA) serves Omaha with regional connections; the city sits at the junction of I-80 and I-29, the crossroads where the transcontinental Interstate meets the north-south route along the Missouri. Council Bluffs, Iowa lies just across the river - Omahans cross for casino gambling (illegal in Nebraska). The California Zephyr stops at Omaha's Amtrak station en route between Chicago and the Bay Area. From altitude, Omaha appears as a city on river bluffs - the Missouri River marking the Iowa border, downtown visible against the water, the orderly grid extending west into Nebraska prairie. What appears from the air as a mid-sized Plains city is where Warren Buffett chose to stay, where college baseball crowns champions, and where a world-class zoo proves great things happen in unlikely places.
