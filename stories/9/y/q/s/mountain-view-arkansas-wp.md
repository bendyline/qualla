# Mountain View, Arkansas

An Ozark county seat that calls itself the Folk Music Capital of the World, where fiddlers and banjo pickers gather on the courthouse square and a gold toilet seat trophy awaits the fastest outhouse.

**Location:** 35.8650, -92.1086

**Categories:** Cities in Stone County, Arkansas, County seats in Arkansas, Folk music, Ozarks

## Introduction

Early on a Saturday morning in late October, about a thousand pounds of pinto beans begin to simmer in big antique iron kettles set up around the Stone County Courthouse square. At noon, free beans and cornbread are served to the waiting crowd. Then comes the main event: teams racing handcrafted, people-powered outhouses on wheels for the coveted gold toilet seat trophy. The Arkansas Bean Fest and Great Championship Outhouse Races tell you most of what you need to know about Mountain View, a town of about 2,900 people that takes its fun, and its music, seriously.

## Folk Music Capital of the World

Mountain View's economy rests largely on that title. Whenever the weather is fine in spring, summer and fall, people drift into the courthouse square with instruments and start to play. Bluegrass dominates, but folk, swing, country and gospel ring out too, played by local townsfolk and by pickers who travel hundreds of miles to join in. A few dedicated musicians play only the old-time tunes handed down through generations. The tradition took formal shape with the Arkansas Folk Festival, first held in April 1963, when songwriter Jimmy Driftwood, born in nearby Timbo and famous for "The Battle of New Orleans," gathered local amateur musicians to perform. The festival still returns each April with an old-fashioned hometown parade of school bands, veterans groups, show horses and decorated floats. Its success inspired the Ozark Folk Center State Park, which opened in May 1973 and hosts the Arkansas Fiddle and Banjo Championships, concerts and workshops in banjo, dulcimer and other folk crafts.

## A Valley Under Blue Mountain

The town takes its name from its setting, a valley bordered by the Blue Mountain Range of the Ozarks. Before Mountain View existed, a settlement called Riggsville, said to have been founded in 1819 by Thomas Augustus Riggs, stood just to the east and lasted until after the Civil War. Mountain View was incorporated on August 14, 1890, and grew into the largest city and county seat of Stone County. Its history has not always been gentle. On February 5, 2008, an EF4 tornado from the Super Tuesday outbreak caused major damage to the town. Beyond tourism, the local economy leans on cattle and chicken farming and a few small manufacturers, among them the Stone County Iron Works, a nationally known maker of iron furniture and housewares, and the town has recently seen growth in personal aircraft maintenance and repair. Stone County remains dry. Hollywood star Dick Powell, singer, actor and co-founder of Four Star Television, was born here.

## Caverns, Trout and Crowds

Music is only part of the draw. Visitors come for Blanchard Springs Caverns in nearby Fifty-Six, for the world-renowned trout fishing of the White River, and for camping, hiking and mountain biking in the Ozark National Forest. Variety shows such as the White River Hoedown mix country, gospel and bluegrass with local humor. The biggest crowds, though, arrive for the two major festivals, when lodging often has to be booked a year ahead. Average attendance runs between 40,000 and 60,000, and some locals claim the town has swelled to 100,000 during past festivals, though that figure is likely an exaggeration. Over the years the calendar has also included bluegrass festivals in March and November, an old-fashioned Fourth of July, a Mountains, Music and Motorcycles weekend in August, and a Christmas tree lighting on the first Saturday of December.

## Flight Context

Mountain View lies at about 35.87°N, 92.11°W in Stone County, in a valley bordered by the Blue Mountain Range of the Arkansas Ozarks. From the air, look for the compact town and its courthouse square set among forested ridges, with the White River corridor to the east and the Ozark National Forest spreading across the surrounding highlands. The terrain rises and falls steeply, so a pass at 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL keeps a safe margin over the ridges while showing the valley well. Batesville Regional Airport (KBVX) lies about 24 nm to the east-southeast, and Baxter County Airport (KBPK) at Mountain Home about 35 nm to the north-northwest. Valley fog is common on cool mornings, and fall foliage makes October flights especially scenic.
