# Yellville, Arkansas

A small Ozark county seat named for a governor who reputedly never paid for the honor, famous for a turkey festival, and perched between the Buffalo National River and Bull Shoals Lake.

**Location:** 36.2297, -92.6853

**Categories:** Cities in Marion County, Arkansas, County seats in Arkansas, Ozarks, Festivals in Arkansas

## Introduction

For decades, the most famous moment of the year in Yellville came from the sky. Each October, during the Turkey Trot festival, live turkeys were dropped from airplanes over the town square while crowds watched from below. The tradition began in 1946, when the first turkeys were dropped from the roof of the Marion County Courthouse, and over the years it drew criticism from People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals and others. The Chamber of Commerce stopped sponsoring the drops after 1989, though a private pilot kept them going, and in 2018, amid animal-welfare backlash, the Rotary Club that took over the festival banned them. The festival goes on in this small Ozark county seat of about 1,200 people.

## A Name Bought for Fifty Dollars

Yellville did not start with that name. When Marion County was created in 1836, the year Arkansas became a state, the county seat was a place called Shawneetown. Residents chose a new name to honor Archibald Yell, the new state's first representative in the U.S. Congress. According to community tradition, Yell wanted the town named for him and offered its leaders fifty dollars for the honor, then never paid. The debt stood for 169 years. In 2005 two of Yell's descendants, one from Michigan and one from Georgia, visited Yellville and presented the city with fifty dollars in their ancestor's name. Yell himself went on to be elected governor of Arkansas in 1840 and was killed at the Battle of Buena Vista during the Mexican-American War. Yellville College was established on College Street in 1890; it was demolished in 1925.

## The Trot and the Lakes

The Turkey Trot, sponsored by the Mid-Marion County Rotary Club, is one of Yellville's longest-running traditions, held on the second weekend of October. Alongside a pageant, dinners, live music, a 5-kilometer run and a parade, streetside booths around the town square sell crafts and gear for hunting wild turkeys, and the festival's turkey calling contest, sponsored by the National Wild Turkey Federation, is nationally recognized. In summer the attention turns to water. Most of northern Marion County lies under the lake created by Bull Shoals Dam, and the White River marks the county's eastern border. Water skiing, pontoon boating and fishing fill the warm months, and Yellville, about 15 miles southwest of the town of Bull Shoals, serves as an alternate base for visitors not staying at the resorts on the lake, along the White River or near Buffalo City, where the Buffalo meets the White.

## Crooked Creek and the Buffalo

Yellville's setting is its other great draw. In 1999 Fred Berry, a former teacher and counselor in the local schools, donated a former dairy farm for a conservation education center, and with additional donations, the Fred Berry Conservation Education Center on Crooked Creek opened on June 17, 2005. One of nine nature centers run by the Arkansas Game and Fish Commission, it covers 421 acres along the creek. The Buffalo National River lies about 16 miles south on Highway 14, and in summer Yellville becomes a hub for visitors who come to canoe, ride horseback and rent cabins. Marion County holds more than 1,000 caves. Near Buffalo Point stands Rush, a ghost town from the county's zinc mining heyday, where abandoned mines, now fenced for safety, still line the hiking trails, though arson has reportedly destroyed a few of its historic buildings.

## Flight Context

Yellville sits at about 36.23°N, 92.69°W in Marion County in the Arkansas Ozarks, along U.S. 62/412. Look for the small town grid around the courthouse square beside Crooked Creek, set among forested ridges. Bull Shoals Lake spreads across much of northern Marion County, and the town of Bull Shoals lies about 15 miles to the northeast, while the White River marks the county's eastern border. The Buffalo National River winds through the hills about 16 miles south along Highway 14. A pass at 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL clears the surrounding ridges and shows the lakes and river valleys well. Baxter County Airport (KBPK) at Mountain Home lies about 13 nm to the northeast, and Boone County Airport (KHRO) at Harrison about 23 nm to the west. Morning valley fog is common, especially in fall.
