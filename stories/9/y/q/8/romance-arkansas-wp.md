# Romance, Arkansas

Named, legend says, by a schoolteacher smitten with the view from its bluffs, the White County crossroads of Romance keeps a small post office that valentines and wedding invitations seek out from across the country.

**Location:** 35.2400, -92.0667

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Arkansas, Unincorporated communities in White County, Arkansas, Post offices, Valentine's Day

## Introduction

Every year, when Valentine's Day approaches, envelopes from around the United States make their way to a small branch post office in the hills of White County, Arkansas. Their senders want one thing: a postmark that reads Romance. Wedding invitations come for the same reason. The community behind the name has no municipal government and no exact boundaries, but it does have a story about how it got its name, and a post office that has turned that name into something people will go out of their way for.

## A Teacher on the Bluffs

As legend has it, Romance was named many years ago by a schoolteacher who found the view from the bluffs on the community's eastern side particularly romantic. Schooling has a long history here. A local school was operating as early as 1895, when the Harper School stood on Blackjack Mountain. In the 1950s the Romance school district was consolidated into the Rose Bud district, and today most local children attend school in nearby Rose Bud, from pre-K through twelfth grade. Romance has no fixed edges, and its families are split among districts: some eastern areas near neighboring Floyd belong to the Beebe schools, and pockets toward the county's western edge, near Highway 310 and Hammons Chapel Road, belong to Mount Vernon–Enola. A store building from 1930, the Romance Store, still stands.

## Love by Postmark

Romance's most visible business is a small branch of the United States Postal Service. It is one of several post offices around the country that draw attention for their love-themed names, but it earned special notice in 1990, when it became the first place to issue that year's stamp in the USPS Love Stamp series, an honor the Postal Service gave it again for the 2024 Love stamp. It is popular with people across the country who want Valentine's cards and wedding invitations to carry its postmark.

The name has even inspired a side venture. In recent years a small number of weddings have been performed on the grounds of the post office, though the practice has nothing to do with the Postal Service itself. For an unincorporated community without exact boundaries, the little post office has become its best-known address.

## Between Rose Bud and El Paso

Romance sits in west-central White County, roughly halfway between the town of Rose Bud to the north and the unincorporated community of El Paso to the south, about 16 miles northwest of Beebe and less than 5 miles east of Mount Vernon. Much of it falls within Marshall and Kentucky townships. Counted by its ZIP code, 72136, the area was home to 1,772 people at the 2010 census.

Without a municipal government, Romance falls under the county judge of White County and the county's ordinances, with law enforcement from the White County Sheriff's Department and the township constables. Two state highways meet here. Highway 5 runs north toward the Missouri line and south to Cabot, while Highway 31 begins in Romance and runs south toward Pine Bluff. The sky overhead has its own traffic: a parachute drop zone for Little Rock Air Force Base was established near Romance in the 1980s.

## Flight Context

Romance lies at 35.24°N, 92.067°W in west-central White County, where Arkansas Highway 31 ends at Highway 5. From 2,500-3,500 ft AGL, follow Highway 5 between Rose Bud to the north and El Paso to the south to find the small crossroads. Important: a parachute drop zone serving Little Rock Air Force Base is located near Romance, so check NOTAMs and expect military airdrop activity. Little Rock Air Force Base (KLRF) is about 20 nm south. Searcy Municipal Airport (KSRC) is about 16 nm east, and Heber Springs Municipal Airport (KHBZ) is about 16.5 nm north. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
