# Greers Ferry Dam

A concrete wall across the Little Red River in north-central Arkansas, Greers Ferry Dam created one of the state's leading lake destinations, and in October 1963 it drew John F. Kennedy to Heber Springs just seven weeks before Dallas.

**Location:** 35.5208, -91.9933

## Introduction

"A rising tide lifts all the boats," John F. Kennedy told his audience in Heber Springs, Arkansas, on October 3, 1963, "and as Arkansas becomes more prosperous so does the United States." He had come to dedicate a wall of concrete across the Little Red River, a dam that had spent almost four years rising out of the Cleburne County hills. The occasion put a sitting president in Cleburne County for the only time in its history. Seven weeks later, Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas. The lake he dedicated that autumn day is still here, spread across the valleys north of Little Rock, and so is the dam that holds it back.

## A Boomtown Beside the Construction Site

Work on Greers Ferry Dam began in March 1959, and it pulled people toward the Little Red River from the start. Hundreds of workers showed up looking for jobs. Once hired, they rented whatever empty houses stood near the construction site, and some simply built their own homes on the ground beside the future lake. Local business owners saw their chance. Motels, shops and stores went up next to the project so the crews would have somewhere to spend their pay and their evenings, and farmers found new demand for their livestock and crops. The dam was completed in December 1962. When the crews were done, the finished concrete wall stood 243 feet high and stretched 1,704 feet across the valley, and the towns around it had learned what a federal project could do for a local economy.

## The President in Heber Springs

The dedication brought Arkansas's political leadership out in force. Kennedy opened his remarks by greeting Senators John McClellan and J. William Fulbright, Governor Orval Faubus, and Congressman Wilbur Mills among others. Then he made his case for projects like this one. "I regard this as an investment by the people of the United States in the United States," he said, and he urged the country to spend the decade "preparing as we must for all of the people who will come after us." In hindsight the visit carries an ache that no one present could have felt: within two months, the man speaking would be gone.

## What the Water Brought

Kennedy's prediction held. The reservoir behind the dam, Greers Ferry Lake, was built mainly for flood control, chiefly to ease flooding downstream along the White River, along with power generation and recreation. After the lake filled, tourism boomed. Businesses opened, others reopened, and Greers Ferry Lake became one of Arkansas's leading destinations. Today, resort communities dot its shores. At the dam, a power plant runs two generating units, each rated at 48,000 kilowatts, turning the weight of the lake into electricity. Below the wall, the Little Red River continues on its way, released from the reservoir that the construction crews of the late 1950s and early 1960s built.

## Flight Context

Greers Ferry Dam sits at 35.521°N, 91.993°W, just northeast of Heber Springs in Cleburne County, Arkansas, roughly 55 miles north-northeast of Little Rock. From 3,000-4,000 feet MSL, the pale concrete face of the dam shows clearly at the southeastern end of Greers Ferry Lake, whose long, branching arms reach west and northwest into the wooded hills. Follow the Little Red River downstream to the southeast toward Searcy. Heber Springs Municipal Airport (KHBZ) is only a few miles away, with Searcy Municipal (KSRC) to the southeast and Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport (KLIT) in Little Rock to the south. The lake surface makes an easy visual reference in clear conditions; morning haze can settle in the valleys during warm months.
