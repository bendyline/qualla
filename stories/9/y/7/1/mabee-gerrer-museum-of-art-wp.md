# Mabee-Gerrer Museum of Art

A Benedictine monk who painted the official portrait of Pope Pius X gathered art from four continents for a museum in Shawnee, Oklahoma, where Old Masters now share space with Oklahoma's only Egyptian mummies.

**Location:** 35.3668, -96.9537

**Categories:** Art museums and galleries in Oklahoma, Museums in Pottawatomie County, Oklahoma, St. Gregory's University, Egyptian antiquities, Art museums and galleries established in 1919

## Introduction

Several artists competed to paint the official portrait of Pope Pius X. When the pope saw the finished portrait by one of them, he selected it, because the artist had painted him true to life and had not minimized his facial warts. That artist was Gregory Gerrer, a Benedictine monk from St. Gregory's Abbey in Shawnee, Oklahoma, and the official portrait of Pius X is now in the museum that carries his name. The Mabee-Gerrer Museum of Art holds more than 3,500 works spanning over 8,000 years of human creativity, and it has outlasted the university whose campus it shares.

## The Monk with a Paintbrush

Gerrer was born on July 23, 1867, in Lautenbach, Alsace. He became a Benedictine monk at St. Gregory's Abbey and was both an art historian and a collector. From 1900 to 1904 he studied art in Rome, and it was his portrait of Pius X that the pope selected as his official image. Gerrer traveled widely in the United States, Europe, Africa and South America, gathering art and artifacts to bring back to Oklahoma. The collection began modestly. In 1903, while traveling in the Holy Land, Gerrer was given an Egyptian scarab carved with the hieroglyphic symbol of a goose. By 1919 the collection had moved into Benedictine Hall as the St. Gregory's Abbey Art Gallery and Museum. Gerrer died in Oklahoma on August 24, 1946.

## A Gallery That Made an Artist

The museum shaped at least one Oklahoma career. G. Patrick Riley visited as a child in the early 1940s and credits those visits with inspiring him to become an artist. In 2022 a large sculpture called "Trinity Eagle," created by Riley and fellow artist Glen Henry, was installed in front of the museum. The collection itself traveled for a time. Starting in 1962, it was on long-term loan to the Oklahoma Science and Arts Foundation in Oklahoma City. Eventually St. Gregory's Abbey built a permanent home for it in Shawnee with matching funds from the Mabee Foundation. The Mabee-Gerrer Museum of Art, designed by Chadsey/Architects of Tulsa, opened in 1979. It is a member of the Oklahoma Association of Museums.

## Eight Thousand Years in Shawnee

The collection covers cultures from around the world. Visitors can see Roman glass, ancient Greek pottery, Chinese terracotta figures, European ivories and icons, Venetian armor, Mesoamerican stone carvings, and African masks and bronzes. The paintings include works by Guido Reni, Tintoretto, Guercino, Bartolomé Esteban Murillo, Jean-Léon Gérôme, William-Adolphe Bouguereau, William Merritt Chase and Albert Bierstadt. The Spanish Colonial collection includes retablos, small religious paintings often done on tin, wooden figures of saints, and an anonymous early 19th-century painting, Christ of Ixmiquilpan, which depicts a miraculous altar scene in Mexico City. The collection traveled to the Arlington Museum of Art in Texas in 2013. In 2004 the museum became the first place to show publicly the gold jewelry from the collection of Prince Fabrizio Alliata di Montereale, an exhibition later featured in a documentary on Oklahoma public television.

## What the Scans Revealed

The museum also holds Oklahoma's only Egyptian mummies, along with a sarcophagus and a number of animal mummies. Modern medicine has helped tell their story. In 1991 the museum's two Egyptian mummies were taken to a Shawnee hospital for X-rays. In August 2015 they returned to the same hospital, SSM Health St. Anthony Hospital–Shawnee, for CT scans. The images showed that the organs of the woman known as Tutu had each been mummified separately and then placed back inside her chest and abdomen before her body was wrapped in linen. It was careful, deliberate work by the ancient embalmers who prepared her.

## The Museum That Stayed

The museum stands on land that has changed hands around it. St. Gregory's University closed, and the grounds became the Green Campus of Oklahoma Baptist University. Through all of this, the museum operated independently of both schools and kept its doors open. It remained a member agency of Oklahoma City's Allied Arts, and from 2009 until 2020 it hosted the annual Arts Trek festival. Its gift shop sells educational toys, books tied to its exhibitions, and arts and crafts by local artisans. In June 2024, more than six years after St. Gregory's University closed, it was announced that the land would return to the monks of St. Gregory's Abbey, the same community that produced Father Gerrer more than a century ago.

## Flight Context

The Mabee-Gerrer Museum of Art is in Shawnee, Oklahoma, at about 35.3668°N, 96.9537°W, on the grounds of St. Gregory's Abbey and the former St. Gregory's University. Shawnee lies about 28 nm east-southeast of downtown Oklahoma City. Shawnee Regional Airport (KSNL) is less than 1 nm southeast of the museum, so the abbey grounds are right beside the airport's traffic, and you should follow local pattern procedures. A pass at 2,500–3,000 ft AGL, clear of the pattern, shows the abbey grounds and the town together. Tinker Air Force Base (KTIK) is about 21 nm to the west, so check charts for military airspace if you approach from Oklahoma City. Visibility is usually good, but spring and summer thunderstorms can build quickly over central Oklahoma.
