# Philbrook Museum of Art

Oil pioneer Waite Phillips's glittering Italian Renaissance-style villa in Tulsa became the Philbrook Museum of Art, where painted-plaster illusions, a flapper mural and gardens modeled on a 16th-century Roman estate frame collections from around the world.

**Location:** 36.1239, -95.9700

**Categories:** Art museums and galleries in Oklahoma, Museums in Tulsa, Oklahoma, Historic house museums in Oklahoma, Villas in the United States, Gardens

## Introduction

The walls of Villa Philbrook glitter. Ground white marble was mixed into the stucco of this Italian Renaissance-style mansion in Tulsa, and its corners are quoined with Kasota limestone from Minnesota, chosen to resemble Italian travertine. Oklahoma oil pioneer Waite Phillips and his wife, Genevieve, built the three-story house in the 1920s, and in 1938 they gave it to the community of Tulsa. It opened on October 25, 1939, as the Philbrook Museum of Art. Today its nine collections span the globe, with a cornerstone collection of Native American art: basketry, pottery, paintings and jewelry.

## An Oilman's Italian Dream

Phillips commissioned the prominent Kansas City architect Edward Buehler Delk to design the mansion in 1926. The John Long Company of Kansas City began construction that same year and finished the following year. Beneath the Italianate finery, Villa Philbrook was built on a framework of steel and reinforced concrete, a choice that later meant surprisingly little remodeling was needed to turn a family home into a public museum. At the rear, a loggia of five arches carried on Corinthian columns opens onto a terrace overlooking the formal gardens, and the roof spreads wide eaves under oversized Italianate tiles. Inside were 72 rooms, finished with travertine and marble fireplaces and fountains, floors of teak, walnut and oak, and ornate ceilings in the manner of Italian villas. The same Kasota stonework decorates the doors and windows.

## Rooms That Play Tricks

Step through the front door and the house unfolds in a procession. A double staircase frames the entrance hall, which flows directly into the receiving hall. Between them runs a slightly raised cross-corridor with a groin-vaulted ceiling painted with Italian Renaissance designs, set off by twisted Corinthian columns and ironwork. Look up in the receiving hall and the beams and pierced screenwork appear to be carved wood, but they are actually painted plaster. Behind a large tapestry hides a two-manual Aeolian pipe organ. The wood-paneled library centers on a globe light fixture that reproduces a map from the time of Leonardo da Vinci, and the dining and living rooms carry heavily carved coffered ceilings. The airiest surprise is the music room, where a wall mural shows bobbed-hair flappers in Grecian garb acting out four musical tempos: scherzo, andante, rondo and allegro. These principal first-floor rooms were left virtually untouched when the house became a museum.

## A Garden Borrowed from Rome

The villa sits on 25 acres of formal and informal gardens, originally designed by the firm Hare & Hare and inspired by Villa Lante, an Italian country estate north of Rome designed by Giacomo Barozzi da Vignola in 1566. The formal gardens are part of the original design: rills and diagonal walks descend from the mansion to a rustic pool, graced by a classical tempietto. The southern gardens, reaching toward the summerhouse, came later and were completed in 2004. They take a different approach, featuring native Oklahoma plants and a refurbished creek. Here, too, sits the bronze Thinker on a Rock by the sculptor Barry Flanagan.

## From Pueblo Pottery to Renaissance Saints

Under its first director, Eugene Kingman, the museum opened with a permanent collection drawn from the Tulsa Art Association and Villa Philbrook itself. Studio art classes began in 1940, a touring program for schoolchildren followed the next year, and that program led to a Children's Museum in 1949. Gifts built the collections steadily: Native American pottery and basketry from Clark Field beginning in 1942, the Roberta Campbell Lawson collection in 1947, paintings from Laura Rutherford Clubb that same year, and Italian Renaissance paintings and sculptures from the Samuel H. Kress Foundation in 1961. The collection now ranges from Pueblo pottery and a Navajo saddle blanket to Auguste Rodin's Eternal Springtime and Thomas Moran's An Angry Sea. Between 2007 and 2015, director Randall Suffolk reorganized programming to be more family-friendly, and attendance rose 63 percent. From 2013 to 2020, a satellite, Philbrook Downtown, showed modern and contemporary works in the Brady Arts District before closing to make way for the Bob Dylan Center.

## Flight Context

Philbrook stands at approximately 36.12°N, 95.97°W in midtown Tulsa, Oklahoma, a few miles south-southeast of the downtown towers. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for the pale villa and its terraced formal gardens descending toward a pool, east of the Arkansas River. Tulsa International Airport (KTUL) is about 7 miles northeast and Richard Lloyd Jones Jr. Airport (KRVS) about 6 miles south. The museum lies within Tulsa's Class C airspace, so coordinate with Tulsa Approach, and expect spring thunderstorms in northeastern Oklahoma.
