# Bell's Amusement Park

What began as a miniature train looping around Robert Bell's Tulsa house grew into a 55-year family amusement park at the fairgrounds, crowned by the wooden coaster Zingo, until a lease went unrenewed and the rides went into storage.

**Location:** 36.1344, -95.9311

**Categories:** Defunct amusement parks in the United States, Amusement parks in Oklahoma, Tulsa State Fair, Roller coasters, Economy of Tulsa, Oklahoma

## Introduction

It started with a toy-sized railroad circling a family home. In 1948, Robert Bell built a miniature train around his house in Tulsa, and for a time he ran children's rides at the Admiral Twin drive-in. In March 1951 he took the idea to Expo Square, part of the Tulsa State Fairgrounds, with a small collection of amusements: a three-car train and a Shetland pony ride. For the next 55 years, Bell's Amusement Park grew up alongside the Tulsa kids who rode it, until the county declined to renew its lease in 2006 and the lights on the midway went out. The family has spent years trying to switch them back on.

## From Pony Rides to Bumper Cars

Growth came one ride at a time. The first attraction built for adults, a bumper car ride, arrived in 1955. Two years later came a Tilt-A-Whirl and a miniature golf course. Over the decades the little lot of kiddie rides became a family-owned park with several dozen rides and attractions, a patchwork of midway classics and odder creations. Visitors could brave Phantasmagoria, a dark ride, or spin on the Scrambler, the Himalaya and the Super Round-Up. Mind Melt, a Larson drop tower, lifted riders high before letting go, while Der Wellenflug, a wave swinger, whirled them outward on chains. There was Pharaoh's Fury, the Spider and a Ferris wheel, all packed into a corner of the fairgrounds that Tulsa families knew for decades.

## The Wooden Giant

The ride that defined Bell's was Zingo, a large wooden roller coaster designed by John C. Allen and built between 1966 and 1968. It gave a modest, family-run park a signature attraction, and it was the ride Bell's was best known for. Wooden coasters have a particular voice: the clatter of the chain lift, the roar of wheels on timber, the creak of a structure flexing under speed. Zingo carried that sound across Expo Square for nearly four decades, from the late 1960s until the park closed, a landmark as much as a ride.

## April 20, 1997

The park's darkest day came on its other coaster, the Wildcat. On April 20, 1997, mechanical failures caused a car near the top of a chain hill to disengage and roll backward into another car. A fourteen-year-old was killed and six other people were injured. Behind that plain sentence is a young life cut short and a family left to carry the loss. The Wildcat was taken apart after the accident and later relocated to Jolly Roger Amusement Park in Ocean City, Maryland, where it ran for a year under the name "Avalanche."

## The Lease That Ran Out

At the end of the 2006 season, the county did not renew the park's lease at Expo Square, and Bell's had to leave. The reason given was a nonviable business plan. The park disputed that picture, saying 2006 had been its most successful season in years and that expansion plans were underway. Bell's had paid the Expo $135,000 in 2006 and $12.5 million in total since 1951. That same year, the carnival company that ran the Tulsa State Fair's midway received a ten-year, non-competitive contract that included the right of first refusal to expand into the park's tract during the fair if Bell's was no longer a tenant. The next fair, in 2007, saw a 7 percent drop in attendance and a 29 percent drop in midway ticket sales. Some vendors blamed the lost park; the fairgrounds' chief executive said there was no theory yet to explain it. Attendance rose again in 2008, according to the Tulsa World.

## Rides in a Warehouse

The Bells announced plans to reopen elsewhere, but the rides went into a warehouse instead. In 2010, Wagoner County negotiated a deal to bring the park to Coweta, and on May 25 Robbie Bell signed a 50-year lease with an optional 25-year extension. The plan depended on voters approving a quarter-cent tax increase that July, but after two of the three county commissioners questioned whether it was financially viable, they pulled it from the ballot by a 2-1 vote. Early in 2012 the family set up a few rides at the Saturday Flea Market in West Tulsa, and by August 2013 Robby Bell III, Robert's grandson, was adding attractions and restoring more. In November 2021, a press conference announced land purchased for a new Bell's in Broken Arrow. On May 10, 2023, city officials said the park was no longer coming, and the landowner planned to sell.

## Flight Context

The former park site at Expo Square, the Tulsa State Fairgrounds, lies at approximately 36.13°N, 95.93°W in midtown Tulsa. From 2,000-3,000 ft AGL, look for the large fairgrounds complex with its expansive parking lots and exhibition halls; downtown Tulsa's towers stand nearly 4 miles to the west-northwest. The site is close to Tulsa International (KTUL), about 4 nm to the north-northeast, so expect to be inside Tulsa's terminal airspace and coordinate with approach. Tulsa Riverside (KRVS) is about 6 nm to the south-southwest.
