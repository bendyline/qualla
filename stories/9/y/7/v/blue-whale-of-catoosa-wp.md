# Blue Whale of Catoosa

Built as a surprise anniversary gift for a woman who collected whale figurines, the Blue Whale of Catoosa became a Route 66 swimming hole, fell to ruin and was rescued by its neighbors.

**Location:** 36.1937, -95.7331

**Categories:** Roadside attractions, Route 66, Folk art, Landmarks, Rogers County, Oklahoma

## Introduction

For their 34th wedding anniversary, Hugh Davis gave his wife a whale. Zelta Davis collected whale figurines, and on September 7, 1972, she was shown the biggest one she would ever own: a blue giant of iron and concrete sitting in a pond just east of Catoosa, Oklahoma. Hugh, a local zoologist and artist, had built it with his friend Harold Thomas as a surprise. It was meant for the family. It ended up belonging, in a sense, to everyone who ever drove old Route 66, where it has become one of the road's most recognizable attractions.

## A Swimming Hole With a Tail

The pond around the whale was spring fed and intended only for family use. Locals had other ideas. So many began showing up to enjoy the water that Davis brought in tons of sand, built picnic tables, hired lifeguards and opened the place to the public. The Blue Whale and its pond became a favorite swimming hole for both local families and travelers on Route 66, a place to cool off on the way somewhere else. Davis first called the property Nature's Acres, and he kept adding to it. Eventually the roadside attraction included The Fun and Swim Blue Whale and the A.R.K., short for Animal Reptile Kingdom, a fitting addition from a zoologist.

## Family on Both Sides of the Highway

The attraction was a family affair. Hugh's brother-in-law, Wolf Robe Hunt, an Acoma artist famous in his own right for his paintings and as a highly skilled silversmith, was part of it. Hunt once ran the Arrowood Trading Post across the highway from the whale, so travelers who pulled off Route 66 could find a swim on one side of the road and an accomplished artist's work on the other. For years the whale drew crowds. By 1988, though, the Davises could no longer manage the attraction, and they closed it to the public. Hugh died in January 1990. Zelta lived until 2001, long enough to see what came next.

## Neighbors to the Rescue

Left alone, the park fell into disrepair, crumbling from neglect and weather. Then the people of Catoosa, joined by employees of the Hampton Inn, launched a fundraising and volunteer effort to save the Route 66 landmark. In 1997 the whale was restored and repainted its original brilliant blue. A gift one man had made for one woman had been saved by the people who lived around it. The picnic area beside it was restored as well, though swimming in the whale's pond is no longer allowed. A sign reading "kissing whales" hangs above the whale's right-hand entrance, a small joke that suits a monument built as a love letter.

## A Whale on Screen

The restored whale became a star. On July 15, 2002, it appeared in the syndicated comic strip Zippy the Pinhead. It turned up on the British series An Idiot Abroad when the show traveled Route 66, and it appears almost immediately in the opening credits of Rob Zombie's film 31. Food Network's The Great Food Truck Race featured it on September 20, 2015, in an episode titled "Roadside Attractions," and American Pickers highlighted it on January 13, 2016. That November 11, it became the third location to sell Snapchat's new Spectacles. Mercedes-Benz used it in a 2018 television ad for the 2019 GLC, and in December 2021 a Phillips 66 commercial retold the story of how the whale came to be: a husband, a wife and a very large anniversary present.

## Flight Context

The Blue Whale sits at 36.1937°N, 95.7331°W beside its pond just east of Catoosa, Oklahoma, along historic Route 66. It is a small target: descend to 1,500–2,000 feet AGL (where permitted) and look for the bright blue whale shape in a pond by the old highway east of town. Tulsa International Airport (KTUL) lies only about 7 nm W, so this area sits under busy Tulsa approach traffic; coordinate with approach control. Other nearby fields are Claremore Regional (KGCM), about 14 nm ENE, and Tulsa Riverside (KRVS), about 15 nm SW. Spring and summer thunderstorms are common in northeast Oklahoma.
