# Rock Café

Built during the Dust Bowl from five dollars' worth of leftover Route 66 sandstone, Stroud's Rock Café has outlasted a bypass, a tornado and a fire, and its owner helped inspire a character in Pixar's Cars.

**Location:** 35.7490, -96.6544

**Categories:** Buildings and structures on U.S. Route 66, U.S. Route 66 in Oklahoma, Diners on the National Register of Historic Places, Restaurants in Oklahoma, Restaurants established in 1939

## Introduction

The stone cost five dollars. When Roy Rieves started building a café in Stroud, Oklahoma, in 1936, he bought local Kellyville sandstone left over from the construction of U.S. Route 66, which had reached Stroud a decade earlier. The land cost another hundred. Rieves did much of the work himself or hired students to help, and the stone walls he raised gave the place its name. In the decades since, the Rock Café has survived a bypass, a tornado and a fire.

## Built from the Road Itself

Rieves began the café in the depths of the Great Depression, during the Dust Bowl years, and he relied on whatever local materials he could find. That was how leftover road-building stone ended up forming the café's distinctive exterior. The café finally opened in August 1939 under a manager named Miss Thelma Holloway. During World War II it was a busy local Greyhound bus stop. Route 66 had literally supplied its walls, and the highway's travelers supplied much of its business. Depending on the highway became a problem when the cross-country truckers found another route. The construction of Interstate 44 in Oklahoma, a toll road known as the Turner Turnpike, pulled much of that business away, although the old highway remained as a free alternative. When U.S. Route 66 was decommissioned in 1985, the road through Stroud became Oklahoma State Highway 66. In 1993 Dawn Welch bought the café from Ed Smalley. She became a longtime promoter of Route 66 in Oklahoma, and the café kept alive the memory of an earlier figure, Mamie J. Mayfield, who lived from 1912 to 1994, with a souvenir shop called Mamie's General Store.

## Neon That Wouldn't Light

In 1999 an F3 tornado tore through Stroud. It destroyed the town's 53-store Tanger Outlet mall and wiped out a handful of key local employers, including a Sygma food service distribution center. The Rock Café survived, though its neon sign was damaged, and with the local economy shaken, business fell by half. In 2001 the café was listed on the National Register of Historic Places, which made it eligible for matching grants and loans. That same year the first Pixar crews researching Route 66 stopped by, while repairs were still unfinished, and the neon flickered briefly but never came to life. A federal grant and a $30,000 loan in matching funds eventually paid for new heating and air conditioning, new wiring, restored neon and a return to the building's original layout. Welch herself became the basis for the animated character Sally Carrera in Pixar's film Cars.

## Out of the Fire

In 2008 fire struck the café. Historic preservationist David Burke led extensive repairs, and the Rock Café reopened on May 29, 2009. The restaurant's cookbook, written during the rebuilding, came out that November. The Oklahoman named Welch Oklahoma's 2009 Woman of the Year. In January 2010 she cooked and promoted her book on NBC's Today show, and Oklahoma Living magazine named the Rock Café the state's best diner, with Welch on its cover. The café had already drawn attention before the fire. Traveling artist Bob Waldmire depicted it in 2007, and it appeared on Food Network's Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives. Stroud's own recovery was slower. The outlet mall was never rebuilt, but oil and gas work, oilfield equipment manufacturing and tourism have helped the town recover.

## Flight Context

The Rock Café sits on old Route 66, now State Highway 66, in Stroud, Oklahoma, at about 35.7490°N, 96.6544°W, roughly halfway between Oklahoma City and Tulsa. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL, follow Highway 66 through town and look for the Turner Turnpike (I-44) running nearby. Stroud Municipal Airport (KSUD) is about 2.5 nm north of the café. Stillwater Regional (KSWO) is about 33 nm northwest, and Tulsa Riverside Airport (KRVS) about 37 nm northeast. Visibility is generally good, but spring storms, including tornadoes like the one that struck Stroud in 1999, are a real hazard in this part of Oklahoma.
