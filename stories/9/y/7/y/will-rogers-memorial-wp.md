# Will Rogers Memorial

On the Claremore hilltop Will Rogers bought for his retirement, a 1938 limestone memorial now holds his papers, his films, a famous Jo Davidson statue, and the family tomb where he rests.

**Location:** 36.3208, -95.6317

**Categories:** Cultural depictions of Will Rogers, Biographical museums in Oklahoma, Museums in Rogers County, Oklahoma, Literary museums in the United States, Monuments and memorials in Oklahoma, Museums established in 1938, 1938 establishments in Oklahoma

## Introduction

In 1911 Will Rogers bought twenty acres on a hill above Claremore, reportedly paying $500 an acre, as the site for a home he would retire to someday. Retirement never came. On August 15, 1935, the humorist was killed alongside aviator Wiley Post when their plane crashed near Barrow, Alaska. Three years later, on what would have been his 59th birthday, a limestone memorial was completed on the hilltop he had chosen. Today Will Rogers rests there in a tomb that looks out over the town.

## A Hilltop Above the Tiawah Valley

Rogers was born on November 4, 1879, in Indian Territory near Oologah, the son of Clement Vann Rogers and Mary America Schrimsher Rogers, both of them part Cherokee. His father had built a house and a successful ranch on tribal land there. The Claremore property Will purchased decades later covered twenty acres overlooking the Tiawah Valley. After his death it passed to members of the Rogers family, and in 1937, during the Great Depression, his widow Betty donated it to the state of Oklahoma as the site of a memorial to her husband. She gave more than land. Betty also donated all of Will's papers and many of his personal effects, which became the core of the memorial's archives.

## Finished on His Birthday

The Oklahoma legislature appropriated roughly $200,000 to build a museum on the site, and architect John Duncan Forsyth designed a limestone building for it. At the groundbreaking on April 21, 1938, it was Will's sister, Sallie Rogers McSpadden, who turned the first spadeful of dirt. Construction wrapped up on November 4, 1938, the date that would have marked Rogers' 59th birthday, and the museum opened that year, three years after his death. In the early 1980s an addition nearly doubled the space, bringing a theater, a larger gift shop, offices and archives, and roomier galleries where the collections are grouped by the many arenas of Rogers' life and career. In the theater, visitors can watch documentaries, his speeches, and the movies he starred in.

## Never Met a Man

The rotunda belongs to a single figure: a famous sculpture of Will Rogers by artist Jo Davidson. Its pedestal carries his famous line, "...I never met a man I didn't like." This statue is another cast of the work Davidson created in 1938, which has represented Oklahoma in the National Statuary Hall at the United States Capitol. Beyond the galleries, a library of about 2,400 square feet serves scholars and writers, holding more than 2,000 volumes on Rogers and on subjects around him, including American Indian history, genealogy, vaudeville, early motion pictures, and the years from 1879 to 1935. The archives go deeper still, with an estimated 18,000 photographs, thousands of original manuscripts, private letters, contracts, and personal papers, plus motion pictures, home movies, and audio tapes.

## A Family on the Hill

Outside the building, the tomb of Will Rogers overlooks the city of Claremore, and he does not lie there alone. His wife Betty, who lived from 1879 to 1944, is interred beside him, as are three of their four children. Fred Stone Rogers lived only from 1918 to 1920. Mary Amelia Rogers Brooks lived until 1989, and James Blake Rogers, known as Jim, until 2000. Jim's wife, Marguerite Astrea Kemmler Rogers, rests there too, along with Jim's oldest son, James Kemmler Rogers, called Kem, who died in 2014. The hilltop that Will once imagined as a quiet place to grow old became instead a place where generations of his family came home.

## Protecting the Legacy

Until 2016 the museum was run by the Will Rogers Memorial Commission, a state agency that also governed the Dog Iron Ranch, Rogers' birthplace in Oologah. Its director, Tad Jones, noted that the memorial had been targeted by budget cutters for several years, and a bill introduced the year before would have eliminated state contributions to the museum. On March 7, 2016, Governor Mary Fallin signed Senate Bill 1570, placing both sites under the Oklahoma Historical Society, effective immediately. Jones believed the move would ease pressure on the museum's funding. Rogers' descendants approved enthusiastically, according to his great-granddaughter Jennifer Rogers-Etcheverry, and the law requires the governor to appoint a direct descendant of Rogers to the Historical Society's board.

## Flight Context

The Will Rogers Memorial sits at 36.3208°N, 95.6317°W on a hilltop overlooking Claremore and Rogers State University in northeastern Oklahoma. From about 2,000 feet AGL, look for the limestone building on its rise above town. Claremore Regional Airport (KGCM) is about 7.5 nm east-southeast, and Tulsa International Airport (KTUL) lies about 14 nm west-southwest, so expect Tulsa approach traffic on the western side. Rogers' birthplace at the Dog Iron Ranch near Oologah lies about 9 nm to the north and makes a natural companion stop. Clear days bring the best visibility across the valley.
