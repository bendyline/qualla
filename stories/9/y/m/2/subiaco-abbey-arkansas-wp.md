# Subiaco Abbey (Arkansas)

Born of an 1877 railroad land deal to settle German Catholics in western Arkansas, Subiaco Abbey grew from three monk-missionaries into a sandstone monastery and school whose monks now also raise Angus cattle, brew beer and bottle hot sauce.

**Location:** 35.3011, -93.6333

**Categories:** Benedictine monasteries in the United States, Religious buildings and structures in Arkansas, German-American culture in Arkansas, Buildings and structures in Logan County, Arkansas, 1878 establishments in Arkansas

## Introduction

The monastery began as a sales pitch. In 1877 the Little Rock and Fort Smith Railroad owned thousands of open acres in Arkansas and wanted settlers on them, and it had decided to offer the land only to German Catholics. To serve those immigrants it wanted a monastery and a school, so the railroad went looking for monks. The result, still standing in the Arkansas River valley of Logan County, is Subiaco Abbey, a Benedictine community named for the Italian town where Saint Benedict founded his first monastery.

## Three Men and 640 Acres

The railroad approached Martin Marty, Abbot of St. Meinrad Abbey in Indiana, offering land in western Arkansas south of the Arkansas River if his monks would build a monastery and school to serve the German immigrants it was bringing in. Marty negotiated a grant of 640 acres for a monastery of monks and 100 more for a future community of Benedictine nuns. Edward Fitzgerald, Bishop of Little Rock, backed the plan because he needed German-speaking priests for the newcomers in his diocese. On March 15, 1878, three monk-missionaries from St. Meinrad arrived in Logan County: Father Wolfgang Schlumpf, the prior, with Brother Kaspar Hildesheim and Brother Hilarin Benetz. They called the foundation St. Benedict's Priory, and it struggled with money and manpower. In 1886 it became a conventual priory, independent of St. Meinrad.

## The Eight Beatitudes

Help came from Switzerland. St. Meinrad had asked its own mother house, Einsiedeln Abbey in Switzerland, to support the Arkansas foundation, and in the fall of 1887 Einsiedeln sent Father Gall D'Aujourd'hui with eight candidates for the monastery. In the community's tradition, those eight are remembered as the Eight Beatitudes. In 1891 Pope Leo XIII raised the priory to an abbey and gave it the name Subiaco. The monks elected their first abbot in March 1892: Ignatius Conrad, a monk of Einsiedeln who had been serving in the Diocese of Kansas City-Saint Joseph in western Missouri. He would lead the community into the mid-1920s, and under him the monks began building in stone.

## Sandstone and Fire

Construction of a new abbey from local sandstone began in 1898. In December 1901, just as the work neared completion, the original wooden abbey, about a mile away, burned to the ground, and the monks moved to the new site. Fire struck again in 1927, gutting the building, and the community rebuilt. The third abbot, Paul Nahlen, secured Pope Pius XII's blessing for a new abbey church, completed in 1959; that moment is depicted in one of the 182 stained-glass windows of St. Benedict Abbey Church. The monks also reached abroad. A foundation in Nigeria opened in 1964 but had to close in 1968 because of the Biafran War, and Santa Familia Priory in Belize, founded in 1971, operated until 2002. Like other Catholic institutions in Arkansas, the abbey has also had to reckon with abuse. In January 2020, acting independently of the Diocese of Little Rock, it released its own report naming three deceased monks with established allegations of abuse.

## Angus, Ale and Hot Sauce

Today about thirty Benedictine monks live at Subiaco, part of the Swiss-American Congregation, and the abbey and its preparatory school, Subiaco Academy, are major features of the town that shares its name. The Academy enrolls American students alongside international students from China, Hong Kong, Mexico, Curaçao and South Korea, and more than half of its students board. The community keeps busy in worldly ways, too. The monks breed black Angus cattle and make their own hot sauce, and they have brewed beer for their own table since the 1920s; in 2018 they began selling it to the public as Country Monks Brewing, with a taproom, though production was temporarily halted in late 2023. These ventures have drawn segments from CNN's Great Big Story, the American Angus Association and Little Rock's KTHV. Though it sits within the Diocese of Little Rock, the abbey answers to its own authority as a Benedictine institution, a community still living by the rule its founders carried here from Indiana and Switzerland.

## Flight Context

Subiaco Abbey sits at 35.301°N, 93.633°W in the Arkansas River valley of Logan County, just east of the town of Paris. Look for the abbey's large stone buildings just east of Paris. The best landmark is Mount Magazine, Arkansas's highest point at 2,753 ft, whose flat-topped mesa rises about 9 miles to the south; keep terrain clearance in mind if routing near it. A pass at 1,500-2,500 ft AGL gives a good view of the campus. Nearby airports: Paris Municipal (FAA identifier 7M6; no ICAO code), about 2 nm west; Clarksville Municipal (FAA identifier H35, GPS code KH35), about 14 nm northeast; Fort Smith Regional (KFSM), about 36 nm west. Low cloud can obscure Mount Magazine's summit.
