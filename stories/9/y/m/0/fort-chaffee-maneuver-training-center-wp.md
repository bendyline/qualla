# Fort Chaffee Maneuver Training Center

Born the day Pearl Harbor was bombed, this western Arkansas post has been a tank school, a POW camp, a doorway for refugees, a film set and a mock city built for war games.

**Location:** 35.2700, -94.2020

**Categories:** Forts in Arkansas, Military installations in Arkansas, Arkansas National Guard, Cuban refugees, Historic districts on the National Register of Historic Places in Arkansas, Cold War military installations of the United States

## Introduction

The first soldiers reached Camp Chaffee on December 7, 1941, the same day Japanese planes struck Pearl Harbor. They arrived at a post still being carved out of the farmland beside Fort Smith, where ground had been broken less than three months earlier. In the decades since, this stretch of country along the Arkansas River has been a tank school, a prison camp, a first stop in America for people fleeing war, a shelter from a hurricane, a Hollywood backlot and a practice city built to be fought over. That is a remarkable number of lives for one patch of ground in a single lifetime.

## A General Who Bet on Tanks

The camp took its name from Major General Adna R. Chaffee Jr., a cavalry officer who concluded in Europe during World War I that horse cavalry was outmoded. Unlike many of his fellow cavalrymen, he argued for tanks, so it was fitting that his namesake became an armored training ground. Groundbreaking came on September 20, 1941, part of the War Department's push to double the size of the U.S. Army. That month the government acquired 15,163 acres from 712 property owners, a roll that included families, farms, businesses, churches and schools. Building the entire base took only sixteen months. Activated on March 27, 1942, the post trained the 6th, 14th and 16th Armored Divisions through 1946, and nearby Barling boomed with new housing and businesses. The war brought other residents too: about 3,000 German prisoners of war were held here.

## A Haircut and a Doorway

Camp Chaffee became Fort Chaffee in 1956, and two years later it hosted its most famous recruit. On March 25, 1958, a newly drafted Elvis Presley sat down in the post barbershop for his regulation Army haircut, a moment the barbershop, now a museum, still commemorates. The fort's larger legacy, though, belongs to people who arrived with far less fanfare. In 1975 and 1976 it served as a processing center for refugees from Southeast Asia, screening 50,809 people displaced by the Vietnam War and matching them with American sponsors. In May 1980, after Cuba allowed boats to carry emigrants from the port of Mariel, Fort Chaffee became a resettlement center for Cuban refugees. Frustration over their confinement erupted on June 1, when detainees rioted, burned buildings and clashed with state police and National Guardsmen. In September 2005 the empty barracks opened again, sheltering more than 10,000 evacuees from Hurricane Katrina. Many of them stayed and made Fort Smith their home.

## Standing Down, Opening Up

The Army's Joint Readiness Training Center began training soldiers here in 1987, but it moved to Fort Polk, Louisiana, in 1993. Two years later the Base Realignment and Closure Commission recommended closing Fort Chaffee, on the condition that essential ranges and training areas be kept for reserve forces. In late 1995 the federal government declared 7,192 of the fort's 76,075 acres surplus and turned them over to the state, while the remaining 66,000 acres went to the Arkansas National Guard. When the Army garrison was deactivated on September 27, 1997, command passed to the Arkansas Army National Guard, and the post now operates as the Fort Chaffee Joint Maneuver Training Center. For a few years the place was an open post. Gatehouses were abandoned, most of the fencing came down and military police patrols ended, until the September 11, 2001, attacks closed every Army installation in the country. Meanwhile a redevelopment authority demolished more than 700 buildings on the surplus land, which is now Chaffee Crossing, with homes, shops, industry, a nature center and a college of osteopathic medicine.

## Fire, Film and a Mock City

Empty buildings burn. On the morning of January 29, 2008, high winds drove a brush fire from an electrical source across about 100 acres, damaging or destroying 150 abandoned buildings and exposing asbestos, the only environmental contamination found at the site so far. On August 3, 2011, the hottest day in Fort Chaffee's history at 115 degrees Fahrenheit according to the National Weather Service, fire swept the 90-acre former medical complex, destroying the hospital and nearly 120 buildings. The cause turned out to be a cigarette dropped by a Kentucky National Guardsman. Hollywood found the old barracks useful, filming A Soldier's Story, Biloxi Blues and The Tuskegee Airmen here. In 2011, 44 World War II barracks built for two tank destroyer battalions joined the National Register of Historic Places. Training never stopped, either. A $26 million complex opened in 2014 with 18 buildings imitating a small city, watched by 189 cameras, and federal agents learn to guard nuclear shipments here in an 18-week courier academy.

## Flight Context

Fort Chaffee lies at about 35.27°N, 94.20°W, immediately southeast of Fort Smith, Arkansas. Fort Smith Regional Airport (KFSM) sits about 9 nm west-northwest of that point, close to the post's northwest edge, so arrivals and departures there pass close by. The Arkansas River traces the northern boundary and Interstate 40 runs about 5 miles north across the river, both useful for orientation. From 3,000 to 5,000 feet AGL the grid of wartime barracks and the redeveloped Chaffee Crossing area stand out against the wooded training lands stretching south and east. Much of the remaining acreage is an active National Guard training area with its own ranges, so check current airspace restrictions before overflying. Best visibility comes on clear fall and winter days; summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common.
