# World's Largest Tuned Musical Windchime

South of Eureka Springs, Arkansas, a 35-foot aluminum windchime raised in 2004 by artist Ranaga Farbiarz in honor of his father, a Holocaust survivor, earned a 2006 Guinness World Records certificate for the largest set of windchimes, a title Guinness now credits to a larger chime in Casey, Illinois.

## Introduction

When the wind finds the longest tube, twenty feet of thick-walled aluminum begins to hum. Its deepest note has been described as sounding like "a combination Cathedral Bell, Tibetan Singing Bowl and Jet engine," which is not a sound most people expect to hear in a parking lot in the Arkansas Ozarks. But this is no ordinary porch ornament. South of Eureka Springs, windchime artist Ranaga Farbiarz built a chime so big that Guinness World Records took notice, and he dedicated every note of it to his late father.

## Raising It Up

The chime went up on Thursday, November 4, 2004, in the parking area of Celestial Windz Harmonic Bizaar, south of Eureka Springs. Farbiarz had prepared its components ahead of time, and the assembly on site took a crowd effort: a bucket truck, a 100-foot-tall oak tree, his son David, and numerous friends. About 100 onlookers gathered to watch the giant take shape. For all its playful spirit, the chime carries a serious dedication. Farbiarz raised it in memory of his father, Ignatz Farbiarz, a Holocaust survivor, so that every gust that sets it ringing sounds a tribute to a man who lived through one of history's darkest chapters.

## Six Notes, One Octave

Six metal pipes make the music, tuned to a pentatonic scale like the black keys on a piano. The longest and shortest tubes sound notes an octave apart. At twenty feet, the deepest-toned tube produces that bell-bowl-and-jet rumble, while the shortest, at fourteen feet, gives off a rich, cathedral-like tone. Each is cut from four-inch-diameter, thick-walled aluminum tubing. Measured from top to bottom, the whole instrument stretches 35 feet 10 inches, easily beating the previous record of 16 feet 8 inches, more than doubling it.

## 653 Pounds of Music

Everything about the chime is outsized, down to its carefully tallied parts list. The finished instrument is listed at 653 pounds. The musical tubing alone accounts for 310 of them. Above the pipes sits a pyramid-shaped wooden top measuring six by eight feet and weighing 225 pounds, and at the center hangs a circular wooden knocker three and a half feet across and weighing 64 pounds. Catching the wind is a four-foot-long, two-foot-wide Celestial Wind Dervish, which Farbiarz makes from recycled local and imported woods, and it adds just 18 pounds. Down rods and miscellaneous hardware make up the last 36. Farbiarz also gave the chime a wry nickname, the "Wind Chime of Mass Distraction," or WMD, because it is made from the same size and grade of aluminum that weapons inspectors had been seeking in Iraq.

## A Letter from Guinness

Recognition arrived by mail. In a formal letter dated February 9, 2006, the Guinness World Records Management Team congratulated Farbiarz on achieving the world record for the largest set of windchimes, and enclosed a certificate to commemorate it. The news traveled well beyond the Ozarks: National Public Radio reported that the chime had put the Arkansas town in the record books. The title did not last: in 2012 Guinness World Records recognized a 42-foot chime in Casey, Illinois, as the largest windchime, and visitor reports from 2013 and 2016 describe the Eureka Springs chime as no longer hanging outdoors, leaving its status since then unclear. For a craftsman who builds instruments to be played by the weather, it was a fitting kind of fame, earned not by volume alone but by patience, careful tuning, and a son's wish to honor his father.
