# Hemmed-In-Hollow Falls

Tucked into a box canyon in the Ponca Wilderness of the Buffalo National River, Hemmed-In-Hollow Falls drops 209 feet in a single plunge, a waterfall the National Park Service calls the highest between the Rockies and the Appalachians.

**Location:** 36.0721, -93.3074

**Categories:** Landforms of Newton County, Arkansas, Tourist attractions in Newton County, Arkansas, Waterfalls of Arkansas, Plunge waterfalls, Buffalo National River

## Introduction

Stand at the base and look up, and the waterfall comes apart before your eyes. Somewhere on the long way down its 209-foot drop, the stream breaks into thousands of individual droplets, and the wind swirling inside the canyon pushes them one way and then another so the whole column seems to dance. This is Hemmed-In-Hollow Falls, a single-drop waterfall in the Ponca Wilderness Area of the Buffalo National River in northern Arkansas. According to the National Park Service, it is the "highest waterfall between the Rockies and the Appalachians."

## Walled In on Three Sides

The name tells you what the place feels like. The falls pour into Hemmed-In Hollow, a small valley closed in on three sides by bluffs about 200 feet high, so that anyone standing inside it is surrounded by stone on every side but one. The effect is of a great natural amphitheater, with the waterfall as its only performer. The hollow opens toward the Buffalo River, which in 1972 became the first national river in the United States when it was placed under the protection of the National Park Service. The river still flows freely for 135 miles, one of the few remaining undammed rivers in the lower 48 states. The Ponca Wilderness around it is rugged, protected country.

## The Hard Way In, and the Harder Way Out

At least three trails lead to the falls. For hikers who want to park and walk in, the Centerpoint and Compton trailheads along State Highway 43 north of Ponca are probably the best choices, and Compton offers the most direct route. From there the falls are 2.5 miles away, on a trail that is usually well marked and well maintained. The catch is the elevation. The trail descends nearly 1,400 feet from the Compton Trailhead to the bluffs along the river, and every foot of that has to be regained on the way back. The hike out of Hemmed-In Hollow is notoriously steep and difficult, and the National Park Service counts it among the most frequent search-and-rescue locations on the Buffalo National River. Carry water, and save your strength for the climb.

## From the River

There is a gentler approach for those who come by water. Floaters on the Buffalo River can reach the falls by an easy half-mile walk from the river trailhead, with no punishing climb waiting at the end of the visit. From Kyle's Landing, it is a three-mile hike. The river approach gives a different sense of the place. Instead of dropping down from the ridge top into the hollow, visitors start near the bottom and walk in, the bluffs closing around them on three sides until the waterfall comes into view, hanging over the far end of the canyon like a silver thread. For anyone who wants the view without the long climb, the Buffalo itself is the easy road in.

## Wait for the Rain

Timing matters here. For most of the year the flow over Hemmed-In-Hollow Falls is subdued, and during dry spells it can dwindle to almost nothing. That is when the falls are at their most delicate: a thin stream that breaks into droplets long before it reaches the bottom of its 209-foot drop, swaying in the wind that swirls inside the canyon. Then come the heavy rains, typically in late winter or early spring, and the falls transform. Water spills heavily off the cliff, and the hollow echoes with it. Both versions have their admirers. The low-flow falls has an almost weightless beauty. The high-water falls is raw power. Either way, the walk out will be just as steep.

## Flight Context

Hemmed-In-Hollow Falls lies in the Ponca Wilderness Area of the Buffalo National River at 36.0721°N, 93.3074°W, southwest of Harrison, Arkansas. The falls themselves are hidden inside a narrow hollow, so use the winding course of the Buffalo River and the tall bluffs lining it as your references. Pilots are requested to stay at least 2,000 feet above the surface over National Park Service lands, and the wilderness terrain is steep and remote. Boone County Airport (KHRO) in Harrison is about 14 nm north-northeast. Low cloud and fog can settle in the river valley, especially after the heavy rains that bring the falls to full flow.
