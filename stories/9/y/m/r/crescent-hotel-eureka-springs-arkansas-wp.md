# Crescent Hotel (Eureka Springs, Arkansas)

Built of hand-carved limestone in 1886 as a resort for the wealthy, Eureka Springs' Crescent Hotel has been a girls' college, a fraudulent cancer hospital and a restored grand hotel, and it now bills itself as America's most haunted.

**Location:** 36.4084, -93.7373

## Introduction

In 1937 a man with no medical training bought a grand stone hotel on a hilltop in Eureka Springs, Arkansas, and turned it into a cancer hospital. Norman G. Baker called himself a doctor, and the sick people who came to him in hope of a cure were given worthless injections and the local spring water to drink. That chapter lasted only a few years, but it has shadowed the Crescent Hotel ever since. Today the hotel at 75 Prospect Avenue bills itself as "America's most haunted hotel" and sells tickets to a ghost tour, and the stories it tells begin with the people who passed through its many lives.

## A Castle of Limestone

The Crescent was built in 1886 by the Eureka Improvement Company, whose president was the former governor Powell Clayton, to a design by the architect Isaac S. Taylor. It was meant as a resort for the rich and famous, and it was built to impress. Its walls were raised from massive limestone blocks, some eighteen inches thick, hand-carved from a quarry on the White River. The hotel opened its doors on May 20, 1886, perched high above the winding streets of a town that had grown up around its springs. The glamour did not last. The Crescent quickly proved unmanageable as a business, and before long the great stone building that had opened with such ambition was falling into disrepair.

## Young Women and Summer Guests

The building found a second life in education. In 1908 it reopened as the Crescent College and Conservatory for Young Women, filling the old resort's halls with students instead of vacationers. The school closed in 1924, then reopened in 1930 as a junior college, which lasted until 1934. After the college shut its doors for the final time, the Crescent was leased out as a summer hotel. For a building designed to draw wealthy guests, it was a humbler existence, a place that opened with the warm weather and waited through the rest of the year for someone to find a lasting use for it. That someone, when he arrived, turned out to be a far more troubling figure than any summer guest.

## The False Cure

Norman Baker was a millionaire, an inventor and a radio personality, and he styled himself a doctor despite never having had any medical training. He claimed to have discovered cures for a number of ailments, including cancer, and he attacked organized medicine as corrupt and driven by profit. Iowa had already run him out for practicing medicine without a license. Baker simply moved his cancer patients to Arkansas and advertised his new health resort at the Crescent. His "cure" consisted of worthless injections and drinking the area's natural spring water. The people who came to him were seriously ill, and they spent money and precious time on a treatment that could not help them. In 1939 he was arrested on federal mail fraud charges; convicted in January 1940, he was sentenced to four years in prison.

## Restoration and Ghosts

In 1997, Marty and Elise Roenigk bought the hotel and set out to bring it back. They oversaw a six-year restoration and renovation of the guest rooms and returned the roofline to its original appearance. Marty Roenigk was killed in a car crash in Iowa, and Elise Roenigk carried on as owner until her death in July 2026, after which the hotel is to pass to a trust she established. In 2016 the building was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. By then the Crescent had embraced the darker side of its past. In 2005 it appeared on the television show Ghost Hunters, whose cast claimed to have seen what they called "a full-body apparition" on their thermal imaging camera. Visitors who take the ghost tour today walk the same limestone halls that knew wealthy guests, young students and desperate patients, and it is the memory of those real people that gives the stories their weight.

## Flight Context

The Crescent Hotel crowns a hilltop in Eureka Springs, Arkansas, at 36.4084°N, 93.7373°W, and its large limestone bulk is easy to pick out above the town's winding streets from about 1,500-2,500 feet AGL. Look east across town for the Christ of the Ozarks statue on its own hilltop. The terrain is steep and wooded, so keep a safe margin above the ridges. Northwest Arkansas National Airport (KXNA) is about 28 nm west-southwest, and Branson Airport (KBBG) in Missouri is about 27 nm east-northeast. Valley fog is common on autumn mornings in the Ozarks.
