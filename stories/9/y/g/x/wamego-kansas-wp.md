# Wamego, Kansas

A Kansas River farm town named for a Potawatomi chief, Wamego keeps a stone windmill that was carried into town on 35 horse-drawn wagons and a museum holding thousands of artifacts from the Land of Oz.

**Location:** 39.2042, -96.3083

**Categories:** kansas, small-towns, wizard-of-oz, museums, windmills

## Introduction

Wamego keeps two unlikely treasures. One is a stone windmill that was taken apart, hauled twelve miles and rebuilt. The other is a museum holding thousands of artifacts from a land that exists only in a story. Around them lies a small Kansas town, home to 4,841 people at the 2020 census, platted in 1866 and named for a Potawatomi chief, with the Kansas River running along its southern edge and farmland spreading across the broad valley.

## A Mill That Moved

In 1879, an immigrant named John Schonhoff built a sturdy windmill on his farm twelve miles north of Wamego, at a time when Kansas farmers were still grinding wheat and corn by hand. Its 40-foot stone tower was built of red sandstone quarried on the farm, with white limestone trim. Decades later, the town set out to preserve it. In the mid-1920s workers took it down stone by stone, numbering each piece, and 35 horse-drawn wagons hauled it into Wamego. Rebuilt on a 25-foot mound in City Park, the Dutch Mill was restored in 1988 by a milling expert hired by the city, and it grinds grain again.

## The Park at the Center

The mill anchors a 12-acre park in the middle of town, and the park holds much of Wamego's memory. The Wamego Historical Museum stands there, along with Prairie Town Village, a collection of buildings from the 1800s that were part of the town's early life. Around them are the ordinary pleasures of a Kansas summer: a swimming pool, tennis courts, a playground and a fishing pond crossed by a footbridge. Every year Wamego marks Independence Day with a festival that includes a carnival, a parade and fireworks.

## Somewhere in Kansas, Oz

In April 2004, Wamego opened a museum devoted to The Wizard of Oz, built around a large collection of Oz artifacts, reportedly more than 25,000, on permanent loan from Friar Johnpaul Cafiero. The museum turned out to be a seed. Other small businesses sprouted around it with the same theme, and the cluster came to be known as the "Oz Cluster": the Oz Winery and Toto's Tacoz among them. There is a yellow brick road path, Toto sculptures and Oz murals dot the town, and each October Wamego throws OZtoberFEST, an Oktoberfest-style celebration with an Oz theme.

## When River and Ground Moved

The Kansas River gives Wamego its fertile valley, but it has not always stayed politely along the town's edge. On July 13, 1951, a date some in Kansas call Black Friday, the river crested at every official gaging station from Manhattan to Bonner Springs, 4 to 6 feet above any previously recorded crest. At Wamego the gauge read 30.56 feet, more than 11 feet above flood stage. Even the ground here has a story. On April 24, 1867, barely six months after Wamego's first post office opened, the strongest earthquake known to originate in Kansas struck near Manhattan. On a farm three miles south of Wamego the ground liquefied, and waves roughly two feet high rolled along the river.

## Alfalfa, Cattle and Turbines

Behind the whimsy, Wamego is a working town. Agriculture is the area's primary industry. The broad river valley grows alfalfa, sweet corn, field corn and wheat, while rocky pastures in the surrounding hills support herds of cattle, and an alfalfa processing plant operates in town. The other major employer is a factory building turbines for Solar Turbines, a company wholly owned by Caterpillar. Many residents commute to Manhattan, about fourteen miles west, or to Topeka. The town's reach shows up in its list of natives, too: Walter Chrysler, founder of the Chrysler Corporation, was born in Wamego, though his parents moved to Ellis, Kansas, soon after his birth.

## Flight Context

Wamego lies at approximately 39.20°N, 96.31°W at the intersection of U.S. Route 24 and K-99, with the Kansas River along the town's southern edge. From 3,000-4,500 feet AGL, follow the broad, farmed Kansas River valley; Wamego lies about 14 miles east of Manhattan, and the City Park with its small lake sits near the center of town. Manhattan Regional Airport (KMHK) is about 18 nm to the west-southwest. Topeka's Philip Billard Municipal Airport (KTOP) is roughly 33 nm to the east-southeast. Marshall Army Airfield (KFRI) at Fort Riley lies farther west; check NOTAMs for military activity. Visibility is usually good over the valley, but summer thunderstorms can develop quickly.
