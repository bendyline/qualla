# Council Grove, Kansas

A Flint Hills town named for an 1825 treaty council beneath an oak, Council Grove was where Santa Fe Trail wagons banded together, where the Kaw people lived until their forced removal, and where the 1857 Hays House is said to be the oldest continuously operating restaurant west of the Mississippi.

**Location:** 38.6606, -96.4897

**Categories:** kansas, santa-fe-trail, native-american-history, kaw-nation, flint-hills, small-towns

## Introduction

Wagon trains did not cross the plains alone if they could help it. At a grove of trees on the Santa Fe Trail, pioneers gathered so their wagons could band together for the long trip west, and the spot took its name from a meeting held there. On August 10, 1825, United States commissioners met Osage leaders beneath an oak and concluded a treaty granting a right-of-way for the trail. One of those commissioners, George Sibley, wrote in his journal that he suggested the name himself: Council Grove. Today it is the county seat of Morris County, Kansas, a town of 2,140 people whose Main Street still follows the old trail.

## Stone Along the Trail

The trail left more than a name. Walk Main Street and you are walking the route itself, the same path that carried freight and families toward Santa Fe. At 803 West Main stands the Rawlinson-Terwilliger Home, the oldest stone home on the Santa Fe Trail, which now houses the Trail Days Cafe & Museum. A post office opened on February 26, 1855, and the territorial legislature incorporated the town in 1858. The streets kept adding landmarks: the 1857 Last Chance Store, the Farmers and Drovers Bank, and a Carnegie Library that now serves as home to the Morris County Historical Society.

## The Kaw Reservation

Council Grove's story is also the story of the Kaw, or Kanza, people. In 1846 a treaty with the United States left the Kanza a diminished reservation centered here. The town's first European-American settler, Seth M. Hays, a great-grandson of Daniel Boone, arrived in 1847 to trade with them. In 1851 a native stone mission was completed, designed to board 50 Kanza boys as students; it survives today as the Kaw Mission State Historic Site. The reservation did not last. In 1873, despite an impassioned plea by Chief Allegawaho, the U.S. government removed 533 Kanza to Indian Territory, in present-day Oklahoma. Allegawaho Memorial Heritage Park preserves the site of the last Kaw village in Kansas before that forced removal, and each June, Washunga Days brings intertribal powwow dances to town. Charles Curtis, a member of the Kaw tribe who served as Vice President under Herbert Hoover from 1929 to 1933, is counted among Council Grove's notable people.

## Supper at the Hays House

Seth Hays did not only trade. In 1857 he opened a restaurant, the Hays House, which is said to be the oldest continuously operating restaurant west of the Mississippi River. It sits among a cluster of sites that make Council Grove feel like an open-air museum of the trail years: the Kaw Mission, the Last Chance Store, the Trail Days Historic Site and the Council Oak, where the 1825 treaty was signed. Each one marks a different chapter, from treaty council to frontier store to mission school, and together they tell the town's story in wood and stone.

## Rails, Roads and a Pioneer Mother

The Missouri-Kansas-Texas Railroad reached Council Grove from Junction City and built a depot on the 500 block of East Main Street. That building burned on May 18, 1894. Work on a replacement began immediately, and the new depot opened on December 31 of the same year. It no longer serves trains, but it still stands, listed on the National Register of Historic Places. In 1912 the town found itself on the National Old Trails Road, also called the Ocean-to-Ocean Highway. On September 7, 1928, the Daughters of the American Revolution dedicated a Madonna of the Trail statue here, one of 12 identical monuments by sculptor August Leimbach honoring pioneer women, placed in the former Santa Fe camp ground now known as Madonna Park.

## Water in the Flint Hills

Modern Council Grove sits about fifty-five miles southwest of Topeka, where the Flint Hills National Scenic Byway begins at the Kaw Mission and runs about 47 miles south on K-177 to Cassoday. Two lakes draw people outdoors. The larger Council Grove Reservoir restricts building along its shore but offers well-placed campsites, while most of the land around the City Lake is leased to the owners of more than three hundred lake houses. Both are open to motorized boats, and both hold walleye, crappie and white bass. The town celebrates its layered past at the Heritage Rendezvous and at Washunga Days, keeping both the trail and the Kaw in view.

## Flight Context

Council Grove lies at approximately 38.66°N, 96.49°W in the Flint Hills of east-central Kansas, about 55 miles southwest of Topeka. From 3,000-5,000 feet AGL, look for the town straddling the Neosho River valley with Council Grove Reservoir just to the northwest and the smaller City Lake nearby; K-177, the Flint Hills National Scenic Byway, runs south from town toward Cassoday. Council Grove Municipal Airport (FAA identifier K63) serves the town directly. Emporia Municipal Airport (KEMP) is about 24 nm to the southeast, and Manhattan Regional Airport (KMHK) is about 30 nm to the north. Visibility over the open tallgrass is usually excellent, though spring and summer thunderstorms can develop quickly.
