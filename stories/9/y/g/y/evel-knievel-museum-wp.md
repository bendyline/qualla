# Evel Knievel Museum

A stalled restoration of Evel Knievel's Mack truck brought the daredevil's legacy to a Topeka Harley-Davidson dealership, where a museum of more than 1,000 artifacts ran until it closed in 2024 and moved to Las Vegas.

**Location:** 39.0302, -95.6836

**Categories:** Motorcycle museums in the United States, Museums in Topeka, Kansas, History museums in Kansas, Stunt performers, Evel Knievel

## Introduction

The museum started with a stalled restoration. Evel Knievel's Mack truck, nicknamed "Big Red," needed a new home, and in 2013 Lathan McKay and Marilyn Stemp, frustrated with the lack of progress, set out to find one. The answer came from Topeka, Kansas, where a Harley-Davidson dealer's team had just rebuilt Jerry Lee Lewis's 1959 Panhead from the ground up. That connection, through a rock and roll legend and a big red truck, laid the foundation for the Evel Knievel Museum.

## Big Red Finds a Garage

A business partner made the phone call to Mike Patterson, the Topeka dealer. His team had never restored a Mack truck, but it had access to the expertise, and nearly 100 people signed on to the project. Over the 18 months of that restoration, Patterson, McKay and James Caplinger decided to build a museum. The idea was older than the truck work. Knievel himself had spoken during his lifetime of wanting a museum to honor his legacy, and in February 2012 an effort called Evel Archaeology had begun re-collecting his motorcycles, vehicles, wardrobe, artifacts and personal effects. McKay, a historian, producer and former skateboarder among other pursuits, amassed what the museum billed as the largest collection of Evel Knievel memorabilia in the world. Patterson built an addition onto his Harley-Davidson dealership to hold it.

## A Family Dealership on Topeka Boulevard

The dealership itself had deep Topeka roots. Patterson's grandfather, Henry, bought Topeka Harley-Davidson in March 1949, and the business stayed in the family through the decades until Mike, a former professional flat track racer, entered it in the late 1980s. The museum and the historic dealership shared a 13,000-square-foot, two-story building beside the Kansas Expocentre (since 2019 the Stormont Vail Events Center), at the corner of SW Topeka Boulevard and SW 21st Street. The museum was a non-profit, officially authorized by the Knievel estate. Its exhibits were arranged along a timeline, following Knievel from his early years in Butte, Montana, to the height of his fame in the 1970s, with more than 1,000 artifacts on display, from personal memorabilia and motorcycles to ephemera and interactive exhibits.

## Leathers, Skycycles and a Cane with a Flask

Robert Craig Knievel, born on October 17, 1938, and known to the world as Evel Knievel, attempted more than 75 ramp-to-ramp motorcycle jumps over his career, and he was especially popular with kids, helped along by a line of Evel Knievel toys. The museum gathered the physical record of that life. Big Red, the 1974 Mack truck and trailer, was its star. Around it stood Knievel's original jump bikes, his star-spangled helmets and performance leathers, and his first 1970 Harley-Davidson XR-750 iron head jump bike. There was the Skycycle X-2 from the Snake River Canyon jump, the helmets from his crashes at Caesars Palace and Wembley Stadium, memorabilia used on screen in Viva Knievel, and his famed flask-filled walking cane. A custom red Cadillac pickup, a 1977 J. C. Agajanian special Evel Knievel Indianapolis race car and an Ideal Toy Company exhibit rounded out the collection.

## Jump Without the Risk

Knievel died on November 30, 2007, but the museum found ways to let visitors share a little of his experience without breaking anything. Its "4D Virtual Reality Jump" exhibit, featuring Doug Danger, let them experience Knievel's stunt thrills first-hand but without the risks. There was an interactive broken-bones x-ray exhibit, an interactive jump planner, and an Evel Knievel movie theater, along with Knievel's crash car and his Harley-Davidson Sportster trike. The recognition came quickly. USA Today named the museum one of the top 10 best new attractions of 2017, and in April 2019 the Themed Entertainment Association gave it a Thea award as one of the best new attractions in the United States. Then came a new ramp of its own. In May 2024 it was officially announced that the museum would close its Topeka location and move to Las Vegas, Nevada. The Topeka museum closed for good on November 2, 2024, and the collection reopened in June 2026 as the Evel Knievel Experience in the Arts District of downtown Las Vegas.

## Flight Context

The former Topeka home of the Evel Knievel Museum is at SW Topeka Boulevard and SW 21st Street in Topeka, Kansas, at approximately 39.030°N, 95.684°W, beside the Stormont Vail Events Center (formerly the Kansas Expocentre); the museum closed here in November 2024 and reopened in Las Vegas in 2026 as the Evel Knievel Experience, so this marks where its Topeka chapter played out. From about 2,000-2,500 feet AGL, the large events center grounds make the easiest reference, with the Kansas State Capitol dome just over a mile to the north. Philip Billard Municipal Airport (KTOP) is about 4 nm northeast, and Topeka Regional Airport (KFOE) about 5 nm south.
