# Kansas State Capitol

Topeka's statehouse took 37 years to build, crowned itself with a dome taller than the U.S. Capitol's, and hangs one of America's most dramatic murals, a raging John Brown caught between fire and a tornado.

**Location:** 39.0480, -95.6782

**Categories:** State capitols in the United States, Government buildings with domes, Government buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in Kansas, Buildings and structures in Topeka, Kansas, Murals, Tourist attractions in Topeka, Kansas

## Introduction

It takes 296 steps to reach the top. Visitors to the Kansas State Capitol in Topeka climb them from the fifth floor up through a dome that rises to 306 feet, taller than the 288-foot dome of the United States Capitol in Washington. The Kansas dome is slimmer, about 66 feet across at its base compared with the national capitol's 96, but it holds a rare distinction: this is one of the few capitols in the country that still offers tours all the way to the top.

## Thirty-Seven Years of Limestone

Topeka has been the capital since Kansas became a state in 1861, but the statehouse was a long time coming. During the territorial years, from 1854 to 1861, an earlier capitol was begun but never completed in Lecompton, and territorial legislatures met in smaller buildings in Lecompton and Topeka. The land for Capitol Square was donated in 1862 by Cyrus K. Holliday through his Topeka Town Company. Edward Townsend Mix was the master architect, with the wings designed by John G. Haskell. Work on the East Wing began in 1866 using native limestone from Geary County. The West Wing followed in 1879, built with limestone from Cottonwood Falls, and in 1881 the legislature funded a central building to join the two. Construction on that center began in 1886, the dome contract was let in May 1889, and in 1903, after 37 years, the building was declared officially complete.

## John Brown in Flames

Climb to the second floor and the building's most famous painting confronts you. Starting in 1937, John Steuart Curry painted murals there, and the best known is Tragic Prelude, which shows an oversize, raging John Brown wedged between the warring sides of the American Civil War, flanked by flames and a tornado. Curry's work gained considerable notoriety for depicting unsavory aspects of Kansas history. He left the murals unsigned, and he did not complete a commission to paint murals in the rotunda, leaving that space for later artists. His portrait of Brown is believed to be the only instance of a person convicted of treason being featured in a state capitol.

## Kansas Told in Paint

Curry was only the beginning. Between 1951 and 1953, David Hicks Overmyer painted a series of murals in the first-floor rotunda, a panorama of Kansas history in eight scenes: The Coming of the Spaniards, The Battle of Arickaree, The Battle of Mine Creek, Building a Sod House, Lewis and Clark in Kansas, Westward Ho, Arrival of the Railroad and Chisholm Trail. From 1976 to 1978, Lumen Martin Winter painted the murals in the second-floor rotunda, filling the space Curry had left unfinished. A recent addition arrived in January 2025. Titled "Rebel Women," Phyllis Garibay-Coon's mural on the east side of the first-floor rotunda depicts Kansas suffragists, among them Clarina I. H. Nichols, Carrie Langston Hughes, Lutie Lytle, Laura M. Johns and Annie Le Porte Diggs. It is the first art installation by any woman artist in the Kansas Statehouse.

## To the Stars Through Difficulty

At the very top of the dome stands a sculpture by Richard Bergen, chosen from 27 entries. It depicts a Kansa Native American drawing a bow, the arrow aimed at the North Star. Its title, Ad Astra, shortens the state motto, Ad Astra Per Aspera, "to the stars through difficulty." The building has become an emblem in its own right. It appeared prominently on Kansas license plates issued from January 2001 until April 2007, and the Capitol returns on the standard plate issued beginning August 19, 2024. Keeping the emblem sound proved expensive. In December 2001 the Statehouse began a $120 million modernization led by Treanor Architects, restoring its five floors, expanding the basement and restoring the exterior masonry and copper roof and dome. By the time the work finished in spring 2014, scope creep and delays had pushed the total to $332 million, covering new heating and cooling systems, greater security and restroom accessibility, a new parking garage, a visitor center, underground office space and a replaced roof and dome.

## Flight Context

The Kansas State Capitol stands in downtown Topeka at approximately 39.048°N, 95.678°W. Its 306-foot dome, topped by the Ad Astra statue, is easy to pick out over the downtown from about 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, and Capitol Square gives it an open setting. Philip Billard Municipal Airport (KTOP) is about 3 nm northeast, and Topeka Regional Airport (KFOE) is about 6 nm south. Check airspace and traffic for both fields before a low pass, and watch for gusty winds typical of the Kansas plains.
