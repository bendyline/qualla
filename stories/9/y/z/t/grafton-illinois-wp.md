# Grafton, Illinois

Once a booming quarry-and-river town, the oldest city in Jersey County now counts a few hundred residents, a flood-scarred past and a winter crowd of bald eagles at the meeting of the Illinois and Mississippi Rivers.

**Location:** 38.9700, -90.4314

**Categories:** Cities in Illinois, Illinois populated places on the Mississippi River, Cities in Jersey County, Illinois, Metro East, Populated places established in 1832

## Introduction

An 1834 description of Grafton needed only a single line: "a post office, one store, one tavern, and a number of families." James Mason had founded the settlement two years earlier near the confluence of the Illinois and Mississippi Rivers, naming it after his birthplace of Grafton, Massachusetts. What came next was a boom built on stone, boats and fish, then a long fade, then a flood that drove a third of the town away. The oldest city in Jersey County counted 626 residents in the 2020 census, yet its streets still carry the imprint of a much larger place.

## Limestone That Left Town

The area was being settled two decades before Mason's town existed. As early as 1811 or 1812, a militia blockhouse was built near the confluence for protection. The ground itself would become Grafton's fortune. At one point five quarries operated in and around the town, employing nearly 2,000 men, and their limestone went into buildings far beyond Jersey County: the Eads Bridge in St. Louis, government buildings in Rock Island, and the Jersey County Courthouse in nearby Jerseyville. Quarrying was only one draw. Boat building and commercial fishing brought work too, and local tradition holds that the boom-era population topped 10,000, although the census counted just 1,266 residents in 1860. Grafton was not formally incorporated as a city until May 16, 1907, long after the boom had passed.

## Nets and Paddle Wheels

The river supplied the town's other livelihoods. In the late 19th century, the Shafer's Wharf Historic District was one of the largest commercial fishing centers along the Mississippi River. South of Main Street, the Old Boatworks housed a paint house and a machine shop where paddle wheelers were built, and later PT boats. The building has since traded hulls for handiwork. On the fourth weekend of each month from May through October, it fills with antique and craft stalls. Two ferries keep the old habit of crossing water alive. The Grafton Ferry carries travelers over the Mississippi to St. Charles County, Missouri, while the Brussels Ferry crosses the Illinois River to Calhoun County, Illinois.

## When the Rivers Came In

Living where two great rivers meet carries a price. The Great Flood of 1993 caused significant damage to many of Grafton's structures, and a third of the city's residents moved away. The effects are still evident, and the population has not recovered. The town that remains skews older: in 2020 the median age was 53.8, and a third of residents were 65 or older. Still, the decade from 2000 to 2010 brought modest growth, including new housing and restaurants, the Grafton Elementary School, the Grafton Harbor marina, and a lighthouse along the Mississippi River. It was no return to the boom years, but people were building at the confluence again.

## The Winter Home of the Bald Eagle

Tourism is Grafton's main industry now. The city sits at the center of the region's bald eagle watching area and proudly calls itself "The Winter Home of The Bald Eagle." Main Street is lined with restaurants and antique, craft and wine shops, which makes it a popular stop for cyclists on the Sam Vadalabene Bike Trail and for visitors chasing fall color. In the warmer months the two rivers take over, with boating, canoeing and parasailing. Illinois Route 100 runs through town as part of the Great River Road and the Meeting of the Great Rivers Scenic Byway, and Illinois Route 3 reaches its northern terminus here. Five miles west lies Pere Marquette State Park, one of Illinois' largest state parks at more than 8,000 acres.

## Names on the Register

The clearest record of Grafton's busier past sits on the National Register of Historic Places, which lists at least eight places and sites here. Six were added in 1994: the Grafton Historic District, Grafton Bank, the John and Amelia McClintock House, the Ruebel Hotel, the Slaten-LaMarsh House and the Paris Mason Building. The Charles Brainerd House followed in 1998 and the Grafton Boat Works in 2022. The town's roll of notable people is short but memorable. Ed Baecht pitched for the Philadelphia Phillies, the Chicago Cubs and the St. Louis Browns, and Lambert Redd won the silver medal in the long jump at the 1932 Olympics.

## Flight Context

Grafton sits at approximately 38.97 N, 90.43 W, on the Illinois bank at the confluence of the Illinois and Mississippi Rivers. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL the meeting of the two rivers is the unmistakable landmark, with the town strung along Illinois Route 100 (the Great River Road) on the riverfront; look for the Grafton Harbor marina and the two ferry crossings, the Grafton Ferry over the Mississippi to the south and the Brussels Ferry over the Illinois to the west. Pere Marquette State Park lies about five miles west. Nearest airport is St. Charles County Regional Airport/Smartt Field (KSET), about 2.4 nm south across the Mississippi. St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN) at Alton is about 19 nm east-southeast, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 14 nm south-southeast, so remain aware of the St. Louis Class B airspace. Spring is the wettest season and brings severe weather; summer humidity can reduce visibility. Bald eagles winter in the area, so watch for large birds along the river corridor.
