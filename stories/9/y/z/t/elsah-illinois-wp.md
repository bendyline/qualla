# Elsah, Illinois

A Mississippi River village founded on an offer of free lots for stone houses, Elsah stopped growing in 1861 and has been listed in its entirety on the National Register of Historic Places since 1973.

**Location:** 38.9536, -90.3547

**Categories:** Metro East, Villages in Jersey County, Illinois, Illinois populated places on the Mississippi River, Populated places established in 1853, 1853 establishments in Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

According to tradition, the last outcropping James Semple's family saw of Scotland as they left for the United States was Ailsa Craig. Years later, in 1853, Semple founded a village on the Illinois side of the Mississippi River, and it is believed he named it for that final glimpse of home: Elsah. Then he made an unusual offer. Anyone who built a house with stone from his quarry could have a lot for free. The result is a village that stopped growing in 1861, and whose old stone core still stands much as its first builders left it.

## Built of Semple's Stone

Semple was no ordinary land promoter. He was a local lawyer and prominent politician who served as a United States Senator from Illinois, and at one point as a diplomat in New Granada. His stone-for-land bargain worked. By 1861 Elsah had grown to its current size, and then it simply stopped, hemmed in by geographic and economic limits that prevented any further expansion. That early ceiling is the secret of the village's character. Because Elsah never sprawled, the town that stands today is largely the town those first builders made. On LaSalle Street, the historic commercial district runs three blocks inland from the river and consists mainly of stone buildings. All but one of its pre-1861 buildings still stand, including all four of the village's historic taverns.

## Not Quite New England

Elsah has been described as the "New England of the Midwest," and it is easy to see why. The comparison is a little misleading, though. Elsah is not a copy of 18th-century colonial New England but a catalog of 19th-century American fashions: Greek Revival, Gothic Revival, Mansard, Italianate, Saltbox and Gambrel. Two residential districts line Mill Street. The larger runs three blocks with an assortment of stone and brick houses, while the smaller holds a single block of Greek Revival homes. The homes are privately owned, and in 1973 the entire village was placed on the National Register of Historic Places. The river that made Elsah has also tested it. The Great Flood of 1993 caused significant damage to many of the village's structures.

## From River Landing to College Town

In its early years Elsah prospered as the main shipping point for the agricultural goods of Jersey County's farmers. The railroad changed that, and the village's importance faded until Principia College, a four-year liberal arts college for Christian Scientists, was established in Elsah in the 1930s and gave it new life. On the college grounds stands Eliestoun House, completed in 1890 by Alexander Wadsworth Longfellow Jr. for Henry Turner and his wife Ada Ames, a descendant of James Semple. Its name reaches back to Scotland once more, recalling Elliestoun Tower on Castle Semple Loch. The campus was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1993, and its alumni include the Academy Award-winning actor Robert Duvall. The area could have had a very different future: in 1954 the Alton area, in a plan that would have meant buying Principia College, was one of three finalists for the site of the Air Force Academy.

## Eagles, Bicycles and the Great River Road

Elsah stayed mostly quiet until the mid-1960s, when the Great River Road, Illinois Route 100, was built along the river. Today the village is a popular stop for cyclists on the Sam Vadalabene Bike Trail in every season, and the Meeting of the Great Rivers Scenic Byway passes through. In fall and winter, the draw is bald eagles, since Elsah sits close to popular eagle-watching spots. The census numbers tell their own story about how the college shapes the place. Of the 519 people counted in 2020, nearly four in five were between 18 and 24, and the median age was just 20.7. The village covers barely more than a square mile, yet it holds a century and a half of stone and river history within those limits.

## Flight Context

Elsah sits at 38.9536°N, 90.3547°W on the Illinois side of the Mississippi River in Jersey County, upstream of Alton. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow the river and the Great River Road (Illinois Route 100) along the Illinois shore; the village appears as a small cluster of rooftops near the water, with the Principia College campus nearby. St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN) near Alton lies roughly 17 miles to the east-southeast and is a convenient field for a sightseeing flight. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 14 miles to the south, so keep clear of the St. Louis Class B airspace or coordinate with approach control. Fall and winter are eagle-watching season along this stretch of river; watch for fog over the water on cold mornings.
