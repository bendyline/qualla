# Chase Park Plaza Hotel

Two rival hotels on Kingshighway, one lost to Depression foreclosure and won back, became St. Louis's grandest address, a stage for nightclub stars and televised wrestling, a place Jackie Robinson helped open to Black guests, and a comeback story after a decade in the dark.

## Introduction

Sam Koplar opened the Park Plaza Hotel in 1929 as a rival to the Chase Hotel next door. Then the Great Depression took it from him. Koplar lost the Park Plaza to foreclosure and ended up taking a job at his rival, managing the Chase. It was a humbling turn, and it did not last. Within two decades he had bought his own hotel back and become the majority owner of the one that had employed him. Today the two buildings stand together on Kingshighway Boulevard in St. Louis's Central West End as the Royal Sonesta Chase Park Plaza, a historic hotel and residential complex whose story runs from Jazz Age luxury through collapse and back again.

## Rivals on Kingshighway

The Chase Hotel opened on September 29, 1922, built by the developer Chase Ullman, and it quickly supplanted the nearby Buckingham Hotel as the most luxurious hotel in the city. Seven years later the Art Deco Park Plaza tower rose next door as a direct rival. The foreclosure that cost Koplar his tower did not end his ambitions for it. With help from a rabbi friend, he raised the money to buy the Park Plaza back from the insurance company in 1944, and within a few years he became the Chase's majority owner. In 1961 the Koplars merged the two into a single hotel, The Chase-Park Plaza, joining the former competitors under one name.

## Headliners and Headlocks

For decades the Chase was a place to be entertained. The ground-floor Chase Club opened in 1933 and became a popular venue for nationally known entertainers until it closed in 1972. The hotel also gave its name to something far rowdier. From 1959 to 1983, Wrestling at the Chase was produced and televised by KPLR-TV, channel 11, which operated from the hotel and the adjoining Park Plaza, all owned by Sam's son, Harold Koplar. Famous wrestlers appeared on the program, among them Buddy Rogers and the St. Louis native Lou Thesz. For viewers at home, the name of a luxury hotel became tied to body slams and ringside drama.

## The Door Jackie Robinson Opened

Luxury did not mean welcome for everyone. Jackie Robinson broke Major League Baseball's color line on April 15, 1947, when the Brooklyn Dodgers started him at first base, becoming the first African American to play in the major leagues in the modern era. Breaking that line on the field did not end segregation off it. Black players on visiting teams were turned away from the Chase. In 1953 the hotel began admitting some Black guests, but not to its bar, dining room or pool, and in April 1954 Robinson was the only one of six Black Dodgers who agreed to stay there under those limits, calling it an "opening wedge" for the players who would follow. After protests from the St. Louis NAACP and the local Black press, the Chase opened its dining room and lobby to Black guests that spring, and in June 1954 the whole Dodgers team stayed there together. The bar and pool stayed closed to Black guests for years longer. The change came because people were willing to name an injustice in public and keep naming it.

## Ten Years in the Dark

The hotel's decline came quickly once the Koplar era ended. In 1981 the family sold it to a group of investors called the Chase Hotel Redevelopment Corporation. They defaulted on their loan, and in 1983 their lender, GE Capital, seized the 1,700-room hotel. GE closed the hotel rooms in the Park Plaza tower in 1985 and spent $12 million converting it to an apartment building, which opened in 1988. The following year, with business declining, GE shut the hotel in the remaining Chase wing, and in 1991 the building's contents were auctioned off. The Chase wing then sat vacant for nearly a decade, a grand hotel gone dark.

## Lights On Again

Rescue came in 1997, when developer Jim Smith bought the property. He renovated the hotel at a cost of $125 million and reopened it in 1999 as The Chase Park Plaza, quietly dropping the hyphen from the old name. In 2006 the complex sold to Behringer Harvard for $180 million. In 2017 Boston-based Hospitality Properties Trust bought it for $94 million, $60 million of which went to pay off the hotel's debts, and Sonesta Hotels took over management under the new name The Royal Sonesta Chase Park Plaza St. Louis. Today the complex includes a cinema along with several restaurants and bars, and it belongs to the National Trust for Historic Preservation's Historic Hotels of America program.

## Flight Context

The Chase Park Plaza stands at 212 N. Kingshighway Boulevard in the Central West End of St. Louis, Missouri, at 38.644N, 90.264W, near the northeast corner of Forest Park. From 1,500 to 3,000 feet AGL, look for the Art Deco Park Plaza tower rising above the neighborhood beside Kingshighway, with the broad green of Forest Park just to the southwest. The Gateway Arch and downtown lie about 4.5 miles to the east. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is roughly 9 miles to the northwest, and the area lies beneath its Class B airspace. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is about 8 miles to the southeast across the Mississippi River.
