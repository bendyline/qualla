# Maya Angelou Birthplace

The brick house at 3130 Hickory Street, already standing by 1875, is where Marguerite Johnson - the world would come to know her as Maya Angelou - spent her first three years in a segregated St. Louis neighborhood, and in 2015 it became City Landmark No. 129.

**Location:** 38.6228, -90.2297

**Categories:** Homes of American writers, Houses in St. Louis

## Introduction

Before the world knew her as Maya Angelou, she was Marguerite Johnson, a baby in a two-story brick house on Hickory Street in St. Louis. The house was full of family: her brother Bailey, her parents and her grandparents all lived under its roof. She spent only the first three years of her life there, and her childhood would carry her back and forth between Missouri, Arkansas and California. But the little house at 3130 Hickory Street is where the story of the writer, poet and activist Maya Angelou began.

## A House on the 1875 Map

The house predates Angelou by more than half a century. In 1875, Compton and Dry's Pictorial St. Louis captured the city as a topographical survey drawn in perspective, and the Hickory Street house is visible in it - which means it was built in 1875 or earlier. It is a modest 19th-century home of brick, two stories tall. When Angelou's family lived here, the area was part of the neighborhood called Compton Hill. Today the same block lies within the Gate District of St. Louis, but the building itself has outlasted the name on the map.

## Lines Drawn Around Compton Hill

Compton Hill began as a working-class white neighborhood. Then, in 1916, St. Louis passed a segregation law that barred any prospective homeowner from moving into a block where more than 75 percent of the residents were of another race. By the time Marguerite Johnson was born, Compton Hill had become a segregated African American community. The house on Hickory Street was her family's world, but it sat inside boundaries that law and custom had drawn around the people who lived there.

## Stamps and Back Again

In 1931 her parents divorced, and three-year-old Marguerite and Bailey were sent south to live with their paternal grandmother, Annie Henderson, in Stamps, Arkansas. In 1935 the children came back to St. Louis and lived in a different home in the same neighborhood. The return ended in trauma. In 1936, after she was raped by her mother's boyfriend, she and her brother were sent back to their grandmother in Arkansas. In 1942, at 14, she moved to Oakland, California, to live with her mother. She would later write about her childhood in her 1969 autobiography, I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings.

## Landmark No. 129

Maya Angelou died in 2014, having become a renowned performer, author and outspoken civil rights activist. The following year the city of her birth formally honored the house where her life began. Under Ordinance 70135, effective December 12, 2015, the City of St. Louis designated the Maya Angelou Birthplace as City Landmark No. 129. The recognition does not erase the harder parts of her St. Louis story. It simply marks, on an ordinary residential street, the place where a girl named Marguerite spent her first years among family.

## Flight Context

The Maya Angelou Birthplace stands at approximately 38.6228 N, 90.2297 W, at 3130 Hickory Street in the Gate District, southwest of downtown St. Louis. From the air, look for the dense residential grid just east of Compton Avenue and south of the rail corridor that runs west from downtown; Saint Louis University's campus lies to the north. Downtown and the Gateway Arch are about 2.5 miles to the east. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is roughly 5.5 miles to the southeast across the Mississippi, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) about 11.5 miles to the northwest; the area lies beneath St. Louis Class B airspace, so coordinate with ATC. A viewing altitude of 1,500-2,500 feet AGL suits the tight urban neighborhoods. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are the usual visibility concerns.
