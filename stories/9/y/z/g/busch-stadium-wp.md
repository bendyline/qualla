# Busch Stadium

The third St. Louis ballpark to carry a name born when a Budweiser Stadium plan was dropped, Busch Stadium has hosted World Series clinchers, a hockey game in the fog and a rain-soaked Stanley Cup celebration.

**Location:** 38.6225, -90.1931

**Categories:** Baseball venues in St. Louis, St. Louis Cardinals stadiums, Major League Baseball venues, Downtown St. Louis, Tourist attractions in St. Louis

## Introduction

August Busch Jr. wanted to call it Budweiser Stadium. On April 10, 1953, he announced that name and withdrew it the same day, saying a brand name would not be appropriate; some accounts say baseball's leadership had objected to a beer-brand name. The Cardinals' owner named Sportsman's Park Busch Stadium instead, in memory of the company's founder and past presidents, and the Anheuser-Busch company later introduced a beer called Busch. The workaround outlived the ballpark. Today's Busch Stadium, which opened in downtown St. Louis on April 10, 2006, is the third in the city to carry the name, and home to Major League Baseball's St. Louis Cardinals, with room for roughly 44,000 fans.

## Three Ballparks, One Name

The first Busch Stadium closed in 1966, when both the baseball Cardinals and the NFL team of the same name, now the Arizona Cardinals, moved into a new multipurpose stadium called Busch Memorial Stadium. That second Busch was enclosed on every side in the "cookie-cutter" style shared by Riverfront, Veterans and Three Rivers stadiums. The third took the opposite approach. Designed in the "retro-classic" manner of many Populous ballparks, it opens outward to a panoramic view of the downtown skyline rather than walling the city out. Even the old site found new life: across the street, where Busch Memorial Stadium once stood, the residential and entertainment complex called Ballpark Village broke ground in February 2013, after years of delays, and opened its first phase in time for Opening Day 2014.

## The Ballpark That Nearly Crossed the River

Getting it built took a decade of maneuvering. Team ownership began lobbying for a new downtown ballpark in 1995 but could not secure funding for years. In June 2001 the Missouri state government signed a contract with the team, only to see the funding bill struck down in May 2002. For a while the owners looked east, toward a site near Madison, Illinois, next to Gateway International Raceway, until the city of St. Louis drafted a financing plan that kept the team downtown. Private bonds, bank loans, a long-term loan from St. Louis County and money from the owners paid for a stadium that cost about $365 million, part of a development projected at roughly $665 million. It opened unfinished: capacity on opening day was 37,962, and the seating was completed only in late May, in time for a May 29 game against the Houston Astros.

## October at Home

The new park wasted no time. In its first season, the Cardinals reached the 2006 World Series against the Detroit Tigers, their third meeting in the Fall Classic and the first in 38 years. Because the American League had won the All-Star Game, Detroit held home-field advantage, which meant St. Louis hosted only Games 3, 4 and 5. It won all three, clinching the franchise's 10th championship at home on October 27 with a 4-2 win. Five years later, the ballpark saw something even more dramatic: David Freese's walk-off home run won Game 6 of the 2011 World Series against the Texas Rangers, and the Cardinals took Game 7, 6-2, for the title. The 2013 World Series brought stranger endings: Game 3 finished on an obstruction call at third base that awarded St. Louis the winning run, and Game 4 on a pickoff at first, before Boston took the series. The biggest baseball crowd came on August 6, 2022, when 48,581 fans watched a 1-0 win over the New York Yankees.

## Fog, Pucks and a Downpour

The diamond has made room for other games. At the 2009 All-Star Game, President Barack Obama threw the ceremonial first pitch wearing a Chicago White Sox jacket. In May 2013, English clubs Manchester City and Chelsea played an exhibition that sold out within 20 minutes, with City winning 4-3 before 48,263 people. Then came hockey. The St. Louis Blues hosted the first outdoor game in their 50-year history at the 2017 NHL Winter Classic, beating the Chicago Blackhawks 4-1 through thick fog and spotty rain before a sellout crowd of 46,556. Two years later, on June 12, 2019, the Blues' own watch party sold out within a minute, so the Cardinals opened their empty ballpark. More than 25,000 fans stood through a heavy rainstorm watching the scoreboards as the Blues beat the Boston Bruins in Game 7 to win their first Stanley Cup.

## Bronze Legends and Toasted Ravioli

Fans arriving at Gate 3 on the west side pass beneath a large bridge-like span resembling the Eads Bridge, near a bronze statue of Stan "The Man" Musial. More statues stand at the corner of Clark and 8th streets, among them Bob Gibson, Ozzie Smith, Lou Brock, broadcaster Jack Buck and Cool Papa Bell of the Negro league St. Louis Stars. Inside, the menu leans local, with pork steak sandwiches and toasted ravioli alongside bratwurst and peanuts, and because the team has permitted some outside food and drink within posted limits, it is not uncommon to find vendors selling discounted bags of peanuts and bottles of water. The broadcasters have their own honors. The press box is named for writers Bob Broeg and Rick Hummel, both enshrined in Cooperstown, and in 2021 the KMOX radio booth was named for Mike Shannon, who had called Cardinals games since 1972.

## Flight Context

Busch Stadium sits at 38.6225°N, 90.1931°W in downtown St. Louis, close to the Mississippi River; the ballpark is visible from the top of the nearby Gateway Arch. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL the open bowl, green field and the Ballpark Village blocks across the street stand out against the downtown grid, with the river and the Illinois shore just to the east. The area sits under busy St. Louis terminal airspace, so check NOTAMs for any restrictions on game days. Nearby airports: St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS), about 3.6 nm south-southeast across the river in Cahokia, Illinois; St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL), about 11 nm northwest; and St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN) at Alton, about 17 nm north. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common over the river valley.
