# Thurtene Carnival

Every spring, Washington University students build theatrical facades from scratch in a campus parking lot for a carnival whose roots reach back to a 1907 spectacle called the "Younivee Surrkuss."

**Location:** 38.6478, -90.3122

**Categories:** Washington University in St. Louis, 1908 establishments in Missouri, Festivals established in 1908, April

## Introduction

On May 8, 1907, the senior men's honorary at Washington University in St. Louis staged a seven-hour event it billed as the "Grand Gigantic Galaxy of Gorgeous Glittering Generalities." The honorary was called Pralma, and it named its creation the "Younivee Surrkuss." More than a century later, that joke of a title has grown into ThurtenE Carnival, held each spring on the Washington University campus and billed as the oldest and largest student-run carnival in the United States. It still runs on the same fuel: student imagination, a lot of lumber, and a stubborn refusal to take itself too seriously.

## Surrkuss, Surkuss, Surrkuss

For its first nine years the event stayed at the university's Francis Field and kept changing its name in what seemed to be an ongoing joke. It went by "Younivee Surrkuss," "U-N-I-V Surrkuss" and "Pikeway Surrkuss" before settling on "Univee Surrkuss" in 1912. Rides arrived in 1914 with a "Freshman Powered Merry-Go-Round," and in 1916 the drama club Thyrsus made that year's show one of the most talked about. Then the First World War intervened. The May 5, 1917 edition of Student Life simply headlined "No Surkuss," and the event lost two years to the war before returning to Francis Field in the spring of 1919. Films of the 1921 show played in local theaters, a kind of early television coverage. The Surkuss fell dormant from 1927 to 1929 and vanished entirely from 1930 to 1933.

## Thirteen Yellow Jackets

The way back ran through the campus honoraries. A chapter of Omicron Delta Kappa was established at Washington University on February 24, 1933, and in May 1934 it was announced that Pralma would be absorbed by it. In the spring of 1935 the Dean of Men, George Stephens, spoke with Harry White, the president of ThurtenE, and on April 23 Student Life announced that ThurtenE would back a carnival that May. The honorary, formerly a secret society, still runs the event. Undergraduates may apply for a place on the honorary committee in their junior year, and those chosen commit for two years: the juniors officially plan the carnival while the seniors oversee and support them. The committee has thirteen members. Each member has a specific role, from working with vendors to supervising other student groups. On campus they are easy to spot in bright yellow jackets bearing their initials and the honorary's symbol. The net proceeds go to a local community partner chosen by the honorary each year.

## Facades From Scratch

The carnival's signature is what students build. Philanthropic organizations, mostly social and professional sororities and fraternities along with a few other clubs, spend the weeks before the weekend at "the lot," constructing facades and small themed buildings from scratch with their own creativity and engineering skill. About a week to a week and a half before opening, the structures are raised to stand freely with limited supports. Past themes have included a Mad Science Lab, a Beehive, a Ski Lodge, a River Boat and a Pirate Ship, and the themes are closely guarded secrets until the carnival opens. Inside, groups stage theatrical performances, sell food or run games. The tradition has a long memory: Phi Delta Theta introduced an annual movie in 1955 that easily took first prize, and in 1962 fierce winds ripped through the grounds and collapsed the wooden structures. At the close, awards honor categories such as best construction, best production and best spirit.

## Parking Lots and Missing Springs

For a carnival built of temporary walls, ThurtenE has moved often. In 1949 it relocated to the tennis court parking lots, staying until construction of new athletic facilities forced another move. The 1983 carnival, the first held in front of Brookings Hall, was considered at the time to be the "best carnival ever." Major construction on the east end of campus in 2017 pushed it again, and it now occupies the parking lots of the Athletic Complex and Simon Hall. War and pandemic have interrupted it. In 1943, during the Second World War, the carnival was not held, and in March 2020 the COVID-19 pandemic forced a cancellation for only the third time in its history. It came back in 2022. Tens of thousands of visitors come each year, and the chalk symbols and posted names of new honorary members that blanket campus beforehand announce that the carnival is coming.

## Flight Context

ThurtenE Carnival takes place on the Washington University campus, centered near 38.6478°N, 90.3122°W, on the western edge of St. Louis. The best visual reference is the broad green expanse of Forest Park, which lies immediately east of the campus. The event runs over a weekend in spring, and from 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL the rows of student-built facades in the campus parking lots may be visible as a cluster of colorful temporary structures. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 8 miles to the north-northwest, so this is inside busy Class B airspace and requires coordination with approach control. Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) lies farther west in Chesterfield. Spring weather in St. Louis can bring fast-moving thunderstorms, so check the forecast.
