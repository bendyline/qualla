# Saint Louis Chess Club

Founded with Rex Sinquefield's fortune, the Saint Louis Chess Club in the Central West End made St. Louis a center of American chess, hosting national championships and the Sinquefield Cup, while facing a public reckoning over its handling of sexual assault allegations.

## Introduction

In 2014, the strongest chess tournament in history, measured by the ratings of its players, was not held in Moscow or Reykjavik. It was played in St. Louis. That year's Sinquefield Cup gathered a field of elite grandmasters with an average rating of 2802, and the tournament had been founded just a year earlier at the Saint Louis Chess Club, in the city's Central West End. The club was the opening move in billionaire Rex Sinquefield's campaign to improve American chess and turn St. Louis into an international chess center, a campaign that also drew the World Chess Hall of Fame into a building across the street.

## A Billionaire's Opening Move

Sinquefield, a billionaire and libertarian activist, founded the Chess Club and Scholastic Center of Saint Louis in 2007, with his wife, Dr. Jeanne Cairns Sinquefield, as co-founder. It was the first major expenditure in his effort to boost chess in St. Louis and across the United States, and it would not be the last: by 2018 he would spend an estimated $50 million on chess-related philanthropy. On July 17, 2008, the club opened its doors as the St. Louis Chess Club, in facilities that held a tournament hall and a basement broadcast studio. A broadcast studio in a chess club hinted at ambitions reaching well beyond the board, and backed by Sinquefield's largesse, the club quickly grew to prominence in the American chess community.

## Championships Come to Town

Within a year, the national championships started arriving. In 2009 the club began hosting the annual U.S. Championships and U.S. Women's Championships, and the following year it added the annual Junior Closed Championship. The United States Chess Federation took notice. After the success of the 2009 and 2010 championships, it named the club its 2010 Chess Club of the Year and recognized executive director Tony Rich as Organizer of the Year for both years. In August 2010, Sinquefield provided seed funding to bring the World Chess Hall of Fame to St. Louis, citing the club's presence and reputation. The club also experiments with the game itself. It holds yearly tournaments in the chess960 variant, which it trademarks as Chess 9LX.

## Grandmasters Next Door

The Sinquefield Cup, launched by Sinquefield and the club in 2013, is a super-grandmaster event featuring many of the world's strongest players, and it is the only U.S. stop on the Grand Chess Tour. Elite competition is only part of the club's work. A Grandmaster-in-Residence gives lectures, lessons and camps for the local community, and a roster of titled players has held the post over the years, among them Ben Finegold, who served until 2012. The club's home has shifted too. On September 19, 2022, operations moved temporarily into the adjacent space that once housed the chess-themed Kingside Diner, with tournaments spread among that space, the basement of The Chase Park Plaza Hotel, Il Monastero at Saint Louis University and the World Chess Hall of Fame. The club's expanded home campus, about 30,000 square feet, reopened in October 2025.

## A Reckoning at the Board

The club drew national attention of a different kind in 2023. In 2020, club officials had received allegations of sexual assault against Alejandro Ramirez, a resident grandmaster who was for at least two years the club's highest-paid employee. In 2022, two-time women's national champion Jennifer Shahade filed a formal complaint with the United States Chess Federation alleging that Ramirez had twice sexually assaulted her. After she went public in February 2023, Ramirez resigned from the club; he has denied the allegations. In August 2023, after the Wall Street Journal reported that allegations against him had gone unaddressed for years, Chess.com and Lichess announced they would no longer support the club or cover its tournaments. Two months later, the club's board said it "should have done more to address the allegations made by those who bravely came forward," and hired a legal team led by Catherine Hanaway to review its safety guidelines and practices. Chess.com then resumed its support and coverage of the club's events, and in June 2025 Lichess lifted its boycott of the club after it adopted new safety policies. Separately, in July 2024 Shahade sued the U.S. Chess Federation, claiming she was constructively discharged from her job after a "sham mediation" and public statements meant to discredit her; the federation has disputed her allegations.

## Flight Context

The Saint Louis Chess Club is located at approximately 38.6444 N, 90.2611 W in the Central West End of St. Louis. From the air, the green expanse of Forest Park lies just to the west and southwest, the landmark Chase Park Plaza Hotel stands about 0.15 mile to the west, and the domes of the Cathedral Basilica of Saint Louis rise less than half a mile to the east-southeast. The Gateway Arch is about 4.3 miles to the east-southeast. A viewing altitude of 2,000-3,000 feet AGL gives a good view of the neighborhood against the park. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 9 miles to the northwest and St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) about 7.5 miles to the southeast across the Mississippi.
