# St. Francis de Sales Oratory (St. Louis)

Rebuilt after the 1896 tornado with a portal echoing Munich and a 300-foot spire inspired by Ulm, St. Louis's St. Francis de Sales Oratory, the 'Cathedral of South St. Louis,' now rings with Latin liturgy and Gregorian chant.

**Location:** 38.6042, -90.2258

## Introduction

The first celebration at St. Francis de Sales nearly ended in disaster. On September 15, 1867, as the cornerstone was laid for a new church in south St. Louis, a stand collapsed during the ceremony. Vandals had sawed through some of its supporting timbers the night before. No one was seriously injured, and the parish went on to outlast far greater blows, including a tornado that destroyed its church just as a grander replacement was rising. What rose in its place is the neo-Gothic giant now known as the St. Francis de Sales Oratory, often described as the second largest church in St. Louis and popularly called the "Cathedral of South St. Louis."

## Sermons in German

The parish was formed in 1867 to serve a growing German Catholic community whose members had been attending the church of Saints Peter and Paul in Soulard. Its patron was Saint Francis de Sales, a bishop and doctor of the Church who died in 1622. The congregation was eager. Although the church was still under construction, the first Mass was celebrated that Christmas, and by the time the building was dedicated the following May, the parish counted 800 members. Schools for Catholic children of elementary and high school age grew up on the campus. Because most parishioners were ethnic Germans, for many years the sermon at Mass was preached in German, and classes at the parish school were taught in German as well.

## After the Storm

In 1896 the parish had a new church, designed by Engelbert Seibertz, under construction. Then came the great St. Louis Tornado of 1896, which destroyed the original church before the new building's basement was finished; that basement served as the church until 1908. The task of starting over fell to Frederick George Holweck, a German-American scholar and church historian who had once served at St. Francis de Sales as curate. He returned as pastor in 1903 and was charged with building a new church to meet the needs of the growing parish. The result was completed in 1908. A century later, in November 2008, the congregation marked the anniversary of its dedication with a solemn pontifical Mass celebrated by Bishop Robert J. Hermann, followed by a festive reception of traditional German food, and announced a capital campaign for important restoration work.

## Munich's Portal, Ulm's Tower

Holweck's church was designed in the neo-Gothic style by the St. Louis architect Viktor Klutho, who looked to two great German cathedrals for inspiration. The front portal mirrors the Gothic portal of the Cathedral of Munich, while the tower shows the influence of the Cathedral at Ulm. With its main spire, the church stands about 300 feet tall, among the tallest church spires in the United States. Light enters through stained glass windows crafted by the St. Louis glazier Emil Frei, Sr. The building has gathered honors over the decades. In 1968 the local chapter of the American Institute of Architects named it a significant historic building, and in 1978 it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. In 2017 it won the "Church Madness" internet contest run by Granda Liturgical Arts and the blog Art & Liturgy, billed as America's most beautiful church.

## Chant Beneath the Spire

Since 2005 the church has been operated by the Institute of Christ the King Sovereign Priest, which practices the Latin liturgy and places strong emphasis on the liturgical arts. As an oratory, it has no geographic boundaries like a parish, though it has permission to conduct baptisms, weddings and funerals along with Mass and confessions. Music fills the nave. The oratory's highly developed music program includes several choirs specializing in Gregorian chant and polyphony, and in the summer of 2009 it organized a course in Gregorian chant for beginners. It has also hosted the St. Louis Chamber Chorus. One former rector saw the church's role reaching well beyond its walls. "St. Francis de Sales is the anchor of this neighborhood," former rector Canon Michael Wiener told the St. Louis Post-Dispatch in 2010, "and when we revitalize, the whole neighborhood revitalizes."

## Flight Context

St. Francis de Sales Oratory stands at approximately 38.6042 N, 90.2258 W in south St. Louis. Its 300-foot Gothic spire makes an excellent visual marker. The Soulard neighborhood and the Anheuser-Busch brewery lie a little under a mile to the east, and the Gateway Arch rises about 2.6 miles to the east-northeast. A viewing altitude of 2,000-2,500 feet AGL keeps the spire well below you while showing its relationship to the city grid. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is about 4.5 miles to the east-southeast across the Mississippi, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 12.5 miles to the northwest. The area lies beneath busy St. Louis terminal airspace, so coordinate with ATC; summer haze can reduce visibility.
