# Delmar Loop

Named for a streetcar turnaround and a street christened by neighbors from Delaware and Maryland, St. Louis's Delmar Loop grew from a 1950s teen hangout into a music, food and arts strip the American Planning Association ranked among America's great streets.

**Location:** 38.6559, -90.3038

**Categories:** Entertainment districts in the United States, Streets in St. Louis, Culture of St. Louis, Restaurant districts and streets in the United States, Tourist attractions in St. Louis

## Introduction

Delmar Boulevard's name is a compromise. According to Norbury L. Wayman's History of St. Louis Neighborhoods, the road once known as Morgan Street was renamed by two early landowners who lived on opposite sides of it, one from Delaware and one from Maryland, who blended the names of their home states. The town of Delmar, on the Delaware-Maryland border, got its name the same way. Today the stretch St. Louisans simply call the Loop, straddling University City and the western edge of St. Louis near Washington University and Forest Park, is one of the region's busiest entertainment districts, and in 2007 the American Planning Association named it one of the 10 Great Streets in America.

## Where the Streetcars Turned Around

The Loop takes its name from a streetcar turnaround that once sat here. By the 1950s the district was the meeting place for University City's teenagers. The Varsity Theater and the Tivoli showed first-run movies, and Ed's Billiards, squeezed between the two, was always packed with teens. Restaurants ran up and down the strip. Enright Avenue, part of the old streetcar turnaround, held a drug store, three restaurants and a record store, and another drug store stood at Delmar and Kingsland, both of them with soda fountains. Out at Skinker, not then considered part of the Loop, Garaveli's Restaurant faced Davy "Nose" Bold's, a well-known nightclub. The late 1960s brought a new flavor, when Streetside Records and head shops dominated the retail scene and gave the street the eclectic crowd it still draws, helped along by the nearby university.

## Between the Lions and the City Line

The Loop has loose but well-known borders. Its western end is generally marked by the U. City Lions, a male and a female lion on pedestals flanking Delmar just west of University City City Hall; beyond them the boulevard turns largely residential. The eastern edge was traditionally the St. Louis city limit, punctuated by The Delmar Lounge at Eastgate. Around 2000 the district began spilling across that line into the Skinker DeBaliviere neighborhood, largely through the redevelopment work of Joe and Linda Edwards, owners of Blueberry Hill, The Pageant, Pin-Up Bowl, Magic Mini Golf, the Moonrise Hotel and Eclipse Restaurant. The St. Louis Regional Arts Commission opened its new headquarters on Delmar in 2003, with performance and office space for theater groups, and a MetroLink Red Line station now anchors the eastern end.

## Names Set in Brass

Walk the sidewalks and you are walking over the St. Louis Walk of Fame, a line of brass plaques set into the pavement along Delmar to honor famous St. Louisans. Musicians Chuck Berry, Miles Davis and Tina Turner are here, along with actor John Goodman, bridge-builder James Eads and the sex researchers Masters and Johnson. The music is not only commemorated. Across from the Arts Commission, The Pageant has become one of the city's main venues for mid-size concerts, hosting rap, rock and country acts, including hometown stars Chuck Berry and Nelly. Nelly filmed the video for "Air Force Ones" in the Loop, and his hit "Country Grammar" references the district again and again.

## The Trolley That Came Back

The Loop Trolley, a 2.2-mile fixed-track heritage line, was built to link the district with MetroLink and the attractions of Forest Park. Its start was rocky. The trolley ran for several weeks in fall 2018 and a full season in 2019, then shut down because fare revenue fell far short of expectations. It returned in August 2022, after the federal government threatened to demand repayment of the tens of millions of dollars in federal grants that had helped build the line. Since then the cars have rolled Thursdays through Sundays from April to October, and riders pay no fare at all as the cars glide along Delmar past landmarks like the Tivoli Theatre.

## Records, Books and a Long Menu

Much of the Loop's character lives in its storefronts. The Tivoli Theater, Blueberry Hill, Fitz's Restaurant and Bottling Company, Subterranean Books and the Vintage Vinyl record store share the street with Washington University's 560 Music Center and the COCA Center for Creative Arts. The food spans continents, from Al-Tarboush deli and Ranoush Mediterranean Cuisine to Seoul Taco, Gokul Indian Restaurant, Meshuggah Cafe and four Thai restaurants run by Pat's Thai Restaurants. Menus also come and go. Cicero's Italian Restaurant closed in 2017, Piccione Pastry in 2019, Three Kings Public House after a 2023 fire and the Peacock Loop Diner in January 2025.

## Flight Context

The Delmar Loop is centered near 38.6559 N, 90.3038 W along Delmar Boulevard in University City, at the western edge of the City of St. Louis. From the air, look for Forest Park's large green expanse about 1.5 miles to the southeast and the Washington University campus just to the south; downtown St. Louis and the Gateway Arch are roughly 7 miles east. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 7 miles to the north-northwest, so the Loop lies deep within busy St. Louis terminal airspace and any low sightseeing requires coordination with ATC. Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) is about 19 miles west. A viewing altitude of around 2,500 feet AGL shows the street's linear commercial strip against the surrounding grid of homes. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are the main visibility concerns.
