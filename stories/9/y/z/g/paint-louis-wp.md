# Paint Louis

What began in 1995 as a St. Louis graffiti jam has grown into Paint Louis, an international hip-hop gathering where hundreds of writers cover the Mississippi riverfront floodwall in 20-foot murals each Labor Day weekend.

**Location:** 38.6147, -90.1880

**Categories:** Street art festivals in the United States, Music festivals in Missouri, Culture of Missouri, Graffiti in the United States, 1995 establishments in Missouri

## Introduction

"This is like the Super Bowl of graffiti." That was how Jona Anderson, the writer better known as Stun or Stun1, summed up Paint Louis, and the comparison fits a weekend that draws graffiti writers to St. Louis from around the world. Each year around the American Labor Day holiday, the event brings together all four elements of hip hop, graffiti, breakdancing, rapping and DJing, for three days of creation and performance. Its canvas is the Mississippi River floodwall along the riverfront south of the Gateway Arch, known as the Mural Mile, where spray paint turns plain concrete into an open-air gallery.

## A Jam Among Friends

Paint Louis started informally in 1995, when a group of local St. Louis graffiti writers came together for a "graffiti jam." It became more formalized in 1997, which is why the event marked its 20th anniversary in 2017, a celebration held that September 22 to 24. That original run ended around 2001, and after a decade-long gap a few original members revived it, starting with a low-key repainting in 2012. By 2013 it hosted 300 artists and drew around 1,000 people, and that year it took place earlier than Labor Day, from June 21 to 23. The 2014 edition stretched over four days, from August 29 to September 1, with 200 artists and a mile of wall to work on. Organizers John Harrington of Midwest Avengers and AJ Sanchez spent more than $10,000 to get that year's event off the ground.

## Too Many Cans

Success brought its own trouble. For Paint Louis 2015, 200 writers were invited to participate and propose their works so that wall space could be allocated. More than 350 writers showed up who had not gone through the submission process, and the overload created complications. Some artists were unhappy and took action, painting unsanctioned areas all over town, and afterward Harrington said he feared Paint Louis would be cancelled in the future. It was not. Paint Louis 2016 went ahead over Labor Day with international artists taking part, and the wall itself was reported to measure "20-feet-by-4-miles." By 2019, with the focus on women's presence in the art form, 212 artists had registered and more than 250 took part, painting 20-foot-high murals on what Harrington called the longest graffiti wall in the world.

## A Virtual Year and a Return

In 2020 the pandemic forced a rethink. Paint Louis moved online as an open call for participation, and Harrington, the event's co-founder and organizer, explained the thinking behind it. "Paint Louis has always been about showcasing local St. Louis artists and Midwest artists as a whole," he said. "Over the years, we expanded it to all street and mural artists and it has become an international event." Because of COVID-19, the organizers opened the virtual event to all kinds of visual artists, from canvas painters and graphic designers to digital artists, air brushers, muralists and graffiti writers. The in-person event returned from September 3 to 5, 2021, when more than 300 artists took part, among them the artist Norm4eva.

## Legends on the Riverfront

The anniversaries brought headliners. For the 25th annual Paint Louis in 2022, more than 500 artists from around the world took part, and hip-hop pioneer KRS-One performed for free. The 26th edition in 2023 again drew more than 500 artists, brought Souls of Mischief to perform for free and launched "Paint Littles," a rebranded event to teach kids how to paint. In 2024, the 27th year, golden age rapper Rakim gave a sponsored free concert for everyone attending, while artists including Peat Eyez, Matt Minetta, Jason "Killer Napkins," Max Presur and Sase 1 WST worked the wall. Fox 2 reported more than 35,000 attendees in 2025, when Kristian Blackmon, Marquis Terrel and Lindy Drew painted a mural honoring St. Louis civil rights activist Percy Green. That same year, the graffiti archive Art Crimes announced an archive of Paint Louis works from 2021 through 2024, keeping a record of four years of work from the wall.

## Flight Context

Paint Louis is centered at approximately 38.6147 N, 90.1880 W on the Mississippi River floodwall south of downtown St. Louis. The painted Mural Mile runs along the riverfront between Victor and Chouteau avenues, south of the Gateway Arch, which rises about 0.7 mile to the north-northeast and makes the easiest reference point. Follow the Missouri bank of the river south from the Arch at 1,500-2,500 feet AGL to trace the wall's long, colorful ribbon of concrete. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is about 3.5 miles to the south-southeast across the river, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 13.5 miles to the northwest. The riverfront lies beneath busy St. Louis terminal airspace, so coordinate with ATC. Late-summer haze can soften visibility over the water.
