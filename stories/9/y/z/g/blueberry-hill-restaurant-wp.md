# Blueberry Hill (restaurant)

A Delmar Loop restaurant opened in 1972 grew to fill an entire block, and in a basement room named for his duck walk, Chuck Berry played 209 consecutive monthly shows until 2014.

**Location:** 38.6558, -90.3050

**Categories:** Restaurants in Missouri, Music venues in St. Louis, Restaurants established in 1972, 1972 establishments in Missouri, Buildings and structures in St. Louis County, Missouri, Tourist attractions in St. Louis

## Introduction

For 209 consecutive months, Chuck Berry came to a basement on Delmar Boulevard and played. The room was called the Duck Room, named for his famous duck walk, and it sits beneath Blueberry Hill, a restaurant and music club in the Delmar Loop neighborhood of University City, just outside St. Louis. Berry kept up his shows there on the third Wednesday of each month until October 2014. By then Blueberry Hill had been serving cheeseburgers and music to the city for more than four decades, and the restaurant had become a St. Louis landmark in its own right.

## A Jukebox and a Dream, 1972

Joe and Linda Edwards opened Blueberry Hill on September 8, 1972. A Seeburg jukebox was spinning records from the first day, and music has been part of the place ever since. The restaurant kept growing, spreading into the adjacent spaces to the east and to the west until it came to occupy an entire block of Delmar Boulevard. It settled into a daily rhythm that still defines it. Tourists and locals fill it during the day, and in the evening it becomes a popular hangout for university students, most of them from nearby Washington University.

## A Room Built for the Duck Walk

The Duck Room was born in 1997, and making it took some digging. To give the basement room the high ceilings a live music venue needs, the floor was lowered five feet. It was named for Chuck Berry's signature onstage move, and Berry made it his home stage, playing 209 consecutive monthly concerts there. Picture a packed basement, the band a few feet from the crowd, and the duck walk that gave the room its name, month after month.

## Cases, Darts and Cheeseburgers

Blueberry Hill is famous for its decor, its cheeseburger and its darts room. The decor is where the restaurant truly shows its personality. Nearly one hundred glowing display cases, curated personally by Joe Edwards, hold pop culture and music memorabilia, including collections devoted to Star Wars, The Simpsons and Elvis, along with vintage Pez dispensers. The restaurant describes its Chuck Berry holdings as the largest collection of his artifacts and memorabilia on public display in the world, and it says its jukebox has been named the best in the United States by Billboard and Esquire. Eating a burger here means eating it surrounded by decades of American pop culture.

## Stars on the Sidewalk

Joe Edwards's influence reaches past the restaurant's front door. Outside Blueberry Hill runs the St. Louis Walk of Fame, which he founded in the late 1980s, with the first stars installed in 1989. It lines the sidewalks on both sides of Delmar with stars and informative biographical plaques honoring people from the St. Louis area who have made major national contributions to American cultural heritage. A stroll along the Loop becomes a slow lesson in the region's reach, one plaque at a time, and it leads back to the restaurant whose owner set the first stars in place.

## Flight Context

Blueberry Hill sits on Delmar Boulevard in the Delmar Loop at 38.656N, 90.305W, in University City, Missouri, just west of the St. Louis city limits. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow Delmar Boulevard as it runs east and west through a dense commercial strip; the Washington University campus lies just to the south, and the broad green of Forest Park opens up beyond it to the southeast. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is only about 7 miles to the north-northwest, so the area lies well within its Class B airspace. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is about 10 miles to the southeast across the Mississippi River. Evening, when the Loop's lights come on, is when the street looks most alive from the air.
