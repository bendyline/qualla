# Shrine of St. Joseph, St. Louis

Founded by Jesuits for German immigrants in the early 1840s, St. Louis's Shrine of St. Joseph is the site of a healing declared miraculous by Rome and home to the Altar of Answered Prayers, built in thanks for a parish's deliverance from cholera.

## Introduction

On Wednesday, March 16, 1864, a man named Ignatius Strecker, suffering from a severe chest injury, managed to drag himself to St. Joseph's church in St. Louis. He arrived just as the visiting Jesuit Father Francis Xavier Weninger was blessing the congregation with a relic of Blessed Peter Claver. According to the shrine's own history, Strecker was allowed to kiss the relic, began to heal and within a few days was back at his job. Rome would later declare his recovery an authentic miracle, and the church, now the Shrine of St. Joseph in the Columbus Square neighborhood, is described as the site of the only Vatican-authenticated miracle in St. Louis.

## A Parish for German Newcomers

The Jesuits founded St. Joseph parish in the early 1840s to serve a residential community made up mostly of German immigrants, and they immediately set out to build a church. A wealthy widow known for her many philanthropic deeds, Mrs. Ann Biddle, donated the land. Bishop Kenrick blessed the cornerstone on April 14, 1844, and the finished church, a modest building facing west toward 11th Street, was dedicated on the first Sunday of August 1846, with Father James Van de Velde, later Bishop of Chicago, officiating. The parish soon became a very active community. In 1862 a large parish school was built nearby to care for the many children of the area, staffed by the School Sisters of Notre Dame.

## A Cure Examined in Rome

Strecker's recovery did not simply pass into parish legend. According to the shrine's history, two years after the cure the Most Reverend Michael O'Connor, SJ, carried out a thorough canonical investigation of the incident, and in 1887 Cardinal Bianchi in Rome formally declared the miracle authentic. The Holy See's judgment carried weight far beyond St. Louis. Strecker's healing was chosen as one of the two miracles required in the canonization process of Peter Claver, the saint he had invoked just before his recovery. A statue of Saint Peter Claver has its place in the shrine, a reminder that a moment inside this church helped make a saint.

## A Vow Against Cholera

As the parish grew, the original church became too small. The congregation decided on a large addition and a reworked structure with its entrance facing Biddle Street, and Archbishop Kenrick laid the cornerstone for this second St. Joseph in 1865. Then, in 1866, cholera struck St. Louis. The shrine's history records that St. Joseph's parish alone saw as many as 20 burials a day. Its pastor, Father Joseph Weber, gathered the parishioners, and they made a solemn vow to St. Joseph: if God, through his intercession, protected them from the epidemic, they would erect a suitable monument to him in thanksgiving. From that day on, the shrine says, not a single member of any family who had signed the vow and pledge died of the disease. The remodeled church was dedicated on December 30, 1866, with Father Pierre Jean De Smet, noted for his missionary work among Native American nations, officiating.

## Go to Joseph

The parishioners kept their promise, raising even more than they had pledged. They hired the Bueschers of Chicago, famous for their religious art, to carve an elaborate altar modeled on the Altar of St. Ignatius in the Jesuit Gesù Church in Rome, with one change: figures of St. Joseph and the Christ Child take the place of St. Ignatius. Beneath them appear the words "Ite ad Joseph," Go to Joseph. For a congregation that had seen as many as 20 burials in a single day, the phrase was more than decoration. Installed early in 1867 at a total cost of $6,131, it became known as the Altar of Answered Prayers, and it still serves as the shrine's central altar.

## Towers Raised and Lowered

The church grew once more in 1880. A remodeling completed in 1881 gave it an elaborate Romanesque face and twin towers topped with delicate cupolas. That profile did not last. In 1954, under the pastor, Father Anthony Corey, the towers were shortened for safety and their cupolas replaced with heavier hexagonal caps, considerably altering the building's exterior. The church was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1978. The Jesuits gave the church to the Archdiocese of St. Louis in 1965, and today it is owned by the archdiocese and leased to The Friends of The Shrine of St. Joseph, Inc., a nonprofit corporation formed in 1979 that took on its stewardship in 1980.

## Flight Context

The Shrine of St. Joseph stands at approximately 38.6373 N, 90.1926 W in the Columbus Square neighborhood just north of downtown St. Louis. The Gateway Arch rises about 1 mile to the south-southeast, and the Mississippi River riverfront lies a short distance to the east. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, look for the church's twin towers with their hexagonal caps just north of the downtown core. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is about 5 miles to the south-southeast across the river, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 12 miles to the northwest. Downtown St. Louis lies beneath busy terminal airspace, so coordinate with ATC; summer haze over the river can reduce visibility.
