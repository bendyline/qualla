# St. Louis Walk of Fame

Set into the sidewalks of Delmar Boulevard since 1989, the St. Louis Walk of Fame's brass stars and bronze plaques honor more than 150 people with St. Louis roots, from Chuck Berry and T. S. Eliot to Dred and Harriet Scott.

## Introduction

Chuck Berry, T. S. Eliot, Scott Joplin and Charles Lindbergh share a stretch of sidewalk in the Delmar Loop. So do Katherine Dunham, James B. Eads, Stan Musial, Vincent Price, Joseph Pulitzer and Tennessee Williams. They were the first ten names set into the pavement of the St. Louis Walk of Fame in 1989, and since then the brass stars and bronze plaques along Delmar Boulevard have multiplied to more than 150. Each bears an inductee's name and a summary of his or her accomplishments, and together they turn an evening stroll into a walk through the city's contributions to American culture. According to the Loop's own tourism site, the Delmar Loop as a whole draws more than four million visitors a year.

## Stars Underfoot

The stars and plaques are set into the sidewalks along a stretch of Delmar Boulevard in the Delmar Loop, a stretch that spans the border between the City of St. Louis and University City. The rules for getting one are simple to state. Every inductee was either born in the Greater St. Louis area or spent formative or creative years there, and the contribution can come in any field. Most of the people honored so far made their names in acting, entertainment, music, sports, art and architecture, broadcasting, journalism, science and education, or literature. The walk has become a popular cultural landmark for residents and tourists alike.

## The Class of 1989

The walk was founded by developer Joe Edwards, owner of the Blueberry Hill pub and restaurant and other establishments along the route. Its first stars and plaques were installed in 1989, honoring musician Chuck Berry, dancer and choreographer Katherine Dunham, bridge builder James B. Eads, poet T. S. Eliot, ragtime composer Scott Joplin, aviator Charles Lindbergh, baseball player Stan Musial, actor Vincent Price, newspaper publisher Joseph Pulitzer and playwright Tennessee Williams. To get the walk established quickly, ten more inductees were selected in each of the next four years. Starting in 1994 the pace slowed sharply, and no more than three have been honored in any year since. The walk has grown in space as well as in names. Edwards has extended it, and the boundaries of the Delmar Loop in general, eastward as he continues to invest in the area's redevelopment.

## How a Star Is Chosen

Earning a star takes more than a nomination. The walk's founder and director culls about 30 to 40 finalists from the nominees. Those finalists then go to a selection committee of 120 St. Louisans, a group that has been described as "the chancellors of all area universities, key people from local libraries, arts organizations and historical societies, media journalists, and other citizens with an informed understanding of St. Louis' cultural heritage." With room for only a few new names each year, the committee's choices carry weight, and the walk grows slowly, one handful of stars at a time. For now, though, the process is on pause: the Walk of Fame says it is not accepting new nominations and hopes to resume in one to two years.

## A City's Roll Call

Scan the list of honorees and St. Louis starts to reveal itself. Maya Angelou and Miles Davis are here, as are Josephine Baker, Tina Turner and Ike Turner. Yogi Berra, Bob Gibson and Jackie Joyner-Kersee share the pavement with Ulysses S. Grant, William T. Sherman, Pierre Laclede and Auguste Chouteau. Some honors go to partnerships rather than individuals: Dred and Harriet Scott, Carl and Gerty Cori, and Masters and Johnson each share a single entry. Groups have their places too, including The Fifth Dimension, The Isley Brothers and The Rockettes. Nelly and Cedric the Entertainer appear alongside Kate Chopin, Elijah Lovejoy and Elizabeth Keckley, a roll call as varied as the city that produced it.
