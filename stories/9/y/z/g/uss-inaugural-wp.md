# USS Inaugural

A World War II minesweeper that survived the waters off Okinawa and cleared 82 mines, USS Inaugural became a floating museum at St. Louis, only to be torn loose by the Great Flood of 1993; her wreck still surfaces when the Mississippi runs low.

**Location:** 38.6115, -90.1881

**Categories:** Admirable-class minesweepers, Shipwrecks of the Mississippi River, Ships built in Winslow, Washington, 1944 ships, World War II minesweepers of the United States, Former National Register of Historic Places in Missouri, Former National Historic Landmarks of the United States, Museums established in 1968, 1968 establishments in the United States, 1968 establishments in Missouri

## Introduction

When the Mississippi runs low at St. Louis, a warship rises from the riverbed. Rusting and rolled onto her side, the hulk of USS Inaugural lies on the Missouri bank, half a mile south of the Poplar Street Bridge not far downstream from the Gateway Arch where she was once moored as a museum. She fought off Okinawa while suicide pilots targeted the invasion fleet, and she cleared mines in the approaches to Japan and Korea. What finally defeated her was the river, in the Great Flood of 1993.

## Launched at Winslow

Inaugural was an Admirable-class fleet minesweeper, part of one of the largest and most successful classes of U.S. minesweepers ordered during World War II. The Winslow Marine Railway and Shipbuilding Company of Winslow, Washington, built and launched her on October 1, 1944, and she was commissioned less than three months later, on December 30. After a shakedown off the California coast, she sailed for Pearl Harbor on March 14, 1945, arriving eight days later. For the next two months, the new minesweeper performed patrol and escort duties between Hawaii and the islands of the western Pacific, a long apprenticeship before the hardest weeks of her career.

## The Waters Off Okinawa

On May 22, 1945, Inaugural left Saipan with a convoy bound for Okinawa, the site of the last and largest amphibious operation of the Pacific war. The voyage was punctuated by several attacks on suspected submarines, and the convoy reached Okinawa on May 30. There, in the desperate struggle for the island, Inaugural patrolled the surrounding seas and often fired at Japanese aircraft as their pilots flew suicide attacks against the ships supporting the invasion. Apart from a few days between July 19 and 24, when she put to sea to ride out a great typhoon, she stayed in those dangerous waters until August 30, 1945. By then the war was over, but her most important work was only beginning.

## Clearing the Way to Peace

Occupation required safe harbors, and safe harbors required minesweepers. In September 1945 Inaugural swept the approaches to Jinsen, Korea, the port now known as Incheon, and later cleared mines in the waters off Nagasaki and Sasebo in Japan. After taking on needed equipment at Okinawa from October 14 to 24, she returned to sweeping around the Japanese home islands. In all, Inaugural cleared 82 mines and earned two battle stars for her World War II service. She sailed for home on December 24, reaching San Pedro, California, on February 7, 1946, then continued to Galveston, Texas, where she was decommissioned on September 9, 1946. Reclassified MSF-242 in 1955, she waited in the Atlantic Reserve Fleet's Texas Group until she was struck from the Navy List on March 1, 1967, and sold.

## A Museum Beneath the Arch

Rather than a scrapyard, Inaugural found a second life. In 1968 she was towed up to St. Louis and established as a floating museum on the Mississippi, moored at the Gateway Arch. Visitors could step aboard a genuine Pacific veteran rather than simply read about one. Her significance was formally recognized on January 14, 1986, when she was designated a National Historic Landmark as one of only two surviving Admirable-class minesweepers that had served in the Pacific during World War II. For a quarter century, the little warship was part of the St. Louis riverfront.

## The River Takes Her

On August 1, 1993, during the great Mississippi flood, Inaugural broke loose from her moorings. Her hull was breached, she took on water, and she rolled onto her port side before sinking on the Missouri side of the river. Plans to salvage her in one piece and restore her were judged unfeasible, and the ship was declared a total loss. Her Landmark designation was withdrawn on August 7, 2001, because she no longer met the criteria. The river has never quite let her go. In January 2013 the water dropped so low that people could walk up to the ship, and scrapping was set to begin before the river rose again, leaving only a few pieces of metal gone. In the autumn of 2021, a historic low level of the Mississippi exposed the hulk once more, and low water showed it again in October 2022.

## Flight Context

The wreck of USS Inaugural lies at approximately 38.6115°N, 90.1881°W on the Missouri bank of the Mississippi River, about half a mile south of the Poplar Street Bridge in downtown St. Louis. The Gateway Arch, just upriver, makes an unmistakable landmark. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow the riverfront south from the Arch; the hulk is visible only when the river is at or below average level. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) in Cahokia, Illinois, is only about 3 nm south-southeast, and St. Louis Lambert International Airport (KSTL) about 12 nm northwest, so coordinate with St. Louis approach in this busy airspace.
