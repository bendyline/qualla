# The Muny

Built in 49 days in 1917 on a Forest Park lawn between two oak trees, the Muny became what is often described as the first municipally owned outdoor theater in the United States and still stages summer musicals for about 10,800, with the back rows free.

**Location:** 38.6401, -90.2801

**Categories:** Outdoor theatres, Amphitheaters in the United States, Theatres in St. Louis, Forest Park (St. Louis), Tourist attractions in St. Louis

## Introduction

The last nine rows cost nothing. Every summer, about 1,500 of the roughly 10,800 seats at the St. Louis Municipal Opera Theatre, known to everyone simply as the Muny, are given away free on a first-come, first-served basis. It is a fitting tradition for a theater that began as a civic project in the middle of a public park. From mid-June to late August each year (June 15 to August 23 in 2026), a nonprofit organization fills this vast outdoor amphitheater in Forest Park with musicals performed under the open sky, carrying on a story that started more than a century ago with a Shakespeare comedy staged on the grass.

## Between Two Oaks

Outdoor theater in Forest Park began with Luther Ely Smith, who started staging pageant-masques on Art Hill in 1914. Two years later, for the 300th anniversary of Shakespeare's death, a grassy area between two oak trees, on the site where the Muny stands today, was chosen for a production of As You Like It. Margaret Anglin produced it, Sydney Greenstreet starred, and the cast included "1,000 St. Louis folk dancers and folk singers." There was no grand auditorium. The audience sat in portable chairs set out on a gravel floor, watching the play unfold against the trees of the park.

## Forty-Nine Days

The permanent theater came together in a rush. The Convention Board of the St. Louis Advertising Club needed an entertainment feature for its 13th annual convention, set for June 3, 1917. Mayor Henry Kiel, attorney Guy Golterman and Parks Commissioner Nelson Cunliff stepped in and, in 49 days, not counting seven lost to rain, created what is often described as the first municipally owned outdoor theater in the United States, a place that now bills itself as the nation's oldest and largest outdoor musical theatre. On June 5, 1917, the opera Aida was performed on what would become the Muny stage. Two years later the theater got its formal name, and the first show under the Muny banner, Robin Hood, opened on June 16, 1919, with the mayor himself in the role of King Richard.

## Building a Summer Institution

That opening night went badly for the equipment: a flood damaged it, and by the 1921 season the theater had a new permanent stage on a concrete base designed to withstand future floods. Improvements came quickly. In 1922 the Muny added a pergola, 750 permanent opera chairs, 500 parking spaces for automobiles and "comfort stations." The next year brought 1,800 more permanent seats, a rehearsal stage and a sound amplifier so people at the back could hear as well as those in front. In 1924 Kiel stepped down as president of the Municipal Theater Association, saying the enterprise should be led by businessmen, and telephone executive H. J. Pettengill was elected to replace him. In 1925, 2,400 numbered chairs were installed in the old 25-cent section, and every paid seat became reserved. A turntable arrived on the stage in 1930.

## Eleven Days to Opening Night

The Muny produces all of its shows itself, typically seven musicals a season. Through the winter a full-time staff of 35 plans the coming summer; once the season begins, the staff swells to more than 500 people. Each show is rehearsed in just 11 days, with two technical rehearsals, one in costume and one without, in the final two or three days before opening. Most productions run for seven days, though recent seasons have each included at least one extended run, and a break of two or three days between shows allows crews to change sets and floors. The stage turntable, rebuilt in 1997, was replaced during renovations in 2018 and 2019. Young performers have their own place here too: the Muny Kids, ages 7 to 13, formed in 1994, and the Muny Teens, ages 14 to 18, followed in 1998.

## When the Stage Rocked

For decades the Muny also hosted concerts. Whitney Houston, Patti LaBelle, Sting, Barry Manilow, Bob Dylan, the Allman Brothers, Hank Williams Jr., the Beach Boys and James Taylor all played this stage. The concert era ended in 1991, the year Riverport Amphitheatre opened, with a final show by the Moody Blues and Kansas on August 29. More than 30 years later, in 2024, the Muny revived the tradition with a one-night-only concert by John Legend, backed by the St. Louis Symphony Orchestra. President and CEO Kwofe Coleman called it a chance to deliver "this bit of nostalgia" to a community where "generations of St. Louisans have memories of seeing legendary music acts on our magnificent stage." Since 2015, the Muny has also presented Muny Magic at The Sheldon, cabaret-style concerts featuring artists from its own productions.

## Flight Context

The Muny sits at approximately 38.6401 N, 90.2801 W in the heart of Forest Park, St. Louis. From the air, the large fan-shaped seating bowl of the roughly 10,800-seat amphitheater is easy to spot among the park's trees and lawns. The Saint Louis Art Museum on Art Hill lies less than a mile to the west, and the Gateway Arch is about 5.3 miles to the east-southeast. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is roughly 9 miles to the north-northwest, so the park lies within busy St. Louis terminal airspace; coordinate with ATC. A viewing altitude of 2,000-2,500 feet AGL shows the theater and surrounding park well. Performances run from mid-June to late August; watch for afternoon thunderstorms and summer haze.
