# National Shrine of Our Lady of the Snows

In Belleville, Illinois, a Catholic shrine named for a legendary summer snowfall in Rome grew from a seminary chapel's corner altar into grounds of gardens, bells and more than a million Christmas lights.

**Location:** 38.5612, -90.0923

## Introduction

Legend says that snow once fell in the summertime in Rome, on the site of the Basilica of Saint Mary Major. That impossible snowfall gives its name to a Catholic shrine to the Blessed Virgin Mary about seven miles southeast of downtown St. Louis, in Belleville, Illinois. Here the snow arrives in another form. Every Advent and Christmas season, more than 1.5 million colored and white lights spread across the grounds of the National Shrine of Our Lady of the Snows, and tens of thousands of visitors come each year to see them. The shrine sits within the Roman Catholic Diocese of Belleville, but it is run by the United States Province of the Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate, the order of the priest who first carried this devotion to the Midwest.

## A Corner of a Seminary Chapel

The shrine began small. In 1941 a missioner named Father Paul Schulte, OMI, introduced devotion to Our Lady of the Snows to the Midwest, and a modest corner shrine was set up in a chapel at St. Henry's seminary in Belleville. Two years later, in 1943, the Oblates began holding a novena in her honor. Schulte, though, was thinking on a far larger scale. He spent years developing the idea of a full shrine before work finally began in 1958. The design fell to two collaborators, the sculptor William C. Severson and the architect Richard Cummings, and together they turned a devotion that had once fit into the corner of a chapel into a landscape that visitors could walk, drive and pray through.

## Bells From a Vanished Church

By 1963 the shrine's reflecting pool was already complete. That September, the Sarasota Herald-Tribune ran a story under the headline "Largest Shrine Is Amid Corn, Wheat," a headline that placed the new shrine among farm fields. The pool carries an unusual inheritance: it holds four large bells that came from a demolished Chicago church, a gift of St. Bruno's Parish, bells originally cast in Germany. Their church is gone, but the bells found a second home beside the water. Around them the grounds filled with places for quiet reflection. There is a church, a natural outdoor amphitheater and the Stations of the Cross, along with a Resurrection Garden and an Annunciation Garden. A depiction of the Lourdes Grotto stands on the grounds, as does a devotional site for Our Lady of Guadalupe.

## A Mile and a Half of Light

Winter is the shrine's most famous season. During Advent and Christmas it hosts the "Way of Lights," an outdoor display of more than 1.5 million colored and white lights stretched along a route a mile and a half long. How you travel it is part of the experience. Some visitors stay in their cars, and in past seasons others have ridden a heated trolley or taken a horse-drawn carriage, moving slowly through the glow on a cold Illinois night. Summer brings a different kind of gathering. During the warmer months, people from around the world come to worship and pray during the Our Lady of the Snows Healing and Hope Novena, filling the grounds that had begun as one priest's long-held idea.

## A Village Around the Shrine

The shrine has grown into something close to a small community of its own. Beyond its gardens it includes the 52-room Shrine Guesthouse, an apartment complex for retired people, a residence for the Oblates, a visitors and conference center and a large gift shop, and its facilities can be rented for weddings, conferences, conventions and other events. Not everything has lasted. The Shrine Restaurant & Banquet Center closed in 2020 as a result of the COVID-19 pandemic, shortly after a $70,000 renovation earlier that year, while the Shrine Guesthouse had its interior repainted in a renovation in January 2021. The Oblates, led at the shrine by its director, Father Mike Powell, OMI, run youth ministry, adult spirituality, Hispanic ministry, liturgy and events programs throughout the year for pilgrims from the local community, the wider Midwest and across North America.

## Flight Context

The National Shrine of Our Lady of the Snows lies at approximately 38.5612 N, 90.0923 W in Belleville, Illinois, on the Illinois side of the St. Louis metro area. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for the shrine's reflecting pool and landscaped grounds among the surrounding suburbs; the Mississippi River and the Gateway Arch lie about 6.7 miles to the northwest. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is roughly 3.5 miles to the west, Scott AFB/MidAmerica St. Louis Airport (KBLV) is about 14 miles to the east, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 20 miles to the northwest. The area sits beneath busy St. Louis terminal airspace, so coordinate with ATC. Evening flights during the Advent and Christmas season may show the Way of Lights display glowing on the grounds; summer haze can reduce visibility over the river valley.
