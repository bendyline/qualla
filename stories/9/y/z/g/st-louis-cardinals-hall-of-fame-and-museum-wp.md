# St. Louis Cardinals Hall of Fame and Museum

Across Clark Street from Busch Stadium, the St. Louis Cardinals Hall of Fame and Museum in Ballpark Village holds more than 25,000 pieces of memorabilia, and inducts club greats through a blend of expert selection and fan voting.

## Introduction

Babe Ruth's autograph appears on a baseball celebrating his own team's defeat. The ball was signed by the Cardinals team that won the 1926 World Series, and among the signatures is Ruth's, who had played for the opponent, the New York Yankees. It is one of the treasures at the St. Louis Cardinals Hall of Fame and Museum, which tells the story of the franchise's players, personnel and history from inside Ballpark Village, the mixed-use development beside Busch Stadium in downtown St. Louis. The collection runs to more than 25,000 pieces of memorabilia, the largest of any Major League club and second only to the National Baseball Hall of Fame.

## From Bowling Pins to Ballpark Village

The Cardinals' hall of fame once had an unlikely roommate. It was housed in downtown St. Louis in the same building as the International Bowling Museum and the World Bowling Writers International Bowling Hall of Fame, near the sites of both the old and new Busch Stadiums. When the bowling museum closed its St. Louis site in November 2008 and moved to Arlington, Texas, the Cardinals museum closed too and suspended public operations. Its staff used the pause to design a new hall of fame and museum. The team moved it to Ballpark Village, across Clark Street from Busch Stadium, and it opened there in 2014, putting the franchise's history within a short walk of the field where the current team plays.

## Red Ribbons and Red Jackets

In January 2014 Cardinals chairman and chief executive officer William DeWitt Jr. announced an annual, formal induction process. The 22 former Cardinals already enshrined at the National Baseball Hall of Fame in Cooperstown, or whose numbers the team had retired, entered the inaugural class automatically when the museum was dedicated that spring. On August 16, 2014, the first four honorees chosen under the new process were enshrined, bringing the first roll to 26 members. From then on the rules were set. A player needs three seasons with the Cardinals and at least three years out of Major League Baseball, and those retired 40 or more years count as veterans. Each year a "Red Ribbon" committee of baseball experts selects one veteran for induction and nominates a ballot of players (6 to 10 under the original plan, four modern players in 2026) for a spring online fan vote at cardinals.com, followed by a summer enshrinement ceremony.

## The Honor Freese Turned Down

Fan voting originally sent the top two vote-getters into the hall, and beginning in 2021 only one modern player was to be elected each year, though in 2026 the fans' top two, Yadier Molina and Albert Pujols, were both elected. The team may also elect people who served in other roles, such as coaches, broadcasters and front office personnel. Every member is honored with a plaque made by the same company that creates Cooperstown's, and receives a red jacket to be worn at all official team ceremonies and functions. Getting on the ballot is no guarantee. Joaquín Andújar, Steve Carlton, George Hendrick, Brian Jordan and Lee Smith have all been nominated without being inducted. One vote produced a genuine surprise. After receiving the most fan votes on the 2023 ballot, David Freese later declined his induction into the Cardinals Hall of Fame. Even so, the roll keeps growing, and with the 2026 class of Molina, Pujols and the veteran Bill Sherdel it now numbers 61 members.

## Rings, Programs and a Trophy

The museum's displays range across the Cardinals' former ballparks, championship teams, uniform history and current players, and they also remember the St. Louis Browns and the Negro League St. Louis Stars. Visitors can record their own broadcast call of a classic Cardinals play or hold a bat that a Cardinals star of the past once used in a game. Some of the most striking pieces are championship memorabilia. A 1931 World Series ring awarded to Sparky Adams still sits in its original green case. A program survives from 1944, the first and only all-St. Louis World Series, played against the Browns, who are now the Baltimore Orioles. The original Commissioner's Trophy awarded to the 1967 champions, who defeated the Boston Red Sox, is here too. A special exhibit area changes each season, with past subjects including the Cardinal farm system, Stan Musial's life and career, the team's radio partnership with KMOX and the 2011 World Series team, and home game weekends bring autograph sessions and panels with former players and staff.

## Flight Context

The Cardinals Hall of Fame and Museum is located at approximately 38.6251 N, 90.1935 W in Ballpark Village, directly north of Busch Stadium across Clark Street in downtown St. Louis. From the air, the stadium's open bowl is the easiest landmark, with the Gateway Arch about half a mile to the east beside the Mississippi River. A viewing altitude of 2,000-3,000 feet AGL gives a clear view of the stadium, Ballpark Village and the Arch together. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) is about 4 miles to the south-southeast across the river, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 13 miles to the northwest. Downtown lies beneath busy St. Louis terminal airspace, so coordinate with ATC, and be aware of stadium flight restrictions during games and major events. Summer haze can reduce visibility.
