# Powell Hall

Built in 1925 as a Rapp & Rapp movie palace for vaudeville and films, St. Louis's Powell Hall became home to the St. Louis Symphony in 1968 and reopened in 2025 after its first major revitalization in more than half a century.

**Location:** 38.6402, -90.2301

## Introduction

The last film to play the old St. Louis Theatre was The Sound of Music, in 1966. It was a fitting farewell. After four decades of vaudeville acts and motion pictures, the theater in what is now St. Louis's Grand Center Arts District was about to give itself over entirely to music, as the new home of the St. Louis Symphony. Renamed Powell Symphony Hall, and now simply Powell Hall, it has hosted the orchestra since 1968, and in September 2025 it reopened after its first major revitalization since 1968.

## A Movie Palace for Four Thousand

The building began life in 1925 as the St. Louis Theatre, designed by the Chicago architectural firm of Rapp & Rapp. It was built big, with 4,100 seats, nearly twice as many as the concert hall holds today, and for its first 40 years it served as a stage for live vaudeville as well as a screen for motion pictures. That double life ended in 1966, when the final reel of The Sound of Music ran through the projector and the building's next act began. Today the building is a contributing property of the Midtown Historic District on the National Register of Historic Places. It is also part of the Grand Center Arts District, St. Louis's cluster of cultural venues.

## Curtain Up for the Symphony

In 1966 the St. Louis Symphony Society acquired the theater for $500,000, through a gift from Oscar Johnson Jr. It was renamed Powell Symphony Hall after Walter S. Powell, a local St. Louis businessman whose widow, Helen Lamb Powell, left the Symphony Society a $1 million charitable trust that helped endow the hall. Turning a movie palace into a concert hall took time and money. The symphony spent an additional $2 million to update and renovate the theater, and in January 1968 the hall reopened as the new home of the St. Louis Symphony Orchestra. Music has not been its only use. Powell Hall is also home to the St. Louis Speakers Series, now presented by Clayco, which offers seven events each season; its 2026/27 season is the series' 28th.

## A Rescue From the Brink

One donor looms large in the orchestra's recent history. In 2000, Jack C. Taylor, the founder of Enterprise Rent-A-Car and a prolific supporter of St. Louis arts organizations, contributed $40 million to help establish the orchestra's endowment trust, a donation noted at the time as the largest ever given to an American orchestra. According to an SLSO statement, the gift saved the institution "from the brink of bankruptcy." Taylor also founded the Crawford Taylor Foundation, which made a $30 million gift to support the renovation and expansion of Powell Hall. On September 18, 2024, symphony leaders announced that the expansion would be named the Jack C. Taylor Music Center, a name that takes in Powell Hall itself along with a 64,000-square-foot expansion that includes an entrance lobby, an education center and a backstage wing for performers.

## Rebuilt Around the Music

In January 2023 the St. Louis Symphony announced a major revitalization of Powell Hall, the first since 1968, with work starting that spring. "We will preserve the history of Powell Hall while creating a state-of-the-art center for community, innovation, and powerful music experiences," said SLSO President and CEO Marie-Hélène Bernard. The plans included a three-story lobby with terraces overlooking it, far more backstage space for musicians, from dressing rooms and a lounge to a music library, practice rooms and recording studios, plus more accessible entryways and outdoor green spaces where audiences could gather. During construction the orchestra took its music on the road, performing mostly at the Stifel Theatre, Lindenwood University's J. Scheidegger Center for the Arts and UMSL's Touhill Performing Arts Center. New seats reduced the hall's capacity by about 500, to roughly 2,150, in line with halls for similarly sized American orchestras. The revitalized Powell Hall opened in September 2025.

## Flight Context

Powell Hall stands at approximately 38.6402 N, 90.2301 W in the Grand Center Arts District of midtown St. Louis. From the air, look for the cluster of theaters and institutional buildings along Grand Boulevard; the Fox Theatre is only a couple of blocks to the south-southwest and the Saint Louis University campus lies just beyond it. The Gateway Arch is about 2.7 miles to the east-southeast, giving a reliable reference on the riverfront. A viewing altitude of 2,000-3,000 feet AGL shows the arts district and the new expansion wing clearly. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 10.5 miles to the northwest and St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) about 6 miles to the southeast across the Mississippi. The area lies beneath busy St. Louis terminal airspace, so coordinate with ATC; summer haze can soften visibility.
