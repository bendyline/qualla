# Chain of Rocks Bridge

A former Route 66 crossing with a 22-degree kink in the middle of the Mississippi, the old Chain of Rocks Bridge survived decades of abandonment to become one of the St. Louis area's walking and cycling routes over the river.

**Location:** 38.7606, -90.1764

## Introduction

Halfway across the Mississippi River, the old Chain of Rocks Bridge changes its mind. The deck swings through a 22-degree bend at the middle of the crossing, a kink that was found to be necessary during construction because of a water intake in the river and because parts of the bedrock could not carry the bridge's weight. Drivers on U.S. Route 66 once steered through that turn above the water. Today the traffic is on foot and on bicycles, and the bend remains the bridge's most notable feature.

## Rocks That Nearly Stopped a River

The bridge takes its name from the Chain of Rocks, a large shoal of rocky rapids that made this stretch of the Mississippi extremely dangerous to navigate. The hazard ran deeper than a single riffle. The Chain of Rocks Reach was a 17-mile series of rock ledges beginning just north of St. Louis, and after 1940 it was the only impediment left to a safe, reliable nine-foot navigation channel all the way from St. Paul, Minnesota, to New Orleans. In the late 1940s and early 1950s, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers solved the problem by going around it, digging an 8.4-mile canal to bypass the reach. To keep enough depth in the pool below the old Lock and Dam 26, engineers built a fixed low-water dam, Dam No. 27, just downstream of the old bridge, and installed Lock No. 27 at the canal's south end.

## Water for a City

Long before the canal, the riverbank here had another job. In 1865 the area was chosen as the site for the St. Louis waterworks. Construction began in 1887, and the water plant opened in 1894. A filter plant followed in 1915, and at the time it was built it was the largest filter plant in the world. Water infrastructure shaped the bridge itself. A water intake in the river was one of the reasons the builders could not run a straight line from bank to bank, and a water intake stands in the river beside one of the bridge's spans. The eastern end of the crossing lands on Chouteau Island, part of Madison, Illinois, while the western end rests on the Missouri shore.

## Route 66, Then Silence

The bridge opened as a motor route and for a time carried U.S. Route 66 across the Mississippi. In 1966 a new bridge was built immediately to its north to carry Interstate 270, and in February 1970 the old Chain of Rocks Bridge was closed. For nearly three decades its fate was uncertain, and demolition seemed the most likely end. The high cost of tearing it down kept delaying that end until a new use could be found. In those years the empty span developed a reputation for crime and violence. In April 1991, sisters Julie and Robin Kerry were murdered there, a loss that is still part of the bridge's story. Hollywood found its own use for the closed structure. In 1980 it served as a filming site for Escape from New York, released the following year, standing in as the "69th Street Bridge."

## A Bridge for Walkers

Rescue came in 1998, when the bridge was leased to Trailnet, a local trails group. Roughly $4.5 million to $5 million went into renovating it for pedestrians and cyclists, and in 2006 it was added to the National Register of Historic Places. In December 2014, Trailnet transferred the lease to the Great Rivers Greenway District, which has maintained the bridge and the Missouri-side grounds ever since. The crossing now marks the northern point of the Mississippi Greenway. Visitors reach it by the old approach, now called Chain of Rocks Road, which ends near Roman Road, where parking waits at the start of the walk. In August 2021 the National Park Service awarded a $990,000 grant toward a visitor-area project estimated at nearly $5 million, and on April 14, 2024, Great Rivers Greenway celebrated the opening of the 16-acre Chain of Rocks Park on the Missouri side, with a gated parking lot, picnic pavilion, event lawn, restrooms and drinking fountains, restored wetlands and woodlands, and direct access to the bridge.

## Flight Context

The old Chain of Rocks Bridge crosses the Mississippi at 38.7606°N, 90.1764°W on the northern edge of St. Louis, linking the Missouri shore with Chouteau Island in Illinois. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, the 22-degree bend in the middle of the span is unmistakable, and the New Chain of Rocks Bridge carrying Interstate 270 runs immediately to the north for comparison. Look for the water intake structures in the river beside the bridge and the low-water Dam No. 27 just downstream. The Gateway Arch lies about 9 miles to the south along the river. St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN) is roughly 11 miles to the northeast, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) about 10 miles to the west; the bridge sits beneath the St. Louis Class B airspace, so coordinate with approach control. Morning fog can linger over the river in spring and fall.
