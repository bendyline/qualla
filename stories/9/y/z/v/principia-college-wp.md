# Principia College

On the Mississippi bluffs at Elsah, a college founded to serve Christian Science became what is often called architect Bernard Maybeck's largest commission, complete with a test building students still call the "Mistake House."

**Location:** 38.9489, -90.3489

**Categories:** Liberal arts colleges in Illinois, Christian Science in Illinois, Education in Jersey County, Illinois, Buildings and structures in Jersey County, Illinois, Architecture

## Introduction

Before Bernard Maybeck trusted his designs to a campus on the Mississippi River bluffs, he tried them out on a small building first. There he experimented with forms and materials to make sure they would succeed, and Principians came to know the little structure, affectionately, as the "Mistake House." What rose around it at Principia College in Elsah, Illinois, is often described as the largest commission of Maybeck's career. Today a portion of the 2,600-acre campus, perched between Alton and Grafton about 25 miles north of St. Louis, is a National Historic Landmark District.

## A College for a Cause

The college grew out of The Principia, the school Mary Kimball Morgan founded in 1898. As her school expanded, Mary Baker Eddy, the founder of Christian Science, approved its reference as a Christian Science school. Morgan founded the college itself in 1912 with the purpose of "serving the Cause of Christian Science." She drew a careful line in her book Education at the Principia: "Although the College is not affiliated with the Christian Science Church, the practice of Christian Science is the cornerstone of campus life." Christian Science is not taught as a subject, but its teachings shape community life. The first Upper School class graduated in 1906, and from that class a junior college was established whose first alumni graduated in 1917. The Higher Learning Commission has accredited Principia College since 1923.

## Maybeck on the Bluffs

Principia commissioned Maybeck, a former University of California, Berkeley professor and AIA Gold Medal winner, to design a new campus in Elsah, and by 1931 ground was broken. While the move was under way, guests including Morgan and Maybeck stayed at Eliestoun House, a residence on the grounds designed by Alexander Wadsworth Longfellow Jr. and completed in 1890. The college graduated its first class as a full four-year institution in 1934 and officially moved to Elsah in 1935. Maybeck designed six of the ten dormitories, Anderson Hall, Rackham Court, Howard House, Sylvester House, Buck House and Brooks House, along with the chapel, and he set out to give each of them a different architectural style and building technique. On April 19, 1993, the U.S. Department of the Interior designated about 300 acres of the campus a National Historic Landmark, and for the 2018 Illinois Bicentennial the campus was named one of the Illinois 200 Great Places by AIA Illinois.

## A Stage for Big Voices

For a small school, Principia has drawn remarkable visitors. Its Public Affairs Conference, held every year since 1939, is one of the oldest student-led conferences in America, and an International Perspectives Conference takes on global issues such as human rights in Africa. The college's Speaker Series has brought Maya Angelou, Elie Wiesel, Coretta Scott King, David McCullough and Margaret Thatcher to the bluffs, along with former U.S. presidents Jimmy Carter and George H. W. Bush, Barack Obama, then a U.S. senator, and retired four-star general Colin Powell. The campus once had a chance at a very different future, too. It was considered as the site for the United States Air Force Academy, though the Air Force ultimately chose Colorado Springs, Colorado.

## Solar Cars and a Perfect Season

Principia's most distinguished technical program is its study of solar energy. The college has competed in solar car world events since 1995, finishing second in the North American Solar Challenge of 2008 and seventh in the World Solar Challenge of 2009. Its athletes, the Panthers, compete in NCAA Division III; the panther replaced the college's former "Indian" mascot in 1984. In 2022 the men's rugby team completed an undefeated season by winning the Division II National Collegiate Rugby Championship XVs title, beating Indiana University of Pennsylvania 47–16 in the final. Alumni include Robert Duvall, who earned his B.A. in 1953 and later won the Academy Award for Best Actor for Tender Mercies, and Ketti Frings, winner of the 1958 Pulitzer Prize for Drama for Look Homeward, Angel.

## Opening the Gates

The 21st century has asked Principia to change. Enrollment has declined along with the dwindling number of Christian Scientists, and the college no longer requires its students or their parents to be Christian Scientists or church members. As of 2024 it began accepting students with no affiliation with Christian Science at all. Its student body now represents 26 countries and a variety of faith backgrounds. The finances have been volatile as well: the endowment fell by more than $100 million in the decade before 2018, stood at $696.2 million as of June 2020, and was listed at $963.4 million in 2025. The Maybeck buildings still stand on the bluffs above the Mississippi, and the Mistake House recalls the architect's willingness to test an idea before committing to it.

## Flight Context

Principia College lies at approximately 38.95 N, 90.35 W on the Illinois bluffs above the Mississippi River at Elsah, between Alton to the east and Grafton to the west. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL, follow the Great River Road (Illinois Route 100) along the base of the bluffs; the campus buildings sit on the high ground above the river, and the confluence of the Illinois and Mississippi at Grafton lies a few miles upstream. St. Charles County Smartt Airport (KSET) is about 4 nm west-southwest across the river, St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) about 12 nm south, and St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN) about 15 nm east. Stay clear of the St. Louis Class B airspace to the south. Expect summer haze and humid conditions over the river valley.
