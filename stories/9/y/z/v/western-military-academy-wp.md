# Western Military Academy

For 92 years a boys' military school in Upper Alton, Illinois, shaped cadets who went on to run CBS, win the Medal of Honor and fly the Enola Gay, until the Vietnam era emptied its barracks.

**Location:** 38.9076, -90.1440

**Categories:** Defunct United States military academies, Educational institutions established in 1879, 1879 establishments in Illinois, Alton, Illinois, Military history

## Introduction

Two cadets who marched across the same campus in Upper Alton, Illinois, a year apart, would become two of the most decorated American pilots of World War II. Edward O'Hare, class of 1932, earned the Medal of Honor in naval air combat before he was killed in action in 1943; Chicago's O'Hare International Airport carries his name. Paul Tibbets, class of 1933, piloted the bomber Enola Gay on August 6, 1945, when it dropped the first atomic bomb on Hiroshima, Japan. Both were products of Western Military Academy, a private boarding school whose motto promised "a sound mind in a sound body."

## A School for the Western Frontier

Edward Wyman, an 1835 Amherst graduate and a respected educator in the St. Louis public schools, opened a boarding school for boys in Upper Alton in 1879. A school circular explained his reasoning: the region then called the western United States needed a "boarding school for the proper education of young men." In 1887 Wyman hired Albert M. Jackson, a Princeton graduate who had spent two years teaching mathematics and Latin at Blair Academy in New Jersey. When Wyman died in 1888, ownership passed to Col. Willis Brown, with Jackson as principal, and during those years the school took the name Western Military Academy and introduced military training. In 1896 Jackson and the academy's financial officer, George D. Eaton, bought the school. The Jackson family would own it for the rest of its life, and Jackson's son, Col. R. L. Jackson, class of 1906, served as superintendent from 1919 into the 1950s.

## Out of the Ashes

In February 1903, fire destroyed the administration building and the main barracks, closing the academy for the rest of the term. It came back that September with a new administration building, two new barracks and 132 students, and a third barracks went up before the year was out. Recognition followed. The War Department designated Western an Honor Military School, and by 1920 its standing gave it the right to one appointment without examination to both the Regular Army and West Point. By 1924 the last two barracks were finished, giving the campus the look it kept until the end, and the first yearbook, The Recall, appeared. From 1912 through the 1920s the school ran at a full complement of more than 300 cadets. William S. Paley, class of 1918 and later chairman of the Columbia Broadcasting System, remembered his time there as the "turning point of my life."

## The Memorial Gate

The school kept careful records of its graduates at war. Of the 402 alumni who graduated after 1909, 295, or 73 percent, served in World War I; four died in service, and others came home listed as "wounded" or "lightly gassed." World War II drew far more. Over 1,000 graduates, more than half of all alumni at the time, served, and more than 40 were killed in action. A partial count of their decorations includes one Medal of Honor, four Distinguished Service Crosses, two Navy Crosses, 23 Silver Stars and 15 Distinguished Flying Crosses. The records for Korea and Vietnam are incomplete. The school's answer to that cost stands in stone: the front gate of the campus is the Memorial Gate, named for the alumni who died in their country's service.

## Sinatra, Dempsey and Thanksgiving Football

Western believed that amusements, "indulged in to a reasonable extent," improved a cadet's deportment, and its reputation brought remarkable guests. Hall of Famers Rogers Hornsby, Hank Greenberg and Dizzy Dean talked baseball with the cadets. Jack Dempsey addressed the Corps, Tommy Dorsey and Frank Sinatra performed, and Amelia Earhart spoke. Football, labeled "The King of Sports" in a 1930s catalog, was the school's centerpiece. The school fielded four teams so boys of every size could play, and for most of its history well over half the Corps suited up. According to the St. Louis Globe-Democrat, Western's 1895 game against Smith Academy was the first high school football game ever played in St. Louis. From 1904, its Thanksgiving Day game against Alton High School became, in the Alton Evening Telegraph's words, "the biggest event on the Alton sports calendar," drawing thousands until the rivalry ended in 1952.

## The Last Commencement

From 1940 to 1970, enrollment held steady between 300 and 325, and the academy kept a waiting list through the 1960s. Then the ground shifted quickly. Rising costs and inflation pressed on the budget, and the anti-military sentiment stirred by the Vietnam War drove families away. In 1967-68 the barracks were full, with 325 cadets. Three years later the 1971 yearbook counted just 154. In June 1971, Western held its 92nd and final commencement and closed. The campus, now part of a historic district on the National Register of Historic Places, later housed the Mississippi Valley Christian School, and by 2026 the nearly 40-acre property was listed for sale. Among the names the academy left behind are the painter Thomas Hart Benton, who attended but did not graduate, Mexican racing driver Pedro Rodríguez, Illinois governor John Stelle, alongside the two aviators of the classes of 1932 and 1933.

## Flight Context

The former Western Military Academy campus lies at 38.9076°N, 90.1440°W in Upper Alton, Illinois, north of St. Louis. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, look for the compact former campus amid Alton's residential streets, with the Mississippi River and Alton's riverfront to the south and west. Nearby airports: St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN), about 5 nm east-southeast, is the closest airport; St. Charles County Smartt Airport (KSET) lies about 13 nm west across the river, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) about 14 nm southwest. River-valley haze and summer thunderstorms are common.
