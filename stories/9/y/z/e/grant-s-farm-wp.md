# Grant's Farm

What is billed as the only surviving building hand-built by a future U.S. president stands on a Busch family estate outside St. Louis, where a wedding gift of eighty acres became a free animal park famous for its Clydesdales.

**Location:** 38.5481, -90.3575

**Categories:** Anheuser-Busch, Zoos in Missouri, Ulysses S. Grant, Tourist attractions in St. Louis, 1954 establishments in Missouri

## Introduction

Long before he commanded armies or took the oath of office, Ulysses S. Grant built a log cabin with his own hands. His family lived in it for barely a season, yet the cabin survived him by more than a century. Today it stands on Grant's Farm in Grantwood Village, Missouri, described as the only remaining structure hand-built by a U.S. president before he assumed office. Its neighbors are not what you would expect. Camels, emus and bison wander a former deer park nearby, and the Budweiser Clydesdales stand in their own barn. The cabin and the horses share this ground because of a wedding gift, a brewing dynasty, and a family dispute in which one bid topped another by a single dollar.

## Eighty Acres and a Wedding

The story begins at Jefferson Barracks, where the young Grant was assigned in 1843 after West Point. At the academy he had roomed with Julia Dent's brother, and the Dent family farm lay just five miles from the post. Julia's father, Colonel Frederick Dent, was a plantation owner who held hundreds of acres along Gravois Creek, ten miles southwest of St. Louis, and enslaved people worked that land for him. Grant and Julia married in 1848, and her father gave the couple 80 acres of the farm as a wedding present, on what is now Rock Hill Road. The Dent family home, called White Haven, became Grant's residence in the years between the Mexican and Civil Wars. By 1871, when the magazine Every Saturday published an engraving of the place, the caption already called it "The Old Dent Homestead on President Grant's Farm."

## A House Called Hardscrabble

Grant raised his cabin on the wedding land himself, working on it from 1855 into 1856. There was nothing grand about it. The place was called Hardscrabble, and life there seems to have matched the name. Grant moved his family into the log cabin in late 1856, but within three months Julia, unhappy with the living conditions, moved everyone back to White Haven. The cabin's own journey was just beginning. It was shown at the 1904 World's Fair in St. Louis, and afterward it was relocated to Grant's Farm. A photograph from 1912 labels it simply "Grant's Log Cabin on Busch Farm." Standing before its rough walls today, you are looking at a home built by Grant's own labor, years before anyone imagined where his life would lead.

## From Deer Park to Open Gate

The Busch family has owned the property since the early 1900s. The family once controlled the Anheuser-Busch Brewing Company, which was sold to InBev in 2008, but the farm stayed in Busch hands. At first the land served as the family's private deer park. Since 1954 it has been an animal reserve, and it is open to the public with free general admission, and a parking fee helps maintain the farm and care for the animals. Most visitors meet the residents from a tram that winds through the old deer park, where buffalo, emus, camels, donkeys, goats and peacocks share the grounds and macaws flash their colors. The iconic Budweiser Clydesdales keep to their own barn and pastures nearby. The Busch mansion, known as the "Big House," stands on the property as well, alongside the Bauernhof beer garden.

## A Dollar's Difference

Keeping a family estate free and open has not always been simple. The Saint Louis Zoo Association offered to buy Grant's Farm, but in March 2016 it withdrew that offer, citing disagreement within the Busch family as one of the primary reasons. The dispute continued into April, when Billy Busch outbid his siblings' offer by exactly one dollar, proposing $26,000,001 for the farm along with $8 million to purchase the family mansion. A resolution came in 2017, when five family members, Andrew Busch, Peter Busch, Robert Hermann Jr., Trudy Valentine and Beatrice von Gontard, announced a plan to purchase the farm with the aim of keeping it free and open to the public. So the gate stays open, the tram keeps rolling past the camels, and the cabin a future president built by hand still waits at the end of the path.

## Flight Context

Grant's Farm sits at 38.548N, 90.358W in Grantwood Village, in south St. Louis County, Missouri. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, look for a large wooded tract broken by open pastures and paddocks amid the surrounding suburbs; the Mississippi River and the Jefferson Barracks area lie several miles to the east and southeast. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 14 miles to the north, and its Class B airspace covers much of the metro area. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) lies across the Mississippi in Cahokia, Illinois, roughly 11 miles to the east, and Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) is about 18 miles to the west-northwest. Summer haze over the river valley can soften visibility; the pastures stand out best in clear spring or autumn light.
