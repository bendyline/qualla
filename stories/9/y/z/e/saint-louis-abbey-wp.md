# Saint Louis Abbey

Three English monks arrived in St. Louis in 1955 to found a Benedictine school, and their community grew into an abbey whose floating tiers of white concrete arches became a landmark of modern church architecture.

**Location:** 38.6442, -90.4789

## Introduction

On a green campus in Creve Coeur, Missouri, three tiers of whitewashed concrete arches rise in a circle, the highest forming a bell tower, and the whole structure seems to float upward from the grass beneath it. This is the Abbey Church of Saint Louis Abbey, home to a community of English Benedictine monks in suburban St. Louis County. The building looks like a leap into the future, yet the life inside follows the old Benedictine discipline of prayer and work: the Divine Office prayed five times a day, daily Masses in English and Latin, and hours spent in parishes and classrooms. It all began with three men who crossed the Atlantic from Yorkshire.

## Three Monks from Yorkshire

In 1955 Ampleforth Abbey in North Yorkshire, England, dispatched three monks to plant a new foundation in St. Louis. They came at the invitation of Cardinal Joseph E. Ritter and a group of prominent St. Louis lay Catholics, who wanted a boys' school in their community run according to the English Benedictine educational tradition. Father Columba Cary-Elwes, Father Luke Rigby and Father Timothy Horner arrived in the summer of 1955 and set about forming a new monastic community. Father Columba became its founding prior, and Father Timothy became the founding headmaster of the Saint Louis Priory School. Horner later served as an editor of the widely used 1980 edition of the Rule of Saint Benedict, and he wrote an account of those early years called In Good Soil.

## From Priory to Abbey

Father Columba led the community until 1967, when he departed for missionary work in Africa, and Father Luke succeeded him as prior. The St. Louis Priory became independent of Ampleforth in 1973, and in 1989 it was elevated to an abbey, with Luke Rigby elected as its first abbot. His successor, Father Thomas Frerking, brought an unusual range to the office: a Rhodes Scholar, a former headmaster of the Priory School and a Thomist scholar, he was elected on July 12, 1995, and served until retiring in June 2018. That month, on June 8, Father Gregory Mohrman, a member of the Priory School's class of 1976, was announced as abbot. The school had come full circle, producing a leader for the monks who founded it. As of 2020, the abbey was home to 26 monks.

## Arches That Seem to Float

The Abbey Church, also known as the Church of St. Mary and St. Louis and as the Priory Chapel, was completed in 1962. Gyo Obata of Hellmuth, Obata and Kassabaum designed it, with the Italian architect and engineer Pier Luigi Nervi serving as consultant. The commission became an important, name-making project for HOK, a firm that has since grown into one of the largest architectural practices in the world. The church's circular facade is built from three tiers of thin-poured concrete parabolic arches, each one whitewashed a brilliant white, and the top tier doubles as the bell tower. The arches are faced with dark insulated-fiberglass polyester window walls, which look somber from the lawn but create a meditative translucency when viewed from within.

## Old Treasures, New Walls

For all its modernity, the church shelters objects far older than St. Louis itself. A 14th-century sculpture of the Madonna and Christ child stands inside, along with a 17th-century holy water font in the Della Robbia style. Newer sacred art comes from artists in the United States, Great Britain, Spain and France, and music comes from a tracker action organ built by Gregor Hradetzky of Krems. Outside, life-size figures keep watch over the grounds: a Saint Benedict by the Lithuanian-born artist Wiktor Szostalo, and a Virgin Mary, titled "Holy Blessed Virgin Mother Mary, Our Lady of Grace," by the American sculptor Philip Howie. The monastery itself dates to the 1950s but was rebuilt and expanded in 2000 and 2001.

## Rugby Coaches in Habits

The monks' work reaches well beyond the chapel. They serve two parishes under the abbey's pastoral care and run the Saint Louis Priory School, which shares the 150-acre campus. Many brothers teach in its classrooms, covering theology, Latin, fine arts, English, mathematics and science, and some coach the school's tennis and rugby teams. Father J. Augustine Wetta, the abbey's director of vocations, is an author, public speaker and essayist whose books include Humility Rules and The Eighth Arrow: Odysseus in the Underworld. In keeping with the Benedictine tradition of hospitality to travelers, the abbey keeps a guest wing on the south side of the monastery, with five rooms and two gardens, and the brethren make themselves available to guests to hear confessions or offer counsel.

## Flight Context

Saint Louis Abbey stands at 38.644N, 90.479W in Creve Coeur, in west St. Louis County, Missouri. The circular white Abbey Church, with its stacked tiers of parabolic arches topped by a bell tower, is a distinctive target on a large green 150-acre campus surrounded by suburban neighborhoods; it shows best from 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL in good light. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is about 9 miles to the northeast, and the abbey sits beneath the Lambert Class B airspace, so coordinate accordingly. Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) is roughly 9 miles to the west. Low sun in early morning or late afternoon throws shadows through the arches and makes the church easier to spot.
