# Castlewood State Park

On the Meramec River west of St. Louis, a Prohibition-era resort that once drew about 10,000 weekend visitors by train has faded into foundations in the woods, leaving an 1,818-acre state park of trails and river access where swift currents and sudden drop-offs demand respect.

**Location:** 38.5511, -90.5411

**Categories:** State parks of Missouri, Protected areas established in 1974, Protected areas of St. Louis County, Missouri, 1974 establishments in Missouri, Parks established in the 1970s

## Introduction

On summer weekends in the 1920s, the trains kept coming. The Missouri Pacific Railroad ran regular service from St. Louis to a stretch of the Meramec River known as Lincoln Beach, and at its height the resort drew around 10,000 visitors on a summer weekend. They came for the river, the dance halls and, during Prohibition, a private club that served liquor illegally. Today almost all of it has vanished into the woods of Castlewood State Park, an 1,818-acre preserve that straddles the Meramec in St. Louis County, Missouri.

## Lincoln Beach and the Lone Wolf Club

Lincoln Beach operated from about 1915 into the 1940s, and it was at its most popular in the 1920s. By one account there were a couple hundred buildings here, ranging from clubhouses to dance halls to general stores. The popular Lincoln Lodge welcomed visitors, while the Lone Wolf Club offered something more discreet: it was a private speakeasy, serving liquor illegally during the Prohibition years of 1920 to 1933. For city dwellers, it was an escape to the river that was only a train ride away.

## When the Crowds Stopped Coming

The resort's decline came with modern comforts. After World War II, visitor numbers fell as air conditioning made summers in the city more bearable and automobile travel reduced the dependence on railroad destinations. The buildings that had served the crowds were left to the river, and over the decades floods, sediment and the returning forest swallowed most of them. By the 21st century only a few foundations and ruins survived, scattered in the woods for hikers to discover. In 1974, the land entered a new chapter when it was established as a Missouri state park.

## Trails on Both Banks

Castlewood today is a place for moving under your own power. The park offers more than 26 miles of trails for hiking, mountain biking and horseback riding, along with fishing and boating on the Meramec. The most visited section lies on the north side of the river. The acreage on the south bank is reached through Lone Elk County Park and includes the World Bird Sanctuary. The result is a patchwork of public land where river, forest and trail meet, only a short drive from the suburbs of St. Louis, on the same ground where visitors once arrived by train.

## Respect for the River

The Meramec that drew the crowds is also dangerous. Gary Dyhouse, a retired hydrologist for the Army Corps of Engineers, observed that the river's slope is steeper than that of all other rivers in the area, and Metro West Fire Chief Mike Krause has pointed to sudden drop-offs combined with swift currents as the cause of drownings here. At least a dozen people, many of them teenagers and children, have drowned at the park since 2004. The worst loss came on July 9, 2006, when five young people between the ages of 10 and 17 died during a church outing after they went into the river to help someone in trouble and were swept away. Their courage is part of this place's story, and so is the lesson: admire the Meramec from its banks and trails, and treat its currents with care.

## Flight Context

Castlewood State Park lies at approximately 38.5511° N, 90.5411° W along the Meramec River in western St. Louis County, Missouri, south of Ballwin and just west of Valley Park. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet above ground level, follow the winding Meramec River as it loops through wooded terrain; the park's forested land on both banks stands out against the surrounding suburbs, and Interstate 44 runs to the south. Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) in Chesterfield lies to the northwest, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is to the northeast. The park lies close to or beneath the St. Louis Class B airspace, so check the sectional for shelf floors and communicate with ATC as required. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common, and autumn color along the river valley makes for the best viewing.
