# Kimmswick, Missouri

A river town of about 130 people platted by Theodore Kimm in 1859, Kimmswick sits beside the Mississippi as an enclave of Imperial, home to a historic district, a former Anheuser family estate and a bakery whose towering apple pie commemorates the Great Flood of 1993.

**Location:** 38.3667, -90.3639

**Categories:** Missouri populated places on the Mississippi River, Cities in Jefferson County, Missouri, Cities in Missouri

## Introduction

During the Great Flood of 1993, the Mississippi River crested near 50 feet on the St. Louis gauge and, as the restaurant tells it, crept so high at Kimmswick, Missouri, that it licked the deck of The Blue Owl. Volunteers and the National Guard had built an earthen levee outside the back door, and the levee held. By the restaurant's own telling, it held back the water by just a tenth of an inch, and the bakery commemorated the escape the way bakers do: with a pie. The Levee High Caramel Apple Pecan Pie, piled with over a dozen layers of apples, has become a calling card for a town of about 130 people that has spent more than a century and a half beside the river.

## Mr. Kimm's River Town

Kimmswick takes its name from Theodore Kimm, who platted the town in 1859 and gave it his last name. A post office called Kimmswick was established even earlier, in 1858, and it remains open today. The town sits right next to the Mississippi River and covers just 0.23 square miles. Its neighbor grew larger: nearby Imperial, now an unincorporated census-designated place, was once known as West Kimmswick, and today Kimmswick is an enclave entirely surrounded by it, a small town wrapped inside a larger community. At the 2020 census, 133 people lived here, down from 157 in 2010. The Kimmswick Historic District is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, as is the Windsor Harbor Road Bridge.

## The Brewer's Great-Grandson

One Kimmswick estate carries a famous name. The Anheuser House, an estate built in 1867, overlooks the Mississippi River. The Anheuser family bought it in 1916, and it later belonged to Fred and Mabel Ruth Anheuser, who owned it until their deaths in 1984 and 2000. Fred's great-grandfather was Eberhard Anheuser, who established the E. Anheuser Brewing Company, the business that eventually evolved into Anheuser-Busch. Mabel Ruth Anheuser made sure the house would outlive her family's ownership. She left the home and grounds to the city of Kimmswick, along with funds for the estate's upkeep, reported as $1.5 million. The house now opens on occasion for tours and can be rented for special events.

## Pastries, Pies and High Water

The Blue Owl began with home baking. Mary Hostetter started a baking business in 1983, selling pastries and cheesecakes at weekend craft shows, and in August 1985 she opened her own restaurant in Kimmswick. When the Great Flood of 1993 came, the restaurant was evacuated, and the river, as the restaurant tells it, pushed right up to its deck. The pie that came out of that summer honors the levee that held back the water, and it is built on a scale to match: over a dozen layers of apples piled inside a homemade crust. For a town this small, it has become an unlikely ambassador, a reminder that Kimmswick's history has always been written in relation to the river beside it.

## Flight Context

Kimmswick lies at approximately 38.3667° N, 90.3639° W on the west bank of the Mississippi River in Jefferson County, Missouri, about 20 miles south-southwest of downtown St. Louis, and about 124 meters (407 feet) above sea level. From 2,000 to 2,500 feet above ground level, look for a small grid of streets pressed against the river, surrounded by Imperial; Interstate 55 runs just to the west, and the flat farmland of the Illinois floodplain lies across the river. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) in Cahokia, Illinois, lies to the northeast across the river, and St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) is farther north; the St. Louis Class B airspace overlies much of the metro area, so plan routes and altitudes accordingly. River fog is common on cool mornings, and summer brings haze and thunderstorms.
