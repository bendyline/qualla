# Augusta, Missouri

A German-settled river town of about 270 people in the Missouri Rhineland, Augusta lost its boat landing when the Missouri River moved away, then won a very different distinction in 1980 as the first American Viticultural Area in the United States, about seven months ahead of Napa Valley.

**Location:** 38.5722, -90.8819

**Categories:** Cities in St. Charles County, Missouri, Missouri Rhineland, 1836 establishments in Missouri, Cities in Missouri

## Introduction

Napa Valley had to wait its turn. When the federal government began formally recognizing American wine regions, the first American Viticultural Area it established was not in California but around a town of a few hundred people on the Missouri River. The Augusta AVA was recognized on June 20, 1980, about seven months before Napa Valley. Today Augusta, in St. Charles County, counts 270 residents at the 2020 census, spread across less than a square mile, and it sits at the heart of a region that has come to be called the Missouri Rhineland.

## Mount Pleasant by Another Name

Augusta's beginnings come with more than one version. One account credits its founding in 1836 to Heinrich Knoernschild of Tiefengrün, Germany, and notes stories that another man named the town after his wife, stories that cannot be proven. The town's own history tells it differently: Leonard Harold laid out the town on his land in 1836 and registered it with the courts as Mt. Pleasant. Because the state already had another Mt. Pleasant, the Missouri Legislature required Harold's town to change its name, and why Augusta was chosen is not clear. The German thread runs through every version. By 1831, immigrants inspired by the writer Gottfried Duden were already coming up the river to the Augusta area. The old name survives in one of the town's best-known businesses, Mount Pleasant Winery.

## When the River Walked Away

Augusta was built to face the water. Its landing on the north bank of the Missouri tied the young town to the steamboats and river traffic that carried trade through the region. Then, in the 1870s, the river gradually abandoned the Augusta waterfront, shifting its channel and leaving the town without its boat landing. Augusta adapted. By the 1880s and 1890s it was a confident small town with rail connections, serving the surrounding grape-growing and farming country. That railroad has since given way to something gentler. The Katy Trail, a 240-mile bike and walking path built along a former railroad right-of-way, runs past Augusta, and the town has become a popular stop for cyclists and hikers rolling along the river bottoms.

## Grapes in the Rhineland

Augusta's place in the Missouri Rhineland gives it its identity. Augusta Winery and Mount Pleasant Winery both make their home here, and the recognition of the Augusta viticultural area in 1980 put the town ahead of every other wine region in the country on the federal list. The wider state has grown along with it: vineyards and wineries have multiplied over the decades, and Missouri now has more than 130 wineries. For a place so small, the density of things to do is striking. Visitors will find wineries, antique shops, restaurants, bed-and-breakfasts, a wood shop, a glass studio, a massage therapist, a historic museum and The Augusta Brewery, all within a city covering 0.92 square miles.

## A Bet on the Future

Starting in 2020, Augusta became the focus of a major investment. David Hoffmann and the Hoffmann Family of Companies set out to grow the region into a wine-tourism destination, buying up properties, including four wineries and six vineyards by May 2021, in an investment reported at more than $100 million. The town's population, meanwhile, has edged upward, from 253 at the 2010 census to 270 in 2020. It remains a place where children attend Augusta Elementary School and the public library is a branch of the St. Charles City-County District Library, a working small town as much as a destination. The balance between those two identities, the river village and the wine country showpiece, is the story Augusta is still writing.

## Flight Context

Augusta lies at approximately 38.5722° N, 90.8819° W on the north side of the Missouri River valley in western St. Charles County, Missouri, about 171 meters (561 feet) above sea level. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet above ground level, follow the Missouri River: the town sits at the edge of the wide river bottoms, with wooded hills and vineyards behind it, and the Katy Trail follows the valley along the old railroad line. The city of Washington, Missouri, lies upriver to the southwest on the opposite bank. Washington Regional Airport (KFYG) is a few miles to the west, and Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) in Chesterfield lies to the east; the St. Louis Class B airspace begins farther east. Autumn, with harvest in the vineyards and turning leaves on the bluffs, offers the best visibility and scenery, while summer brings haze and afternoon thunderstorms.
