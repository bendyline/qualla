# Cuba, Missouri

A railroad town named for a Caribbean island, Cuba, Missouri, has hosted Amelia Earhart, Bette Davis and Harry Truman, and now tells those stories on the walls of its buildings as the state's official Route 66 Mural City.

**Location:** 38.0631, -91.4033

**Categories:** Cities in Crawford County, Missouri, Populated places established in 1857, Cities in Missouri, 1857 establishments in Missouri

## Introduction

On September 4, 1928, Amelia Earhart came down out of the sky near a small Missouri town with an unlikely name. She made an emergency landing near Cuba, found no great damage to her Avro airplane and flew on. The town never forgot. Today her visit is painted on a wall along the old Route 66, one of a series of outdoor murals that earned Cuba, Missouri, a title from the state legislature: the Route 66 Mural City. For a railroad town of a few thousand people named after a Caribbean island, it is quite a claim to fame.

## A Town Laid Out for the Rails

Cuba owes its existence to the railroad. The town was platted in 1857, once it was certain the line would be extended to this point, and it was named after the island of Cuba. It grew into the largest city situated entirely within Crawford County, with 3,181 residents at the 2020 census, on a compact 3.2 square miles of land. Cuba sits on Missouri Route 19, about seven miles northeast of Steelville, and Interstate 44 passes along the north side of town. Before the interstate, though, it was Route 66 that carried travelers through, and the Mother Road shaped the town's identity as surely as the railroad created it.

## Famous Visitors

Earhart was not the only well-known name to pass through. In November 1948, the actress Bette Davis stayed at the Southern Hotel in Cuba. A nineteen-year-old local photographer took her picture without permission, and her male companion gave chase; the photographer got away and the photo ran in the Cuba News and Review the next week. Harry S. Truman came through as well, and a mural recalls his 1940 campaign stop as a U.S. senator seeking reelection. While in Cuba he surveyed the property that would eventually become Indian Hills Lake. The lake was originally called Indian Head Lake, because the skull of a Native American was found during its excavation, a stark reminder that people had lived on this land long before the railroad and the highway arrived.

## Stories on the Walls

Cuba decided to tell those stories where everyone driving through could see them. A local beautification group, Viva Cuba, launched an outdoor mural project and consulted with Michelle Loughery, a Canadian muralist who helped shape the vision and painted two of the murals herself. Starting in 2001, the group set out to commission twelve outdoor murals along the Route 66 corridor by the town's 150th anniversary in 2007, and it now maintains fourteen. They memorialize the visits of Truman, Earhart and Davis, among other scenes from the town's past. In recognition of the project, the Missouri legislature designated Cuba the Route 66 Mural City. The murals turned an ordinary drive down Washington Street into a walk through local history.

## Along the Old Road

The mural trail is only part of what draws Route 66 travelers. In 2009, renovations began on the Wagon Wheel's guest cottages and its old cafe building. The Crawford County History Museum is in town, and in front of it on Smith Street stands a Veterans Memorial bearing almost 1,000 names of veterans. Four miles west of Cuba on Route 66, a rocking chair 42 feet high, once billed as the World's Largest Rocking Chair, draws many Route 66 travelers stopping for photos. It is the kind of roadside spectacle that the old highway was built on, and Cuba has made a point of keeping that spirit alive.

## Flight Context

Cuba lies at 38.0631°N, 91.4033°W in Crawford County, Missouri, at about 1,000 feet elevation along Interstate 44 and historic Route 66. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, the interstate running along the north side of town and Missouri Route 19 heading south toward Steelville make Cuba easy to identify. Cuba Municipal Airport (KUBX) sits about a mile northwest of town, a fitting place to land in the community where Amelia Earhart once came down unexpectedly. Sullivan Regional Airport (KUUV) is roughly 15 nm to the northeast, and Rolla National Airport (KVIH) about 18 nm to the west. The surrounding Ozark hills are wooded, with fog common in the valleys on cool mornings.
