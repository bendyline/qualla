# Wagon Wheel Motel, Café and Station

Built of Ozark sandstone in Cuba, Missouri, the Wagon Wheel has been taking in Route 66 travelers since the 1930s, outlasting the interstate bypass under the glow of neon signage first hung in the late 1940s.

**Location:** 38.0646, -91.3966

## Introduction

The neon is still out front. After John and Winifred Mathis bought a cluster of stone cabins in Cuba, Missouri, in 1947, they installed neon signage and renamed the place the Wagon Wheel Motel, and that sign still stands today, calling travelers in off old Route 66. Behind it, set back from the road, stand sandstone buildings that have been taking in overnight guests without interruption since the 1930s, making the Wagon Wheel one of the oldest motels on Route 66.

## Ozark Sandstone, Tudor Style

The story began in 1936 with a café. By 1938 the Wagon Wheel Cabins consisted of three stone lodging buildings, set back 200 feet from the highway, while the restaurant, filling station, and signs stood at the roadside. Stonemason and builder Leo Friesenhan designed them in the Tudor Revival style and built them from local Ozark sandstone. They were generous for their day. Each building held a few rooms plus an office and garages for motorcars, at a time when most tourist courts offered simple single-room cabins. Original owners Margaret and Robert Martin ran the motel until 1946, expanding it to fourteen rooms by converting garages into guest units. Out front, the filling station was leased to Marathon Oil Company from 1936 to 1941 and run by Joe and Clara Slowensky.

## Neon and a New Name

The Mathis years left marks that remain. Besides the neon and the new name, they added two buildings at the rear of the property, one of them providing more rooms for travelers. They also joined the Best Western Motels referral chain early on, back when that network was a loose coalition of independent, locally owned motels. The café took its own path. Bill and Sadie Mae Pratt acquired it and ran it independently of the motel, winning favorable reviews in the Duncan Hines travel guide. In 1954 the popular eatery moved to a larger building east of town as the Wagon Wheel Restaurant, which was sold in December 1956.

## Surviving the Bypass

U.S. 66, known in town as East Washington Street, was the area's main road from 1926 until Interstate 44 bypassed Cuba in 1969. Along Route 66 in Missouri, plenty of motor courts became ghosts after that, and others found new lives serving different purposes. The Wagon Wheel hung on through the slow years. For forty of those years it belonged to Pauline Roberts, later Pauline Armstrong, who bought the property in 1963 and ran it until her death in 2003. For a time she operated a small coffee and doughnut shop from a frame building behind the motel, a little business that closed when her first husband died in 1980. Under her ownership, in 2003, the 5.34-acre Wagon Wheel Motel Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

## What the Register Protects

The historic district captures the whole roadside ensemble. It includes the original 1936 stone café and gas station buildings, all three original stone motel buildings, a concrete block motel building, and the frame building added around 1950 that once held Pauline's coffee shop and later the motel laundry and now serves as a pavilion for gatherings and a dry place to park motorcycles. The listing also covers two historic signs, the neon "Wagon Wheel Motel" and "office" signs, and a concrete gas pump island. The filling station no longer pumps fuel, and the original café building is now a retail shop stocked with jewelry, purses, décor, Route 66 books, T-shirts, and a few original Wagon Wheel antiques.

## Restored Room by Room

After Pauline's death and that of her widower Harold in 2008, the motel kept operating, but the property slowly declined. Connie Echols bought it from Harold's son, James Armstrong, in September 2009 and set about painstakingly restoring each room with new plumbing, wiring, and bedding, finishing in 2010 with modern touches like HDTV and wireless internet across all nineteen rooms. In November 2009 she removed a temporary frame structure that had hidden much of the old fuel station, and a $12,733 federal matching grant in 2012 paid for a new roof on one building. In April 2026 Echols sold the motel to Rich and Christina Dinkela, who say they plan repairs and minor improvements. Guests arrive from all over the world. In 2010 Henry Cole brought a documentary crew from London to film The World's Greatest Motorcycle Rides for the Travel Channel, and in 2011 the Wagon Wheel marked 75 years with a Bonnie and Clyde-themed party, complete with historic cars and period costume.

## Flight Context

The Wagon Wheel sits at 38.0646°N, 91.3966°W on the old Route 66 alignment (East Washington Street) in Cuba, Missouri, with Interstate 44 running past town as a clear visual guide. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL, follow I-44 to Cuba and look for the old highway through town. Cuba Municipal Airport (UBX) is only about 1.5 nm west, Sullivan Regional Airport (UUV) about 15 nm northeast, and Rolla National Airport (KVIH) about 18 nm west-northwest. Clear days make it easy to trace both the old road and the interstate that bypassed it.
