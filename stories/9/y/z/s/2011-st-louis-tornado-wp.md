# 2011 St. Louis tornado

On the evening of Good Friday 2011, an EF4 tornado cut a 21-mile path across the St. Louis suburbs, damaged some 2,700 buildings, shut down Lambert International Airport for nearly a day, and killed no one.

**Location:** 38.7155, -90.4938

**Categories:** F4, EF4 and IF4 tornadoes, Tornadoes in Missouri, Tornadoes in Illinois, Tornadoes of 2011, 2011 natural disasters in the United States, April 2011 in the United States

## Introduction

Just before 8 on the evening of Friday, April 22, 2011, a tornado touched down along the northern edge of Creve Coeur Lake, near Creve Coeur Mill Road. It was Good Friday. At first the storm did little more than strip and break trees. A little over ten minutes later it was crossing the eastern section of St. Louis Lambert International Airport, pushing an aircraft away from its jetway with passengers still aboard. Before it dissipated in Illinois, the tornado had grown into an EF4 with estimated winds of 170 mph, the strongest to hit St. Louis County or City since January 1967, and had damaged some 2,700 buildings along a 21.3-mile track.

## Gathering Strength in Maryland Heights

The early damage along Creve Coeur Mill Road was rated EF0 to EF1, mostly to trees. As the tornado moved east it quickly intensified. Near a golf clubhouse it reached EF2 strength, pushing over a flag pole and a light. It weakened briefly, then strengthened again to EF3 in a subdivision in the northern part of Maryland Heights, where most of the walls of one house collapsed. Around it, other homes took EF2 damage, with lighter EF1 damage along the northern edge of the path. A condominium lost a large portion of its roof and decking. Nearby, a power station and a few retail buildings were nearly flattened at EF3 intensity. All of this happened within the first few miles of the track, before the storm had even reached the airport.

## A Tornado at the Gate

Turning slightly north, the tornado struck the eastern part of Lambert International at around 8:10 p.m. with EF2 winds. One aircraft was shoved away from its jetway with passengers still aboard. On the ramp, the wind drove a baggage conveyor belt into a Southwest Airlines plane. American Airlines reported four of its aircraft damaged, two of them significantly: one had just landed and was taxiing in when 80 mph crosswinds buffeted it, and the other may have suffered damage to its landing gear. Security cameras recorded the storm on the airport's light rail platform and inside its C Concourse, which was heavily damaged. As of 8:54 p.m. the airport was closed indefinitely. The first arrivals came in late on Saturday, April 23, and about 70 percent of flight operations resumed on Easter Sunday, less than 48 hours after the storm. Concourse C stayed closed for almost a year, reopening on April 2, 2012.

## Bridgeton to Granite City

The worst damage of the entire track came in the Bridgeton area, just west of the airport and a few minutes before the storm reached its terminals, where the tornado peaked at EF4 and a few homes were completely leveled. Beyond the airport, northeast of downtown Ferguson, houses took low-end EF2 damage. Farther east the tornado weakened to EF1, lightly damaging Griffith Elementary School, then passed north of the main part of Dellwood, where homes and trees suffered EF1 damage. East of Dellwood it flared again, snapping several large power poles at EF2 strength. Then it crossed the Mississippi River into Illinois, where it tore the roofs off homes in Granite City before finally dissipating. By then it had traveled 21.3 miles across the metropolitan area. Another tornado also hit New Melle in St. Charles County, about 30 miles west of the airport.

## The Morning After

Daylight revealed the scale of the damage. The National Weather Service counted about 2,700 damaged structures, including 251 homes heavily damaged or destroyed and 137 commercial buildings with large or total losses. At the peak, about 47,000 Ameren customers were without power. Yet the most remarkable number was the smallest. No one died, and fewer than two dozen people suffered even minor injuries, five of them hurt by flying debris at the airport, an extraordinary outcome for a violent tornado that ran through dense suburbs and a major airport on a Friday night. Homes could be rebuilt; the people who lived in them had come through. The storm belonged to a period of high tornado activity that came just before the 2011 Super Outbreak.

## Flight Context

The tornado's touchdown point lies near 38.7155°N, 90.4938°W, just northwest of Creve Coeur Lake in west St. Louis County. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL you can trace the 21.3-mile track eastward: past Maryland Heights, across St. Louis Lambert International Airport (KSTL) about 7 miles to the east-northeast, through Bridgeton, Ferguson and Dellwood, and over the Mississippi River into Granite City, Illinois. Lambert's Class B airspace dominates the route, so the flight requires coordination with St. Louis approach control, and the airport itself is the clearest landmark on the path. Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS) in Chesterfield, roughly 9 miles to the west-southwest, is the natural departure point for general aviation. St. Louis Regional Airport (KALN) near Alton is another option on the Illinois side. Spring in this region can bring severe thunderstorms with little warning, so check convective forecasts before flying.
