# St. Charles, Missouri

Founded as Les Petites Côtes on the Missouri River, St. Charles sent Lewis and Clark west, hosted Missouri's first state government above a hardware store, and still fills its old Main Street with festivals.

**Location:** 38.7886, -90.5117

**Categories:** Capitals of Missouri, Missouri populated places on the Missouri River, County seats in Missouri, Lewis and Clark Expedition, Pre-statehood history of Missouri

## Introduction

"I called the place 'Les Petites Côtes,'" Bernard Guillet is supposed to have said, "from the sides of the hills that you see." The exchange comes from Hopewell's Legends of the Missouri and Mississippi, which sets it in 1765, when the French-Canadian fur trader Louis Blanchette met Guillet on a bank of the Missouri River and asked whether the spot had a name yet. Blanchette liked the answer. "By that name shall it be called," he replied, "for it is the echo of nature—beautiful from its simplicity." The little hillsides became St. Charles, a city of 70,493 at the 2020 census, near the mouth of the Missouri and about 20 miles northwest of St. Louis.

## San Carlos of the Missouri

Native American peoples lived here at least as early as 11,000 B.C., and when European colonists arrived, the Illini, Osage and Missouri tribes inhabited the area. Blanchette settled around 1769 under the authority of the Spanish governor of Upper Louisiana, which France had given up after losing the French and Indian War, and he served as the settlement's civil and military leader until his death in 1793. Spain held the land on paper, but the settlers were mostly Native American and French Canadian, and most of them spoke French. Their first church, built in 1791, honored the Italian saint Charles Borromeo under the Spanish form of his name, and the town became San Carlos del Misuri. Its original location is lost, but a replica stands just off Main Street. Before the Louisiana Purchase, the Spanish even appointed Daniel Boone commandant of the Femme Osage District, and the name San Carlos was later anglicized to St. Charles.

## The Last Town for More Than Two Years

William Clark arrived on May 16, 1804, with 40 men and three boats, and spent several days making final preparations while waiting for Meriwether Lewis to come from St. Louis. The townspeople, excited to be part of the national expedition, treated the explorers warmly, and the men attended dances, dinners and a church service. Lewis arrived via St. Charles Rock Road on May 20. The next afternoon, around half past three, the expedition pushed off upriver in a keelboat. The explorers considered St. Charles the last "civilized" stop, and, tiny La Charrette a few days upriver aside, it would be the last established European-American town they visited for more than two years. The riverfront went on serving travelers heading the same way. St. Charles was a river port for westward trade, including with Native American tribes on the upper Missouri, and the traditional starting point of the Boone's Lick Road, the main overland route for settling central and western Missouri, which fed into the Santa Fe Trail.

## A Capitol Above the Hardware Store

When Missouri became a state in 1821, lawmakers decided to build a new capital, the "City of Jefferson," in the undeveloped center of the state. They needed somewhere to meet in the meantime, and St. Charles beat eight other cities for the honor by offering free space in rooms above a hardware store. The legislature met there until the buildings in Jefferson City were finished in 1826, and the rooms survive as the First Missouri State Capitol State Historic Site, open for tours. Another visitor left a bigger mark. Gottfried Duden, a German who came to Missouri in 1824 and farmed along the Missouri River west of St. Charles for about three years, published glowing accounts in 1829 after returning home, and his impressions drew waves of German immigrants beginning in the 1830s. Much later, St. Charles claimed another first: the first interstate highway project, started here in 1956. Kansas and Pennsylvania make the same claim.

## Main Street in Every Season

The heart of the city is still its riverfront and Historic Main Street, where restored buildings hold restaurants, specialty shops and offices, and the city lies near the eastern end of the Katy Trail, a 240-mile state park built on old railroad right-of-way. The calendar seldom sits empty. From the day after Thanksgiving through Christmas Eve, the Christmas Traditions festival, which traces its roots to 1975, sends costumed Legends of Christmas and Victorian carolers into the streets. Every August since 1971, the Festival of the Little Hills has echoed the town's first name, with living-history re-enactors, including Daniel and Rebecca Boone impersonators, showing life in the early 1800s. Oktoberfest celebrates the city's German heritage, Missouri Tartan Day brings bagpipers and caber tossing, and in January ice carvers take over North Main Street. The city even has a place in the movies: Star-Lord of Guardians of the Galaxy is from St. Charles, though the scenes set here in the second film were shot in Georgia.

## Flight Context

St. Charles sits at 38.7886°N, 90.5117°W on the north bank of the Missouri River, near its confluence with the Mississippi and about 20 miles northwest of downtown St. Louis. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, follow the Missouri River and look for the historic riverfront and Main Street along the bank, with Interstate 70 running through town and the broad river bottoms beyond. Lambert International lies only about 7 nm away, so expect busy terminal airspace. Nearby airports: Creve Coeur Airport (FAA identifier 1H0, no ICAO code), about 4 nm south across the river; St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL), about 7 nm east-southeast; St. Charles County Regional Airport/Smartt Field (KSET), about 9 nm north-northeast; and Spirit of St. Louis Airport (KSUS), about 10 nm southwest. Spring is the wettest season, and summer brings haze and thunderstorms.
