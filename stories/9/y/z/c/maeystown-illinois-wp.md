# Maeystown, Illinois

Founded in 1852 by German immigrant Jacob Maeys around a spring he bought for its waterpower, the limestone village of Maeystown held church services in German until 1943 and is today almost entirely a National Register historic district, home to about 150 people.

**Location:** 38.2256, -90.2336

**Categories:** Villages in Monroe County, Illinois, Populated places established in 1904, Metro East, National Register of Historic Places in Monroe County, Illinois, Villages in Illinois

## Introduction

Jacob Maeys bought this land for its water. In 1848 the German immigrant purchased a tract in the hills of southern Illinois because of the large spring on it, planning to harness its flow to power a sawmill. He brought his German bride, Barbara Fischer, to the property, and in 1852 he founded the village that still bears his name. Today Maeystown, in Monroe County, is home to about 150 people, and nearly every inch of it, save a small strip on its northeast side, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

## Children of 1848

Maeystown was established by German immigrants of the 1848 movement, people who left their homeland for political and economic reasons after the upheavals of that year. They settled in the hills and among the spring-fed streams of this corner of Illinois and set about building a community that looked and sounded like home. The craftspeople who came included cobblers, shoemakers, blacksmiths, tailors and undertakers, the trades a small, self-reliant village needed. Maeys's own log house still stands among the buildings they left behind, alongside the village's original church and its mill.

## Built From the Hills

What sets Maeystown apart is stone. The immigrants built with local limestone, in a manner similar to the Bavarian stone houses of their native Germany, and almost all of the village's stone-walled buildings date from the 1850s to about 1910. Some sixty historic structures survive, built of limestone, brick and wood. Among them are the old mill, which now houses a local history museum, and a small stone bridge that has become the village's signature image. Even the streets carry the craftsmanship: Maeystown's 400-foot stone gutter is said to be the longest stretch of its kind in the United States. In 1978 the village was placed on the National Register of Historic Places as a historic district, recognized for its significance in architecture and engineering.

## German Spoken Here

For nearly a century, Maeystown kept its language along with its building traditions. German was spoken on the street, and the original stone church held services in German, at least intermittently, until 1943. The village has since become a destination, drawing visitors to its limestone lanes and hosting German celebrations such as Oktoberfest, an apple butter festival and an old-fashioned German Christmas. Yet it has stayed small. The population stood at 148 in 2000 and 150 at the 2020 census, a quiet village whose buildings still reflect the plans of the people who arrived here in the 1850s.

## Flight Context

Maeystown lies at approximately 38.2256° N, 90.2336° W in the hills of western Monroe County, Illinois, roughly 145 meters (about 470 feet) above sea level, a short distance east of the broad Mississippi River floodplain. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet above ground level, look for a small cluster of pale limestone buildings tucked into wooded, creek-cut hills, with the flat bottomland farms of the American Bottom opening out to the west. Illinois Caverns State Natural Area and its sinkhole-pocked fields lie a few miles to the east, and Waterloo is to the north-northeast. St. Louis Downtown Airport (KCPS) lies to the north, and Sparta Community Airport (KSAR) is to the east; stay clear of the St. Louis Class B airspace to the north. Morning fog can pool in the river bottoms, and summer haze is common.
