# Medicine Park, Oklahoma

Oklahoma's red-cobblestone resort at the foot of the Wichita Mountains, founded by a future U.S. senator and once a weekend playground where swing bands, socialites and outlaws crossed paths.

**Location:** 34.7292, -98.5026

**Categories:** Towns in Comanche County, Oklahoma, Cobblestone architecture, Resort towns, Populated places established in 1908, Wichita Mountains

## Introduction

The stones are round, rust-red and heavy, and once you notice them you see them everywhere. Many of Medicine Park's original buildings are made of naturally formed cobblestones of red granite, stones unique to the Wichita Mountains that rise around the town. The effect is part storybook, part frontier, and it has been drawing visitors for more than a century. Medicine Park sits in Comanche County near the entrance to the Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge, roughly 60,000 acres of protected land, and just up the road from Lawton and Fort Sill. It was a small place of 411 people at the 2020 census, but its history is crowded with far bigger names.

## A Tent on Medicine Creek

The town began as one man's plan. In the spring of 1906, five years after the Wichita Mountains National Forest was established, a young lawyer named Elmer Thomas saw a double need in the area: a place for recreation, and a permanent water source for the newly founded city of Lawton. Thomas, who had just become a member of the Oklahoma State Senate, founded Medicine Park on July 4, 1908. The beginnings were humble. The first Medicine Park consisted of a large surplus Army tent with a wooden floor, where hot meals were served to visitors, plus a limited number of campsites. Two dams built on Medicine Creek created the Bath Lake Swimming Hole. Thomas went on to a long political career, serving in the state senate until 1920, in the U.S. House of Representatives from 1923 to 1927, and in the U.S. Senate from 1927 until 1951.

## Swing Music in the Hills

Tourists came from all around Oklahoma and North Texas. Two inns soon went up, the Outside Inn and the Apache Inn. Then came the music. Local histories say Bob Wills was a regular at the town's Dance Hall from 1929 through the late 1930s, leading the Texas Playboys once he formed them in the mid-1930s, and he was hardly the only act in town. Many famous bands of the era stopped here on their way to big-city venues in Oklahoma City, Dallas and Fort Worth. Between the dance floor, the swimming hole, nearby Lake Lawtonka and the wildlife refuge, thousands of people poured in every weekend, and the crowds kept coming through the seasons.

## Senators, Socialites and Outlaws

Medicine Park earned a reputation as the playground of Oklahoma's rich, famous and notorious, and the mix could be startling. Outlaws and horse thieves mixed in the new cobblestone community with noted politicians and businessmen, with soldiers and officers from Fort Sill, with families and socialites. Local lore fills the town's history with a remarkable roster of visitors: Will Rogers and aviator Wiley Post, oilman Frank Phillips, jazz musician Lil Hardin Armstrong and bandleader Les Brown, Colonel Jack Abernathy, and cowboy stars Roy Rogers and Dale Evans. The same lore also includes the era's most infamous names, among them Al Capone, Bonnie and Clyde, and Pretty Boy Floyd. Few towns of a few hundred people can claim a history that stretches from the halls of the U.S. Senate to the era's most notorious outlaws.

## Stone Stages and a Second Act

Some of the old resort has earned formal protection. The Medicine Park Hotel and Annex on East Lake Drive is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, as is the nearby Holy City of the Wichitas Historic District. That site, inside the wildlife refuge, stages the Prince of Peace Easter pageant, billed as North America's longest continuously running, held every year since 1926 on permanent sets built of native granite. The town has changed in quieter ways, too. Its own school district closed in 1990, splitting local students between Elgin and Lawton. Yet the cobblestone village keeps finding new admirers. In June 2018 an Oklahoma television station reported that Expedia had named Medicine Park the fifth prettiest town in the United States, and one mountain-biking site ranks the Lake Lawtonka trails around town among the best riding areas in Oklahoma, second among its 68 listed trails at last check.

## Flight Context

Medicine Park lies at 34.72924 N, 98.50256 W at the southeastern edge of the Wichita Mountains in Comanche County, Oklahoma, just north of Fort Sill. From 3,000-4,500 feet AGL, look for the town's cluster of buildings beside the waters of Lake Lawtonka, with Mount Scott and the rugged granite hills of the Wichita Mountains Wildlife Refuge rising to the west. Fort Sill's artillery ranges to the south lie beneath restricted airspace; check charts and NOTAMs. Nearest airports: Lawton-Fort Sill Regional Airport (KLAW), south of Lawton, and Henry Post Army Airfield (KFSI) on Fort Sill (military use). Expect strong spring thunderstorms and gusty plains winds.
