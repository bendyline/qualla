# Bauxite, Arkansas

Named for the aluminum ore beneath it, Bauxite, Arkansas, boomed as an ALCOA company town through two world wars, ran its mines around the clock in 1943, and today keeps its mining past alive in a 1926 community center and a bronze miner's family.

**Location:** 34.5592, -92.5142

**Categories:** Towns in Saline County, Arkansas, Company towns in Arkansas, Bauxite mining, Alcoa, Mining history

## Introduction

Italy Camp, Mexico Camp, Africa Camp: the camps that grew up around the mines here were often segregated by race or ethnicity, and their names said so. The town that eventually took shape among them did not bother with anything poetic. It simply took the name of the ore in the ground, bauxite, the raw material of aluminum. Bauxite, Arkansas, about 18 miles southwest of downtown Little Rock, is small today, with 629 residents in 2020, but in two world wars its mines fed the nation's demand for aluminum.

## A Company Buys the Ground

Bauxite was discovered in the area in the early 1890s, and the General Bauxite Company mined it until 1905. That year the Pittsburgh Reduction Company, an aluminum refiner that had learned of the high-quality ore being shipped out of Saline County, bought vast tracts of land and bought out the local producers, General Bauxite among them. Pittsburgh Reduction would later incorporate as the Aluminum Company of America, better known as ALCOA. Production climbed fast. From 200,000 short tons in 1914, during World War I, output rose to 560,000 short tons by the war's end in 1918. That war brought the town's greatest surge of growth, as camps sprang up in and around the present-day city, often segregated by race or ethnicity.

## Lean Years, Then Round the Clock

Peace brought falling demand, and with new sources of ore opening in South America, Arkansas production rose and fell with the market, dropping as low as 60,000 short tons a year in the mid-1930s. Even so, through the Great Depression ALCOA gave its employees a standard of living that was rarely matched in those hard years. In 1926 the company built a Colonial-style community center to serve as a boarding house and gathering place for the mining families. Then came World War II and an urgent need for refined aluminum for airplanes and other materiel. Donald M. Nelson asked ALCOA chairman Arthur V. Davis to put the mines on a three-shift, 24-hour schedule, and Davis brought in miners from across the country to keep them running without stopping. In 1943 production reached more than 6,000,000 tons of ore.

## After the Boom

Production slowed as the war drew to a close, but the wartime rush had already reshaped the area, adding new communities and a larger school district. One of them, Pine Haven, established during the war, is still home to an elementary school. ALCOA and the Reynolds Metal Company continued to refine bauxite in the area, and Reynolds finally ceased operations in the early 1980s. The population shrank as the ore's output fell. Bauxite did not incorporate as a town until 1973. It lies along Arkansas Highway 183, bordered by Benton to the west and Bryant to the north, and its school district kept its independence after a public outcry when it faced consolidation under Governor Mike Huckabee's school plan.

## Miners on the Corner

The 1926 community center still stands and now houses the Bauxite Museum, an informal collection of photographs, documents and mining equipment. In 2008 the museum placed "Unsung Heroes" at the corner of the property, a life-size group of bronze statues by sculptor Gary Alsum showing a mining family, with the father setting out for work at the mines. Football carries the rest of the town's pride. The Bauxite Miners began playing around 1920 and have won 24 conference championships and the 1996 state title. George Cole, who played for Bauxite from 1920 to 1923, went on to coach and serve as athletic director at the University of Arkansas, and Leon "Muscles" Campbell, a Bauxite player from 1943 to 1945, went on to the NFL with the Baltimore Colts, Chicago Bears and Pittsburgh Steelers.

## Flight Context

Bauxite lies at about 34.559°N, 92.514°W in Saline County, Arkansas, along Arkansas Highway 183 between Benton to the west and Bryant to the north, about 18 miles southwest of downtown Little Rock. From 2,000-3,000 ft AGL, use Benton, Bryant and the Interstate 30 corridor to the north as references. Nearby airports: Saline County Regional at Bryant (FAA identifier SUZ, GPS code KSUZ; no ICAO code listed), about 3 nm northeast; Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT), about 18 nm northeast. Summer thunderstorms and haze are common.
