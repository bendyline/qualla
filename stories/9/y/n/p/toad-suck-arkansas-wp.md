# Toad Suck, Arkansas

A tiny Perry County community with one of America's most talked-about place names lends it to a lock and dam on the Arkansas River and to a spring festival where children race toads.

**Location:** 35.0756, -92.5600

**Categories:** Unincorporated communities in Perry County, Arkansas, Unincorporated communities in Arkansas

## Introduction

Nobody agrees on how Toad Suck got its name, and that may be the best thing about it. The favorite story involves thirsty rivermen and a tavern. Rival theories involve a French phrase and the shape of the Arkansas River. Either way, the name of this small unincorporated community in Perry County, Arkansas, has turned up again and again on lists of unusual American place names, and it has spread well beyond the community itself, onto a lock and dam, a road, a park and a spring festival in nearby Conway.

## Swelled Up Like Toads

The most popular explanation goes back to the steamboat era on the Arkansas River. Boatmen stopped at a spot here to wait for the water to rise or fall, and while they waited they gathered at the local tavern. There, the story goes, they would "suck on the bottle 'til they swell up like toads." It is a vivid image, and it has done most of the work of making the name famous. Others hear something more sober in it. Some believe Toad Suck is a corruption of a French phrase, translated variously as "a narrow channel in the river" or "sweet water," while others say it was simply a common river name for a sheltered eddy where boats could tie up. The origin remains disputed, and the community has never needed to settle the argument to keep the name.

## Where the Highway Crosses the River

The name's most visible home is the Toad Suck Ferry Lock and Dam, one of the navigation structures that step boats up and down the Arkansas River. Its full name recalls the ferry crossing that once served travelers here. The dam does double duty as a bridge, carrying Arkansas Highway 60, known locally as Prince Street, across the river. The community straddles that crossing in its own way. Toad Suck Road runs east of the river, while inland to the west, at the junction of Highway 60 and Bethel AME Road, stands the Bigelow Rosenwald School, listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Toad Suck is its nearest community. Rosenwald schools were built across the rural South in the early twentieth century with support from a fund created by Sears, Roebuck and Company president Julius Rosenwald, to educate Black children whom segregated public systems left underserved.

## Daze in May

In 1982, John Ward, managing editor of Conway's newspaper, the Log Cabin Democrat, had an idea. Residents were enduring hard economic times, and Ward wanted to lift their spirits with a festival. He wanted somehow to involve Toad Suck, and he added "Daze" to capture the euphoria of spring. Toad Suck Daze was born. It began at Toad Suck Park near the lock and dam, but flooding on the river in 1990 pushed it into downtown Conway, where it has stayed ever since. It is held every year on the first weekend in May, and early on its organizers agreed that its proceeds would fund scholarships at local colleges.

## Blow on Your Toad

The signature event is exactly what the name promises. Days before the festival begins, toads are rounded up so that children can pick one out to race. On race day, young competitors are allowed to encourage their toads by blowing on them, patting the racing mat behind them, or shouting words of encouragement. It is hard to imagine a better use for a name that began, if the tavern story is true, with rivermen swelling up like toads. A rough joke from the steamboat days has become a family tradition, and a little Perry County community has seen its odd name carried across the river and into the heart of a college town.

## Flight Context

Toad Suck lies at 35.0756°N, 92.5600°W in Perry County, on the west side of the Arkansas River opposite Conway. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL, look for the Toad Suck Ferry Lock and Dam spanning the river and carrying Highway 60 west from Conway; the town of Conway spreads out to the east. Conway Regional Airport at Cantrell Field (KCXW) is about 3.4 nm to the south, and Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport (KLIT) in Little Rock is roughly 27 nm to the southeast. Spring festival weekends bring crowds to downtown Conway; watch for summer haze and thunderstorms over the river valley.
