# Tucker Unit

On a Jefferson County prison farm where Arkansas once carried out its executions and where guards once tortured inmates with a device built from a crank telephone, a cinder-block chapel funded partly by Johnny Cash still stands.

**Location:** 34.4545, -91.9156

**Categories:** Prisons in Arkansas, Capital punishment in Arkansas, Buildings and structures in Jefferson County, Arkansas, Prison reform, 1916 establishments in Arkansas

## Introduction

The Island of Hope Chapel is built of cinder blocks. It has a sloping ceiling and, behind the altar, a plexiglas window made to look like leaded glass. Johnny Cash was among the donors whose gifts, $80,000 in all, paid for it, and officials of the Arkansas Department of Correction believe it was the first freestanding prison chapel in the Southern United States. It stands at the Tucker Unit, a prison farm in the flat fields of Jefferson County whose history also includes an execution chamber and a notorious instrument of torture.

## Fields Behind the Fence

The State of Arkansas bought about 4,400 acres of land in 1916 to build the Tucker Unit, in Dudley Lake Township about 25 miles northeast of Pine Bluff. It sits near, but not inside, the small community of Tucker. Its role grew in 1933, when Governor Junius Marion Futrell closed the old Arkansas State Penitentiary, known as "The Walls," and some of its prisoners were moved to Tucker. The unit also once held some Black women prisoners. Today it is one of the state's "parent units" for men, one of several places where newly processed male prisoners receive their first assignment, and its land is still farmed in rice and soybeans. A separate Maximum Security Unit was built a short distance to the north in 1983.

## Where Arkansas Carried Out Executions

When The Walls closed in 1933, the state's execution chamber moved to Tucker, along with the electric chair called "Old Sparky." For decades, men condemned in Arkansas died here. In 1964 Charles Fields became the last prisoner executed at Tucker before the U.S. Supreme Court declared capital punishment unconstitutional. The death chamber's era at Tucker ended in stages. In 1974 death row inmates held at Tucker were moved to the Cummins Unit, and in 1978 a new death chamber opened at Cummins, so Tucker was no longer the state's place of execution. On August 22, 2003, the state's entire death row, 39 men, was transferred to the Supermax at the Varner Unit.

## The Tucker Telephone

The Tucker Unit's name is attached to a notorious chapter in Arkansas prison history. The Arkansas prison scandal of the late 1960s played out here and at the Cummins Unit, and one of its most notorious instruments was the "Tucker Telephone," a torture device assembled from the parts of a crank telephone. Its generator was wired to batteries so that it could deliver electric shocks to a prisoner's body, and its use on inmates was substantiated until 1968. Those who suffered it were prisoners in the state's custody. As of 2000, visitors to the unit regularly asked the warden whether the telephone on the desk was the Tucker Telephone. The question sounds like a grim joke, but it is also a measure of how deeply the abuse marked the prison's name.

## Island of Hope

Education reached the unit in 1968, when the England School District started a night program, and Tucker now houses a campus of the Riverside Vocational Technical School. The Island of Hope Chapel was dedicated in November 1969. Over the decades rainwater leaked into the building, and a $175,000 renovation brought a new roof, refinished floors, pews and walls, and new panes of glass before the chapel was rededicated on May 13, 2011. The state could pay for those repairs, but under the Establishment Clause of the U.S. Constitution, it cannot build a new religious facility. More recent history has had its own crises. On August 7, 2017, a group of prisoners held guards hostage before releasing them.

## Flight Context

The Tucker Unit lies at approximately 34.4545°N, 91.9156°W in the flat farmland of Jefferson County, near the community of Tucker. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL, look for the prison compound set among large rectangular rice and soybean fields; the Maximum Security Unit sits a short distance to the north. Overflight of correctional facilities at low altitude is discouraged, so keep a respectful height. Pine Bluff Regional Airport, Grider Field (KPBF), is about 17 nm to the south, Stuttgart Municipal Airport (KSGT) is about 19 nm to the east-northeast, and Bill & Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT) is about 22 nm to the northwest. Summer brings haze and thunderstorms; winter gives the crispest views across the fields.
