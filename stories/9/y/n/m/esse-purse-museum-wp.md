# ESSE Purse Museum

On Little Rock's South Main Street, a museum reads the twentieth century through women's handbags and the everyday things they carried, decade by decade.

**Location:** 34.7340, -92.2733

**Categories:** Fashion museums in the United States, Museums established in 2013, 2013 establishments in Arkansas, Museums of American art, Buildings and structures in Little Rock, Arkansas, Bags (fashion)

## Introduction

Unclasp a handbag and you find out what its owner expected from her day. The ESSE Purse Museum in Little Rock, Arkansas, is built on that simple idea. Its cases hold women's handbags alongside the day-to-day items carried in them, and together they tell the stories of American women's lives through the 1900s. Even the name looks inward rather than at the fashion: esse is the Latin infinitive for "to be."

## A Collector's Eye

The museum grew out of one woman's collecting habit. Its founder, Anita Davis, came to handbags through an interest in folk art, and over the years her personal collection grew into the thousands. In 2006 that collection was organized into a traveling exhibition, and for the next five years it toured the United States, finishing its run in 2011. What had begun as a private passion became a show seen across the country. When the tour ended, the collection settled into a permanent home in Little Rock, and the museum opened there in June 2013.

## An Address on South Main

The museum occupies a historic building at 1510 South Main Street, in the district locals shorten to "SoMa." The building itself dates to 1946, which gives it a pleasing overlap with the collection: it went up in the middle of the very century the purses inside it document. Inside are permanent galleries, a space for special topic exhibits and, fittingly enough for a museum about bags, a gift shop. The setting suits the subject. These are objects from ordinary lives, and they are shown in a building that has lived through most of the same decades they have.

## A Century in Display Cases

The permanent exhibits concentrate on the handbags most women actually owned, from the early 1900s through 1999. The display cases are arranged by decade. Each one shows the styles of purse women carried and the contents they kept inside, so a visitor walking the room is walking through time. The arrangement makes an argument without needing to state it. War, fashion and economics all shaped what a purse looked like and what it was for, and the changes show up case by case as the decades pass. Among the objects are purses made of reptile skin, of fabric and of metal, each material a small clue to its own era and its owner's circumstances.

## Rooms That Keep Changing

Alongside the permanent galleries, the museum hosts special exhibits that stretch its theme in unexpected directions. In 2014 it showed vintage Barbie dolls. In 2017 it presented two very different shows: a local artist's series of paintings of women and their purses, and an exhibit of images and objects from African American women spanning 1891 to 1987. The following year brought paper dresses from the 1960s, and in July 2020 a temporary exhibit turned to eyewear. The ESSE is not alone in the world. The Simone Handbag Museum in Seoul shares its subject, as did Amsterdam's Museum of Bags and Purses until it closed for good in 2020, but the Little Rock museum's focus on ordinary women's everyday bags gives it a character of its own.

## Flight Context

The ESSE Purse Museum sits at 34.7340°N, 92.2733°W on South Main Street, just south of downtown Little Rock. A single building is hard to pick out from the air, so fly the neighborhood rather than the address: from about 2,000 ft AGL, the downtown towers and the Arkansas River lie a short distance to the north, and the SoMa district runs south from downtown along Main Street.
