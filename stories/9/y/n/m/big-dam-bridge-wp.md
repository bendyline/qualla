# Big Dam Bridge

Named after a county judge's defiant quip, the Big Dam Bridge carries walkers and cyclists 4,226 feet across the Arkansas River and Murray Lock and Dam between Little Rock and North Little Rock, and no car or train has ever crossed it.

## Introduction

"We're going to build that dam bridge." Pulaski County Judge F. G. "Buddy" Villines said it while worrying over how to pay for the project, and he always insisted he was talking about the bridge's location on a dam, not reaching for a mild expletive. Either way, the line stuck. The bridge that was supposed to be called the Murray Bridge became the Big Dam Bridge, and today it carries walkers, runners and cyclists across the Arkansas River between Little Rock and North Little Rock, high above the water and the dam that gave it its name.

## A Name and a Price Tag

Villines had reason to be concerned about the money. Estimates of the bridge's cost range from about $12.8 million to more than $16 million, a considerable sum for a crossing that would never carry a single car. The bridge was originally intended to be called the Murray Bridge, a name shared with the lock and dam it crosses and the park at its southern end. The judge's remark changed that. It was the kind of line that sounds like a slip and works like a slogan, and the nickname won out, leaving a serious piece of public engineering with a name few people forget. The bridge opened with a ceremony on Saturday, September 30, 2006, and local politicians boasted that it would boost the local economy and contribute to the health of the population.

## Four-Fifths of a Mile, No Cars Allowed

The Big Dam Bridge is 4,226 feet long, or about 1,288 meters, and it is open only to pedestrian and bicycle traffic. By that measure it is the longest pedestrian and bicycle bridge in North America that has never been used by trains or motor vehicles, a careful qualifier that limits the claim to bridges built for people on foot and on bicycles from the very start. The span over the river itself runs 3,463 feet, and ramps on either bank make up the rest of the length. At its highest the deck rises 90 feet above the Arkansas River and 30 feet above the dam. The southern end touches down near Little Rock's Murray Park, and the northern end lands at Cook's Landing Park in North Little Rock, linking green space on both shores.

## Races, Awards and a Trail

The bridge was built to be used, and it is. It carries the Arkansas River Trail across the water, and the bridge and its neighboring parks host several races, including the Big Dam Bridge 100 cycling tour, the BDB Twilight 5K and the BDB Duathlon. It has also collected formal recognition. In 2012 the Society of American Travel Writers named it one of the top ten bridges to visit in the United States, and in 2016 the Arkansas Democrat-Gazette's annual poll named it the Best Place to Bike. Its LED lighting gives the bridge a second life after dark, when the long deck glows above the river. For a structure named after an offhand remark, the Big Dam Bridge has earned a remarkably serious reputation.

## Flight Context

The Big Dam Bridge crosses the Arkansas River at Murray Lock and Dam, on the western side of Little Rock and North Little Rock, at 34.7939°N, 92.3581°W. The long deck and the dam beneath it stand out clearly against the river from 1,500-2,500 feet AGL. The bridge lies within the Little Rock Class C airspace, so contact approach before transiting. Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport (KLIT) is about 8 nm east-southeast, North Little Rock Municipal Airport (KORK) is about 6 nm east-northeast, and Little Rock Air Force Base (KLRF) is about 13 nm northeast. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common over central Arkansas.
