# Tower Building of the Little Rock Arsenal

Built in 1840-41 to store ammunition, the octagon-towered arsenal building in Little Rock's MacArthur Park was seized by the state on the eve of the Civil War, is traditionally held to be Douglas MacArthur's birthplace, and now houses the MacArthur Museum of Arkansas Military History.

**Location:** 34.7383, -92.2652

**Categories:** American Civil War sites in Arkansas, Military museums in Arkansas, Government buildings in Little Rock, Arkansas, Douglas MacArthur, 1841 establishments in Arkansas

## Introduction

Douglas MacArthur's first home was built to store ammunition. The brick building with the octagonal tower in Little Rock's MacArthur Park went up in 1840 and 1841 as the main building of a U.S. arsenal, with exterior walls three feet thick and, at first, a single rear basement door. By 1880, when the future general is said to have been born on its northwest upper floor, it had become quarters for married officers. It is the last surviving structure of the city's first U.S. military installation and one of the oldest buildings in central Arkansas.

## A Racetrack Becomes an Arsenal

Governor James S. Conway asked for the arsenal out of concern over the dangers of frontier life and possible confrontations with Native peoples, forced by the U.S. government from their homelands in the South, who were passing through Arkansas on their way to Indian Territory. Major Robert B. Lee of the U.S. Army appropriated thirty-six acres on the edge of Little Rock, land the local jockey club had used as a racetrack, and John Wormley Walker supervised construction. The original $14,000 budget proved inadequate and was raised to $30,000. The Tower Building, the first permanent structure, was planned in stone but built of masonry instead, and the Arkansas Gazette called it "a splendid specimen of masonry." Inside, the tower worked as a hoist, lifting munitions between floors. For years the place was a simple arms depot staffed by a handful of soldiers.

## Standoff of 1861

In November 1860, with war approaching, sixty-five men of the Second U.S. Artillery arrived under Captain James Totten. Then came a telegram. Local attorney John M. Harrell, composing the first telegraph dispatch ever sent from Arkansas's capital over the new line to Memphis, reported unconfirmed rumors that federal reinforcements were on the way. Some Arkansans read it as a call from the governor to assemble and drive out the federal troops, and by February 5, 1861, six militia units numbering 1,000 men had gathered in Little Rock. Governor Henry M. Rector denied having summoned them, but with armed men assembled in his name, he demanded the arsenal's surrender. Totten, receiving no instructions from Washington, agreed on terms that let his men leave as soldiers under orders rather than as a defeated garrison. On February 8, 1861, the arsenal passed to state officials. Inside, the Confederates later counted some 10,247 weapons, 250,000 musket cartridges and 520,000 percussion caps.

## Armies Come and Go

Arkansas's Confederate authorities ran the arsenal until late August 1863, when nearly all its stores were packed and sent to Arkadelphia ahead of the advancing Union army of Frederick Steele. Once Little Rock fell and the arsenal was recaptured, it became a launching point: in 1864 Steele marched 8,500 troops out of the arsenal to begin the Camden Expedition. In 1874, during the Brooks-Baxter War, supporters of Joseph Brooks briefly seized it once more. By 1868 the main building had gained porches, interior walls and stairs, including the central staircase that remains today, and in 1873 the arsenal was renamed Little Rock Barracks. Captain Arthur MacArthur was stationed here when his son Douglas was born in 1880. That son later became supreme commander of Allied forces in the Southwest Pacific during World War II. In the 1880s the government began closing small arsenals, and Little Rock was ordered abandoned by October 1, 1890.

## The Club That Kept the Lights On

In 1894 the Little Rock Æsthetic Club, one of the oldest women's societies west of the Mississippi River, moved in, and it has been headquartered here ever since. For almost fifty years, apart from the club's meetings, the building sat largely empty and deteriorating, and the Æsthetic Club supported it financially, even paying the electric bill through the Great Depression. The building still found public uses: it was headquarters for the United Confederate Veterans Reunion of May 1911, a gathering of more than 106,000, and in 1912 its second floor became Little Rock's first public library. In 1942 the park was renamed for MacArthur, and the renovated building became home to the Arkansas Museum of Natural History and Antiquities for about fifty-five years. Since May 19, 2001, it has housed the MacArthur Museum of Arkansas Military History, telling the stories of Arkansas soldiers in the wars of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

## Flight Context

The Tower Building stands in MacArthur Park on the southeast side of downtown Little Rock at 34.738°N, 92.265°W, a short distance south of the Arkansas River. The park's green space amid the downtown grid is the reference; the building itself is modest in size. It lies only about 2 nm west-northwest of Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT), within Little Rock's Class C airspace, so contact approach control and expect airline traffic. North Little Rock Municipal (FAA identifier ORK, GPS code KORK; no ICAO code listed) is about 6 nm north. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
