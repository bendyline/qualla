# Clinton Presidential Center

Cantilevered over the Arkansas River in downtown Little Rock, the Clinton Presidential Center turned a run-down warehouse district into a riverfront park and holds the records of the 42nd president, from some 78 million pages of documents to a Cadillac One.

**Location:** 34.7464, -92.2585

**Categories:** Presidential libraries, Bill Clinton, Museums in Little Rock, Arkansas, Buildings and structures in Little Rock, Arkansas, Landmarks in Arkansas, 2004 establishments in Arkansas

## Introduction

The building does not sit on the riverbank so much as reach out over it. The main hall of the William J. Clinton Presidential Library and Museum cantilevers above the Arkansas River in Little Rock, a long span meant to echo Bill Clinton's campaign promise of "building a bridge to the 21st century." Inside, the 42nd president's two terms are laid out in objects and paper. Outside, the ground it stands on tells its own story of a riverfront remade.

## Rain, Four Presidents and Bono

The center was dedicated on November 18, 2004, in the rain. About 30,000 people came anyway, among them Willie Mays, Robin Williams and Barbra Streisand. Four presidents shared the stage, Clinton, Jimmy Carter, George H. W. Bush and George W. Bush, while Gerald R. Ford stayed away for health reasons, and all three of the other presidents spoke. Clinton, who had recently undergone bypass surgery, gave a 20-minute speech. Bono performed, as did the African Drum Ballet and the Philander Smith Collegiate Choir, Floyd Flake gave the invocation, and Nelson Mandela sent a video tribute. It was the thirteenth presidential library completed in the United States, the eleventh operated by the National Archives and Records Administration, and the third to comply with the Presidential Records Act of 1978. Its archives hold about 78 million pages of official records, 20 million e-mails and 2.6 million photographs, and its museum collection includes more than 100,000 objects and works of art.

## A Long Room for a Presidency

Polshek Partnership designed the five-story main building, with contributions from Polk Stanley Wilcox Architects and exhibits by Ralph Appelbaum Associates. Clinton was deeply involved; Appelbaum called him "the editor-in-chief, the curator-in-chief and in many times the art director of the exhibits." The layout of the galleries was inspired by the Long Room of the Old Library at Trinity College, Dublin, which Clinton first saw as a Rhodes Scholar. The second floor holds a 110-foot timeline of his years in office and 14 alcoves on events of his terms, along with full-scale replicas of the Clinton-era Oval Office and Cabinet Room. Cadillac One is parked on the first floor. One alcove, titled The Fight for Power, covers the Lewinsky affair, the Whitewater scandal and the Republican Contract with America. Critics said the library did not include enough about the Lewinsky scandal and the impeachment; Appelbaum said Clinton "insisted on a transparent and illuminating exhibition," and also that "this is the way the president wanted to see his legacy defined."

## From Warehouses to a Riverfront Park

The center's 17 acres beside the river and Interstate 30 lie in what was formerly a run-down warehouse district, next to the abandoned tracks of the defunct Chicago, Rock Island and Pacific Railroad. Not everything old survived: in 2001 an 1899 rail depot was found inside a warehouse on the site, preservationists lost a court battle to save it, and it was demolished. What rose instead is the nearly 30-acre Clinton Presidential Park, centered on the Celebration Circle fountain plaza. The building leans green, with floors of recycled rubber tires, solar panels, and a rooftop garden added in 2007 that collects runoff. The surrounding district changed quickly. New hotels, restaurants, housing, offices and shops followed, the River Market dining and retail district was revived, more than $1 billion in new real estate investment has flowed into downtown Little Rock, and Heifer International has its world headquarters just beyond the library.

## Walking Across the River

The old railroad left one more gift. The 1899 Rock Island Railroad Bridge, originally built by the Choctaw and Memphis Railroad, once carried trains across the Arkansas River. The Clinton Foundation planned to convert it for pedestrians, but money ran short. An $8 million earmark failed in the state legislature, and in 2009 Governor Mike Beebe agreed to put $2.5 million in stimulus funds toward the work. Fundraising was finally completed in 2010, construction began on May 28 of that year, and the renamed Clinton Park Bridge opened to the public on October 2, 2011, linking the park to North Little Rock. Today visitors can walk out over the same water the museum overhangs, and the center admits school groups free with a reservation and opens its doors free on a few days each year, including Presidents' Day.

## Flight Context

The Clinton Presidential Center stands on the south bank of the Arkansas River in downtown Little Rock at 34.746°N, 92.258°W, beside Interstate 30. Look for the long main building cantilevered toward the river just east of downtown, with the converted Clinton Park Bridge crossing to North Little Rock beside it. The site is only about 2 nm west-northwest of Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT), inside Little Rock's Class C airspace, so coordinate with approach control and expect airline traffic. North Little Rock Municipal (FAA identifier ORK, GPS code KORK; no ICAO code listed) lies about 5 nm north. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common along the river.
