# War Memorial Stadium (Arkansas)

Dedicated in 1948 to Arkansas's war dead, Little Rock's War Memorial Stadium has been a second home for the Razorbacks, the stage for the state's high school championships and a venue for presidents, preachers and rock stars.

**Location:** 34.7499, -92.3300

## Introduction

Before the first kickoff came the dedication. On September 18, 1948, Maurice Britt, a former Arkansas Razorback and a Medal of Honor recipient, stood in Little Rock's new stadium and dedicated it to "the memory of her native sons and daughters who have given so much that we might have our freedom." Then the Razorbacks took the field and beat the Abilene Christian Wildcats 40-6. In the decades since, War Memorial Stadium has been where Arkansas gathers, for football, for music and for faith.

## Built for a Crowd

Designed by the architects Burks & Anderson, the stadium was finished in 1947 at a cost of $1.2 million, with 31,075 seats. In its early years it hosted college teams from all over the state, among them Philander Smith College, Ouachita Baptist College and Arkansas Tech, along with the forerunners of today's University of Arkansas campuses at Little Rock, Pine Bluff and Monticello and the University of Central Arkansas. Professional football came through too: the Los Angeles Rams and Philadelphia Eagles met here in 1949 and again in 1951, and the Eagles played the Detroit Lions in 1952. The field itself has changed repeatedly, from AstroTurf and new lights for the 1970 season to natural grass in 1994 and back to artificial surfaces in the 2000s. A $7.3 million renovation of the club facility and press box, completed in 2010, added a Sports Media Legends Wall of Honor for Arkansas sports journalists.

## The Razorbacks' Other Home

The University of Arkansas plays its home games in Fayetteville, but for decades it also brought the Razorbacks to Little Rock. By 2012 Arkansas had played nearly 200 games at War Memorial, usually two or three a year and in some seasons of the 1970s and 1980s as many as four. In 2000 athletic director Frank Broyles stirred controversy by moving one home game to Fayetteville, where Razorback Stadium was to grow from 50,000 to 72,000 seats. Despite objections from Little Rock, the schedule there kept shrinking. In 2013 the university announced one game a year through 2018, after which the team would play only in Fayetteville, but a new agreement in 2018 allowed one non-conference game every other year. The tradition still produced memorable games late in its run. In 2021 Arkansas played the University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff, its first game against an in-state opponent since 1944, and in 2025 it faced the Arkansas State Red Wolves for the first time ever, before a sold-out crowd of more than 54,000.

## A Championship Louisiana Would Not Host

In 1956 the National Association of Intercollegiate Athletics was looking for a city to host its first football championship. The game seemed bound for Shreveport, Louisiana, until the Louisiana legislature passed a bill banning integrated sporting events. Some NAIA colleges had African-American players, so the association looked elsewhere. Stadium general manager Allen Berry, working with local businesses and the Little Rock Chamber of Commerce, raised $25,000 to bring the game to Little Rock. The Aluminum Bowl ended in a scoreless tie, and the two teams were named co-champions. Only about 5,000 spectators came despite local promotion, and in 1957 the game moved to St. Petersburg, Florida.

## Salt, Song and Sermons

Every year the Arkansas Activities Association holds its high school football championship games for all classifications here, and for years Benton and Bryant opened their seasons against each other here in the Salt Bowl, named for the nineteenth-century salt works that gave Saline County its name. It sometimes drew as many as 30,000 fans, before the game moved to the high school stadium in Benton in 2025. The stadium has held far more than football. President Harry S. Truman spoke here in June 1949, and Bob Hope performed in 1949 and again in 1991. Billy Graham preached crusades to thousands here, including in 1959 a young Bill Clinton, and returned in 1989. In 1995 Billy Joel and Elton John played to a sellout crowd of 41,274, and the Eagles, the Rolling Stones, George Strait and 'N Sync have taken the stage as well.

## Flight Context

War Memorial Stadium sits in War Memorial Park in west-central Little Rock at 34.750°N, 92.330°W, a few miles west of downtown. The large bowl is easy to pick out from 2,000-3,000 ft AGL. It lies about 5 nm west-northwest of Bill and Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT), within Little Rock's Class C airspace, so contact approach control. On major game days, check NOTAMs for stadium temporary flight restrictions. North Little Rock Municipal (FAA identifier ORK, GPS code KORK; no ICAO code listed) is about 6 nm northeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
