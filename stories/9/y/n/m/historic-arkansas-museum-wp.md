# Historic Arkansas Museum

When Little Rock's oldest building faced condemnation, a determined planning commissioner sold the city on the idea of a "town of three Capitols" and saved a half-block of early houses that became the state's history museum.

**Location:** 34.7464, -92.2690

**Categories:** History museums in Arkansas, Museums in Little Rock, Arkansas, Historic preservation, Smithsonian Institution affiliates, National Register of Historic Places in Little Rock, Arkansas

## Introduction

In the late 1930s, the oldest building in Little Rock was on a list of houses slated for condemnation. The Hinderliter House stood with its neighbors near Cumberland and East 3rd Streets downtown, and it might have vanished if Louise Loughborough had not decided otherwise. What she built instead, the Historic Arkansas Museum, opened on July 19, 1941, and it still occupies that half-block of rescued houses, telling the story of Arkansas through the things its people made, carried and kept.

## The Town of Three Capitols

Loughborough had been named to the Little Rock Planning Commission in 1935. To save the Hinderliter House, she launched a public relations campaign that recast Little Rock as a "town of three Capitols": the Hinderliter House, the Old State House, and the current Arkansas State Capitol. By placing a modest old house alongside two grand statehouses, she asked the city to see it as a landmark rather than a teardown. The idea was persuasive, even if part of it rested on legend. Popular folklore holds that the Hinderliter House was the last meeting place of the Arkansas Territorial Legislature, yet no one knows whether it was ever used that way. The campaign worked regardless.

## Act 388 and a Half-Block Saved

In 1939 the Arkansas General Assembly passed Act 388, creating the Arkansas Territorial Capitol Restoration Commission and naming Loughborough its chairwoman. She gathered support from the Works Progress Administration, the legislature and private donors, and worked with architect Max Mayer to restore the half-block of houses at Cumberland and East 3rd Street. The museum opened in the summer of 1941. Nearly eight decades later, in 2019, the museum property was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Arkansas Territorial Restoration Historic District. Today the museum is accredited by the American Alliance of Museums and is a Smithsonian Institution affiliate.

## Horns, Blades and a Hair Brooch

The collections are strongest when an object carries a name. Most of the furniture, from chairs and library tables to a clothes press and sugar chests, came to the museum through the Loughborough Trust Purchase. A small group of firearms includes a caplock pistol made by A.R. Mendenhall and dated 1855, a Colt Navy revolver dated 1860, and a caplock rifle by W.O. Robertson dated 1870. Ten bowie-style knives span roughly a century, from the mid-1800s to the mid-1900s. There are hunting horns too, among them one ornamented with a hunting dog, dated 1860, and an Arkansas Traveler horn from 1880. Smaller treasures feel more personal: a hair brooch dated 1868 that belonged to Sarah Downs, a pocketwatch dated 1870 that belonged to A.A. Turner, and a bracelet and pendant made by Elsie Freund in 1930.

## Five Centuries of Clay

The pottery collection reaches deepest into the past. Several Caddo pieces were made between 1500 and 1600, long before any territorial legislature met anywhere in Arkansas. Later pieces include a jar by George Washington Cranston from 1860, churns made by J.R. Wilbur in 1867 and by Lafayette Glass Pottery in 1870, and 1920s pieces from Niloak Pottery and Camden Pottery, along with a 1926 vase by Charles Stehm. The photograph collection adds names such as Thomas W. Bankes, B.S. Alford, Diehl and Kennedy, and the Keystone View Company. Together they make the museum a portrait of Arkansas assembled from fragments, housed in the buildings one woman fought to save.

## Flight Context

The Historic Arkansas Museum sits at 34.7464°N, 92.2690°W near Cumberland and East 3rd Streets in downtown Little Rock, a few blocks south of the Arkansas River. The half-block of historic houses is small, so orient on the river bridges and downtown towers from 1,500–2,500 ft AGL. The Old State House, one of Loughborough's "three Capitols," stands a short distance to the northwest near the riverfront. Bill & Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT) lies about 2.5 nm to the southeast; expect airline traffic and coordinate with approach control. North Little Rock Municipal Airport (FAA identifier ORK; no ICAO code listed) is about 5 nm north across the river. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
