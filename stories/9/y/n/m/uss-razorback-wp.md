# USS Razorback

A World War II submarine that witnessed Japan's surrender, served Turkey for three decades and was floated up the shallow Arkansas River on barges now rests as a museum ship in North Little Rock.

**Location:** 34.7536, -92.2636

**Categories:** Balao-class submarines, Museum ships in Arkansas, World War II submarines of the United States, Cold War submarines of the United States, Tourist attractions in North Little Rock, Arkansas

## Introduction

In March 2004, the river city of North Little Rock, in landlocked Arkansas, bought a submarine from Turkey for $37,500. Private donations paid for the purchase and for the long tow home. The boat had been a Turkish warship for three decades, and before that an American one that had been in Tokyo Bay for the surrender of Japan. She is USS Razorback, and she is arguably the longest-serving combat front-line submarine still in existence, with 56 years of active duty under two flags.

## Four Launches in One Day

Razorback's keel was laid at the Portsmouth Navy Yard in Kittery, Maine, on 9 September 1943. On 27 January 1944 she slid into the water alongside Redfish and Ronquil, and Scabbardfish followed a few hours later, making it the only time the U.S. Navy has launched four submarines at one shipyard in a single day. Her sponsor was Hazel DuMont (Grant) Davis. Razorback is the only Navy ship named for the razorback, the fin whale known to science as Balaenoptera physalus, and despite what Arkansans might assume, she has nothing to do with the University of Arkansas mascot. She was commissioned on 3 April 1944 under Lieutenant Commander A.M. Bontier. In Block Island Sound, off the New England coast, she once ran aground on Race Point Ledge, between Fisher Island's Race Point and Race Rock. A board of inquiry removed the captain, replacing him with Commander Roy S. Benson, and her executive officer was replaced as well.

## Witness in Tokyo Bay

Razorback earned five battle stars for her World War II service. On 31 August 1945 she entered Tokyo Bay with 11 other submarines to take part in the formal Japanese surrender, departing on 3 September. The war's end did not end her career. In August 1952 she was decommissioned for conversion to a GUPPY IIA-type submarine, and she returned to service in January 1954. Her American service stretched across 26 years and into another war. By the end, she had collected four campaign stars for the Vietnam War and two awards of the Armed Forces Expeditionary Medal. Not long after a final return to the West Coast, she was decommissioned at Hunters Point Naval Shipyard on 30 November and, on the same day, transferred to the Turkish Navy.

## Muratreis

Turkey recommissioned the submarine on 17 December 1971 as TCG Muratreis (S-336), naming her for Murat Reis, the celebrated Ottoman admiral. She served the Turkish Navy for three decades before decommissioning on 8 August 2001. Together, her two careers add up to 56 years of active duty, the basis for calling her, arguably, the longest-serving combat front-line submarine still in existence. Her Turkish name honored a sailor of an earlier age, and her crews kept her on duty into a new century. Less than three years after Turkey retired her, North Little Rock made its offer, and the old Muratreis began a final voyage that would return her to her first name, Razorback.

## Lifted Up the River

Razorback left Turkey under tow on 5 May 2004, crossed the Mediterranean to Gibraltar and then the Atlantic, reaching Key West, Florida, on the evening of 13 June. From there she was towed to New Orleans, arriving on 19 June, and began the slow climb up the Mississippi and Arkansas rivers. After a stop for the dedication of the Montgomery Point Lock and Dam on 16 July, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers held her up over safety concerns. The problem was water. She drew 11.5 feet at the bow and nearly 15 feet at the stern, and parts of the Arkansas River were less than 9 feet deep. The solution was a pair of barges used as pontoons, lifting the submarine a few feet off the bottom while keeping her low enough to pass under the bridges, just as USS Batfish had done 32 years earlier. On 29 August 2004 she reached her berth at the Arkansas Inland Maritime Museum, and she opened to the public on 15 May 2005. Guided tours run most Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, with more days added in summer.

## Flight Context

USS Razorback is berthed at 34.7536°N, 92.2636°W on the North Little Rock bank of the Arkansas River, across from downtown Little Rock. From 1,000–2,000 ft AGL, follow the river and look for the long dark hull moored along the shoreline near the downtown bridges. North Little Rock Municipal Airport (FAA identifier ORK; no ICAO code listed) is about 5 nm to the north, and Bill & Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT) is about 2.5 nm to the southeast, so expect airline traffic and coordinate with approach control. The river corridor gives a clear visual line in most conditions, though summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
