# Ray Winder Field

For 74 years of summer nights, Little Rock's Travelers played in a ballpark named for the ticket seller who spent sixty years running the club; today its scoreboard still stands over a parking lot.

**Location:** 34.7453, -92.3275

**Categories:** Baseball venues, Defunct minor league baseball venues, Sports venues in Little Rock, Arkansas, Arkansas Travelers, Sports venues demolished in 2012

## Introduction

Over a paved lot in Little Rock, a baseball scoreboard still stands. There is no infield below it and no grandstand, only pavement. It is what remains of Ray Winder Field, where the Arkansas Travelers played for 74 years. The park was built in the Depression, saved once by a public stock drive that bought the city a new team, and loved into old age by fans who came for what one promoter called "old-time baseball."

## Travelers Field

The ballpark went up during 1931 as a new home for the Little Rock Travelers, the minor league club that would later become the Arkansas Travelers. The team left Kavanaugh Field, near Little Rock Central High School, and opened its 1932 season at the new park on April 13. Fittingly, the place was called Travelers Field at first. Home plate sat roughly in the north-northwest corner of the property. Beyond right field, West 8th Street and later Interstate 630 bounded the park on the south. Jonesboro Drive bordered the right field corner and South Monroe Street ran behind the first-base stands, while buildings along South Palm Street hemmed in left field.

## A Lifetime in the Front Office

Raymond Winder gave the club something like sixty years. He started as a ticket seller in 1915, became the Travelers' business manager in 1931 and bought a share of the team in 1944. Then, after the 1958 season, the franchise left for Shreveport, and for the first time since 1914 Little Rock had no baseball club. The park sat empty through 1959 while efforts continued to bring the game back. A public stock drive raised enough money to buy a bankrupt New Orleans franchise, and the Travelers were reborn in Little Rock for the 1960 season. Winder was asked once again to handle the day-to-day work of rebuilding the club. On August 26, 1966, Travelers Field was renamed Ray Winder Field in his honor.

## Selling Nostalgia

In 1976 the Travelers hired Bill Valentine, a former American League umpire, as general manager. He held the job for 31 years, until 2007, and stayed on as executive vice president for two more seasons before retiring in March 2009. Valentine inherited an aging ballpark and limited funds, so he turned the park's age into its selling point. He promoted Ray Winder Field as history, and through his efforts it gained recognition as one of the oldest active parks in minor league baseball. Fans were encouraged to travel to Little Rock just to experience it. His pitch was simple: "A great place to see old-time baseball, wholesome entertainment aimed squarely at the family market."

## The Last Out and a Long Goodbye

Construction of a new ballpark across the river in North Little Rock began in 2005. On September 3, 2006, the last game of the season, a crowd of more than 8,000 filled Ray Winder Field one final time and watched the Travelers beat the Springfield Cardinals 7–3. From 2007 on, the team played at Dickey-Stephens Park. The old park sat vacant for almost five years while its future was argued. Some citizens wanted it restored for city and college teams, as Birmingham had done with Rickwood Field. Others wanted parking for the nearby University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences, and the Arkansas Zoological Foundation proposed turning the grandstand into a viewing area for an Asian elephant exhibit at the Little Rock Zoo. The city showed no interest in preserving it, and the park was sold to the university for parking. On May 14, 2011, the gates opened a last time as the remaining wooden seats, dating to 1932, were dismantled and handed to fans on a first-come, first-served basis. In July 2012 the rest of the ballpark was demolished and the ground was paved over, with the scoreboard left standing.

## Flight Context

The former site of Ray Winder Field lies at 34.7453°N, 92.3275°W, west of downtown Little Rock beside the Little Rock Zoo and the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences campus, just north of Interstate 630. From 1,500–2,000 ft AGL, look for the paved lot with the surviving scoreboard near the zoo grounds. Bill & Hillary Clinton National Airport/Adams Field (KLIT) is about 5 nm to the east-southeast, so expect airline traffic and coordinate with approach control. North Little Rock Municipal Airport (FAA identifier ORK; no ICAO code listed) lies about 6 nm to the northeast across the Arkansas River, and Dickey-Stephens Park, the Travelers' current home, sits near the North Little Rock end of the Broadway Bridge. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
