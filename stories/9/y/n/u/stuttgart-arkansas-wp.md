# Stuttgart, Arkansas

Named by a homesick German pastor in 1880, the Grand Prairie town of Stuttgart grew into the self-styled Rice and Duck Capital of the World, home to rice cooperatives and a world duck-calling championship.

**Location:** 34.4969, -91.5508

**Categories:** Cities in Arkansas, County seats in Arkansas, German-American culture in Arkansas, Agriculture, Waterfowl hunting

## Introduction

For a few days every Thanksgiving week, the sound that defines Stuttgart, Arkansas, is a duck call. Callers of all ages arrive from around the world to compete in the World Championship Duck Calling Contest, the centerpiece of a November festival in a town of about 8,300 people. Known as the "Rice and Duck Capital of the World," Stuttgart earns both halves of the title. It is an international destination for waterfowl hunting along the Mississippi Flyway, and it sits on the Arkansas Grand Prairie, a region known for rice. Its name, though, points somewhere else entirely: to southern Germany, and to a pastor who needed something to call his post office.

## A Pastor's Homesick Name

The Reverend Adam Bürkle came from Plattenhardt, in Württemberg, in what is now Germany. He moved to the United States in 1852, lived for a time in Ohio, and then founded a settlement at a place called Gum Pond on the Arkansas prairie. On April 30, 1880, Bürkle opened a post office, and a post office needs a name. He chose one in honor of his homeland: Stuttgart.

The German thread has never quite come loose. The town still holds an annual German Heritage Festival. Meanwhile the little village grew into a crossroads, where three north-south U.S. highways, 63, 79 and 165, meet about 45 miles southeast of Little Rock and roughly 110 miles southwest of Memphis. The Union Pacific Railroad runs through too, on its way between Memphis and Pine Bluff.

## Grain of the Grand Prairie

Rice is the backbone here. Riceland Foods, a farmer-owned agricultural marketing cooperative and the world's largest miller and marketer of rice, is headquartered in Stuttgart. So is Producers Rice Mill, another farmer-owned cooperative that ranks among the largest millers and marketers of rice. About seven miles east of town, researchers at the University of Arkansas System's Rice Research and Extension Center breed new varieties of rice, alongside the USDA's Dale Bumpers National Rice Research Center next door.

The supporting trades follow the water. Layne Arkansas installs the wells that pump irrigation water onto farms, along with city water systems. Delta Plastics of the South makes plastic irrigation tubes at a plant in Stuttgart, a replacement for the rigid plastic or aluminum pipe that farmers once used to water their crops. Stratton Seed markets soybeans and soybean seed, and a Lennox factory builds air conditioners. For the surrounding counties, the 49-bed Baptist Health Medical Center-Stuttgart, opened in 1957 as Stuttgart Memorial Hospital, is a regional anchor and one of the town's largest employers.

## Where the Flyway Comes Down

Duck hunting is one of Stuttgart's leading tourist attractions, and the town is built to serve it. Mack's Prairie Wings, known around the world as a waterfowl outfitter through its store and its online presence, is based here.

Then comes November. Over Thanksgiving weekend, the Wings Over the Prairie Festival takes over town with carnival rides, craft tents and the Queen Mallard Pageant, all of it celebrating the hunt. The main event is the World Championship Duck Calling Contest, which draws competitors of all ages from around the world to show off what they can do with a duck call. For a few days, a sound most people only hear from a blind on a cold morning becomes a stage performance, judged and applauded in a farm town on the prairie.

## Professors on the Frontier

Stuttgart bet on education early. From 1889 to 1915 the town was home to Stuttgart College, also known as the Stuttgart Training School and the Stuttgart Normal School. Its affiliations shifted over the years, from local control to the Methodist Episcopal Church, South, alongside Henderson State University and Hendrix College, and later to the United Presbyterian Church, when it was linked with Monmouth College and Tarkio College. The school brought many well-educated professors to what was still a small frontier town and served as an early regional center of learning on the Grand Prairie. It failed for lack of financial support, and because Stuttgart High School opened in 1912.

That instinct to gather the region's story in one place survives. The Museum of the Arkansas Grand Prairie celebrates the history of Stuttgart and the surrounding prairie, while the Grand Prairie Center, on the local campus of Phillips Community College of the University of Arkansas, brings in live bands, stage plays and an annual arts festival.

## A Prairie Town on Film

Stuttgart has had a second life on screen. The 1989 film Rosalie Goes Shopping, directed by Percy Adlon and starring Marianne Sägebrecht, was set here. Some of the town's scenery appears in two films by director Jeff Nichols: Shotgun Stories (2007), starring Michael Shannon, and Mud (2012), starring Matthew McConaughey and Reese Witherspoon. And one of its native sons, Douglas A. Blackmon, went on to win a Pulitzer Prize for Slavery by Another Name.

The population has eased from 9,745 in 2000 to 8,264 in 2020, but the town Adam Bürkle named for his homeland still draws visitors from around the world every Thanksgiving week, all of them listening for the same sound.

## Flight Context

Stuttgart lies at 34.497°N, 91.551°W on the flat Arkansas Grand Prairie, about 45 miles southeast of Little Rock. From 3,000-4,500 ft AGL the town stands out as a compact grid amid broad, geometric farm fields; look for the junction of US 63, US 79 and US 165 and the Union Pacific line running through town. Stuttgart Municipal Airport / Carl Humphrey Field (KSGT) is about 6 nm north of town in Prairie County. Almyra Municipal Airport (FAA identifier M73; no ICAO code) is about 6.5 nm southeast. The terrain is nearly featureless, so use highways and rail lines for orientation. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are common over the Delta; cooler months usually bring clearer air.
