# Roundtop Filling Station

A high school senior nicknamed "Happy" started pumping gas by hand at this oddly shaped 1936 station on U.S. 67, and the station wore five oil brands before it closed in 1981 and began a long second life as a landmark.

**Location:** 34.8122, -92.1783

**Categories:** Gas stations on the National Register of Historic Places in Arkansas, Roadside architecture, U.S. Route 67, Sherwood, Arkansas, 1936 establishments in Arkansas

## Introduction

In 1936 Wallace David "Happy" Williford was still a senior at Jacksonville High School when the Pierce Oil Company asked him to run its brand-new filling station on U.S. Highway 67. He said yes. He worked the pumps before school and after it, and he paid men to cover the hours he spent in class, though he later said it was hard to find anyone willing to work for just $3.50 a week. The little station he ran, known as the Roundtop, now stands in Sherwood, Arkansas, as one of only two places in the city listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

## A Baby Standard on Highway 67

Pierce Oil was one of the "baby Standards," the companies formed after the U.S. government broke up John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil Company in 1911. It operated gas stations across Arkansas, southern Missouri, western Louisiana, Oklahoma, Texas and Mexico. In 1936 it hired the Justin Matthews Company to build a uniquely shaped station along U.S. 67, and the Roundtop's name hints at what made it stand out. The design is believed to be the work of Frank Carmean, the Matthews company's architect, and the building went up under the hands of two brothers, C.C. and Powderley Eubanks. Sherwood's only other National Register listing, the Sylvan Hills Country Club Golf Course, makes for unlikely company.

## Two Cents a Gallon

Williford had already been working at another Pierce-owned station in Jacksonville when the company offered him the Roundtop. The terms were simple: for rent, Pierce took two cents for every gallon of gas he sold. The work was physical. At first the station had no electric pumps, so Williford and his helpers hand-pumped gasoline up into a glass bowl before they could fill each car's tank. Around 1940, when the Sinclair Oil Company bought Pierce, the Roundtop became a Sinclair station, and electric pumps arrived at about the same time. By the mid-1950s Williford had saved $8,000, enough to buy the station outright.

## Five Brands, One Building

Business was good enough that Williford expanded. He opened another station at the foot of the Broadway Bridge in North Little Rock, near where Dickey-Stephens Park stands today, and later closed it to open one at 914 East Broadway. While he ran those, he leased the Roundtop to others. Meanwhile the sign out front kept changing. In the early 1950s Sinclair sold many of its Arkansas stations, the Roundtop among them, to Phillips Petroleum, and it became a Phillips 66. In the 1970s it flew the DX brand, and when Sun Oil acquired DX, the Roundtop ended up a Sunoco. Williford came back in the mid-1970s and ran the station himself until he retired and closed it in 1981.

## Auction, Film and Fire

In 1989 local auctioneer Wayne Ball, who had worked at the station in his youth, sold the Roundtop at auction on Williford's behalf. North Little Rock businessman George E. Brown won it and planned to renovate the dilapidated building, but he died before he could. In 1999 his heirs donated the station to the City of Sherwood, which had annexed the area in 1975. For years it sat abandoned and suffered vandalism and theft. In the mid-2000s Becki Vassar, a former Sherwood city council member, pushed for its restoration and succeeded in getting it listed on the National Register in 2008. The station even got a star turn: in 2010 it appeared in "The Last Ride," a film about Hank Williams Sr. directed by Arkansas native Harry Thomason. In 2013 the city won a $50,000 historic preservation grant for the project, and the new Sherwood History and Heritage Committee weighed restoring the station to its original condition as a police substation. Then, on November 2, 2014, just days before it was set to reopen to the public, a fire damaged its exterior. Police recorded it as an arson attempt. The work was finished on January 3, 2015, and the Sherwood Police Department now uses the restored building as its Southside Substation.

## Flight Context

The station is tiny, so orient on the highway and surrounding commercial strip from 1,000–1,500 ft AGL.
