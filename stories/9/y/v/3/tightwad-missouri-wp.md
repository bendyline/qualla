# Tightwad, Missouri

A Henry County village of fewer than 60 people turned a legend about an overpriced watermelon into a name, and the name into a bank whose publicity alone drew millions in deposits.

**Location:** 38.3061, -93.5514

**Categories:** Villages in Henry County, Missouri, Villages in Missouri, Unusual place names, Banking history

## Introduction

The story goes that a storekeeper here once cheated a customer, a postman, by charging him an extra 50 cents for a better watermelon. Some tellings swap the watermelon for a rooster. Whatever changed hands, the name that came out of the transaction stuck to this small crossroads on Missouri Route 7 in Henry County, and it has traveled much farther than the village itself. Fewer than 60 people lived in Tightwad at the 2020 census. Its name, meanwhile, once helped a tiny branch bank gather millions of dollars in deposits.

## Before the Joke

The village began with a gentler name. It was originally called Edgewood, for the woods near the original town site. How it became Tightwad rests on a story rather than a record: the new name is said to come from that storekeeper's overcharge, and even the tellers cannot agree whether he was selling a watermelon or a rooster. The village is still small enough to take in at a glance, covering about one square mile, nearly all of it land, strung along Route 7 not far from the shores of Truman Reservoir.

## Banking on a Name

In 1984, the Citizens Bank of Windsor opened a branch in Tightwad, operating first out of a portable trailer and eventually from a one-story brick building. Publicity over the unusual name drew deposits totaling more than $2 million, a remarkable sum for a village of a few dozen people. The hopes riding on it were bigger still. Thanks to nearby Truman Reservoir, Tightwad saw some limited growth starting in the mid-1980s, but the business boom the bank expected from the lake never materialized. The bank's luck ran worse than the town's: it fell victim to armed robbery twice in the 1990s. In November 2006, UMB, the bank's owner at the time, announced that the Tightwad branch would close and its accounts would move to UMB locations in Warsaw and Clinton.

## A Second Act From Kansas

The name proved too good to waste. In May 2008, Tightwad Bank reopened under new ownership as an FDIC-insured institution, a full-service branch of the former Reading State Bank of Reading, Kansas. The owners went further and renamed the Reading location Tightwad Bank as well, so that two towns in two states banked under the same cheerfully unflattering label. "We're seeking the customers with a sense of humor," co-owner Donald Higdon admitted to The Washington Post in 2008. The strategy worked for a time. In 2010 the two Tightwad Bank locations reported combined deposits of more than $20 million. By July 2018, however, the branch in Tightwad itself had closed, and by April 2019 the former bank building was in private, personal use.

## A Small Town, Growing Older

In 2010, Tightwad's business district came down to four establishments: the bank, a café, a tavern and a convenience store. The community skews older, with a median age of 53.5 at the 2010 census and 29 percent of residents aged 65 or over. The bank that made the village briefly famous is gone now, but the name remains on the map, still attached to a story nobody can quite pin down: a watermelon or a rooster, a storekeeper, and a postman who was charged more than he should have been.

## Flight Context

Tightwad sits at approximately 38.31°N, 93.55°W along Missouri Route 7 in Henry County, west-central Missouri. It is a very small village and hard to pick out; from 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, use Route 7 and the nearby arms of Truman Reservoir as references. Nearby airports: Clinton Regional (KGLY), about 7 nm west-northwest; Warsaw Municipal (KRAW), about 10 nm east-northeast; Lincoln Municipal (FAA identifier 0R2, no ICAO code), about 12 nm east-northeast. Summer haze can reduce visibility over the reservoir country.
