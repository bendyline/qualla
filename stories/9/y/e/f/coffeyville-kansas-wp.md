# Coffeyville, Kansas

Named on a coin toss, Coffeyville is the Kansas border town whose citizens stopped the Dalton Gang in 1892, built parts for the Norden bombsight, and rebuilt after the oil-fouled flood of 2007.

**Location:** 37.0378, -95.6264

**Categories:** Cities in Kansas, Cities in Montgomery County, Kansas, Old West history, Dalton Gang, Populated places established in 1869

## Introduction

The fake beards did not work. On October 5, 1892, members of the Dalton Gang rode into Coffeyville, Kansas, disguised and planning to rob two banks at once, the First National and the Condon, which faced each other across the street. Law officers and ordinary townspeople recognized them anyway, and when the outlaws came out of one of the banks, the town opened fire. Coffeyville still marks that day every October. It is only one chapter in the life of a border town that has been shaped by chance, by industry and, more than once, by trouble.

## Named by a Coin Toss

Colonel James A. Coffey founded the settlement in 1869 as an Indian trading post, serving the people living just across the border in what was then Indian Territory. Two years later the Leavenworth, Lawrence & Galveston Railroad made it a stop, tying it to wider markets, and a young surveyor, Napoleon B. Blanton, a U.S. Army captain, was sent to lay out the town. Who would name it was settled by the toss of a coin between Coffey and Blanton. Coffey won, and the town became Coffeyville. Even incorporation took two tries: the 1872 charter was voided as illegal, and the city was incorporated again in March 1873.

## Twelve Minutes in October

The Dalton raid lasted about twelve minutes. When it was over, four of the gang were dead: Bob and Grat Dalton, Bill Power and Dick Broadwell. The town paid dearly too. Three civilians, Lucius Baldwin, George Cubine and Charles Brown, died defending the town, along with the marshal, Charles T. Connelly, who fell in the alley now known as Death Alley. Emmett Dalton survived with 23 gunshot wounds, pleaded guilty to second-degree murder, and served 14 years before he was pardoned, though he later insisted he never fired a shot. The Dalton Defenders Museum, opened on October 5, 1963, honors the townspeople who died protecting Coffeyville.

## Gas, Clay and a Bombsight

Plentiful natural gas and abundant clay turned Coffeyville into a boomtown, and between 1890 and 1910 its population grew more than fivefold, from 2,282 to 12,687. Many of the buildings now on the National Register of Historic Places date from those years or soon after, including the old Condon National Bank, now the Perkins Building; the Brown Mansion, built around 1904 to 1907; the larger Bethel African Methodist Episcopal Church of about 1907; and the 1928 Midland Theater. Industry reached the world stage during World War II, when the local industrialist Douglas Brown founded Coffeyville Multiscope, which produced components of the Norden bombsight, whose accuracy and drift correction helped perfect precision daylight bombing. The town also claims some famous residents: the baseball great Walter Johnson lived here, and Wendell Willkie lived and taught school here.

## When the Verdigris Rose

On July 1, 2007, the Verdigris River crested ten feet above flood stage and flooded about a third of Coffeyville. The water topped the levees of the local refinery by four feet, and roughly 1,700 barrels, some 71,000 gallons, of crude oil mixed into the floodwater. Animals were found dead or injured in the flooded areas, coated in oil, and the EPA worked to keep the spill from reaching Oologah Lake downstream in Oklahoma, a source of drinking water; only a minimal amount got there. The city lost its drinkable water on July 3 until service returned on July 7. Displaced residents stayed with family and friends, in two churches and in a senior apartment complex, and rescued pets went to a temporary shelter at LeClere Park. Cleanup of the east side ran into early 2009, and the refinery's owner bought many flood-damaged homes to compensate their owners.

## Low Point, Center Point

Coffeyville sits in the southeast corner of Kansas, about half a mile north of the Oklahoma line on the west bank of the Verdigris, some 75 miles north of Tulsa. At 679 feet above sea level, it holds the lowest point in the state. It is the most populous city in Montgomery County and home to Coffeyville Community College, though the county seat is Independence, 16 miles northwest. The town even has an accidental place on the digital map: a spot just north of the Coffeyville Country Club is the default center of Google Maps' starting view of the 48 contiguous states, an accident of where that view happened to fall. It is a fitting honor for a town that has so often found itself at the center of things.

## Flight Context

Coffeyville lies at 37.038°N, 95.626°W in the southeast corner of Kansas, about half a mile north of the Oklahoma line on the west bank of the Verdigris River, roughly 75 miles north of Tulsa. From 2,500-4,000 feet the winding Verdigris and the town's refinery are useful landmarks; South Coffeyville, Oklahoma, sits about a mile south. Coffeyville Municipal Airport (KCFV), a former World War II Army airfield, lies about four miles northeast of the city along US-169, and Tulsa International Airport (KTUL) is the nearest major airport. Summers are hot and humid, with thunderstorms and occasional river flooding in wet seasons.
