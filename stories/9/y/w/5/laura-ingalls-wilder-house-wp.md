# Laura Ingalls Wilder House

At Rocky Ridge Farm outside Mansfield, Missouri, Laura Ingalls Wilder lived for more than sixty years and began the Little House books, in a farmhouse Almanzo built largely by hand and a stone cottage their daughter Rose gave them.

**Location:** 37.0994, -92.5561

## Introduction

The Little House books follow a family always moving on, from the Big Woods of Wisconsin to the prairie and beyond. The woman who wrote them, though, finally stayed put. From 1894 until her death in 1957, Laura Ingalls Wilder made her home at Rocky Ridge Farm, on what is now Highway A outside Mansfield, Missouri, in the Ozarks. More than sixty years in one place. It was here, not on any prairie, that she began writing the series that would carry her childhood to generations of readers, and her farmhouse is now a museum devoted to her and her work.

## A House That Grew by Hand

The farmhouse on Rocky Ridge was not bought finished. Almanzo Wilder did most of the building himself, with help from local carpenters and from Laura, using materials from the area, and the house grew over years into the shape it holds today. That slow growth shows. It is an irregularly shaped wood-frame building with a raised attic that approaches two full stories in places, while the original kitchen and one other room stay at a single story. The house has several porches, and dormers and gable windows bring light and air to the upper floor. Six rooms fill the main level, and three more sit above. It reads as a house that grew along with its family rather than one drawn up all at once.

## Rose's Gift

In 1928 the Wilders' daughter, Rose Wilder Lane, decided her parents should have a new house with modern conveniences. She took a Sears, Roebuck catalog design as her inspiration, had it adapted rather than ordered as a kit, and had it finished in local stone. The family called it the Rock House. Laura and Almanzo moved in at Christmas that year. Today a pathway about a third of a mile long leads from the farmhouse to the Rock House, which can also be reached from its own parking area. Together, the two buildings are among the few surviving places where Laura Ingalls Wilder lived.

## Where the Little Houses Were Written

It was in the Rock House that Laura, by then in her sixties, began writing the Little House books. The pioneer girl of the stories was now a farm wife in the Missouri hills, looking back across roughly half a century to the frontier of her childhood, and she set those memories down in a stone cottage her daughter had given her. The early books of the series took shape there. In 1936 Laura and Almanzo left the Rock House and returned to the farmhouse Almanzo had built, where they would live out their days. Laura died in 1957, and the farm that had been her home for more than six decades was about to become something new.

## Keeping Rocky Ridge

Shortly after Laura's death, local residents took legal steps to acquire the house, incorporating a non-profit organization to preserve her legacy. That group, the Laura Ingalls Wilder Home Association, still owns the property and opens the house to the public for tours. National recognition followed. The house was placed on the National Register of Historic Places on May 19, 1970, and designated a National Historic Landmark on July 17, 1991. Visitors today tour the farmhouse Almanzo built, then follow the path to the Rock House where the Little House books began, tracing the last long chapter of a life that started on the frontier.

## Flight Context

Rocky Ridge Farm lies at 37.0994°N, 92.5561°W just east of Mansfield in Wright County, in the Missouri Ozarks. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, find Mansfield along the U.S. 60 corridor; the farm sits on its outskirts amid rolling, partly wooded hills and pasture. The buildings themselves are small and hard to pick out, so use the town as your reference. Springfield–Branson National Airport (KSGF) is about 41 nm to the west. Ozark weather is generally benign, but watch for morning fog in the valleys and for spring and summer thunderstorms.
