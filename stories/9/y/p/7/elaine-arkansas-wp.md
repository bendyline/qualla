# Elaine, Arkansas

A small Phillips County town in the Arkansas Delta, Elaine is best known for the 1919 massacre in which white mobs killed hundreds of Black people, by some estimates, after sharecroppers tried to organize a union.

**Location:** 34.3086, -90.8542

**Categories:** Cities in Phillips County, Arkansas, Cities in Arkansas

## Introduction

On the night of September 30, 1919, about a hundred Black farmers gathered in a church at Hoop Spur, near the town of Elaine in Phillips County, Arkansas. They were sharecroppers, and they had come to organize. What followed over the next two days became one of the worst episodes of racial and labor violence in American history. Elaine today is a small town of a few hundred people in the Arkansas Delta, but its name has never been separated from what happened in the fields and woods around it that autumn.

## A Meeting at Hoop Spur

The farmers at the church were members of the Progressive Farmers and Household Union of America, founded by Robert L. Hill, a Black farmer from Winchester, Arkansas. Sharecroppers worked land they did not own, for planters who held most of the power, and organizing was a way to face those planters together. The planters resisted. That night two white men, including a local deputy, tried to break up the meeting. In a confrontation with the guards posted outside, one of the white men was killed. That death set off everything that came after, as word spread to town and across the surrounding countryside.

## Two Days of Terror

Hundreds of white men from Phillips County and neighboring areas rushed to the Elaine area. They did not confine themselves to the union members. They attacked the Black community at large, and mobs spread throughout the county. Most of the violence was suppressed after October 1, but by then an unknown number of Black people had been murdered; estimates run from a few dozen to several hundred, and 237 is a figure often cited. Five white people died in the events. Governor Charles Brough went to the area with more than 500 U.S. soldiers from Camp Pike. The troops disarmed both sides and arrested 285 Black residents. Behind every one of those estimates were people with families and homes in the county, killed because they were Black.

## Trials Without Justice

In October and November 1919, an all-white state grand jury indicted 122 Black people. Twelve of the defendants, who became known as the Elaine Twelve, were convicted and sentenced to death. Many of their trials lasted less than an hour, and the juries took fewer than ten minutes to deliberate. The Black attorney Scipio Jones took up their defense, and the appeals reached the U.S. Supreme Court. In Moore v. Dempsey in 1923, the Court held that a trial dominated by a mob could violate a defendant's constitutional right to due process, a significant ruling in American law. The last of the defendants were freed in 1925, when, hours before Governor Thomas McRae left office, indefinite furloughs were issued for them. In September 2019, one hundred years after the killings, an Elaine Massacre Memorial was unveiled.

## Voices from the Delta

Elaine's history is not only one of violence. The town has a long list of people connected to it who shaped American music and letters. The blues musician Jimmy McCracklin is counted among its natives, though sources differ on whether he was born here or in St. Louis, and the author Richard Wright lived in Elaine for a time. The musician Levon Helm and the country musician John Hughey are also counted among its notable people, and the Texas politician Barry Williamson was raised here. Together they reflect the Delta's deep musical and literary roots, and the way a small town can send its people far beyond its borders.

## A Small Town Today

Elaine has grown smaller. The 2000 census counted 865 people; by 2020 the population was 509. At the turn of the century the town was about 58 percent Black and 39 percent white, and 41.1 percent of its residents lived below the poverty line. The surrounding agriculture includes rice production, which draws seasonal migrant laborers from Mexico and Latin America to the town, to nearby Lake View and to the Helena area. Schools have consolidated as well. The Elaine School District once ran Elaine High School and Lucilia Wood Elementary School, but on July 1, 2006, it merged into the Marvell district, and Elaine's students now attend schools in Marvell.

## Flight Context

Elaine lies at 34.3086°N, 90.8542°W in Phillips County, in the flat farmland of the Arkansas Delta west of the Mississippi River. From 1,500–2,500 ft AGL, the small town grid stands out among large rice and row-crop fields; the Mississippi River and its levees lie to the east, and Helena–West Helena is to the northeast. Thompson–Robbins Airport (KHEE), northwest of Helena–West Helena, is about 18 nm to the north-northeast. Delta weather brings summer haze and thunderstorms, and winter fog can settle over the fields in the early morning.
