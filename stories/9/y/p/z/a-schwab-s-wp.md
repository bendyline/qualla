# A. Schwab's

Founded in 1876 by an Alsatian Jewish immigrant, A. Schwab's dry goods store is the last of Beale Street's original businesses, a family shop turned museum, curiosity cabinet and ice-cream stop.

**Location:** 35.1395, -90.0527

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Memphis, Tennessee, Retail companies established in 1876, Historic district contributing properties in Tennessee, 1876 establishments in Tennessee, National Register of Historic Places in Memphis, Tennessee, Tourist attractions in Memphis, Tennessee

## Introduction

A store on Beale Street in Memphis, Tennessee, was once billed as the retailer of the largest overalls in the world. It sold two pairs a year. That detail says a great deal about A. Schwab's, a dry goods store that kept odd things in stock, kept the same family in charge for 136 years, and kept its doors open while nearly everything around it changed. Today it is the sole remaining original business on Beale Street, a street better known for the blues than for boots, bolts of cloth and hardware.

## Fleeing a War

The store's founder, Abraham Joseph Schwab, was an Alsatian Jewish immigrant. He fled to the United States to escape German enlistment during the Franco-Prussian War, and he found his way to Memphis. There he joined the Hirsch family in business, and in 1876 they opened a store at 66 Beale selling men's shoes and boots under the name "Hirsch, Schwab & Co." The partnership prospered. Over the following decade the store expanded beyond footwear into clothing, hardware and home appliances. In 1886 the two families went their separate ways, and Schwab carried on independently. From then on the business would bear his own name, and his family's.

## Up and Down 163 Beale

After the split, Schwab moved his shop to 149 Beale under the name A. Schwab. In 1911 the business passed to his sons, Sam, Elias and Leo, who moved it to the first floor of its current home at 163 Beale. In 1924 the store grew to the size it is today, adding the building's second floor and the adjacent storefront at 165 Beale, which had previously been one of the original Piggly Wiggly grocery stores. The family held on through the generations. Sam's daughter, Eleanor Schwab Braslow, and Elias's son, Abram Schwab, inherited the store and divided the work between them. Eleanor kept the books and managed the stock. Abram served as the business's public face, the Schwab customers met across the counter.

## The Last Original

Beale Street has gone through many lives, and A. Schwab's has outlasted every other business that opened with it. It is often called the oldest store in the Mid-South, and it is described as occupying the oldest remaining building on Beale Street. Think about what that building has seen from its windows: the street's rise as a center of Black commerce and music in Memphis, its years of decline, and its rebirth as an entertainment district. Through all of it, the same family owned the store. That run ended at the end of 2011, when, after 136 years of Schwab ownership, the family sold the business. The A. Schwab name carries on, and so does its place in the story of the street. For visitors walking Beale today, the old dry goods store offers a link to a time when people came here to shop for shoes, clothing and hardware, not only to hear the music.

## Hoodoo, Memorabilia and Ice Cream

A. Schwab's is now a local tourist attraction, and each floor has its own personality. The first floor sells quirky merchandise and tourist memorabilia. Up on the balcony mezzanine, the Beale Street Museum displays a collection of memorabilia from the street's history, along with items and records from the Schwab family itself. The second floor serves as an event venue and houses the store's hoodoo items and musical goods, goods tied to the folk traditions and music of the African American South. And the old next-door shop, the one that once held a Piggly Wiggly, has been converted into an old-fashioned soda fountain serving gelato, milkshakes and malts, so a visit can end with something sweet.
