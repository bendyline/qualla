# Mud Island, Memphis

Built by the river itself out of silt, gravel and sand, Memphis's Mud Island has been a downtown airport, a riverside museum and concert stage, a walkable model of the Mississippi, and a neighborhood.

**Location:** 35.1460, -90.0580

**Categories:** Landforms of Shelby County, Tennessee, Neighborhoods in Memphis, Tennessee, Parks in Tennessee, Peninsulas of Tennessee, River islands of Tennessee, Tourist attractions in Memphis, Tennessee

## Introduction

For eleven years, pilots could land almost on the doorstep of downtown Memphis. From 1959 to 1970, the Memphis Downtown Airport operated on Mud Island, a strip of land just across a narrow harbor from the city's riverfront, used mainly by wealthy businessmen who wanted quick access to downtown. Then the Hernando de Soto Bridge came through, and the airport was shut down. It was only one of the lives this restless piece of ground has lived. Mud Island has been an airfield, a museum, a concert venue and a neighborhood, and it did not even exist in its present form until the river built it.

## Made by the River

Mud Island is exactly what its name suggests. A buildup of silt, gravel and sand deposited by the river formed it, with a peninsula already showing on an 1884 map, and for a time it went by a different name, City Island. Although it is called an island, it is really a peninsula. The Mississippi River runs along its western side, the Wolf River borders it to the north, and Wolf River Harbor separates it from downtown Memphis to the east. That position, surrounded by water yet within sight of the city's skyline, has shaped everything built on it since, from the short-lived airport to the park and homes that occupy it today.

## A River You Can Walk

The island's most unusual feature is a replica of the Mississippi River carved out of cement, a scale model that lets visitors walk the lower river on foot. It traces the river from Cairo, Illinois, down to the Gulf of Mexico, at a scale where one step covers roughly a mile, and it was built to end in a broad pool standing in for the Gulf itself. The model is part of Mud Island River Park, which opened in 1982 and is now managed and operated by the Memphis River Parks Partnership. A monorail, the Memphis Suspension Railway, once connected the island to downtown, though it has been out of service since 2018.

## Ten Thousand Years Under One Roof

From 1982 to 2019, Mud Island was also home to the Mississippi River Museum. Its 18 galleries and exhibits presented the history of the lower Mississippi River Valley across the last 10,000 years, and it displayed more than 5,000 artifacts. The old museum building has a new tenant: the first phase of an immersive attraction, Baron Von Opperbean & The River of Time, opened there on May 1, 2026. Nearby, the Mud Island Amphitheater, built of concrete the same year the museum opened, could seat up to 5,000 people. Its stage hosted concerts and shows for more than three decades, with performers including Bob Dylan, Journey, Heart and Peter Frampton, though it has gone unused since 2018. The island has had its moment on screen as well: it is the setting for the climactic chase in the 1993 film The Firm.

## Harbor Town

Much of Mud Island today is taken up by Harbor Town, a residential neighborhood of mansions, single-family homes and apartment complexes. Residents have trails and ponds close at hand, along with a shopping district and a private school. The River Inn of Harbor Town lists several dining venues, among them Paulette's Restaurant, Tug's Casual Grill and the Terrace rooftop lounge, and Cordelia's Market is the island's main grocery store. A section of the Wolf River Greenway runs across the island, giving walkers, runners and cyclists a trail that connects to Greenbelt Park. On land built up from river mud, a whole neighborhood now stands.

## Flight Context

Mud Island centers on 35.1460°N, 90.0580°W, the long, narrow peninsula between the Mississippi River and Wolf River Harbor directly west of downtown Memphis. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL it is one of the easiest landmarks in the city to spot: follow the harbor channel separating the island from the downtown bluff, with the Hernando de Soto Bridge crossing the river at its northern end and the Memphis Pyramid just across the harbor. The island once hosted the Memphis Downtown Airport (closed in 1970); today the nearest field is General DeWitt Spain Airport (FAA identifier M01), about 3.3 nm to the north, and the main airport, Frederick W. Smith International Airport (KMEM), is about 7 nm to the south-southeast. Downtown lies under Memphis Class B airspace, so coordinate with Memphis Approach and expect heavy cargo traffic. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
