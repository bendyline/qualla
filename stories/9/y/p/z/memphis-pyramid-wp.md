# Memphis Pyramid

Dreamed up by a Memphis artist in 1954 as a nod to the city's Egyptian namesake, the 321-foot Memphis Pyramid has been a basketball arena, a heavyweight boxing stage and, since 2015, a Bass Pro Shops megastore with a hotel inside.

**Location:** 35.1556, -90.0519

**Categories:** Pyramids in the United States, Landmarks in Tennessee, Former NBA venues, Memphis Tigers basketball venues, Buildings and structures in Memphis, Tennessee

## Introduction

Inside a 32-story pyramid on the bank of the Mississippi River, people go bowling, try an archery range, shop for fishing gear and check into a hotel. At the top, an elevator delivers visitors to an observation deck beside the apex. The building is the Memphis Pyramid, a play on the Tennessee city's namesake in Egypt, famous for pyramids of its own. It stands 321 feet tall on base sides of 591 feet, by some measures the tenth-tallest pyramid in the world, and it has had more lives than most buildings get.

## An Artist's Vision on the Bluffs

The idea began around 1954 with Mark C. Hartz, a Memphis artist. His original concept called for three pyramids on the south bluffs of Memphis, overlooking the Mississippi. The largest would have been two-thirds the size of the Great Pyramid of Giza, near Memphis, Egypt, and the two flanking it two-thirds the size of the main one. Then the project languished for three decades, until Hartz's younger son, Jon Brent Hartz, revived it. The elder Hartz, well known for his architectural renderings, drew a new pyramid glazed in bronze glass. After years of negotiation, entrepreneur John Tigrett adopted the younger Hartz's concept as a symbol for the city. Ground was broken on September 15, 1989, and what was then called the Great American Pyramid opened on November 9, 1991.

## Plans That Never Were

Tigrett brought in Sidney Shlenker, a part-owner of the Denver Nuggets and several entertainment companies, to manage construction and develop tourist attractions in and around the building. The ideas were grand. There were plans for a shortwave radio station broadcasting Memphis music, an observation deck reached by an inclinator running up the side of the pyramid, a Hard Rock Cafe, a music museum, a College Football Hall of Fame and a theme park on Mud Island. None of it happened. A falling-out between Tigrett and Shlenker, along with Shlenker's financial troubles, sank the plans, and the Pyramid opened as what it was at heart: a 20,142-seat arena.

## Hoops, Heavyweights and the Grateful Dead

The University of Memphis men's basketball team made the Pyramid its home court starting in 1991. When the NBA's Grizzlies relocated from Vancouver in 2001, they played there too, and the Memphis Pharaohs of the Arena Football League used it for the 1995 and 1996 seasons. The Grateful Dead played two concerts there on April 1 and 2, 1995. The building's biggest night may have been June 8, 2002, when it hosted Lennox Lewis against Mike Tyson, one of the biggest professional boxing events in history; Lewis won by knockout in the eighth round. But the arena years were short. Both basketball teams left in November 2004 for the new $250 million FedExForum, built as a condition of the Grizzlies' move. Bob Seger & The Silver Bullet Band played the last concert in February 2007, and the Pyramid has not been regularly used for sports or entertainment since.

## Big Cypress and the Lookout

For years the empty pyramid waited for a purpose. It was owned jointly by the city of Memphis and Shelby County until April 2009, when the county sold its share to the city. On April 29, 2015, the Pyramid reopened as a Bass Pro Shops megastore, packed with shopping, restaurants, a bowling alley and an archery range. At its base is the Big Cypress Lodge hotel, with roughly 100 rooms. Ducks Unlimited runs a museum inside on waterfowl hunting and wetlands conservation. For the view, what is billed as the tallest freestanding elevator in America carries visitors to the Lookout at the Pyramid, near the apex, where an outdoor observation deck looks out over the river and the city. More than six decades after Mark Hartz first conceived his pyramids, the building finally had its view from the top.

## Flight Context

The Memphis Pyramid stands at 35.1556°N, 90.0519°W on the east bank of the Mississippi River at the north end of downtown Memphis. Its pyramid shape is unmistakable from the air, especially in low evening sun; 2,000–3,000 ft AGL gives a good view with the river bridges alongside. General DeWitt Spain Airport (FAA identifier M01; no ICAO code listed) is less than 3 nm to the north, and West Memphis Municipal Airport (KAWM) is about 9 nm to the west across the river in Arkansas. Memphis's main airport, Frederick W. Smith International Airport (KMEM), is about 7.7 nm to the southeast inside busy Class B airspace, so obtain clearance before overflying downtown. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common along the river.
