# Carson Mounds

Near Clarksdale, Mississippi, the earthen mounds of the Carson site mark a Mississippian town of the Yazoo Basin whose people raised monuments, marked their elite from infancy and may have met de Soto's expedition as the chiefdom of Quizquiz.

**Location:** 34.2937, -90.6711

**Categories:** Mississippian culture, Archaeological sites in Mississippi, Mounds in Mississippi, Native American history, Coahoma County, Mississippi

## Introduction

About 2,800 years ago, the Mississippi River spread a fan of sediment, a crevasse splay, across the northern Yazoo Basin in what is now Coahoma County, Mississippi. Nearly two thousand years later, people were living on that river-made ground, and after 1200 CE they raised great earthen mounds and villages there. Only a few of the mounds survive today near Clarksdale, but archaeologists have suggested that the place they mark, the Carson site, is one of the more important archaeological sites in the state of Mississippi.

## Ground Laid by the River

Carson sits roughly 500 kilometers north of the Gulf of Mexico and 120 kilometers south of Memphis, Tennessee. The Yazoo Basin around it is a floodplain of the Mississippi, a landscape shaped over several thousand years by the river's meandering channels, which have left a variety of landforms behind. The builders of Carson chose a spot on the crevasse splay, ground the river itself had deposited centuries before. The site goes by several names. It is recorded as 22 CO 505, and it is also known as the Carson Site and as Carson-Montgomery. Its builders belonged to the Mississippian culture.

## Six Mounds and an Enclosed Village

Radiocarbon dates show that people were living at Carson as early as 1040 CE. The large earthen monuments and villages came after 1200 CE, and the most significant occupation spanned from about 1420 to 1660. Of the seven large mounds mapped in 1894, five survive, and the surviving mounds range from 3 meters to 12 meters high. Beside Mound A lay a large enclosed village, where archaeologists have so far recovered well over 30 residential buildings. The burials at Carson reveal something about how the community was organized. Remains from burials show that the inhabitants practiced deliberate cranial modification, flattening the forehead and the back of the head in a form known as fronto-occipital or fronto-vertico-occipital. It is more complex than the incidental flattening caused by cradleboarding infants, and because it had to be done in early childhood, before anyone could earn status, archaeologists think it marked hereditary elite rank.

## Was This Quizquiz?

Members of de Soto's expedition traveled down the Mississippi River in July 1543, passing within 50 to 75 kilometers of Carson at the very least. No physical evidence shows that they ever visited the site. Still, some archaeologists and historians place the polity of Quizquiz, one of the societies the expedition encountered, in the general area of Carson and its neighbors, with the polity of Quigualtam farther downstream among the protohistoric Natchez of the Natchez Bluffs. Others argue for a more northerly Quizquiz, at the Walls phase sites near Memphis. Whatever the answer, the consequences of contact were grave. Eurasian diseases and the social disruption the expedition caused broke native polities apart. By the time French missionaries and explorers arrived in the late 1600s, the paramount chiefdoms de Soto's expedition had recorded were gone.

## Surveyors on the Oasis Plantation

Scientific interest in Carson began in the late nineteenth century. Surveyors for the Smithsonian's Bureau of American Ethnology, including Colonel Philetus W. Norris and William Henry Holmes, visited the mounds, which then stood on the Oasis Plantation owned by the Carson family. Cyrus Thomas published a map of the landscape and its mounds in 1894, in the Bureau's 12th Annual Report. Later investigators included a Harvard survey team, Ian Brown, Jay K. Johnson, John Connaway and Jayur Madhusudan Mehta, each adding to what is known of the town. The Carson Mounds site was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1979, and it is now a stop on the Mississippi Mound Trail, though the mounds stand on private property.

## Flight Context

The Carson Mounds lie at 34.2937°N, 90.6711°W in the flat farmland of the northern Yazoo Basin, northwest of Clarksdale, Mississippi. From 1,500–2,500 ft AGL, look for low grassy rises standing out from the level fields; the Mississippi River lies to the west. Clarksdale's Fletcher Field (KCKM) is about 8 nm to the east, Thompson-Robbins Airport (KHEE) in West Helena, Arkansas, is about 17 nm to the north across the river, and Tunica Municipal Airport (KUTA) is about 28 nm to the north-northeast. Low morning mist is common over the Delta fields, and summer brings haze and thunderstorms.
