# Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley National Monument

Three sites in Chicago and the Mississippi Delta, a church, a riverbank and a courthouse, together honor Emmett Till, murdered at 14 in 1955, and his mother Mamie Till-Mobley, who made the world look.

**Location:** 33.8606, -90.2747

## Introduction

July 25, 2023, would have been Emmett Till's 82nd birthday. Instead of celebrating it, the country marked it with a national monument. That day President Joe Biden established the Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley National Monument, honoring the 14-year-old African American boy who was abducted, tortured and lynched in Mississippi in 1955, and the mother who refused to let his death be hidden. The monument is unusual in shape. It covers just 5.7 acres, split among three sites in two states, one in Illinois and two in Mississippi, each marking a different chapter of the same story.

## A Boy from Chicago

Emmett Till was born and raised in Chicago. In the summer of 1955 he traveled south to visit relatives near Money, Mississippi, staying with his great-uncle, Mose Wright. After Emmett was accused of offending a white woman in a grocery store in Money, Roy Bryant and J. W. Milam drove to Mose Wright's house in the early morning hours of August 28, sometime between 2:00 and 3:30 a.m., and took him. Three days later, two boys fishing in the Tallahatchie River found his body. He was fourteen years old. It is worth holding onto that number, because everything the monument commemorates flows from two facts: a child was killed, and the men who killed him were never punished.

## An Open Casket in Bronzeville

The Illinois site is the Roberts Temple Church of God in Christ in Chicago's Bronzeville neighborhood. Here, in September 1955, Mamie Till made a decision that would echo far beyond Chicago. She insisted on an open casket funeral for her son, so that the world could see what had been done to him. More than ten thousand people attended the services. Photographs of Emmett's body circulated around the country, most notably in Jet magazine, and people who had never set foot in Mississippi saw for themselves what had been done there. Mamie Till went on to become an advocate in the civil rights movement, turning private grief into public testimony.

## Graball Landing

Near the small Delta town of Glendora, a quiet stretch of the Tallahatchie River at Graball Landing is believed to be the place where Emmett's body was pulled from the water. Some 4.31 acres there were donated to the National Park Service for the monument. The site's recent history shows how raw the story remains. A marker placed at the river was torn down in 2008. The signs that replaced it were shot, collecting more than 100 bullet holes over the next few years. A fourth sign went up in 2019. Standing on the bank today, visitors look at an unremarkable river landscape and have to imagine what two boys fishing the river found in the late summer of 1955.

## The Courthouse at Sumner

The third site is the Tallahatchie County Second District Courthouse in Sumner. In September 1955 Roy Bryant and J. W. Milam were tried here for Emmett Till's murder. The jury was all white and all male; women and Black people were barred from serving. After a 67-minute deliberation, it acquitted both men. The next year, safe from being tried again, Bryant and Milam struck a deal with Look magazine to tell their story, in which they admitted killing Emmett, to journalist William Bradford Huie, for a few thousand dollars. Decades later, the courthouse became part of the National Park System, with partners including Tallahatchie County, the National Park Foundation and the Emmett Till Interpretive Center. The building where justice failed now belongs to a monument dedicated to remembering exactly how it failed.

## Flight Context

The monument's Mississippi coordinates, 33.8606°N, 90.2747°W, mark the Graball Landing area on the Tallahatchie River near Glendora in Tallahatchie County; the courthouse site is in the town of Sumner, roughly 8 nm to the northwest, and the Illinois unit is in Chicago's Bronzeville neighborhood. From 1,500–2,500 ft AGL over the Delta, follow the winding, tree-lined Tallahatchie River through flat cropland; Glendora sits beside it. Greenwood–Leflore Airport (KGWO) is about 24 nm to the south-southeast, and Cleveland Municipal Airport (KRNV) is about 25 nm to the west-southwest. Expect Delta haze and summer thunderstorms.
