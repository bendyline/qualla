# Gillett Coon Supper

Every January, the Delta town of Gillett, Arkansas, serves barbecued raccoon to more than a thousand guests, raising scholarship money and giving the state's politicians a hall full of hands to shake.

**Location:** 34.1148, -91.3827

**Categories:** 1944 establishments in Arkansas, Festivals in Arkansas, Arkansas County, Arkansas, Dining events

## Introduction

In January 1986, the people of Gillett, Arkansas, served more than 2,000 pounds of barbecued raccoon in a single evening. The women of the community added ten bushels of sweet potatoes, 100 pounds of barbecue rice, 2,000 rolls, assorted cakes and fourteen hams for guests who could not bring themselves to try the main course. It was the forty-third Gillett Coon Supper, an event whose yearly crowd is larger than the town that hosts it. That imbalance is the charm of the thing: a small Delta community that, one weekend a year, becomes a popular stop for anyone seeking statewide office in Arkansas.

## After the Hunt

The supper began as something far simpler. In 1933 local hunters started gathering after their annual hunt to share the results, in this case raccoon, and the informal meal served mainly as a social occasion. World War II brought it to a stop. In 1944, with the war still on, the community revived the idea in organized form, this time as a fundraiser for the Gillett High School football team. That first organized supper was held in the high school's auditorium, and the money it raised bought jackets for the team. It is a modest origin for a tradition that would eventually outlive the school itself.

## The Club and the Gym

In 1947 this rural, agricultural community organized the Gillett Farmer's and Businessmen's Club, a nonprofit group, and the raccoon supper became its premier event. The club has sponsored it every year since. The supper settled into a fixed spot on the calendar, the second weekend in January, and into the Gillett High School gymnasium, the only venue in town large enough to hold it. The numbers explain why. In 2000 Gillett had a population of 819, yet nearly 1,200 people were attending the supper each year. Today it still draws more than a thousand guests to the former high school gym, where plates of wild game, namely raccoon, remain the centerpiece of the meal.

## No Speeches for Challengers

Over the decades the supper became something more: a veritable rite of passage, in the Encyclopedia of Arkansas's words, for people seeking election to political office. The gym fills with candidates working the tables. There is a house rule about it, too. Candidates are not allowed to make speeches, but incumbents are, which gives officeholders a small edge. Bill Clinton, the former governor and president, attended multiple times, and Marion Berry, who represented Arkansas's 1st congressional district, made numerous appearances. Former Governor and U.S. Senator Dale Bumpers came, as did Winston Bryant, Tommy F. Robinson, Tom Cotton and Leslie Rutledge. The supper's pull even reached beyond the state line: in 2014 Illinois Senator Dick Durbin and Maine Senator Angus King joined the weekend's pre-supper gathering at Marion Berry's farm.

## A Supper That Outlived Its School

As Gillett's population declined, its schools could not hold on. In 2003 the Gillett School District consolidated into the DeWitt School District, and by 2009 Gillett High School had closed, with its students moving to DeWitt High School. The supper had been born to support that school, and for a time its future looked uncertain. The DeWitt School District settled the question by allowing the event to continue, with the money it raises going to scholarships for students who live within the old Gillett School District boundaries. So the tradition carries on in the old gym every January. What began with hunters sharing their catch and a football team in need of jackets now helps send Gillett-area seniors on to whatever comes next.

## Flight Context

Gillett lies at 34.1148°N, 91.3827°W in Arkansas County, in the flat, heavily farmed Arkansas Delta between the Arkansas and White rivers. From 1,500–2,500 ft AGL, the small town grid stands out against surrounding fields; Arkansas Post National Memorial, on the Arkansas River, lies a few miles to the south. Grider Field at Pine Bluff (KPBF) is about 28 nm to the west, and Stuttgart Municipal Airport (KSGT) is roughly 31 nm to the north-northwest. January, when the supper is held, can bring low ceilings, fog and cold rain to the Delta, so check conditions carefully.
