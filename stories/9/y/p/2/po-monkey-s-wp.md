# Po' Monkey's

For more than half a century, a tin-and-plywood shack outside Merigold, Mississippi, was one of the last rural juke joints in the Delta, and its host Willie "Po' Monkey" Seaberry was its brightest attraction.

**Location:** 33.8317, -90.7584

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Mississippi, Buildings and structures in Bolivar County, Mississippi, Tourist attractions in Bolivar County, Mississippi

## Introduction

The sign outside laid down the house rules: "No Loud Music, No Dope Smoking, No Rap Music." It hung on a juke joint, which made the first rule a little hard to take at face value. Po' Monkey's stood in unincorporated Bolivar County, Mississippi, outside the small town of Merigold, and from its founding in the early 1960s it outlasted almost everything like it. By the time it closed, it was one of the last rural juke joints in the Mississippi Delta, a place blues travelers went out of their way to find.

## Tin, Plywood and Wire

The building began as sharecroppers' quarters, a reminder of the farm labor system that shaped Black life across the Delta for generations. Willie Seaberry, known as Po' Monkey, turned it into something else. He built the joint himself from tin and plywood, holding the loosely fashioned structure together with nails, staples and wires. It sat at the end of a gravel road past farm fields. It was not much to look at, and for more than fifty years that did not matter. Seaberry also held a life estate in the property, meaning he could use it for as long as he lived. That legal arrangement would matter later. For decades, though, the only thing that mattered was that the lights were on.

## Thursday Nights in the Delta

Especially in its earlier years, Po' Monkey's was an incubator for the Delta blues scene. Thursday was the night, and for decades neighbors, friends and travelers gathered under a low ceiling hung with stuffed monkeys and Christmas lights. In 1990 photographer Birney Imes featured the club in his book Juke Joint. By the 1990s the crowd had become a mix of everyone: college students driving up from Delta State University in nearby Cleveland, blues fans, and people simply drawn to the atmosphere of a real juke joint. Cans of beer went for two dollars. The nights could get rowdy, too; through that decade the crowd grew raunchier, and the dancing more provocative.

## A Man of Many Suits

Po' Monkey himself was the main event. Seaberry was best known for his colorful suits, and he would often change several times in a single night. Sometimes he added accessories to the outfit, some of them humorous and some of them deliberately cheeky. He ran the place until his death in July 2016. Afterward Billy Nowell, then the mayor of nearby Cleveland, called Seaberry a "positive influence" on Bolivar County. It was a fitting description for a man who had kept a rural tradition alive long after most of its other keepers had closed their doors.

## Marker, Cameras and Silence

Recognition came in waves. In 2009 the Mississippi Blues Commission placed a historic marker at the lounge, adding it to the Mississippi Blues Trail for its contribution to the development of the blues and as one of the few authentic juke joints still operating. In May 2014 the club was featured in an episode of Anthony Bourdain's Parts Unknown. A year later, writer Richard Grant described a visit in vivid detail in his book Dispatches from Pluto: Lost and Found in the Mississippi Delta and put a photograph of the joint on the cover. After Seaberry died, Po' Monkey's fell silent. Under his life estate, the property reverted to the Hiter family, while his heirs sold the building's contents at auction in 2018.

## Flight Context

Po' Monkey's stands at 33.8317°N, 90.7584°W in the flat cotton-and-soybean country of Bolivar County, just outside Merigold and a few miles north of Cleveland, Mississippi. From 1,000–2,000 ft AGL, follow U.S. Highway 61 north from Cleveland toward Merigold; the site is a small building among open fields, so the town grid and highway make better references than the joint itself. Cleveland Municipal Airport (KRNV) is only about 4 nm to the south, and the airport at Greenville (KGLH) lies roughly 24 nm to the south-southwest. Expect Delta haze, summer thunderstorms and occasional crop-dusting traffic at low altitude.
