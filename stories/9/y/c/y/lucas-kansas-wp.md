# Lucas, Kansas

A prairie town of a few hundred people turned one Civil War veteran's concrete Garden of Eden into a whole community of folk art, earning the title Grassroots Art Capital of Kansas.

**Location:** 39.0589, -98.5375

**Categories:** Cities in Kansas, Cities in Russell County, Kansas, Folk art, Outsider art environments, Roadside attractions

## Introduction

The man who made Lucas famous never really left. Samuel P. Dinsmoor lies in a mausoleum he built at the corner of his own sculpture garden, and as part of a tour, visitors can look at him through the glass lid of his concrete coffin. It is a fitting introduction to a town of 332 people that has made a civic identity out of doing things its own way. In 1996, Kansas Governor Bill Graves named Lucas the Grassroots Art Capital of Kansas, and a walk down its streets explains why.

## A Veteran's Concrete Eden

Dinsmoor, born in 1843, served in the Union Army during the Civil War and worked as a teacher before moving his family to Kansas in 1888 to farm; they settled in the Lucas area by 1891. Between 1905 and 1927 he built the Garden of Eden, a permanent outdoor sculpture exhibit wrapped around his home. The house itself is a "log cabin" made not of logs but of carved limestone. Around it stand more than 150 sculptures that set out his interpretation of the Biblical creation and of world history, shaped as well by his belief in the Populist movement and his religious convictions. The mausoleum holds the remains of Dinsmoor, who died in 1932, and of his first wife. The whole site, a backyard vision carried through for more than two decades, was placed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1977.

## Gardens That Grew from One

Dinsmoor's example proved contagious. Inspired by his garden, local resident Florence Deeble built a rock garden around her own home, using stones she gathered on her travels to create works representing the places she had visited. Her house has a second life now. Since 2002 it has served as a gallery called the Garden of Isis, showing works made from recycled materials by the visual artist Mri-Pilar. Downtown, the Grassroots Arts Center, a non-profit gallery, promotes and exhibits the work of Kansas folk artists, giving makers from across the state a place to be seen. It was this density of folk art sites, in a town this small, that led to the town's official title as the state's grassroots art capital.

## The World's Smallest Largest Things

Lucas has a sense of humor, and it shows. The Bowl Plaza, a public restroom, is built in the shape of a giant toilet, with a raised lid framing its entrance and mosaics covering its walls inside and out. Elsewhere in town, visitors find Fork Art Park, Miller's Park, a historical mural and the World's Largest Travel Plate. There is Flying Pig Studio & Gallery, created by the late porcelain artist Eric Abraham. And there is a traveling museum whose name is a small work of art in itself: The World's Largest Collection of the World's Smallest Version of the World's Largest Things, a playful nod to the giant roadside attractions it shrinks down to size.

## From Blue Stem to Lucas

The town began as the community of Blue Stem in 1877 and took the name Lucas in 1887, after Lucas Place in St. Louis, Missouri. It sits in the Smoky Hills region of the Great Plains, at the junction of Kansas Highway 18 and K-232, the Post Rock Scenic Byway, about 21 miles northeast of Russell, the county seat. Wolf Creek, a tributary of the Saline River, flows east along the southern edge of town, and Wilson Lake lies roughly 8 miles to the south. The town has been getting smaller: 436 residents in 2000, 393 in 2010 and 332 in 2020, with a median age near 49. About 12 miles north stands the Meades Ranch Triangulation Station, the geodetic base point for the North American Datum of 1927, which served as a reference point until 1989.

## Apples, Highland Games and Baseball

The calendar keeps the town lively. On the Saturday before Labor Day weekend, Lucas holds its annual community celebration, the Adams Apple Festival, with an art show, a fun run, Scottish Highland Games and contests. Children parade in costume at the Spook Parade on the Saturday before Halloween, and Santa Claus Day falls on the Saturday before Christmas. Since 1949 Lucas has also hosted the K-18 Baseball state tournament, named for the highway that runs through town, for players aged 13 to 16 from communities across western and central Kansas. A softball tournament joined it in 2020.

## Flight Context

Lucas sits at 39.059°N, 98.538°W in the Smoky Hills of north-central Kansas, at the junction of K-18 and K-232 (the Post Rock Scenic Byway). From 2,500-4,000 feet, look for the small street grid with Wolf Creek along its southern edge; Wilson Lake, a large reservoir about 8 miles south, is the best visual anchor, with Interstate 70 beyond it. Russell lies 21 miles southwest, where Russell Municipal Airport (KRSL) serves general aviation, and Salina Regional Airport (KSLN) is farther east. Visibility is usually excellent over the open plains, though summer thunderstorms and gusty winds are common.
