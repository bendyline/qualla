# Basilica of St. Fidelis

Hauled to the site wagonload by wagonload by its own parishioners, the limestone "Cathedral of the Plains" raises twin 141-foot towers over the Kansas prairie at Victoria.

**Location:** 38.8564, -99.1508

**Categories:** Basilica churches in the United States, Romanesque Revival church buildings in Kansas, Churches on the National Register of Historic Places in Kansas, Churches in Ellis County, Kansas, Churches in the Roman Catholic Diocese of Salina

## Introduction

The parish did not ask only for money. Parishioners were asked to give $45 and to haul six wagonloads of Fencepost limestone and four loads of sand each year until their new church was finished, and some families brought as many as 70 to 80 wagonloads of stone. What rose from that labor between 1908 and 1911 is the Basilica of St. Fidelis in Victoria, Kansas, a Romanesque church so imposing for its setting that it is commonly called the Cathedral of the Plains, even though it has always been a parish church rather than a bishop's seat.

## Churches Outgrown

The building is the last in a line. The Volga German Catholics who settled Herzog, the village that later merged into Victoria, first worshipped in the open around a cross they had raised, then in the farmhouse of Alois Dreiling. Crowds soon strained its floor, so a wood-framed addition was built to hold services. By 1878 Sir Walter C. Maxwell, the only Catholic among Victoria's British colonists, had completed a stone church for the town, and it was overcrowded almost as soon as it opened. After the Kansas Pacific Railroad donated 10 acres for religious use in 1879, the parish built the original St. Fidelis Church, seating 600 and consecrated in 1884. Even that was soon too small for Herzog and the surrounding Catholic villages, and so the parish resolved to build again, on a far grander scale.

## Stone from Seven Miles South

The design came from John T. Comès of Pittsburgh, with modifications and supervision by the Topeka architect Joseph Marshall. The stone came from much closer to home: native limestone quarried seven miles south of Victoria and carried in by the parishioners themselves. The result is laid out as a great cross, 220 feet long and 110 feet wide across the transepts, with a 75-foot-wide nave. Its twin towers stand 141 feet tall, and they are what most travelers notice first, because they are clearly visible from Interstate 70. That visibility has made the basilica a major landmark of western Kansas, a stone silhouette rising above the surrounding farm country.

## A Nickname and Munich Glass

The building's famous name came from an outsider. William Jennings Bryan, the former presidential candidate, visited the town in 1912 and called the church The Cathedral of the Plains, and the phrase stuck. Four years later, in 1916, the Munich Studio of Chicago installed the church's 48 stained-glass windows at a cost of $3,700. Those windows are now valued at more than $1 million, and on a bright prairie day they wash the nave in colored light. The church was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1971.

## Keeping the Cathedral Standing

A building raised by hand needs tending by hand, too. In 1994 the congregation began a series of restoration efforts, spending more than $265,000 to weatherproof the exterior, re-plaster and repaint the interior, and update the sound, electrical and heating systems. The work included a new marble floor in the sanctuary, installed for $60,000, and the replacement of old carpeting and linoleum elsewhere in the church. In 2006 the parish re-shingled the roof for $137,000. For a parish church in a small town these were large sums, spent to keep a pioneer-era landmark sound for another century.

## Friars and a Basilica

Since the late 19th century, the Catholic faithful of Ellis County and their places of worship, St. Fidelis among them, have been overseen by the Capuchins, a branch of the Franciscan order. Their three-story friary still stands beside the church and in recent years has housed retired and aging Capuchin priests. In 2014 the Vatican approved raising the church to the rank of minor basilica, the first in Kansas, and on Saturday, June 7, 2014, Bishop Edward Weisenburger of the Diocese of Salina dedicated it as the Basilica of St. Fidelis. The name changed. The nickname Bryan gave it more than a century earlier did not.

## Flight Context

The Basilica of St. Fidelis stands at 38.856°N, 99.151°W in Victoria, Kansas. Its twin 141-foot limestone towers rise far above the town's rooftops and are easy to pick out from 2,000-4,000 feet above ground; Interstate 70 and U.S. Route 40 run east-west about a mile north of Victoria, with K-255 linking the highway to the town. The site of the former Walker Army Airfield lies about three miles northeast. Hays Regional Airport (KHYS) is the nearest airport with services, a short distance to the west. Western Kansas usually offers long visibility, but strong winds and summer thunderstorms are common.
