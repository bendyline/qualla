# Elms Hotel (Excelsior Springs, Missouri)

Twice destroyed by fire before a stone version finally opened in 1912, the Elms Hotel in Excelsior Springs has hosted Jack Dempsey, the New York Giants and an election-night Harry Truman, and survived closures and changes of ownership to reach its second century.

## Introduction

The hotel on this corner of Excelsior Springs burned down twice before anyone got it right. The first Elms lasted less than ten years. The second lasted barely more than one. The third, built of native stone by the Elms Realty Company, opened on September 7, 1912, and this Tudor Revival resort is still receiving guests more than a century later. Standing at Regent and Elms Boulevard, the Elms Hotel and Spa is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and sits within The Elms Historic District.

## Two Hotels, Two Fires

Excelsior Springs grew up as a health resort built around mineral springs, and in the late 19th and early 20th centuries that made it a regional destination. The first Elms Hotel opened here in July 1888 as a 200-room resort, and a year later, in 1889, a 75-room annex was added. On May 9, 1898, fire destroyed it. More than a decade passed before a successor rose on the site: a larger, 300-room hotel that opened on July 31, 1909. It fared even worse. On October 30, 1910, just over a year after its grand opening, the second Elms burned too. In little more than two decades, two grand hotels on the same ground had been lost to flames.

## Third Time, in Stone

The Elms that stands today broke the pattern. Built of native stone, it opened in 1912 in the Tudor Revival style, a solid, rugged presence in a town whose fortunes rested on visitors coming to take the waters. Its location suited that purpose. The hotel sits within walking distance of the Hall of Waters and downtown Excelsior Springs, close to the springs that had drawn people here in the first place. For guests arriving in search of a cure or simply a rest, the Elms offered a grand base, and this time the building endured.

## Fighters, Footballers and a President

Over the decades the Elms drew a remarkable roll of visitors. Heavyweight boxer Jack Dempsey used it as a training camp. In 1948 the New York Giants football team made it their summer camp. That same year brought the hotel's most famous night. On Election Night 1948, Harry S. Truman retreated to the Elms while the country voted on whether to keep him in the White House. He went to sleep there with the race looking grim, and in the early hours of the morning his aides woke him with the news that he had won. The hotel has also been linked in contemporary accounts to Prohibition-era activity in Excelsior Springs, adding a shadier thread to its guest list.

## A Hotel That Kept Changing Hands

The Elms passed through many owners. In 1956 the Eppley chain sold it to the Sheraton Corporation as part of the second largest hotel sale in United States history. Sheraton quickly sold it on, then took it back around 1960 when it went bankrupt and renamed it the Sheraton-Elms Hotel. Eventually Sheraton sold it, along with seventeen other aging properties, to Gotham Hotels, and the Elms regained its original name. It stayed open only until about 1971. After several years dark, it was bought by local citizens and reopened in 1977. The City of Excelsior Springs acquired the property in 1995, and after a major restoration the hotel reopened in 1998. Another rehabilitation followed, timed so the Elms could reopen for its centennial in 2012.

## Second Century

Today the hotel operates as The Elms Hotel and Spa, a Destination by Hyatt, with 153 guestrooms including suites, an indoor lap pool, a spa grotto and several places to eat. Recent upgrades kept the historic finishes while modernizing mechanical systems, guest rooms and spa facilities, in keeping with the property's focus on wellness. Weddings and events fill its rooms now, but the spirit is the one Excelsior Springs was founded on: people traveling to this small Missouri town to be restored.

## Flight Context

The Elms Hotel stands at 39.3372°N, 94.2256°W in Excelsior Springs, Clay County, Missouri, northeast of the Kansas City metro. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, look for the compact downtown of Excelsior Springs; the hotel is a large stone building a short walk from the Hall of Waters and the town center. Midwest National Air Center (KGPH) at Mosby lies about 4 nm to the west, and Kansas City International (KMCI) is roughly 23 nm west, with its Class B airspace extending toward the area. The countryside around town is rolling and wooded, so the town grid stands out well in clear weather; summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are the usual visibility concerns.
