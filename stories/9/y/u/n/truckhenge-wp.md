# Truckhenge

Ordered by a judge to "pick up the trucks" on his farm outside Topeka, Ron Lessman did exactly that, standing antique trucks on end in concrete and turning a legal fight into a roadside Stonehenge.

**Location:** 39.0767, -95.6014

**Categories:** Installation art works, Replicas and derivatives of Stonehenge, Outdoor sculptures in Kansas, Buildings and structures in Shawnee County, Kansas

## Introduction

The judge told Ron Lessman to pick up the trucks. So he picked them up. After a legal battle with Shawnee County over the old vehicles on his farm between Tecumseh and Topeka, Lessman obeyed the order with ruthless literalness, hoisting antique trucks off the ground and setting them on end in concrete. The result is Truckhenge, a grassroots art exhibit that answers a court order with a monument, and a small act of bureaucratic defiance that has become one of the stranger sights in eastern Kansas.

## An Order, Taken Literally

Lessman began creating Truckhenge in May 2000, working with antique trucks and a bus he had collected over the years. Shawnee County saw a hazard rather than a collection, with officials fearing that if the nearby Kansas River flooded again, loose vehicles could be swept downstream as dangerous projectiles. After a legal battle with the county, a judge ordered Lessman to "pick up the trucks." He did - just not in the way the court had in mind. Instead of hauling the vehicles away, he raised them up and anchored each one into the earth with more than 20 tons of concrete. Then he gave them a voice: each truck carries several quotes by Lessman himself, so the sculptures double as a running commentary from the man who made them.

## Beer Bottle City and the Catfish Pond

Truckhenge is only one part of a larger, deeply personal landscape. The site belongs to Lessman's Farm & Catfish Pond, and scattered among the upended trucks are sculptures and structures built from beer bottles, together known as Beer-Bottle City. The effect is less a gallery than a working farm that sprouted art, with bottle glass catching the Kansas sun a short walk from the catfish pond. Lessman kept building. In July 2007 he began adding onto Truckhenge with several small boats, extending the fleet of reclaimed vehicles from road to water. Like the trucks, the boats turned castoffs into something that demands a second look. The whole place carries a single maker's fingerprints, part junkyard and part manifesto, and it refuses to be tidied into anything more conventional.

## A Home for the Irreverent

Recognition came from an unusual direction. On July 5, 2006, Truckhenge was officially dedicated as part of the Kaw Region Art Park after the Shawnee County Recycling and Preservation Association presented Lessman with a plaque honoring his work; Truckhenge and Beer Bottle City both belong to the park. Two days later, the farm opened its gates to politics. Shawnee County officials had blocked a Libertarian Party of Kansas fundraiser from its original location at Lake Edun, so on July 7, 2006, the event moved to the Lessman farm instead. The following summer, from June 1 to June 3, 2007, the farm hosted a concert with a deliberately profane name aimed at Fred Phelps, the Topeka preacher whose pickets had made him infamous. The band Bite Boy headlined. A place born from a fight with authority seemed to attract others spoiling for one.

## Parked in the Spotlight

What began as a quarrel over junk vehicles has become a destination. Truckhenge has been featured in videos from the Kansas Department of Health and Environment, from Roadside America and KS Travel, from Rare Visions and Roadside Revelations, and even from the Filip and Frederik Show. For visitors, the appeal is easy to grasp. Here are rusting antique trucks rising from a Kansas field in a deliberate echo of Stonehenge, each one carrying its creator's words, surrounded by glittering bottle architecture and a catfish pond. Few monuments owe their existence to a judge's exact phrasing. This one does, and it still stands as proof that a stubborn farmer with a sense of humor and enough concrete can have the final say.

## Flight Context

Truckhenge lies at 39.077°N, 95.601°W on the Kansas River floodplain on the eastern edge of Topeka, between the city and Tecumseh. It sits only about a mile east of Topeka's Philip Billard Municipal Airport (KTOP), so pilots flying the pattern there are close by. From 1,000-2,000 feet AGL, look for a farmstead with a pond and a scattering of upright vehicles and glinting bottle structures in open fields near the river. The Kansas State Capitol dome in downtown Topeka stands a few miles to the west-southwest, and Topeka Regional Airport at Forbes Field (KFOE) is about 8 nm south-southwest. Lawrence Regional Airport (KLWC) is roughly 18 nm east, and Kansas City International (KMCI) about 43 nm east-northeast. The small scale of the trucks means low altitude and good light are essential; summer haze over the river valley can mute detail.
