# Peculiar, Missouri

A frustrated postmaster's complaint to Washington gave a Cass County town south of Kansas City one of the most memorable names in America, and Peculiar has been playing along with it ever since.

**Location:** 38.7208, -94.4567

**Categories:** Cities in Cass County, Missouri, Cities in Missouri, Populated places established in 1868, Kansas City metropolitan area, Place name origins

## Introduction

Edgar Thomson just wanted a post office. The first postmaster of a new community in western Missouri, he proposed calling it "Excelsior," only to learn that the name was already taken by a town in Atchison County. Several more suggestions came back rejected. According to the story, the exasperated Thomson finally wrote to the postmaster general himself: "We don't care what name you give us so long as it is sort of 'peculiar.'" So he submitted exactly that. The name "Peculiar" was approved, the post office opened on June 22, 1868, and a small Cass County town gained an identity it still carries. Its slogan today: "Where the 'odds' are with you."

## Two Stories for One Name

Peculiar's naming story comes in more than one version. Missouri folklorist Margot Ford McMillen recorded an alternate account in which early settlers, searching for land to farm, crested a small rise and looked down at the ground below. "Well that's peculiar!" one of them remarked. "It's the very place I saw in a vision back in Connecticut." The land was bought, a village eventually grew up on it, and it was named Peculiar. One version turns on a bureaucratic dead end, the other on a vision fulfilled. Whichever you prefer, the result is the same. The town that could not be Excelsior became something far harder to forget.

## By Riverboat and Survey Chain

The people who built Peculiar came from all over. Early settlers traveled to western Missouri by riverboat from Illinois, Iowa, Michigan, Pennsylvania and Ohio, while other families arrived from Tennessee, Kentucky and Virginia. On July 29, 1868, just weeks after the post office opened, county surveyor Robert Cass platted the community, and it was filed as "The Town of Peculiar." Peculiar incorporated as a village in the 1890s and did not become a fourth-class city under Missouri law until 1953. Today it is governed by a mayor elected to two-year terms and a six-member Board of Aldermen, two from each of the city's three wards, with one seat in every ward up for election each year.

## Old Peculiar and the New

Modern Peculiar sits beside Interstate 49 and U.S. Route 71 and along the Burlington Northern Santa Fe Railway, part of the Kansas City metropolitan area. The original settlement, known as Old Peculiar, lies a mile to the east on the banks of the East Fork East Branch of the South Grand River. The city covers about 8.45 square miles, nearly all of it land. It has grown: 4,608 people lived here at the 2010 census, and 5,621 by 2020. It is a young town, too. In 2010 the median age was 32.8, and almost three in ten residents were under 18. Peculiar has its own branch library, and its schools belong to the Raymore-Peculiar school district.

## A Name That Travels

A name like Peculiar is hard for artists to resist. In 2022, folksinger Willi Carlisle released an album titled Peculiar, Missouri, named for the town. Author Jim Butcher's "AAAA Wizardry," a short story in his Dresden Files universe, is reported to be set in Peculiar; it first appeared in 2010 in The Dresden Files Roleplaying Game, Volume 2: Our World, and was republished in the 2018 collection Brief Cases. The town has also sent people out into the wider world, including the decathlete and track and field coach Brutus Hamilton and several professional football players. For a place named in a moment of postal frustration, Peculiar has done well by its odd inheritance.

## Flight Context

Peculiar lies at approximately 38.7208 N, 94.4567 W in Cass County, Missouri, south of Kansas City. From 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, follow Interstate 49 / U.S. Route 71 south from the Kansas City suburbs; the town sits alongside the highway and the BNSF rail line, with Old Peculiar a mile to the east near the East Fork of the South Grand River. Lawrence Smith Memorial Airport (KLRY) at Harrisonville is about 8.5 nm to the southeast, Kansas City - Lee's Summit Regional Airport (KLXT, formerly Lee's Summit Municipal) about 15 nm north, and Johnson County Executive Airport (KOJC) about 15 nm west-northwest. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC) is about 25 nm north. Expect spring thunderstorms and summer haze; fall and winter often bring the clearest views.
