# Unity Village, Missouri

What began in 1919 as a weekend farm for the staff of a Kansas City spiritual movement grew into Unity Village, a tiny incorporated town of Cotswold cottages, a landmark tower and a one-of-a-kind dam that serves as the world headquarters of Unity.

**Location:** 38.9464, -94.3994

**Categories:** Villages in Jackson County, Missouri, Unity Church, Villages in Missouri, Religious headquarters, National Register of Historic Places in Missouri

## Introduction

Picture 7,500 apple trees in bloom, a 400-tree peach orchard, twelve acres of grapevines and a henhouse holding 2,000 white leghorns. That was Unity Farm in its heyday, a working farm southeast of Kansas City, and its eggs helped keep a meatless menu going at the Unity Inn cafeteria downtown. The farm belonged to Charles and Myrtle Fillmore, founders of the Unity spiritual movement. Over the decades it became something more than a farm: a village of its own, incorporated by the State of Missouri, with a population of just 66 at the 2020 census and a campus that serves as Unity's world headquarters.

## A Weekend Getaway Grows

In 1919 the Fillmores bought a 58-acre farm as a weekend retreat for the employees of their downtown Kansas City headquarters. By March 1920 the land was known as Unity Farm, and purchases of twelve surrounding farms expanded it to nearly 1,500 acres. The fields and orchards were remarkably varied. Besides the apples, peaches and grapes, Unity Farm grew cherry and plum trees along with fields of oats, corn, wheat, strawberries, asparagus and soybeans. The poultry house supplied eggs for the cafeteria in the city. After World War II, the Fillmores' work was consolidated here, and on March 15, 1953, Missouri officially incorporated the land as Unity Village.

## Cotswold Cottages and a Tower

Unity Village is a campus of historic buildings, and some of its most distinctive were shaped by the family itself. Waldo Rickert Fillmore, known as Rickert, the second son of Charles and Myrtle, designed Mediterranean-inspired buildings for the grounds, which also hold dwellings in the English Cotswold style. Among them is the Tower, which opened in 1929 along with an office building then used by the Silent Unity prayer ministry. Both are listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Between the English cottages and the Mediterranean lines of Rickert's buildings, the headquarters of a Kansas City spiritual movement took on a distinctly old-world look.

## A Dam Like No Other in Missouri

Water was a practical problem for a large farm, and in 1926 Unity solved it by making a lake. A crew of 100 men built a concrete buttress dam at a cost of $100,000, described as the only dam of its kind in Missouri and one of the few west of the Mississippi River. Behind it formed Lake Charles R. Fillmore, named for the founders' grandson. It reaches 42 feet deep, spreads across 21 surface acres and holds about 75 million gallons of water. The lake supplied the farm and orchard that Unity kept running until the 1980s, and its water was pumped to a treatment plant on campus. It is one of two artificial lakes in the village.

## The Smallest of Towns

Unity Village lies about 15 miles southeast of downtown Kansas City, bordered by Kansas City and Lee's Summit, and covers just under two square miles. Its population is tiny and has been shrinking: 140 people in 2000, 99 in 2010 and 66 in 2020. At the 2010 census, nearly three-quarters of its households were people living alone, and the median age was 52.5. Even small places get their share of weather; on July 1, 2015, a tornado damaged roofs in the village. More than a century after the Fillmores bought their weekend farm, the farm and orchard that Unity kept until the 1980s are no longer worked, but the lake, the cottages and the Tower still carry the shape of that first idea.

## Flight Context

Unity Village lies at approximately 38.9464 N, 94.3994 W, between Kansas City and Lee's Summit in Jackson County, Missouri, about 15 miles southeast of downtown Kansas City. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for Lake Charles R. Fillmore and the village's campus. Kansas City - Lee's Summit Regional Airport (KLXT) is only about 1.5 nm to the northeast, so the village sits close to its traffic pattern; plan altitude and radio calls accordingly. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC) is about 14 nm northwest and East Kansas City Airport (FAA identifier 3GV) at Grain Valley about 9.5 nm northeast. Summer haze and thunderstorms are common; clear fall days show the lake and campus best.
