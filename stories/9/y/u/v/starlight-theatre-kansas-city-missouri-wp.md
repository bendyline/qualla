# Starlight Theatre (Kansas City, Missouri)

Born from a concert for a visiting queen and opened with a pageant that ran a real locomotive across its stage, Kansas City's Starlight Theatre has spent more than seventy years bringing Broadway to an open-air amphitheater in Swope Park.

**Location:** 39.0074, -94.5328

**Categories:** Theatres in Kansas City, Missouri, Outdoor theatres, Amphitheaters in the United States, Theatres completed in 1950, Swope Park

## Introduction

Night after night in the summer of 1950, a locomotive rolled across the stage. It was no replica. The engine that had crossed Kansas City's Hannibal Bridge 81 years earlier chugged over specially built rails in front of thousands of spectators, part of a historical revue called Thrills of a Century staged for the city's 100th birthday. The theater hosting it was not even finished. Starlight Theatre opened anyway on June 3, 1950, in Swope Park, and it has been lighting up summer nights ever since. Today it seats more than 7,700 people under the open sky, and it is one of the few major self-producing outdoor theatres still operating in the United States.

## A Concert for a Queen

The idea goes back to the 1920s, when Romania's Queen Marie paid a visit to Kansas City. To celebrate, the Kansas City Federation of Music organized a showcase of local talent for the queen, open to the public as well. The profits went into a city trust, and the talk turned to building an outdoor theater. Finding a home for it proved harder than raising the money. One proposed site, where the Kansas City Art Institute now stands, drew objections from neighbors who did not want so large a structure in their midst. Another, just north of what is now the University of Missouri–Kansas City, was shelved because officials feared an outdoor theater would compete with the newly completed Municipal Auditorium. The plans sat for years.

## Thrills of a Century

What finally moved the project was a birthday. Kansas City needed a venue for its centennial celebrations, so a committee was quickly chosen, Swope Park was named the site, and construction began in December 1949. Six months later, on June 3, 1950, Thrills of a Century opened in a facility that was still incomplete. The pageant played nightly through July 10, with hundreds of local citizens in the cast and thousands in the audience each evening. Along with the famous locomotive, the show restaged the Civil War's Battle of Westport. Its success led to the formation of the Starlight Theatre Association of Kansas City, a nonprofit, with John A. Moore as its first president. The first Broadway season opened on June 25, 1951, with The Desert Song.

## Stars on the Stage

For its first two decades the shows were in the hands of Richard Berger, a New York veteran hired as the association's first producing director, a post he held through 1971. The stage itself changed shape more than once. In 1958 Jerry Lewis paid for an extension that covered the orchestra pit, and in the early 1980s the stage was permanently extended over the pit, bringing the action closer to the audience. The theater has also produced memorable nights off the script. During a 1960s production of the musical Mr. President, Harry S. Truman made a guest appearance on opening night, only for an attack of appendicitis to send him away from Starlight by ambulance during intermission.

## Ten Stories of Stage

The biggest transformation came in 2000, when the ten-story Jeanette and Jerome Cohen Community Stage replaced the old extension. The new stagehouse made Starlight one of the largest roadhouses in the country, and that same year the theater began presenting major national touring productions, bringing recent Broadway musicals to Kansas City alongside its own shows. In 2015 Starlight looked beyond the summer season with Starlight Indoors, a live series of small comedies, musicals, parodies and other unconventional shows staged inside the heated performance space of the Cohen stagehouse. The outdoor theater that began with a pageant for a city's birthday now reaches audiences year-round.

## Flight Context

Starlight Theatre sits in Swope Park in southeast Kansas City, Missouri, at approximately 39.0075 N, 94.5328 W. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for the large open-air amphitheater bowl and its tall stagehouse amid the heavy woodland of Swope Park, with the Blue River valley running through the park nearby. On summer evenings the lit stage can stand out against the dark trees. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC) is about 7.5 nm north-northwest, Kansas City - Lee's Summit Regional Airport (KLXT) about 8 nm east-southeast, and Johnson County Executive Airport (KOJC) about 13.5 nm southwest. Summer haze and evening thunderstorms are common during the outdoor season.
