# Grinter Place

Above an old Kansas River crossing, the 1857 brick home of ferryman Moses Grinter and his Lenape wife Annie holds the story of a frontier trading post, a crossroads of displaced nations, and a woman who stayed.

**Location:** 39.0756, -94.7599

## Introduction

Her name was Windagamen, and it meant "Sweetness." Better known as Annie Grinter, she was of Lenape (Delaware) heritage, and when her tribe was moved south to what is now Oklahoma, she remained in the brick house above the Kansas River that she shared with her husband, Moses. She was one of about 69 Delaware who stayed in Kansas as U.S. citizens under an 1866 treaty, and she lived in that house until her death in 1905. Today Grinter Place, in the Muncie neighborhood of Kansas City, Kansas, is a state historic site - and a window onto the crossings, trades and upheavals that shaped this bend of the river.

## The Ferry at Delaware Crossing

Long before the house existed, the river crossing mattered. Here an old Native trail met the waters of the Kaw, and the crossing became known as the Delaware Crossing, the "Military Crossing," or sometimes "the Secondine." Around 1831, Moses Grinter, a Kentucky native and one of the earliest permanent white settlers in the area, set up a ferry on this spot. Business came from every direction. Traders and freighters used it, as did soldiers traveling the military road between Fort Leavenworth and Fort Scott, and other travelers crossed here on their way to Santa Fe. The area was also home to the first non-military post office in Kansas. Where those routes met the Kaw, Grinter's ferry carried them across.

## A Crossroads of Displaced Nations

The land around the ferry was already layered with history. By the 1820s, the family of François Gesseau Chouteau, part of the American Fur Company, operated trading posts in the vicinity. Then, beginning in the 1830s, the federal government relocated several Eastern nations here - the Delaware, Wyandot, Munsee and Shawnee - far from the homelands they had been forced to leave. The Delaware agency, a smithy, and Baptist and Methodist missions all stood near Grinter Place. Grinter traded with his neighbors too. From 1855 to 1860 he ran a trading post, first on the site and later in the house itself, selling around 160 kinds of merchandise to Delaware customers in exchange for cash and furs. Commerce and dispossession unfolded side by side.

## A Brick House and a Choice

Grinter completed the two-story brick house in 1857, and the Kansas Historical Society preserves it as the oldest home in Wyandotte County. The world around it kept changing fast. Between 1863 and 1864, the Union Pacific Eastern Division laid a railway between the house and the river, and in 1869 the Union Pacific pushed rails through the area and on toward the state's western border. By the 1870s, the federal government had forced the Eastern tribes it had earlier moved to Kansas to relocate once again, this time south to what is now Oklahoma. Annie Grinter did not go. Having chosen citizenship, she stayed on the land she knew while many of her people were uprooted yet again. Moses died in 1878. Annie lived another 27 years, and their shared tombstone stands about a quarter-mile behind the house.

## Fried Chicken to Historic Site

The Grinter family held onto the property until 1950. Then the house took an unexpected turn: it was sold and became a chicken dinner restaurant, serving diners until the mid-1960s. It was an unlikely second act for a frontier landmark. In 1971, the state of Kansas bought the property, with help from the Junior League of Kansas City, Kansas, and a group called the Grinter Place Friends. That same year, on January 25, 1971, Grinter Place was listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The Kansas Historical Society now administers it as Grinter Place State Historic Site, where visitors can stand above the Kaw and picture the ferry, the traders and the woman named Sweetness who chose to stay. The society's site lists the house as closed from October 4, 2026, with reopening planned for April 2027.
