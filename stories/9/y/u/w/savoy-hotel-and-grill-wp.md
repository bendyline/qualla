# Savoy Hotel and Grill

Built with coffee money in 1888, Kansas City's Savoy welcomed rail travelers, fed presidents in Booth No. 4, and ran for more than a century billed as the oldest continuously operating hotel west of the Mississippi.

**Location:** 39.1036, -94.5867

## Introduction

Four presidents are said to have sat in Booth No. 4. Warren Harding, Harry S. Truman, Gerald Ford and Ronald Reagan all passed through the Savoy Grill at 9th and Central in downtown Kansas City, and the booth they shared earned a nickname to match: the presidents' booth. Around it grew a legend of railroad-era luxury. For well over a century, the Savoy Hotel and its grill were fixtures of the city - the hotel was long billed as the oldest continuously operating one in the United States west of the Mississippi River until it closed for renovations in 2016, and the grill, founded in 1903, was billed as the oldest restaurant in Kansas City.

## Built on Coffee

The Savoy's story begins with coffee. In 1888, the owners of the Arbuckle Coffee Company built the structure, and it opened in 1889 as the Hotel Thorne. Five years later, in 1894, it took the name it would carry into the next century: the Hotel Savoy. A major remodeling came in 1903, when a west wing was added to house a new dining room, the Savoy Grill. That addition would give the building its most enduring identity. Hotels come and go, and this one would have its ups and downs, but the grill became a Kansas City institution in its own right. Today the building is counted among Missouri's examples of Art Nouveau architecture, a reminder of the turn-of-the-century taste in which it came of age.

## First Sight from the Depot

In the early 1900s, Kansas City was a railroad town, and the Savoy was perfectly placed to catch arriving travelers' eyes. It was the first hotel visitors saw as they entered the city from the old Union Depot, and it became a luxury destination for passengers stepping off the trains. Fortunes turned with the times. The Depression of the 1930s and the later shift toward suburban living took their toll, and the hotel began to fall into disrepair. Yet the restaurant never lost its footing, remaining active even as the rooms upstairs faded. Recognition came in the form of historic status: the Savoy was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on December 30, 1974.

## Pioneers on the Walls

Diners in the original Grill Room ate surrounded by history. The Savoy Murals, painted by Edward Holslag in 1903 when he was in his early thirties, wrap the room with scenes of pioneers departing from Westport Landing and following the Santa Fe Trail west. Holslag had studied at the National Academy of Design and under John LaFarge, and his work is represented at the Library of Congress in Washington, D.C. The Savoy Murals themselves were included in the Smithsonian Institution's Bicentennial Inventory of American Paintings. The subject suited the city, whose Westport Landing had sent so many of those journeys on their way. Here, in a restaurant built for a later age of travel, the wagon trains set out again every evening over dinner.

## Lunch with a Haberdasher

Long before he was president, Harry S. Truman was a regular. When he owned and operated a downtown haberdashery, he made frequent visits to the Savoy Grill over his lunch hour, a local businessman taking a midday break. He was not the only notable figure to find his way to the grill. Many well-known visitors passed through over the decades, and Booth No. 4, the presidents' booth, became the restaurant's most storied seat, reputedly hosting Harding, Truman, Ford and Reagan. For Kansas Citians, the Savoy Grill was where history and routine overlapped: a place to celebrate, to do business, or simply to eat lunch a few steps from where a future president once did the same.

## Smoke, Art and a Name Restored

On October 23, 2014, a major fire destroyed the grill's kitchen. It began with a cook who was not paying attention. The historic dining room survived but suffered significant smoke damage, the restaurant closed, and the hotel was evacuated. By 2016 the entire property had closed for an extensive renovation costing about $47.5 million, ending its long run of continuous operation. It reopened in 2018 as the 21c Museum Hotel, part of a chain themed around contemporary art. The story did not end there. Avion Hospitality later assumed management, and the building returned to its historic name as Hotel Savoy Kansas City, part of Hilton's Tapestry Collection. After more than 130 years, the Savoy is still here, still welcoming travelers.
