# Muehlebach Hotel

Truman's Kansas City office, a stage for Republican conventions and the storm-stranded birthplace of the Barbershop Harmony Society, downtown's Hotel Muehlebach is slated to be reborn as apartments above a Marriott.

**Location:** 39.0994, -94.5849

**Categories:** Hotels in Kansas City, Missouri, Hotel buildings completed in 1915, Projects by Holabird & Root, Harry S. Truman, Downtown Kansas City

## Introduction

In March 1938, bad weather stranded two businessmen from Tulsa, Oklahoma, at Kansas City's Hotel Muehlebach. They met by chance in the lobby, found two more men to make a quartet, and sang their way through the storm. A few weeks later they gathered like-minded singers for a meeting in Tulsa, and from that meeting grew the Barbershop Harmony Society, an international organization that counted about 14,000 members in 2023. A plaque in the hotel's restored original lobby marks where it began. It is one of the gentler stories told about a building that has hosted presidents, conventions and more than a few legends.

## From Church Lot to Grand Hotel

The site was once home to the First Baptist Church. In 1914 the Muehlebach Estate Co., owned by George E. Muehlebach, whose father had founded the Muehlebach Beer Company, acquired the property. Muehlebach demolished the church and built a 12-story, 144-foot brown brick hotel designed by Holabird & Roche at a cost of $2 million, opening it as the Hotel Muehlebach in May 1915. Manager Barney Allis took over in 1931 and ran it through a lengthy tenure. In 1952 a 17-story western annex, the Muehlebach Tower, was added along with a parking lot, and a windowless addition from the early 1950s holds ballrooms and meeting and conference rooms. Allis sold the hotel in the 1960s.

## The President's Other Office

Harry S. Truman made the Muehlebach his White House headquarters during frequent visits to his home in nearby Independence, Missouri. He slept in Independence but conducted business in the Presidential Suite in the hotel's penthouse. On election night in 1948, Truman predicted his upset victory to staffers at the hotel, though he spent the night itself out of the media spotlight at the Elms Hotel in Excelsior Springs. Presidents and would-be presidents passed through for decades. Herbert Hoover frequented the hotel during the 1928 Republican National Convention, held across the street at Convention Hall. In the fall of 1974, President Gerald Ford stayed there while in town to deliver the keynote at the National FFA Convention, shaking hands with FFA band members lined up in the lobby. Two years later the hotel served as headquarters for the 1976 Republican National Convention, where Ford and Ronald Reagan both made their pitches for delegates.

## A Guest Register of Legends

Howard Hughes took the presidential suite during a 1945 stay. Babe Ruth, the Beatles, the Jimi Hendrix Experience and Elvis Presley all stayed at the Muehlebach. In 1959 the Society of American Registered Architects, founded three years earlier by architect Wilfred Gregson, held its first national conference there, where Gregson told the assembled architects, "You are the ones who have made the first great step toward a unified profession of architects." Right after the 1976 Republican convention, the hotel and the Hotel Phillips across the street hosted the 34th World Science Fiction Convention over Labor Day weekend. Its Guest of Honor, Robert A. Heinlein, was booked into the Harry S. Truman Presidential Suite for all five days.

## Dark Floors and a Second Life

The later decades were harder. The Equitable Life Assurance Society of the United States foreclosed on the hotel in the early 1970s, then renovated it in 1974 at a cost of $7 million and brought in Minneapolis-based Radisson Hotels to manage it as the Radisson Muehlebach. The hotel closed permanently in 1986. In 1996 Marriott bought the building and made it an extension of the Kansas City Marriott Downtown, and today its lower levels, including the lobby and function rooms, operate as one of that hotel's three wings. The upper guest-room floors sat abandoned for many years. They were offered for sale in 2024, and in 2025 Flint Development announced plans to remodel them into 152 apartments. Reporting in 2025 described the plan as still being explored, with the developer expecting to seek historic tax credits and tax abatement; if it goes ahead, the old guest floors will become homes.

## Flight Context

The Hotel Muehlebach stands at approximately 39.0994 N, 94.5849 W in downtown Kansas City, Missouri, directly across the street from the Hotel Phillips. From 2,500-3,500 ft MSL, locate the downtown core south of the Missouri River; the Art Deco Power and Light Building's lantern-topped tower is only a couple of blocks to the south and makes a handy reference. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC) is about 1.5 nm north-northwest across the river, and Kansas City International (KMCI) is about 13.5 nm north-northwest. Expect busy controlled airspace over downtown, with summer haze and thunderstorms common.
