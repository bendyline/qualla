# Westport, Kansas City, Missouri

Once the outfitting town for the Santa Fe and Oregon trails, Westport built the road to the river landing that became Kansas City - and was eventually swallowed by the city it helped create.

**Location:** 39.0533, -94.5919

**Categories:** Neighborhoods in Kansas City, Missouri, History of Kansas City, Missouri, Oregon Trail, Santa Fe Trail, Populated places established in 1831, Former municipalities in Missouri

## Introduction

To compete with Independence for the westbound trade, John Calvin McCoy cut a road four miles north from West Port to a rocky landing on the Missouri River. Supplies came ashore there at what became known as Westport's Landing. The settlement that grew around that landing became the Town of Kansas, then the City of Kansas, then Kansas City - which in the 1890s annexed the very town that had built the road. Today Westport is a historic neighborhood and one of Kansas City's main entertainment districts, but its story starts with a surveyor, a missionary family and a wagon road.

## Missionaries and a Surveyor

Tennessee native Daniel Yoacham and his family were the first to settle the area, about three miles due south of what became downtown Kansas City. The Reverend Isaac McCoy, a pioneer and tribal missionary, followed, building a large log house on a hill at what became 43rd and Wornall. He brought his son John Calvin McCoy as surveyor, while his son-in-law, the Reverend Johnston Lykins, bought land. John McCoy built a two-story residence and trading post at what became the northeast corner of Westport Road and Pennsylvania Avenue, platted West Port on a parcel bought from Lykins, and saw the town formally incorporated in February 1857. A degreed surveyor by trade, he is generally considered the "father of Kansas City." When his landing grew popular enough to draw business from Independence's landing, fourteen investors including McCoy formed the Town of Kansas company to buy the land around it, including parcels from Lykins and from Gabriel Prud'homme.

## Gateway to the Trails

Westport's trade with Native tribes reached as far as the Great Plains and the Rocky Mountains. By 1850, the combined settlements of West Port and Kansas had surpassed Independence as the outfitting and starting point for traders, trappers and emigrants bound west on the Santa Fe and Oregon trails, and the town's greatest prosperity came between 1854 and 1860. That trade carried a darker cargo, too. West Port became a gateway for border ruffians into Bleeding Kansas. Around 1850 the Ewing Brothers had built a store at the northwest corner of Westport Road and Pennsylvania; Albert G. Boone, grandson of Daniel Boone, bought it to provision the booming trails into Kansas Territory and used it as an organizing place for his pro-slavery forces. That building survives as Kelly's Westport Inn, often called the oldest surviving building in Kansas City, Missouri - a title the neighboring Chouteau Store also claims.

## Cholera and a Shared Cemetery

The frontier boom had its toll. The cholera pandemic of 1849 struck both boomtowns, West Port and Kansas, filling each one's only cemetery. The search for a new burial ground led to a site halfway between the two, meant to unite both communities. Union Cemetery was founded on November 9, 1857, when the City of Kansas had a population of about 4,000, and it remains the city's oldest surviving public cemetery.

## Battle, Decline and a New Life

The Civil War brought many skirmishes between pro- and anti-slavery groups, and in October 1864 the Battle of Westport was fought here. After the war the trade fell off sharply and never recovered, as railroads made the wagon trails obsolete, and West Port declined to the status of a rural town. The City of Kansas became Kansas City in 1889, and within a decade it had annexed Westport. The old town found a second life inside the city. East of Broadway, in the historic Nutterville area, James B. Nutter Sr. saw that the old homes were restored and adapted as business offices, and the houses are colorfully painted and landscaped. Westport itself grew into a main entertainment district - a place where people still gather at the crossroads that once sent wagons west.
