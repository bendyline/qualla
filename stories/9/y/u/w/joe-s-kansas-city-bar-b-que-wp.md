# Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que

Born on the competition barbecue circuit and opened inside a Kansas City, Kansas, gas station, Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que became one of America's most celebrated barbecue restaurants - and had to change its name to protect it.

**Location:** 39.0444, -94.6207

**Categories:** Barbecue restaurants in the United States, Kansas City-style barbecue, Culture of Kansas City, Kansas, Restaurants in Kansas

## Introduction

Anthony Bourdain put it on his 2009 Men's Health list of 13 places to eat before you die. The place he meant was a barbecue restaurant that had opened inside a gas station at 47th Avenue and Mission Road in Kansas City, Kansas. Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que, known for most of its life as Oklahoma Joe's, opened its first Kansas City-area location there in August 1996, and it has spent the years since collecting the kind of praise that most restaurants never see. The story behind it, though, begins not in a kitchen but on the competition circuit, with a smoker and a team called Slaughterhouse Five.

## Smoke Before Business

Jeff Stehney caught the barbecue bug joining friends at the American Royal and The Great Lenexa BBQ Battle, and it inspired him to start cooking on his own. His first smoker was an Oklahoma Joe's 24-inch model, christened in April 1991. With it, he and his competition team, Slaughterhouse Five, went on a remarkable run: eight Grand Championships, including the prestigious American Royal, three Reserve Grand Championships, and the KCBS Grand Champion "Team of the Year" title in 1993. Over the following seasons the team won dozens more awards and was generally recognized as one of the top competition barbecue teams in the country. Along the way, the Stehneys befriended Joe Davidson, founder and owner of the Oklahoma Joe's Smoker Company - the maker of that very first smoker - and Davidson and Jeff Stehney decided to go into business together.

## Brisket at the Filling Station

The original location drew the spotlight. In 2010 the Kansas City, Kansas, restaurant appeared on a Kansas City episode of the Travel Channel's Man v. Food, where host Adam Richman helped prepare the restaurant's signature brisket and burnt ends. On July 30, 2014, Joe's filled a $1,400 order for President Barack Obama, which was picked up by Air Force One staff. The recognition piled up. Men's Health named Joe's America's manliest restaurant, Zagat called it Kansas City's best barbecue, and USA Today said its ribs were the tastiest in America. Yelp ranked it No. 3 among the top 100 places to eat in the United States, and Thrillist included it among the "33 Best BBQ Joints in America" in both 2013 and 2016. In July 2017, the New York Post put it at number one on its list of the 28 best barbecue restaurants in America.

## Three Kitchens, One Family

Jeff and Joy Stehney own and operate the restaurants, and they expanded step by step. In 2005 they opened a second location in a former nightclub at 119th and Strang Line Road in Olathe, Kansas. A third followed in July 2012 in Leawood, Kansas, near 119th and Roe Avenue, in an extensively remodeled building that had once housed a TGI Friday's. Critics kept noticing. Author and food critic Johnny Fugitt ranked Joe's third in his 2015 book The 100 Best Barbecue Restaurants in America, and TripAdvisor placed it second on its list of the top 10 barbecue restaurants for 2015. In May 2026 a fourth spot, the more casual Joe's Next Door, opened across the street from the original.

## What's in a Name

The name that had carried the business for years eventually became a problem. Joe Davidson had moved back to Oklahoma, and after 14 years out of the restaurant business he reopened his own version of Oklahoma Joe's in Broken Arrow in 2011, followed by a second location in Tulsa in 2012. The menus and recipes differed from those in Kansas City. The Stehneys did not own the rights to the Oklahoma Joe's name outside the Kansas City area, and they worried that other restaurants could dilute their brand at home. So they met with Davidson and agreed to change their name.

They did it gradually. In 2011 a new barbecue sauce appeared as Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que Sauce, with no "Oklahoma" on the label. A French fry seasoning followed, and the T-shirts changed too. In August 2014 came the official announcement: as of November 17, 2014, all three restaurants would be called Joe's Kansas City Bar-B-Que. The owners, and the barbecue, stayed the same.
