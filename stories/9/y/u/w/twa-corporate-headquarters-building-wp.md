# TWA Corporate Headquarters Building

Trans World Airlines ran its business from this red-and-white 1956 building in Kansas City's Crossroads, whose rooftop Moonliner rocket went missing for decades before a replica returned to the same corner.

**Location:** 39.0924, -94.5845

**Categories:** Trans World Airlines, Airline company headquarters in the United States, Office buildings completed in 1956, National Register of Historic Places in Kansas City, Missouri, Crossroads, Kansas City

## Introduction

A red-and-white rocket stands on the southwest corner of a low building at 1735 Baltimore Avenue, pointed at the sky above Kansas City's Crossroads neighborhood. It has stood there before. The original, the TWA Moonliner II, crowned Trans World Airlines' headquarters in the airline's heyday, then vanished from the roof, rusted beside an interstate for decades and was restored for display elsewhere. The rocket on the roof today is a replica, installed on September 29, 2006, at the very same spot - the finishing touch on the restoration of a building that once ran one of America's great airlines.

## An Airline's Home Office

Kansas City's selection as TWA's headquarters was said to have been made by Charles Lindbergh. The airline had grown out of the merger of Transcontinental Air Transport and Western Air Express, and was initially known as T&WA. Its headquarters building was designed by architects Raymond E. Bales Jr. and Morris Schechter and built by the Long Construction Company of Kansas City. Site work began in May 1955, and construction was completed on October 31, 1956. The three-story, L-shaped building was dominated by aluminum panels and corrugated concrete paneling, and its exterior carried TWA's signature red and white. Even the construction method was efficient: under the Youtz-Slick method, steel support beams went up first, concrete slabs were poured at ground level, and hydraulic jacks lifted the slabs into place to be bolted and welded to the beams, cutting both cost and construction time.

## Departures

By 1964 TWA had become a major international force in aviation, and the airline moved its executive offices to New York City. The Baltimore Avenue building did not empty all at once. It remained headquarters for TWA's accounting department, ticket office, credit department and cargo department until 1969, and the airline trained its flight attendants there until the Breech Academy opened in suburban Overland Park, Kansas, that same year. Afterward the building's fortunes faded. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2002 but had stood vacant for several years, and plans as late as 2003 called for turning it into residential condominiums under the name TWA Lofts LLC. Instead, in early 2005, the Kansas City urban development firm The Nicholson Group hired local architectural firm el dorado inc to design and coordinate a restoration. The advertising agency Barkley Inc. leased the renovated building and moved in on November 14, 2006.

## The Rocket That Went Missing

The Moonliner II was modeled on the original 76-foot TWA Moonliner at Disneyland's Tomorrowland, itself a one-third-scale vision of what a TWA atomic-powered spaceliner might look like in the faraway year of 1986. TWA sponsored the Disneyland attraction until the early 1960s. When the Hughes-Disney partnership ended in 1961, the airline's management took the rooftop rocket down, and in 1962 it was sold to SpaceCraft, a Kansas City travel-trailer company. When SpaceCraft moved to Concordia, Missouri, in 1970, the rocket, all white by then, went along, and for about the next 25 years it stood on a campground near the trailer plant, just south of Interstate 70 between Kansas City and St. Louis, slowly falling into disrepair. A longtime Disney collector then bought it and carefully restored it to its original red-and-white TWA markings. The original now stands at the TWA Museum at Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport, about two miles from the rooftop it left.

## A Twin for the Roof

The restoration of the headquarters building inspired a return. In March 2006, the Kansas City office of the Bratton Corporation began fabricating a brand-new Moonliner II replica, and on September 29 of that year it was installed on the southwest corner of the roof, exactly where the original had stood. Barkley moved in less than two months later. The building that once housed an airline's ambitions now carries a reminder of its most playful one: a rocket built to promote an airline's vision of space travel, back on the job above Baltimore Avenue.

## Flight Context

The former TWA headquarters stands at approximately 39.0924 N, 94.5845 W at 1735 Baltimore Avenue in the Crossroads neighborhood, just south of downtown Kansas City, Missouri. At 2,000-3,000 ft MSL the three-story building is small, so use the downtown skyline to the north as a reference and look for the red-and-white rocket on its southwest corner. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC), home of the TWA Museum where the original Moonliner II is displayed, is about 1.9 nm north-northwest across the Missouri River; Kansas City International (KMCI) is about 14 nm north-northwest. Expect busy controlled airspace over the city; summer haze and thunderstorms are common.
