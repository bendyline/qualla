# Lansing Correctional Facility

Built by prisoners in the 1860s as the Kansas State Penitentiary, Lansing held outlaws from the Dalton Gang to the killers of In Cold Blood, hanged prisoners until 1965, and in 2024 handed its old main prison to the local historical society.

**Location:** 39.2504, -94.8923

## Introduction

In December 1899, a crew of prisoners at the Kansas State Penitentiary spent several days digging up the prison graveyard. The ground near the northeast corner of the main wall was needed for clay for a new brick plant, so 130 bodies had to be moved. Every one of them was a convict except one: a stranger who had died beside the prison walls two years earlier while trying to find shelter from the cold. That small, sad detail belongs to the long history of the place now called Lansing Correctional Facility.

## Walls Raised by Prisoners

The Kansas State Penitentiary was built by prison labor in the 1860s. Its cell houses were finished in 1867, and in July 1868 it began taking in Kansas felons. From 1889 to 1909 it also held felons from Oklahoma. Disease could shut the gates: in the spring of 1896 and again in January 1900, the prison temporarily stopped admitting prisoners as smallpox spread through Kansas. In 1990 it was renamed Lansing Correctional Facility. Run by the Kansas Department of Corrections, it is one of four major prisons, alongside the federal penitentiary, the Army's Disciplinary Barracks and a joint regional facility at Fort Leavenworth, that give the Leavenworth area its reputation as a corrections center.

## A Graveyard on the Ridge

The Kansas City Star covered the graveyard move in a short dispatch dated December 6, 1899. Warden Tomlinson had put prisoners to work shifting the "convict graveyard," which lay within a stone's throw of the women's department, and the paper predicted the job would be finished within the week if the pleasant weather held. The dead were reburied on a ridge a quarter of a mile southeast of the walls, which the reporter described as "a beautiful place overlooking the surrounding country." Among them lay the unnamed stranger, a man who had come to the prison's walls seeking warmth and ended up in its cemetery. The prison held women as well: the dispatch mentions a women's department, and the East Unit later housed women inmates until they were moved to Topeka Correctional Facility.

## Outlaws and the Gallows

Lansing's roll of prisoners reads like a history of American crime. Emmett Dalton was sentenced to life here after the Dalton Gang's 1892 bank robbery attempt at Coffeyville, and Harvey Bailey, an associate of Machine Gun Kelly, also served time here. Until 1965, state, federal and military prisoners were executed by hanging at the penitentiary. Perry Smith and Richard Hickock, convicted of murdering four members of the Clutter family in 1959, were hanged here in 1965, and Truman Capote's In Cold Blood carried the story of the killings and the executions to a national audience. George York and James Latham, who went on a killing spree in 1961, remain the last people executed by the state of Kansas. When Kansas reinstated the death penalty in 1994, Lansing was designated the site for lethal injections, and a chamber was built in 2001, but no executions have taken place since.

## Life on The Hill

For years the minimum-security East Unit stood on higher ground east of the main compound, known locally as "The Hill." Some of its dormitories housed men in cubicles of four, each inmate carrying a color-coded ID card that told staff at a glance which dorm he lived in. Many worked. Some held private-industry jobs paying minimum wage or more, which let them pay fines and child support while serving their sentences. One pod, nicknamed the "dog run," housed a dog resocialization program along with disabled inmates and their inmate aides. A faith-based program begun as the InnerChange Freedom Initiative continued as Brothers in Blue after Prison Fellowship withdrew funding. Out front of the gym and visit center stood picnic tables and a small play structure where children could play during visits. Those units have since closed, replaced by newly built housing.

## A New Prison and an Old One Remembered

A modern crisis arrived in 2020 with COVID-19. In late April, testing of 240 men from one open-dormitory unit found about three-quarters of the first samples positive, and by the end of the month two prisoners had died of the disease. The prison has long been split by security level, with an 11-acre maximum-security facility and a 46-acre medium-security facility in its Central Unit and an 85-acre minimum-security site on The Hill. Today it operates from a new main facility that opened in 2020. The old main prison, whose story reaches back to the cell houses finished in 1867, passed in 2024 to the Lansing Historical Society and Museum, turning a century and a half of confinement into local history.
