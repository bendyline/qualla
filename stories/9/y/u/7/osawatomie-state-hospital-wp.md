# Osawatomie State Hospital

Kansas's oldest operating psychiatric hospital began in 1866 as a renovated farmhouse for a dozen patients, and its first half-century traces the swing between hopeful reform and overcrowded custodial care.

**Location:** 38.5102, -94.9401

**Categories:** Psychiatric hospitals in Kansas, Kirkbride Plan hospitals, Hospitals established in 1863, Buildings and structures in Miami County, Kansas

## Introduction

The first patient arrived on November 5, 1866, four days after the doors officially opened. There was no grand hospital waiting, only a small two-story farmhouse, renovated and renamed "The Lodge," with room for ten or twelve people. From that modest start in Osawatomie grew what is now the oldest operating psychiatric hospital in Kansas. Its early names, "Kansas Insane Asylum" and "State Insane Asylum," carry the language of their era. Its early decades carry something more complicated: a running argument over whether people living with mental illness should be treated or merely kept.

## From Guardianship to Asylum

The roots reach back to the territorial legislature's act of 1855, which provided legal guardians for people judged to have an "unsound mind" and, beginning in 1859, for those labeled "habitual drunkards." Guardians managed their wards' estates and reported to judicial authorities. The Kansas legislature established an asylum in 1863, and on November 1, 1866, the Kansas Hospital for the Insane opened at Osawatomie. It was also called the State Insane Asylum and the State Lunatic Asylum, names that reflect how the era spoke about mental illness. Its first superintendent, Dr. Charles O. Gause, was a proponent of rehabilitation and "moral treatment." His wife, Levisa, served as matron. Among those who helped bring the institution into being was the Reverend Samuel Adair, the local minister whose name would later appear on one of its buildings.

## Light, Air and Old Main

To make room for more patients, two new wards went up in 1868, and that same year the legislature set aside money for a large permanent building. Architect John G. Haskell designed it on the recommendations of Dr. Thomas Story Kirkbride, a leading figure in asylum design, whose plan aimed to improve patients' living conditions and give them better natural light and ventilation. The project became known as "Old Main," and it took more than a decade to finish. Even then it was not safe from disaster. On March 8, 1880, a fire broke out in the attic of the executive building and caused heavy damage, though the east and west wings were saved.

## Economy Over Care

Dr. Abram H. Knapp ran the institution from 1873 to 1877 and again from 1878 to 1892. He favored far stricter patient management and practices built around efficiency and economy, and many staff objected so strongly that they walked out. The Knapp Building, opened in 1892 for chronically ill men, bears his name. His years also exposed a shift in philosophy: moral treatment gained ground early, but money pressures and a growing patient population pushed institutions toward custodial models. The Adair Building followed in 1895 for chronically ill women, named for the minister who had done so much to get the institution started. By the end of the century, articles described understaffed, overworked attendants and raised concerns about how many patients each one was expected to care for.

## A Ball Team, a Nursing School, a New Name

The appointment of Dr. Lyman Uhls as superintendent in 1899 turned the institution in a new direction. Uhls pushed for a more therapeutic approach and set up educational programs so staff could give better care. He also fostered a more collaborative relationship with the surrounding community. Under his watch the hospital gained a baseball team and a two-year nursing school, adopted a policy allowing female nurses to care for male patients, and introduced electrotherapy as a treatment. In 1901 the name changed from Kansas State Insane Asylum to Kansas State Hospital at Osawatomie, a signal that treatment and rehabilitation were now the goal. Construction began that year on an infirmary for patients with infectious diseases. Uhls resigned in 1913 to found a new hospital in Overland Park. In less than half a century, the place that began with a dozen patients in a farmhouse had grown into a campus with its own nursing school.

## Flight Context

Osawatomie State Hospital lies about a mile northeast of downtown Osawatomie, Kansas, at approximately 38.5102 N, 94.9401 W. From 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, look for the hospital grounds on the town's northeast side; the Marais des Cygnes River flows past the north side of Osawatomie and Pottawatomie Creek past the south side. Miami County Airport near Paola (FAA identifier K81) is only about 2 nm to the north-northeast, so expect pattern traffic. Ottawa Municipal Airport (KOWI) is about 15 nm west and New Century AirCenter (KIXD) about 19 nm north. Summer haze and spring thunderstorms are common in eastern Kansas; fall and winter mornings are often clearest.
