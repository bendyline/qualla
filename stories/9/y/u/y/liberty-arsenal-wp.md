# Liberty Arsenal

A small federal arms depot outside Liberty, Missouri, was raided twice by pro-Southern forces, once in the Bleeding Kansas conflict and again days after Fort Sumter, and today survives as little more than a crumbling wall on private land and a roadside marker.

**Location:** 39.1991, -94.4065

**Categories:** Missouri in the American Civil War, United States Army arsenals, Bleeding Kansas, Liberty, Missouri, Military history

## Introduction

The "Old Sacramento" cannon had already been through one war when it reached Liberty, Missouri. Captured by the United States during the Mexican–American War in 1847, it was brought to a quiet federal arms depot in Clay County, where it might have been expected to rest. Instead, pro-slavery raiders carried it off in the December 1855 raid on the arsenal, and by some accounts it was fired during the Sacking of Lawrence, Kansas, in 1856. The cannon's journey is a small version of the arsenal's own story. Built to supply the western frontier, the Liberty Arsenal was seized twice by Southern partisans, first in the Kansas troubles of 1855 and again at the very start of the Civil War.

## Ten Acres on the Frontier

President Andrew Jackson signed the bill authorizing an arsenal on Missouri's western frontier on July 2, 1836. On June 30, 1837, the federal government obtained a deed to ten acres near Liberty, and the arms depot, known to federal authorities as the Missouri Depot, was completed in 1839. By September 30, 1841, the War Department could report exactly what it had built: one building for officers' quarters and one for the men, a storehouse, a magazine, three workshops, an armory, two gun sheds and two laboratories. The whole establishment had cost $26,435.96. It was a modest outpost, but it stored something that would soon matter a great deal on the Missouri–Kansas border: federal weapons, guarded by a handful of employees.

## The Wakarusa Raid

In the 1850s, whether Kansas would enter the Union as a free or a slave state was still an open question. Pro-slavery residents of Clay County frequently crossed the border to vote in Kansas territorial elections and joined armed incursions against Free State supporters in the territory, a period remembered as "Bleeding Kansas". On December 4, 1855, during the so-called Wakarusa War, more than 100 armed Clay County volunteers, led by Judge James Turner Vance Thompson and Missouri Major Ebenezer Price, seized the arsenal. They arrested its custodian, U.S. Major Luther Leonard, and his six employees. The arsenal then held three brass six-pounder cannon, 55 rifles, 67 cavalry sabres, 100 dragoon pistols and 20 Colt revolvers, and much of its arms and equipment was carried off. Captain William N. R. Beall of the 1st U.S. Cavalry arrived on December 10. The Wakarusa War had ended with a treaty a few days earlier, and after Beall talked with prominent local citizens, most of the material came back.

## Missouri's Fort Sumter

On March 21, 1861, Missouri's constitutional convention voted 98–1 to stay in the Union while supplying neither men nor money to either side. Three weeks later, the firing on Fort Sumter began the Civil War, and western Missouri's ardent secessionists looked to Liberty once more. On April 20, 1861, about 200 militiamen, a large Clay County company under Colonel Henry Lewis Routt and a Jackson County company captained by John H. McMurray of Independence, forced their way into the depot. Its caretaker, Major Nathaniel Grant, had only two employees and could do nothing but protest. The raid has sometimes been called the "Missouri Fort Sumter", and it left the St. Louis Arsenal as the only other federal military arsenal in the state. Fearing that the much larger St. Louis Arsenal could also fall, Union Captain Nathaniel Lyon moved to secure it before the month was out.

## Powder in the Countryside

The haul was considerable: three brass six-pounders with carriages, twelve unmounted iron six-pounders, 1,180 percussion muskets, 923 percussion pistols, 400,000 cartridges and thousands of pounds of powder. Part of the secessionist force held the depot for a week while the materials were removed. Some went to Routt's home on East Franklin Street, near what is now William Jewell College, where his ice-house became an armory for distributing weapons to Missouri militiamen and later to the Missouri State Guard. The brass field pieces became Captain Henry Guibor's State Guard battery, and kegs of powder were hidden throughout the countryside. Not everyone in Liberty approved. Captain O. P. Moss, a Mexican War veteran who led the Unionist Liberty Home Guards, put it bluntly: "Col. Routt had no more right to capture the Liberty Arsenal, with its government arms and munitions, than John Brown had to seize the one at Harper's Ferry."

## A Marker by the Highway

Major Grant stayed on as custodian until the depot was sold in 1869, under an act of Congress passed on July 25, 1868. The Old Sacramento cannon suffered its own indignity: years later, packed with clay and wet grass and fired in an attempt to raise a drowned man's body from a river, the old gun burst. The arsenal had stood generally west of the junction of Missouri Route 291 and old Highway 210, and on October 6, 2012, a historic marker was placed at the highway intersection near the site. That marker is now the main sign of what happened here. The land is privately owned, and little remains beyond a crumbling wall.

## Flight Context

The Liberty Arsenal site lies at approximately 39.1991° N, 94.4065° W, near Liberty in Clay County, by the junction of Missouri Route 291 and old Highway 210. Nothing of the arsenal survives to be seen from the air; from 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, use the Route 291 corridor and the town of Liberty to orient. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC) is about 18 km to the west-southwest, Midwest National Air Center (KGPH) near Excelsior Springs about 17 km to the north-northeast, and Kansas City International (KMCI) roughly 29 km to the west-northwest. Be mindful of Kansas City terminal airspace to the west and southwest.
