# Arrowhead Stadium

Built in 1972 with a steep upper deck no modern stadium could copy, the Chiefs' home on the eastern edge of Kansas City became famous for noise, reclaiming the loudest-stadium record at 142.2 decibels.

**Location:** 39.0489, -94.4839

**Categories:** NFL venues, Kansas City Chiefs stadiums, Sports venues in Kansas City, Missouri, Sports venues completed in 1972, 2026 FIFA World Cup venues

## Introduction

On September 29, 2014, during a Monday night game against the New England Patriots, the crowd at Arrowhead Stadium hit 142.2 decibels and took back the Guinness World Record for the loudest stadium. The number capped a long campaign. For more than half a century, noise has been part of the identity of this bowl on Kansas City's eastern edge, a stadium born from a lost baseball team, a league rulebook and a roof that was never built.

## Born of a Lost Team

Arrowhead grew out of loss and a rulebook. When the Dallas Texans moved to Kansas City in 1963 and became the Chiefs, they played at Municipal Stadium, which seated only about 35,000 for football. The AFL–NFL merger announced in 1966 required NFL stadiums to seat at least 50,000, and when baseball's Athletics left for Oakland after the 1967 season, the shock fed a growing sense among fans and civic leaders that public money would be needed to keep major league teams. Kansas City could not find a site, so Jackson County offered land near the interchange of Interstate 70 and Interstate 435. In 1967 voters approved a $102 million bond issue for a two-stadium complex. The first plan called for side-by-side baseball and football stadiums sharing a roof that would roll between them, but it proved too complicated and costly, and both were built open to the sky.

## A Steep and Stubborn Design

Construction began in 1968 on a concept from Denver architect Charles Deaton and Chiefs general manager Jack Steadman, carried out by the Kansas City firm Kivett & Myers. Arrowhead and its neighbor, Kauffman Stadium, look very different but share utilities, parking and underground storage. To add seats without widening the footprint, the upper sections were pitched at a steep incline that cannot be replicated today because of accessibility rules. Lamar Hunt added an owner's suite with three bedrooms, a kitchen and a living room. Some consider the stadium an influence on several later NFL designs. On November 5, 1972, a crowd of 82,094, the largest ever for a game at Arrowhead, watched the Chiefs beat the Oakland Raiders 27–14 for their first regular-season win in the new home.

## Arrows on the Field

Arrowhead left a small, permanent mark on football itself. In 1973 it became the first NFL stadium to put arrows on the yard markers pointing toward the nearer goal line; at first they looked like little arrowheads. The idea spread through the league during the 1970s, became mandatory in 1978 after appearing in Super Bowl XII, and is now close to universal at lower levels of the game. The stadium has also hosted a Pro Bowl, the Drum Corps International World Championships, five Big 12 championship games and the 2007 Kansas–Missouri showdown dubbed "Armageddon at Arrowhead," which drew 80,537. In 2024, a crowd of 72,610 came to see Lionel Messi and Inter Miami play Sporting Kansas City, and Taylor Swift and Beyoncé have both played the stage.

## The Decibel Duel

The crowd's power was obvious long before anyone measured it. In 1990, with Denver quarterback John Elway backed up to his own goal line and unable even to run a play, he appealed to referee Gordon McCarter, who announced: "Any further crowd-noise problem will result in a charged timeout against Kansas City. Thank you for your cooperation." The formal fight for the loudest-stadium title came later and turned into a two-city rivalry. On October 13, 2013, against the Oakland Raiders, Chiefs fans set the Guinness World Record at 137.5 decibels. Less than two months later, on December 2, Seattle Seahawks fans edged past them at 137.6. Kansas City answered the following September with its 142.2-decibel roar against New England.

## The Last Seasons at Arrowhead

A $375 million renovation, completed in 2010, added the Chiefs Hall of Honor, a tribute to Lamar Hunt and outdoor "horizon level" luxury seating. From 2021 until August 2026 the stadium was officially GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium; the team and GEHA then dropped the sponsor name, and it is once again simply Arrowhead Stadium. With 76,416 seats it is the largest sports facility in Missouri and the oldest stadium in the AFC. It was chosen as one of 16 venues for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, assigned six matches including a quarterfinal under the temporary name Kansas City Stadium. Its future as the Chiefs' home is limited, though. A proposed Jackson County sales-tax extension that would have funded further renovations failed at the polls, and on December 22, 2025, the team and Kansas officials announced plans to leave for a new stadium in Wyandotte County, Kansas, scheduled to open for the 2031 season.

## Flight Context

Arrowhead Stadium sits at 39.0489°N, 94.4839°W in the Truman Sports Complex on Kansas City's eastern edge, beside Interstate 70 near its interchange with Interstate 435. From 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, look for the paired stadiums, Arrowhead's large bowl and Kauffman Stadium's baseball diamond right beside it, surrounded by acres of parking. Charles B. Wheeler Downtown Airport (KMKC) is about 7 nm northwest, Kansas City - Lee's Summit Regional Airport (KLXT, formerly Lee's Summit Municipal) about 7 nm southeast, and Kansas City International (KMCI) about 18 nm northwest; check Kansas City Class B boundaries before descending. Game days bring temporary flight restrictions over the stadium, so check NOTAMs before flying.
