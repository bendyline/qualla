# Lindsborg, Kansas

Founded by Swedish immigrants in 1869, Lindsborg has kept its heritage so vividly that Kansans call it Little Sweden, a town of Dala horses, a World's Fair pavilion, Birger Sandzén's paintings and festivals honoring the pioneers.

## Introduction

In April 1976, during his royal tour of the United States, King Carl XVI Gustaf of Sweden came to a small town in the middle of Kansas. Lindsborg had earned the attention. Settled by Swedish immigrants in 1869, the town has kept so much of its founders' heritage that it is known as Little Sweden. Its downtown shops sell Dala horses in various sizes, Dala horse figures custom-painted by local artists stand throughout town, and in October of odd-numbered years the whole community celebrates its pioneers at the Svensk Hyllningsfest.

## Four Linds and a Pastor

The first settlers arrived in the spring of 1869, a group of immigrants from the Swedish province of Värmland led by Pastor Olof Olsson. McPherson County, which included their land, had been established just two years earlier. The name Lindsborg translates as Linden Castle, after the lind, or linden tree, but it also honors four men: N.P. Linde, S.P. Lindgren, S.A. Lindell and J.O. Lindh. All four held prominent positions in the First Swedish Agricultural Company, a Chicago organization of Swedish farmers that was involved in settling Lindsborg until 1877. The year 1879 brought two milestones at once. Lindsborg incorporated as a city, and the Kansas Pacific Railway completed a depot, with the first train pulling in on July 4. That depot now stands in Heritage Square, relocated there in 1974 after it closed to freight in 1972.

## A Pavilion from the World's Fair

Bethany College anchors the town's cultural life, and one of its most unusual gifts came from St. Louis. The Swedish Pavilion was built as an exposition building for the 1904 World's Fair. After the fair, W.W. Thomas, the U.S. Minister to Sweden and Norway, bought it and presented it to Bethany as a memorial to his friend, the Rev. Dr. Carl Aaron Swensson, the college's recently deceased founder. For decades the art department used it as a classroom, library and museum. In 1969 the pavilion was moved to the Lindsborg Old Mill & Swedish Heritage Museum. The town's roster of historic places also includes the Smoky Valley Roller Mill, built in 1898.

## Sandzén's Town

Art runs deep in Lindsborg. The Swedish-born painter and teacher Birger Sandzén lived in the town, and on October 20, 1957, the Birger Sandzén Memorial Gallery was dedicated on the Bethany campus. It is described as holding the largest and most extensive collection of his paintings, prints and drawings anywhere in the world. The tradition has multiplied. Visitors can also find the Red Barn Studio Museum, the Clara Hatton Center and the Smoky Valley Arts and Folk Life Center, and each February the Chocolate Lovers Affair & Art Auction raises money in support of the arts community and graduates pursuing art. Broadway RFD, established in 1959, bills itself as the longest-running outdoor theater in Kansas.

## Waffles, Messiah and Lucia

The calendar in Lindsborg reads like a Swedish almanac. Våffeldagen, or Waffle Day, arrives each March. The Messiah Festival of the Arts builds toward a full-length performance of Handel's Messiah, a tradition that began with the founding of the Bethany Oratorio Society in December 1881 and its first performance in March 1882. May brings Millfest, with folk music, woodcarving and wheat and fiber weaving, and summer brings a Midsummer festival for the solstice. The Svensk Hyllningsfest, a biennial tribute to the Swedish pioneers, has been held since 1941. December is devoted to Christmas in Lindsborg, a month of events that includes a Snowflake Parade, a St. Lucia Festival, Julotta and Annandag Jul. Between the festivals, the town keeps a surprising sideline: in 2004 the United States Chess Federation named Lindsborg Chess City of the Year, and downtown was home to the Anatoly Karpov International School of Chess, run by the International Chess Institute of the Midwest.

## Little Sweden Today

Heritage here is not only a performance. Roughly thirty percent of residents are reported to be of Swedish descent, and the town counted 3,776 people in the 2020 census. The pioneers' first homes survive in places, such as the Höglund Dugout, where Gustaf and Maria Höglund's family lived while building a larger home, and Fremont Lutheran Church, built in 1870, stands nearby. Coronado Heights, in neighboring Saline County, is another favorite landmark. And at the Hemslöjd shop, which specializes in imported Swedish goods, personalized wooden Dala horse pieces are still made in its own workshop. Little Sweden is not a theme park. It is a Kansas town that decided, long ago, not to forget where it came from.

## Flight Context

Lindsborg lies at 38.574°N, 97.675°W in the Smoky Valley of central Kansas, along Kansas Highway 4 between Salina and McPherson. From 2,000-3,500 feet, the compact town and the Bethany College campus stand out among surrounding farmland, and the hill of Coronado Heights rises from the countryside to the north. Salina Regional Airport (KSLN) lies to the north, and McPherson Airport (KMPR) is to the south. Central Kansas usually offers good visibility, but strong winds and spring and summer thunderstorms are common.
