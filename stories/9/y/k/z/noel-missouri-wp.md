# Noel, Missouri

A small Elk River town in Missouri's southwest corner, Noel stamps tens of thousands of Christmas cards each December, and in recent decades it has become home to newcomers from Somalia, Sudan, Latin America and the Pacific.

**Location:** 36.5433, -94.4861

## Introduction

Every December, tens of thousands of Christmas cards and letters travel to the post office in Noel, Missouri, for one reason: a postmark that reads "Noel, Mo. - The Christmas City in the Ozark Vacation Land." Yet for most of the year, the people who live here do not say the name like the carol at all. Around town, Noel rhymes with "mole." That gap, between the name the world hears and the name the neighbors use, is a good way into a small river town that has always been more complicated than its postmark.

## Brothers, a Sawmill and a Railroad

Noel grew up where the Elk River meets Butler and Mill creeks in McDonald County, three miles north of the Arkansas line. The town was founded after the Kansas City, Pittsburg and Gulf Railroad arrived, and a post office called Noel has operated since 1886. The name came from Clark Wallace "C.W." Noel and William Jasper "W.J." Noel, brothers who were stockmen and owned a sawmill. The Elk flows through the city and on to the northwest, joining the Neosho River in Oklahoma. Missouri Route 90 serves as Main Street, running 13 miles east to Jane and 8 miles west to Southwest City at the Oklahoma border. The railroad that made Noel also brought its worst day. On August 3, 1969, a freight train exploded as it passed through town, spraying fragments of metal through houses and buildings across a six-block area. One person was killed, and 40 others were injured.

## Kate Smith Tells the Noel Story

Noel shares its seasonal fame with a small club of Christmas-named towns, among them North Pole, Alaska; Christmas, Michigan; Santa Claus, Indiana; and Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. Its moment came in the late 1940s, when the radio and television singer Kate Smith began telling the "Noel Story" on her broadcasts. Sending holiday mail through Noel, so that it would carry the town's name on the way to its destination, caught on, and the custom has lasted. Each holiday season the cards still arrive by the tens of thousands, a big annual rush for the post office of a town of around two thousand people. The town itself has grown a little: in 1982 the neighboring community of Spring Valley, on the south bank of the Elk just northwest of Noel, merged into the city.

## A Main Street of Many Languages

Noel was home to a Tyson Foods chicken-processing plant, which closed in October 2023, and the work drew new residents from far away. By 2010 an estimated 400 to 500 Somali refugees and 60 to 70 Sudanese refugees lived among the town's roughly 1,800 residents, most of them working for Tyson. A mosque opened on Main Street in 2009, and an African grocery selling headscarves and rugs followed in 2010. In 2011, 130 Muslim employees temporarily stopped working after they were allegedly discouraged from praying five times a day; Tyson later described the episode as a cross-cultural misunderstanding. By the 2020 census, Noel's 2,124 residents were 41.5 percent Hispanic or Latino, and one in ten identified as Native Hawaiian or other Pacific Islander. Early on December 28, 2020, fire destroyed the African grocery and spread to the mosque and other businesses; several people were hurt, and one person inside the store is reported to have died of severe burns. Within days the Islamic Society of Joplin launched a campaign to find the mosque a new home, and it met its goal, raising $100,480.

## Flight Context

Noel sits at 36.543°N, 94.486°W in the Elk River valley of far southwest Missouri, about 3 miles north of the Arkansas line and 8 miles east of the Oklahoma border. The river's bends and wooded hills frame the town; follow Route 90 and the Elk to find it. A pass at 2,000-3,000 ft AGL shows the river and Main Street. Nearby airports: Grove Regional in Oklahoma (FAA identifier GMJ, GPS code KGMJ; no ICAO code listed), about 13 nm west-northwest; Neosho Hugh Robinson Airport (KEOS), about 17 nm north-northeast; Northwest Arkansas National (KXNA), about 18 nm south-southeast. Valley fog is common on calm mornings along the Elk.
