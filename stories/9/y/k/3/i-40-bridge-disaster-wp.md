# I-40 bridge disaster

On May 26, 2002, a towboat captain lost consciousness on the Arkansas River near Webbers Falls, Oklahoma, his barges struck the Interstate 40 bridge, and 14 people died when a 503-foot section fell into the water.

**Location:** 35.4861, -95.0991

**Categories:** Bridge disasters in the United States, Collisions between ships and bridges, 2002 disasters in the United States, Transportation in Muskogee County, Oklahoma, Arkansas River, Memorials

## Introduction

It was about 7:45 in the morning on May 26, 2002, a mild, calm morning on the Arkansas River southeast of Webbers Falls, Oklahoma. Out on the Robert S. Kerr Reservoir, Joe Dedmon was guiding the towboat Robert Y. Love with two empty barges when he lost consciousness. With the tow out of control, the barges collided with a pier supporting the Interstate 40 bridge. The pier failed, and a 503-foot section of the highway dropped into the river. Drivers heading across had no way of knowing that the road ahead was gone.

## The Road That Wasn't There

By the time traffic stopped and drivers understood what had happened, eight passenger vehicles and three semi-trucks had gone over the edge, some into the river and some onto the broken pieces of the collapsed bridge. Fourteen people died and five more were injured. The youngest of those who died was a three-year-old girl. Each of the fourteen had a life and people who loved them, and each was simply traveling an interstate on a spring morning, and the suddenness of it is part of what makes the day so hard to contemplate.

## Anglers Turned Rescuers

The first help came from the water. Fishermen competing in a bass tournament on the river near the bridge saw the disaster happen and moved at once. Some tried to aid the people who had fallen; others tried to stop the vehicles still heading toward the break. One fisherman fired a flare at an approaching tractor-trailer to get its driver to stop. Others threw ropes to people in vehicles in the water, trying to pull them out. Three people who had fallen into the river were pulled from the water by tournament boats and taken to a boat ramp at Webbers Falls. The response also drew an impostor: a man who posed as an Army captain at the scene for days, claiming command of the recovery effort before fleeing after confrontations with local and state officials. He was later caught in Canada and sentenced to prison for impersonating a federal officer and for possessing a firearm.

## Sixty-Four Days

Losing a section of Interstate 40 meant rerouting about 19,200 vehicles a day, by the state's 2001 count. Crews set to work rebuilding the bridge, and traffic resumed on July 29, 2002, just two months after the collapse. A project like that would normally be expected to take about six months, and the Federal Highway Administration called the reopening record-setting. For the families of the fourteen, the restored highway could not undo the loss, but it did mean the crossing where their loved ones died was whole again within a single summer.

## A Heartbeat in the Wheelhouse

Investigators looked hard at what had happened aboard the Robert Y. Love. Reports noted that the towboat had had a steering problem in 1994, but Coast Guard Lt. j.g. Natalie Magnino stated that it had been caused by debris jamming the rudder, not by mechanical failure or captain error. In 2004 the National Transportation Safety Board concluded that the collapse resulted from Dedmon's loss of consciousness, possibly due to an unforeseeable abnormal heart rhythm. The board recommended that the U.S. Coast Guard Research and Development Center evaluate alerter systems for the wheelhouses of inland towing vessels, and that the Federal Highway Administration develop a warning system to stop motorists when a bridge partly or totally collapses. In May 2003, the Mississippi-based towboat company, Magnolia Marine Transport Co., settled with the victims for an undisclosed amount.

## A Girl Releasing a Dove

Webbers Falls chose to remember in bronze. The town's memorial is a 14-foot sculpture that incorporates pieces of the wreckage and is topped by the figure of a young girl reaching upward to release a dove. She represents the youngest victim, the three-year-old girl. The sculptor, Shahla Rahimi-Reynolds, was chosen by the Webbers Falls Committee to design it, and fourteen granite plaques honor each of the people who died. The memorial, funded with $120,000 in federal transportation money approved through the state and $30,000 from the town, was dedicated on May 26, 2003, one year to the day after the collapse. On the 15th anniversary, the town held a special ceremony at Webbers Falls Historical City Park beside the monument, keeping faith with those who were lost and those who were left behind.

## Flight Context

The rebuilt Interstate 40 bridge crosses the Arkansas River at the Robert S. Kerr Reservoir southeast of Webbers Falls, Oklahoma, at approximately 35.486°N, 95.099°W. From about 2,000-3,000 feet AGL, follow I-40 southeast from Webbers Falls to the bridge over the reservoir; the memorial stands in the town's historical city park. Nearby airports: Muskogee-Davis Regional Airport (KMKO), about 17 nm northwest; Stigler Regional Airport, about 12 nm south (FAA identifier GZL); and Sallisaw Municipal Airport, about 15 nm east (FAA identifier JSV).
