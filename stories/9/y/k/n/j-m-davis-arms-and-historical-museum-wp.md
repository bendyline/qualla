# J. M. Davis Arms and Historical Museum

A boy's first gun at age seven grew into a lifelong obsession that filled the walls of a Claremore hotel and finally a state museum holding more than 12,000 firearms and thousands of other artifacts, plus beer steins, boot jacks and much else besides.

**Location:** 36.3153, -95.6150

**Categories:** Military and war museums in Oklahoma, History museums in Oklahoma, Museums in Rogers County, Oklahoma

## Introduction

J. M. Davis was seven years old when someone handed him his first gun. That gift lit an ambition to learn about every kind of firearm he could find, and the ambition never cooled. It carried him for seventy-eight years, until his death in 1973, and it left behind a collection big enough to fill a museum. Today the J. M. Davis Arms and Historical Museum in Claremore holds more than 12,000 firearms and thousands of other artifacts, gathered from all around the world by one man who simply never stopped looking.

## Guns on the Hotel Walls

In the late 1910s Davis came from Arkansas to Claremore and took over the Mason Hotel. Over the next dozen years his collection kept growing, and by 1929 it had reached 99 guns. Around that time he began hanging his firearms on the hotel's walls, turning a working hotel into an informal gallery. The range of what he collected was astonishing, stretching from a centuries-old Chinese hand cannon to what has been described as the world's smallest manufactured gun. The collection that would later be promoted as the "World's Largest Privately Owned Gun Collection" started as one man's passion displayed on the walls of the place he ran.

## A Gift to Oklahoma

Davis wanted the collection to outlive him. In 1965 he transferred ownership to a trust, the J. M. Davis Memorial Foundation, Inc., and the foundation reached an agreement with the state. Under its terms the State of Oklahoma took the entire collection on a ninety-nine-year lease for one dollar, on the understanding that the state would house, preserve and display it for the general public with no admission charge. It was an act of generosity aimed squarely at the future. His goal was to preserve a piece of history so that others might enjoy it for generations to come. Within four years, a modern facility of 40,000 square feet opened to an enthusiastic public, and the date was well chosen. The doors opened on Davis's 82nd birthday, so the man who had started collecting as a boy lived to see his life's work in a permanent home.

## Twelve Thousand Firearms and More

Walking the galleries is a tour through centuries of firearms history. There are Kentucky rifles, the long guns of the American frontier, and a Gatling gun. Black-powder guns of every type share space with cannons. Rare Colts and Winchesters, names that shaped the popular image of the Old West, sit near guns built for very particular purposes: elephant guns, whaling guns and guns that belonged to outlaws. The breadth is the point. Davis did not collect only the showpieces; he wanted to know about all kinds of guns, and the museum reflects that curiosity, placing the humble and the rare side by side so that visitors can trace how the tools changed across time and around the world.

## Steins, Boot Jacks and Brands

For all its fame as a gun museum, the collection wanders well beyond firearms. There are 1,200 German beer steins. There are statues by the sculptor John Rogers and music boxes from the late 19th century. Swords and knives fill cases of their own, joined by Native American artifacts and household antiques. A large collection of boot jacks, the humble devices that help pull off a pair of riding boots, sits alongside a record of local cattle brands, hundreds of animal horns and trophy heads, and a set of World War I posters. The mix feels less like a curated exhibition than like the accumulated treasure of a man who found almost everything interesting. That is exactly what it is.
