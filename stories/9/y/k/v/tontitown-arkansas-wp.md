# Tontitown, Arkansas

Founded in 1898 by Italian immigrants who fled a malarial Delta plantation for the Ozark hills, Tontitown turned ten-acre plots into vineyards and has celebrated with a spaghetti-and-fried-chicken festival for well over a century.

**Location:** 36.1792, -94.2467

**Categories:** Cities in Washington County, Arkansas, Northwest Arkansas, Italian-American culture in Arkansas, Immigrant history, 1898 establishments in Arkansas

## Introduction

The plate tells the whole story: spaghetti on one side, fried chicken on the other. That pairing, Italian and Southern on the same table, is how the Tontitown Grape Festival carries on a celebration that began in 1898, the year a band of Italian immigrants arrived in the Ozark hills of northern Washington County, Arkansas. It started as a thanksgiving picnic, became a harvest festival, and has been held ever since with rare interruptions. They came here after a disaster elsewhere in the state, and they built something on these slopes that has lasted well over a century.

## Fever on the Delta

The settlers' American story began not in the hills but in the flat, humid Arkansas Delta, on Sunnyside Plantation in Chicot County. Their leader was Pietro Bandini, a Catholic priest from Forlì who had already founded the Saint Raphael Society in New York to protect Italian immigrants from usury and labor exploitation. Before that he had served as a Jesuit missionary in the Montana Territory, and in New York he also founded the chapel that grew into Our Lady of Pompeii Church. Bandini believed Italians would prosper in an agricultural colony that resembled home, and the plantation's owner, Austin Corbin, was looking for laborers. It went badly. According to Bandini's biography, more than 125 of the people who moved to Sunnyside died of malaria, blamed on unsanitary conditions, unsafe drinking water and a climate that favored mosquitoes. When the plantation changed hands, conditions worsened, and the families who could afford it went back to Italy.

## Ten Acres Apiece

Bandini looked north. He bought 800 acres in northwest Arkansas, reasoning that the land's upland elevation and workable soil came close to the climate the immigrants had known in Northern Italy. The community settled here in 1898 and took the name of the Italian explorer Henri de Tonti. That first winter was hard, spent in abandoned farm buildings on meager food. Then the work began. Bandini established a schoolhouse and a church, and the first 35 to 40 families each received a 10-acre plot, the allotments handed out at random. They planted gardens and vineyards, and by 1909, when Tontitown incorporated, the community had grown to 1,400 acres. Bandini eventually became its mayor.

## Barns on Fire

Not every neighbor welcomed them. Some residents resisted and resented the newcomers, who largely spoke Italian and kept to themselves. The resentment turned into threats and then arson aimed at barns and the school. Bandini confronted a group to put a stop to the campaign of terror against the young settlement. The colony held. Its vineyards became commercially successful, and in 1905 the Italian ambassador to the United States visited and spoke approvingly of what the settlers had built. Bandini later returned to Italy, where his ideas about immigrant colonies impressed Pope Pius X and Queen Mother Margherita, and he died in Little Rock in 1917.

## Grapes, Then Growth

Tontitown's grapes eventually became a major crop across Washington County once the Welch company built a plant near the railroad in neighboring Springdale. Local farms also grew apples, blackberries, peaches, strawberries and tomatoes for canning in Springdale. Many of the original families still live in Tontitown or nearby Springdale, and the harvest festival goes on, its spaghetti-and-chicken dinner carrying the tradition forward. The town itself has changed quickly. Once a farm colony, it now serves as a bedroom community for Fayetteville and Springdale in the fast-growing Northwest Arkansas region. Its population grew 160 percent between the 2000 and 2010 censuses, from 942 residents in 2000, and reached 4,301 in 2020. The Ozark Catholic Academy, a Roman Catholic high school, is here, and the Jim D. Rollins School of Innovation, an elementary school built around an open-classroom model from New Zealand, opened in 2021.

## Flight Context

Tontitown sits at about 36.179°N, 94.247°W on the Ozark Plateau of northwest Arkansas, just west of Springdale, with U.S. Highway 412 running through town. From 2,500-3,500 ft AGL the town reads as a mix of new subdivisions and remaining farmland at the western edge of the Springdale-Fayetteville urban corridor. Nearby airports: Springdale Municipal (KASG), about 6 nm east; Northwest Arkansas National (KXNA), about 7 nm north-northwest; Drake Field at Fayetteville (KFYV), about 11 nm south-southeast. Expect busy airline and general-aviation traffic around KXNA and occasional morning fog in the valleys.
