# Natural Falls State Park

Once a roadside stop called Dripping Springs and a filming location for Where the Red Fern Grows, this Ozark park near the Arkansas line shelters a 77-foot waterfall that ranks among Oklahoma's tallest.

**Location:** 36.1736, -94.6669

**Categories:** State parks, Waterfalls, Ozarks, Film locations, Delaware County, Oklahoma

## Introduction

Natural Falls has a small secret. To keep its waterfall running all year long, the park can pump water from the pool at the base of the falls back up to the top. It is a practical fix for a spectacular sight. The waterfall here drops 77 feet in the Ozarks of Delaware County, Oklahoma, making it among the tallest waterfalls in the state, and it sits just off U.S. Highway 412, close to the Arkansas line.

## Before It Was Natural Falls

For years the property was privately owned and known as Dripping Springs. Its owners ran it as an attraction and a rest stop for travelers on the highway, complete with a swimming pool and gardens. In 1990 the State of Oklahoma bought the land. Local residents still call the waterfall Dripping Springs Falls, but the state gave the park a new name, Natural Falls, to avoid confusion with Dripping Springs State Park, now known as Dripping Springs Park, and other Oklahoma places with similar names. Today the park also includes Fox Meadow, a separate 120-acre hike-in preservation area, and a parking lot sits about 150 feet from a viewing platform at the head of the falls. The route between them complies with the Americans with Disabilities Act, so almost anyone can stand where the water tips over the edge.

## A Rival for Turner Falls

At 77 feet, the waterfall matches the commonly cited height of Turner Falls in the Arbuckle Mountains, and the two are often named among the tallest waterfalls in Oklahoma, though measurements and rankings vary. That is a remarkable claim for such a compact park. The falls do more than impress visitors. The waterfall creates a moist environment where ferns, mosses and liverworts thrive, a pocket of lush green only a short walk from the highway. Beyond the falls, the park offers a chance to observe a wide variety of plant and animal life. Most visitors arrive by private car, since there is no public transportation in the area, and the park sits right on U.S. 412.

## Where the Red Fern Grows

Filmmakers found this place before the state did. The site was used in the production of the 1974 film Where the Red Fern Grows. Directed by Norman Tokar and based on Wilson Rawls's 1961 novel, the film follows a boy in the Ozarks who earns the money to buy two coonhound puppies, and it was shot on location in Oklahoma, including at this very waterfall. The park remembers the connection in the name of its Red Fern Reunion Center, which is available for group functions. Away from the falls, hiking and nature trails totaling about 4.5 miles wind through a dense forest of maples, chinquapin and white oaks. On the cool forest floor grow flowering dogwood, sassafras, coralberry, spicebush, redbud and pawpaw. The park also offers a picnic shelter, playgrounds, volleyball, horseshoes, a basketball court, catch-and-release fishing and a formal garden. For visitors who want to stay the night, the park has installed five yurts for rent. Pets are welcome on a leash.

## Flight Context

Natural Falls State Park is at 36.1736°N, 94.6669°W along U.S. Highway 412 in Delaware County, Oklahoma, a few miles west of the Arkansas state line in the Ozarks. The falls are tucked into dense forest, so they are hard to see from altitude; a pass at 1,500–2,500 feet AGL (where permitted) is best for picking out the park along the highway. Nearby airports: Smith Field (KSLG) at Siloam Springs, Arkansas, about 9 nm E; Northwest Arkansas National (KXNA), about 19 nm ENE; and Grove Regional (KGMJ), about 26 nm N. Ozark hills and afternoon thunderstorms in spring and summer are the main considerations here.
