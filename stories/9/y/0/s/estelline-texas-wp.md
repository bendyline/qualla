# Estelline, Texas

A fading railroad junction in the Texas Panhandle that learned to pay its bills from the drivers passing through - until a lawsuit laid the numbers bare.

**Location:** 34.5461, -100.4370

**Categories:** Towns in Texas, Towns in Hall County, Texas, Texas Panhandle, Railroad towns, Speed traps

## Introduction

In fiscal year 2012, more than 89 percent of Estelline's gross revenue came from traffic fines and asset forfeiture. That number came to light around a federal civil rights case, and it confirmed what drivers on U.S. Highway 287 had long muttered about this stretch of Hall County. Estelline sits where 287 meets State Highway 86, about fourteen miles southeast of Memphis and fifteen miles northwest of Childress, and just 121 people lived here at the 2020 census. The community prefers to call itself the "Town of Estelline," complete with a Town Hall and a Town Council, even though Texas law treats every incorporated municipality as a city. In 2012 the National Motorists Association ranked it first on its list of the "Worst Speed Trap Cities" in North America among places with fewer than 50,000 people.

## Brass Bands and Branch Lines

Estelline once had a busier sound. The 1920s brought permanent brick school buildings, a champion basketball team and a town band conducted by Paul James. In 1927 the railroad began building a branch line out of Estelline to Plainview in Hale County, confirming the town's role as a junction on the plains. Then the Great Depression closed the bank. By 1940 the population had slipped to 603, and that same year two fires destroyed several businesses. Better highways did the rest, steadily eroding the importance of a railroad junction town. Fifteen businesses operated here in 1947; by 1980 only four remained, and the population stood at 258. Estelline stayed incorporated and kept its post office, but the school district was declared dormant in 1987 and its students began riding to schools in Memphis. The census counts tell the rest quietly: 194 residents in 1990, 168 in 2000, 145 in 2010.

## The Highway as a Revenue Stream

As the old economy drained away, the traffic kept coming. Estelline built a reputation as one of Texas's most prominent speed traps, relying on speeders to pay its bills. Its police force has been a single officer, and drivers describe the job as waiting for speeders. State officials took notice long before the national rankings did. Auditors examined the town's books in 1999 and again in 2003, and the first audit found that Estelline owed the state $15,025 in excess traffic fines. The 2012 speed-trap ranking turned a regional grumble into a national headline, and a lawsuit the following year supplied the hard figures behind it.

## Smoke, Seizures and a Settlement

In 2012 Estelline's Town Hall was mysteriously gutted by fire, and both the Texas Rangers and the State Fire Marshal described it as arson. Months later came a case that drew wider attention. On November 28, 2012, a woman driving through Estelline was arrested on a felony money laundering charge, and officers took more than $29,000 from her pickup, though all but $1,400 was later returned. In September 2013 she filed a federal suit against Estelline, Memphis and two officers, arguing that her Fourth Amendment protection against unreasonable search and seizure had been violated. The case revealed that the town kept no written records of past searches or seizures. The cities and officers denied her claims, but in July 2014 Estelline and Memphis settled with her for $77,500. Trouble followed the badge afterward too. In 2015 an Estelline police chief surrendered his peace officer license after being charged with official oppression over an off-duty traffic stop in Childress County, and in 2017 a later chief was arrested, then indicted, on a civil rights charge involving a person in custody; his peace officer status was revoked in 2018.

## Under a Restless Sky

Beyond the headlines, Estelline is 0.7 square miles of semi-arid plains, a place with a Köppen classification of BSk and a long view in every direction. Amarillo, the nearest major city, lies 102 miles to the northwest. The town is often described as sitting at the core of Tornado Alley, although the National Weather Service has never drawn official boundaries for that region, and the National Severe Storms Laboratory calls it a media term for areas with high tornado counts. The weather here has shown its teeth all the same. On March 10, 2007, a brief tornado embedded inside a downburst collapsed two old brick buildings, blew in doors and windows, and destroyed a mobile home. It was rated EF1. Local organizers have worked to bring people in for gentler reasons, too. In earlier years the nonprofit Estelline Outdoor Entertainment Association staged the Red River Festival and Motorcycle Rally and the Ozark Trail Barbecue Cook-off, which the Texas Senate recognized as a Texas State Barbecue Championship competition.

## Flight Context

Estelline lies at 34.5461 N, 100.437 W in east-central Hall County, Texas, at the junction of U.S. Highway 287 and State Highway 86. From 3,000-5,000 feet AGL the town appears as a small grid on open semi-arid plains; follow US 287 northwest about 14 miles to Memphis or southeast about 15 miles to Childress to orient. Nearest airports: Childress Municipal Airport (KCDS), about 4 nautical miles west of Childress, and Memphis Municipal Airport (FAA identifier F21, no ICAO code), just northeast of Memphis. This is severe-weather country in spring; check convective forecasts before flying.
