# JA Ranch

Founded in 1876 in Palo Duro Canyon by trail driver Charles Goodnight and Irish landowner John George Adair, the JA is the oldest cattle ranching operation in the Texas Panhandle and is still run by the descendants of Adair's widow, Cornelia.

**Location:** 34.8169, -101.1885

**Categories:** National Register of Historic Places in Armstrong County, Texas, Buildings and structures completed in 1879, National Historic Landmarks in Texas, Ranches in Texas, 1979 establishments in Texas, Agricultural buildings and structures on the National Register of Historic Places in Texas

## Introduction

The brand is two letters, and neither belongs to the man who actually ran the place. When Charles Goodnight settled on a name for the cattle operation he was building in Palo Duro Canyon, he suggested the initials of his partner, John George Adair, a wealthy landowner from County Laois in Ireland who put up the money while Goodnight ran the operation. Founded in 1876 southeast of present-day Amarillo, the JA Ranch is the oldest cattle ranching operation in the Texas Panhandle. A century and a half later, it is still an ongoing business, and it is still run by the descendants of Adair's widow, Cornelia.

## From Ranger to Trail Boss

Goodnight was born in Illinois in 1836 and became a Texas Ranger in 1857, years before cattle made his name. The Civil War left many thousands of head of cattle across Texas unsupervised, and after the fighting ended Goodnight threw himself into the work of gathering those herds and driving them to new ground. In the process he blazed major herding trails across West Texas. He went on to start a ranching operation in eastern Colorado, but the Panhandle pulled him back. In the summer and fall of 1876, he drove some 1,600 longhorns from Pueblo, Colorado, down into Palo Duro Canyon. That herd was the beginning of the JA.

## A Canyon Emptied by War

The canyon Goodnight chose had been the scene of violence only two years earlier. In the late summer of 1874, bands of Comanche, Kiowa, Southern Cheyenne and Arapaho people left their assigned reservations and took refuge on the canyon floor, hundreds of feet below the rim. On the morning of September 28, Colonel Ranald Mackenzie's Fourth U.S. Cavalry followed their trail to the edge, descended the steep slopes and fell on the villages by surprise. The people scattered and abandoned their camps. The soldiers captured more than 1,400 of their horses, gave some to their scouts, and shot the rest so the animals could never be recovered. It was the major battle of the Red River War, a campaign that ended with the southern Plains peoples confined to reservations in Indian Territory. When Goodnight's longhorns arrived in 1876, they were moving into a landscape its earlier inhabitants had been forced to leave.

## Irish Money, Texas Management

The partnership was simple in its division of labor: Adair funded the ranch's expansion, and Goodnight managed it. The two men drew up a five-year contract, and by the time it expired in 1882 they had bought 93,000 acres. The growth did not stop there. Under their oversight the JA eventually spread across more than 700,000 acres (by some counts about 1.3 million at its height), and by 1889 its herd had grown past 100,000 head. The arrangement even outlived Adair himself. When he died in 1885, his widow, Cornelia Wadsworth Ritchie Adair, stepped into the partnership alongside Goodnight. By the end of the decade Goodnight had moved on to a smaller ranch nearby, and the JA passed into the keeping of Cornelia's family. Her descendants maintained the ranch into the 21st century.

## Stone Below, Timber Above

The ranch headquarters sits a short way south of U.S. Route 287, and its buildings still show the outlines of that frontier enterprise. Goodnight began building there in 1879. The main house is two stories tall, its oldest portion a log cabin, while its larger main section pairs rough stone walls on the ground floor with a wood-framed second story above. Nearby stand the original 19th-century stables and corral, along with a bunkhouse for the ranch hands who did the daily work of moving and tending cattle. In 1960 the headquarters area was designated a National Historic Landmark for its association with Goodnight, one of the most influential cattle barons of the late 19th century. The honor looks backward, but the JA itself is no relic. Cattle ranching remains its business, and the two letters Goodnight chose for his partner still name the place.

## Flight Context

The JA Ranch headquarters lies at approximately 34.8169° N, 101.1885° W in Armstrong County, Texas, a short distance south of U.S. Route 287 and southeast of Amarillo. From 3,000 to 5,000 feet above ground level, the landmark to find is Palo Duro Canyon itself: a long, winding gash of red and tan rock cut into the otherwise flat, farmed High Plains. U.S. 287 runs northwest to southeast across the plains just north of the canyon country, making a useful line to follow toward the ranch. Rick Husband Amarillo International Airport (KAMA) lies roughly 40 miles to the northwest and is the nearest major airport. The Panhandle is known for strong, gusty winds, and spring and summer afternoons can bring fast-building thunderstorms, so morning flights usually offer the smoothest air and clearest views into the canyon.
