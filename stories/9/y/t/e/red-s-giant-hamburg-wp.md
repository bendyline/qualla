# Red's Giant Hamburg

On Route 66 in Springfield, Missouri, Red and Julia Chaney's burger stand, named when a too-tall sign lost its last two letters, served for nearly four decades and is believed to have been the world's first drive-through restaurant.

## Introduction

The sign was supposed to say "Giant Hamburger." But when Sheldon "Red" Chaney put it up beside U.S. Route 66 in Springfield, Missouri, he realized it stood too tall and threatened to hit the overhead power lines. So he sawed off the "er." The truncated word stuck, and for nearly four decades travelers and locals alike came to Red's Giant Hamburg, a roadside stand whose owners raised their own beef and which is believed to have been the world's first drive-through restaurant. The building is long gone now, but Springfield has never quite let it go.

## A Gas Station, a Bride and a Herd of Cattle

Red Chaney arrived in Springfield after World War II with a bride, Julia, and a business degree. He bought a small Sinclair gas station that came with several wooden motor court cabins tucked among the trees at the back of the property. A café was added in 1947. Over time the couple grew weary of pumping gas and running the motor court, and they decided a restaurant would make a better living. They already owned a small herd of beef cattle, so the choice of menu was an easy one: hamburgers. The Chaneys would keep raising their own beef until the day the business closed, serving their giant hamburgs alongside homemade root beer to a steady stream of customers pulling in off the Mother Road.

## Through the Window

What made Red's famous was not just the burgers but the way customers could get them. Red's is credited with the first known fast-food drive-through window, letting drivers order and collect their food without leaving the car. The claim is not uncontested: another Route 66 business, the Maid-Rite Sandwich Shop in Springfield, Illinois, also claims the honor. Either way, Red's became a staple of Route 66 and of the Springfield area. In 1982, the local rock and roll band The Morells filmed a tribute song, simply titled "Red's," at the restaurant itself. Two years later, in 1984, Red and Julia retired, and the doors closed. Red died on June 2, 1997, shortly after the restaurant building was demolished. Julia died in 2006.

## Raising the Sign Again

Springfield missed its famous stand. In 2013, a fundraising campaign set out to recreate the Red's Giant Hamburg sign, and the replica was installed in 2014 at the Birthplace of Route 66 Roadside Park, missing "er" and all. Then, in early 2019, the Springfield Business Journal announced that Red's Giant Hamburg itself would make a comeback, 35 years after the original closed. The project was led by David Campbell, owner of the local barbecue chain Buckingham's, and insurance agent Greg Iott, and the new restaurant, built on Route 413, was designed as a recreation of the original Route 66 location. It opened in August 2019 and served for almost six years before closing in May 2025, when the building was sold to a local investor. The sign, at least, still stands as a reminder of the Chaneys' corner of Route 66.

## Flight Context

The original Red's Giant Hamburg site lies at about 37.183°N, 93.325°W along Route 66 on the west side of Springfield, Missouri. The restaurant building was demolished in 1997, so there is no structure to find, but from 2,000-3,000 feet MSL you can trace Route 66's path through the city's west side. Springfield-Branson National Airport (KSGF) is only about 5 miles to the northwest, so the site lies within Springfield's terminal airspace; coordinate with Springfield approach before any sightseeing. Visibility is generally good, with spring thunderstorms the main weather concern in southwest Missouri.
