# United States Medical Center for Federal Prisoners

Born of a Depression-era land offer from Springfield, Missouri, this federal prison hospital has treated Mafia bosses, held wartime traitors and the Birdman of Alcatraz, and turned up in fiction from The Sopranos to Black Bird.

**Location:** 37.1776, -93.3247

**Categories:** Prisons in Missouri, Prison hospitals, Federal Bureau of Prisons Administrative Facilities, Buildings and structures in Springfield, Missouri, 1933 establishments in Missouri

## Introduction

It opened in the early 1930s under a name no government would choose today: the United States Hospital for Defective Delinquents. The wording belonged to its era. What it described, a hospital for people in federal custody, has lasted far longer than the name. Now called the United States Medical Center for Federal Prisoners, or MCFP Springfield, it is a federal prison for male offenders run by the Federal Bureau of Prisons, part of the Department of Justice.

## A Town Bids for a Prison

The prison came to Springfield because the city went after it. During the Great Depression, Springfield offered the federal government 620 acres of land for the project, an effort led by John T. Woodruff, a local business leader and president of the Chamber of Commerce. Congress authorized construction in 1930, and the hospital opened within a few years. For its first three decades, the institution was also a working farm. Prisoners farmed the surrounding land until 1966, and in 1977 the federal government handed some of the original 620 acres back to the city.

## Voices From the Wrong Side

Some of the prison's most striking early inmates were Americans who had spoken for the enemy. Robert Henry Best, a South Carolina minister's son, had settled in Vienna in 1923 as a freelance foreign correspondent; he later broadcast Nazi propaganda under the name "Mr. Guess Who." Herbert John Burgman, born in Hokah, Minnesota, had worked as a clerk and economic statistician at the U.S. embassy in Germany, and went on the air as "Joe Scanlon." A Boston federal court convicted Best of treason in 1948 and sentenced him to life. Burgman was convicted of treason in 1949. Both served their time at Springfield, and neither left. Best died there of a brain hemorrhage on December 16, 1952. Burgman died there exactly one year later, on December 16, 1953.

## Where the Bosses Came for Treatment

Because it is a medical center, MCFP Springfield has drawn prisoners whose names were once front-page news. Several high-profile Mafia bosses were treated here: Joseph Bonanno of the Bonanno crime family, Vito Genovese and Vincent Gigante of the Genovese family, and John Gotti of the Gambino family. For three of them it was the last stop. Genovese died at the prison in 1969, Gotti in 2002 and Gigante in 2005. Robert Stroud, known as the "Birdman of Alcatraz," was held here for treatment and died at Springfield in 1963. Terrorism convicts passed through as well, among them Omar Abdel Rahman, who was held here for a brief period.

## A Death on the Cell Floor

Not every chapter involves famous names. On January 26, 2010, Victor Castro-Rodriguez, 51, was found dead on the floor of his cell. He had been convicted of assault and resisting arrest in federal court in southern Florida, and he was at MCFP Springfield because he had a mental illness, sent to a place meant to treat him. Two fellow inmates were charged in his death. On May 7, 2014, both were convicted of first-degree murder, and on June 2, 2014, both were sentenced to death. In December 2024 President Joe Biden commuted both death sentences, and the two men will spend the rest of their lives in prison.

## The Prison on Screen

Popular culture has found its way to Springfield more than once. In The Sopranos, John Sacrimoni, boss of the fictional Lupertazzi crime family, dies at MCFP Springfield in the season 6 episode "Stage 5," and the show mentions in its first season that the fictional boss Ercole "Eckley" DiMeo is imprisoned here. The limited series Black Bird went further, following the true story of Jimmy Keene, a convicted drug dealer who went into the prison as an FBI informant. It was based on Keene's 2010 book In with the Devil: a Fallen Hero, a Serial Killer, and a Dangerous Bargain for Redemption, written with Hillel Levin.

## Flight Context

MCFP Springfield sits in the city of Springfield in southwest Missouri, at about 37.1776°N, 93.3247°W. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet AGL, the prison is one feature in a large urban area, so orient on the city itself. Springfield-Branson National Airport (KSGF), with scheduled airline service, is about 5 nm to the northwest, and Springfield's Downtown Airport (FAA identifier 3DW; no ICAO code) is about 4.5 nm to the northeast. Ozark haze is common in summer.
