# Pensmore

Hidden in the Ozark hills near Highlandville, Missouri, the 72,000-square-foot château called Pensmore was built of insulated concrete as a showcase for construction meant to shrug off earthquakes, EF5 tornadoes and bomb blasts, and its owner says it should stand for 2,000 years.

**Location:** 36.8797, -93.2125

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Christian County, Missouri, Châteauesque architecture in the United States

## Introduction

Most homeowners hope their house will outlast the mortgage. Steven T. Huff set his sights a little further out. "The house should stand for 2,000 years," he told The Kansas City Star in 2015, speaking of Pensmore, the château-style mansion rising in the Ozark Mountains near Highlandville, Missouri. At some 72,000 square feet, it is one of the largest homes in the United States, with five stories, 13 bedrooms and 14 baths. Its exterior walls are 12 inches thick. But its size is only half the story. Pensmore was designed to survive earthquakes, tornadoes and bomb blasts, and it was built as an experiment as much as a residence.

## A House Built Like a Bunker

Pensmore's secret is in its bones. It is an insulated concrete form structure, a method in which concrete is poured into insulating forms that stay in place as part of the wall. At Pensmore, the technique was meant to showcase sustainable construction on a very large scale. The design goals read like a list of every threat a building might face. The house was meant to be earthquake resistant, bullet resistant and blast resistant, capable of withstanding an EF5 tornado, and resistant to both fire and bugs. Behind those thick walls, the ambition was not simply to build a fortress but to demonstrate that such toughness could go hand in hand with sustainable construction. The outside, meanwhile, takes the form of a château, an Old World silhouette wrapped around very modern engineering.

## Why the Ozarks

The location was a deliberate choice. The Ozarks experience both high and low temperatures, as well as tornadoes, and the region offered Huff a proving ground under a broad range of climate conditions. The home's large size served the same purpose, allowing him to explore how these construction concepts would work on a commercial scale. There was also a practical freedom here: the site lay in an area not subject to government building inspections or regulations. In 2008, the Steven T. Huff Family LLC applied for a construction permit, and work on the mansion began soon after. Building something this ambitious took years. Construction is often dated as finished in 2016, but as late as 2022 local reporting still described Pensmore as a work in progress, a fitting status for a house conceived as an ongoing experiment.

## The Missing Fiber

A house meant to last two millennia depends on what goes into its concrete, and that became the subject of a legal fight. By the summer of 2016, news reports described a federal suit by Huff's lawyers against Monarch Cement Company of Humboldt, Kansas, and its Springfield subsidiary, City Wide Construction Products, seeking $63 million in damages. The suit alleged that Pensmore had been shorted more than 70,000 pounds of a crucial steel fiber called Helix, an alternative to steel rebar that was invented in a laboratory at the University of Michigan and developed for the United States Army Corps of Engineers. The dispute did not go to a verdict. A settlement was reached on July 21, 2017, and its terms were never made public, leaving the story of the fiber, like the house itself, partly hidden behind closed doors.

## Flight Context

Pensmore stands at about 36.880°N, 93.213°W near Highlandville in southern Christian County, Missouri, in the wooded Ozark hills between Springfield and Branson. From 2,500-3,500 feet MSL, look for the large château-style building standing out against the surrounding forest and farmland. It is a private residence, so enjoy it from the air only. Springfield-Branson National Airport (KSGF) lies about 27 miles to the north-northwest, and Branson Airport (KBBG) is roughly 24 miles to the south. The Ozark terrain rolls steadily here, and morning valley fog is common in cooler months.
