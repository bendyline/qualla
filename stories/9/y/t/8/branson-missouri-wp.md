# Branson, Missouri

A general store in the Ozark hills became, by way of a best-selling novel, two dams, a hillbilly jamboree and more than 50 theaters, the self-proclaimed live music show capital of the world.

**Location:** 36.6378, -93.2550

**Categories:** Cities in Taney County, Missouri, Branson, Missouri, Ozarks, Music venues, Tourist attractions in Missouri

## Introduction

"The live music capital of the entire universe." That was how 60 Minutes described Branson, Missouri, in an episode aired on December 8, 1991, and the town has never stopped leaning into the label. Branson would eventually count more than 50 theaters, most of them on Highway 76, now 76 Country Boulevard. Nationally known stars opened their own stages here, and home-grown shows filled theaters alongside them. Yet this resort city in the Ozark Mountains, home to 12,638 people at the 2020 census, began as nothing grander than a general store.

## A Store, Two Dams and a Novel

In 1882 Reuben Branson opened a general store and post office here, and the community took his name. Branson was formally incorporated on April 1, 1912. The nearby Powersite Dam on the White River formed Lake Taneycomo, which curls around the east and south sides of the city. Visitors had already begun to come. Harold Bell Wright's 1907 novel The Shepherd of the Hills, set in the Ozarks, became an international best seller and established Branson and Southwest Missouri as a tourist destination. In 1960 the Old Mill Theater launched an outdoor production based on the book, and The Shepherd of the Hills Outdoor Drama was still running in its 61st season in 2021. A second dam on the White River, Table Rock, was completed in 1958 and created Table Rock Lake.

## Caves, Hillbillies and a Jamboree

Tourism grew in stages. In 1894 William Henry Lynch began charging visitors to tour Marble Cave, later renamed Marvel Cave. Hugo and Mary Herschend leased it for 99 years in 1950, hosted square dances underground, added electricity and concrete staircases, and in 1960 opened Silver Dollar City, a re-created frontier town. Jim Owen, an entrepreneur who served 12 years as mayor, built the town's first theater in 1934 on Commercial Street, "The Hillbilly Theater." In 1959 the Mabe Brothers formed a band that in 1961 became Branson's first music show, the Baldknobber Hillbilly Jamboree, named for the parts they played in the Shepherd of the Hills drama. Television added its own twist in 1962, when Paul Henning, inspired by a Boy Scout camping trip to the Ozarks, created The Beverly Hillbillies. He later donated 1,534 acres for a conservation area near Branson, and the show's modified 1921 Oldsmobile truck now sits in the Ralph Foster Museum at College of the Ozarks.

## Life on the Strip

In 1967 the Presley family became the first to move their show, Presleys' Country Jubilee, to Highway 76, and the Baldknobbers followed a year later. In 1987 Boxcar Willie became the first internationally known entertainer to buy a theater in Branson and perform on a permanent schedule. Shoji Tabuchi opened his first theater in 1989; Mel Tillis, Jim Stafford, Ray Stevens, Mickey Gilley and Moe Bandy soon had stages of their own. The 76 Music Hall became the first theater with three different shows a day, and in 1991 local producer Bob Nichols opened the first morning show. The theaters kept coming: Andy Williams opened his Moon River Theatre on May 1, 1992, Glen Campbell's Goodtime Theatre followed in 1994, and Tony Orlando and Bobby Vinton headlined their own houses. The Acrobats of China arrived in 1998, one of the first international shows to call Branson home.

## The Leap Day Storm

Just after 1 a.m. on February 29, 2012, an EF2 tornado from the Leap Day outbreak cut a path of more than 20 miles from near Kimberling City across Table Rock Lake, along the entertainment strip and into downtown. It blew out or cracked windows in more than 200 of the 294 rooms of the 12-story Hilton Branson Convention Center Hotel and badly damaged three theaters. Forty-seven people were injured along its path, most with cuts and bruises, and one person was critically hurt when a roof collapsed. At least 37 buildings in Branson were destroyed and damage in Taney County reached $15 million, yet most attractions stayed open, and by November 2012 most of the damage had been repaired.

## Built for Families

Branson markets itself on family-oriented fun, so casino gambling is frowned upon and absent here. Instead there are cave tours and ziplines, a Titanic Museum built as a half-scale replica of the ship and its iceberg, and a Ripley's Odditorium housed in a building made to look as if an earthquake had cracked it wide open. Branson Landing opened on the Lake Taneycomo waterfront in 2006 with a boardwalk and fountains, and a ferris wheel moved from Chicago's Navy Pier in 2016 lifts riders 150 feet above the Ozarks. The Branson Cross stands nearby in Walnut Shade, billed as the largest cross in North America.

## Flight Context

Branson lies at 36.6378°N, 93.2550°W in the Ozark Mountains of southwest Missouri, wrapped on its east and south sides by the bend of Lake Taneycomo on the White River. From 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, follow 76 Country Boulevard, the theater strip, running west from downtown, and look for Table Rock Lake and Table Rock Dam just upstream to the southwest. The hilly, wooded terrain rises well above the lakes, so keep terrain clearance in mind. M. Graham Clark Downtown Airport (KPLK) at Point Lookout is only about 1.5 nm east-southeast, Branson Airport (KBBG) about 7 nm south-southeast, and Springfield–Branson National Airport (KSGF) about 37 nm north. Morning fog can settle in the river valleys.
