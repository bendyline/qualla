# Titanic Museum (Branson, Missouri)

Hundreds of miles from any ocean, the bow of a half-scale Titanic rises beside Branson's Country Boulevard, where visitors carry the name of a real passenger or crew member through 20 galleries and learn at the end whether that person survived.

**Location:** 36.6382, -93.2800

## Introduction

Every visitor walks in carrying someone else's name. At the Titanic Museum Attraction in Branson, Missouri, guests pass through an artificial iceberg and receive a boarding ticket bearing the name and a short biography of an actual passenger or crew member from the doomed liner. For the next stretch of the afternoon, that stranger's story travels with them. Only at the end of the tour do they learn whether the person on their ticket survived the sinking. It is a simple device, and a surprisingly affecting one, in a building that looks improbable from the road: the front half of an ocean liner, first two funnels and all, sitting beside a busy boulevard in the Ozark hills.

## An Ocean Liner on Country Boulevard

The museum stands at 76 Country Boulevard, on the entertainment strip that runs through Branson. Its exterior is a partial mockup of the original ship, built as a half-scale replica of the front half of the Titanic, with the bow and the first two funnels rising above the street. Inside, the two-story building is a self-guided walk-through. The museum opened on April 10, 2006, on the anniversary of the day in 1912 when the Titanic set out from Southampton on its maiden voyage. Its founder, John Joslyn, had a personal connection to the story: he co-led a 1987 expedition to the ship's final resting place on the floor of the North Atlantic. Branson's museum is one of two Titanic attractions he founded; the other stands in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, and shares the same ship-shaped design.

## Four Hundred Objects, Twenty Rooms

The collection brings together more than 400 artifacts, displayed across 20 galleries. The tour is built around people rather than engineering. As visitors move from room to room, they learn the individual stories of several passengers whose lives intersected on one voyage in April 1912. The centerpiece is a replica of the ship's First Class grand staircase, long associated with the liner's first-class elegance, and visitors can pose for souvenir photographs on its steps. The staircase offers a glimpse of the luxury the ship promised, while the boarding passes keep the focus on the people aboard. More than 1,500 people died when the Titanic sank, and the museum's approach makes that number personal, one name at a time.

## Relics and Rumors

Objects connected to a tragedy of this scale inevitably attract stories of their own. In 2017, the Travel Channel series Ghost Adventures sent Zak Bagans and his crew to investigate the museum, drawn by staff accounts of paranormal activity at the museum. Whatever visitors make of such claims, the episode speaks to the pull the Titanic still exerts more than a century later. In Branson, that pull has turned remembrance into a roadside spectacle: an iceberg at the door, a grand staircase inside, and at the end of the tour, a quiet moment when each visitor learns the fate of a stranger they have come to know.

## Flight Context

The Titanic Museum Attraction sits at about 36.638°N, 93.280°W on 76 Country Boulevard in Branson, Missouri. From 2,000-3,000 feet MSL, the ship-shaped building with its two funnels stands out along Branson's commercial strip, among the theaters and attractions that line the boulevard. Lake Taneycomo winds past downtown Branson to the east, and Table Rock Lake spreads to the southwest. Branson Airport (KBBG) is about 9 miles to the south-southeast, and Springfield-Branson National Airport (KSGF) lies roughly 42 miles to the north. The surrounding Ozark hills are forested and rugged, and valley fog can form on cool mornings.
