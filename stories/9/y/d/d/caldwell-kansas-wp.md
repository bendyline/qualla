# Caldwell, Kansas

Once the rowdy "Border Queen" of the Chisholm Trail cattle trade and the jumping-off point for the Cherokee Strip Land Run, tiny Caldwell now calls itself the Ornate Box Turtle Capital of the World.

**Location:** 37.0342, -97.6089

**Categories:** Cities in Kansas, Cities in Sumner County, Kansas, Cowtowns, Chisholm Trail, Populated places established in 1871

## Introduction

"As we go to press, hell is again in session in Caldwell." A Wichita newspaper wrote that line during the years when this little town on the Kansas-Oklahoma line was burning through lawmen, with violence claiming a string of its city marshals between 1879 and 1885. Caldwell was the Border Queen then, a cattle town sitting almost on top of Indian Territory. Today it is a quiet community of about a thousand people with a very different claim to fame: by a 1986 mayoral proclamation, it calls itself the Ornate Box Turtle Capital of the World.

## Before the Trail

People lived on this land long before any town did. Near Caldwell lies the Buresh site, a precontact Plains Village period settlement that has yielded clues about Native American lifeways before the arrival of people of European and African descent. The town itself came in 1871, founded by investors from Wichita and named for U.S. Senator Alexander Caldwell. A post office opened that May. The reason for the location ran right past the east side of the community: from 1867 to 1871, the Chisholm Trail carried Texas cattle north along this route. For most of the 1870s, though, Caldwell remained a small place, not incorporated as a city until 1879.

## Queen of the Border

The cattle business made Caldwell famous. During the 1880s it challenged Dodge City for the cattle market, and although it lay within the quarantined area, it sat so near the border that it could do business without trouble. Nearly 100,000 head of Texas longhorns were shipped out on the Santa Fe line in 1882 and 1883 alone. Life in Caldwell took on the same rowdy character seen in other cattle towns during its cowtown period, from 1880 to 1885, and it was a hard era for anyone wearing a badge. Farming settlement pushed the cattle shipping trade westward by 1886, and the Border Queen's rough reign came to an end.

## The Marshal Who Robbed a Bank

The strangest lawman story came on April 30, 1884, when the citizens of Caldwell were shocked to learn that their own marshal and his assistant were bank robbers and murderers. Marshal Henry Newton Brown and assistant marshal Ben Wheeler had ridden west with two cowboys from the Cherokee Outlet to rob the Medicine Valley Bank in Medicine Lodge. The bank's president, Wylie Payne, and its cashier, George Geppert, were shot and killed, though Geppert sealed the vault before he died and the robbers left empty-handed. A posse trapped the four in a box canyon. When the prisoners tried to break free, Brown was shot dead, and Wheeler and the two cowboys were hanged from a nearby elm tree.

## Gateway to the Last Great Rush

Caldwell's position on the border made it important one more time. In 1893 Congress opened the Cherokee Outlet, just two miles south of town, for settlement, and thousands of land-hungry pioneers crowded into Caldwell before making the run known as the Cherokee Strip Land Run, often described as the last great land rush in America. Railroads kept the town connected. In 1887 the Chicago, Kansas and Nebraska Railway built a branch line north to south from Herington to Caldwell, stitching together a string of towns including Marion, Peabody, Wichita and Wellington, and in 1888 the line was extended to Pond Creek, Oklahoma.

## Turtles on Main Street

Modern Caldwell found its mascot in a small, patterned reptile. A sixth-grade class in Caldwell nominated the ornate box turtle as the state reptile of Kansas and lobbied until Governor John Carlin signed the bill on April 14, 1986, and during the town's fall Homecoming celebration in 1986 the mayor signed a proclamation declaring that Caldwell would forever be known as the Ornate Box Turtle Capital of the World. A couple of years later, a professional wildlife artist painted a portrait of the turtle on the side of a downtown building on Main Street. The town has shrunk slowly, from 1,284 residents in 2000 to 1,025 in 2020, but it still gathers for its Chisholm Trail Festival, Oktoberfest and a lighted Christmas parade. Its notable natives include the fiddler Byron Berline, the singing Dinning Sisters and the actor Max Showalter.

## Flight Context

Caldwell lies at 37.034°N, 97.609°W in Sumner County, just north of the Kansas-Oklahoma state line, about two miles above the old Cherokee Outlet boundary. From 2,000-3,500 feet, the compact town grid stands out against flat farm country, with the state line just to the south. Caldwell Municipal Airport (FAA identifier 01K), a small turf strip, lies about one nautical mile east of downtown; Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National Airport (KICT) is the nearest major airport, to the north. Visibility is usually good, though spring storms and strong south winds are common on the southern Kansas plains.
