# Kansas Aviation Museum

Wichita's 1935 Art Deco airport terminal, which once watched a takeoff or landing every 90 seconds, now houses the Kansas Aviation Museum, where visitors can climb aboard some of the aircraft on display during its annual Play on a Plane Day.

**Location:** 37.6322, -97.2736

**Categories:** Aerospace museums in Kansas, Museums in Wichita, Kansas, Art Deco airports, National Register of Historic Places in Wichita, Kansas, Aviation history

## Introduction

By 1944, an airplane was taking off or landing at Wichita Municipal Airport every ninety seconds. The Art Deco terminal that presided over all that traffic still stands near 31st South and George Washington Boulevard, a road to which the Wichita City Council added the honorary name "Air Capital Blvd" in February 2024 in recognition of the city's aviation impact. Designed by Glen H. Thomas, the building served as the airport's terminal from 1935 to 1954. Today it is home to the Kansas Aviation Museum, which has occupied it since 1991 and whose collection of bombers, airliners and trainers has given the old terminal a second life.

## Groundbreaking in Hard Times

The story starts with open prairie. In the late 1920s the city bought 640 acres of it, land that had already hosted airshows as far back as 1924. At a ceremony in June 1930, L.W. Clapp broke ground on the administration building, but the Great Depression stalled the work until Works Progress Administration funds became available in 1934. The building was finally dedicated on March 31, 1935. In the years that followed, a remarkable procession passed through its doors: Charles Lindbergh, Amelia Earhart, Wiley Post and Howard Hughes, along with Fred Astaire, Eleanore Whitney and Hopalong Cassidy. In 1990 the building was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the "Administration Building."

## Ninety Seconds Apart

War changed the field. In 1941 the United States Army Air Corps leased the airport, added the upper control tower and began operations by March 1942. Wings went up on both sides of the terminal as the Army's procurement division oversaw deliveries of Boeing Kaydet trainers and B-29 bombers, and by 1944 the pace had reached a takeoff or landing every ninety seconds. After World War II the airport returned to civilian use, but not for long. On May 31, 1951, the U.S. Air Force took title to Wichita Municipal Airport, and the city was awarded $9.4 million to build a new airfield for its own use, the one now known as Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National Airport. The old field became McConnell Air Force Base, named for the flying McConnell brothers of Wichita, among them Fred and Thomas, who were pilots in World War II.

## Come Aboard

The museum's collection runs from the 1920s to the jet age. Among the aircraft on display are an early-1920s Laird Swallow, a 1926 Swallow, a 1930 Watkins Skylark SL, a Stearman 4D, the Texaco 11, a Stearman Trainer and a 1944 Beech Staggerwing. The jet age arrives with a Republic F-84F Thunderstreak, a Lockheed T-33, a Cessna T-37, a Learjet 23 and the Cessna 500/501 prototype, alongside a Mooney Mite, a U-8 Seminole and the Beech Starship. The big iron includes a WB-47E Stratojet, a B-52D Stratofortress, a KC-135 Stratotanker, a Boeing 727 and a Boeing 737-2H4. Unusually, this is one of only a few museums that let visitors step inside its exhibition aircraft, though only once a year, on its "Play on a Plane Day." More recently, a storm on June 17, 2025, caused the museum light water damage.

## Flight Context

The Kansas Aviation Museum stands at approximately 37.63°N, 97.27°W on the northern edge of McConnell Air Force Base (KIAB) in Wichita, Kansas, well under a mile from the base, so it sits squarely in military and Wichita terminal airspace; coordinate with Wichita approach and expect restrictions near the base. From 2,500-3,500 ft AGL, McConnell's long runways are the unmistakable reference, with the museum's terminal building just beside them. Cessna Aircraft Field (KCEA) is about 1.5 nm northeast, Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National (KICT), the airport that replaced this one for civilian service, is about 7 nm west, and Beech Factory Airport (KBEC) is about 5 nm northeast.
