# Old Cowtown Museum

Beside the Arkansas River, Old Cowtown Museum preserves the cattle-drive era that gave Wichita its nickname, with 54 historic and re-created buildings on 23 acres off the original Chisholm Trail.

**Location:** 37.6936, -97.3569

**Categories:** Living museums in Kansas, Museums in Wichita, Kansas, American West museums, Chisholm Trail, Museums established in 1952

## Introduction

Wichita came by its nickname honestly. In 1872, the Wichita and Southwestern Railroad finished a branch line connecting the young town to the Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway at Newton, and cattle drives from Texas began ending here. Wichita became a railhead, and the herds gave it a name it never shook: "Cowtown." Beside the Arkansas River in central Wichita, Old Cowtown Museum keeps that era close enough to walk through. Established in 1952 on 23 acres off the original Chisholm Trail, it is one of the oldest open-air history museums in the central United States, with 54 historic and re-created buildings, among them a period farm and its out-buildings.

## Grass Houses and a Trading Post

Before the cattle came, the Wichita people were here. The first permanent settlement on the site of the city was a collection of grass houses where members of the Wichita tribe lived in 1864. In the 1860s the pioneer trader Jesse Chisholm set up a trading post at the site, and his name passed to the great cattle route, the Chisholm Trail, which ran along the east side of Wichita from 1867 to 1871. Businessmen founded the town of Wichita in 1868, and it was incorporated as a city in 1870. Within two years, the arrival of the railroad would turn the trail town into a railhead, and the cattle trade would define it.

## Two Banks of the Arkansas

At the end of the trail, the cowboys found their entertainment across the river. Delano, the neighboring town on the west bank of the Arkansas, was a village of saloons and brothels with a particular reputation for lawlessness, and it largely catered to the rough cattlemen passing through. Together, Wichita and Delano gained a wild name. The east bank, Wichita proper, stayed more civil, thanks to a series of well-known lawmen hired to keep the rowdy cowboys in line, Wyatt Earp among them. The boom could not last. As the railroads pushed farther west and south, Wichita lost the cattle drives and the cowboys along with them. In 1880 the city annexed Delano, a speculative land boom swept through in the late 1880s, and by 1890 Wichita's population had exploded to nearly 24,000.

## A Frontier Town, Rebuilt

Old Cowtown is several things at once: an attraction, a museum, a living history site and a historic preservation project. Its 54 buildings, some historic and some re-created, turn the story of the cattle-town years into streets a visitor can actually walk, and the period farm carries the picture out beyond the town. The museum is accredited by the American Alliance of Museums, and the City of Wichita works with the board of Historic Wichita Cowtown, Inc., a not-for-profit organization, to carry out its mission. The frontier setting has also drawn filmmakers. Productions shot here include Skylark in 1992, Touched by Fire in 2004, Bloody Dawn in 2006, Wichita in 2013 and Midnight Shanghai in 2016.
