# Museum of World Treasures

Born from one Wichita collector's holdings in 2001, the Museum of World Treasures now fills a three-story Old Town warehouse with Ivan the T. rex, Roman coins, presidential signatures and a piece of the Berlin Wall.

**Location:** 37.6800, -97.3400

**Categories:** Museums in Wichita, Kansas, Natural history museums in Kansas, History museums in Kansas, Dinosaur museums in the United States, Museums established in 2001

## Introduction

The glass elevator at the Museum of World Treasures rises above a dinosaur. Riders float up over the towering fossil exhibit on their way to galleries of medieval documents, trench-warfare artifacts and manuscripts by Mozart and Beethoven. That is the whole spirit of this Wichita museum in a single ride. Rather than confine itself to one era or one discipline, it gathers a Tyrannosaurus, Egyptian mummies, the signatures of every American president, a section of the Berlin Wall and much more under one roof in Old Town. The result is less a single story than a cabinet of wonders, three floors deep.

## A Collection That Outgrew Its Walls

The museum opened on April 22, 2001, as the Museum of Ancient Treasures, in the Garvey Building in downtown Wichita. Most of its first exhibits came from the collection of its founder, Dr. Jon Kardatzke. It did not stay small for long. In less than a year, the holdings had grown to more than 30 private collections spanning prehistory to the present, and the purchase of three complete fossil dinosaur skeletons forced a search for more room. In 2003 the museum moved to the Farm and Art Market in Old Town and took its current name. Its home is a reconstructed three-story warehouse, completely renovated for the collection. More than 300 collectors have since placed items there as loans or donations, and the museum owns about three-quarters of what it shows. It is a not-for-profit Kansas corporation governed by a board of directors, and a member of the American Alliance of Museums, though not accredited by it.

## Ivan and the Sea Creatures

The first floor belongs to deep time and the ancient world. Its star is Ivan the T. rex, unearthed in northwestern South Dakota by Alan Komrosky and Gary Olsen of Hell Creek Relics and dedicated at the museum in 2007. Ivan is about 65 percent real fossil, and his tail is reported to be among the most complete of any T. rex yet found. Nearby, "Sea Creatures of the Plains" displays marine fossils from Kansas, among them a 34-foot Tylosaurus, a Xiphactinus and a mosasaur, reminders that this landlocked state was once home to giants of the water. The floor also holds a geology room, the preserved remains of women from ancient Egypt, and a 50-foot timeline of gold, silver and bronze coins from nearly every Roman emperor, each accompanied by accounts of their exploits and conquests. Showcases trace Greek, Roman, Etruscan, Egyptian, Pre-Columbian, African, Southeast Asian and ancient Near Eastern civilizations.

## Knights, Trenches and a President's Desk

Climb to the second floor and the subject turns to power and conflict. "More Than Darkness" gathers documents and artifacts from the Middle Ages, while "A Touch of Classicism" follows the cultural phenomenon of the Renaissance. The Military Gallery carries visitors from the Revolutionary War through the Civil War, both world wars, Korea and Vietnam, and it includes a large-scale recreation of World War I trench warfare. Across the floor, the American Presidents Gallery holds the signature of every American president, along with a replica of the famous Resolute Desk, and the floor's themes reach beyond American leaders to royalty from around the world.

## From Mozart to Sinatra

The top floor is devoted largely to American history. "All Are Not Free" uses Cold War artifacts to tell the story of life east of the Iron Curtain. "Romancing the West" turns to frontier living, cowboys, Native Americans and the Pony Express. Music lovers can linger over original manuscripts from Mozart, Beethoven, Elvis and Frank Sinatra, and the Kardatzke Historic Authors Collection gathers material from writers and poets such as Robert Frost and Mark Twain. For younger visitors there is Ivan's PlayHouse, a play space with educational touches, and some exhibits rotate through for a limited time. The museum's mission is to educate, entertain and inspire, and it pursues it with camp-ins, scout programs, educational tours, lectures and evening events. The top floor also has a large banquet room that is rented out for weddings, dinners and meetings.
