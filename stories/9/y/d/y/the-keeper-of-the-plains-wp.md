# The Keeper of the Plains

Kiowa-Comanche artist Blackbear Bosin's 44-foot steel figure has stood at the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas rivers since 1974, raised on a rock promontory and ringed by fire pits as one of Wichita's most beloved symbols.

**Location:** 37.6914, -97.3497

## Introduction

As evening comes to Wichita, flames rise from stone fire pits where two rivers meet, and a towering steel figure glows against the darkening sky. He stands above the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas rivers, arms lifted, face turned upward, rising 44 feet from his base. This is The Keeper of the Plains, the work of Kiowa-Comanche artist Blackbear Bosin. Since 1974 it has grown into one of Wichita's most recognized and beloved symbols, an image the city has carried onto the tails of Air Force tankers and even into a Jeopardy! clue.

## The Painter Who Built in Steel

Blackbear Bosin was born in 1921 in Oklahoma, of Kiowa-Comanche heritage, and came to Wichita in 1940. It became his adopted city. He started out in commercial work, as a color separator and plate maker for Western Lithograph and as an illustrator for Boeing Aircraft, but his real calling was art, and he was almost entirely self-taught. Bosin built his reputation as a painter whose work drew on Native spiritual traditions. The Keeper of the Plains was something different: a monumental sculpture in Cor-Ten steel, standing 13.4 meters, or 44 feet, tall. According to the Kansas Historical Society, Bosin donated the sculpture to the city. He lived in Wichita for the rest of his life and died there on August 9, 1980, six years after his Keeper first took its place above the rivers.

## A Bicentennial Figure at the Confluence

The sculpture was erected in May 1974, a gift to the city to mark the United States Bicentennial. Its site carries meaning of its own. The Keeper stands at the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas rivers, adjacent to the Mid-America All-Indian Center, on ground where Native peoples lived long before Wichita existed. Around the statue's base, a series of displays describes the local tribes that once inhabited the area, turning the monument into an outdoor lesson in the region's first history. Cor-Ten, the steel Bosin chose, is a weathering steel, so the figure wears a rich rust-brown finish that suits its setting on the plains. From the riverbanks, the silhouette reads clearly against the open Kansas sky.

## Raised on Stone, Ringed by Fire

In the spring and summer of 2006, a major project lifted the Keeper onto a 30-foot rock promontory so that he could be seen from farther away. The site's other signature feature makes evening visits memorable: the fire pits known as the Rings of Fire. For public safety they are lit by hand, and they burn in 15-minute increments. They are lit once a night, at 9 p.m. during daylight saving time and 7 p.m. during standard time, though not in high winds, rain or when the river runs too high. When the flames rise around the base, they illuminate the steel figure above them. It is a simple effect, fire and steel beside moving water, but it transforms a daytime landmark into a nighttime spectacle.

## An Emblem That Took Flight

The Keeper's reach extends well beyond the riverbank. A profile of the statue became the motif of the 22nd Air Refueling Wing, the U.S. Air Force unit based at nearby McConnell Air Force Base. From 1993 through 2004, an image of the Keeper, along with the words "Keeper of the Plains," appeared on the tails of the wing's KC-135 air refueling tankers, carrying Bosin's figure across the skies. The statue has turned up in music too: Christian musician Rich Mullins mentioned it in his 1991 song "Calling Out Your Name." On March 11, 2021, a photo of the Keeper appeared in a Jeopardy! clue about U.S. landmarks. Wichita has even shared its symbol abroad, donating a copy of the statue to its sister city of Tlalnepantla, Mexico.

## Flight Context

The Keeper of the Plains stands at 37.6914°N, 97.3497°W at the confluence of the Arkansas and Little Arkansas rivers, just west of downtown Wichita, Kansas. From the air, the Y-shaped meeting of the two rivers is the key landmark, with downtown's towers just to the east. The statue on its rock promontory is visible from low altitude, and after dark, when the Rings of Fire are lit, the glow at the river point makes a striking sight. Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National Airport (KICT) is about 5 miles to the west-southwest, McConnell Air Force Base (KIAB) about 7 miles to the southeast, and Colonel James Jabara Airport (KAAO) about 8 miles to the east-northeast. The site lies within Wichita's Class C airspace, so contact Wichita Approach and plan on around 2,000 feet AGL. Summer brings thunderstorms and gusty south winds.
