# 1991 Andover tornado

On April 26, 1991, an F5 tornado carved a 46-mile path from Clearwater past Wichita, skirted a line of B-1B bombers at McConnell Air Force Base, and destroyed Andover's Golden Spur Mobile Home Park, where a community storm shelter protected 149 residents.

**Location:** 37.6900, -97.1361

**Categories:** F5, EF5 and IF5 tornadoes, Tornadoes in Kansas, Tornadoes of 1991, Butler County, Kansas, Sedgwick County, Kansas

## Introduction

The sirens were not working. So at 6:31 on the evening of April 26, 1991, police officers in Andover, Kansas, drove through the Golden Spur Mobile Home Park and through the rest of town, warning residents to take shelter. About a quarter of an hour later the tornado arrived. It had already cut across the southeastern edge of the Wichita area and skirted a line of bombers at McConnell Air Force Base, and it reached Andover at the top of the Fujita scale, F5, with winds greater than 260 miles per hour. By the time it lifted, it had killed seventeen people, injured more than two hundred and left an estimated $300 million in damage.

## A Day Marked in Advance

This was not a storm that caught forecasters by surprise. On April 25, the National Weather Service warned that computer models were "indicating this to be a very significant severe weather producer with tornadoes occurring across the Central/Southern Plains." By the morning of April 26, it had placed the Great Plains under a High risk of severe weather. The ingredients were in place. A low-level jet of winds up to 60 knots streamed over south-central Kansas and on north into eastern Nebraska, while a surface low over southwestern Nebraska anchored a dry line stretching south into Texas and a warm front reaching southeast across eastern Kansas and Oklahoma. Early attempts at thunderstorms along the dry line failed. The later ones did not.

## Past the Bombers

The tornado first touched down near Clearwater and moved northeast as a weak F0 and F1 storm. It did not stay weak for long. Approaching Haysville it intensified rapidly, and it was a strong F2 or F3 when it struck the town directly, leaving heavy damage behind. From there it crossed into residential subdivisions in eastern Wichita. In the Greenwich Heights Subdivision, even some well-built houses were completely leveled, and four people were killed. Those four deaths, all in Sedgwick County, came before the storm reached its full strength. At 6:24 p.m. the tornado, now violent and growing wider, struck McConnell Air Force Base. The base was already on alert, its civil defense siren blaring as the funnel crossed the grounds. Parked on the tarmac was a lineup of ten B-1B bombers, each worth about $280 million in 1991 dollars, two of them reportedly equipped with nuclear warheads. The tornado narrowly missed them. Beyond the base, the storm kept strengthening, and it reached F5 intensity for the first time as it entered Andover.

## Golden Spur

The tornado entered southern Andover around 6:43 p.m. and scored a direct hit on the Golden Spur Mobile Home Park. Most of the homes there were destroyed; many were swept away entirely or obliterated down to their bare metal frames. A National Weather Service surveyor, writing in a post-event document that December, said "the mobile home devastation was some of the worst that this surveyor has ever seen." Thirteen people in the park were killed, and dozens more were hurt as it was torn apart. The toll could have been far worse. Before the tornado arrived, 149 residents had taken refuge in the park's community storm shelter, and it is likely that many more people would have died had that shelter not existed.

## Forty-Six Miles

After leaving heavy damage across Andover, the tornado began to weaken. It pushed on to the northeast, brushing the outskirts of Towanda, and near El Dorado it struck an oil field. There it lofted a full oil tank some 0.8 miles, a tank that was never recovered, and obliterated two more. At 7:10 p.m. it dissipated west of El Dorado, north of the Kansas Turnpike, after tracking 46 miles in nearly an hour and a half. The Federal Emergency Management Agency designated much of the mobile home park and other hard-hit areas a federal disaster area. In all, the tornado took seventeen lives and injured 225 people.

## Flight Context

Andover lies at approximately 37.69°N, 97.14°W, just east of Wichita in Butler County, Kansas. The tornado's 46-mile track ran southwest to northeast, from near Clearwater through Haysville, eastern Wichita and McConnell Air Force Base to Andover and on toward El Dorado, so a northeast-bound leg from south of Wichita traces the path. From 2,500-3,500 ft AGL the flat terrain makes towns and road grids easy to pick out. McConnell Air Force Base (KIAB) is about 7.5 nm west-southwest of Andover; the base, along with Wichita Dwight D. Eisenhower National (KICT) about 14 nm west, means close coordination with Wichita approach. Nearby civil fields include Beech Factory Airport (KBEC), about 4 nm west, Colonel James Jabara Airport (FAA identifier AAO), about 5 nm northwest, and Augusta Municipal (FAA identifier 3AU, no ICAO code), about 3 nm east-southeast. Spring afternoons here can turn severe quickly; respect the convective forecast.
