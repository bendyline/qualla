# Red River Station, Texas

Herd after herd on the Chisholm Trail crossed the Red River at this Montague County settlement, the last stop in Texas before Indian Territory, yet a bypassing railroad and a tornado left nothing behind but a cemetery.

**Location:** 33.8731, -97.8086

**Categories:** Geography of Montague County, Texas, Ghost towns in North Texas

## Introduction

Herd after herd of longhorns driven north on the Chisholm Trail passed this way. Herds bound for Kansas converged on a crossing of the Red River at Salt Creek, in what is now northwestern Montague County, Texas, and the settlement that grew beside it was the last stop in Texas before the river and the Indian Territory beyond. Red River Station had a post office, a change of name and, for a few years, the traffic of the Chisholm Trail flowing past its door. Today nothing remains of the community except its cemetery.

## A Border on the Water

Long before anyone drove cattle here, Native Americans used the fertile land near the Red River for hunting. American settlers began arriving in 1859 and 1860, as settlement and conflict pushed Native peoples out of the area. The river was more than a landmark. It marked the border between Texas and the Indian Territory, and when the Civil War came, that border took on military weight. Confederate troops were stationed at the site, part of the Frontier Regiment, and they patrolled along the south bank of the Red River, watching the line between the settlements and the territory to the north. For the families who had only just arrived, the war brought soldiers to their doorstep and made the river crossing a point to be guarded.

## The Winter of 1863

In December 1863, the violence that border patrols were meant to prevent came across the water. A destructive raid, launched from the Indian Territory, crossed the Red River a few miles east of Red River Station and swept through Montague County and on into neighboring Cooke County. The raiders numbered between 200 and 300, and settlers in their path were killed. Confederate troops gave chase, but the raiders disappeared back into the Indian Territory. For the young settlements along the river, the raid was devastating, and it showed how exposed this edge of Texas remained. It also underlined what the river crossings here were: gateways that worked in both directions, open to whoever chose to use them.

## Last Stop in Texas

After the war, the crossing found a new purpose. Cattle drives began moving from south and central Texas toward Kansas, and in 1867 Joseph McCoy built stockyards on the Kansas Pacific railroad at Abilene, Kansas, giving Texas herds a railhead to aim for. About 35,000 head made the trip that first year. The route they followed became the Chisholm Trail, and once its main line shifted west in the early 1870s, Red River Station was its last stop in Texas. Herd after herd crossed the river here, and a community grew to serve the traffic. In 1873 its citizens opened a post office under the name Salt Creek, sharing its name with the stream beside the settlement. In 1884 the post office was renamed Red River Station, though neither the post office nor the community would last much longer.

## Bypassed and Blown Away

The station's fortunes turned on a railroad. When a new line was built across northern Montague County in 1886 and 1887, its right-of-way ran south of Red River Station, through present-day Nocona and Belcherville. New towns sprang up along the tracks, and as the cattle drives came to an end, the crossing that had once drawn herd after herd lost its reason to exist. Then, in the late 1880s, a tornado struck and destroyed much of the community. Red River Station never recovered. Rather than rebuild, most of its people moved south to the towns along the rail line. The post office closed in 1887, and the land went back to farming. Only the Red River Station Cemetery, west of Salt Creek and due south of the river, remains. Texas has not forgotten the place: a historical marker commemorating the settlement was placed in 1963, and a second marker, telling of its role on the Chisholm Trail, followed in 1971.

## Flight Context

Red River Station lies at approximately 33.8731° N, 97.8086° W, on the south bank of the Red River at the mouth of Salt Creek in northwestern Montague County, Texas. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet above ground level, follow the Red River, which forms the Texas-Oklahoma border here, and look for Salt Creek's junction with the river; the site today is farmland, with the small cemetery west of the creek. The town of Nocona, which grew along the railroad that bypassed the station, lies a few miles to the southeast and makes a useful reference. Sheppard Air Force Base / Wichita Falls Municipal Airport (KSPS) is roughly 40 miles to the west, and Gainesville Municipal Airport (KGLE) is about 38 miles to the east-southeast. North Texas skies are generally clear, but spring brings strong thunderstorms and the Red River valley can be gusty.
