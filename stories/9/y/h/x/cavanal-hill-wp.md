# Cavanal Hill

Poteau, Oklahoma, bills Cavanal Hill as the "World's Highest Hill" on a technicality, but the summit above town has hosted French trappers, a health resort, a coal town and a senator's summer home.

**Location:** 35.0708, -94.6794

**Categories:** Hills of Oklahoma, Landforms of Le Flore County, Oklahoma, Roadside attractions, Choctaw Nation history

## Introduction

The sign at the base makes a careful boast: "World's Highest Hill - Elevation: 1,999 feet." The number is doing clever work. The claim rests on a distinction between a hill and a mountain, with a mountain defined, by one rule of thumb, as rising 2,000 feet or more above its base. Other sources use 1,000 feet. Stop one foot short and, by that logic, you have the tallest hill anywhere. The actual summit of Cavanal Hill, officially Cavanal Mountain, stands 2,385 feet above sea level near the town of Poteau, Oklahoma, and some other summits in the United States that are named "hill" rise more than 2,000 feet above their surroundings. The sign makes its boast anyway, and the Poteau Chamber of Commerce repeats it.

## A French Name on an Oklahoma Hill

Long before the sign, Cavanal was a landmark for French explorers who traveled this area in the 18th century, and they gave it its name. Where the word came from is less certain. One source claims it derives from a French word meaning "cave," and Oklahoma historian Muriel H. Wright wrote that "cavanol" is a corruption of the French word for "cavernous." In the late 1700s, French fur trappers set up a camp at the base of the hill and called it Poteau, meaning "post," a name the town below still carries. In 1819 Thomas Nuttall came to study the plant life here, and he learned about other natural wonders of the region from the French trappers and the Native peoples who lived nearby.

## Springs, Coal and a Railroad Grade

During territorial times, Cavanal Hill sat on the border between Skullyville County and Sugar Loaf County, two of the counties that made up the Moshulatubbee District of the Choctaw Nation. In the late 1800s Walter Beard opened a health resort at the summit, a modest wood-frame building. Visitors came for the view and for the natural springs that occur throughout the region. Industry followed. Halfway up the hill, George Witte founded Witteville, a coal mining company town whose mines lay three miles to the east. Witteville still appears on some maps, at the "Y" in the road leading up to the top, and much of that road follows the bed of the original railroad that once served the town. Around 1960 the hill drew a former governor. Robert S. Kerr, a businessman who had founded a petroleum company, served as the state's 12th governor from 1943 to 1947 and was elected three times to the United States Senate, and Senator Kerr established a summer residence at the foot of Cavanal Hill.

## The Killer Five Miles

Today the hill is a place to test legs and lungs. It hosts mountain bike races and the Poteau Chamber of Commerce's annual Cavanal Killer 8K, a one-way run of about five miles up to the summit, whose name suggests how the grade feels on foot. A 4.5-mile blacktopped road climbs to the summit, where visitors look out over the Poteau River Valley. On a clear day, reportedly, the view reaches all the way to Mount Magazine in Arkansas, officially Magazine Mountain, the highest point in that state, where Signal Hill tops out at 2,753 feet. Whether you call it the world's highest hill or simply a mountain that knows how to market itself, the summit rewards the climb, and the landmark that once guided French explorers through this country now guides runners, riders and sightseers up its flanks.

## Flight Context

Cavanal Hill rises near Poteau in Le Flore County, eastern Oklahoma, at approximately 35.071°N, 94.679°W, with a summit elevation of 2,385 feet MSL. The field at Poteau sits far lower, so the hill stands out clearly; stay well above the summit, at 3,500 feet MSL or higher, and watch for towers or antennas on high ground. Robert S. Kerr Airport (KRKR), at 451 feet elevation, is about 4 nm southeast, and Fort Smith Regional Airport (KFSM) about 22 nm northeast in Arkansas. On clear days, Mount Magazine in Arkansas may be visible to the east.
