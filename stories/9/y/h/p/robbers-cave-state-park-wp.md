# Robbers Cave State Park

In the sandstone hills of southeast Oklahoma, a cave with an outlaw reputation became a Boy Scout camp, a Civilian Conservation Corps showpiece, and the setting of one of social psychology's best-known experiments.

**Location:** 35.0028, -95.3414

**Categories:** state-parks, oklahoma, outlaws, civilian-conservation-corps, psychology, outdoor-recreation

## Introduction

The park's name promises outlaws, and local legend supplies them: Belle Starr, Jesse James and a string of fugitives who are said to have hidden among these sandstone bluffs. But the rival gangs that put Robbers Cave into psychology textbooks were eleven- and twelve-year-old boys who called themselves the Eagles and the Rattlers. Both stories belong to the same wooded hills, five miles north of Wilburton in Latimer County, Oklahoma, where a state park and its adjoining wildlife management area now cover more than 8,000 acres.

## Hills Without Wood

The park sits in the Sans Bois Mountains, whose French name means "without wood," an odd label for hills now known for their woodlands. People hunted here for hundreds of years before any park existed. Archaeological evidence suggests the earliest inhabitants were related to the builders of the Spiro Mounds, and by the 1600s the Osage and Caddo dominated the area. French hunters and explorers passed through as well, naming prominent landmarks with names still in use. In 1850 the land around the cave was assigned to the newly established Sans Bois County, part of the Moshulatubbee District of the Choctaw Nation.

## A Hideout With a Reputation

After the Civil War, this rough country became legendary as a refuge for people running from the law. The cave reportedly sheltered Civil War deserters and outlaws, with the Younger brothers, the Dalton Gang, the Rufus Buck Gang and Belle Starr among the names attached to it, and Jesse James is often added to the list. How many of them actually crouched in this particular cave is the kind of question legends are built to resist. The park has embraced the story anyway. Its lodge is named the Belle Starr View Lodge, and one trail northwest of the cave area loops around Lost Lake.

## Scouts and Stonemasons

The park's modern history began with a gift. In 1929 Carlton Weaver, a Wilburton newspaper editor and state representative, donated 120 acres around the cave to the Boy Scouts of America for a camp, first called Camp Tom Hale, and he also provided land for a large state game preserve. In 1933 Civilian Conservation Corps Company 1825 set up at the preserve, and between 1935 and 1941 its workers built a bathhouse, cabins, trails and roads from native stone. In 1937 the CCC and the Works Progress Administration created Lake Carlton, named for Weaver. Originally known as Latimer State Park, the park took the name Robbers Cave in 1936, and it was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2002.

## The Eagles and the Rattlers

In the summer of 1954, twenty-two boys arrived at the Boy Scout camp inside the park believing they had come for an ordinary summer camp. They were part of a study led by psychologist Muzafer Sherif. The boys came in two groups on different days, lived in cabins at opposite ends of the camp, and spent the first week unaware of each other. Each group formed its own identity, with a name and a flag. Then the researchers staged a tournament between them, and friction grew as the contest went on: taunts, insults and raids on the rival cabin. The Robbers Cave study became a landmark in research on realistic conflict theory, with the Sherifs concluding that shared goals which required both groups to cooperate did far more to ease hostility than simple contact.

## Cliffs, Trout and Autumn Color

Today Robbers Cave is a favorite of rappellers, hikers, equestrians and anyone who likes the outdoors. Sandstone cliffs draw climbers, horses can be rented for miles of trails, and three lakes, including Lake Carlton, Lake Wayne Wallace and Coon Creek Lake, offer boating and seasonal trout fishing. Hunting and mountain biking fill out the options, and a nature center runs naturalist programs. Visitors can stay in cabins, group camps, campsites or the lodge. In autumn the woodlands of the Sans Bois turn color, and fall foliage viewing becomes one of the park's chief pleasures.

## Flight Context

Robbers Cave State Park lies at approximately 35.00°N, 95.34°W in the Sans Bois Mountains of Latimer County, about five miles north of Wilburton along Oklahoma State Highway 2. From 3,000-4,500 feet AGL, look for the park's small lakes set among forested ridges and sandstone bluffs north of town. McAlester Regional Airport (KMLC) is about 23 nm to the west-southwest, and Robert S. Kerr Airport (KRKR) at Poteau lies roughly 35 nm to the east. The terrain is hilly and forested, so keep a safe margin over the ridges. Autumn brings the best color; spring can bring strong thunderstorms.
