# Stafford Air & Space Museum

On the airfield of his Oklahoma hometown, a Smithsonian-affiliated museum honors astronaut Thomas P. Stafford with the Gemini capsule he flew, the suit he wore around the Moon, and a docking ring from the mission where he shook hands with a Soviet cosmonaut.

**Location:** 35.5444, -98.6700

**Categories:** Aerospace museums in Oklahoma, Museums in Custer County, Oklahoma, Human spaceflight, Thomas P. Stafford, Smithsonian Affiliates

## Introduction

In 1975, high above the Earth, an American astronaut reached through a hatch and shook hands with a Soviet cosmonaut. The astronaut was Thomas P. Stafford, commanding the American capsule of the Apollo-Soyuz Test Project, and the man on the other side was Alexei Leonov. A docking ring from that program, the same kind of hardware he reached through, now sits in a museum on the airfield of Weatherford, Oklahoma, the small western Oklahoma city where Stafford was born. Around it, the Stafford Air & Space Museum has gathered spacecraft, rockets and aircraft that trace the arc of flight from the Wright brothers to the space station era, and since 2011 it has been a Smithsonian Affiliate.

## Weatherford's Astronaut

Stafford is one of only 24 people who have traveled to the Moon. A graduate of the U.S. Naval Academy who rose to the rank of lieutenant general, he flew four space missions. He piloted Gemini 6A in 1965 and commanded Gemini 9A in 1966. In 1969 he flew Apollo 10, the dress rehearsal that took a crew into orbit around the Moon ahead of the first landing. Then, in 1975, he commanded the American side of Apollo-Soyuz, the first linkup between U.S. and Soviet spacecraft. He received the Congressional Space Medal of Honor. Stafford died in March 2024 at the age of 93, but the museum in his hometown keeps his story close to the runway where it stands.

## Two Rooms to Four Buildings

The museum began modestly, opening in 1993 in just two rooms at the Weatherford airport, now named Thomas P. Stafford Airport. By 2006 it had grown to four buildings. It bought a Space Shuttle simulator in 2016, and in 2017 it began raising money for an 18,000-square-foot expansion. Some of its most important pieces arrived along the way. By March 2018 it had acquired a Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II, the ground-attack jet nicknamed the "Warthog," which now sits outside. That July, the Gemini 6A spacecraft that Stafford himself had piloted in 1965 was moved to the museum.

## A Rocket Across the Room

Inside, the scale can be startling. The museum has one of the few Titan II launch vehicles on display anywhere, its huge body stretching across the back of a gallery from one side of the building to the other. The collection also includes a Mark 6 re-entry vehicle, a Moon rock, and artifacts from the Space Shuttle program and the Mir space station, and a one-fifteenth-scale model of the Hubble Space Telescope. Items from the Smithsonian include a Gemini flight suit, space food, and survival gear that flew to the Moon on Apollo 11. The pressure suit Stafford wore on Apollo 10 is here, too, along with a TP-82 cosmonaut survival pistol that Leonov gave to Stafford, a gift passed across the Cold War divide.

## From the Wright Flyer to the Simulator

The aviation side of the museum is just as broad. More than a dozen historic aircraft are on display, among them a Russian MiG-21R and an F-16, alongside full-size replicas of the Wright Flyer, the Spirit of St. Louis, an Apollo Command Module and a Gemini spacecraft. For younger visitors, the Rose & Tom Luczo Educational Center offers a flight simulation computer lab, a kids' library and a Talon A3 motion-based flight simulator that runs X-Plane 11 and Microsoft Flight Simulator X. It is a fitting final stop in a museum built around a man who went from a small Oklahoma town to lunar orbit.

## Flight Context

The museum sits at 35.5444 N, 98.67 W on Thomas P. Stafford Airport (KOJA) at Weatherford, Oklahoma, just north of Interstate 40 in Custer County. From 1,500-3,000 feet AGL, the museum buildings and the A-10 displayed outside are near the airport's facilities, and Interstate 40 running east-west just south of the field is the best reference line. KOJA is the obvious airport for a visit. Western Oklahoma is known for strong, gusty winds, and spring thunderstorms can develop quickly.
