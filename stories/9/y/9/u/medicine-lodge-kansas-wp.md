# Medicine Lodge, Kansas

Named for a Kiowa sun dance lodge and remembered for the 1867 peace treaties signed nearby, the Red Hills town of Medicine Lodge is also where Carrie Nation began her crusade against the saloon.

**Location:** 37.2844, -98.5811

**Categories:** Cities in Barber County, Kansas, County seats in Kansas, Medicine Lodge Treaty, Temperance movement, Kiowa history

## Introduction

In the summer of 1866, Kiowa people raised an arbor of tree trunks and leafy branches in a river valley in what is now south-central Kansas, a medicine lodge for the celebration of their annual sun dance. The lodge stood several miles downstream of today's town, but it gave its name to the river, and the river gave its name to Medicine Lodge. Since then this small Red Hills city has hosted a major treaty council, raised a frontier stockade, and sent a hymn-singing temperance crusader named Carrie Nation out into the world.

## Timber-Hill River

In their own language, the Kiowa called the stream A-ya-dalda P'a, meaning "Timber-hill River." They considered the site sacred because of the high content of Epsom salts in its water. The town that took the river's English name sits in the Red Hills region of the Great Plains, immediately north of the point where Elm Creek joins the Medicine Lodge River. Elm Creek curls south around the eastern and southern sides of the city, and a dammed tributary just north of town forms Barber County State Lake. It can be a hot country. The highest temperature ever recorded in Medicine Lodge was 118 degrees Fahrenheit, on August 12, 1936.

## Council by the River

In October 1867, representatives of the United States met the Kiowa, Comanche, Plains Apache, Southern Cheyenne and Southern Arapaho in this valley and signed a set of three agreements known together as the Medicine Lodge Treaty. The Peace Council camp lay about three miles upstream of the future town, on the same side of the river. The town has never let that moment go. Medicine Lodge first staged a pageant commemorating the treaty in 1927, presented it every three years from 1961 to 2006, and has staged it every five years since. A Peace Treaty Statue stands on North Main Street, and a Kansas historical marker for the Medicine Lodge Peace Treaties sits about a mile east of town.

## Behind the Stockade

Settlers led by John Hutchinson founded Medicine Lodge in February 1873, just north of the confluence of Elm Creek and the river. The community grew quickly, adding a hotel, stores and a post office within a year, and it was incorporated as a city in 1879. A reproduction of the 1873 stockade is open to the public today. Along North Main Street, the Wisner Fountain recalls the era of horses, when it served to water them. The city remains the seat of Barber County, home to 1,781 people at the 2020 census, and it still sits at a crossroads: U.S. Routes 160 and 281 meet in its southwest corner.

## Hymns in the Saloon

Carrie Nation moved to Medicine Lodge around 1890. There she threw herself into church and civic life, spoke out against alcohol and tobacco, organized a local chapter of the Woman's Christian Temperance Union and campaigned to enforce Kansas's liquor laws. Kansas had outlawed liquor in 1880, yet saloons persisted. In 1899, Nation and fellow temperance worker Mrs. Wesley Cain closed the first of the town's saloons by praying and singing hymns inside it. By the Kansas Historical Society's account, she and the local temperance union closed all seven of the town's illegal liquor outlets without violence. She went on to become famous for smashing Kansas saloons with a hatchet, but the crusade started here, with a song. Her former home, the Carrie Nation House, is a National Historic Landmark and stands alongside the stockade as one of the town's attractions.

## Hometown Voices

Medicine Lodge's list of notable people is surprisingly long for a town its size. It includes Dorothy DeLay, the violin instructor who taught at Juilliard, Sarah Lawrence College and the University of Cincinnati, along with Chester Long, who served Kansas as both a U.S. representative and a U.S. senator, the journalist and historian Carleton Beals, All-American basketball player B. H. Born, and Roman Catholic Archbishop Edward Hunkeler. From a Kiowa sun dance arbor to a treaty council, a stockade and a hymn sung in a saloon, this corner of the Red Hills has a way of turning ordinary ground into a stage.

## Flight Context

Medicine Lodge lies at approximately 37.28°N, 98.58°W in Barber County, Kansas, amid the Red Hills (also called the Gypsum Hills) of south-central Kansas. From 2,000-3,500 feet AGL, look for the town at the junction of U.S. Routes 160 and 281, just north of where Elm Creek meets the Medicine Lodge River, with Barber County State Lake immediately north of town. Medicine Lodge Airport (FAA identifier K51) lies just southeast of the city, and Pratt Regional Airport (KPTT) is about 30 miles to the north-northwest. Expect gusty winds and spring and summer thunderstorms.
