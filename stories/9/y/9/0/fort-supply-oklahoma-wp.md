# Fort Supply (Oklahoma)

Founded in 1868 as a depot for a winter war on the Southern Plains, Fort Supply went on to guard reservation lands, house Oklahoma's first state asylum and become a historic site, all on the same patch of the old Cherokee Outlet.

**Location:** 36.5764, -99.5600

**Categories:** Indian Territory, Forts on the National Register of Historic Places in Oklahoma, Military and war museums in Oklahoma, Oklahoma Historical Society, Cheyenne history

## Introduction

The grounds just east of the town of Fort Supply, in northwestern Oklahoma, have been many things: a depot for a winter war, a guardian of reservation lands, the state's first state-run asylum, a prison site and a historic site. It began on November 18, 1868, as the "Camp of Supply," set up in what was then the Cherokee Outlet of Indian Territory to support General Philip Sheridan's winter campaign against the tribes of the Southern Plains. Its name described its purpose exactly. Within days of its founding, it became the starting point for Custer's attack on the Washita.

## Ride to the Washita

From Camp Supply, George Armstrong Custer led the Seventh U.S. Cavalry south to the Washita River to destroy the village of the Cheyenne chief Black Kettle. At dawn on November 27, 1868, the troopers attacked the sleeping camp. Black Kettle was a peace chief and a survivor of the 1864 Sand Creek Massacre, and that month he had asked U.S. authorities to let his people move to safety near Fort Cobb, a request that was denied. He was killed in the attack, and women, children and elderly people were among the dead. It became known as the Battle of the Washita, though many accounts since have called it a massacre.

## Holding the Line

The post's role shifted in the years that followed. Camp Supply came to protect the Cheyenne and Arapaho reservations, under the Darlington Agency, from incursions by whites, and after its part in the Red River War of 1874 to 1875 it was renamed Fort Supply in 1878. By 1880 the Indian Wars on the Southern Plains were nearly over and the fort was in bad repair, and Army officers in the Department of the Missouri recommended abandoning it. Sheridan, who would later become General of the Army, objected and worked to establish the Fort Supply Military Reservation, giving the post permanence and a 36-square-mile reserve. One of its final missions came in the summer of 1894, when troops from Fort Supply were sent into the Enid-Pond Creek Railroad War, in which residents of both towns had been wrecking trains, tearing up track and demolishing trestles. Troops from Fort Reno and U.S. Marshal E. D. Dix had been unable to stop it, and the dispute was finally settled by an act of Congress signed that August.

## A Post of Many Lives

Fort Supply officially closed in September 1894 after the Cherokee Outlet opened to settlement, and on February 26, 1895, the last troops handed the old post to the Department of the Interior. In 1908, Oklahoma's first state-run institution for the mentally ill, an "insane asylum" in the language of the time, opened on the grounds; it is now the Western State Psychiatric Center. The Fort Supply Historic District was placed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1971, and in 1988 the legislature designated the remaining buildings a historic district, with the William S. Key Correctional Center opening at the site shortly afterward; the prison closed in 2021. Today the Oklahoma Historical Society operates the Fort Supply Historic Site, which preserves five original structures: picket-style log quarters from 1875 and 1882, a frame Commanding Officer's Quarters from 1878, a duplex officers' quarters from 1882 and a brick guard house from 1892, alongside a replica of the stockade. Because the prison closed, the society lists the site as temporarily closed until further notice, with plans to reopen it in partnership with the Friends of Historic Fort Supply, so check with the society before planning a visit.

## Flight Context

The Fort Supply historic site lies at approximately 36.58°N, 99.56°W, just east of the town of Fort Supply in Woodward County, Oklahoma. From 1,500-2,500 feet AGL, look for the cluster of old post and institutional buildings beside the small town, with Fort Supply Lake, a reservoir on Wolf Creek, about a mile south of town. West Woodward Airport (KWWR) is roughly 10 miles to the south. Expect gusty winds and spring and summer thunderstorms typical of northwestern Oklahoma.
