# Langston University

Founded in 1897 in an all-Black prairie town, Langston University is Oklahoma's only historically Black college and the westernmost four-year public HBCU in the country.

**Location:** 35.9447, -97.2614

**Categories:** Historically black universities and colleges in the United States, Land-grant universities and colleges, African-American history of Oklahoma, Public universities and colleges in Oklahoma, Education in Logan County, Oklahoma

## Introduction

In 1948 the state of Oklahoma opened an entire law school to keep one woman out of another. Ada Lois Sipuel Fisher, a graduate of Langston University, had been refused admission to the University of Oklahoma's law school because she was Black. She took her case to the U.S. Supreme Court and won. Oklahoma responded by creating a law school at Langston, her alma mater. Fisher went back to court, arguing that the new school was not equal to the one at OU, and in 1949 she was admitted to OU's law school. The episode captures the purpose Langston University was created to serve, and the history it has had to outgrow.

## A Town Built on a Promise

The town came first. On April 22, 1890, Edward P. McCabe, a Black political figure from Kansas, founded Langston as an all-Black town. It was one of about fifty identifiable Black towns and settlements created in Oklahoma between 1865 and 1920. McCabe named it for John Mercer Langston, a Black congressman from Virginia. That same year Congress passed the second Morrill Act, which required states with land-grant colleges, including what was then Oklahoma A&M, to either admit African American students or provide a separate school for them in order to receive federal funds. In 1897 the territorial legislature created the Colored Agricultural and Normal University at Langston. Inman E. Page led it from 1898 to 1916. The school was a product of segregation, yet it also became a place where Black Oklahomans could pursue a college education.

## The Name on the Gate

Everyone always called the school "Langston," but the name did not become official until 1941. That year the university was renamed for John Mercer Langston, who lived from 1829 to 1897. He was a civil rights pioneer and the first African American member of Congress from Virginia. He also founded the law school at Howard University and served as the American consul-general to Haiti. The school attracted notable teachers as well. The poet Melvin B. Tolson taught at Langston from 1947 to 1964, and Denzel Washington later played him in the film The Great Debaters. Langston's ties to that wider tradition continue today, since the university is a member school of the Thurgood Marshall College Fund. The town that gave the school its home also endures. It had 1,619 residents at the 2020 census.

## Rural Roots, Urban Reach

The main campus still sits in the countryside, about 10 miles east of Guthrie. Langston is Oklahoma's only historically Black college and the westernmost four-year public HBCU in the United States. It also has urban centers in Oklahoma City and in Tulsa, where it shares a campus with OSU-Tulsa. Its six schools offer 30 undergraduate and six graduate degree programs. Langston's Doctor of Physical Therapy program, accredited in 2005, is its only doctoral program. The top students can join the Edwin P. McCabe Honors Program, and Langston has the lowest total cost per credit hour of any four-year university in Oklahoma. In 2021 the university used COVID-19 relief money to forgive $4.65 million in student debt. A 17,000-square-foot allied health building opened on the Tulsa campus in 2023. In March 2025 the Carnegie classification named Langston a Research College and University, and that same month Ruth Ray Jackson was formally invested as its 17th president.

## The Marching Pride

On game days, one of Langston's biggest ambassadors is its band. The Langston University Marching Pride backs the Lions, who compete in the NAIA's Sooner Athletic Conference and once belonged to the Southwestern Athletic Conference, from 1931 to 1957. The band also trains students who want careers in music. Charlie Wilson of The Gap Band once served as its drum major. The Marching Pride has performed for the Denver Nuggets, the Oklahoma City Thunder and the Kansas City Chiefs, and it has played in the Georgia Dome, AT&T Stadium and St. Louis's Edward Jones Dome. It has taken part in four Honda Battle of the Bands events, often in front of crowds of more than 40,000. Members often receive an out-of-state fee waiver, along with additional scholarships based on their auditions.

## Flight Context

Langston University's main campus is at about 35.9447°N, 97.2614°W, in the small town of Langston roughly 10 miles east of Guthrie. The campus sits at the edge of the small town, surrounded by open country. Fly at about 2,500–3,000 ft AGL and use Guthrie to the west as your reference. Guthrie–Edmond Regional Airport (KGOK) is about 9 nm southwest, and Stillwater Regional Airport (KSWO) is about 16 nm to the northeast. Oklahoma City's busier airspace lies to the south. Visibility is usually good, but spring and summer can bring strong thunderstorms.
