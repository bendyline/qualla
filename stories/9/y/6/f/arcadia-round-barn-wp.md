# Arcadia Round Barn

Built in 1898 from green bur oak bent into curves, the Arcadia Round Barn hosted dances in its loft, watched Route 66 arrive and fade, lost its roof in 1988, and was rebuilt by a crew of retirees who called themselves the Over-the-Hill Gang.

## Introduction

The roof did not crash so much as give up. At 12:09 p.m. on June 29, 1988, after decades of decay, the great domed roof of the Arcadia Round Barn, in the words the barn's own history preserves, "just kind of sighed and fell in, like a soufflé." Ninety years after a farmer named William Harrison Odor raised it beside what would become Route 66, the landmark barn of Arcadia, Oklahoma, stood open to the sky. It might have ended there. Instead, a group of retirees decided that it would not.

## Bending the Oak

Odor built the barn in 1898, and the challenge he set himself was geometry. A round barn needs curved walls and curved roof rafters, and straight lumber does not curve on its own. His solution was to use native bur oak while it was still green. The boards were soaked in the river and then dried in curved forms, bending them into the shapes the walls and rafters required. The finished barn measures 60 feet across and stands 43 feet tall, its rounded roof rising like a dome above the Oklahoma countryside. It was a practical farm building, but it was also a feat of patient craftsmanship, the kind of structure that invites people to stop and wonder how it was done. More than a century later, visitors still do exactly that, studying its architectural and engineering details.

## A Dance Floor in the Loft

The barn's second life began almost immediately. While building it, Odor's workers realized that the loft would make an ideal place for a dance. They offered to pay the difference in cost if they could hold three dances up there, and the upper level became a community gathering place. It is easy to picture the scene: a rural Oklahoma town in the 1890s and after, with neighbors climbing into a round loft beneath a curving oak roof for music and company. A barn built to shelter animals and hay had become one of the social centers of Arcadia. That dual purpose, working barn below and gathering place above, helps explain why the town would one day fight to save it.

## Life Beside the Mother Road

Arcadia grew and prospered after the railroad arrived. Then, in the 1920s, the newly commissioned U.S. Route 66 was aligned straight through town, passing right beside the Round Barn and putting it in front of a steady stream of travelers crossing the country. For a few decades the barn and the highway grew up together. The good times did not last. When the Interstate arrived and traffic drained away from Route 66, Arcadia and its barn declined along with the old road. The structure's significance was still recognized: by 1977 the Arcadia Round Barn had been placed on the National Register of Historic Places. But recognition could not stop decay, and eleven years later the roof gave way.

## The Over-the-Hill Gang

After the collapse, Luther Robison stepped forward to lead a team of volunteers in rebuilding the barn. Most of them were retirees like Robison himself, and they gave themselves a fitting name: the Over-the-Hill Gang. For several years they worked to restore the structure, and on April 4, 1992, the rebuilt Arcadia Round Barn was officially dedicated. Today it is a landmark and tourist attraction on historic Route 66, managed by volunteers, and travelers pull off the road to admire the curves that William Odor first coaxed out of wet oak in 1898. The barn that sighed and fell in is standing again, proof that a community determined to keep its landmark can bring it back from the ground up.

## Flight Context

The Arcadia Round Barn stands at approximately 35.6622° N, 97.3258° W in the small town of Arcadia, Oklahoma County, beside historic U.S. Route 66 northeast of Edmond and Oklahoma City. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet above ground level, look for the old highway running east-west through town and the barn's distinctive round, domed roof beside it; Arcadia Lake lies a short distance to the west and makes a helpful reference.
