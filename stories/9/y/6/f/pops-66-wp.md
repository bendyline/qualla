# Pops 66

A 66-foot soda bottle, a 100-foot cantilever and walls lined with hundreds of kinds of soda make Pops, opened in 2007 in Arcadia, Oklahoma, a modern roadside attraction built for the old romance of Route 66.

**Location:** 35.6586, -97.3351

## Introduction

The bottle is 66 feet tall, and that is no accident. The giant soda bottle that towers over Pops in Arcadia, Oklahoma, takes its height from the highway it stands beside, historic U.S. Route 66. It weighs four tons, and although it looks as if it were built from neon tubes, it is actually lit by LEDs that put on a light show every night. Pops opened in 2007, long after the old highway's heyday, but it was built in the spirit of the classic roadside attractions that once lured travelers off the road, part restaurant, part gas station and part monument to soda pop.

## A Rainbow in the Windows

Inside, the theme carries through to the walls themselves. The restaurant's glass walls are lined with shelves holding hundreds of different kinds of soda pop, arranged by the color of the drink, so that the windows glow in bands of color. The architects describe it as a rainbow of pop from around the world, some 12,000 bottles in all. Many of the sodas are rare or otherwise hard to find. This is not just decoration. Many of the bottles are for sale, and visitors can pull their choices cold from the huge refrigerator at the western end of the building, turning a quick stop on Route 66 into a tasting expedition.

## Steel Over the Pumps

The building makes a statement before anyone steps inside. Its structure incorporates a cantilevered truss that extends 100 feet out over the gas pumps and parking area in the forecourt, sheltering travelers as they fill their tanks. The architects designed it to recall the great steel bridges of the old road, a modern echo of the steel trusses that carried the old highway. Pops was designed by Elliott + Associates Architects, the firm of Oklahoma City architect Rand Elliott, and the building has collected several architectural awards, including American Institute of Architects Central States Region Honor Awards in 2008 and 2010. The architects also report that in 2016 the design publication DesignCurial ranked it first on its list of the world's ten best filling stations. For a gas station, that is rare company indeed.

## Named for a Father

Pops was owned by the Oklahoma oil and gas magnate Aubrey McClendon. According to its architects, the name honors the client's father, an Oklahoma City oil company executive, which lends a personal note to a place that feels designed for crowds. The idea proved popular enough to travel. In 2011, two Pops gift shops opened in Will Rogers World Airport in Oklahoma City. In 2016, another Pops opened in Nichols Hills, Oklahoma, borrowing the original's idea of glass walls lined with color-sorted bottles in a more modern design. There, the bottles were sold only from the refrigerator, and the shop added a wide variety of candy and sweets. The Nichols Hills location closed in December 2018, but the original on Route 66 carried on.

## Old Road, New Landmark

Pops sits just down the road from the Arcadia Round Barn, built in 1898, and the two make an unlikely pair. One is a monument to patient frontier craftsmanship, bent from green oak by a farmer's hand. The other is a glowing, steel-and-glass piece of contemporary architecture designed to catch the eye of drivers at highway speed. Yet both belong to the same story. Route 66 made Arcadia a place people passed through, and each generation has found its own way to make them stop. When the sun goes down and the giant bottle begins its nightly show of shifting light, Pops does exactly what roadside attractions along the Mother Road have always tried to do: it gives travelers a reason to pull over and remember where they were.

## Flight Context

Pops lies at approximately 35.6586° N, 97.3351° W on historic U.S. Route 66 in Arcadia, Oklahoma County, northeast of Edmond and Oklahoma City, and less than a mile west-southwest of the Arcadia Round Barn. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet above ground level, look for the long cantilevered canopy over the fuel pumps and the tall bottle-shaped sign beside the old highway; after dark the LED-lit bottle is a vivid beacon. Arcadia Lake lies a short distance to the west. Guthrie-Edmond Regional Airport (KGOK) is roughly 13 miles to the north-northwest, and Wiley Post Airport (KPWA) about 19 miles to the west-southwest. Central Oklahoma sees strong spring thunderstorms and gusty winds.
