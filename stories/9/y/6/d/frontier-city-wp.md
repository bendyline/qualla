# Frontier City

Built from the props of Oklahoma's 1957 semicentennial, the western-themed Frontier City survived a developer's plan to bulldoze it, and its owners went on to buy the Six Flags chain.

**Location:** 35.5848, -97.4410

**Categories:** Amusement parks in Oklahoma, Western (genre) theme parks, Tourist attractions in Oklahoma City, Amusement parks opened in 1958, Route 66

## Introduction

Frontier City does not open with a pair of scissors. Instead of the usual ribbon cutting, the park aims an old-fashioned six-shooter at a rope stretched across its stockade entrance, a ritual planned for its first opening in 1958 and still used today. The western-themed amusement park sits on the north side of Oklahoma City, along the old Route 66 corridor that is now Interstate 35. It was built from leftover props of the state's 50th birthday celebration, it nearly became a real estate development, and its owners went on to buy Six Flags.

## Boomtown, Made Permanent

The park began with a Hollywood publicist who thought his hometown was the natural place for a western-themed park. James Burge had spent twenty years promoting clients such as Joan Crawford and Robert Taylor, and in 1955 he visited Disneyland in its opening year and came away impressed by the theme park business. In Oklahoma City he was appointed to lead the committee planning the 1957 Oklahoma Semi-Centennial Exposition. One of its exhibits was Boomtown, a replica pioneer town at the state fairgrounds. When the exposition ended, Burge negotiated with the fair board to buy many of Boomtown's buildings and props. He laid out the park with four initial investors and then brought in Jack Williams, a wealthy businessman who owned a chain of laundries. Williams was first interested in the park's linen contract, but Burge's vision won him over, and he became the principal investor.

## Four Blocks of the Old West

Frontier City opened in 1958 on four square blocks of frontier streets. Visitors found a marshal's office, a saloon, a bank, a fire department, a hotel and a post office with its own postmark, along with rows of storefronts. There was a haunted farm and a mine train, and staged robberies and jails helped bring the Old West to life. Admission was free, but the gunfight shows cost a quarter. During the 1960s and 1970s the park added spinning rides, roller coasters and a log flume, and the frontier town slowly became a full amusement park. Some of its rides had lives elsewhere first. The Silver Bullet coaster came from Jolly Roger Amusement Park in 1986, and the Wildcat arrived from Fairyland Park in Kansas City in 1990 or 1991.

## The Developer Who Kept a Park

In the fall of 1981, the Tierco Group, a local real estate company, bought Frontier City intending to dismantle it and develop the land. Then an oil crunch cooled the local real estate boom, and the company found itself running a sagging amusement park. Its president realized that Oklahoma City needed a local park, but he also knew that simply spending a few million dollars would not fix its problems. In 1983 the owners hired a management company, and Gary Story became general manager in 1984. From there the story took a remarkable turn. Tierco renamed itself Premier Parks in 1995. On February 9, 1998, Premier Parks announced it would buy the Six Flags chain from Time Warner for $1.9 billion and take the Six Flags name. Until 2006 the world headquarters of Six Flags stood at the southeast corner of Frontier City's property.

## Many Owners, One Six-Shooter

When Six Flags put the park up for sale in January 2006 and announced it was moving its offices to New York City and Grand Prairie, Texas, many Oklahomans thought Frontier City itself was being shut down and moved to New York. It stayed put. In 2007 PARC Management bought the park, which was then sold to CNL Income Properties and leased back to PARC. In 2008 the park marked its fiftieth anniversary with a suspended coaster called Steel Lasso. After PARC defaulted on its obligations in 2010, former Six Flags executives Kieran Burke and Gary Story began managing the park under the name Premier Parks, LLC. They added a water play area, the Winged Warrior, the seven-story looping Brain Drain and the Gunslinger, and in 2016 they re-tracked much of the Wildcat. EPR Properties bought the park after the 2016 season, and in 2018 Six Flags returned to operate it. When the COVID-19 pandemic shut every Six Flags property in 2020, Frontier City reopened on June 5, reported as the company's first park to do so.

## Flight Context

Frontier City lies along Interstate 35 on the northeast side of Oklahoma City, at about 35.5848°N, 97.4410°W. From 2,500–3,500 ft AGL, follow I-35 north from downtown and look for the tangle of coaster track and the Western-town rooftops beside the highway. Wiley Post Airport (KPWA) is about 10 nm to the west-southwest. Tinker Air Force Base (KTIK) is about 11 nm south-southeast, and Will Rogers World Airport (KOKC) about 14 nm southwest. This is busy airspace, so get ATC clearance where required. Summer also brings afternoon thunderstorms to central Oklahoma.
