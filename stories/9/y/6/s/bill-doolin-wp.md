# Bill Doolin

An Arkansas cowboy turned leader of the Wild Bunch, Bill Doolin robbed banks and trains across the Plains, killed a lawman at Ingalls, and was finally brought down by Heck Thomas; he now lies in Guthrie's Boot Hill.

**Location:** 35.8958, -97.4033

**Categories:** Outlaws of the American Old West, Dalton Gang, American bank robbers, Guthrie, Oklahoma, Gunslingers of the American Old West

## Introduction

Bill Doolin's foot never healed properly. He had been shot in it during a shootout after a train robbery near Cimarron, Kansas, and rheumatism later set in. So in late 1895 the leader of the Wild Bunch, who was hiding out with his wife near Burden, Kansas, crossed into Arkansas and went to the resort town of Eureka Springs. There he soaked his foot in the sulfur springs of its bathhouses. It was in one of those bathhouses, in early 1896, that Deputy U.S. Marshal Bill Tilghman caught up with him. By the end of that summer Doolin was dead. Today he lies in the Boot Hill section of Summit View Cemetery in Guthrie, Oklahoma.

## Cowboy Days

William Doolin was born in 1858 in Johnson County, Arkansas, the son of Michael Doolin and Artemina Beller. In 1881 he left home to work as a cowboy in Indian Territory for Oscar Halsell, a Texas-born cattleman. Many of the men he worked with in those years became outlaws too, among them George "Bitter Creek" Newcomb, Charley Pierce, Bill Power, Dick Broadwell, Bill "Tulsa Jack" Blake, Dan "Dynamite Dick" Clifton, "Little Bill" Raidler and Emmett Dalton. Doolin's first run-in with the law came on July 4, 1891, in Coffeyville, Kansas. He and some friends were drinking in public, and when lawmen tried to take their liquor away, a gunfight broke out. Two of the officers were wounded, and Doolin got away.

## The Sixth Man?

Soon after, Doolin joined the Dalton Gang. On October 5, 1892, the Daltons tried to rob two Coffeyville banks at the same time. The raid was a disaster. Townspeople and lawmen fought back, four of the five gang members were killed, and Emmett Dalton was captured, tried and sent to prison. Historians have speculated that a sixth man waited in an alley holding the horses and escaped. He has never been identified, and some believe he was Bill Doolin. By late 1892 Doolin had formed his own gang, the Wild Bunch, sometimes called the Doolin–Dalton Gang. On November 1, 1892, it robbed a bank in Spearville, Kansas, and a string of bank and train robberies followed. In March 1893 Doolin married Edith Ellsworth in the town of Ingalls. Not long afterward the gang robbed a train near Cimarron, Kansas, and in the shootout with lawmen that followed, Doolin was seriously wounded in the foot.

## Gunfire at Ingalls

Ingalls had become a refuge for the gang, and on September 1, 1893, fourteen deputy U.S. marshals rode in to capture them. Three of the lawmen died as a result of the battle that followed, among them Special Deputy Marshal Richard Speed, whom Doolin shot and killed. Deputy Marshals Thomas Hueston and Lafayette Shadley were also mortally wounded. An Ingalls bystander, Young Simmons, was fatally hit by a stray round as he tried to take cover in a saloon. Most of the gang escaped, Doolin among them. Eventually the gang was wiped out, a few by capture but most killed by deputy marshals, and most were brought down by the Three Guardsmen: Heck Thomas, Bill Tilghman and Chris Madsen.

## The Bathhouse and the Shotgun

After Tilghman arrested him in the Eureka Springs bathhouse, Doolin did not stay in custody long. He escaped from jail on July 5, 1896, and went to his wife at Lawson in Oklahoma Territory. Heck Thomas tracked him there. On August 25, in a confrontation with Thomas and his posse, Doolin was killed by a shotgun blast. He was about 38. He was buried in Guthrie, in the Boot Hill section of Summit View Cemetery. His neighbor there is Elmer McCurdy, another outlaw with a strange story of his own. Doolin's legend kept growing after his death. Randolph Scott played him in The Doolins of Oklahoma in 1949, and Audie Murphy starred as a fictionalized version of him in The Cimarron Kid in 1952. In 1981 Burt Lancaster, then in his mid-sixties, played him as a man in his thirties in Cattle Annie and Little Britches. The Eagles' album Desperado includes songs titled "Doolin-Dalton" and "Bitter Creek."
