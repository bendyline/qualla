# Blessed Stanley Rother Shrine

Beside I-35 in south Oklahoma City, a church modeled on a Guatemalan parish holds the body of the Okarche-born priest who refused to abandon his people and became the first U.S.-born martyr beatified by the Catholic Church.

**Location:** 35.3757, -97.4978

**Categories:** Religious sites, Catholic shrines, Pilgrimage sites, Oklahoma City, Museums

## Introduction

"The shepherd cannot run at the first sign of danger." Stanley Rother wrote those words in December 1980, explaining why he would stay with his parishioners in Guatemala in the face of physical harm. Seven months later he was dead. Today his body rests within the altar of a chapel beside Interstate 35 on the south side of Oklahoma City, at the center of the Blessed Stanley Rother Shrine. Part church, part museum and part pilgrimage site, it drew roughly 120,000 pilgrims and visitors in its first year.

## From Okarche to Atitlán

Rother was born on March 27, 1935, in Okarche, Oklahoma, and was ordained a priest on May 25, 1963. In 1968 he went to Guatemala as a missionary and settled into the parish of Santiago Atitlán. He learned Tz'utujil so he could speak with his parishioners in their own language, then helped translate the New Testament into it. A radio station on the mission property, which he supported, sent out daily lessons in language and mathematics. Drawing on his knowledge of farming, he helped local farmers grow new crops, and he co-founded a small hospital known as the Hospitalito. For thirteen years, this was his life.

## The Shepherd Who Stayed

Violence reached into his parish. Catholic catechists and parishioners disappeared and were later found dead, their bodies bearing signs of beating and torture. In mid-January 1981, friends warned Rother that his name was on a death squad's assassination list, and he reluctantly went home to Oklahoma. He asked Archbishop Charles Salatka for permission to return for Easter, and by April he was back in Santiago Atitlán. Just after midnight on July 28, 1981, three masked men broke into the rectory of Rother's church and shot him. He was 46 years old. His parishioners asked to keep part of him close, and at their request church authorities allowed his heart to be removed and buried behind the altar of his church in Santiago Atitlán. His body was buried in Holy Trinity Cemetery in his hometown of Okarche; in May 2017 it was exhumed for examination and moved to Oklahoma City's Resurrection Memorial Cemetery. In 2016, Pope Francis confirmed that Rother had died a martyr, and on September 23, 2017, he was beatified in Oklahoma City. He is the first U.S.-born priest and martyr to be beatified by the Catholic Church, and the second person beatified on American soil, after Miriam Teresa Demjanovich in 2014.

## From Fairways to Holy Ground

The shrine's 53 acres were once a nine-hole golf course, which the Archdiocese of Oklahoma City bought in 2016. After the beatification, the archdiocese began designing a shrine on the property. Ground was broken on November 3, 2019, and construction truly began in April 2020 with The Boldt Co. as general contractor. The COVID-19 pandemic complicated the work, yet completion was only slightly delayed. In the week before the dedication, Rother's body was carried from Resurrection Memorial Cemetery to the altar of the shrine's chapel. Archbishop Paul S. Coakley celebrated the dedication Mass on February 17, 2023, before roughly 3,000 people from around the world. Dedication week drew dozens of bishops and members of the Rother family, among them Stanley's sister, Marita.

## Guatemala on the Prairie

The 35,000-square-foot church is the largest Catholic church in Oklahoma. Its Spanish colonial style was inspired by the parish in Santiago Atitlán where Rother lived, served and died, tying the Oklahoma building to the Guatemalan town that shaped his ministry. On the church's east side, a smaller chapel holds his body within the altar. Its apse carries a mural designed by EverGreene Studios of Brooklyn, New York, showing Rother's arrival in heaven, where Jesus Christ and a gathering of martyr-saints welcome him. In the adjacent Pilgrim Center, a museum tells the story of his life and legacy through many of his own belongings. It was designed by Exhibit Concepts, the firm that also designed the Oklahoma City Bombing Memorial Museum. More is planned, including a rectory and a two-story ministry building with classrooms and space for events.

## Flight Context

The shrine sits at 35.3757°N, 97.4978°W along the Interstate 35 corridor on the south side of Oklahoma City. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, look for the large Spanish colonial–style church on its 53-acre campus beside the interstate. Nearby airports: Oklahoma City's Will Rogers airport (KOKC), about 5 nm WNW; Tinker Air Force Base (KTIK, military), about 6 nm ENE; and University of Oklahoma Westheimer Airport (KOUN) in Norman, about 8 nm S.
