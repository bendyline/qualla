# Ninety-Nines

Named for the number of women pilots who answered a 1929 invitation, the Ninety-Nines grew from a hangar meeting on Long Island into a worldwide organization headquartered at Oklahoma City's airport.

**Location:** 35.4066, -97.5976

**Categories:** Aviation organizations based in the United States, Professional associations for women, Organizations based in Oklahoma City, Women in aviation, Amelia Earhart

## Introduction

The name is a head count. In 1929 invitations to an organizing meeting went out to every licensed woman pilot in the country, all 117 of them. Amelia Earhart suggested naming the new group for the number of its charter members, so the founders waited to see who would sign on. Based on the responses that arrived by Christmas, they settled on a name: the Ninety-Nines. Nearly a century later, the organization those women started has its international headquarters and a museum of women pilots on the grounds of Will Rogers World Airport in Oklahoma City.

## After the Powder Puff Derby

The idea came out of a race. On August 18, 1929, the Women's Air Derby left Clover Field in Santa Monica, California, bound for Cleveland, Ohio. The humorist Will Rogers dubbed it the Powder Puff Derby, and that is the name most people remember. It was a dangerous contest. On August 19 Marvel Crosson died in a crash that was apparently caused by carbon monoxide poisoning. Louise Thaden finished first on August 26. Afterward a small group of women pilots met informally in Cleveland and agreed that women needed an organization of their own in the fast-growing field of aviation. On November 2, 1929, twenty-six licensed women pilots gathered at Curtiss Field, near Valley Stream on Long Island, and founded it for the mutual support and advancement of "Women Pilots."

## Earhart's Club

In 1931 the members elected Amelia Earhart as their first president, and she served until 1933. Charter members also included Mary C. Alexander and Nellie Zabel Willhite. Over the decades the membership roll has grown into a long list of notable aviators. Jackie Cochran led the organization from 1941 to 1943, and Edna Gardner Whyte was president from 1955 to 1957. Other members have included Pancho Barnes, Bobbi Trout, Elsie MacGill, Nancy-Bird Walton, Sheila Scott, Patty Wagstaff, Linda M. Godwin, Pamela Melroy and Shannon Walker. The group now describes its mission as promoting world fellowship through flight, supporting women in aviation with networking and scholarships, fostering aviation education and preserving the history of women pilots. By 2017 it had 5,159 members in 30 countries, and in late 2025 Flying magazine described it as having nearly 8,000 members, student pilots included.

## Keepers of the Record

Preserving that history is part of the mission. Virginia Thompson joined in 1954 and became the first historian of what is now the Mid-Atlantic Section. On July 26, 1963, she was one of six women aviators, among them charter member and former international president Blanche Noyes, who joined President John F. Kennedy at the White House when he honored women pilots at the presentation of the Amelia Earhart First Day Cover. Thompson later served for many years as the Ninety-Nines' international historian, and she was also a Smithsonian archivist. She remained an active member for nearly 65 years, until her death in 2019. The organization also owns and cares for the Amelia Earhart Birthplace Museum in Atchison, Kansas. The house is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and has been returned to its turn-of-the-century appearance.

## Paint on the Runway

At the Oklahoma City headquarters, the 99s Museum of Women Pilots preserves papers, personal belongings, photographs, video and oral histories from women aviators around the world. Much of the organization's work, though, looks forward. The Amelia Earhart Memorial Scholarship Fund helps members pay for flight training, technical training and academic study. Its "First Wings" award provides up to $6,000 to help a student pilot finish her private pilot certificate, and many chapters also offer their own scholarships. Volunteers paint airport names and compass roses on airfields so pilots can identify them from the air, and members regularly serve as judges at National Intercollegiate Flying Association competitions. As of 2022 there were 153 chapters in 27 sections worldwide, including Ambassador 99s, an online chapter for members who move around too much to settle in one region. The organization's honors include the National Aeronautic Association's Frank G. Brewer Trophy in 2001 and induction into the International Air & Space Hall of Fame in 2014.

## Flight Context

The Ninety-Nines headquarters and the 99s Museum of Women Pilots are on the grounds of Will Rogers World Airport (KOKC), at about 35.4066°N, 97.5976°W, on the southwest side of Oklahoma City. You can reach the museum directly by flying into KOKC, which is the region's main commercial airport and busy with airline traffic. Wiley Post Airport (KPWA), about 8 nm to the north, is a general aviation alternative. Tinker Air Force Base (KTIK) lies about 10 nm to the east. From 3,000–4,000 ft AGL, the airport's long runways and the downtown skyline to the northeast make easy references. Central Oklahoma usually has good visibility, but spring and early summer bring frequent strong thunderstorms.
