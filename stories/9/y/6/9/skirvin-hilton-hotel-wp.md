# Skirvin Hilton Hotel

Oklahoma City's oldest hotel opened in 1911, stood empty for nearly two decades, came back to life in 2007, and still unsettles visiting NBA teams with a ghost story that has no evidence behind it.

**Location:** 35.4689, -97.5136

**Categories:** Hotels in Oklahoma City, Hotel buildings on the National Register of Historic Places in Oklahoma, Hotels established in 1911, Reportedly haunted hotels, Neoclassical architecture in Oklahoma

## Introduction

In 2010 the New York Knicks lost to the Oklahoma City Thunder and blamed the loss on a haunting at their hotel. They were not the only visiting team uneasy at the Skirvin. That same year Chicago Bulls rookie Taj Gibson said his bathroom door slammed shut on its own, and teammates reported strange noises in the hallway. The Skirvin Hilton is a high-rise in downtown Oklahoma City and the city's oldest hotel. It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and it has built a reputation that no amount of room service can shake. Behind the ghost stories is a real history of growth, abandonment and a long, expensive rescue.

## Bill Skirvin's Hotel

The Skirvin Hotel opened on September 26, 1911. It started with roughly 225 rooms in a ten-story tower with two wings. Its founder, William Balser "Bill" Skirvin, gave it his name, and the hotel kept growing. A third wing, twelve stories tall, was added in 1925. In 1929 and 1930 all three wings were raised to the hotel's full height, bringing it to 525 rooms. The family's reach extended well beyond Oklahoma. Skirvin's daughter, Perle Mesta, later became ambassador to Luxembourg under President Harry Truman. The hotel's good years did not last forever, though. By 1988 occupancy had fallen so low that the Skirvin closed its doors.

## Nearly Two Decades in the Dark

For nearly two decades the hotel sat empty. In 1999 Mayor Kirk Humphreys appointed a Skirvin Solutions Committee to decide whether the building could be saved. Its members toured other restored historic hotels to see how those projects had been paid for, and in October 2000 they recommended a public-private partnership to reopen the Skirvin. The city bought the building in May 2002 for $2.875 million and later that year asked developers for proposals. Partners in Development, a firm put together by John Weeman, proposed a $42.1 million renovation that would reopen the building as a full-service Hilton run by Marcus Hotels & Resorts. The Oklahoma City Urban Renewal Authority chose the firm in January 2004, and Weeman bought the building in 2005. Combining his own investment with tax increment financing, grants, Empowerment Zone credits, historic tax credits and New Markets tax credits, he completed the renovation for more than $50 million.

## Lights On Again

The Skirvin Hilton reopened in 2007. The restoration brought back the original exterior finish and installed historically accurate windows. It also restored the guest elevators and reconfigured the rooms, making them larger. That brought the total down to 225 rooms, with 22,000 square feet of meeting rooms and common areas. The hotel is a member of Historic Hotels of America, the official program of the National Trust for Historic Preservation, and in 2025 it completed another renovation, this one costing $22 million. The ghost stories have outlasted every renovation. The Miami Heat stayed at the Skirvin during the NBA Finals in June 2012, and the haunting made national news again. The following year the Baylor Lady Bears, the defending NCAA women's basketball champions, stayed there and then lost 82–81 to Louisville in a regional semifinal, one of the biggest upsets in tournament history. In 2019 Kyrie Irving announced he was producing a feature film about the hotel's reported paranormal activity.

## The Legend of Room 1015

Most of the stories center on Room 1015, which is rumored to be the most haunted room in the hotel. According to the legend, Bill Skirvin had an affair with a maid known as "Effie," and she became pregnant. To avoid a scandal, the story goes, he locked her in Room 1015, and she eventually jumped from its window, killing herself and her baby. Hotel staff have reported objects moving on their own and strange noises at night. The facts are less tidy. According to Steve Lackmeyer, who co-wrote a book about the hotel, and Jason Kersey, both reporters for The Oklahoman, Skirvin was "a notorious womanizer and drinker," and the tenth floor was known for gambling and other vices. Yet there is no real-life evidence behind the story of Effie. Skirvin's family did believe that his assistant and bookkeeper, Mabel Luty, had been his mistress, but she outlived him.

## Flight Context

The Skirvin Hilton stands in downtown Oklahoma City at about 35.4689°N, 97.5136°W, among the high-rises of the central business district. From 3,000–3,500 ft AGL, the downtown skyline is the landmark to look for, though you will not be able to pick out the hotel itself. Downtown sits inside busy terminal airspace. Will Rogers World Airport (KOKC) is about 6 nm southwest, Wiley Post Airport (KPWA) about 8 nm northwest, and Tinker Air Force Base (KTIK) about 7 nm southeast. Get ATC clearance where required. Visibility is usually good, but spring and early summer bring strong thunderstorms.
