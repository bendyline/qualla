# Eskimo Joe's

Opened in 1975 by two Oklahoma State graduates as a bar promising the coldest beer in town, Eskimo Joe's grew into a Stillwater landmark whose cheese fries earned praise from two presidents.

**Location:** 36.1219, -97.0642

**Categories:** Theme restaurants, Restaurants in Oklahoma, Buildings and structures in Stillwater, Oklahoma, Restaurants established in 1975, Tourist attractions in Stillwater, Oklahoma

## Introduction

The name was a promise about temperature. When two young Oklahoma State University graduates opened a bar across the street from campus in the summer of 1975, co-founder Steve File wanted its name to say one thing above all: this place had the coldest beer in town. He landed on Eskimo Joe's. More than fifty years later, the bar has become a restaurant, the restaurant has become a Stillwater landmark, and the place has collected shout-outs from two presidents and two late-night television hosts. Not bad for a joint that opened with 72 T-shirts for sale behind the bar.

## Built for the Coldest Beer in Town

Eskimo Joe's opened its doors on July 21, 1975, the creation of Steve File and Stan Clark, two OSU graduates who knew exactly who their customers would be. The building at 501 West Elm sits across the street from the university's main campus, and it functions as an extension of "The Strip," the cluster of bars centered on Washington Street that anchors Stillwater's college nightlife. In the beginning, Joe's was strictly a bar. That changed in 1984, when the drinking age was raised from 18 to 21. For a business built on selling beer to college students, the new law demanded a new plan, and Eskimo Joe's became a restaurant as well. Somewhere along the way the partnership changed too: Clark bought out File's interest and became the sole owner of the business the two had started together.

## A Magic Marker and a Dog Named Buffy

Before the bar had even opened, it had a face. Bill Thompson, a freshman commercial art student, drew the logo with a magic marker: a grinning Eskimo Joe alongside his dog, Buffy. The pair went straight onto T-shirts, and 72 of them were available on opening day, sold from behind the bar between drink orders. That first batch sold out within a week, and the shirts soon became so popular that the wait staff could no longer keep up with the demand. The concept later spawned sibling restaurants with mascots of their own. Mexico Joe's dresses Joe in a sombrero and poncho and gives him a donkey named Bucky, while Joseppi's, now closed, put him in a chef's outfit with a twirly Sicilian mustache and paired him with a monkey named Peppino. Neither variation has caught on like the original. The first Joe, with Buffy at his side, is still the one people know.

## Under the Joe Dome

Locals know it as "Stillwater's Jumpin' Little Juke Joint," a nickname that sits cheerfully alongside a two-story building with a showpiece at its center. That showpiece is an atrium dubbed the "Joe Dome." Construction began in 1991 on the expansion, which doubled the restaurant's seating and added a retractable glass roof that slides open or closed depending on the weather. On a mild Oklahoma evening, the sky becomes part of the dining room. Joe's remains a favorite hangout for OSU students and a regular stop in the late-night college scene, hosting concerts along with promotions such as Ladies Night and Coin Beer. In early 2003 it launched "Thirsty Thursday," which charged a $5 cover and gave patrons all the domestic beer they cared to drink. That promotion has since evolved into "bottomless bottles."

## Cheese Fries on the National Stage

The restaurant's best-known menu item owes a good part of its fame to the White House. In 1990, President George H. W. Bush used his commencement speech to endorse Eskimo Joe's cheese fries. Sixteen years later, in 2006, President George W. Bush followed in his father's footsteps with a commencement speech of his own that joked about a "romantic dinner for two" at the restaurant. It is a presidential shout-out handed down, fittingly, from father to son. Late-night television came calling too. On October 6, 2009, Jimmy Fallon mentioned Eskimo Joe's after visiting the establishment for Orange Peel 2009. In 2018 the restaurant surfaced again on The Late Show with Stephen Colbert, in a segment done with Stillwater native James Marsden. For all that attention, the formula has barely changed since 1975: cold drinks, loud music, and a cartoon grinning from the front of a T-shirt.
