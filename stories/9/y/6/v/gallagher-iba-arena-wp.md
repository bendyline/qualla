# Gallagher-Iba Arena

Oklahoma State's 1938 arena was rebuilt around itself without losing its original maple floor, and it remains a wrestling fortress, a basketball cauldron and a place of remembrance known as the Madison Square Garden of the Plains.

**Location:** 36.1258, -97.0650

**Categories:** College basketball venues in Oklahoma, College wrestling venues in the United States, Oklahoma State Cowboys and Cowgirls basketball, Buildings and structures in Stillwater, Oklahoma, Sports venues completed in 1938

## Introduction

"It got so loud, a lot of the lights busted in Gallagher." That is how former Oklahoma State wrestling coach Myron Roderick remembered the 1978 Big 8 Conference finals. By his account, more than 9,000 fans packed the old building to watch unranked Daryl Monasmith beat Iowa State's Frank Santana, the defending national champion. "That is the loudest I've ever heard it," Roderick said. "It was unbelievable." Gallagher-Iba Arena in Stillwater, Oklahoma, has gone by nicknames like "The Rowdiest Arena in the Country" and "The Madison Square Garden of the Plains." Since 1938 it has been home to Oklahoma State basketball and wrestling.

## The Most Expensive Floor in America

The building opened in 1938 as the 4-H Club and Student Activities Building. It was soon renamed Gallagher Hall in honor of wrestling coach Ed Gallagher. Its maple floor was the most expensive of its kind in America when it was installed, and it is still in use today, the oldest original basketball court floor still in play. The first game on it was played on December 9, 1938. Henry Iba's Oklahoma A&M Aggies beat Phog Allen's Kansas Jayhawks 21–15, a matchup between two of the nation's early basketball powers. Iba was the school's longtime basketball coach and an innovator in the game, and in 1987, after renovations, the building took the name Gallagher-Iba Arena to honor him as well. Two seats in the southeast concourse are permanently reserved for the two men.

## A Fortress on the Mat

The first wrestling dual in Gallagher Hall came in early 1939, when A&M beat Indiana 18–6. The first Aggie to wrestle there was Joe McDaniel of Sulphur, Oklahoma, a three-time NCAA champion, who won his match 13–2. It was the start of a remarkable home record. From the arena's opening until February 16, 1951, Oklahoma State won 37 straight home duals without a tie. From 1959 to 1967 it went 61 duals without a loss, finishing 60–0–1, before rival Oklahoma ended the streak 19–13. The Cowboys have won 34 NCAA team titles in all, 25 of them since wrestling began in the building. The worst home defeat came on January 30, 1993, when Penn State won 38–7 to end a 50-dual home winning streak. On February 19, 2017, a dual with Penn State drew 14,059 spectators, the largest crowd in the arena's history.

## Building Around the Old Barn

By the 1990s Oklahoma State badly needed a bigger basketball arena. Instead of building a new one off campus, the university decided to grow the old one, expanding it from 6,381 seats to 13,611. Crews built the new structure around the old gym during the basketball season so that games could continue inside. Once the new exterior was nearly finished, they took apart the old roof. The original sightlines and the white maple floor both stayed. The $55 million expansion, designed by Gary Sparks and built by Manhattan Construction, was ready for the 2000 season, and the Cowboys opened it with a 70–60 win over Arkansas-Little Rock. In 2001 CBS SportsLine.com named it the best college gymnasium. In 2005 the court was named for coach Eddie Sutton. In 2008 a sellout crowd of 13,611 watched the Cowgirls beat Oklahoma 82–63, at the time the best-attended women's sporting event in Oklahoma's history.

## Banners and Remembrance

Championship banners hang from the rafters, and photographs and trophies fill Heritage Hall on the west side of the first floor. Two banners honor loss. One remembers ten members of the Oklahoma State basketball family who were killed in a 2001 plane crash in Colorado. The other honors four people, including the women's basketball coaches, who died when their plane went down over Arkansas in 2011. Outside the Sherman E. Smith training facility, across from Boone Pickens Stadium, the original Spirit Rider statue shows a cowboy on a black horse named Bullet, carrying an OSU flag. Inside the arena, in the southwest corner, a second Spirit Rider kneels with his hat off in front of a memorial to the ten men lost in 2001.

## Camp Sutton

For big games, students camp out. The tent cities pitched on the north and south sides of the arena became known as Camp Sutton in January 2006, which turned out to be Eddie Sutton's last season as head coach. Tents of up to ten people register in order of arrival starting four days before a game, and at least one member has to be present at all times. That has meant freezing nights with little sleep in the Oklahoma winter. The first game under the system was the 2006 Bedlam game against Oklahoma. More than 55 tents registered, and around 400 students were admitted in six minutes. In 2007 campers from the Northside Nasties and the Southside Savages joined forces under more than 1,700 square feet of tarps taped and tied together. They called it the Thunderdome.

## Flight Context

Gallagher-Iba Arena is on the Oklahoma State University campus in Stillwater, at about 36.1258°N, 97.0650°W, next to Boone Pickens Stadium. The stadium's large bowl is the easiest landmark from the air, and the arena stands beside it. A pass at about 2,000–3,000 ft AGL takes in the campus and the town. Stillwater Regional Airport (KSWO) is only about 2.5 nm north-northwest of the arena, so stay clear of its traffic pattern and follow local procedures. Central Oklahoma usually has good visibility, but winter brings strong north winds and spring brings severe thunderstorms.
