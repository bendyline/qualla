# National Wrestling Hall of Fame and Museum

Stillwater beat Waterloo, Iowa, for amateur wrestling's national hall of fame in 1972, then decades later ended up running a museum in Waterloo too.

**Location:** 36.1265, -97.0633

**Categories:** Sports museums, Halls of fame, Wrestling, Oklahoma State University, Stillwater, Oklahoma

## Introduction

In 1972, two wrestling towns wanted the same prize. The United States Wrestling Federation had a national hall of fame for amateur wrestling to place, and it chose Stillwater, Oklahoma, over a competing bid from Waterloo, Iowa. Nearly four decades later the story came full circle. In 2010 the National Wrestling Hall of Fame, still headquartered in Stillwater, began operating the Dan Gable Museum in Waterloo, so the losing bidder ended up with a branch of the institution it had once hoped to host.

## Six Oklahomans and a 99-Year Lease

Six people from Oklahoma formed the Hall of Fame corporation: Myron Roderick, Dr. Melvin D. Jones, Ralph Ball, Robert L. McCormick, Bill Aufleger and Veldo Brewer. The first name on that list carried weight in Stillwater: Roderick had been Oklahoma State's head wrestling coach from 1957 to 1969, years in which the school won seven NCAA team championships, and in 1976 he entered the Hall's inaugural class as a Distinguished Member. Oklahoma State University, through its then-president Dr. Robert B. Kamm, provided the land at no cost on a 99-year renewable lease, giving the sport's history a home on university land. The museum opened its doors in 1976, four years after Stillwater won the bid. It runs on a mix of private donations and state funding.

## Granite, Film and Singlets

The Stillwater building covers around 15,000 square feet and brings together several institutions under one roof. In the John T. Vaughan Hall of Honors, the greatest names in wrestling are recognized, with Distinguished Members honored on granite plaques. The Paul K. Scott Museum of Wrestling History displays a portion of what is described as the world's largest collection of wrestling artifacts and memorabilia, including collegiate and Olympic uniforms. The Cliff Keen Theater and the William S. Hein Library round out the complex, along with interactive exhibits and digital kiosks where visitors can watch NCAA Championship matches from the 1930s to the present. The international side of the sport has a place here too: the building also contains the United World Wrestling Hall of Fame, which honors some of the sport's greatest wrestlers and coaches from around the world.

## Waterloo, After All

The Dan Gable Museum had its own winding path. It opened in 1998 in Newton, Iowa, run by the Dan Gable International Wrestling Institute and Museum, and moved to Waterloo in 2006. Four years later, the National Wrestling Hall of Fame absorbed it. Today the Waterloo museum houses several halls of its own: the Glen Brand Wrestling Hall of Fame of Iowa; the George Tragos/Lou Thesz Professional Wrestling Hall of Fame, which honors professional wrestlers with a strong background in amateur wrestling; and the Alan and Gloria Rice Greco-Roman Hall of Champions. It also includes the Dan Gable Teaching Center and its wrestling room, where young people from the area can train, so the museum is a place where the sport is practiced as well as remembered.

## Guarding the Honor Roll

In 1993, the Board of Governors created the State Chapter program to pay tribute to the coaches, officials and contributors who represent the best qualities of the sport and share them with young people every day. The Hall of Fame now has chapters in 36 states, carrying its recognition far beyond Stillwater. Honors can also be taken away. In May 2016, the Hall voted to revoke all honors it had given to Dennis Hastert after his conviction. It was the first time the Hall of Fame had ever revoked honors from a former inductee.

## Flight Context

The National Wrestling Hall of Fame stands at 36.1265°N, 97.0633°W in Stillwater, Oklahoma, on land provided by Oklahoma State University. From 1,500–2,500 feet AGL, the university campus is the landmark to find. Stillwater Regional Airport (KSWO) is only about 2 nm NNW, so expect traffic in its pattern; Oklahoma City's Will Rogers airport (KOKC) lies about 51 nm SSW and Tulsa International (KTUL) about 57 nm E. North-central Oklahoma usually offers good visibility, with thunderstorms most common in spring and summer.
