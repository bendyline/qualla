# Boots Court Motel

A 1939 streamline moderne motor court at the crossroads of Route 66 and US 71 in Carthage, Missouri, slid to the edge of demolition before a long, room-by-room rescue brought back its neon, its radios and, for one memorable night, its $2.50 rate.

**Location:** 37.1783, -94.3143

**Categories:** Buildings and structures on U.S. Route 66, Motels in the United States, Hotels established in 1939, National Register of Historic Places in Jasper County, Missouri, Carthage, Missouri, Streamline Moderne architecture

## Introduction

One of the first guests at the newly restored Boots Court, in 2012, had a family claim on the place. Bob Boots, son of the couple who built the motel, travelled from Tulsa to Carthage, Missouri, and checked in at the opening-year rate of $2.50 a night. He reportedly paid it in 1939 currency. The gesture fit a building determined to turn back its clocks: a streamline moderne motor court at the old "crossroads of America," where US 66 met US 71, brought back from the brink one room at a time.

## A Radio in Every Room

Arthur and Ilda Boots opened the Boots Court at 107 South Garrison Avenue in 1939. The design was pure machine-age optimism, streamline moderne and art deco, its roofline and walls accented in black Carrara glass and green neon. The Boots advertised "a radio in every room," and each room came with its own covered carport. When it opened, during the Great Depression, a filling station briefly operated at the front of the property, but the motel's office soon replaced it. The name was already known on Missouri highways: Arthur's brother, Loyd A. Boots, had opened a Boots Cottage Court on US 54 in Eldon around 1930.

## Room Fourteen

Ples and Grace Neeley bought the motel in 1942 and expanded it to 13 rooms in 1946, though no door carries that number; the thirteenth room is numbered 14 to dodge bad luck. In the mid-1940s Arthur Boots opened a drive-in across the street serving fountain drinks and breakfast, and local station KDMO AM 1490 broadcast on-location interviews with travellers under the title "Breakfast at the Crossroads of America." Rachel Asplin bought the motel with her husband Ruben in 1948 and ran it until her death in 1991, at age 91. Reported guests over the years included Clark Gable, Mickey Mantle and Gene Autry. Then Interstate 44 pulled traffic seven miles south, and the drive-in closed around 1970.

## Close to the Wrecking Ball

Under later owners the property declined. In 2003 an attempt to flip it for demolition and redevelopment as a Walgreens failed amid public outrage; afterward it served for a time as single-room, low-income housing, and the buildings deteriorated severely. By 2011 the motel ranked among the ten most endangered roadside attractions and among Missouri's most endangered buildings. That year, asking prices reported between $190,000 and $225,000 found no takers, and after foreclosure the property went at auction for roughly $100,000 (reports differ) to its creditor, Hometown Bank. In August 2011 two sisters, Deborah Harvey and Priscilla Bledsaw, bought it and set out to return it to its 1940s appearance.

## Putting the Neon Back

They started small. In May 2012 the sisters reopened five rooms in the 1946 annex, planning to use that income, plus donations and souvenir sales from the old office, to restore the eight original rooms. In April 2013 a gabled roof added in the 1970s came off and the original flat roof returned, a prerequisite for a National Register listing. A $12,000 matching grant from the National Park Service's Route 66 Corridor Preservation Program required an equal sum, raised with donations and a preservation award from the Route 66 Association of Missouri. That same year the original signmaker restored the red-and-white Boots Court neon sign, funded by a $2,500 donation from Ron Jones of Bartlesville, Oklahoma, the "tattoo man" known along the highway for more than eighty tattoos of Route 66 landmarks, the Boots among them. Another matching grant restored the building's architectural neon in 2016.

## Checking In to the Forties

By 2020 seven of the thirteen rooms were open. With travel in decline during the COVID-19 pandemic, the sisters put the motel back on the market for $210,000, and in 2021 the nonprofit Boots Court Foundation was established to buy the motel and finish the job. The National Park Service approved the motel's National Register of Historic Places listing on September 26, 2022, and on February 17, 2023, the last of the thirteen rooms reopened. Guests now find real keys, chrome light fixtures, chenille bedspreads, monogrammed towels, built-in dressers and a radio tuned to 1940s hits. Work has continued on an adjacent Sinclair station as a possible Route 66 visitor centre, and the motel bills itself as the second-oldest on the route, behind the Wagon Wheel in Cuba, Missouri.

## Flight Context

The Boots Court stands at 37.1783°N, 94.3143°W on South Garrison Avenue in Carthage, Missouri, at the historic junction of old US 66 and US 71. From 1,500–2,500 feet AGL, the small motel is hard to single out; use the town of Carthage itself and Interstate 44, about seven miles to the south, as references. Joplin Regional Airport (KJLN) is about 9 nm west, Lamar Municipal (KLLU) about 19 nm north, and Neosho Hugh Robinson Airport (KEOS) about 22 nm south. Southwest Missouri weather is generally good for VFR sightseeing outside of spring storm season.
