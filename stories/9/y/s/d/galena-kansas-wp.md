# Galena, Kansas

A Route 66 town named for the lead ore beneath it, Galena boomed with the Tri-State mines, paid for them with a Superfund listing, and now greets travelers with the truck that inspired Mater.

**Location:** 37.0744, -94.6356

**Categories:** Cities in Cherokee County, Kansas, U.S. Route 66, Tri-State mining district, Superfund sites in Kansas, Mining towns

## Introduction

Outside a restored Kan-O-Tex service station at 119 North Main Street sits an International Harvester L-170 truck. It inspired Mater, the tow-truck character in Disney's Cars, and it gives Galena, Kansas, an easy smile for the travelers who follow Route 66 through the state's roughly 13 miles of the old highway. The town's real story runs deeper, down into the ground. Galena is named for a mineral, the lead ore that built it, and the mining that made the town rich also left behind one of the most serious hazardous waste sites in the country.

## A Town of Many Names

The railroad came through in 1871, but nothing much happened until the spring of 1877, when lead was discovered. The community sprang up that same year, along with its first post office, and it went through names quickly. The Galena Mining and Smelting Company platted it as Cornwall. People actually called it Short Creek, after a nearby stream, and for a brief spell it was Bonanza. Still in 1877, it took its present name, Galena, after the lead ore found all around. The town became part of the Tri-State mining district, which stretched across southeastern Kansas, northeastern Oklahoma and southwestern Missouri, and its fortunes rose and fell with the mines.

## The Plant on the Prairie

World War II brought a different kind of industry. Northwest of town, the Jayhawk Ordnance Works rose as a large ordnance plant producing ammonium nitrate. After the war its operator, Kenneth Aldred Spencer, privatized it, and at one point it was said to be the world's largest producer of ammonium nitrate fertilizer. The plant became the foundation of the Spencer Chemical Company's fortune, much of which was eventually funneled into philanthropies across Missouri and Kansas. The Spencers had come to the area in the first place through their ownership of the Pittsburg & Midway Coal Company mining operation. The plant is still running today as Jayhawk Fine Chemicals Corporation, which CABB sold to India's Anupam Rasayan in February 2026.

## What the Mines Left Behind

The population decreased after the mines closed in the 1970s; overall, Galena has lost 73 percent of its residents since 1900, and the 2020 census counted 2,761 people. The mines also left a harder legacy. Decades of mining and smelting had spread lead, zinc and cadmium across the landscape, and in 1983 the Cherokee County Superfund site was placed on the Environmental Protection Agency's National Priorities List, reserved for the most serious abandoned hazardous waste sites in the nation. The Cherokee County portion of the Tri-State district that it covers spans about 115 square miles, and the cleanup has been run in separate subsites, Galena among them. Acidic water in old mine shafts, piles of chat, tailings impoundments, and the water standing in mine pits and running in streams all carry substantial concentrations of those toxic metals.

## A Ruling Against Separation

Galena's schools hold a notable place in Kansas legal history. In 1916 the Kansas Supreme Court heard Woolridge v. Board of Education, brought by W.E. Woolridge and other parents after the superintendent separated the African American children, described in the language of the day as "colored," and had them taught together, across different grades, by an African American teacher. The court rejected the district's decision, finding that separating the children was without authority of law in a second-class city like Galena. It was one of eleven such cases organized by African American parents across Kansas between 1881 and 1949, the long run-up to Brown v. Board of Education of Topeka. Today the community is served by Galena USD 499 and Galena High School.

## Main Street Memories

Galena keeps its past on display. The Galena Mining and Historical Museum occupies an old Katy Railroad depot at 319 West 7th Street, on the Route 66 alignment itself. The depot was moved there in 1983, the museum opened in 1984, and 42-by-42-foot annexes were added in 1988 and again in 2010. Inside are mining artifacts, school relics, Route 66 memorabilia and a small fleet of early automobiles: a 1919 Model T Touring Car, a 1924 Model T Roadster and a 1931 Model A truck. Over on Main Street, the truck behind Mater waits outside the Cars on the Route diner and souvenir store. Between the two, a visitor can read the whole arc of Galena, from lead rush to roadside nostalgia.

## Flight Context

Galena sits at 37.0744°N, 94.6356°W in Cherokee County, in the far southeastern corner of Kansas, just west of the Missouri line and a few miles north of Oklahoma. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, look for the town's grid beside the chat piles and water-filled mine pits of the old Tri-State mining district; Joplin, Missouri, spreads out to the east. Joplin Regional Airport (KJLN) is about 8 nm northeast and is the closest large field. Miami Regional (KMIO) in Oklahoma is about 16 nm southwest and Atkinson Municipal (KPTS) at Pittsburg about 23 nm north-northwest. Terrain is flat to gently rolling; spring thunderstorms are common across the region.
