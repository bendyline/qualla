# Bonnie & Clyde Garage Apartment

For twelve days in the spring of 1933, a Craftsman garage apartment in south Joplin, Missouri, hid the Barrow Gang, until a shootout at its garage door killed two local lawmen and the film the gang left behind gave the public some of its first photographs of Bonnie and Clyde.

## Introduction

At four o'clock on the afternoon of Thursday, April 13, 1933, Clyde Barrow was standing at the garage door when the lawmen pulled into the driveway. The officers had come to a quiet two-story building on Oak Ridge Drive in Joplin, Missouri, with a search warrant, acting on complaints from neighbors about the strangers renting the apartments upstairs. In the gunfire that followed, two of those officers were mortally wounded, and the five members of the Barrow Gang drove off, still shooting. They left a great deal behind, including a roll of undeveloped film that would soon show the country what Bonnie Parker and Clyde Barrow looked like.

## An Ordinary Building on Oak Ridge Drive

Nothing about the building suggested it would enter American crime history. Built around 1927, it is a two-story structure on a poured concrete foundation, with a gently pitched hipped roof and exposed rafter ends in the American Craftsman style. Two apartments sit above a double garage, and although the address is Oak Ridge Drive, the front door opens onto 34th Street. In the spring of 1933, Buck Barrow rented one of the apartments under a false name. Soon the whole gang had gathered there: Buck and his wife Blanche, his brother Clyde, Bonnie Parker and W. D. Jones, all lying low after a string of robberies in Missouri and neighboring states. Clyde parked his stolen car in the left side of the garage below. The right side was already taken by a neighbor, so Buck and Blanche rented space at a nearby house for their own car.

## Twelve Days of Cards and Beer

For a while, the gang lived something close to a normal life. They passed the hours playing cards, working puzzles and drinking beer, which had only just become legal again. Blanche and Bonnie went out to the movies or shopped for knick-knacks at the Kress store, and Blanche ended up doing much of the cooking and washing for the household. But five strangers keeping late hours drew attention. After twelve days, neighbors reported the suspicious behavior. Highway patrol troopers looked into it and found that the registration on a car did not match the people renting the property, and they passed the matter to the Joplin police, who obtained a search warrant. Five officers went to the building that Thursday afternoon.

## Two Men Who Did Not Go Home

As the officers drove one car into the driveway to keep Clyde from closing the garage door, Newton County Constable John Wesley Harryman stepped out and was struck by a shotgun blast. Joplin Detective Harry McGinnis was hit by shotgun and automatic rifle fire that nearly severed his right arm. Harryman, 42, died on the way to the hospital, leaving behind his wife, Atha. McGinnis, 53, a widower, died at eleven that night. The gang fired as they fled, and all five escaped. The two men who died that afternoon had gone to work on an ordinary Thursday to serve a warrant in their own community. Both are remembered today on the Missouri Law Enforcement Memorial.

## What the Gang Left Behind

In their hurry, the gang abandoned a trove of evidence. Among it were Buck Barrow's recent pardon papers, guns, stolen merchandise that conclusively tied the gang to a robbery in Joplin during their stay, and undeveloped rolls of film. Police had the film developed at The Joplin Globe and found photographs of Clyde, Bonnie and W. D. Jones posing with weapons, even pointing guns at one another in mock menace. They were among the first photographs of Bonnie and Clyde to reach the public, and they helped authorities eventually bring the gang down. The pictures also gave the pair a public face, one that would travel far beyond Joplin. The building itself was later listed on the National Register of Historic Places, a Craftsman garage apartment remembered for twelve days in 1933.
