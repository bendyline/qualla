# Taum Sauk Mountain

Missouri's highest point is a forested volcanic ridge in the ancient St. Francois Mountains, home to the state's tallest waterfall and a peak that was once spared from a power plant.

**Location:** 37.5703, -90.7279

**Categories:** Mountains of Missouri, St. Francois Mountains, Highest points of U.S. states, State parks of Missouri, Waterfalls, Geology

## Introduction

For much of the Paleozoic Era, while shallow seas spread across what is now the Ozarks and laid down the sandstones, limestones and shales that cover most of the region, the peaks of the St. Francois Mountains stood above the water as islands. Geologists believe Taum Sauk Mountain and its neighbors may be among the few places in the United States that were never submerged by those ancient seas. Weathering and erosion wore at them instead, and the sediment stripped from these old summits became part of the surrounding rock layers. Today Taum Sauk is the highest point in Missouri, a quiet, forested ridge at 1,772 feet.

## Older Than the Appalachians

Taum Sauk looks modest beside higher ranges, but it is a true mountain. Most of the Ozarks' relief comes from rivers cutting down through layers of sedimentary rock. The St. Francois Mountains are something else entirely: an ancient Precambrian igneous uplift, the product of volcanic mountain building, several times older than the Appalachians. Missouri State Parks describes the rocks that resulted, fine-grained rhyolite that formed at the surface and coarse-grained granite that formed below. Taum Sauk itself is less a peak than an elongated ridge running north-northwest to south-southeast. The mountain is said to be named for a Piankeshaw chief called Sauk-Ton-Qua.

## High, but Not the Tallest Rise

Being Missouri's highest point does not make Taum Sauk its most dramatic. The mountain rises only 522 feet from an already elevated base. Mudlick Mountain climbs 693 feet from a lower base to reach just 1,313 feet, and Black Mountain in Madison County is credited with the greatest rise in the state, climbing just under 1,000 feet from the St. Francis River to a summit of 1,502 feet. In 1991 Missouri created Taum Sauk Mountain State Park, 7,448 acres covering the mountain, with a rustic campground and a paved trail to the high point, which is marked by a polished granite plaque. Dense forest hides the view from most spots on the mountain, so the best look out over the hills comes from a lookout tower.

## Falls on Volcanic Ledges

A rugged trail makes a three-mile loop from the high-point parking area to Mina Sauk Falls, the highest waterfall in Missouri. In wet weather, according to Missouri State Parks, it drops 132 feet down a series of volcanic ledges into a rock-bottomed pool. In dry spells it shrinks to a trickle or less, so timing matters. The park shares a common jurisdiction with nearby Johnson's Shut-Ins State Park, and together they form the second-largest state park in Missouri, 15,961.5 acres. With the adjacent Bell Mountain Wilderness, they are part of a large wild area popular with hikers and backpackers. The 33-mile Taum Sauk section of the Ozark Trail runs through it, and the Ozark Trail Association considers it one of the finest trails in the state.

## The Peak That Was Spared

In the late 1950s, engineers looking for a site for Union Electric's new pumped-storage power plant intended to recommend Taum Sauk, in part because it was the highest point in the state. Strong political opposition to developing the scenic mountain ruled it out, and the plant went instead to Proffit Mountain, roughly seven miles to the southwest, though it kept the Taum Sauk name. Proffit Mountain is no small hill either: at 1,720 feet it is the sixth-highest point in Missouri. Decades later, on December 14, 2005, the plant's upper reservoir failed there and sent a flash flood 20 feet deep down the Black River. The flood came off Proffit Mountain, not Taum Sauk, whose forests and falls remain protected as a state park.

## Flight Context

Taum Sauk Mountain's high point lies at approximately 37.57°N, 90.73°W in Iron County, in the St. Francois Mountains of southeastern Missouri. The summit is a broad, forested ridge rather than a sharp peak, so it is hard to pick out; use the distinctive ring-shaped upper reservoir of the Taum Sauk power plant atop Proffit Mountain, roughly seven miles to the southwest, as a reference. Terrain reaches 1,772 feet MSL, so plan at least 3,500–4,500 feet MSL for sightseeing. Farmington Regional Airport (KFAM) is an airport with services, roughly 20 miles to the northeast. Hills here can hold low cloud and valley fog, especially on cool mornings.
