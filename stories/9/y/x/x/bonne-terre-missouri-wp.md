# Bonne Terre, Missouri

A Missouri lead-mining town named for its "good soil," where a flooded mine beneath the streets draws scuba divers and the waste above ground is still being cleaned up.

**Location:** 37.9217, -90.5517

**Categories:** Cities in St. Francois County, Missouri, French colonial settlements of Illinois Country, Lead mining, Superfund sites, Scuba diving

## Introduction

Beneath the streets of Bonne Terre, Missouri, scuba divers glide through the flooded galleries of a lead mine. When the Bonne Terre Mine closed in 1962, its workings gradually filled with rain and spring water, and the result is now billed as the world's largest freshwater dive resort, with surface, underground and underwater tours. In 1983 Jacques Cousteau's team came to explore it for a documentary. It is a strange afterlife for a hole in the ground, and a fitting one for a town that lead has shaped since the 1720s, for better and for worse.

## The Good Soil

The story starts with French miners. In 1719 La Motte and Philip Francois Renault left France with 200 miners, searching for minerals for the Company of the West, and in 1720 Frenchmen settled here after discovering lead ore in what was then French Louisiana. The land sat on a trail branching off the Camino Real, and the French village served as a campsite along it. Around 1825 surface miners were calling the place La Bonne Terre, "the good soil," and they meant the lead in it rather than anything that grew. In 1828 St. Anne, the first Catholic chapel in St. Francois County, was built here.

## St. Joe Goes Underground

Industrial mining arrived in 1864, when the newly incorporated St. Joseph Lead Company of New York established the Bonne Terre Mine. A year later a new superintendent, J. C. Winslow, brought in a number of miners from Cornwall. During the Civil War the mine was a target of Price's Raid. Bonne Terre was platted as a town in 1880, and the mine became the first in a network of St. Joe mines across the Southeast Missouri Lead District, which eventually made Missouri the source of 70 percent of the nation's lead. The mining era is still visible downtown: the 1869 Shepard house, the town's oldest building, a former post office from 1878, a bathhouse from 1889, a limestone library in Italian Renaissance style built in 1905 by St. Joe director Dwight Jones, and a 1909 Tudor-style city hall reportedly modeled on Shakespeare's home.

## A Lake Where the Miners Worked

The Bonne Terre Mine ran for almost a century before it closed in 1962. With the pumps no longer holding back groundwater, water crept into the old passages, turning the lowest levels into an underground lake that visitors now know as Billion Gallon Lake. The mine is often called the largest man-made cavern in the world, and in 1974 it was added to the National Register of Historic Places; the 1909 Bonne Terre Depot and the St. Joe Lead Company Administration Building are on the Register too. When the mine is open, visitors explore the old workings on foot and underwater, moving through the vast spaces where generations of miners once labored.

## What the Mines Left Behind

Lead left a harder legacy above ground. Chat dumps, the piles of mine tailings, still stand around town. As they eroded, lead dust spread across the surrounding area and became a hazard to residents, while runoff washed tailings into the Big River. It was not until 1992 that the Big River Mine Tailings site, of which the Bonne Terre tailings are one of eight source areas, was listed as a Superfund site, and cleanup is still under way: by an October 2021 EPA fact sheet, more than 1,940 residential yards across the site had been remediated, and the Bonne Terre East Tailings Flat is still used as a repository for contaminated soil. Because lead levels in fish downstream exceed World Health Organization standards, Missouri advises people not to eat fish caught from the Big River downstream of the site. The town of 6,903 people, as counted in 2020, is also home to the Eastern Reception, Diagnostic and Correctional Center, a roughly 2,700-bed state prison where Missouri has carried out its executions since 2005.

## Flight Context

Bonne Terre lies at approximately 37.92°N, 90.55°W in St. Francois County, in the Southeast Missouri Lead District south of St. Louis. From 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, look for the town grid and the flat expanses of old chat dumps and tailings flats around it, distinctive marks of the lead-mining era; the Big River runs nearby. Farmington Regional Airport (KFAM) is about 13 miles to the south-southeast. St. Louis Lambert International (KSTL) and its Class B airspace lie well to the north. Expect summer haze and thunderstorms, and fog in the river valleys on calm mornings.
