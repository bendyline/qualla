# Tyronza, Arkansas

The small Delta town of Tyronza gave birth to the Southern Tenant Farmers Union in 1934 and was home to a country doctor who took chickens as payment and advised on the creation of Medicare.

**Location:** 35.4833, -90.3500

**Categories:** Cities in Poinsett County, Arkansas, Cities in Arkansas, Labor history, Southern Tenant Farmers Union, Arkansas Delta

## Introduction

Dr. L. H. McDaniel made house calls all over eastern Arkansas, and when his patients could not pay in cash, he took live chickens, baked goods and garden vegetables instead. He treated people regardless of their ability to pay. The same small-town physician is said to have counted President Lyndon B. Johnson as a friend and has been remembered as a close advisor to Johnson and to Congressman Wilbur D. Mills in creating Medicare and Medicaid, the programs that began in 1965, the year McDaniel died. His hometown was Tyronza, a Delta town of 716 people that, three decades before Medicare, had already started a national argument about who deserved a fair share.

## A Union in the Cotton Fields

Tyronza is one of the oldest towns in Poinsett County, with roots in the late nineteenth century. In the 1930s, the land around it was worked largely by sharecroppers, Black and white, who farmed plantation land owned by others. When the federal Agricultural Adjustment Administration paid subsidies to landowners, that money was supposed to be passed on to tenants. Most landowners did not share it.

In July 1934, H. L. Mitchell and Clay East, local socialists, helped found the Southern Tenant Farmers Union at the Sunnyside School just south of Tyronza, where a group of Black and white men agreed to organize sharecroppers and win them better arrangements. It was one of the few unions of the 1930s open to all races, and it set out to bring Black and white farmworkers together, pursuing its goals through nonviolent protest.

## A National Outcry

What began in Tyronza grew into a national outcry against the abuses sharecroppers suffered at the hands of wealthy landowners. The cost for those who organized was high. Union leaders were often harassed and attacked, and the violence sometimes ended in murder; the union's president, William H. Stultz, was arrested and threatened with death. The union's first strike came in 1935, when cotton pickers demanded higher wages.

Behind the movement were families who worked land they did not own and endured abusive treatment from wealthy landowners. That they organized across the color line, in the Arkansas Delta of the 1930s, is what gives a town this small its place in the history of American labor.

## Tents on the Edge of Town

McDaniel brought his own kind of attention to Tyronza. A national delegate to Democratic conventions in the 1950s, he organized conferences in huge 1,000-seat tents on the outskirts of town and drew national political and medical leaders to this corner of the Delta. His friendship with Johnson grew out of that same work in Democratic Party politics.

The town has grown quieter since. It had 918 residents in 2000 and 716 in 2020, and on July 1, 1986, its school district was folded into the East Poinsett County School District. Tyronza is now part of the Jonesboro metropolitan area, a small grid of streets in the flat farmland of the Arkansas Delta, with a history larger than its size.

## Flight Context

Tyronza lies at 35.483°N, 90.35°W in Poinsett County, in the flat cropland of the Arkansas Delta roughly between Jonesboro and Memphis. From 2,000-3,000 ft AGL, look for a small town grid surrounded by large rectangular fields. Marked Tree Municipal Airport (FAA identifier 6M8; no ICAO code) is about 4 nm northwest. Jonesboro Municipal Airport (KJBR) is about 25 nm northwest, West Memphis Municipal Airport (KAWM) about 22 nm south, and Millington-Memphis Airport (KNQA) about 25 nm east across the Mississippi in Tennessee. The terrain is nearly featureless, so use highways and towns for reference; expect summer haze and thunderstorms.
