# Pfeiffer House and Carriage House

In a small town in Arkansas's northeastern corner, the family home of Pauline Pfeiffer and a barn turned writing studio preserve the years when Ernest Hemingway came to Piggott as a son-in-law and worked on portions of A Farewell to Arms.

**Location:** 36.3845, -90.2001

## Introduction

Ernest Hemingway's name conjures Paris cafés, Spanish bullrings, African plains and the Florida Keys. It rarely conjures Piggott, Arkansas. Yet at 10th and Cherry Streets in this small Clay County town stands a house where the writer came as a son-in-law, and behind it a barn he turned into a place to work. Portions of his 1929 novel A Farewell to Arms were written here, far from the Italian front where the book is set, in the home of the family that had raised his second wife, Pauline Pfeiffer.

## A Prominent Family in a Small Town

Paul and Mary Pfeiffer bought the house in 1913 and moved their family down from St. Louis. They became prominent citizens of northeastern Arkansas, and their holdings grew to more than 60,000 acres of land. Their daughter Pauline grew up in the home and went on to become Hemingway's second wife, and her family took a generous interest in the couple. Her uncle, Gustavus Pfeiffer, became a benefactor to the couple. It was Uncle Gus who financed their African safari, the journey that inspired Hemingway's Green Hills of Africa. So the Pfeiffers of Piggott stand behind more than one chapter of the Hemingway story, even if their name rarely appears on the covers.

## The Studio in the Barn

Hemingway needed privacy to write, and he found it out back. He did his writing in a barn behind the home, converted into a studio where he could work in privacy while visiting Piggott. According to Arkansas State University, several of his short stories took shape there as well. Today the studio is furnished with items of the kind that would have been found in it when Hemingway used it, giving visitors a sense of the room where he sat down to work. The main house, built in the Colonial Revival style, and the barn studio were listed together on the National Register of Historic Places in 1982, recognized as the Pfeiffer House and Carriage House.

## Honoring the Whole Family

Arkansas State University restored the property and opened it to the public in 1999, timed to the centennial of Hemingway's birth. It is now home to the university's Hemingway-Pfeiffer Museum and Educational Center, whose stated mission is to "contribute to the regional, national and global understanding of the 1920s and 1930s eras by focusing on the internationally connected Pfeiffer family, of Piggott, Arkansas, and their son-in-law Ernest Hemingway." That framing matters: this is as much a story about the Pfeiffers as about their famous son-in-law. The Pfeiffer story widened in 2004, when the Tudor-style home where Pauline's brother Karl and his wife Matilda had lived opened to the public as a museum and study center. The Hemingway-Pfeiffer museum also serves as the visitor center for the Crowley's Ridge Parkway, which makes this literary landmark a starting point for travelers exploring the ridge.

## Flight Context

The Hemingway-Pfeiffer House stands at about 36.385°N, 90.200°W in Piggott, Clay County, in the northeastern corner of Arkansas, about eight miles south of the Missouri state line. From 2,500-3,500 feet MSL, look for the wooded rise of Crowley's Ridge running north-south through the area, with flat Delta farmland spreading out on either side; Piggott sits along the ridge. The house itself is a modest residential landmark in town, so the town grid is your best reference. Kirk Field (KPGR) in Paragould lies about 28 miles to the southwest, and Jonesboro Municipal Airport (KJBR) is roughly 45 miles southwest. Expect good visibility much of the year, with summer haze over the Delta.
