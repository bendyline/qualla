# Devil's Icebox (cave)

Beneath Rock Bridge Memorial State Park in Columbia, Missouri, the Devil's Icebox breathes cold air year-round, runs for miles underground, and shelters one of the richest cave ecosystems in the state, including the only known natural home of the pink planarian.

**Location:** 38.8700, -92.3266

**Categories:** Caves of Missouri, Sinkholes of the United States, Geography of Columbia, Missouri, Landforms of Boone County, Missouri, Limestone caves

## Introduction

On a sweltering Missouri summer afternoon, the cold reaches you before the cave comes into view. It spills out of the ground at Rock Bridge Memorial State Park, on the edge of Columbia, carried on air that leaks from the cave all year long. People living here in the early 1800s probably gave the place its name for exactly that chill, and it has stuck: the Devil's Icebox. Behind the name lies what is said to be one of the longest caves in Missouri, and one of the most crowded with life, a place so fragile that it is now closed to the public.

## A Name on the Wind

The cave's name most likely came from its breath. The "icebox" in Devil's Icebox probably refers to the very cold air that flows out of the cave in every season, a strange and memorable thing to encounter in the Missouri heat. The name was probably coined by people living in the area in the early 1800s. It first appeared in print in 1926, in an article describing the earliest exploration of the cave, which had taken place two years earlier, in 1924. Long before anyone mapped it, though, the cave was already useful. The stream that flows through the Devil's Icebox was a source of power for settlers in the 19th century.

## Miles Beneath the Park

The entrance is a double sinkhole, a place where the ground has collapsed into the cave system below, offering a glimpse of the underground stream. From there the passages run on for a remarkable distance. A survey in 1986 estimated the cave's length at 5.76 miles, and Missouri State Parks now cites a total mapped passage distance of 6.5 miles; it is said to be one of the longest caves in the state. Its water carries a heavy load of dissolved rock; in January 1994, a stream in the cave was found to be oversaturated with calcite and dolomite, minerals dissolved from the surrounding rock. In 2017 scientists studied the cave's stalagmites, whose slow-growing layers can preserve a record of the climate above. Nearby, part of an old cave collapsed long ago and left behind the natural rock bridge that gives the park its name.

## Life in the Dark

For all its cold, the Devil's Icebox is teeming. According to the U.S. Government Printing Office, it has the second highest number of species of the more than 6,000 caves in Missouri, and by 2007, 80 species had been identified inside. Salamanders, frogs, millipedes, spiders and springtails share the passages with troglobites, animals adapted to spend their entire lives in darkness. One resident is known from nowhere else. The cave is the only known natural home of Kenkia glandulosa, the pink planarian, a small free-living flatworm. The rarity of the cave's inhabitants was once even used as an argument against building a shopping mall in the area, a reminder that what lives unseen underground can depend on what happens on the surface.

## Closed for the Bats

The cave is also a refuge for endangered bats, among them the gray bat, and protecting them has meant closing the doors. Access to the Devil's Icebox was restricted in 2006. In 2010, public tours stopped entirely to guard the bats against white-nose syndrome, a disease that threatens bats. The precaution could not hold it off forever; the disease reached the caves of Rock Bridge in 2013. Visitors can still feel the Icebox from outside. A boardwalk leads over the 125-foot-long natural tunnel beneath the rock bridge to the sinkhole, where the cold air rises and the underground stream shows itself, and Connor's Cave, downstream, remains open to explore on your own.

## Flight Context

The Devil's Icebox lies at 38.8700°N, 92.3266°W in Rock Bridge Memorial State Park on the southern edge of Columbia, Missouri. The cave and its sinkhole entrance are hidden beneath the park's forest canopy, so navigate by the wooded parkland just south of the city's developed edge. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, Columbia's grid and the University of Missouri campus to the north give easy orientation. Columbia Regional Airport (KCOU) is only about 6 nm to the east-southeast; coordinate with KCOU tower and watch for arriving and departing traffic. Summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms are the usual weather concerns.
