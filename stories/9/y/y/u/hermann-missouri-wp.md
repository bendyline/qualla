# Hermann, Missouri

Founded in 1837 by a Philadelphia society dreaming of a "heart of German America," the Missouri River town of Hermann built a wine country, lost it to Prohibition, and brought it back.

**Location:** 38.6992, -91.4392

**Categories:** German-American history, Missouri Rhineland, Cities in Gasconade County, Missouri, Missouri populated places on the Missouri River, Wine regions

## Introduction

In 2009, a small Missouri River town celebrated the 2,000th anniversary of a battle fought in a German forest. In 9 CE, the Germanic leader Hermann der Cherusker destroyed three Roman legions in the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest, and when German settlers laid out a town on the Missouri more than eighteen centuries later, they named it for him. A bronze statue of Hermann now stands in the town's park. The choice said everything about what the founders hoped this place would be: not merely a settlement in the American interior, but a home where German culture could take root and endure.

## The Philadelphia Plan

Hermann began as an idea in Pennsylvania. In 1837, the Deutsche Ansiedlungs-Gesellschaft zu Philadelphia, the German Settlement Society of Philadelphia, founded the town on land George Bayer had bought on the society's behalf. The area had been promoted by Gottfried Duden, who wrote about it in his Report of a Journey to the Western States of Northern America. The society's goals were almost utopian: a "heart of German America" that would perpetuate traditional German culture and grow into a self-supporting colony built on farming, commerce and industry. The town was platted after the society sold shares in the 11,300 acres of Gasconade River valley land it had purchased. An early group of settlers arrived in November 1837, while Bayer, waylaid by illness in Pittsburgh, reached Hermann in the spring of 1838 at the head of another group of families. In 1842 Hermann became the seat of Gasconade County, a role it still holds.

## Wreck at the Gasconade

On November 1, 1855, Hermann was the nearest town to a railroad disaster. The Pacific Railroad's inaugural train, carrying some 600 invited guests and dignitaries, reached the bridge over the Gasconade River, and as it started across, the span between the east bank and the first pier collapsed. At least thirty-one people were killed, and newspapers named 136 as injured. The Leimer Hotel in Hermann was turned into a temporary hospital for the wounded. The town's response was not remembered entirely kindly: some survivors later complained that they had been refused aid in Hermann and charged excessive prices for supplies, in contrast to the hospitality they found at Miller's Landing, later New Haven.

## Vines, Lost and Found

In the mid-19th century, settlers from the Rhineland established vineyards and wineries around Hermann. Prohibition shut the industry down. Revival began in the 1960s, and in 1983 the area was designated the Hermann American Viticultural Area, the nation's 36th federally recognized AVA and Missouri's second, in recognition of that renaissance. Wine-trail guides credit its seven wineries with roughly a third of Missouri's wine. Stone Hill Winery, founded in 1847, and Hermannhof Winery are in town, and Adam Puchta Winery, just south of town on Frene Creek Road, has stayed in one family since 1855 and calls itself America's oldest continuously family-owned farm winery. The Hermann AVA also takes in OakGlenn Vineyards and Winery east of town, Bias Vineyards near Berger and Röbbler Vineyards near New Haven.

## Maifest and Oktoberfest

Hermann, home to 2,185 people at the 2020 census, still wears its heritage openly. It holds a Maifest on the third weekend in May and an Oktoberfest on weekends throughout October, and it calls itself the sausage-making capital of Missouri. The Hermann Historic District and the Old Stone Hill Historic District are on the National Register of Historic Places, as are the Kotthoff-Weeks Farm Complex, the William Poeschel House, the Rotunda and the Vallet-Danuser House. The 240-mile Katy Trail passes through McKittrick, directly across the Missouri River from Hermann. Nearly two centuries after the Philadelphia society's plan, the heart of German America it imagined beats on in a Missouri river town.

## Flight Context

Hermann is at approximately 38.70°N, 91.44°W on the south bank of the Missouri River in Gasconade County, near the center of the Missouri Rhineland. From 2,000–3,500 feet AGL, look for the compact town grid against the river, with the Gasconade River joining the Missouri a few miles to the west. Hermann Municipal Airport (FAA identifier 63M, no ICAO code) is about 2.5 nm west; Washington Regional (KFYG) is about 22 nm east-southeast. The Missouri River valley can hold morning fog, especially in spring and fall.
