# USS Aries (PHM-5)

A Navy hydrofoil built to skim the sea at 48 knots, USS Aries chased drug smugglers out of Key West in the 1980s, then was bought for scrap and saved as a museum on a Missouri riverbank.

**Location:** 38.6708, -91.5528

**Categories:** Pegasus-class hydrofoils, Patrol vessels of the United States Navy, Museum ships in Missouri, Ships built in Renton, Washington, Military history

## Introduction

Of all the places to find a warship designed to fly above the waves at 48 knots, a riverbank in the middle of Missouri may be the least expected. USS Aries is 133 feet long, and in her working life she could lift her hull clear of the water on foils and race across the Caribbean armed with a 76 mm gun. In 1996, three men who knew nothing about hydrofoils bought her for scrap. They found her in such good shape that she was brought from Charleston, South Carolina, all the way to Missouri, and they decided to save her instead.

## Built to Fly

Aries was the fifth ship of the Navy's Pegasus class, guided-missile hydrofoil gunboats designed for high speed and mobility. Small as they were, they were designed to carry a heavy punch: a 76 mm gun and eight Harpoon missiles, although the missiles were seldom actually carried because they made the class top-heavy. The trick was in the propulsion. Cruising hullborne like an ordinary boat, Aries ran on two Mercedes-Benz marine diesels at about 12 knots; to fly, she switched to a General Electric LM2500 gas turbine of 18,000 shaft horsepower, rose onto her foils and reached 48 knots. Boeing laid her down on 7 January 1980 at Renton, Washington, and launched her on 5 November 1981, sponsored by the wife of Vice Admiral Earl B. Fowler, commander of Naval Sea Systems Command. She was commissioned at the Puget Sound Naval Shipyard on 18 September 1982 and named for the constellation Aries. Her crew numbered just four officers and seventeen enlisted sailors.

## Down the Coast to Key West

On 23 November 1982, Aries left Puget Sound for her assigned home port of Key West, Florida. She called at San Francisco and San Diego on the way down the California coast, passed through the Panama Canal on 11 December and reached Key West on the 15th. Her first law enforcement mission came in February 1983, an operation against drug smugglers carried out with the Coast Guard. More followed that spring. On each, Aries embarked a detachment of Coast Guardsmen and sailed under the operational control of the commandant of Coast Guard District 7 in Miami, a naval warship lending her speed to the effort to stem the flow of illegal drugs into the United States. Between patrols came six weeks of repairs at Atlantic Drydock in Jacksonville, another anti-smuggling sweep in August, and a port visit to Nassau in the Bahamas.

## Across the Caribbean

In 1984 Aries widened her range to the Gulf of Mexico and the east coast of Central America. She joined Exercise Ocean Venture 84 that spring, then sailed to the waters around Puerto Rico for the opening phase of UNITAS XXV, the annual multinational exercises held with Latin American navies. The next year brought a readiness exercise off Puerto Rico, Exercise Solid Shield 85 at Onslow Beach, and UNITAS XXVI. In 1986, after a three-month availability at Mayport, Florida, she sailed on another UNITAS cruise, calling at La Guaira, Venezuela, and St. John's, Antigua. That October she headed north to Little Creek, Virginia, for a special project for the Chief of Naval Operations, dividing the next two months between Key West and the Virginia Capes before returning home on 17 December.

## From Scrap to Shrine

Aries was decommissioned on 30 July 1993. Three years later, Bill and Bob Meinhardt and Eliot James changed her story. After buying her for scrap in 1996 and finding her sound, they set about restoring her, and USS Aries PHM-5 Hydrofoil Memorial, Inc. took her on as a memorial on the Grand River at Brunswick, Missouri. She has since moved. As of September 2025 she rests at the historic Gasconade Boatyard in Gasconade, Missouri, at the confluence of the Gasconade and Missouri Rivers. The USS Aries Hydrofoil Museum, dedicated to preserving hydrofoils of all types, is built around her, and it is open seasonally, with tours by appointment. On a quiet Missouri river, a ship built for open-water sprints now sits still beside the current, far from the Key West patrols that once filled her logbook.

## Flight Context

The ship's present location is at approximately 38.67°N, 91.55°W, at the confluence of the Gasconade and Missouri Rivers, at Gasconade in east-central Missouri. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, find the junction of the two rivers; the vessel is small against the bank, so a slow pass helps.
