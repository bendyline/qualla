# Kingdom City, Missouri

Born at the crossing of U.S. 40 and U.S. 54 and named by an oil company after Callaway County's Civil War nickname, Kingdom City is a highway village that grew out of a fight over where two roads should meet.

**Location:** 38.9494, -91.9369

**Categories:** Villages in Callaway County, Missouri, Jefferson City metropolitan area, Highway towns, Unusual place names

## Introduction

At first the place had no name at all. It was just a patch of farmland and forest south of the village of McCredie, and once the highways arrived, people called it simply the "Y." Fulton wanted to call it North Fulton. McCredie preferred South McCredie. The name that won came from neither town but from an oil company. The Kingdom Oil Company, owned by B.P. (Bernard Parker) Beamer, suggested Kingdom City, borrowing from the nickname of Callaway County itself, and so a highway crossroads in central Missouri came to carry a Civil War legend.

## 54-40 or Fight

Kingdom City owes its existence to a road-building decision. In 1925 U.S. Route 40 was being built across Missouri while U.S. Route 54 was being planned to intersect it somewhere along the way. Fulton hoped U.S. 40 would run from Columbia through Fulton and meet U.S. 54 inside the city. McCredie wanted the crossing too, and its residents fought so hard for it that they paraded through the heart of Fulton carrying banners reading "54-40 or Fight," echoing the "Fifty-four Forty or Fight!" slogan of the 1840s Oregon boundary dispute. McCredie won, in a sense. The highways met on open ground just south of town. While the roads were built, huge numbers of workers poured in, and McCredie became a boom town. The future Kingdom City got its first gas station and a two-story hotel, though the hotel burned down in 1930.

## The Kingdom of Callaway

The name Beamer's company proposed reached back to 1861. As Callaway County's history tells it, word arrived that Union troops had advanced to a nearby county, and Colonel Jefferson F. Jones of eastern Callaway County assembled men to defend it. The defense was largely an illusion: logs set up by the troops looked like artillery in the shadows of their campfires, and they deterred the Union force. Talks went on for several days and produced a mutual ceasefire agreement between the United States of America and Callaway County, as though the county were a sovereign power. Ever since, it has been known as "the Kingdom of Callaway." The county itself, organized in 1820, was named for Captain James Callaway, a grandson of Daniel Boone. Decades later, that defiant nickname found its way onto a highway junction.

## Life at the Interchange

Through the 1920s and 1930s, dance halls, restaurants, cafés and hotels came and went at the junction, serving the traffic that gave the place its reason to exist. In 1965 Gasper's opened for business and became a local landmark. A major change came in 1970, when the McCredie Post Office moved to Kingdom City and took its name, and the village, which had incorporated in 1967 by its own account, took in McCredie. The crossroads had swallowed the town that fought to bring it there. Today the village sits along U.S. 54 about half a mile north of Interstate 70, with McKinney Creek along its southern edge and Auxvasse Creek two miles to the north. It had 134 residents at the 2020 census. In May 2026 the Missouri Department of Transportation was moving traffic onto a new roundabout interchange at U.S. 54 and I-70, with construction continuing as part of a larger expansion of I-70 across Missouri.

## Flight Context

Kingdom City is at approximately 38.95°N, 91.94°W in Callaway County, central Missouri, just north of the junction of Interstate 70 and U.S. Route 54. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL the interchange is an unmistakable landmark, with the small village clustered along U.S. 54 on its north side and the town of Auxvasse about five miles farther north on the same highway. Elton Hensley Memorial Airport at Fulton (KFTT) is about 7 nm south-southwest; Mexico Memorial (KMYJ) is about 14 nm north-northeast; Columbia Regional (KCOU) is about 15 nm west-southwest. Heavy I-70 traffic makes the corridor easy to follow in any clear weather.
