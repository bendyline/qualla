# Bridal Cave

Near Camdenton in the Lake of the Ozarks region, a limestone show cave named for a 1933 legend has hosted more than 4,500 weddings since its first in 1949, with couples marrying beneath a stone formation called the pipe organ.

**Location:** 38.0606, -92.7931

**Categories:** Landforms of Camden County, Missouri, Caves of Missouri, Show caves in the United States, Tourist attractions in Camden County, Missouri

## Introduction

On June 1, 1949, a couple stepped into a limestone cave near Camdenton, Missouri, and were married underground. They were the first. By 2025 more than 4,500 weddings had been held at Bridal Cave, ceremonies held in a chamber whose walls drape down in folds of stone, including a formation known as the pipe organ. The cave had opened to the public only the year before that first ceremony. Its name, though, came earlier, from a book, and from a story far darker than the cheerful weddings that now fill it.

## A Name From a Book

In 1933, R.G. Scott, a former owner of the cave, published a collection called Indian Romances. One chapter tells of an Osage wedding. In Scott's telling, Conwee, the son of an Osage chief, wanted to marry Wasena, the daughter of another Osage chief. Wasena refused him. Conwee and his warriors kidnapped her along with her companion, Irona, and tried to hide the two women in this cave. Wasena escaped and took her own life rather than be bound to Conwee. Irona, who was in love with Conwee's brother, Buffalo, did not try to flee, and she eventually married Buffalo in the same cave. That wedding gave the cave its name.

## Reading the Legend

It helps to remember what kind of story this is. It comes from a book of romances published by a man who owned the cave, and it reads best as a story rather than as history. Even so, its center is not really the wedding that gave the cave its name. It is Wasena, a young woman in the tale who was seized against her will and who chose death over a forced marriage. Her part is easy to overlook beside the happier ending of Irona and Buffalo, but it is the heart of the legend. Visitors who come for the romance of the name are walking into her story too, set in the country that was once the homeland of the Osage.

## Stone and Color

Long before the weddings, people came for the cave itself. It has drawn visitors since the 1850s, admired for its speleothem formations and their mineral coloring. The cave runs close to a mile in length, but only about a quarter of it is open to the public, because underground lakes block the way. One of them, Mystery Lake, is an 18-foot-deep pool that formed when groundwater rose after the completion of Bagnell Dam, the dam that created the Lake of the Ozarks. Bridal Cave opened for public tours in 1948, and the second half of the tour route opened around 1990, after a new tunnel was dug in the late 1980s.

## Thousands of Vows

Weddings take place in the Bridal Chapel, the cave's second room, where a prominent drapery formation known as the pipe organ hangs behind the couples. The tradition began with that first ceremony in June 1949, just a year after the cave opened, and it continues today. Thousands of couples have chosen to begin their marriages underground, in the cool dark of the Ozarks, surrounded by stone that took ages to form. Few places carry a name so literally.

## Flight Context

Bridal Cave is at 38.0606°N, 92.7931°W in Camden County, Missouri, a few miles north of Camdenton in the Lake of the Ozarks region. The cave itself is underground and invisible from the air, so navigate by the lake: from 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, the long, winding arms of the Lake of the Ozarks and the town of Camdenton make clear references. Lee C. Fine Memorial Airport (KAIZ) near Lake Ozark lies about 12 nm to the east. Summer brings heavy boat traffic, haze and afternoon thunderstorms; fog can form over the lake's coves on cool mornings.
