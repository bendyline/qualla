# Party Cove

Officially Anderson Hollow Cove inside Lake of the Ozarks State Park, Party Cove draws hundreds of roped-together boats at a time on summer weekends, a floating party The New York Times called the oldest of its kind in the country, and one state officials have also called the most dangerous spot on the lake.

**Location:** 38.1021, -92.6558

**Categories:** Bodies of water of Camden County, Missouri, Coves of the United States, Lake of the Ozarks, Tourist attractions in Camden County, Missouri

## Introduction

On the map it is Anderson Hollow Cove, a narrow finger of water tucked inside Lake of the Ozarks State Park. Almost nobody calls it that. To the boaters who pack it on summer weekends, it is Party Cove, and The New York Times once described it as the "oldest established permanent floating bacchanal in the country." The cove is only about a mile long and 200 yards wide, yet on a busy summer weekend the Water Patrol has counted anywhere from 500 to 1,000 pleasure boats in it at once and has estimated up to 3,000 over a Fourth of July weekend, many of them lashed together into one enormous, noisy raft.

## A Party Looking for a Home

The gathering did not start here. Since the 1960s, various spots around the Lake of the Ozarks have carried the Party Cove name, but the informal event kept getting pushed out by private landowners. In 1996 it found a place that stuck: Anderson Hollow Cove, at the 4-mile marker of the lake's Grand Glaize Arm, about a mile south of the Grand Glaize Bridge. This time the party stayed put. What had been a wandering event became a fixture, a summer ritual with a permanent address on the water.

## Running the Gauntlet

The ritual has its own rules. Boats line up in two long rows, many of them roped together in the practice known as rafting up. Newcomers motoring in between the rows run a gauntlet of water cannons and taunts, some of them urging the arrivals to strip. Despite its reputation for wild behavior, The Kansas City Star reported that most of the crowd is male, with the atmosphere of a fraternity house afloat. The party has drawn national attention, turning up on A Current Affair and on adult-oriented cable programs, and the Netflix series Ozark mentions it in several episodes, cementing the cove's image as the lake's unruly heart.

## The Cost of the Party

Behind the noise lies a serious safety problem. Missouri officials have called the area around the cove the most dangerous in the entire lake. Policing it has been a challenge: at the time these problems drew attention, the state had just 18 officers assigned to the whole Lake of the Ozarks, and they had historically focused on wake restrictions, boat-driving violations and boating while intoxicated. Efforts to ban rafting up failed to pass the Missouri General Assembly. Then came the summer of 2007. A soldier from Fort Leonard Wood suffered a broken neck in the cove area, and in separate incidents a soldier drowned at the cove and a civilian employee from the base drowned after falling from a dock at a restaurant near it. Two lives were lost that summer, each one a family's grief behind the headlines about a party.

## Crackdowns and Carry-On

The deaths brought action. Missouri announced plans to bring in officers from other districts to crack down on nudity, public sex acts and drug use in the cove. That same year, Major General Bill McCoy, commander of Fort Leonard Wood, declared Party Cove off-limits to Army personnel from the base, citing safety and health concerns. The ban did not last forever. By the April 30, 2010, list of off-limits establishments, Party Cove no longer appeared. Anderson Hollow Cove has been the party's home since 1996, a state-park inlet that on summer weekends can vanish beneath hundreds of boats.

## Flight Context

Party Cove (Anderson Hollow Cove) is at 38.1021°N, 92.6558°W on the Grand Glaize Arm of the Lake of the Ozarks, about a mile south of the Grand Glaize Bridge that carries U.S. 54 near Osage Beach. From 2,000–3,000 feet AGL on a summer weekend, the rafted boats form a dense, unmistakable mass filling the narrow cove. Lee C. Fine Memorial Airport (KAIZ) lies about 5 nm to the east. Stay well clear of the crowd and the heavy boat traffic below, and expect summer haze and afternoon thunderstorms over the lake.
