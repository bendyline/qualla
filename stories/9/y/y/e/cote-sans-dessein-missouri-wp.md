# Cote Sans Dessein, Missouri

A French river town once considered for Missouri's state capital, Cote Sans Dessein moved inland to escape the Missouri River's floods and was drowned out anyway, leaving only its name on a township.

**Location:** 38.5879, -91.9870

**Categories:** Ghost towns in Missouri, Former populated places in Callaway County, Missouri, Missouri River, French colonial history

## Introduction

When Missouri became a state in 1821 and its capital prepared to leave St. Charles, a small river town in what is now southern Callaway County was among the places proposed to replace it. Cote Sans Dessein lost out to Jefferson City, in part, by some accounts, because of well-founded worries that it might flood. Over the following decades the Missouri River proved those worries right, wrecking the town again and again until its people scattered. Today the town is extinct, and its French name survives mainly on Cote Sans Dessein Township.

## Rich Soil on the Floodplain

The town sat on the Missouri River floodplain, directly across the water from Dauphine, also known as Bonnots Mill, in Osage County. A Native American settlement once stood nearby. According to John G. Provines' History of Callaway County, a patent for a survey of the area was granted in 1800 by the Spanish military government of Louisiana, and Provines notes that the area had rich soil and abundant fish and game. Accounts differ on when the town itself began. Some references say a colony of French traders settled at Cote Sans Dessein around 1803, close to the time of the Louisiana Purchase, while other accounts date a mixed French and Native American community here to about 1808. Local historian T. J. Ferguson wrote a first-hand description of the settlement as it looked in 1817.

## A Shore Without Design

The name is often translated as "coast without design". Côte can mean coast or shore, and it is sometimes read as hill; sans usually means without; dessein usually means design or intention. Taken together, the phrase most likely describes a river shoreline, or perhaps a hill or island-like landform that shifted shape as the Missouri's current changed over the years. What the original namers actually intended remains uncertain, since that would require records of their thinking, if any exist. For a town whose fortunes rose and fell with an unpredictable river, a shore without design is a fitting description either way.

## Nearly a Capital

For a while, Cote Sans Dessein had real standing. A post office opened here in 1818, three years before statehood, and the town's name appeared among the candidates for Missouri's new capital when the seat of government was to move from St. Charles. In 1820 it was one of two sites recommended by the commissioners appointed to choose the capital. The decision went to Jefferson City, a short way upriver. Flood concerns are often given as part of the reason, and one county history says the capital would doubtless have been placed here but for a Spanish land claim. It was a practical call, and the river later vindicated it.

## The River Keeps Its Claim

After early floods destroyed the first town, its people rebuilt about one mile inland from the Missouri River shore, in the west-south-central part of the township, roughly two miles southwest of the village of Tebbetts. Old maps record the retreat: those from 1876 show the town on the riverbank, while maps from 1897 place it inland. The move was not enough. Floods substantially destroyed the second location too, and many or most of the survivors moved elsewhere. The post office, open since 1818, closed in 1907. The town was largely abandoned, and its population mostly dispersed.

## Flight Context

The site of Cote Sans Dessein lies at approximately 38.5879° N, 91.9870° W, on the north side of the Missouri River in southern Callaway County, across the river from the village of Bonnots Mill. Nothing of the town remains to be seen; from 2,000–3,000 feet AGL, follow the broad Missouri River floodplain downstream (east) from Jefferson City and look for the farm fields of the river bottoms on the north bank opposite Bonnots Mill. Jefferson City Memorial Airport (KJEF) is about 15 km to the west, Elton Hensley Memorial Airport (KFTT) at Fulton about 28 km to the north, and Columbia Regional Airport (KCOU) about 33 km to the northwest. River valley fog is common on calm mornings.
