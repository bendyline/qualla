# Franklin, Missouri

Now home to about 70 people, the Missouri River town of Franklin was once the eastern terminus of the Santa Fe Trail, the place where William Becknell recruited his traders and a runaway saddler's apprentice named Kit Carson set out for the West.

**Location:** 39.0114, -92.7536

**Categories:** Santa Fe Trail, Cities in Howard County, Missouri, Kit Carson, Missouri River, Westward expansion

## Introduction

"Christopher Carson, a boy about 16 years old, small of his age, but thick set; light hair, ran away from the subscriber, living in Franklin, Howard county, Missouri, to whom he had been bound to learn the saddler's trade." So read the notice placed by David Workman after his apprentice disappeared in the late summer of 1826. The reward Workman offered for the boy's return was one cent. The runaway grew up to be Kit Carson. The town he fled, perched on the Missouri River in the center of the state, stood for a few brief years at the very start of the road west.

## A Notice in the Intelligencer

Franklin was founded by European Americans in 1816 and named for Benjamin Franklin. A few miles northwest of town, William Becknell lived on a farm near a spring called Boone Lick Spring. In 1821 he placed a notice in the Missouri Intelligencer announcing that he was organizing a party to go "westward, for the purpose of trading for horses and mules and catching wild animals of every description." On September 1, 1821, Becknell's party crossed the Missouri River at Arrow Rock and set out along a route that within a few years would be known as the Santa Fe Trail. Becknell is remembered today as the "Father of the Santa Fe Trail," and Franklin, as the trail's eastern terminus, played a major role in the westward expansion of the United States.

## A Boy Among the Boone Lands

Kit Carson was born in Madison County, Kentucky, near Richmond, and came to Franklin as a boy with his family. His father, Lindsey Carson, was a farmer of Scots-Irish descent who had fought in the Revolutionary War under General Wade Hampton, and he had fifteen children: five by his first wife and ten by his second, Rebecca Robinson, Kit's mother. The family settled on a tract owned by the sons of Daniel Boone, who had bought the land from the Spanish during their brief rule here after the Seven Years' War, before the Louisiana Purchase of 1803 brought the territory to the United States. When Kit was eight, his father was killed by a falling tree while clearing land. The loss plunged the family into desperate poverty. Kit left school to work the family farm and help with the hunting.

## The Saddle Shop on the Trail

At fourteen, Kit was apprenticed to David Workman's saddle shop in Franklin. The Santa Fe Trail had opened two years earlier, and many of the shop's customers were trappers and traders, from whom Kit heard stirring tales of the Far West. Carson is reported to have found the work suffocating. "The business did not suit me," he said, "and I concluded to leave." In the late summer of 1826, against his mother's wishes, he slipped away, signing on secretly with a large merchant caravan bound for Santa Fe, purportedly led by the wagon masters Stephen Turley and Jesse B. Turley, friends from nearby Lamine. Workman's one-cent notice followed him into history. For his part, Carson later remembered his old master as "a good man, and I often recall the kind treatment I received."

## What the River Took

Franklin's moment did not last. Between 1826 and 1828, devastating floods of the Missouri River destroyed much of the town, and the worst came in 1828. Its residents rebuilt a short distance away on higher ground, founding New Franklin in 1828. Today Franklin is a small rural city of 0.23 square miles in Howard County, part of the Columbia metropolitan area, and at the 2020 census it had just 70 residents. The river still runs past, broad and brown, as it did when Becknell's traders and a restless teenage saddler's apprentice turned their faces toward Santa Fe.

## Flight Context

Franklin lies at approximately 39.01°N, 92.75°W beside the Missouri River in Howard County, central Missouri. From 2,500–3,500 feet AGL, follow the broad Missouri River valley; Franklin is a very small cluster of buildings in the river bottoms. Jesse Viertel Memorial Airport at Boonville (KVER) is about 5 nm southeast. Columbia Regional (KCOU) is about 28 nm east-southeast, and Marshall Memorial Municipal (KMHL) about 22 nm west-northwest. River fog can form in the valley on calm mornings.
