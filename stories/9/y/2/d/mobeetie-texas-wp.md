# Mobeetie, Texas

Born as a buffalo hunters' camp called Hidetown, Mobeetie was once the rowdiest town in the Texas Panhandle and its first county seat - and it may have named itself after buffalo dung.

**Location:** 35.5333, -100.4392

**Categories:** Cities in Wheeler County, Texas, Texas Panhandle, Old West towns, Buffalo hunting, Former county seats in Texas

## Introduction

When the town applied for a post office in 1879, it ran into a problem: the name Sweetwater was already taken. The residents settled on Mobeetie, believed to be a Native American word for sweetwater. Later came the story, never quite pinned down, that the word actually meant buffalo dung. It is a fitting joke for a place that began as a camp of buffalo hunters and hide skinners on the plains of the Texas Panhandle. Today Mobeetie is a quiet city of 87 people, according to the 2020 census, on Sweetwater Creek and State Highway 152 in northwestern Wheeler County. In the 1870s and 1880s, it was something else entirely.

## Hidetown

Before it had any formal name, the settlement was a buffalo hunters' camp that people unofficially called Hidetown. It grew into a trading post for hunters and trappers beside the Army outpost of Fort Elliott, and for a time it was known as Sweetwater; the outpost beside it began as Cantonment on the Sweetwater. The Jones-Plummer Trail connected it to Dodge City, Kansas, the great cattle-drive town, and stagecoach freight and buffalo skinners came down the trail to reach it. Fort Elliott had been established after the 1874 fight at Adobe Walls to guard the buffalo hunters and keep Native hunting parties out of the Panhandle, at a time when the destruction of the herds was upending the lives of the Plains peoples who depended on them. The fort also fueled the town's growth. As Mobeetie matured, it supplied staple crops to the cattle ranches taking shape within a hundred-mile radius.

## The Hardest Place on the Frontier

On January 24, 1876, gunfire broke out in the town then called Sweetwater. A soldier from Fort Elliott, Melvin A. King, a corporal in Company H of the 4th Cavalry whom legend calls Sergeant King, shot and killed Mollie Brennan, a woman who worked as a dance hall girl. King also wounded Bat Masterson, who then killed him. Accounts of the order of events vary; King may have shot Masterson first and then Brennan. Either way, the Sweetwater Shootout became part of the legend of a rough place. Texas cattleman Charles Goodnight summed up Mobeetie bluntly: "I think it was the hardest place I ever saw on the frontier except Cheyenne, Wyoming."

## First Seat of the Panhandle

With Fort Elliott nearby and Mobeetie serving as a commercial hub, Wheeler County became the first politically organized county in the Texas Panhandle in 1879. Oldham County, centered on Tascosa, followed; Tascosa is now a ghost town. Mobeetie became the first county seat of Wheeler County, and colorful characters followed the business. From 1880 to 1883 the notorious Clay Allison ranched with his brothers twelve miles northeast of town, where Gageby Creek meets the Washita River. Allison is said to have once ridden through Mobeetie drunk and naked, and he married Dora McCulloch there on February 15, 1881. The town reached its peak in 1890, with more than 400 people. That same year, Fort Elliott closed, and the boom days ended with it. A tornado struck on May 1, 1898, bringing further decline, and in 1907 Mobeetie lost the county seat to Wheeler.

## The Town That Moved Two Miles

Mobeetie refused to disappear. In 1929, when the Panhandle and Santa Fe Railway laid tracks nearby, the town picked up and moved two miles to meet them. It grew again, steadily, until the start of World War II brought it to a new peak of around 500 people. Mobeetie can also claim a World Series champion as a native son: second baseman Morrie Rath, born here on Christmas Day in 1886 or 1887, depending on the source, was the leadoff hitter for the 1919 Cincinnati Reds. The modern town covers just 0.6 square miles of semiarid plains. About a mile west along Highway 152, the Fort Elliott historical site marks where the Army post that anchored early Mobeetie once stood. Pampa lies about thirty miles to the west, and Wheeler about ten miles east.

## Flight Context

Mobeetie lies at 35.5333 N, 100.4392 W in northwestern Wheeler County, Texas, along State Highway 152 northeast of Sweetwater Creek. From 2,500-4,000 feet AGL, follow SH 152 between Pampa (about 30 miles west) and Wheeler (about 10 miles east); the small town grid sits near the creek, with the Fort Elliott historical site about a mile west. Nearest airports: Wheeler Municipal Airport (FAA identifier T59, no ICAO code) near Wheeler, and Perry Lefors Field (KPPA) at Pampa. Panhandle winds are strong and spring storms can be severe.
