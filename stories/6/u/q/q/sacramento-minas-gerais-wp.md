# Sacramento, Minas Gerais

A coffee-and-cattle municipality in the Triângulo Mineiro whose old schoolhouse, founded by a teacher who died at 38, still draws visitors from across Brazil.

**Location:** -19.8650, -47.4400

**Categories:** towns, brazil, minas-gerais, history, religion, agriculture

## Introduction

The building has not held a regular class in decades, and people still come to stand in it. They arrive in Sacramento — a municipality of about 26,000 in western Minas Gerais, three hours off the main road from anywhere — to see a schoolhouse founded in 1907 by a local man named Eurípedes Barsanulfo, who taught there for eleven years and then died at thirty-eight. To much of Brazil the place is simply a footnote in the history of education. To the country's several million Spiritists, it is something closer to a source.

## The Teacher of Sacramento

Barsanulfo was born here on 1 May 1880 and never really left. He worked as an educator, a pharmacist and a politician, serving his town as an alderman, and he was known in his lifetime as a Spiritist medium. In 1907 he founded the Colégio Allan Kardec, named for the French writer who codified Spiritism in the mid-nineteenth century. Researchers at the Federal University of Uberlândia regard it as the first regular Spiritist school in Brazil — an ordinary curriculum taught inside an explicitly Spiritist framework, at a moment when doing so in a small Catholic interior town required some nerve. He died on 1 November 1918, during the influenza pandemic that swept the world that autumn. He was thirty-eight years old, and the school was eleven.

## Why People Still Come

Spiritism, as codified by Allan Kardec, holds that spirits survive bodily death, communicate with the living through mediums, and reincarnate across many lives toward moral improvement. It found deeper purchase in Brazil than anywhere else on earth. Within that tradition Barsanulfo is remembered as an *apóstolo da caridade* — an apostle of charity — for the way he taught, treated and gave away. The old college building now holds the Memorial Eurípedes Barsanulfo, the Museu Corina Novelino and the Grupo Espírita Esperança e Caridade, and it receives visitors from across the country. Whether one shares the beliefs or not, the pattern is a familiar human one: a place becomes important because a person was good in it, and the walls take on the residue of that.

## The School That Carried On

Teaching did not stop; it moved. In 1975 Corina and Thomaz Novelino founded the Escola Eurípedes Barsanulfo, which took over the pedagogical work and today serves more than three hundred children. Corina Novelino, whose museum shares the original building, was herself a writer and educator in the tradition. It is a quietly unusual arrangement — the founding institution preserved as memorial and spiritist centre, the actual schooling continuing a few decades and a few streets away under the founder's own name. Neither half pretends to be the other.

## A Valley, a Highway, a Herd

Beyond all this, Sacramento is a working municipality and a large one: 3,071 square kilometres, more than most, holding a population of 26,374 as of 2020. The town centre sits at 832 metres in the Rio das Velhas valley, in the mesoregion of Triângulo Mineiro and Alto Paranaíba and the micro-region of Araxá. It has been a municipality since 1820. Highway MG-190 and the federal BR-262 connect it to Uberaba, 72 kilometres away, where the nearest railhead is; Conquista is 22 kilometres southwest, Araxá 82 kilometres northeast. Belo Horizonte is 453 kilometres off, São Paulo 585. The distances explain a great deal about why the place feels the way it does.

## Coffee at Altitude, Milk in the Pail

The elevation that makes Sacramento cool also makes it good coffee country, and coffee leads a list that runs through potatoes, rice, soybeans, wheat, beans and maize. Around 976 rural producers work some 155,000 hectares, and roughly 3,000 people depend directly on agriculture. Cattle numbered about 22,000 head in 2006, kept mostly for milk and cheese rather than beef — which is the local expression of a much older Minas habit. And there is one more thing on the map worth knowing: Sacramento is one of the six municipalities that share Serra da Canastra National Park, meaning the headwaters of the São Francisco River rise partly within its bounds.

## Flight Context

Sacramento lies at 19.865°S, 47.44°W, with the town centre at 832 m in the Rio das Velhas valley of western Minas Gerais. The surrounding country is high rolling tableland given over to coffee, row crops and dairy pasture — from altitude it reads as a quilt of dark coffee blocks and pale grazing, cut by the red dirt roads that serve them. The BR-262 and MG-190 running northwest toward Uberaba are the clearest linear references. Uberaba Airport (SBUR) is 72 km northwest and is the practical base; Araxá's Romeu Zema Airport (SBAX) sits 82 km northeast. Southeast of town the ground rises toward Serra da Canastra National Park, where peaks approach 1,500 m — plan terrain clearance accordingly if routing that way. A viewing altitude of 3,000–5,000 ft AGL works well. Winter mornings in this part of Minas can produce valley fog that burns off by mid-morning; the dry season delivers long stretches of clear, stable air.
