# Batatais

A sugarcane-belt city in São Paulo named — possibly — for a fire-snake, holding the largest collection of sacred art Cândido Portinari ever painted.

**Location:** -20.8911, -47.5850

**Categories:** Municipalities in São Paulo (state), Populated places established in 1839, Art and artists, Brazil

## Introduction

Nobody can agree on what Batatais means, and the competing answers are far more interesting than a settled one would be. The tidy explanation is agricultural: bandeirantes pushing inland found fields of purple sweet potatoes, batatas, and the name stuck. But local scholars have never been satisfied with potatoes. One theory traces the word to baitatá in the indigenous language once spoken here — a river running over rock, which would describe the waterfalls nearby. Another reaches for mboitatá, the fire-snake, guardian of the fields against burning, and points to the small pockets of gas around the city that can throw up flames on a hot night. A fourth theory belongs to prospectors: batatal was old miners' slang for ground where gold lay close enough to the surface to pick up. This was a stopping place on the road to the Goiás mines, so the men who invented that word certainly passed through.

## The Church Where He Was Baptised

Cândido Portinari was born up the road in Brodowski and baptised in the mother church of Bom Jesus da Cana Verde in Batatais. Decades later, at the invitation of the commission overseeing the church's works, he came back to fill it. The result is the largest body of sacred art he ever produced: fourteen paintings of the Stations of the Cross, five of Bom Jesus da Cana Verde himself, four of Our Lady of Aparecida, and five more drawn from scripture. He supervised the installation personally, fitting the canvases into niches designed to receive them. The altarpiece dominates everything — Bom Jesus da Cana Verde flanked by the twelve apostles, painted with faces so specific they read as portraits and robes so abstract they could be from another century. Portinari spent his career painting Brazilian farm workers. In Batatais he put them in the apostles' place.

## A Mayor Who Became President

Batatais separated from Franca and became an independent municipality on 14 March 1839, and was raised to city status in 1875. Its most consequential resident arrived shortly after. Washington Luís served as mayor of Batatais in 1899, long before anyone would have guessed where he was heading; he went on to become president of Brazil from 1926 to 1930, the last president of the Old Republic, deposed in the revolution that brought Getúlio Vargas to power. The interior of São Paulo produced a remarkable number of Brazil's early republican politicians, and the reason is prosaic: this was where the money was. Coffee wealth bought railways, newspapers, and political careers, and a small city with a courthouse and a plantation elite was a reasonable launching pad.

## Cane Country

The city covers 850 square kilometres at an elevation of 862 metres, and sits squarely in Brazil's sugarcane belt. Coffee shaped the nineteenth century here; cane shaped the twentieth and continues to shape the twenty-first, feeding sugar mills and the ethanol distilleries that make this region one of the engines of Brazil's biofuel industry. Harvest work in the cane fields was for generations brutally hard, done by hand with a machete under a plateau sun, and the mechanisation that has replaced much of it changed the labour question rather than closing it. The fields run to the horizon in every direction, a green corduroy in the wet season and stubble and smoke after the cut. It is the landscape Portinari grew up inside, and it explains the bare feet and bent backs in his paintings better than any catalogue essay could.

## San Gennaro on the Plateau

The 2022 census counted 58,402 people in Batatais, a smaller figure than earlier estimates had projected. A great many of them are descended from Italian immigrants who came to work the coffee at the turn of the twentieth century and stayed, and the city still throws a San Gennaro festival — the feast of Naples, carried nearly six thousand miles across the Atlantic and into the southern hemisphere, celebrated by families whose great-grandparents crossed the Atlantic in steerage. The town also produced an improbable run of footballers: Baldocchi, a world champion with Brazil's 1970 squad, along with Zéca Lopes, Marcelo Batatais, Nhanhá, and Algisto Lorenzato, who was so identified with the place he simply played under the name Batatais.

## Flight Context

Batatais sits at 20.89°S, 47.59°W at 862 m (2,828 ft) elevation, about 12 km northeast of Brodowski on the Ribeirão Preto plateau. From 3,500–5,000 ft AGL the city reads as a dense grid in an ocean of sugarcane; look for the mother church of Bom Jesus da Cana Verde anchoring the central square and the mill complexes with their tall chimneys scattered across the surrounding fields. Nearest paved field is Leite Lopes at Ribeirão Preto (SBRP), roughly 35 km southwest; Viracopos at Campinas (SBKP) is the closest major hub. Terrain is flat to gently rolling with no obstacles above the plateau surface. Dry season from May to September gives the cleanest air, though cane-burning smoke can reduce visibility during the harvest; December through March brings strong afternoon convection.
