# Festa do Peão de Barretos

A dare between exhausted cattle drovers in a São Paulo slaughterhouse town grew into the largest rodeo in the Southern Hemisphere, staged in an arena designed by Oscar Niemeyer.

**Location:** -20.5098, -48.5959

**Categories:** Sports competitions in São Paulo (state), Cultural festivals in Brazil, Rodeos, Festivals in São Paulo, Animal festival or ritual, Rodeo in Brazil

## Introduction

Before there was a stadium, there was a dare. Cowboys who had walked herds down from Minas Gerais, Goiás and the two Mato Grossos would reach Barretos in the afternoon, hand the cattle over to the slaughterhouses, and then look around for the worst-tempered horse in the string. Whoever stayed on the longest won nothing but the telling of it. That afternoon habit is the direct ancestor of an event that now draws roughly a million people to a single São Paulo town every August — the largest rodeo in the Southern Hemisphere, held in an arena designed by the architect of Brasília.

## Where the Cattle Roads Ended

Barretos sits in the northern reach of São Paulo state, and for generations it was where the walking stopped. Cattle fattened on pasture in Minas Gerais, Goiás, Mato Grosso do Sul and Mato Grosso were driven overland to the slaughterhouses here, moved by entourages of riders who spent weeks in the saddle with the herd. The riding contests that filled their idle afternoons were not entertainment for anyone but themselves; they were a way of settling who was best at the thing they all did for a living. Bull riding came later, and from somewhere else. The tradition of climbing onto a bucking bull rather than a horse — harder, heavier, considerably more dangerous — was imported from the United States and grafted onto a Brazilian working culture that already knew what it was looking at.

## Nineteen Fifty-Five

In 1955 a group of bachelors in Barretos organized the first recorded Festa do Peão de Boiadeiro. They called their social club Os Independentes, and more than seventy years later the same organization still runs the festival. The timing was never arbitrary: the event lands each August alongside 25 August, the anniversary of the founding of Barretos, so the rodeo and the city's birthday have always been the same celebration wearing different hats. The fiftieth edition came in 2005; the seventieth in 2025. In that span the festival built a reputation abroad for the quality of its riders and its stock — the cowboys and cowgirls who enter, and the horses and bulls they draw.

## Niemeyer in the Red Dust

Until 1984 the festivities happened in Paulo de Lima Park, in the middle of town, where the cattle expositions of the 1960s through the 1980s were events in their own right. Then Os Independentes did something unusual for a rodeo: they commissioned a permanent home from Oscar Niemeyer. Since 1985 the festival has occupied a 110-hectare park anchored by a stadium built to hold 35,000 people, and the result is one of the odder juxtapositions in Brazilian architecture — the curving concrete vocabulary that shaped Brasília's ministries, wrapped around an arena of churned red dirt, bucking chutes and pickup riders. The scale of the grounds means the festival is not only a rodeo. It is a temporary city, assembled and struck every year.

## The Concert That Started at Midnight

The arena's second life is as a concert stage, and the guest list is not what you would predict from a cattle town: Shakira, Garth Brooks, Alan Jackson, Gloria Gaynor, A-Ha, Mariah Carey. The one the organizers had chased longest was Shania Twain. Negotiations ran more than twenty years before the festival agreed to a fee of R$4 million; the announcement posted to the Festa do Peão page on 26 February 2018 drew 1.3 million views on Facebook alone. One million people came through Parque do Peão that weekend. The Brazilian country star Marília Mendonça and the Swiss pop singer Bastian Baker opened, and Twain herself did not take the stage until midnight, in front of 55,000 people. Brazilian outlets, G1 among them, rated it one of the best performances the festival has ever staged.

## Eight Seconds of Work

Strip away the sound systems and what remains is a competition about staying attached to an animal that would rather you did not. That is worth remembering at Barretos more than at most rodeos, because here the lineage is unusually short and unusually clear. The people in the chutes are a few generations removed from the drovers who invented the contest out of boredom and pride at the end of a cattle road, and the festival still coincides with the founding date of the town those roads led to. A million visitors is a startling number for any event. It is more startling for one that began with a handful of tired riders and a difficult horse.

## Flight Context

Parque do Peão lies at 20.51°S, 48.60°W, on the edge of Barretos in northern São Paulo state, at roughly 550-580 m elevation. The grounds are easy to identify from the air: a 110-hectare block of parking, midway and arena set apart from the dense town grid, with the Niemeyer stadium as the obvious anchor. Chafei Amsei Airport at Barretos (SNBA / BAT) sits just north of the city with a single 1,800 m asphalt runway 08/26 at about 580 m elevation; Ribeirão Preto's Leite Lopes Airport (SBRP) is the nearest larger field, roughly 110 km southeast. Best viewing altitude for the park and the surrounding cane and cattle country is 3,000-5,000 ft AGL. August falls in the dry season here — hazy afternoons from agricultural burning and dust are more likely to limit visibility than cloud. Expect heavy general-aviation and helicopter traffic into Barretos during festival week.
