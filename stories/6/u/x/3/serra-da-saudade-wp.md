# Serra da Saudade

Brazil's least populous municipality is a Minas Gerais hill town of 833 people, named for a word other languages have to borrow.

**Location:** -19.4378, -45.7958

**Categories:** Municipalities in Minas Gerais, Small towns, Brazil, Rural life

## Introduction

Saudade is the word other languages borrow because they have nothing to match it — the ache for something absent, braided with gratitude that it existed at all. A range of hills in central Minas Gerais carries the name. So does the town folded into them. When census takers came through in 2022 they counted 833 residents in Serra da Saudade, fewer than in any other municipality in Brazil. This is not a ghost town or a ruin, and no catastrophe emptied it. It is simply small, has been small for a long time, and shows no particular ambition to be anything else.

## The Fazenda That Named a Town

Before there was a municipality here there were smallholdings and two large estates: the Fazenda do Rancho and the Fazenda Serra da Saudade, the second borrowing its name from the range that wraps the area. The village centre sits at 738 meters, high enough that mornings arrive cool and the horizon stacks itself in ridgelines. Serra da Saudade became a municipality in 1962, carved out of neighbouring Dores do Indaiá, which still lies nineteen kilometers to the southeast down a dirt road. Local tradition attaches an indigenous legend to the name as well, though the fazenda is the documented source. Either way the word fits a landscape people leave and then miss.

## Eight Hundred and Thirty-Three

Consider the arithmetic. The municipality covers 335.66 square kilometers, slightly more ground than Belo Horizonte, the state capital, which packs more than two million people into 331. Out here the density is 2.48 people per square kilometer. Population figures at this scale behave differently than they do in cities. Between February and May of 2024, the local registry office recorded three deaths and a single birth, and the official count slipped from 833 to 831. Two people are a rounding error in São Paulo. In Serra da Saudade, two people are a measurable demographic event — and one that gets discussed.

## Coffee, Beans, and Fifteen Thousand Cattle

Cattle outnumber residents here by roughly eighteen to one. A 2006 survey found about 15,000 head across the municipality, a thousand of them dairy, on land worked by 113 rural producers across some 19,000 hectares. Twenty-five of those farms had tractors. The crops are coffee, beans and corn, the durable trio of the Minas interior, and around 250 people — close to a third of everyone in the municipality — make their living from agriculture. Services account for most of the rest. In 2005 they produced four of the municipality's seven million reais of output, a figure small enough that the school, the clinic and the town hall count among the significant employers.

## Nineteen Kilometers of Dirt

Four municipalities ring Serra da Saudade: Quartel Geral to the north, Dores do Indaiá east, Estrela do Indaiá south, São Gotardo west. The paved link is MG-235, running seventeen kilometers south to Estrela do Indaiá. Luz sits fifty-four kilometers off, first on the MG-235 and then the MG-176. Belo Horizonte is 273 kilometers away, close enough to reach in a morning and far enough that nobody drives it casually. The road east to Dores do Indaiá — the town Serra da Saudade separated from — was still unpaved as of a 2002 survey. Six decades after the split, the mother town remains the nearest neighbour and the rougher drive.

## The Arithmetic of Staying

Poverty here is real and measurable. In 2000 the average monthly income per person was R$217, against a state average of R$276 and a national one of R$297. That put Serra da Saudade below the middle of Minas Gerais, though nowhere near the bottom; the state's lowest that year, Setubinha, came in at R$73. What the statistics cannot register is the thing the name gestures at. Every municipality in Brazil has people who left for Belo Horizonte or São Paulo or the soybean frontier. In a town of 833, you can name every one of them. The hills stay, the coffee comes in, and the count holds — barely, deliberately, one person at a time.

## Flight Context

Serra da Saudade lies at 19.44°S, 45.80°W in the Central Mineira highlands of Minas Gerais, with the village centre at roughly 700–740 meters elevation. There is no airfield in the municipality. Belo Horizonte's Confins (SBCF) and Pampulha (SBBH) airports are about 270 kilometers east-southeast; Araxá (SBAX) and Uberaba (SBUR) lie west and west-southwest. Recommended viewing altitude is 3,000–4,500 feet AGL, low enough to pick out the ridgeline of the Serra da Saudade itself and the thin red line of MG-235 running south toward Estrela do Indaiá. The pattern to look for is negative: a broad tract of pasture, coffee and corn with almost no lights in it after dark. The dry season, roughly May through September, offers the clearest air; the wet season brings afternoon convective build-ups typical of the Minas plateau.
