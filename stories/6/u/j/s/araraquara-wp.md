# Araraquara

A city of a quarter million in central São Paulo state whose Tupi name means lair of the macaws, built by railway workers and orange growers, and known across Brazil as the Abode of the Sun.

**Location:** -21.7939, -48.1758

**Categories:** Cities in Brazil, São Paulo (state), Agriculture, Football, Brazil

## Introduction

The name is the first thing worth slowing down for. Araraquara comes from an expression in the indigenous languages of the region - Tupi-Guarani, or the Língua Geral once spoken across São Paulo, depending on which etymology you follow - built from arara, macaw, and kûara, hole or lair. Lair of the macaws. Whatever flocks gave the place its name are long gone from the city centre, replaced by 250,000 people, orange groves running to the horizon, and a railway heritage that still supplies the local football team its nickname. But the sun the Tupi word carries an echo of has stayed. Araraquara calls itself the Morada do Sol, the abode of the sun, and anyone who has stood in its main square in February will not argue.

## Founded in the Year of the Coffee Frontier

Araraquara was founded in 1817, which makes it old by the standards of interior São Paulo - most of the towns around it are creatures of the late nineteenth or early twentieth century, thrown up as coffee pushed west and the railways followed. The city sits about 270 km north of São Paulo, at the heart of the microregion and mesoregion that both carry its name. What began as a settlement on the coffee frontier became something else entirely across two centuries: a university town, an industrial centre, and one of the highest-scoring Brazilian municipalities on the quality-of-life indices that Brazilians follow closely. Its streets are lined with century-old oiti trees, the dense-canopied shade tree that interior São Paulo planted by the thousand and that turns whole avenues into green tunnels.

## Oranges and Cane

This is citrus country - the heart of the belt that makes Brazil the dominant force in the global orange juice trade, and Araraquara sits at its centre alongside sugarcane. In the harvest months the roads fill with trucks stacked high with fruit, and the processing plants run around the clock. From the air the pattern is unmistakable: dark, regimented rows of orange trees interlocking with the paler green of cane, the two crops carving the red-earth plateau into blocks. It is agriculture at industrial scale, and it has funded the city that stands in the middle of it.

## The Locomotives

In 1950, workers at the Estradas de Ferro de Araraquara - the Araraquara Railroad Company, the EFA - organised a football club. They called it Associação Ferroviária de Esportes, and the nickname wrote itself: the Locomotives. The team plays at the Estádio Dr. Adhemar de Barros, which nobody calls that. Everyone says Fonte Luminosa, luminous fountain, after the landmark nearby. It opened on June 10, 1951, with Vasco da Gama beating Ferroviária 5-0 in the inaugural match; Vasco's Friaça scored the first goal ever recorded there. Floodlights arrived on August 24, 1960, inaugurated with a 3-3 draw against Ponte Preta. The attendance record still stands at 18,041, set on March 22, 1993, when Palmeiras won 1-0. A renovation in 2008 and 2009 pushed capacity past 20,000, and in 2010 Palmeiras liked the ground enough to play its last two home matches of the Série A season there.

## The Women Who Won Everything

The more remarkable Ferroviária story is the newer one. The club organised a women's professional side in 2001, and the trophies started almost immediately: the São Paulo state championship in 2002, then again in 2004, 2005, and 2013. National honours followed - the Copa do Brasil in 2014, the Brasileirão in 2014 and again in 2019. Then came the continent. Ferroviária won the Copa Libertadores Feminina in 2015, and took a second title in the 2020 edition, played in March 2021 after COVID-19 pushed the calendar. Four state titles, three national ones, two Libertadores - all from a railway workers' club in a city of a quarter million, in a country where women's football was formally banned until 1979.

## Who Araraquara Sent Out

The roll of people from here reaches into strange corners of Brazilian life. Careca, the striker who won the Italian league with Napoli alongside Maradona in 1990, is araraquarense. So are Rosa Branca and Roseli Gustavo, Olympic-medal-winning basketball players, and Fernanda Venturini, the volleyball setter who anchored Brazil's national team. Zé Celso - José Celso Martinez Corrêa, who co-founded Teatro Oficina in São Paulo and led it for decades as one of the defining figures of Brazilian stage - was born here in 1937, as did the novelist Ignácio de Loyola Brandão. Herbert Richers built the country's most recognisable film-dubbing studio; his name, spoken at the end of a dubbed film, is a piece of Brazilian audio folklore. More recently, the singer Liniker - who in 2022 became the first openly trans artist to win a Latin Grammy - grew up here too.

## Flight Context

Araraquara sits at 21.79 degrees south, 48.18 degrees west, on the western plateau of São Paulo state at roughly 660 metres elevation, 270 km north-northwest of the city of São Paulo. Bartolomeu de Gusmão Airport (SBAQ) is on the city's edge - named for the Brazilian-born priest who demonstrated a hot-air balloon before the Portuguese court in 1709, which makes it a fitting waypoint. Nearby fields include São Carlos (SDSC) about 40 km southeast and Ribeirão Preto (SBRP) about 70 km northeast. Recommended viewing altitude is 3,500 to 5,000 feet AGL: high enough to read the citrus-and-cane mosaic, low enough to pick out the Fonte Luminosa stadium bowl and the old railway yards on the approach from the southeast. Highways SP-310 (Washington Luis) and SP-255 cross here and make good visual references. Winter, from June to September, gives the sharpest horizons; summer afternoons build towering cumulus fast.
