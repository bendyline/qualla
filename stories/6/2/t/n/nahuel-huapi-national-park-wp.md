# Nahuel Huapi National Park

Argentina's first national park began with one man's gift of land and now sprawls across two million acres of Andean lakes, forests, and steppe around a glacial lake said to hide a monster.

**Location:** -41.0000, -71.5000

**Categories:** National parks of Argentina, Protected areas of Río Negro Province, Protected areas of Neuquén Province, Biosphere reserves of Argentina, Valdivian temperate forests, Patagonia

## Introduction

A scientist gave it away. In September 1903, the explorer Francisco Moreno - known to Argentines as Perito Moreno, the "expert" - wrote a letter donating 75 square kilometers of land he had earned mapping Patagonia for the government, asking that it be made a public park. From that gift grew Nahuel Huapi, the oldest national park in Argentina, formally established in its present form in 1934. It wraps around the deep glacial waters of Lake Nahuel Huapi in the foothills of the Patagonian Andes, and at roughly 7,050 square kilometers - close to two million acres - it remains the largest park in the region.

## Three Worlds in One Park

Few protected areas pack such contrast into their borders. Climb high enough and you reach the Altoandino zone, where snow never fully leaves the peaks above 1,600 meters. Descend and the land softens into Andean-Patagonian forest cloaking the lower slopes. Push east and the trees thin into Patagonian steppe, dry and wind-scoured. There is even a sliver of the lush Valdivian rainforest, fed by Pacific moisture that spills over the cordillera. The western half drips with temperate rainforest; the eastern half is xerophytic and sparse. Between these extremes grow the giants of the southern beech family - the lenga, the coihue, the ñire - alongside Chilean cedar and the brilliant red flowers of the notro. The park's wealth of life follows directly from this collision of altitude and rainfall.

## Before the Park

Europeans first reached these shores by accident of conquest. In the summer of 1552, the governor of Chile sent Francisco de Villagra east over the Andes; he crossed at Mamuil Malal Pass and rode south to the Limay River, near the lake. Later came seekers of the Ciudad de los Césares, a phantom city of silver that explorers chased for generations and never found. Jesuit missionaries from the Chiloé Archipelago established a fragile mission on the lakeshore, where quinoa was once cultivated. But the place stayed remote and contested until the late 1800s, when Moreno's expeditions and his work for the boundary commission helped secure Argentine sovereignty over the region - the same labor that earned him the land he would give back.

## The Monster in the Lake

Every great lake seems to want a monster, and Nahuel Huapi has Nahuelito. Reported since the 1920s, it predates Scotland's Nessie and the dinosaurs of Arthur Conan Doyle's The Lost World, which makes it one of the oldest such legends in the world. The descriptions never settle: a giant humped water snake, a swan with a serpent's head, an overturned boat hull, a floating tree stump. Sightings put its length anywhere from a few meters to fifteen. Whether anything swims down there or not, Nahuelito has become, in the words of one account, an Argentine media star - the imagination's answer to a lake so vast and dark you cannot see its bottom.

## A Park to Move Through

This is a landscape built for travel under your own power. Trails climb toward the glaciers of Tronador, the "Thunderer," an extinct volcano whose ice gives the park its highest drama. Routes thread to mountain refuges at Laguna Negra and Jakob, skirting the granite spires of Cerro Catedral. Ferries cross to Puerto Blest and Victoria Island; the famous Road of the Seven Lakes strings together mirror after mirror of cold blue water. From the modest summit of Cerro Campanario, reachable by chairlift, the whole arrangement lays itself out - lakes Moreno and Nahuel Huapi, peak behind peak - in one of the most photographed panoramas in South America.

## Flight Context

Nahuel Huapi National Park centers near 41.0 degrees south, 71.5 degrees west, straddling the southwest of Neuquén and northwest of Río Negro provinces along the Chilean border. The dominant landmark from the air is Lake Nahuel Huapi itself, a large many-armed glacial lake, with the ice-capped Tronador massif (3,478 m) marking the western frontier. The gateway airport is San Carlos de Bariloche (Teniente Luis Candelaria, ICAO SAZS) on the lake's southeast shore. Recommended viewing altitude is 8,000-12,000 ft to take in the lake-and-cordillera scale; expect strong westerly winds, rapid cloud build-up over the peaks, and frequent winter snow that can reduce mountain visibility sharply.
