# Campinas Cathedral Shooting

On 11 December 2018, five people were killed during Mass at the Metropolitan Cathedral in Campinas, Brazil -- a tragedy that shook a community and drew responses from the Pope and Brazil's president.

**Location:** -22.9060, -47.0606

**Categories:** tragedy, places-of-worship, brazilian-history, community-resilience

## Introduction

They were all praying. On 11 December 2018, just after 1:25 in the afternoon, Mass was underway at the Metropolitan Cathedral of Our Lady of the Conception in Campinas, a city of over a million people in the state of São Paulo. Five men -- ranging in age from 38 to 84, some of them there with family -- would not leave the cathedral alive. Four others were wounded. What should have been an ordinary Tuesday afternoon of worship became one of the deadliest attacks on a house of faith in Brazilian history.

## The People Who Were Lost

The five victims were Cristofer Gonçalves dos Santos, 38 years old; Sidnei Vitor Monteiro, 39, whose mother was among those wounded in the attack; Elpídio Alves Coutinho, 67; José Eudes Gonzaga Ferreira, 68; and Heleno Severo Alves, 84, who had traveled from the neighboring city of Indaiatuba. They were ordinary people gathered in an ordinary act of devotion. One was young enough to have decades ahead of him; another had lived long enough to see his great-grandchildren. What they shared was the simple, unremarkable decision to attend midday Mass on a Tuesday -- a decision that should never have carried any risk at all.

## A Sacred Space Shattered

The Metropolitan Cathedral of Campinas, formally dedicated to Our Lady of the Conception, stands in the heart of the city as it has for generations. It is a place where families mark baptisms and funerals, where holidays are observed and daily routines include a few quiet minutes in the pews. The violence that erupted there was premeditated -- investigators found diary entries dating back a decade -- but the motivation remains unclear. The gunman, who took his own life at the scene, had no connection to the victims and no prior criminal record. The randomness of his target made the tragedy harder to absorb, not easier. That it happened in a cathedral, a space people enter precisely because they believe it to be safe, deepened the wound.

## A Community Responds

The day after the shooting, the cathedral held a memorial Mass. Father Rafael Capelato addressed the grieving congregation directly: "We know that you, family members, are crying -- and so are we." Campinas Mayor Jonas Donizetti declared three days of official mourning. Governor-elect João Doria and President Michel Temer both expressed their grief publicly. The priest who had been saying Mass just before the attack, Father Amaury Thomazzi, posted a video asking for prayers -- not only for the victims and their families, but also, in an extraordinary act of pastoral compassion, for the gunman himself. Pope Francis sent a telegram to the Archdiocese of Campinas urging the community to seek strength in faith and asking that "love prevail over hate and revenge." The Vatican's message, signed by Cardinal Pietro Parolin, invited everyone to find comfort despite the hour of testing.

## What Remains

The Metropolitan Cathedral still stands in central Campinas. Mass is still celebrated there, as it was the very next day after the shooting and every day since. The building's endurance as a place of worship is itself an answer to the violence that briefly overtook it -- not a defiant one, but a quiet, persistent one. The five men who died are remembered by their families and by a city that mourned them collectively. Campinas, a vibrant university city known for its technology sector and its cultural life, carries the memory of 11 December 2018 alongside everything else it is. The cathedral's doors remain open.
