# Serra do Mar State Park

Brazil's largest Atlantic Forest protected area, where 332,000 hectares of ancient escarpment forest shelter half the biome's mammal species, protect São Paulo's water supply, and connect conservation corridors from Rio de Janeiro to Paraná.

**Location:** -23.9680, -46.6499

**Categories:** conservation, nature, ecology, parks

## Introduction

Ninety-two percent. That is the share of amphibian species in the Serra do Mar that exist nowhere else on the planet. It is a number that makes biologists pay attention -- and one that explains why this 332,000-hectare state park, draped across the coastal escarpment of São Paulo like a green curtain between city and sea, has become one of the most important conservation sites in the Southern Hemisphere. Created in 1977 as a byproduct of highway construction, the Serra do Mar State Park has grown into something its founders likely never imagined: the largest continuous protected area of Atlantic Forest in Brazil, and a lifeline for a biome that has lost nearly 90 percent of its original cover.

## An Escarpment Between Worlds

The Serra do Mar mountain range runs along Brazil's southeastern coast like a rampart, separating the humid coastal lowlands from the elevated plateau where São Paulo sprawls. The state park follows this escarpment from the border of Rio de Janeiro state southward to Itariri, spanning 25 municipalities. Its terrain is defined by verticality: steep slopes catch Atlantic moisture, generating rainfall that feeds the streams and rivers supplying drinking water to millions of people in metropolitan São Paulo, the Baixada Santista, the Litoral Norte, and the Paraíba Valley. The park is not merely a refuge for wildlife. It is infrastructure -- a water factory whose forested slopes stabilize the hillsides and moderate the regional climate.

## The Last Great Fragment

The Atlantic Forest once stretched across 1.3 million square kilometers of Brazil's coast. Today, roughly 12 percent remains. What survives in the Serra do Mar is significant not just for its size but for its continuity -- an unbroken corridor of forest connecting the serra's ridges from Rio de Janeiro through the Vale do Ribeira to Paraná state. This connectivity matters. Isolated forest fragments lose species over time as populations shrink below viable thresholds. The Serra do Mar corridor allows genetic exchange across a vast range, sustaining populations of 373 bird species, 111 mammal species, and over 1,265 plant species. In some areas, a single hectare can hold more than 450 tree species. The park forms part of the 221,754-hectare Bocaina Mosaic, created in 2006, and contributes to the UNESCO World Heritage-listed Atlantic Forest South-East Reserves.

## Lives Woven Into the Forest

Conservation in the Serra do Mar is complicated by the fact that people live here -- and have for centuries. Quilombola communities, descended from enslaved Africans who escaped captivity and built settlements in the forest's interior, maintain traditional land-use practices that have coexisted with the ecosystem for generations. Indigenous Amerindian groups hold ancestral claims to portions of the park. Caiçara communities along the coast, and caipira communities in the interior, have developed subsistence patterns with low ecological impact. UNESCO has recognized the authenticity of these settlements, noting that quilombola, caiçara, and indigenous groups maintain traditional practices and ways of life within the park's boundaries. Managing a protected area that is also a homeland requires balancing ecological preservation with cultural continuity -- a challenge the Forestry Foundation of São Paulo, which administers the park, navigates daily.

## The Highway That Built a Park

The Serra do Mar State Park owes its existence to an act of destruction. When the BR-101 coastal highway was built in 1977, cutting through the escarpment's forests, the state of São Paulo created the park to protect the surrounding landscape from further development. It was expanded in 2010 to its current 332,000 hectares. The irony is deliberate: a road that opened the coast to cars and trucks also triggered the preservation of the forest that road bisected. Today, the park's escarpments dominate the coast of São Paulo, visible from the highways that motivated its creation. The forest holds the slopes in place, filters the water, shelters species found nowhere else, and quietly performs the ecological work that keeps one of the world's largest metropolitan regions livable.

## Flight Context

Located at 23.97°S, 46.65°W along the São Paulo coast. From altitude, the Serra do Mar escarpment appears as a sharp green boundary between the densely urbanized São Paulo plateau and the narrow coastal lowlands. The forest cover is visibly continuous -- a rare sight in the fragmented Atlantic Forest biome. Look for the contrast between the dark green canopy and the lighter coastal development of Santos and São Vicente below. Nearest airports: Congonhas-São Paulo (SBSP), Guarulhos International (SBGR), and Santos Dumont (SSZ). Recommended viewing altitude: 5,000-8,000 feet for the full escarpment panorama.
