# Basilica of the Eternal Father, Trindade

The only basilica on earth dedicated to the Divine Eternal Father grew out of a clay medallion found in a Goiás creek bed — and now receives more than four million pilgrims a year.

**Location:** -16.6607, -49.4867

**Categories:** Basilica churches in Brazil, Roman Catholic church buildings in Goiás, Pilgrimage sites, Religious festivals, Brazilian folk Catholicism

## Introduction

It began with something the size of a hand. Around 1840, a farming couple named Constantino Xavier Maria and Ana Rosa turned up a clay medallion near the Barro Preto creek in central Goiás, roughly eight centimetres across, showing the Holy Trinity crowning the Virgin Mary. They had no idea where it had come from. They kept it, prayed the rosary in front of it, and neighbours started coming to pray too. That small gathering has grown into the Romaria do Divino Pai Eterno, a ten-day pilgrimage that draws well over four million people to the town of Trindade every year — and into the only basilica anywhere in the world dedicated to the Divine Eternal Father.

## The Medallion and the Horse

The devotion outgrew the medallion quickly. Constantino built a chapel roofed with buriti palm leaves for the people who kept arriving, and later a larger one on the banks of the Barro Preto. Wanting a proper image for them, he travelled to Pirenópolis to commission one from Veiga Valle, the finest religious woodcarver in Goiás, asking for a version of the Trinity crowning Mary exactly as it appeared on the clay disc. The price came to more than he had brought. So he sold his horse to cover it and walked home to Trindade carrying the carved image himself. That image still stands in the sanctuary. So does the medallion, kept out of sight and brought into the open only on rare occasions — it was displayed in July 2020 for the 180th anniversary of the devotion.

## A Century of Building

Chapels kept giving way to larger chapels. A third was raised in 1876, and in 1912 the first true Shrine of the Divine Eternal Father was inaugurated — the building Trindade still calls the old sanctuary, and the mother church of the parish. In 1943 Dom Emanuel Gomes de Oliveira, then archbishop of Goiás, laid the cornerstone of a new and far more ambitious sanctuary. Twelve years later it was still unfinished. When the Archdiocese of Goiânia was created in 1957, its first archbishop, Dom Fernando Gomes dos Santos, put forward a fresh plan for the building, and by 1974 the novena and the feast of the Divine Eternal Father could finally be held on the site. A major renovation followed in 1994, paid for largely by pilgrims and devotees. In 2006, under Pope Benedict XVI, the church received the title of basilica.

## Four Million on Foot

Trindade has around 142,000 residents. For ten days from late June into early July, it hosts something like thirty times that number. The Romaria do Divino Pai Eterno is one of the largest religious gatherings in Brazil and by far the biggest in the centre-west, and its most recognisable feature is the walking. Pilgrims cover the roughly eighteen kilometres from Goiânia along the GO-060, a road so completely given over to this that it is officially the Rodovia dos Romeiros — the Pilgrims' Highway. Fourteen enormous painted panels by the artist Omar Souto line the route, turning a state highway into a Way of the Cross. People walk it in the dark to beat the heat, in family groups, pushing strollers, carrying water, some of them barefoot, most of them paying off a promise made in a hard year.

## Ox Carts and Promises

The pilgrimage grew out of the countryside, and it has never let go of that. On 2 July each year the Desfile dos Carreiros fills the streets: a parade of ox-cart drivers, horsemen and muleteers, thousands strong, wooden wheels groaning on the asphalt in a sound that has almost vanished from Brazil. These were the people who first came to Trindade — cattlemen and small farmers crossing the cerrado by cart over days, sleeping beside the road. Their descendants now drive to the outskirts and hitch up the oxen for the last stretch. The devotion is transacted in promises: a cure asked for, a job, a child's safety, and in return a walk, a candle, a cart in the parade, a return trip every year for the rest of a life.

## The Church That Keeps Outgrowing Itself

The pattern that began with a palm-leaf chapel has not stopped. Even the basilica cannot absorb the crowds, and in 2011 a foundation stone was laid on the edge of town for a new sanctuary on an entirely different scale — a cruciform building under a dome about 94 metres high, the equivalent of a thirty-storey tower, designed to seat thousands beneath stained glass and an elevated plaza. Work started in 2012 and has run for well over a decade without a firm completion date, funded as everything here has always been funded, by the people who come. Whether it opens next year or the year after, the arithmetic behind it is the same arithmetic that drove Constantino Xavier Maria to build a bigger chapel in the 1840s: more people keep arriving than the building can hold.

## Flight Context

Located at 16.66°S, 49.49°W in Trindade, Goiás, at roughly 2,600 ft elevation on the cerrado plateau, about 18 km west of Goiânia. The basilica and its plaza are the obvious visual anchor of the town, with the GO-060 running arrow-straight back toward the capital — during the Romaria in late June and early July the highway itself is the landmark, choked with pedestrians and traffic for its full length. This sits inside Goiânia's terminal airspace: Santa Genoveva (SBGO, elevation 2,449 ft) is 18 km east and its arrivals and departures dominate the area, so coordinate with approach before overflying. Brasília / Presidente Juscelino Kubitschek (SBBR) is the nearest large alternate, about 200 km east. Sightseeing altitude 2,000-3,000 ft AGL where cleared; expect temporary restrictions and heavy helicopter activity during the pilgrimage. Dry season May-September gives the clearest air.
