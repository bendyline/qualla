# Goiânia

Goiânia is Brazil's great planned city that nobody planned for - a 1930s art-deco experiment that grew wildly past its blueprint and became the green capital of the Central-West.

**Location:** -16.6806, -49.2564

**Categories:** cities, brazil, goiania, planned-cities, capitals, art-deco

## Introduction

There was no city here in 1932. Just pasture and cerrado scrub belonging to a farm owner named Andrelino Rodrigues de Moraes, who donated roughly 242 acres of his land to the state of Goias so that Governor Pedro Ludovico could build a new capital from nothing. The architect Attilio Correia Lima drew the plan. He drew an art-deco city of concentric rings radiating from a central plaza, with tree-lined avenues in the shape of a spoke, the seats of state and municipal government standing face to face across the Praca Civica. Twenty-two of those first buildings are now on Brazil's National Heritage list. The plan accommodated 50,000 people. Goiania currently holds 1,536,097 in the city proper and nearly 2.9 million in the metropolitan area. Everything Correia Lima drew is still there, with about 30 times as many people walking around it.

## A Name Nobody Agreed On

The name came from a newspaper contest held in 1933. Readers submitted candidates: Petronia, Americana, Petrolandia, Goianopolis, Goiania, Bartolomeu Bueno, Campanha, Eldorado, Anhanguera, Liberdade, Goianesia, Patria Nova. One theory holds that the winner was taken from the 1896 epic poem Goyania by Manuel Lopes de Carvalho Ramos, published in Porto in a tiny print run. Another proposes the professor Alfredo Faria de Castro combined Goias with the prefix neo - New Goias, the modern city. There is a Tupi-Guarani word, goyanna, that may also be involved. In 1935, Pedro Ludovico used the name Goiania for the first time in an official decree. Everyone has been arguing about which story is correct ever since. The city does not particularly mind.

## The Art Deco Core

If you walk through central Goiania today, past the new concrete towers and the shopping-mall glass, you find pockets of something older and stranger: clean white buildings with vertical ribs, rounded corners, decorative grillework, and lettering in fonts that belonged to the 1940s. The first buildings of the city were designed in art deco, a style that was meant to signal modernity but now feels almost nostalgic. They were built mostly in the 1940s and 1950s. The National Institute of Historical and Artistic Heritage has recognized 22 of them as heritage sites. Many are in disrepair - maintenance budgets are always the first casualty of municipal politics - but the ensemble still reads, if you know how to look: a young city trying to declare itself serious, built in a style that was already slightly out of date when the workers laid the cornerstones.

## September 13, 1987

Goiania's most famous date is not a municipal holiday. On September 13, 1987, two scrap collectors entered an abandoned medical clinic in the Aeroporto district and removed a radiotherapy machine containing 93 grams of caesium-137 in a salt form. The material gave off a soft blue glow when exposed to air, and over the next two weeks it spread through three neighborhoods - Aeroporto, Central, and Ferroviarios - as people passed fragments to family and friends, fascinated by the glow. Four people died, including the six-year-old Leide das Neves Ferreira. About 112,000 people were tested for contamination. Several city blocks had to be demolished, topsoil stripped, personal belongings incinerated. The International Atomic Energy Agency called it one of the world's worst radiological incidents. The original clinic site is now occupied by the Goiania Convention Center, which holds agricultural trade shows where nobody discusses what happened on the ground beneath the foundations.

## The Spring Capital

Goiania calls itself the spring capital, the capital of flowers. The name is not just boosterism. Correia Lima's plan left more land for trees than almost any other Brazilian city's: about 30 percent of the city area is tree-covered, roughly 3.75 square kilometers of the 11 square kilometers of the original plan. Streets are lined with tropical fruit trees. The Bosque dos Buritis - Buriti Grove - covers 140,000 square meters in the center with its distinctive buriti palms and their yellow fruit. Parque Areiao, in a wealthy district, is dense enough that monkeys still live there. Parque Vaca Brava in the prosperous Setor Bueno holds a lake surrounded by jogging paths. There was a claim that Goiania had the largest green area per inhabitant in Brazil, second in the world only to Edmonton - satellite imagery suggests that claim may need updating. The green has been pushed toward the periphery as the city sprawls. But the city's bones are still green.

## Cars, Bus Lanes, and DJ Alok

Goiania has the highest number of cars per capita of any Brazilian metropolis - over one million registered vehicles - and the traffic shows it. The railway closed in the 1970s, though the art-deco station still stands, open to the public as a relic of an earlier plan. Buses are the only public transport. Anhanguera Avenue carries a dedicated articulated-bus lane installed in the late 1990s, with stations every half kilometer - still a distinctive silver stripe through the center of the avenue. The rock music scene emerged in the 1980s as a response to the dominant sertanejo country style that Goias has long been associated with; sertanejo remains the icon of the region's culture, connected to countryside life, while rock defined an urban, Goianiense identity. The city is also the birthplace of the electronic DJ Alok, one of the highest-earning DJs in the world. A planned city kept unplanning itself into something unexpected.

## Flight Context

Goiania sits at 16.68°S, 49.26°W at 749 m elevation on Brazil's Central Plateau, area approximately 739 km². Santa Genoveva International (SBGO) is the main airport on the northeast side; the current terminal opened in May 2016 with four gates, 23 check-in counters, and capacity for 6.5 million passengers. Aerodromo Nacional de Aviacao handles general aviation. Cruise at 5,000-7,000 feet to see the concentric radial street plan centered on Praca Civica where Avenida Goias (north-south) meets Avenida Anhanguera (east-west). The Meia Ponte River crosses the city. Climate is tropical wet/dry (Koppen Aw): wet Oct-Apr with 1,300 mm annual rainfall, dry May-Sep. Record high 41.2°C on October 7, 2020. Brasilia (SBBR) is 200 km northeast; Sao Paulo Guarulhos (SBGR) is 780 km south.
