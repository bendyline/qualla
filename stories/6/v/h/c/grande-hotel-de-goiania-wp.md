# Grande Hotel de Goiânia

The first hotel in Brazil's newest capital, a three-storey Art Deco box that Claude Lévi-Strauss saw in 1937 and hated - and that Goiânia has been arguing over ever since.

**Location:** -16.6756, -49.2575

**Categories:** Art Deco architecture, Hotels in Brazil, Historic buildings, Goiás

## Introduction

In 1937 Claude Lévi-Strauss came up onto Brazil's central plateau and found what he described as endless flatlands, “half dead ground, half battlefield,” staked out with telegraph poles and surveyors' markers. A hundred or so brand-new houses stood scattered at the four corners of the horizon. “The biggest of these was the hotel,” he wrote in Tristes Tropiques, “a square box of cement, with the look of an air-terminus or a miniature fort.” Goiânia had exactly one hotel that year. The French anthropologist was writing about the Grande Hotel, which had opened its doors on 23 January, and he meant none of it kindly.

## A Bastion of Civilization, Literally

Lévi-Strauss called the building “the bastion of civilization” and then made clear how literally he intended the phrase. Goiânia had not grown; it had been decreed. For two centuries the state of Goiás governed itself from a sleepy colonial town of stucco porticos and pebble-paved streets, far inland and effectively cut off from the coast, and the administration under Pedro Ludovico Teixeira wanted a clean slate. Attilio Corrêa Lima drew it for them. Born in Rome in 1901, trained as an engineer-architect, sent to Paris to study urbanism, he was handed the plan of an entire capital as his first commission and laid out residential, administrative, commercial and industrial zones across open grassland as though working on a drawing board. Among the first structures anyone troubled to build was somewhere for outsiders to sleep.

## Sixty Rooms and Hot Water

Construction ran from 1935 to July 1936, and the hotel opened the following January - the third building finished in the new city. Three floors, sixty rooms, four luxury apartments, garages, 2,178 square metres of floor. Listed proudly among the amenities: bathrooms served by both hot and cold running water. On a plateau where the roads were still cart tracks, that detail was the entire argument. The purpose was frankly transactional, and nobody pretended otherwise. Ofélia Monteiro described the hotel's objective as “to facilitate the entrance of elements from outside to the State and to facilitate with good facilities the stay of as many have business dealing with the government.” Goiânia needed engineers, financiers and federal officials to come. More than that, it needed them to stay long enough to be persuaded.

## Where the New City Learned to Dance

The restaurant-bar accomplished what the master plan could not: it made the place sociable. Goianiense society met there, and the hotel hosted the city's first carnival balls, the Police Military Band trading sets with the Jazz Band Imperial. On Saturdays the leaseholder, Maria de Nazaré, played piano for whoever turned up. The guest register filled faster than the street grid. Getúlio Vargas came, the president then remaking Brazil from the top down, and spoke at the Government Palace on 7 August 1940. Pablo Neruda stayed. So did Monteiro Lobato. So, grumbling, did Lévi-Strauss. Standing at the corner of Avenida Goiás and Rua 3, within easy reach of the Praça Cívica and of every road out of town, the Grande Hotel was for a decade the only address in Goiânia that anyone beyond Goiás could name.

## Listed Three Times, Empty Anyway

Recognition arrived in layers. The municipality listed the building as heritage in 1991, the state followed in 1998, and on 18 November 2003 IPHAN, the national heritage institute, folded it into the protected Art Deco architectural and urban ensemble of Goiânia - one of the largest concentrations of the style anywhere in the Americas. Protection has not settled its future. Decades of remodelling stripped the interior, and what survives from 1936 is essentially the facade. The property belongs to the INSS, Brazil's federal social security institute, though the city government occupied it from around 2004 onward for municipal offices. In 2025 a federal court in Goiás ended that arrangement; the repossession was carried out on 30 May, and the building went back to the INSS with its future unresolved. Lévi-Strauss thought it had no history and nothing to soften its emptiness. Ninety years on it has a great deal of the first and, for the moment, rather too much of the second.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 16.676°S, 49.258°W, in downtown Goiânia at roughly 750 metres elevation. Goiânia's Santa Genoveva Airport (SBGO) lies about 5 km northeast; Brasília (SBBR) is 175 km east-northeast. From 2,000-4,000 ft AGL the 1930s plan is unmistakable: Avenida Goiás runs arrow-straight northeast into the Praça Cívica, from which the radial avenues Anhanguera, Tocantins, Araguaia and Independência fan out. The Grande Hotel sits on the Avenida Goiás axis at Rua 3, a low pale block among far taller neighbours. Dry season (May-September) gives the clearest air; wet-season afternoons build heavy convection over the plateau.
