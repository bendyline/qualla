# Melgaço, Pará

In 2013, a United Nations ranking built on 2010 census data placed Melgaço at the bottom of Brazil's Human Development Index - 5,565th out of 5,565 municipalities - a distinction the town has not yet shaken.

**Location:** -1.8039, -50.7119

**Categories:** Municipalities in Pará

## Introduction

A ranking is a blunt instrument for describing a place. The number is 5,565. That is the position Melgaço held on the Human Development Index for Brazilian municipalities published by the United Nations Development Programme in 2013, using 2010 census data. The list runs from one to 5,565. Melgaço is last. Its HDI-M score of 0.418 placed it behind every other Brazilian town, and that fact - more than anything about the town's own history or geography - is what brings most outside attention to this small river municipality in the interior of Marajó. But a number obscures what the people who live here could tell you. The 27,881 residents recorded in the 2022 census live at an average altitude of twelve meters above sea level, on the western edge of the Marajó archipelago, in a territory of 6,772 square kilometers where the urban area occupies less than two square kilometers.

## Geography Made of Water

Melgaço borders five municipalities: Portel, Breves, Bagre, Gurupá, and Porto de Moz, the interior of the Marajó archipelago and the transitional zone between the Amazon's main channel and the island-delta it forms near the Atlantic. The town sits roughly 250 kilometers west of Belém, the state capital, as the crow flies - not far in absolute terms, but measured in practical terms by river and the seasonal rhythm of water levels. The territory is vast and the population is thin: 4.12 inhabitants per square kilometer, an average of 4.85 people per household. For comparison, Brazil's national population density is about 24 people per square kilometer and Brazilian households average fewer than three people. Melgaço is larger than many European countries and emptier than most American states.

## What the HDI Measures

The Human Development Index combines three dimensions: longevity, income, and education. Melgaço's longevity index stands at 0.776 - not catastrophic, comparable to many middle-income places. The income index is 0.454, reflecting a local economy heavily dependent on extractive activities (fishing, açaí, wood) and formal employment in municipal and state services. But the education index is 0.207, and this is where the ranking falls. The Gini coefficient - a measure of inequality - rose from 0.33 in 2003 to 0.55 in 2010, meaning that inequality within this already-poor town grew by two-thirds in seven years. BBC News Brasil, reporting on the town, found that 14.6 percent of children aged six to fourteen dropped out of primary school, one in five residents over fifteen could not read, and adults averaged five years of schooling.

## The Slow Growth

Between the 2010 and 2022 censuses, the population grew 12.4 percent - an annual geometric rate of 0.98 percent, faster than Pará state as a whole, which grew about 0.6 percent a year. The demographic profile is young: 38.54 percent of residents were under fifteen in 2022, and only 3.55 percent were sixty-five or older. This is a community where the median age is probably not yet twenty, where most households contain children, where the demand for schools and health services runs perpetually ahead of what the local government budget can fund. The sex ratio skews male - 109.43 men per 100 women - likely reflecting a pattern common to extractive-economy communities, where men work in fishing, wood, and river transport while women more often migrate to larger towns for education and service-sector work.

## A Place, Not a Statistic

Melgaço was part of the microregion of Portel in the old mesoregion of Marajó until 2017, when the IBGE redrew Brazil's territorial divisions and Melgaço found itself in the immediate and intermediate geographical region of Breves. The redrawing reflected practical realities - Breves, to the north, is the larger commercial center that actually anchors the economic life of this stretch of the archipelago. For most purposes, Melgaço looks to Breves, not to distant Belém. The day-to-day life of the town is shaped by the rivers that border it, the rainy season that floods the floodplains three or four months each year, the açaí harvest that comes out of the forests, the boats that bring goods upriver and take fish down. The HDI ranking is real, and its consequences are real. But it is not the whole of what Melgaço is. The 27,881 people who live here have names, histories, families, and a territory of forest and water that is, by any measure other than human welfare indices, one of the most biologically productive places on Earth.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.80 degrees S, 50.71 degrees W on the western edge of the Marajó archipelago in Pará, about 250 km west of Belém. The town sits at an average altitude of just 12 meters above sea level, surrounded by the complex channels of the Amazon delta. Best viewed from 3,000-6,000 feet AGL - the labyrinthine river system and extensive floodplain forests dominate the view. Nearest significant air connection is Belém/Val de Cans International Airport (SBBE) to the east.
