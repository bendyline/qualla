# Mangueirão

A stadium that took decades to finish, half-built for years with its exposed concrete painted in Marajoara motifs, now seats over 53,000 and anchors football in the Amazon.

**Location:** -1.3811, -48.4440

**Categories:** Sports venues completed in 1978, Paysandu Sport Club, Clube do Remo, Tuna Luso Brasileira, 1978 establishments in Brazil

## Introduction

For years the stadium had a nickname that worked as gentle mockery: bandola. Half-built. Unfinished. Only one side of the bleachers covered and painted, and because the painting borrowed from the geometric motifs of Marajoara pottery - the pre-Columbian ceramic tradition that defined the mouth of the Amazon - even the partial stadium had something distinctively Paraense about it. The rest was bare concrete, open to the equatorial sky. That was Mangueirão in the decades after 1978, when its first stage of construction was finally declared complete - eight years after the project had started, and well short of the capacity originally planned. Today, after two major renovations, the Estádio Olímpico do Pará seats 53,645 people. That makes it the eighth-largest football stadium in Brazil.

## Alacid Nunes Wants 120,000 Seats

The architectural drawings are dated 1969. The architect was Alcyr Meira, a local; the ambition belonged to the state governor of the era, Alacid Nunes, who ordered a stadium for one hundred and twenty thousand spectators. In a country obsessed with football, in a region Brazilian footballing culture had traditionally overlooked, this was a statement: the Amazon would have a capital stadium worthy of the Maracanã. Construction began in 1970. The ditch was dug, the playing field laid down, the general bleachers begun. Then the numbers were revisited. A year in, the project was scaled back from 120,000 to 70,000 - still immense, but closer to what the budget and the political situation could support. In 1971 construction resumed with the first structural module. And then it slowed. And slowed. And ran, in chunks and pauses, for most of a decade.

## The Mesquita Goal

The first official match was played on March 4, 1978. A Pará All-Stars team - the best players drawn from across the state - beat a Uruguay national youth team 4-0. The first goal scored in the stadium belonged to a Paraense player named Mesquita, a detail that still matters in a city where football history is guarded carefully. An unofficial opening the day before had pitted Clube do Remo against Operário: Remo won 2-0. The stadium was soon hosting the Re-Pa, the derby between Remo and Paysandu Sport Club that is one of the oldest and fiercest rivalries in Brazilian football. Tuna Luso, the third major Pará club, also played there. Mangueirão became what Alacid Nunes had always intended: a shared home for the state's football, a symbolic center where every significant match passed through.

## 57,930 and Boca Juniors

The first major renovation happened between 2000 and 2002, adding the missing covered bleachers and installing an athletics track. On May 5, 2002, the reinaugurated stadium hosted Paysandu versus Remo for the Campeonato Paraense - the old derby, same two clubs, but now in a completed building. The match ended 2-2. The stadium was officially renamed for the journalist Edgar Augusto Proença, though locals still called it Mangueirão. The biggest crowd of the renovated stadium came a year later (the all-time record, about 65,000, had been set at a Re-Pa final on July 11, 1999): May 15, 2003, when Paysandu lost to the Argentine giants Boca Juniors in a Copa Libertadores match. 57,930 people crowded in. Between 2002 and 2009, the stadium hosted the Grande Prêmio Brasil Caixa de Atletismo - the major national track and field meet - and in 2005 set a Latin American record for attendance at an athletics competition, with more than 40,000 spectators. A stadium that had taken so long to finish was, once finished, consistently filling.

## The FIFA Upgrade

On February 23, 2021, a second major renovation was announced. The scope was ambitious: bring Mangueirão up to FIFA standards, install a Video Assistant Referee control center, modernize the infrastructure top to bottom. Two test events came close to the reopening - the March 26 and 29 semi-final matches of the 2023 Copa Verde, both Remo versus Paysandu, the derby that Mangueirão exists for. The official reopening came on April 9, 2023, in a Campeonato Paraense match. Paysandu won 1-0. The current capacity of 53,645 - lower than the first renovation's peak - reflects the trade-off between seats and seat quality, the shift toward all-seater standards, and the prioritization of sightlines over raw counts. What started as a governor's dream of 120,000 seats ended, more than half a century later, as a stadium that balances scale with function. It no longer needs to prove the Amazon takes its football seriously. It just needs to host the next derby.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.38 degrees S, 48.44 degrees W in northern Belém, about 10 km northeast of the historic city center and a few kilometres east of Guajará Bay. Best viewed from 2,000-4,000 feet AGL on approach to Belém/Val de Cans International Airport (SBBE), which lies about 3.5 km to the west. The stadium's distinctive oval shape and large roof make it an easily-identified landmark against the dense urban grid of northern Belém.
