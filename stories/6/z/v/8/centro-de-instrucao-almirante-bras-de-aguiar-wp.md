# Centro de Instrução Almirante Brás de Aguiar

The Brazilian Navy academy 100 kilometers up the Amazon where merchant marine officers earn their officer's bars and their STCW certificates.

**Location:** -1.3700, -48.4790

**Categories:** military, maritime, education, brazil, amazon, merchant-marine

## Introduction

Brazil trains most of its naval officers at Rio de Janeiro. The merchant marine half of the sea service, though, has its northern home in an unlikelier place: Belem, 100 kilometers upriver from the Atlantic, in a tropical rainforest city where afternoon thunderstorms drown the parade ground on a nearly daily schedule. The Centro de Instrucao Almirante Bras de Aguiar, known as CIABA, has prepared ship's officers here since 1892. Today it sends midshipmen to sea carrying degrees in Nautical Science and certificates recognized by the International Maritime Organization.

## Born in 1892

CIABA's lineage traces to Decree number 101 of 13 October 1892, which established a merchant marine school in Para. Its first headquarters sat in a room of the old Navy Arsenal Inspectorate, itself occupying a former convent, Sao Boaventura dos Religiosos da Conceicao do Beira de Minhos. The command of the Brazilian Navy's 4th Naval District still operates from that building today. Decree 1362 in 1893 reorganized the school into the Machinists and Pilots School, reflecting the technological shift from sail to steam that was then remaking merchant fleets around the world. In 1907 new subjects were added, including commissary work and radiotelegraphy, and the school reorganized again to handle them.

## The Name Behind the Academy

The current name dates from 16 January 1973, when Decree 71,718 designated the institution the Almirante Bras de Aguiar Training Center, following a proposal by Para historian Augusto Meira Filho. Bras de Aguiar was an admiral of the Brazilian Navy whose career linked the old Marinha de Guerra to the modernizing merchant marine; attaching his name to this academy carried forward an unusual blending of military and civilian maritime traditions that CIABA itself embodies. Midshipmen trained here serve as Brazilian Navy reservists during their three-year course and graduate with the rank of Second Lieutenant in the Navy's reserve officer corps, even as they are simultaneously credentialed to serve on civilian merchant ships.

## What Cadets Actually Do

Students spend three years learning seamanship and the operational engineering of ships. CIABA offers two undergraduate degree programs, both culminating in the Bachelor of Science: one in Maritime Studies, which produces deck officers, and one in Marine Machines Engineering, which produces engineering officers. The academy is organized into three battalions across six companies, with assignments random, giving cadets no choice about which unit they join. They stand watches, learn celestial navigation alongside electronic positioning, study marine engineering from boilers to modern diesel-electric plants, and rotate through cargo handling, international maritime law, and the shiphandling that differentiates a mate from a passenger. By graduation, each has logged the sea time required under the International Maritime Organization's STCW Convention, the Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers, without which a modern merchant officer cannot work on international voyages.

## A Mixed Cohort

Belem's merchant marine academy attracts students from across Brazil, and also, notably, from other Latin American countries and several African nations. This international dimension reflects the Brazilian Navy's longstanding exchange relationships, particularly with Lusophone Africa, and the legitimacy that an IMO-recognized merchant marine academy carries in the wider developing world. The Merchant Marine Officer Training School, which CIABA houses under the acronym EFOMM, takes these midshipmen through a three-year curriculum that mixes academic study at a maritime university with practical shipboard rotations and the physical, regimented life of a military cadet. They march, they polish, they stand watches in uniform, and they graduate with a Second Lieutenant's commission in the Navy reserve in addition to their civil mariner credentials.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 1.37S, 48.48W. The academy grounds lie near Belem's Val-de-Cans neighborhood. The academy is visible from approach into Val-de-Cans International (SBBE/BEL), which lies about 2 km southwest of the CIABA grounds. Cruising altitude shows the wide Guajara Bay to the south and the Amazon delta extending north and west. Convective weather year-round, particularly active in the afternoons from December through May.
