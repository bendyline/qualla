# Monte Alegre State Park

Paintings on the sandstone walls here are among the oldest in the Americas, discoveries that rewrote the textbook timeline for when humans first reached the Amazon.

**Location:** -2.0434, -54.1692

**Categories:** State parks of Brazil, Protected areas established in 2001, 2001 establishments in Brazil, Protected areas of Pará

## Introduction

The archaeologist was Anna Curtenius Roosevelt. The cave was the Gruta da Pedra Pintada - the Painted Rock Cave - on the eastern flank of the Paytuna Range north of the Amazon River. The year was the early 1990s, and what her excavations produced upended a textbook consensus that had stood for decades. The Amazon, the consensus had held, was an impossibly difficult environment, and humans had arrived late - drawn in from the highlands only when agriculture made it possible to survive there. Roosevelt's dating suggested something different. The red and yellow figures painted on the cave walls, and the material evidence she pulled from the cave floor, pushed the human presence at Monte Alegre back more than eleven millennia. People had been here when the Amazon was still emerging from the last Ice Age, making marks on sandstone walls that would still be visible ten thousand years later.

## Where the River Bends

The Monte Alegre State Park covers 3,678 hectares of hilly terrain in the municipality of Monte Alegre, Pará - a landscape of mountains and valleys, caves and panels, that sits in the curve where the Amazon bends south before turning east toward its ocean mouth. The park is entirely contained within the larger Paytuna Environmental Protection Area, a nested structure common in Brazilian conservation. The hills here are the remnant of much older geological formations, pushed up as isolated ranges above the surrounding floodplain. The caves cut through sandstone walls that have held the paintings for millennia. The sulphur-breasted parakeet (Aratinga maculata), a small green bird with the yellow throat its name describes, is endemic to this region - found nowhere else on Earth. The park's creation, in 2001, was the first conservation unit in Pará where local community members were involved in the design process from the beginning, a small but significant departure from the top-down model that had dominated Brazilian conservation until then.

## The Rock Face at Serra da Lua

The most dramatic of the painted sites is at Serra da Lua - Mountain of the Moon - where a panel of rock paintings extends for more than two hundred meters along a cliff wall in the Serra do Ererê range. The figures are red and yellow, stylized and often abstract, and the most accessible section can be viewed from the foot of the hill. Most visitors come only this far; the full extent of the panel requires climbing and local guidance. Postcards, tourist brochures, and the internet have reproduced the Serra da Lua images so widely that they have become the visual shorthand for Amazonian rock art. The Painel do Pilão and the Gruta do Pilão hold additional paintings, the cave version ranking among the oldest known archaeological sites in the Amazon basin. The Pedra do Mirante offers a climbable rock with a 360-degree panorama of the surrounding landscape. The Pedra do Pilão provides a lookout over the same country. The caves and panels are scattered across the Paytuna and Ererê ranges, each one its own walk, its own altitude, its own view back to the river.

## Caves with Names

The cave inventory reads like a catalog of geological specificity. The Gruta da Pedra Pintada runs 89 meters deep and sits 120 meters above sea level on the eastern flank of Paytuna. The Gruta Itatupaoca - also known as Gruta da Capela - is 56 meters deep, positioned about 120 meters above the Amazon River on the southern slope of the Ererê Range, with an entrance nine and a half meters high divided vertically by a rocky column. The Gruta do Miritiepé extends 87 meters back into the rock, 60 meters above the Amazon on the northwest flank of Paytuna. Its main gallery runs 32 meters long, well-lit, two and a half meters high and wide, opening into the Folhas Chamber - thirteen meters across and six meters high. And the Gruta do Labirinto - the Labyrinth Cave - is more than 90 meters deep, cut 40 meters above the Amazon on the west flank of Paytuna. Each cave is its own microenvironment, its own acoustic space, its own record of geology and human use.

## The Ecotourism Experiment

Monte Alegre has been a case study in how conservation can work when local people are part of the design. The park was the outcome of studies by the Pará Institute of Economic, Social and Environmental Development (IDESP) at the end of the 1980s, formally established by law 6412 in November 2001. A 2013 adjustment transferred 2,122 hectares to the surrounding Paytuna Environmental Protection Area, reducing the park itself to its current 3,678 hectares. In April 2013 the Peabiru Institute announced five initiatives, supported by TAM Linhas Aéreas, to train local women and young people as ecotourism guides - the Monte Alegre Ecotourism Agents project. The model recognized what most conservation-versus-community conflicts refuse to recognize: that paintings 11,000 years old, on walls the current inhabitants can visit and whose names their grandparents taught them, belong in some real sense to both a world heritage and a local patrimony. Protecting the first depends on honoring the second.

## Flight Context

Located at 2.04 degrees S, 54.17 degrees W in the municipality of Monte Alegre, north of the Amazon River in western Pará. The park occupies a hilly area that rises above the surrounding floodplain; the rock art sites are on sandstone ranges 120-150 meters above sea level. Best viewed from 3,000-5,000 feet AGL to appreciate the relationship between the isolated ranges and the broad Amazon curving below. Nearest airport is Monte Alegre Airport (SNMA), with larger connections via Santarém (SBSN) to the east.
