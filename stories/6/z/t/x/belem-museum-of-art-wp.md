# Belem Museum of Art

A 1,500-work municipal collection housed in a neoclassical 19th-century palace that still functions as Belem's city hall, 1,500 square meters of galleries above an operating government.

**Location:** -1.4557, -48.5023

**Categories:** museums, art, brazil, architecture

## Introduction

In most cities, the building where the mayor works and the building where the art lives are different addresses. Belem does not work that way. The Antonio Lemos Palace, a neoclassical block finished in the late 19th century on the Praca Dom Pedro II, holds both: the offices of the municipal government on the lower floors, and the Belem Museum of Art, known by its Portuguese acronym MABE, on seven exhibition rooms spread across the upper floor and two on the first. You can walk past a clerk processing a building permit and climb a marble staircase to a Benedito Calixto landscape of an Amazon river town. The entrance fee is nothing. The paintings were acquired over more than a century.

## A Palace for a Mayor

Antonio Jose de Lemos, known locally as the Paraense intendant, ran Belem from 1897 to 1911, through the peak of the rubber boom when the city was one of the richest in Brazil. He believed the seat of local government should look the part. The palace built in his name, in neoclassical idiom but with tropical adjustments for light and ventilation, was finished in the late 19th century and today is protected at all three levels of Brazilian heritage registry: federal through IPHAN, state through the Para heritage authority, and municipal. The building was never abandoned and never converted. It has always been city hall, and since 1991 it has also been a museum. The dual function means the visitor experiences the building as it was intended: a working civic palace where art lives above the bureaucracy, rather than as an isolated gallery space.

## A Collection Assembled Over a Century

The MABE as an institution is young, founded in 1991. The collection it holds is not. Belem's city hall began acquiring art in the early 20th century, mostly on the occasion of academic exhibitions held in the capital, and kept buying for decades without anywhere permanent to show it. By 1983 the holdings had grown large enough to justify the creation of a Municipal Pinacoteca, responsible for cataloging and preservation. In 1986 the city created a broader Belem City Museum. In 1989 the Cultural Foundation of the Municipality of Belem, FUMBEL, was established to oversee the whole civic culture portfolio. FUMBEL took over the Pinacoteca and the City Museum, and two years later folded them into the new Museu de Arte de Belem. The collection now contains over 1,500 pieces: paintings, sculptures, drawings, engravings, photographs, and applied art including furniture and porcelain, Brazilian and foreign, produced between the 18th and 20th centuries.

## The Para Eye

The strength of the MABE collection is its early-20th-century Para focus. Belem during the rubber era was a cosmopolitan city where European painters passed through and local painters trained abroad and came home. Antonio Parreiras, the Rio de Janeiro landscape painter, made extensive trips to Para and produced a series of Amazon basin paintings now considered landmark works. Benedito Calixto, who trained in Sao Paulo, painted the riverfronts and bay of Belem with a clarity that has lasted a century. Local Para artists, including Ruy Meira whose career is the subject of an academic study mentioned in the museum's bibliography, built a regional school that absorbed European academic technique and applied it to tropical subjects: caboclo fishermen, manioc markets, the slow river light of the estuary. The 18th and 19th century holdings provide context. The 20th century holdings show what Para looked at and how it chose to see itself.

## Two Satellites in the City

MABE does not confine itself to the palace. It maintains two additional exhibition spaces in Belem. The Municipal Art Gallery handles rotating contemporary shows. The Museum of Popular Art, located in the district of Icoaraci some distance from downtown, specializes in the ceramic traditions that made Icoaraci famous: pottery drawn from Marajoara and Tapajonica indigenous styles, reinterpreted by generations of local potters. Between the three spaces, the institution covers the full sweep of Para visual culture from the 18th century through the present. The palace facility itself totals 1,500 square meters, includes a restoration laboratory, a technical reserve for conservation work, an auditorium, a cinema room, and a specialized library of approximately a thousand volumes in visual arts. Three permanent exhibition rooms and two temporary ones rotate regularly.

## What a City Does With Its Art

The existence of MABE is a small argument about what a Brazilian municipality can do with its cultural inheritance when it takes the job seriously over a long time. Belem began buying art in the early 1900s, during the rubber-boom decades when the city had money to spend and reasons to commission images of itself. The money left. The pictures stayed. For most of the 20th century they sat in storage, or on the walls of offices, or in the homes of officials who agreed to look after them. The work of cataloging, preserving, and showing them to the public came slowly, through three successive institutions built on top of one another. Today they are on display in the building where they were mostly acquired, for the price of walking in. The ancillary benefit is that you get to see a functioning 19th-century Brazilian civic palace from the inside, which is not a common experience.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.46 S, 48.50 W in the historic city center of Belem, Para, Brazil, on the Praca Dom Pedro II near the waterfront on Baia do Guajara. Val-de-Cans International Airport (SBBE) lies approximately 12 km north. Best aerial viewing altitudes are FL100-FL200 for context of the colonial-era grid street pattern and the bay it overlooks. The Amazon estuary is to the west and north. Expect tropical convective weather, especially afternoon thunderstorms in the wet season (January-May).
