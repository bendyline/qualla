# Ver-o-Peso Complex

A 35,000-square-meter architectural and landscape ensemble in Belem, dating from 1625, listed by IPHAN in 1977 as one of the most significant heritage sites in northern Brazil.

**Location:** -1.4536, -48.5036

**Categories:** Belém, Pará, Buildings and structures in Belém, Tourism in Brazil, Buildings and structures in Pará, Tourist attractions in Pará

## Introduction

Count the parts. Boulevard Castilhos Franca, one of Belem's first wide avenues, engineered in the art nouveau taste of the Belle Epoque. The Ver-o-Peso Market, a twelve-sided iron structure imported in pieces from England and New York. The Francisco Bolonha Meat Market, the city's first municipal butchery. Siqueira Campos Square with its 1930 iron clock tower, the clock itself a gift from the intendant Antonio Faciola that arrived by ship from England. The Ver-o-Peso Dock. The Acai Fair. The neoclassical Solar da Beira, now a restaurant. Dom Pedro II Square, the first square the city ever built. Together, these pieces make up what IPHAN formally designated in 1977 as the Ver-o-Peso Complex, a 35,000-square-meter architectural and landscape ensemble on Boulevard Castilhos Franca in Belem's Cidade Velha neighborhood. The complex began to take shape in 1625. It has been accumulating for four centuries.

## A Tax Office That Became a City

The seed was practical. In 1625, Portuguese administrators needed a place to weigh and tax goods arriving at the mouth of the Amazon. They built the Casa de Haver-o-Peso, a commercial tax office whose name, haver-o-peso, meaning to have the weight, eventually contracted into ver-o-peso, to see the weight. Belem was then the newest outpost on the Portuguese Amazon frontier, founded in 1616. The tax office sat at the mouth of the Igarape do Piri, a small tidal channel that let canoes from the interior come right up to the customs house. Goods flowed in: fish, turtle oil, cacao, timber, and the cattle that ranchers on Marajo Island drove to the mainland for slaughter. The tax office stood there for nearly two hundred years, weighing and levying, until the nineteenth century arrived with different ideas about what a riverfront should look like.

## When the Igarape Was Filled

In 1803, during the administration of Marcos de Noronha e Brito, the Count of Arcos, the Igarape do Piri was landfilled. Urban Belem was outgrowing its colonial riverside, and the small tidal channel that had once let canoes come directly to the Casa de Haver-o-Peso was now more obstacle than asset. The fill created the Ver-o-Peso Dock and the Pedra do Peixe, a fish-handling platform built from English lioz stone, a cream-colored limestone favored in Portuguese colonial architecture. The tax office continued operating on the new ground above where the channel had been, but the relationship between Belem and the Amazon estuary had shifted. The city now reached into the river rather than waiting on its banks.

## The Iron Market of 1901

In 1897, the firm La Rocque Pinto and Cia won a public tender to build a new municipal fish market. Municipal Law 173 authorized the project. Henrique La Rocque, the architect, designed a twelve-sided dodecagonal structure in the French art nouveau style of the Belle Epoque. Construction began in 1899. That same year, the old Casa de Haver-o-Peso, which had stood since 1625, was demolished to make room. The new building's veille-montaine zinc structural elements were manufactured in England and New York and shipped by river to Belem, where they were assembled on site. The 1,197-square-meter iron market was inaugurated in 1901, and what Belem had is what most of Brazil could not claim: an industrial-age market hall built to European fashion standards at the edge of the Amazon. In the same period, the Meat Market next door was expanded to match.

## The Clock That Came from England

Siqueira Campos Square, also called Clock Square, was built in 1930 in the low, shaded space between the Iron Market and the river. Its centerpiece is an iron tower topped with a clock that was donated by the city's intendant, Antonio Faciola, and imported from England. The clock has kept time for Belem ever since, its bells marking the hours over the fish stalls and the boats at the dock. The square anchors the complex spatially and temporally. It gives the market a public face that is not commercial, a place to sit in the shade and wait for someone, to meet at four o'clock under the clock, to read the paper while the day heats up. In a complex defined mostly by commerce, Siqueira Campos is the exception that gives the rest its rhythm.

## Why IPHAN Listed It

In 1977, the National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage, known by the acronym IPHAN, formally listed the Ver-o-Peso Complex as a protected heritage site. The designation covered the full 35,000 square meters and recognized that what mattered was not any single building but the ensemble: the boulevard, the markets, the squares, the dock, the neoclassical Solar da Beira, the Acai Fair that still runs its open-air stalls behind the Forte do Presepio. The listing acknowledged that Belem's riverside heritage cannot be edited down to a single jewel. The city grew up along this waterfront in layers, colonial atop tidal channel atop indigenous trade route, and each layer left marks that together tell the story of the Amazon estuary's engagement with the world. The Ver-o-Peso Complex is that story made architectural. Visitors walking its perimeter at dawn, when the fish boats come in and the iron roof catches the first light, are not touring monuments. They are inside a working city that has been working since 1625.

## Flight Context
