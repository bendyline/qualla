# Historic and Geographic Institute of Pará

Founded in 1900 on what Brazilians then believed was the 400th anniversary of their country's discovery, the IHGP keeps the Amazon's memory in a manor house given to it by a grateful city.

**Location:** -1.4555, -48.5040

**Categories:** Research institutes in Brazil, History of Pará, Buildings and structures in Pará, Historiography of Brazil, Non-profit organisations based in Brazil

## Introduction

The date was chosen for its weight. May 3, 1900 - exactly four centuries, by the reckoning then accepted, since Pedro Álvares Cabral's fleet touched the coast of Brazil. In Belém, on the far northern edge of that country, the governor's office decided the moment required an institution capable of holding the past in place. On that single day, three were founded at once: the Academy of Letters of Pará, the Humanitarian League, and the Historic and Geographic Institute of Pará. A civic session convened in the hall of the Peace Theater. Henrique Santa Rosa presided. José Olinto Barroso Rebello gave the speech. And Domingos Antônio Raiol - the Baron of Guajará, then considered the dean of Pará's historians - accepted the presidency of what the commission had drafted that spring.

## The Founding Commission

The names assembled to draw up the first statute read like a cross-section of Pará's intellectual life at the turn of the twentieth century. The Barons of Guajará and Marajó. The politicians Justo Chermont and Arthur Lemos. The historian João Lúcio de Azevedo. The jurist Samuel Wallace McDowell. Canon João Ferreira de Andrade Muniz, representing the church. And, most surprising to modern eyes, Emílio Goeldi - the Swiss naturalist whose name would later grace the Amazon's great museum of natural history, sitting on the committee of a humanities institute. The mix says something about how intellectual work was organized then, when a scientist and a theologian could share a founding table and consider it ordinary. The state government of José Paes de Carvalho supported them. So did two governors who would later be named honorary presidents for their ongoing patronage: Lauro Sodré and Joaquim Magalhães Barata.

## The Manor on Dom Pedro II Square

For its first four decades, the institute was an organization without a permanent home - at one point, after members withdrew, almost without an organization at all. That changed in 1943. During Magalhães Barata's second term as governor, Belém City Hall transferred ownership of the manor house that had belonged to the family of Domingos Antônio Raiol himself - the very first president - to the institute as its headquarters. The building, now known as the Solar Barão de Guajará, sits in the old colonial heart of Belém on Dom Pedro II Square. IPHAN listed the house in 1950, adding it to a cluster of protected landmarks nearby: the Ver-o-Peso Dock, the Clock Square, Dom Pedro II Square, the Antônio Lemos Palace, the Lauro Sodré Palace just steps away. The institute did not simply inherit a building. It moved into the memory of its own founder.

## Five Phases, One Mission

The institute has reinvented itself repeatedly. Its third phase began in the 1960s under Ernesto Cruz, who reformed the decades-old statutes, fixed permanent membership at forty seats, and revived the IHGP Magazine with backing from the Federal University of Pará. The fourth phase, opened by Geraldo Mártires Coelho in 1998, democratized admission - abolishing the old requirement that candidates be nominated by three members, replacing it with a public call for applications. The fifth phase began in 2011 with Anaíza Vergolino, the first woman elected president in the association's history. She rebuilt the membership rolls, started the restoration of the archive, and signed a partnership with the state culture secretariat. By 2017, thirty new full members had joined. The electronic magazine resumed publication. An institution that had nearly vanished twice was now, quietly, in its most active period.

## Four Rooms, One Story

Visitors to the manor house's 2023 exhibition found it organized into four thematic spaces, each devoted to an aspect of the story the institute exists to preserve. The first room contextualizes the life and work of Raiol himself - the Baron, the politician, the chronicler of Pará's political riots. The second explores the independence of Brazil as it played out in Grão-Pará, including the Napoleonic wars and the Portuguese expedition that invaded Cayenne from Pará. The third examines how that independence was taught in school groups across the state, shaping generations of schoolchildren's sense of what their country was. The fourth traces the ephemerides of the nation - how independence has been commemorated across different periods, and how each generation rewrote the memory for its own purposes. It is, in miniature, the argument the institute has been making for over a century: that a region often treated as Brazil's periphery was in fact central to its making.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.46 degrees S, 48.50 degrees W in the colonial core of Belém, on Dom Pedro II Square in the Cidade Velha neighborhood. The manor house sits within a compact heritage district that also includes the Lauro Sodré Palace and the Ver-o-Peso Dock along Guajará Bay. Best viewed from 2,000-4,000 feet AGL approaching Belém/Val de Cans International Airport (SBBE) from the south. The bay's broad waters and the city's colonial grid provide clear visual reference.
