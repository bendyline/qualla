# Peru

A republic of 34 million spanning Pacific desert, Andean sierra, and Amazon rainforest - heir to one of the longest continuous histories of civilization on Earth.

**Location:** -10.0000, -76.0000

**Categories:** peru, south-america, countries, megadiverse, inca-empire

## Introduction

At the latitude where the equator just clips the north of the country, Peru runs 2,000 kilometers south to the border with Chile. Along the way it holds 84 of the 104 recognized ecological zones on the planet. More bird species than any other country. A coastal desert where it has not rained in recorded history. An Andean spine with glaciers that are melting faster than anyone wants to admit. And an Amazon basin where the longest river in the world begins as a trickle off the Apurímac and becomes, within Peruvian territory, a channel big enough to float ocean barges. The republic governing all of that has existed, under its current name, only since 1979. The civilizational stack beneath it reaches back to the tenth millennium BCE.

## A Civilizational Stack

The earliest evidence of humans in Peru dates to around 12,500 BCE at Huaca Prieta on the north coast. By 3000 BCE the Caral-Supe civilization was raising stepped pyramids in the Supe Valley, building monumental architecture contemporaneous with the Egyptian pyramids and without pottery. Chavín de Huantar became a religious center around 1500 BCE. The Paracas and Nazca flourished along the south coast. The Moche perfected portrait vessels and massive adobe huacas on the north coast between roughly 100 and 700 CE. The Wari empire ran from Ayacucho between 500 and 1000. The Chimú built Chan Chan, the largest adobe city in the Americas. And then, from a small ethnic group called the Quechuas who emerged in the thirteenth century around Cuzco, the Incas expanded to control most of western South America under Pachacuti and his son Topa Inca Yupanqui. The empire they called Tawantinsuyu - the Four Quarters - held 9 to 16 million people at its peak.

## 1532 and After

The Spanish conquest began with Francisco Pizarro's small party in December 1532. Supported by Chanka, Huanca, Cañari, and Chachapoya auxiliaries who had their own scores to settle with the Incas, Pizarro defeated and captured Atahualpa at the Battle of Cajamarca. The Sapa Inca was executed on August 29, 1533, after filling a room with gold as ransom. Lima was founded in 1535 as La Ciudad de los Reyes, the City of the Kings. Indigenous resistance under Manco Inca and his successors in Vilcabamba held out until 1572, when the Neo-Inca State was annihilated and Tupac Amaru was beheaded in Cuzco. The population collapse that followed was catastrophic: from an estimated 5 to 9 million people in the 1520s to around 600,000 a century later, destroyed mainly by epidemic disease with forced labor and social disruption doing the rest.

## Silver, Wars, and Caudillos

The Viceroyalty of Peru became one of Spain's wealthiest colonies, floating on the silver of Potosí (in modern Bolivia) and Huancavelica mercury, extracted under the mita labor system that devastated the Andean communities it conscripted. Independence came late. José de San Martín landed at Paracas in 1820 and proclaimed Peru free on July 28, 1821. Simón Bolívar and Antonio José de Sucre finished the Spanish army at Junín and Ayacucho in 1824. The nineteenth century brought guano wealth and squandered it, ending in the War of the Pacific against Chile from 1879 to 1884 - the bloodiest war in Peruvian history, which cost the country Tarapacá and left a permanent wound. The twentieth century swung between civilian and military governments: Leguía's Oncenio, the rise of APRA under Haya de la Torre, Velasco's radical military reform of 1968, the Shining Path insurgency from 1980, Fujimori's 1990s stabilization accompanied by forced sterilization of 300,000 poor and indigenous women and the Grupo Colina death squads.

## A Megadiverse Republic

Modern Peru is one of the world's megadiverse countries - 21,462 species of plants and animals reported as of 2003, with 5,855 of them endemic. The Humboldt Current cools the coast and generates a desert where Lima gets less than 20 millimeters of rain annually. The Andes split the country into costa, sierra, and selva. Huascarán at 6,768 meters is the highest peak. Lake Titicaca is shared with Bolivia at 3,812 meters - the highest commercially navigable lake on Earth. The economy runs on mining - Peru is the world's third-largest silver producer and the largest gold miner in Latin America - and increasingly on a cuisine that has made Lima a global culinary capital, home to Central and Maido and other restaurants that keep winning the top spots on international lists. Informal workers make up 70 percent of the labor market. Inequality remains steep. The political system, with a Congress empowered to impeach presidents almost at will, has cycled through six presidents between 2018 and 2023.

## What Peru Keeps

Peruvian culture is, in the deepest sense, layered. Catholic festivals like Corpus Christi still incorporate Inca elements like the Q'oyllur Riti pilgrimage up to a glacier above 5,000 meters. Quechua is spoken by almost 17 percent of the population - more than five million people - and remains a living language, not a museum one. Rose of Lima, the first saint canonized in the Americas, shares patronage with Martín de Porres, the Afro-Peruvian Dominican brother whose altars still draw prayers across the country. In 2025 Cardinal Robert Francis Prevost, a naturalized Peruvian citizen, became Pope Leo XIV - a reminder that the country's religious story is not only historical. The food - ceviche, lomo saltado, anticuchos, pachamanca, causa, aji de gallina, chicha morada - carries the history in it: indigenous ingredients, Spanish techniques, African rhythms in the pounding and seasoning, Chinese soy sauce from the nineteenth-century laborers, Japanese precision from the Nikkei community. To eat in Peru is to taste everything that happened here.

## Flight Context

Peru covers 1,285,216 square kilometers between roughly 0 and 18 degrees S, 69 to 81 degrees W. Main gateway Lima's Jorge Chávez International (SPJC) at sea level. Major regional hubs: Cuzco Alejandro Velasco Astete (SPZO) at 3,399 meters, Arequipa Rodríguez Ballón (SPQU) at 2,559 meters, Iquitos Francisco Secada Vignetta (SPQT) at 91 meters, Trujillo Carlos Martínez de Pinillos (SPRU) at 30 meters. Cross-country cruise FL300-FL400 to clear the Andes. High-altitude airports demand careful performance planning. Dry season May-October ideal for sierra; Amazon flying better in morning hours year-round as afternoon convective activity builds fast over the selva.
