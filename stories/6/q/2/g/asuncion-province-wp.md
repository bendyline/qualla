# Asuncion province

A small Andean province on the eastern slopes of Peru's Cordillera Blanca, where Italian missionaries built workshops to teach traditional crafts and a single town of stone buildings sits at 3,350 meters.

**Location:** -9.1592, -77.3642

**Categories:** Provinces of the Department of Ancash

## Introduction

Chacas, the capital of Asuncion province, is a town of about three thousand people. It sits at 3,359 meters in a basin of the eastern Cordillera Blanca, surrounded by peaks above 5,000 meters. Walk into its central plaza and what catches the eye is not the altitude or the cold - it is the woodwork. Carved doors. Carved beams. Carved balconies. The Operazione Mato Grosso, a movement of Italian Catholic volunteers, started workshops here in the 1970s to teach local craftsmen woodcarving, weaving, glasswork, and stone masonry. The Don Bosco workshops are still running. The town is now Peru's most unlikely center of Italian-influenced Andean baroque furniture making.

## The Province by the Numbers

Asuncion is one of the twenty provinces of the Ancash region in northern Peru, and it is among the smallest of them. Its land area is 528.66 square kilometers, just 1.47 percent of the region. It is divided into two districts: Chacas, which holds 85 percent of the territory, and Acochaca, which holds the remaining 15 percent in the lower valley. The province's altitudes range from about 2,500 meters at its lowest point - in Acochaca, where fruit trees grow - to peaks above 5,000 meters in the Cordillera Blanca on its western edge. From Huaraz, the regional capital, Chacas is 121 kilometers away by road. From Lima, 521 kilometers. Both numbers understate the journey. The road climbs over Punta Olimpica at over 4,800 meters.

## Three Climates Stacked Vertically

Peruvian geographer Javier Pulgar Vidal divided the Andes into eight natural regions defined by altitude, and Asuncion contains three of them. The Quechua zone runs from the lowest altitudes up to about 3,500 meters, with a temperate semi-dry climate that lets fruit trees do well around Acochaca. The Suni or Jalca zone occupies the slope between 3,500 and 4,000 meters - cold, steep, with stretches of high pampa. Potatoes, quinoa, beans, and tubers grow up here. The native plants include kiswar trees, elderberries, cantuta, and tawri. The Janca zone begins at 4,800 meters - bare rock, ice, and snow. The Cordillera Blanca's eastern flank carries Asuncion into the highest of these zones, with named peaks including Wallqan and Yanarahu rising above the threshold.

## Chacas and the Workshops

The town center of Chacas is small enough to walk in twenty minutes. The plaza is plaza-shaped, the church is on one side, and around it sit the houses and shops where carvers work. The Operazione Mato Grosso movement was founded in Brazil in the 1960s by Father Ugo De Censi, an Italian Salesian. He brought it to Chacas in 1976 and stayed nearly four decades, dying in the town in 2018 at age 94. He created a hospital, a hydroelectric plant, schools, and the workshops that taught local people to carve, weld, blow glass, and lay stone. The output is unmistakable - carved wooden balconies and doors, tin lamps, stained glass, baroque furniture - and it has spread well beyond Chacas. Most of the church furnishings in modern Andean Peru's Catholic parishes started in the Don Bosco workshops here.

## The Country Around

Outside the town, the province is country of rivers, hills, and mountains. The Andean slopes rise steeply, and the soils are highly erodible - moderately deep to shallow, low fertility, full of stones. The flat valley floors carry the best farming land, but they are small. Hilly terrain in between gets used for grazing - sheep, cattle, and the woollier camelids that have always lived in these altitudes. About twelve kilometers west of Chacas the Waqramarka waterfall plunges down a cliff popular with canyoners and abseilers. Lakes named Paryaqucha, Runtuqucha, and Yanaqucha sit in the higher basins, fed by glacier melt from the Cordillera Blanca. The summer rainy season runs November to March. June and July nights can drop to biting cold, with chilly winds. May through September brings days of fierce solar heat at high elevation.

## The Other Pace

Chacas is one of those Andean towns that runs on a different clock from the rest of Peru. The market is small. The roads are narrow. The Italian-Andean fusion in the workshops shows up in the way young carvers will hum opera while planing wood, and in the way the church procession on the patronal feast day - August 15, the Assumption, for which the province is named - moves with both Andean drum rhythms and a brass band imported from northern Italy. The Operazione Mato Grosso movement was specific about its goals. It wanted to teach skills, not make charity. The carvers and masons who learned here run their own businesses now, sell to clients across Peru, and pass the craft to the next generation. The province kept its altitude and added a few additional traditions to it.
