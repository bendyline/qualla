# Montañita

A cluster of fishermen's huts on Ecuador's Pacific coast became an international surf capital when foreigners arrived in 1960 and never left.

**Location:** -1.8333, -80.7500

**Categories:** Populated places in Santa Elena Province, Surfing locations in Ecuador, Coastal towns

## Introduction

Two foreigners named Jack and James camped on the beach long enough that they eventually built a cement house. That house, the first of its kind in Montañita, still marks the point where a handful of fishermen's huts became something else entirely. In 1960, travelers linked to the hippie movement had begun arriving on this stretch of the Santa Elena coast and deciding, one after another, that they would stay. Six decades later, the grid of narrow streets behind the beach is crowded with hostels, board shops, cevicherías, and the sounds of a town that has not yet gone to sleep.

## Small Mountain, Big Waves

The name means "small mountain," and the feature in question is the Point, a modest promontory that separates Montañita's beach from the neighboring town of Olón. What the Point lacks in altitude it makes up for in geometry: it bends the Pacific swell into long, consistent rights that have made this one of Ecuador's best surf breaks. The town itself spreads in a grid of small east-west streets, most of them aiming straight at the water. Four distinct areas make up the settlement - the beach, the Point, the Trigrillo neighborhood, and the Center - and on any given afternoon the traffic between them is a shuffle of wet boards, espresso-fueled travelers, and surf instructors bringing students back from a lesson.

## From Hippies to Hostels

The first foreigners who settled here in 1960 found a beach town with no cement, no hotels, and a climate of eternal 28°C afternoons. Water temperatures hover between 13 and 20°C, rain falls mostly at night from December to May, and the sun does its work the rest of the time. That climate, combined with the waves, created the conditions for a slow tourism boom. Schools like Mar Azul and La Escuelita teach private lessons to newcomers. Bodyboarders, scuba divers, and windsurfers share the water. Tourism peaks in January and bottoms out in June, and every February the town shuts down for a Carnival that locals and visitors both circle on their calendars. A reputation for hedonism has attached itself to Montañita over the years - the town has been called a "devil's town" by some, a description locals both embrace and push back against.

## Where the Past Sleeps

About ten kilometers south of Montañita, the sand gives up older stories. The Valdivia archaeological site, excavated in the 1950s, revealed one of South America's oldest known cultures - the Valdivia people, who occupied this coast beginning around 3500 BCE. Further south on the Santa Elena Peninsula lies evidence of the Las Vegas culture, whose tenure on this shoreline stretched from roughly 8800 to 4600 BCE, making it Ecuador's earliest known settled society. The surfers on Montañita's beach are walking, in effect, on top of millennia of continuous human presence. Few of them stop to think about it, but the archaeologists who come through on research trips do.

## Law, Leaf, and Reputation

Montañita is widely known - sometimes notoriously - for its relaxed attitude toward cannabis, made possible by an Ecuadorian law that permits individuals to carry small personal amounts. That legal nuance, combined with the town's compact nightlife district, has pulled in a certain kind of traveler and has given the place a reputation that not everyone appreciates. The 2014 film Sexy Montañita, directed by Alberto Pablo Rivera, leaned into that image. Most visitors, though, come for something simpler: a few days of consistent surf, cheap fresh fish at beachside tables, and nights warm enough to sleep with the windows open. The hippies who arrived in 1960 were looking for exactly that, and six decades on, the town still delivers it.
