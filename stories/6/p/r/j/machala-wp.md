# Machala

Ecuador's sixth-largest city, second-busiest port, and the engine room of the country's banana exports.

**Location:** -3.2667, -79.9667

**Categories:** Ecuador, cities, ports, agriculture

## Introduction

Ecuador exports more bananas than any other country on Earth. That superlative has a mailing address, and it is Machala. The city sits on the coastal lowlands three degrees south of the equator, capital of El Oro Province, sixth-largest city in the country with a population of 288,072 according to the 2022 census. Most of the world's Ecuadorian bananas pass through the adjacent port of Puerto Bolivar on their way to North American grocery stores. The locals have embraced the nickname without apology: Capital Bananera del Mundo, the Banana Capital of the World.

## An Industry Built on One Crop

Bananas are the story Machala tells about itself, but they are also the story of Ecuador's 20th century. The country became the world's dominant banana exporter in the 1950s after a fungal disease called Panama disease devastated plantations across Central America. Ecuadorian farms, planted with resistant varieties and blessed with ideal coastal soil, filled the gap. The El Oro Province around Machala became ground zero for the boom. From Puerto Bolivar, refrigerated container ships carry the fruit to Europe, Russia, the Middle East, and North America, where Chiquita and Dole and Del Monte repackage it for supermarket shelves. The European Union alone takes nearly a third of Ecuador's banana exports. Coffee and cocoa trade through the city too, but bananas are the crop that built the downtown.

## A Working Port

Puerto Bolivar is Machala's economic lung. It is the second-busiest port in Ecuador, after Guayaquil seventy-five miles to the north, and it moves enormous volumes of agricultural exports. The city itself centers on a large Catholic church and the central plaza, rebuilt in the early 2000s around a substantial fountain. Hotels cluster downtown. Beyond the city, the Jambeli Islands lie offshore, reachable only by ferry and popular with weekending Ecuadorians. This is not a tourist destination in the international sense. Most foreigners who show up are passing through on the Pan-American Highway, either heading south into Peru or north to Guayaquil, and Machala's modest restaurants and hotels serve them without ceremony.

## Weather Under a Cold Current

The climate here contradicts the map. Despite sitting almost exactly on the equator, Machala has a hot semi-arid climate rather than the rainforest you might expect. The reason is the Humboldt Current, the cold ocean stream that sweeps up the Pacific coast of South America from Antarctic waters. The current cools the air above it, keeping rainfall low and suppressing the usual tropical storm patterns. The city sees a brief wet season from January to April, when the Humboldt weakens and warmer water pushes down from the north. The rest of the year brings persistent fog and cloudy skies, warm but never quite sweltering. The banana plantations thrive in it.

## Santa Rosa and the Sky Route

Santa Rosa International Airport serves Machala from a spot about ten kilometers south of the city. The airport was expanded in the 2010s to handle international traffic, though most flights still connect domestically to Quito. TAME, the Ecuadorian national carrier, operated the primary routes for decades before its dissolution in 2020, and the airport now sees a mix of smaller operators. Position matters: the airport sits at the edge of the coastal plain, with banana country visible from the window on approach. Pilots landing here often see the Gulf of Guayaquil to the west and the foothills of the Andes rising to the east.

## A City That Plays Football

Machala produces footballers. The list of players born here is long enough to fill several national squads, and the city has sent more than a dozen professionals to teams across Ecuador and beyond. Gabriel Achilier played for the national team. Jordy Caicedo, born in 1997, made it to Europe. The city also produced journalists, notably Tania Tinoco, who spent decades on Ecuadorian television before her death in 2022, and Ismael Perez Pazmino, the early 20th-century newspaperman and politician. Then there is the unsettling entry that appears on most lists of Machala's notable residents: Gilberto Chamba, a serial killer who confessed to murdering women in both Ecuador and Spain. Every city has its shadows. Machala's include the commerce of fruit, the rhythm of the port, and the occasional story it would rather forget.

## Flight Context

Machala is located at 3.27 degrees south, 79.97 degrees west, on Ecuador's southern coastal plain near the Gulf of Guayaquil. Santa Rosa International Airport (ICAO: SETR) lies about 10 kilometers south of the city. From cruising altitude the surrounding banana plantations form a distinctive green quilt across the lowlands, with Puerto Bolivar visible at the coast and the Andes rising eastward. Recommended viewing altitude 10,000 to 15,000 feet.
