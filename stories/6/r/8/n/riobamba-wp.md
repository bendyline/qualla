# Riobamba

The first Spanish city founded in what is now Ecuador, destroyed entirely by the 1797 earthquake and rebuilt on a new plain miles from its original ruins.

**Location:** -1.6731, -78.6483

**Categories:** cities, earthquake-history, colonial-history, andean-highlands, ecuador

## Introduction

On 4 February 1797, the ground beneath Riobamba opened, shook, and then fell. Landslides swept down the flanks of nearby mountains and buried neighborhoods. When the tremors stopped, the first city the Spanish had founded in Ecuador was simply gone, most of its population dead, its churches and streets pulverized into the valley floor. The survivors did not rebuild on the ruins. They walked 21 kilometers northeast to the plains of San Antonio de Aguiscate and started over. The Riobamba that exists today, capital of Chimborazo Province and tenth-largest city in Ecuador, stands on ground that had no town before the earthquake. The old Riobamba lies under a village now called Cajabamba, still shifting occasionally under the weight of the Andes.

## Before the Inca

The Chambo River Valley was Puruha country long before any European or Inca arrived. The Puruha nation fiercely resisted the northward expansion of the Inca Empire in the late 1400s, led by a general remembered as Condorazo. Huayna Capac, the Inca emperor, could not subdue them through force alone. He negotiated an alliance with the Schyris confederation, also called the Caran-Quitu, who were said to rule the highlands under the Duchicela dynasty. The peace was sealed by marriage. Huayna Capac took the Duchicela princess Paccha as his wife, and their son Atahualpa was born into both royal lines. Atahualpa would become the last sovereign ruler of the Inca Empire, captured by Francisco Pizarro at Cajamarca in 1532 and executed in 1533. The 18th-century Jesuit historian Juan de Velasco, a Riobamba native, preserved most of what we know about this period.

## First City

Following the conquest, Diego de Almagro founded Santiago de Quito on 15 August 1534 in the San Miguel plains, and the settlement was soon renamed Riobamba. It is considered the first Spanish city established in what is now Ecuador, predating Quito's founding by four months. The name came together from the Spanish rio, for river, and the Quechua rispampa, for plain. In 1563, when Philip II created the Real Audiencia of Quito, Riobamba became part of the new colonial jurisdiction. For the next two and a half centuries the city grew slowly on its original site, building churches and convents in the Spanish colonial style, accumulating a citizenry that included Jesuit intellectuals and mestizo landowners and an indigenous Puruha majority who worked the fields on the outskirts.

## Everything at Once

The 1797 earthquake is one of the worst seismic events in South American history. Estimates of the death toll range from 6,000 to more than 40,000 across the region, with Riobamba as the epicenter of the catastrophe. Eyewitnesses described the ground liquefying and then collapsing in slow motion, burying entire streets. The church bells rang themselves as they fell. The Jesuit scientist Alexander von Humboldt visited the ruins five years later and wrote one of the first modern descriptions of earthquake landslides, studying how the terrain had moved. The surviving Riobambenos decided the old site was cursed or simply unbuildable. They moved the city to flatter ground near San Antonio de Aguiscate and rebuilt from scratch, using the same street plan and the same church names, so that the second Riobamba feels oddly familiar to anyone who knew the first. The colonial architecture still standing today dates mostly from that post-1797 rebuild.

## Independence and Arsenal

During the Ecuadorian War of Independence, Riobamba declared itself free of Spain on 11 November 1820, the first city in the region to do so. Crown forces retook it within weeks. Independence arrived for real in 1822, when Antonio Jose de Sucre defeated the Spanish on the slopes of Pichincha and Ecuador joined Simon Bolivar's Gran Colombia. In 1830, Ecuador became a sovereign republic. Riobamba settled into its role as a regional capital, prosperous enough that in 1952 the city opened the Monumental Raul Davalos bullring, seating 13,000. That era ended more recently than the architecture suggests. In 2011, Ecuador voted to ban the killing of bulls in corridas, and the associated tourism collapsed. The city absorbed another shock on 20 November 2002, when the munitions depot at the Galapagos Brigade arsenal exploded in a chain of detonations so powerful that residents more than a kilometer away initially thought a volcano had erupted. Seven people died, hundreds were injured by flying glass, and the army later ruled the blast an accident during routine maintenance.

## Climbing Capital

Riobamba is now the trekking and mountaineering capital of Ecuador, a base for ascents of Carihuairazo, Chimborazo, Sangay, El Altar, and the so-called Lost Pyramid of Punay, a pre-Inca earthwork in the southern Chimborazo highlands. The city produced its share of Ecuadorian polymaths: Pedro Vicente Maldonado, the 18th-century geographer who mapped much of the Audiencia of Quito; Juan de Velasco, the Jesuit historian exiled to Italy after the Spanish crown expelled the Society of Jesus from the Americas in 1767; Luz Elisa Borja Martinez, a poet and painter who died in 1927 at twenty-four; and Maria Murgueytio, who in the mid-20th century became the first woman in Ecuador to hold a mayoral office. Chimborazo fills the northwest horizon. On clear mornings, its summit catches the light before the city wakes, a reminder that the mountains here have always been the main event.

## Flight Context

Located at 1.67 S, 78.65 W in the Chambo River Valley of the central Ecuadorian Andes. Elevation 2,754 m. Recommended viewing altitude 5,000 to 6,500 m to take in Chimborazo (6,263 m) directly to the northwest. Nearest major airports: Mariscal Sucre International (SEQM) in Quito, 180 km north. The Pan-American Highway runs through the city. The old colonial site at Cajabamba lies roughly 21 km southwest of the modern city. Sangay National Park covers the volcanic terrain to the east.
