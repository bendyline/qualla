# Whatcom Creek

Four miles of falling water that founded Bellingham in 1854 and, on a June afternoon in 1999, became the site of one of the worst pipeline disasters in American history.

**Location:** 48.7531, -122.4847

**Categories:** Bellingham, Washington, Rivers of Washington (state)

## Introduction

Four miles is not much of a river. Whatcom Creek drops out of Lake Whatcom, falls down a staircase of ledges through a city park, crosses a short plain, and pours into Bellingham Bay - and in that short distance it has done two enormous things. It founded a city, because a man looking for waterpower in the early 1850s found exactly the fall he needed here. And on 10 June 1999, it carried 277,000 gallons of gasoline through the middle of Bellingham and caught fire, killing three young people. The same falls are still there. So are the salmon.

## Noisy Waters

The creek's name in North Straits Salish is X̌'wótqwem, which some accounts render as noisy waters - a description anyone standing in Whatcom Falls Park will accept without argument. The creek begins at the control dam on Lake Whatcom, then loses most of its elevation almost immediately, tumbling from around 350 feet above sea level to about 80 feet over a series of falls packed into the park. After that it settles down, crossing a short plain toward downtown Bellingham before dropping one last time and reaching the bay. Four miles, three hundred and fifty feet, and a city built along the whole length of it.

## A Fall Worth Founding a City On

In the early 1850s, Henry Roeder and his partner Peabody were looking for a waterfall - specifically, one with enough drop and enough flow to turn a sawmill. They heard of a creek to the north called Whatcom. They canoed to Bellingham Bay to look at it, found a fairly large creek coming over a 35-foot fall, and decided that would do. The mill went up in 1854, powered by the falls, and everything that Bellingham subsequently became grew outward from that decision. Cities on this coast usually begin with a harbour or a railhead. This one began with a measurement of vertical drop.

## Who Is Allowed to Fish

The creek supports a modest fishery of trout and salmon, and it operates under a rule that is worth repeating because so few places have one like it: above the falls, only minors may fish. The stretch is reserved for children. Below the falls it is open to everyone, and in the autumn the banks fill with people working the returning run, watching salmon hurl themselves at the drop and occasionally make it. A four-mile creek running through the middle of a city, with a wild salmon run still coming up it, is not a common thing in the lower forty-eight. It is also the reason the creek matters to Bellingham in a way that a storm drain never could.

## The Tenth of June

On that Thursday afternoon in 1999, a 16-inch gasoline pipeline operated by the Olympic Pipe Line Company, crossing above the creek, split along a seam. Investigators later traced it to a pressure surge after an automatic valve closed for reasons never established, and to a pressure relief valve, improperly configured, that failed to open. About 277,000 gallons of gasoline poured into the creek bed and ran downstream through the city. Liam Wood, who was eighteen and fly fishing on the creek, was overcome by the fumes and drowned before the fuel ignited. Minutes later the gasoline exploded. Wade King and Stephen Tsiorvas, both ten years old, were caught in it, and both boys died of their burns the following day. The fire ran down the creek corridor and did not extend past Interstate 5.

## Afterward

Three families lost a son. A city watched a creek it had built itself around become, for a few hours, a channel of burning fuel. In 2002 Olympic, Equilon and several employees were named in a seven-count indictment; the companies pleaded guilty and a $112 million settlement followed, a record at the time and the first conviction of a pipeline company under the Hazardous Liquid Pipeline Safety Act of 1979. Money is a poor instrument for this kind of accounting, and everyone involved knew it. What the case did establish was that a pipeline company could be held criminally responsible for what its pipelines do, which had not been demonstrated before. The creek has since regrown its banks and taken back its salmon run. The falls sound the same as they did when Henry Roeder came to listen to them.

## Flight Context

Whatcom Creek reaches salt water at 48.753°N, 122.485°W, in downtown Bellingham, Washington, at the head of Bellingham Bay. It is only about 4 miles (6.4 km) long, so trace it as a line rather than look for a river: from the air it runs west-northwest from Lake Whatcom - the large forested lake immediately southeast of the city - through the dark green wedge of Whatcom Falls Park, across the built-up plain of Bellingham, and out beside the old Georgia-Pacific mill site and the Whatcom waterway into the bay. Lake Whatcom itself is the easiest identifying feature in the area, and Mount Baker stands snow-covered to the east. Bellingham International Airport (KBLI) lies three miles northwest of downtown, 23 miles from the Peace Arch border crossing and a short hop south of the Canadian line. Expect low cloud coming in off the Strait of Georgia and, in winter, strong cold outflow winds spilling west out of the Fraser Valley.
