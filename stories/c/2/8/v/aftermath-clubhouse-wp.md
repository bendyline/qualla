# Aftermath Clubhouse

A women's reading circle in a raw Washington port town raised its own money, bought its own land, and built what is believed to be the first women's clubhouse in the state.

**Location:** 48.7577, -122.4910

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Bellingham, Washington, Clubhouses on the National Register of Historic Places in Washington (state), Women's club buildings in Washington (state), National Register of Historic Places in Whatcom County, Washington, History

## Introduction

The name is the first clue, and most people miss it. In its older agricultural sense an aftermath is the second growth of grass that comes up after the first mowing - a further crop from ground already harvested. When a group of women in New Whatcom, Washington, formed a literary society in 1895 and called themselves the Aftermath Reading Circle, they were not being gloomy. They were making a joke about their own lives: this was what they grew in the hours left over. A decade later they had raised the money, bought the land, hired an architect and built themselves a two-storey clubhouse - believed to be the first women's clubhouse in the state of Washington.

## What a Reading Circle Was

New Whatcom was a young port on Bellingham Bay, one of the towns that would shortly be consolidated into Bellingham, and it was not a place with much institutional life for women. The Aftermath Reading Circle supplied it. The group was literary and social at first - books discussed, papers read aloud, company kept - but the shape of these organisations across America in the 1890s was reliably the same, and this one followed it. Intellectual discussion turned into organisation, and organisation turned into participation in community affairs. A club that began by reading became a club that did things, and in a town still assembling its institutions, the record of what such a club did is largely the record of civic work that no municipal body had yet been organised to do. What began as an evening for books ended as an instrument for getting things done.

## They Bought It Themselves

The decision to build was the ambitious part. Having formalised the circle into the Aftermath Club, the members took it on themselves to raise the funds, purchase property and put up a permanent clubhouse - not rent a hall, not borrow a church basement, but own the building outright. For many years afterward it was the only clubhouse in Whatcom County owned by its own members. And the club carried the debt and cleared it while continuing its charitable and philanthropic work, which is a harder financial feat than it sounds and says more about how the organisation was run than any minute book could.

## Italian Villa on Broadway

They hired Frank C. Burns, a local architect, and he gave them an Italian Villa - two storeys, wood frame, a style that in 1904 read as cultivated and slightly Continental, which was surely the point. Construction began in October 1904. The building was officially dedicated on 24 April 1905 with a grand reception, and its second floor became, for years afterward, one of the best ballrooms in the area. That detail is worth holding onto. A ballroom is not a lecture hall. These women built a place to think in and a place to dance in, in the same building, and did not appear to see any tension between the two.

## Weddings and Funerals

The clubhouse was added to the National Register of Historic Places on 14 December 1978. It survives today as Broadway Hall, at 1300 Broadway in Bellingham, privately owned and operated by Westford Funeral Home, renovated and upgraded to reflect its original character and rented out across more than five thousand square feet of space. Weddings are held here. So are funerals, parties, dances and community meetings. There is a symmetry in that which the founders might have appreciated: a building raised by a group of women so that the life of a community could have somewhere to happen is still, a hundred and twenty years later, where that community turns up to mark the beginnings and the ends of things.

## Flight Context

The Aftermath Clubhouse - now Broadway Hall - stands at 48.7577 N, 122.491 W, at 1300 Broadway in Bellingham, Washington, in the residential streets just uphill from the downtown core. Recommended viewing altitude is 1,500-3,000 ft AGL. The building itself is a single house-sized structure and will not stand out from altitude, so navigate by the city: Bellingham Bay opens to the west with its working waterfront, log ponds and marine terminals, the downtown grid sits between the bay and the hill, and the campus of Western Washington University occupies the wooded ridge to the south. Lake Whatcom runs back into the hills immediately east of the city, and Mount Baker's glaciated summit stands roughly 50 km east-southeast, visible for enormous distances on a clear day. Lummi Island lies across the bay to the west with the San Juan Islands beyond. Nearest airports: KBLI (Bellingham International) about 4 km northwest, KORS (Orcas Island) about 35 km west, and KAWO (Arlington Municipal) about 55 km south. Expect low ceilings and rain through the winter half of the year.
