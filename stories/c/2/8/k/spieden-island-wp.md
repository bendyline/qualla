# Spieden Island

A private San Juan island with a forested north shore, a bare golden south shore, and herds of Corsican sheep and Asian deer left over from a 1970s big-game venture.

**Location:** 48.6404, -123.1338

**Categories:** washington, san-juan-islands, island, private-island, wildlife, maritime-history

## Introduction

The hunting operation on Spieden Island closed for two reasons, and the second one is the memorable one: the bullets could reach the neighbours. San Juan Island lies less than a mile south across Spieden Channel, it is comparatively well populated, and an errant shot from a game ranch has to land somewhere. Add environmental objections to the arithmetic of ballistics and the venture was finished almost as soon as it started. What it left behind is still out there - mouflon sheep from Corsica, fallow deer from Europe, sika deer from Asia, and the descendants of about two thousand imported birds, grazing a 516-acre island in Washington State that almost nobody is allowed to set foot on.

## One Island, Two Faces

Spieden is roughly two miles long and half a mile across at its widest, rising to about 374 feet. What makes it instantly recognizable from the water is that it appears to be two different islands stuck together along their spines.

The north side is heavily forested - dark, dense, ordinary Pacific Northwest timber. The south side is almost bare, open ground dropping to the shore with hardly a tree on it, scattered with glacial erratics, boulders carried here by ice and dumped where they now sit. Sun and wind explain most of it: a south-facing slope in the rain shadow of the Olympics dries out in a way the shaded north face never does. The effect, seen from a boat in Spieden Channel, is startling. Turn the corner and you appear to have arrived somewhere else entirely.

## Named for a Purser

Charles Wilkes charted these waters during the United States Exploring Expedition of 1838 to 1842, a voyage that produced an enormous amount of survey work and an enormous number of place names - Wilkes was generous with his crew. Spieden Island honours William Spieden, the purser of the Peacock.

A purser is not a glamorous post. He handled accounts, provisions, and pay, the unromantic administration that keeps a ship's company fed and solvent through years at sea. The Peacock itself did not survive the voyage: it struck a shoal crossing the Columbia Bar in July 1841 and broke apart, though the crew got off alive. Spieden the man is otherwise largely forgotten. Spieden the island is on every chart of the San Juans.

## Mouflon and Guinea Fowl

In the 1970s and 1980s somebody looked at that open southern grassland and saw an African savanna. Non-native game animals were imported - mouflon from Corsica, fallow deer from Europe, sika deer from Asia - along with roughly two thousand exotic birds, African guinea fowl among them. A hunting lodge went up, and an airstrip, and a small hangar, so that clients could fly in, shoot, and fly out.

It did not last. Environmental objections mounted, the proximity of populated San Juan Island made the shooting genuinely dangerous, and the operation folded. The animals, however, had no particular interest in the business plan. Mouflon and deer still graze the bare southern slopes, and boaters passing through the channel regularly stop to stare at herds that belong on three different continents standing on a hillside in Washington State.

## Owned, Not Inhabited

The 2000 census recorded no permanent residents, though there are structures scattered across the island. In 1997 it was bought through a holding company by James Jannard, the founder of the sunglasses manufacturer Oakley, and he still held it as of 2019. For a period the Island Institute, an environmental education camp, operated here; it has since closed.

This is the other San Juan Islands - not the ferry-served ones with bakeries and bookshops, but the private holdings that make up a surprising share of the archipelago. Spieden is visible from the water to anyone who wants to look, and from the air on the short hop between Friday Harbor and the mainland, but it is not a place you can visit. There is something appropriate about an island that shows two entirely different faces to the world and lets you approach neither.

## Flight Context

Spieden Island lies at 48.640N, 123.134W in the San Juan archipelago, less than a mile north of San Juan Island across Spieden Channel. From 1,500 to 3,000 feet it is the most visually distinctive island in the group: a two-mile ridge with a dark forested northern flank and an open, tawny, treeless southern slope running down to the water - the contrast is obvious at any altitude and in any season. Watch for game animals on the southern grassland. There is a private airstrip on the island; it is not a public field. Friday Harbor (KFHR) is 5 nm south on San Juan Island, Roche Harbor (WA09) lies southwest, Orcas Island (KORS) east, and Victoria International (CYYJ) sits northwest across Haro Strait - note the international boundary runs through that channel. Expect strong tidal rips in Spieden Channel, summer morning fog, and gusty conditions in the gaps between islands.
