# Fort Whitman

A Navy gunboat squeezed through Deception Pass in 1909 and proved Puget Sound had a back door; the Army answered with four guns on a small island off La Conner that spent almost their entire service life on caretaker status.

**Location:** 48.3636, -122.5359

**Categories:** Forts in Washington (state), Coastal defense, History of Skagit County, Washington, Puget Sound, Military history

## Introduction

In 1909 a United States Navy gunboat did something the Army had assumed was impossible: it threaded Deception Pass, the narrow, tide-scoured gap between Whidbey and Fidalgo islands, and came out into the sheltered water behind them. The whole coastal defence scheme for Puget Sound rested on the idea that an enemy fleet would have to come in the front way, past the heavy guns at Fort Casey, Fort Flagler, and Fort Worden. One gunboat captain had just demonstrated there was a back passage. Two years later the Army had a fort on Goat Island, a scrap of rock and timber off La Conner, watching the channel nobody had bothered to watch before.

## The Back Passage

Look at a chart and the problem is obvious in hindsight. East of Fidalgo Island the water narrows into Skagit Bay, a shallow, confined route that runs down behind Whidbey Island toward the heart of the Sound. It is not comfortable water -- the Skagit River delta is dumping sediment into it constantly, and the tides through Deception Pass run hard enough to stand up standing waves. But a shallow-draught warship could use it, and a shallow-draught warship in Skagit Bay would be entirely behind the fortress line. The site chosen was Goat Island, which sits in the channel like a cork, close enough to La Conner to be supplied and far enough out to command the water. The post was named in 1910 for the missionary Marcus Whitman, and it was garrisoned from 1911.

## Battery Harrison

The armament was modest by the standards of the Endicott Board programme that produced it: a single battery, named Battery Harrison, mounting four six-inch rifles on disappearing carriages. The disappearing carriage was one of the great mechanical conceits of its era -- the gun rises above the parapet only to fire, and the recoil itself throws it back down behind the concrete where the crew reloads it out of sight. Alongside the guns stood the mine control structures, the plotting rooms and casemates from which observers would track a ship crossing a field of electrically detonated submarine mines and choose the exact moment to fire one. In a passage this narrow, the mines were arguably the real weapon and the guns were there to stop anyone clearing them.

## A Fort Nobody Lived In

Except in wartime, Fort Whitman stayed on caretaking status for its entire existence. The barracks, mess, and support buildings were run up as temporary structures, built for the duration of whatever emergency had prompted them and expected to rot afterward; the only permanent quarters on the island were the caretaker's. Picture that job. An island in the Skagit delta, the guns greased and covered, the concrete sweating in the marine air, a boat to La Conner when the weather allowed, and the nearest neighbours a very large number of ducks. For most of three decades, the defence of Puget Sound's back door consisted of one person keeping the damp out.

## Obsolete, Then Gone

By the Second World War the six-inch disappearing guns were relics. Aircraft and fast torpedo boats had rewritten what a harbour needed to fear, and Battery Harrison's main armament became a pair of 37mm anti-motor-torpedo-boat guns -- weapons designed to stop something small and quick rather than something large and slow. The guns spent their working lives aimed at an empty channel. The fort closed in 1947 and was formally disestablished the following year. Coastal batteries are poured in bulk reinforced concrete, which is why they so reliably outlast the armies that build them, and Goat Island has stayed undeveloped public land ever since, carried on the books as state park and wildlife ground. The passage it guarded is now used almost entirely by fishing boats and kayakers, none of whom are being tracked from a plotting room.

## Flight Context

Fort Whitman occupied Goat Island at 48.364N, 122.536W, in Skagit Bay immediately southwest of La Conner, Washington. From the air Goat Island is a distinct forested hump sitting alone in the channel between the Skagit River delta and the southern end of Fidalgo Island -- surrounded by green farmland dikes to the east and tan tideflats to the south. Deception Pass, the gap that prompted the fort's construction, is clearly visible about 8 nm northwest, with the high steel arch of its bridge. Mount Baker dominates the northeast horizon on clear days. Best viewed at 1,500-3,000 feet, which shows the island, the narrow channel, and the delta all at once. Nearest airports: Skagit Regional Airport (KBVS) about 7nm northeast, Anacortes Airport (74S) about 8nm north, NAS Whidbey Island (KNUW) about 10nm west, and Arlington Municipal (KAWO) about 18nm southeast. Note the restricted and military operating areas associated with NAS Whidbey Island nearby.
