# Pearson College UWC

A two-year school on Pedder Bay where a hundred teenagers from more than a hundred countries live together, run a marine protected area, and carry the name of a man who never saw it open.

**Location:** 48.3467, -123.5640

**Categories:** Education, Vancouver Island, British Columbia, Marine Conservation, International History

## Introduction

Lester Pearson never saw the college that carries his name. He died in December 1972, nine months before ground was broken, and almost two years before the first hundred students walked onto the site at Pedder Bay. What he left behind was a conviction formed on a single trip. In 1969, retired from Canadian politics and looking for something to do with the rest of his life, he visited Atlantic College in Wales, then the only United World College in existence, and talked with its students and faculty. He came away certain that there should be more of them, and that one of them belonged on Canada's west coast.

## A Visit to Wales

Pearson had won the Nobel Peace Prize in 1957 and served as prime minister, so when he took up the cause of a school nobody had built yet, people listened. He became honorary chairman of the committee assembled to create what was then simply called the College of the Pacific, and he set out his terms plainly. Students, he said, would be welcomed without regard to race, religion or politics, and scholarships would ensure they came from all levels of society as genuine representatives of their own peoples. Such a system, he thought, could become a revolutionary force in international education. He worked hard on the project in its first, most precarious phase, and then he died with nothing yet standing. The committee renamed the school for him as a living memorial. John Lang Nichol took over as chairman of the board, more than four million dollars was raised, and construction began on 25 September 1973.

## One Hundred Arrivals

Exactly one year later, on 25 September 1974, the inaugural cohort of one hundred students arrived at a campus that had existed only on paper when Pearson's committee first met. Jack Matthews, the founding director, had spent the intervening year recruiting a faculty from Canada and abroad. The official opening came the following year, with the Earl Mountbatten of Burma, then president of the United World Colleges International Council, and Jean Chretien in attendance. Prince Charles, who would also serve as president of UWC and is now King Charles III, established the Prince of Wales Scholarship and returned to the college twice in the early 1980s. In 2009 he met four Prince of Wales scholars at once, from Kenya, Romania, Nicaragua and Canada, which is about as neat a summary of the school's intake as four names can provide.

## The Arithmetic of a Cohort

More than a hundred students are selected for each annual intake, and in 2023-24 they represented over a hundred countries and territories. Read that ratio again: roughly one student per nation. It is a deliberate constraint, and it produces a particular kind of residential life. Students are generally sixteen to nineteen, and the programme runs two years, leading to the International Baccalaureate diploma or to the Climate Action Leadership Diploma, an IB Career-related Programme. Both lean heavily on experiential education, which at Pedder Bay means the learning tends to happen outdoors and on the water. Five residence houses accommodate everyone, each with resident houseparents in a connected apartment and a dayroom for study and for the long arguments that are, arguably, the actual curriculum.

## Race Rocks

The campus sits on the shore of Pedder Bay near Victoria, on the traditional territory of the Sc'ianew, the Beecher Bay First Nation. Offshore lies Race Rocks, a marine protected area of exceptional ecological richness that the college helps maintain, working alongside BC Parks, the Canadian Coast Guard and other partners to keep a resident volunteer ecoguardian present on the rocks at all times. It is an unusual responsibility for a high school. The surrounding woodlands and waters function as the college's laboratory, served by a floating marine sciences building and a fleet of sailboats, kayaks, canoes and scuba gear that students may use once they have been properly trained. Environmental studies here are not abstract; the ecosystem being studied lies just offshore and requires someone to look after it.

## Where They Go

Graduates scatter to universities across the world, from Oxford and Harvard to Sciences Po, McGill, Columbia and the University of Toronto, and many arrive with substantial funding behind them. Fifteen alumni have won Rhodes Scholarships and seventeen graduating students have been named Loran Scholars; others have gone on to the Gates Cambridge, Knight-Hennessy and Schwarzman programmes, and all alumni are eligible for the Davis United World College Scholars Program, which supports undergraduate study in the United States based on need. The alumni list reads like a deliberate refutation of the idea that a small school on a quiet bay cannot reach far: a Booker Prize-winning novelist, a Danish foreign minister, a Maldivian environment minister, a technology executive, a radio host, lawyers, scientists and public servants across six continents. Pearson's revolutionary force in international education turns out to have been a hundred teenagers a year, arriving every September for five decades.

## Flight Context

Pearson College UWC occupies the shore of Pedder Bay at 48.3467 N, 123.564 W, on the southern tip of Vancouver Island near Metchosin, facing the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Best viewing altitude is 1,500 to 3,000 feet, enough to take in the whole bay, the campus buildings along its shore and the Race Rocks group offshore to the south. Key visual landmarks: the narrow east-facing inlet of Pedder Bay itself, the black-and-white banded Race Rocks lighthouse a short distance offshore to the south, Rocky Point to the east, and the Olympic Mountains filling the horizon across the strait to the south. Victoria International Airport (CYYJ) lies about 20 nm northeast and Victoria Inner Harbour Water Aerodrome (CYWH) about 12 nm east; Port Angeles (KCLM) sits across the strait in Washington. This is international border airspace, so check the Victoria terminal procedures carefully. Expect strong westerly flow accelerating through the strait in summer afternoons, and persistent morning fog at the entrance to Pedder Bay.
