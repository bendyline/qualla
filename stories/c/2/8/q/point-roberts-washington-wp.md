# Point Roberts, Washington

Five square miles of the United States hanging below the 49th parallel, reachable from the rest of America only by crossing Canada twice - a town whose entire character was set by a survey line nobody checked.

**Location:** 49.0000, -123.0647

**Categories:** Border Irregularities, Exclaves, Washington, Pacific Northwest, Communities

## Introduction

Britain tried to hand it back. When the Boundary Commission actually went out and surveyed the 49th parallel in the years after 1846, the British government realized what the treaty had done: the tip of the Tsawwassen peninsula would fall on the American side of the line while being physically attached only to British territory. The Foreign Office instructed Captain James Prevost, its boundary commissioner, to raise the matter with his American counterpart and ask that Point Roberts be left to Britain - on the grounds that keeping it would be a great inconvenience to the United States. If the Americans hesitated, Prevost was authorized to offer compensation in the form of a slight adjustment to the boundary elsewhere on the mainland. No record survives of what the American commissioner said. Point Roberts stayed American, and has spent the intervening century and a half proving the British point.

## Isla de Cepeda

The first Europeans to see it thought it was an island, and drew it that way. Members of Francisco de Eliza's 1791 expedition charted it as Isla de Cepeda, and it kept that shape on Spanish maps until the morning of 13 June 1792, when the two ships under Dionisio Alcala Galiano sailed into Boundary Bay, established that the supposed island was joined to the mainland, and demoted it to Punta Cepeda. They then rounded the point and ran almost immediately into HMS Chatham, the second vessel of George Vancouver's expedition. Two navies with competing claims to the entire coast met by accident in a shallow bay and agreed, on the spot, to pool their findings and map the Strait of Georgia together. The English name came later and casually: Vancouver called it after his friend Henry Roberts, who had originally been given command of the expedition and had lost it to Vancouver.

## Fifty-Four Forty, and What Was Actually Signed

The Oregon Country had been jointly occupied for decades, claimed by both Britain and the United States and settled by very few people from either. American expansionists campaigned on Fifty-Four Forty or Fight, demanding everything up to that latitude. What was signed on 15 June 1846 was a good deal less: the 49th parallel run straight to the sea, with Vancouver Island retained whole by Britain because Fort Victoria was expected to become the centre of island settlement. Neither side had precise knowledge of the effects. One of them was Point Roberts. Another was a brief rush of squatters - in 1858, prospectors from Victoria heading for the Fraser gold fields and hoping to dodge colonial tax collection set up on the point in a settlement called Robert's Town. It had six wooden buildings, including a store and a saloon, and it lasted less than a year.

## Wells, Wires, and a Forty-Minute Bus

Being American here has always meant depending on Canada for the practical things. In 1949 there was open talk of seceding and joining Canada outright; a 1952 regional plan floated turning the point into an international park or leasing it out for 99 or 999 years. When a drought in 1973 dried the wells, the dispute turned ugly fast - American residents put up Canadians Go Home signs and threatened to cut off Canadian residents' water unless the municipality of Delta helped, and it took until 1987 to settle the matter with an agreement to buy raw water annually from the Greater Vancouver Water District. The telephones were odder still. Until 1988 service came from BC Tel, and the single 945 exchange, officially inside Washington's area code, was also reachable inside Vancouver's - so calls from metro Vancouver were local and calls from the rest of the United States were international. Then there is school. Point Roberts Primary is a three-room building with about fifteen students and one full-time teacher, classified by the state as remote and necessary, and it goes to third grade. From fourth grade on, American children ride forty minutes through British Columbia and re-enter their own country at Blaine.

## The Quietest Year

The border is the economy, and the border is the risk. After the September 2001 attacks, tightened crossings turned routine errands into long waits. Then in March 2020 Canada closed to non-essential travel, and Point Roberts - which lives on Canadian day-trippers, fuel buyers and weekenders - simply stopped. A study that year found the community had lost eighty per cent of its business and hundreds of seasonal residents. The chamber of commerce called it a ghost town. It also, for a long stretch, had no confirmed COVID-19 cases at all, and was written up nationally as perhaps the safest place in America, which was true and no comfort whatsoever. The Port of Bellingham ran a temporary ferry from August 2020 until the border reopened a year later. In 2025, new American tariffs on Canadian goods and talk of annexation prompted a Canadian consumer boycott; visits fell about thirty per cent, businesses closed or moved north, and residents said it was worse than the pandemic. That same year Delta put up a chain-link fence at the end of English Bluff Road after an elderly man wandered across the line by accident. Locals hated it. Meanwhile the post office boxes stay full: roughly forty times more Canadians hold mailboxes in Point Roberts than there are people living here.

## Flight Context

Located at 49.00 N, 123.065 W - the coordinates say it plainly, since the northern boundary of Point Roberts is the 49th parallel itself, drawn as a dead-straight line across the peninsula and clearly visible from the air as a cleared swath and an abrupt change in road pattern and land use. The exclave measures about 2 miles north to south and 3 miles east to west, 4.884 square miles, all land, with Boundary Bay to the east and the Strait of Georgia south and west. Downtown Vancouver lies 22 miles north; the Tsawwassen ferry terminal and its long causeway are immediately northwest and make an unmistakable landmark; the Fraser River delta spreads north; Mount Baker stands southeast and the North Shore mountains rise behind Vancouver. Recommended viewing altitude is 2,000-4,000 ft MSL to hold the whole peninsula and the border line in one frame. Point Roberts Airpark is a small private strip on the point itself; Bellingham International (KBLI) is roughly 40 km southeast across Boundary Bay, Boundary Bay Airport (CZBB) about 15 km north in Delta, and Vancouver International (CYVR) some 30 km north. This is international airspace on both sides with active ADIZ considerations - check requirements before operating here. Weather is unusually kind: about 40 inches of precipitation a year spread across 146 days, sheltered by Vancouver Island and the Cascades, with more sun than most of the region.
