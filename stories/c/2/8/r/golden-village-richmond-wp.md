# Golden Village, Richmond

Four streets in suburban Richmond holding a dense cluster of Asian shopping malls - built by the people who shop in them, ten minutes from the runways of YVR.

**Location:** 49.1824, -123.1362

**Categories:** Neighbourhoods in Richmond, British Columbia, Chinese-Canadian culture in British Columbia, Ethnic enclaves in British Columbia

## Introduction

Every hour, on the hour, a musical fountain performs inside a shopping mall in suburban Richmond, British Columbia. It was built in deliberate imitation of the one outside the Bellagio in Las Vegas, at smaller scale and indoors, and it sits at the centre of Aberdeen Centre with three floors of shops arranged around it. Nothing about the surrounding landscape prepares you for it - this is flat Fraser River delta, airport approach lighting on one horizon, low industrial roofs on another. The Golden Village is four streets of exactly this kind of surprise, and it exists because of who moved here.

## Four Streets and a Boundary

The district's edges are unglamorous and precise: Sea Island Way to the north, Garden City Road to the east, Alderbridge Way to the south, and a line about 300 metres west of No. 3 Road. Most of the commercial buildings are entered from the west side of Hazelbridge Way, which means the walking experience is nothing like a traditional high street - you move between malls rather than along shopfronts. The Golden Village is properly understood as an extension of Richmond's main commercial district, which grew out of the older core around No. 3 Road and Westminster Highway. What makes it distinct is density: a great many Asian-themed shopping centres packed into a very small area.

## Aberdeen, Built Twice

Aberdeen Centre was one of the first Asian malls in Richmond, opened in 1989 by the developer Thomas Fung with somewhere between fifty and seventy-five stores. It did well enough that in 2001 it was knocked down entirely and rebuilt for roughly $100 million. The replacement opened in 2003 at about three times the original footprint, and today runs to 380,000 square feet with more than 160 stores across three floors - plus the fountain. A further phase, Aberdeen Square, opened in 2013 with three retail and three office levels. The whole property is developed, owned and managed by the Fairchild Group. Very few suburban malls anywhere get demolished at the height of their success and rebuilt bigger. This one did.

## Five Malls, Five Different Rooms

Treating the Golden Village as one thing misses the point, because the malls are not interchangeable. Yaohan Centre is pan-Asian, mixing Chinese, Taiwanese, Vietnamese, Thai and Korean businesses under one roof. President Plaza serves the Taiwanese community in particular and includes a Radisson hotel. Parker Place is Hong Kong in style and strong on Asian fashion. Union Square arrived to serve the newer residential towers going up around the district. Together they cover different origins, different generations and different price points, which is why people drive across Greater Vancouver to shop here rather than at whatever mall is closest to them.

## Fifty-Four Percent

The 2016 Canadian census recorded that 54 percent of Richmond's population identify their ethnic origin as Chinese. That figure is the underlying fact behind everything above. The Golden Village was not conceived by a tourism board as a themed attraction; it was built because a large community needed groceries, banking, haircuts, wedding clothes, herbal medicine and somewhere to eat dinner, and it was easier to build those places than to keep travelling to them. Visitors from elsewhere often describe the district as though it were an exhibit. For the people who live in Richmond it is simply the shops, which is the highest compliment a commercial district can be paid.

## Ten Minutes From the Runway

Getting here is unusually easy for a suburban district. Since 2009 the Canada Line has run SkyTrain service from downtown Vancouver through central Richmond, with Aberdeen station sitting inside the Golden Village itself. TransLink buses connect outward to Richmond Centre and Steveston, to New Westminster and Burnaby, and to the University of British Columbia. Most usefully for anyone arriving from a long way off, the district is linked directly to Sea Island - home of Vancouver International Airport - by the Moray Bridge and the Sea Island Bridge. A traveller with a four-hour layover can be eating in Richmond and back at the gate without hurrying.

## Flight Context

The Golden Village sits at 49.182°N, 123.136°W on Lulu Island in Richmond, on the Fraser River delta south of Vancouver. It is directly under the busiest airspace in western Canada: Vancouver International Airport (CYVR) occupies Sea Island barely two kilometres northwest across the river arm, and the district is joined to it by the Moray and Sea Island bridges. The Vancouver International Water Airport (CAM9) handles floatplanes on the South Arm of the Fraser. From the air the Golden Village reads as a cluster of large flat mall roofs and parking fields in the rectangular street grid between Sea Island Way and Alderbridge Way, with the elevated Canada Line guideway running north-south along No. 3 Road as the clearest linear marker. The North Shore Mountains rise beyond downtown Vancouver 12 km north; the delta is dead flat in every other direction. This is fully controlled airspace with heavy airline traffic - do not transit without a clearance. Fog on the delta is a genuine winter factor.
