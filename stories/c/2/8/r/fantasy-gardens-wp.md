# Fantasy Gardens

A mock-medieval theme park in suburban Richmond with a Bible-shaped hedge, a miniature railway and a Dutch castle - the castle outlived the park, the premier and the government.

**Location:** 49.1353, -123.0902

## Introduction

On 7 September 2010 the demolition crews came for Fantasy Gardens, and by the end of it almost nothing at the corner of Steveston Highway and No. 5 Road was left standing. One building survived: a stone castle, a replica of Coevorden Castle in the Netherlands, donated to the people of Vancouver by the city of Coevorden for Expo 86 and possibly modelled on the ancestral home of Captain George Vancouver's family. It served for a while as the sales centre for the condominiums going up around it. Its destination, once the paperwork settles, is a City of Richmond day care.

## Turtles and Perennials

It started as a garden centre. From 1965 the site was home to an enormous plant store in the Art Knapp's chain - the chain founded by Arthur William Knapp, a longtime Victoria resident - and for a great many Lower Mainland families the draw was not the perennials but the turtle-filled pond, which children would demand to visit whether or not anyone needed potting soil. The store eventually left, relocating about a mile west to a vacant nursery near Alderbridge Way and Minoru Boulevard. What it left behind was a large piece of suburban Richmond already primed to be treated as a destination rather than a shop.

## The Bible Hedge

In 1984 the property was bought by Bill Vander Zalm, then a provincial MLA and soon to be Premier of British Columbia, who gave the place the identity it is remembered for. Stone buildings went up in the manner of the Middle Ages. Behind them lay the Biblical Gardens, filled with religious icons, including a hedge clipped into the shape of a Bible. It was sincere, strange, and enormously photogenic. It was also the beginning of the site's most consequential chapter: his later sale of the park became the subject of conflict-of-interest charges, which led to his resignation in 1991 and contributed to the defeat of the Social Credit government - a provincial era ending, in part, over a theme park.

## The Little Train

From the 1970s into the early 1990s, a small-scale amusement park ran on the grounds, and its biggest attraction was a miniature railroad that carried visitors on a loop through the park and the gardens. Anyone who grew up in Richmond in those decades has a photograph of themselves folded into one of those carriages. When the fair shut down, most of the track was pulled up. The train survives on film: it was shot for the X-Files episode 'The Calusari,' which means the last extended footage of a beloved suburban kiddie railway exists inside an hour of television about a demonic child.

## Standing Set

A permanent medieval village twenty minutes from a major production hub is a gift to a location scout, and Vancouver's film industry used it relentlessly. Fantasy Gardens stood in for Halloweentown in Halloweentown II: Kalabar's Revenge. Stargate SG-1 shot 'Revisions' there and Stargate Atlantis shot 'Irresponsible.' Supernatural used it for 'Monster Movie,' Highlander: The Series for both 'Shadows' and 'Blackmail,' Sliders for 'Into The Mystic,' and Psych for 'Christmas Joy.' The features include I'll Be Home for Christmas, Cousins, and The Boy Who Cried Werewolf. Music videos used it too. For roughly two decades, if a screen needed a castle and a budget did not stretch to Europe, this parking lot in Richmond obliged.

## The Gardens

The property moved on. Vander Zalm sold it in the early 1990s for $16 million to the Chinese-born Filipino-Taiwanese businessman Tan Yu, whose company AsiaWorld held it until 2007, when the local developer Townline bought it and began converting the land into a retail and residential project called, with a certain economy, The Gardens. The southern portion is now a condominium complex with a Loblaws supermarket. The ground the amusement park occupied is slated to become a twelve-acre public park serving the residential buildings around it. Turtle pond to garden centre to Biblical theme park to standing film set to a park and a grocery store - four complete reinventions on one suburban corner, with a Dutch castle looking on through all of them.

## Flight Context

The site sits at 49.1353 N, 123.0902 W in Richmond, British Columbia, at Steveston Highway and No. 5 Road. This is squarely inside Vancouver International's (CYVR) terminal airspace - the airport is about 7 km northwest - so a clearance is mandatory and low transit is not casual. From a cleared 1,500-2,500 ft the landmarks are the grid of Richmond's numbered roads, the south arm of the Fraser River just below the site, the Highway 99 corridor running to the George Massey Tunnel crossing, and the CYVR runways on Sea Island to the northwest. Boundary Bay Airport (CZBB) lies roughly 10 km southeast across the river. The Fraser delta is flat and featureless in poor visibility, and autumn and winter radiation fog can close the whole lowland while the North Shore mountains stay in sun.
