# Saturna Island

An aquarium sent a harpooner to East Point in 1964 to kill an orca for a sculpture. The whale survived the shot — and changed what the world believed about killer whales.

**Location:** 48.7842, -123.1481

**Categories:** Islands of the Gulf Islands, Populated places in the Capital Regional District, National parks of Canada, Whale watching, Orca conservation

## Introduction

In the spring of 1964, the Vancouver Public Aquarium wanted a dead killer whale. The plan was straightforward by the standards of the day: commission a sculptor to harpoon one, then use the carcass as a model for a display. On 20 May, at East Point on Saturna Island, the harpoon struck — and the whale did not die. What happened next undid a century of assumptions. Taken back to Vancouver and named Moby Doll, the animal lived about three months, through the summer of 1964, and in that time researchers listened to a living orca for the first time. They discovered the rapid pulses of clicks it used to see with sound, and they examined a brain with an enormous auditory nerve. The whale that had been shot as a raw material for a statue turned out to be intelligent, communicative, and not remotely the monster the harpoon implied.

## Long Nose

The island has been called several things. The Indigenous name meant Long Nose, describing the long northeastern shoreline that tapers away to a point at East Point — an accurate piece of geography that anyone looking at a chart will recognise immediately. The current name came from a Spanish ship. In 1791 the naval schooner Santa Saturnina, under the pilot José María Narváez, and a longboat from the packet San Carlos explored this coast as part of an expedition commanded by Juan Pantoja y Arriaga. They attached the name to East Point alone. The contraction to Saturna, applied to the whole island, was made the following year by Dionisio Alcalá Galiano. European settlers began arriving in the 1800s, but Saturna developed far more slowly than its neighbours in the Southern Gulf Islands — too isolated, too steep. That slowness is why the island looks the way it does now.

## Surrounded on Three Sides by a Border

Saturna covers about 31 square kilometres and sits roughly halfway between Vancouver Island and the British Columbia mainland, the easternmost of the Gulf Islands. What makes its position remarkable is that the Canada-United States border wraps around it on three sides: Point Roberts, Washington, lies to the north, and the San Juan Islands lie east and south. From the high ground you are looking at another country in three directions. There is a First Nations reserve on the island for the Tsawout and Tseycum Nations. The permanent population runs around 350 people — small enough that the island has no traffic signal and no grocery chain, and large enough to sustain a community club and a heritage committee. In summer the number climbs as cabins fill and the ferries run heavy.

## Half the Island, Given Away

Roughly half of Saturna is inside the Gulf Islands National Park Reserve, established in 2003. It was assembled from several sources, and one of them was simply a gift: Ulla Ressner and John Fry donated ecologically sensitive land, which was combined with existing provincial parks, an Ecological Reserve, and other Crown land. The result is a park you reach on foot. Mount Warburton Pike, the highest summit in the Outer Gulf Islands, carries a communication tower and a view across the whole archipelago. At Narvaez Bay, seven walk-in or kayak-in backcountry campsites sit a kilometre down a trail from the nearest parking lot, with a side path to Monarch Head looking out over Boundary Pass toward the San Juans — no potable water, no campfires, in any season. The Lyall Creek trail follows one of the few salmon-bearing creeks in the Southern Gulf Islands through second-growth forest. At Winter Cove, the tide races through Boat Pass hard enough to make it a genuine shortcut for small boats and a genuine hazard for anyone who misreads it.

## The Fog Alarm Building

East Point is where people come to watch whales from land, which is unusual — most whale watching requires a boat. The southern resident orcas, J, K, and L pods, pass close in during the summer months, and Bigg's transient orcas move through year-round. The southern residents are in serious trouble: listed as endangered in the United States in 2005 and under Canada's Species at Risk Act, their population stood at 73 animals in the July 2024 census, squeezed between depleted Chinook salmon runs, chemical contamination carried in their own tissue, and vessel noise that masks the echolocation Moby Doll first revealed to science. In the old fog alarm building at East Point, the Saturna Island Heritage Committee runs a very small museum where the whale's story is told, alongside an Environment Canada monitoring station and a shoreline of weathered sandstone.

## Lamb, Every Canada Day Since 1950

For all its parkland, Saturna is a working community with its own fixed rituals. Every first of July since 1950, the island has held the Saturna Island Lamb Barbecue, with a share of the proceeds going to the Saturna Community Club — an unbroken run of more than seventy-five years. Roughly sixty geocaches are hidden around the island, built and maintained by Parks Canada and the Saturna Ecological Education Centre, which is either a modern curiosity or exactly the kind of thing a place with this much shoreline and this few people would invent. Thomson Community Park has a pebble beach, a picnic shelter, and a nine-hole disc golf course. BC Ferries sails daily into Lyall Harbour from Swartz Bay and Tsawwassen, floatplanes come in from Vancouver and downtown Victoria, and the government wharf handles the rest. Getting here takes planning. That is the filter that keeps Saturna what it is.

## Flight Context

Saturna Island lies at 48.7842 degrees north, 123.1481 degrees west, the easternmost of British Columbia's Southern Gulf Islands. Best viewed from 3,000 to 5,000 feet — the island's shape is its own best identifier, a mountainous 31-square-kilometre landmass with a long tapering northeastern arm ending at East Point, the feature that gave it its Indigenous name, Long Nose. Mount Warburton Pike, the highest point in the Outer Gulf Islands, carries a visible communication tower. Boundary Pass runs along the southern shore and marks the international border, with the San Juan Islands beyond; Point Roberts, Washington, lies to the north across the Strait of Georgia. Lyall Harbour on the west side is the ferry and floatplane landing. IMPORTANT: the Canada-United States border wraps this island on three sides — verify current airspace boundaries and border-crossing requirements before flying in this area. Nearest airports: Victoria International (CYYJ) to the southwest, Vancouver International (CYVR) to the northeast, and Friday Harbor (KFHR) on San Juan Island, Washington. Strong tidal currents and channel effects can produce mechanical turbulence at low level between the islands.
