# Penelakut Island

The island carried a British captain's name for 159 years. In that time it survived a massacre, a naval bombardment, and a residential school its survivors called Alcatraz.

**Location:** 48.9650, -123.6469

**Categories:** gulf-islands, indigenous-nations, residential-school-history, colonial-history, british-columbia

## Introduction

In 1851 a Royal Navy survey party worked its way through a cluster of five islands in the Strait of Georgia, and, as survey parties did, handed out names. The two largest became Thetis, after the 36-gun frigate they were riding, and Kuper, after her captain, Augustus Kuper. Neither man nor ship had any particular connection to the place. The island already had a name and had had one for a very long time: it belonged to the Penelakut, and was called after them. The colonial name stuck for 159 years. In 2010 the older one came back, which sounds like a small administrative correction and is not.

## Low, Warm, and Awkward to Drain

Penelakut Island covers 8.66 square kilometres and does not look much like its neighbours. Most of the southern Gulf Islands are built of sandstone bluffs and exposed rock; Penelakut is subdued, with few outcrops and a good deal of poorly drained ground. It sits deep in the rain shadow of Vancouver Island and takes only about 850 millimetres of rain a year, giving it a climate of mild winters and warm, dry summers that supports an unusual mix of species for this latitude. Telegraph Harbour indents the west side. Car and passenger ferries run frequently from Chemainus on Vancouver Island, about six kilometres away across Stuart Channel -- close enough to see clearly, far enough that the water matters.

## A Morning in 1861

In 1861 roughly 300 Heiltsuk people, referred to at the time as Bella Bella, were ordered away from Victoria by Governor James Douglas and set out north in nineteen large canoes. On the journey home they stopped here, and at dawn they attacked. The Penelakut community numbered about 400 people. About 225 of them were killed -- more than half the village, in a single morning, while most were still asleep. A visiting Nanaimo chief named Winni-win-chin got away and reached Nanaimo, where he reported what had happened to A.G. Horne of the Hudson's Bay Company post, who sent canoe men down to Victoria with the news. It is one of the deadliest recorded attacks in the region's history, and its chain of causation begins with a colonial governor deciding which Indigenous people were permitted to remain near his capital.

## The Gunboat

Two years later the violence arrived from the other direction. On 20 April 1863, the British gunboat HMS Forward opened fire on the village here. Her captain believed it was sheltering members of the Lamalchi -- a separate people -- who were sought in connection with two recent incidents in the Gulf Islands in which three Europeans had been killed. The Forward's guns levelled the village. Her captives were carried to Victoria, tried, and hanged. Whatever the merits of the pursuit, the community that lost its homes that day was not the community being pursued. Within twenty-four months the Penelakut had been attacked by people arriving from the north and shelled by an empire arriving from the south.

## The School They Called Alcatraz

From 1890 to 1978, the Catholic Church operated a residential school on this island. Children were brought here from communities up and down the coast, and the survivors gave it a name of their own: Alcatraz, after the prison in San Francisco Bay that nobody escaped from. Some children tried anyway. They built rafts and makeshift boats and pushed out into Stuart Channel toward the Vancouver Island shore they could see from the school windows, fleeing hunger and abuse. In 1959 two sisters, Beverly and Patricia Joseph, are presumed to have drowned making that crossing. Their sister, Karen McCallum, is still asking what happened to them. In 2002, a Catholic Oblate brother named Glenn Doughty, who had been employed at the school, was sentenced to three years in prison for crimes committed against eleven children there in the 1960s and 1970s, prosecuted under the charge names the law used at the time. Former students have long said the abuse was not confined to one man. In July 2021 the Penelakut Tribe announced that more than 160 undocumented and unmarked graves had been identified near the school site.

## A Home, Not a Destination

About 300 people live on Penelakut Island today, members of the Penelakut Band, and the community runs its own school. There is a longhouse. Canoe races are held here. The ferry from Chemainus is a commuter service for residents, and the island is not a Gulf Islands holiday stop in the way that Salt Spring or Galiano are -- visitors come by invitation, not by browsing. The restoration of the name in 2010 removed a British officer from the map and put back the word the people here use for themselves. Everything on this island that was taken has not been returned. The name was, and it was the community that took it back.

## Flight Context

Located at 48.97 N, 123.65 W in the southern Gulf Islands of British Columbia, in Stuart Channel between Vancouver Island and Salt Spring Island. From the air Penelakut is distinguishable from its neighbours by its low, flat profile and lack of the sandstone bluffs that define most of the Gulf Islands; it sits immediately south of Thetis Island, the two nearly joined, with the sheltered inlet of Telegraph Harbour between them on the western side. Chemainus lies about 3.5 nm west across the channel, with ferry wakes routinely visible between the two. Best viewed at 2,000-3,500 ft AGL. Nanaimo Airport (CYCD) is roughly 10 nm northwest; Victoria International (CYYJ) about 21 nm southeast; Ganges Water Aerodrome (CAX6) on Salt Spring Island is a few miles east and generates floatplane traffic through the channel. Note that the island is Penelakut reserve land and a residential community -- overflight should be conducted at a respectful altitude. Weather is rain-shadow mild with excellent summer visibility; winter brings low stratus and channel fog.
