# Gulf Islands

An archipelago in the rain shadow of Vancouver Island where olives ripen, Garry oaks survive on the ground nobody wanted, and not one island is connected to another by a bridge.

**Location:** 48.9500, -123.5330

**Categories:** Gulf Islands, Archipelagoes of British Columbia, Strait of Georgia, Ecology, Agriculture in British Columbia

## Introduction

Olives are not a Canadian crop. The country's winters are too long and its summers too wet and short, and across almost all of it an olive tree is a houseplant with delusions. Then there is Salt Spring Island, where a man known locally as Banana Joe Clemente grew dozens of olives from seed starting in 1993 and planted a grove around a bocce pit, and Pender Island, where Andrew Butt put in his first tree in 2001 using Frantoio and Leccino cuttings brought up from California. The Gulf Islands sit in a pocket of Mediterranean climate that has no business existing at 49 degrees north, and everything interesting about them follows from that fact.

## In the Shadow of a Mountain Range

Pacific storms arrive on this coast loaded with water and run straight into the mountainous spine of Vancouver Island, which forces them up, wrings them out, and lets what is left drift east. By the time that air reaches the Gulf Islands it has very little rain to give. The result is a dry-summer climate on islands surrounded by one of the wettest coastlines on earth: arbutus trees peeling their red bark over sandstone shelves worn into honeycomb by salt and wind, dry grass by July, and a growing season that supports crops nobody expects this far north. The same conditions that let olives fruit also gave the islands their reputation as a refuge, and the population reflects it, a mix of farmers, artists, retirees, and people who arrived in the 1960s and never left.

## A Gulf That Officially Isn't

The name is a fossil. When George Vancouver charted these waters he called the body of water the Gulf of Georgia, a designation that then included what are now the San Juan Islands, before the San Juan boundary dispute drew a line through the archipelago and gave those islands to the United States. Geologically the split means nothing; the San Juans and the Gulf Islands are one formation. Later cartography renamed the main body the Strait of Georgia, which strictly refers only to the wide open water between the mainland and Vancouver Island and not to the tangle of channels among the islands themselves. Locals still say the Gulf, meaning all of it: Active Pass between Galiano and Mayne, Trincomali Channel, Sansum Narrows, Malaspina Strait up by Texada. In 2010 the whole system, together with Juan de Fuca Strait and Puget Sound, was given the collective name Salish Sea.

## Nobody Agrees Where They End

BC Geographical Names holds that the Gulf Islands were originally and properly the southern cluster: Gabriola at the north end, Saturna to the southeast, D'Arcy to the southwest, and the hundreds of islands and islets among them. Then in the 1990s people started applying the name to every island in the Strait of Georgia, which forced the invention of Southern Gulf Islands as a distinguishing label, a term the naming authority flatly calls a misnomer. The rest became the Northern Gulf Islands, divided from the southern group by a rough line running from Nanaimo across to the mouth of the Fraser. Quadra Island, well up the coast, is now increasingly described as the northernmost of the Gulf Islands, which would have puzzled anyone using the term a century ago. The Discovery Islands, Howe Sound, and the Fraser delta islands stay out of it entirely.

## The Oaks on the Worthless Ground

The islands hold one of the last strongholds of the Garry oak ecosystem, and its survival here is an accident of poor soil. Garry oak meadows grow on shallow, rocky, exposed ground on valley floors and south-facing slopes, precisely the land that settlers over the past century and a half found useless for farming or building. Everywhere the soil was better, the ecosystem was ploughed under. Roughly five percent of Garry oak habitat survives in a natural state, and of the approximately 350 species it supports, 91 now appear on British Columbia's list of species at risk. These meadows carry more plant diversity than any other terrestrial ecosystem on the coast, including the camas whose blue bulbs were a staple food for Coast Salish peoples. After the first international symposium on the ecosystem met in Victoria in 1999 and declared it endangered, a recovery team of 22 people drawn from government and non-government agencies formed to work on restoration.

## No Bridges

Nothing here connects to anything else by road. BC Ferries links the larger populated islands to terminals near Victoria, Nanaimo, and Vancouver, and that is the whole transportation network. The single exception proves the rule perfectly: North and South Pender were one island until a canal was dug through the isthmus in 1903, and the bridge that now spans it reconnects something that was artificially cut apart. Proposals to link islands to each other or to the mainland surface regularly and die reliably, defeated by cost, terrain, fragile ecosystems, small populations, and the entirely predictable objections of people who moved to an island on purpose. Protection has moved the other way. BC Parks has run provincial parks here for decades, the oldest being Mount Maxwell on Salt Spring; Parks Canada established the Gulf Islands National Park Reserve in 2003; and in 2012 it published a feasibility study for a national marine conservation area in the southern Strait of Georgia to protect the water between the islands as well as the land.

## Flight Context

The Gulf Islands lie centred around 48.95N, 123.53W, filling the southern Strait of Georgia between Vancouver Island and the mainland of British Columbia. From the air they are one of the most distinctive landscapes in Canada: dozens of long, narrow, northwest-southeast trending islands with sandstone shorelines and dark conifer ridges, separated by a maze of channels including Active Pass, Trincomali Channel, and Sansum Narrows. Salt Spring is the largest; Galiano, Mayne, Pender, and Saturna spread southeast toward the international boundary and the San Juan Islands beyond it. Victoria International (CYYJ) sits about 15nm south, Nanaimo (CYCD) about 20nm northwest, and Vancouver International (CYVR) about 25nm northeast, with heavy floatplane and commuter traffic crossing the islands between all three plus water aerodromes at Ganges and Montague Harbour. Best viewed at 2,500 to 5,000 feet. Watch for the HVDC transmission spans crossing Sansum Narrows and between Galiano and Parker Island, and expect summer haze plus strong afternoon thermals over the ridgelines.
