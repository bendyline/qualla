# Trinity Western University

Canada's largest Christian university sits on 157 acres outside Fort Langley — a school that won at the Supreme Court of Canada in 2001 and lost there in 2018, on very nearly the same question.

**Location:** 49.1405, -122.5996

**Categories:** Trinity Western University, Universities and colleges established in 1962, Evangelical universities and colleges in Canada, Langley, British Columbia (district municipality), Private universities and colleges in British Columbia, Canadian law and courts

## Introduction

It won at the Supreme Court of Canada, and seventeen years later it lost there, on a question that looked almost identical. In 2001 the court told British Columbia's teacher regulator that it had no grounds to refuse Trinity Western's education graduates. In June 2018 the same court, over four sets of reasons, ruled 7–2 that law societies in Ontario and British Columbia had been entitled to refuse the school's proposed law graduates. What changed in between was not the university's beliefs, which held steady, and not really the law. What changed was the country around the 157 acres of fields and red brick outside Fort Langley.

## A Campus Chosen on a Map

In 1957 a committee of the Evangelical Free Church of America sat down to decide whether the Pacific Coast needed a Christian liberal arts college, and then where to put one. The answer was strikingly literal: somewhere between Vancouver and Seattle, on rural land in what is now the Township of Langley. Trinity Junior College opened in 1962 as a two-year transfer school, became Trinity Western College a decade later, started awarding bachelor's degrees in 1980, and in 1985 was legislated into a private university by the Trinity Western University Act. That makes it the fourth-oldest university in British Columbia, behind only UBC, the University of Victoria, and Emily Carr. The campus sits on the edge of Fort Langley, the Hudson's Bay Company fur post that James Douglas made the provisional capital of the new colony in 1858 — a residence hall carries Douglas's name. Hanson Chapel, finished in 1962, still stands among the thirty-six buildings that followed it.

## Eighteen Classes Before You Start

Every undergraduate here works through the University Core, eighteen classes spanning fine arts, natural sciences, philosophy, history, sociology and religious studies, plus a two-semester English requirement — and most of them taught by full-time professors rather than teaching assistants. Students assemble their own Core from a wide menu, which surprises people expecting a rigid curriculum. The campus contains an oddity too: Catholic Pacific College, a separately administered Catholic institution on Glover Road, operates as the constituent Catholic college of an evangelical university, with Mass four times a week. ACTS Seminaries, a partnership of several denominations, trains ministers here too. Roughly 48 undergraduate and 19 graduate degrees are on offer, an executive MBA arrived in 2021, and in 2022 the first doctoral cohort in the university's history began work — a PhD in nursing.

## The Covenant and the Courts

The Community Covenant asked students to pledge conduct: no hazing, no harassment, no plagiarism or theft, no illegal drugs, no alcohol on campus — and no sexual relationships outside a marriage between a woman and a man. That last clause is what carried the school into two decades of litigation. When Trinity Western proposed a law school in 2012 and won provincial approval in December 2013, law societies across Canada began voting on whether to accredit its graduates. Just over eight thousand BC lawyers went to a referendum; 74 per cent said no. Advanced Education Minister Amrik Virk pulled the province's approval in December 2014. The courts split badly — Nova Scotia and British Columbia found for the university, Ontario for the law society, whose Divisional Court observed in 2015 that the covenant's exclusions reached not only LGBTQ people but unmarried couples and anyone of another faith. The following year The Province reported gay students' accounts of a culture of shame on campus. For students who were gay and wanted the education, the pledge was not an abstraction; it asked them to sign against themselves.

## August 2018

The Supreme Court ruled on 15 June 2018. Eight weeks later, on 9 August, Trinity Western's board of governors voted to make the Community Covenant optional for current and incoming students, effective that fall. Robert Kuhn, president at the time, framed the decision as making the school "inclusive of all students wishing to learn from a Christian viewpoint and underlying philosophy." Faculty still sign an annual statement of faith. Chapel runs every weekday morning and attendance is voluntary; around four in five undergraduates identify as Christian, and students of other traditions are enrolled and supported. The law school was never built. What the long fight produced instead was a Canadian precedent on how far a private religious institution's rules may reach into a regulated profession — argued, in the end, over a school that had already been operating quietly in the Fraser Valley for more than fifty years.

## Spartans, Ottawa, and What Comes Next

The athletic program has been startlingly successful for a school this size. The Spartans, named when the men's basketball team first took the floor in 1964, have won 14 national championships and 34 Canada West titles, playing out of the Langley Events Centre. The student newspaper, Mars' Hill, which began as an underground paper in 1988 and was shut down after two issues, came back as the official one and won the Associated Collegiate Press National Pacemaker Award in 2008 and again in 2010. Third- and fourth-year students can spend a credited semester at Booth House in Ottawa, the 1901 mansion of lumber baron John R. Booth that the university bought in 2000 and that is now a National Historic Site, interning on Parliament Hill. Today roughly five thousand students study across Langley, two Richmond sites and online, with more than 30,000 alumni scattered worldwide — and in 2025 Trinity Evangelical Divinity School, of Deerfield, Illinois, announced it would join Trinity Western and close its Chicago-area campus.

## Flight Context

Trinity Western's Langley campus sits at 49.1405° N, 122.5996° W, on the south side of the Fraser River just southeast of Fort Langley and about 45 km southeast of downtown Vancouver. Fly it at 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL: the 157-acre campus reads from the air as a tight cluster of brick buildings, playing fields and parking set in otherwise green Township of Langley farmland, with Glover Road running past and the Fraser River and the restored Fort Langley palisade about 4 km north. Langley Regional (CYNJ) is roughly 6 nm west and is the natural base; Pitt Meadows (CYPK) lies about 10 nm north across the river, Abbotsford International (CYXX) about 14 nm east, Boundary Bay (CZBB) about 17 nm southwest, and Vancouver International (CYVR) about 27 nm west. This is busy airspace under the Vancouver terminal area with several nearby circuits — consult the VFR terminal chart. The US border is 8 nm south. Fraser Valley radiation fog is common on calm autumn and winter mornings and can persist into midday; summer afternoons are typically clear with Mount Baker prominent to the southeast.
