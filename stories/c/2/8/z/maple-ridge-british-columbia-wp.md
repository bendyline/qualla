# Maple Ridge, British Columbia

Fifty families on thirty-three thousand acres became a city of ninety thousand, and it took the paperwork exactly 140 years to catch up - to the day.

**Location:** 49.2167, -122.6000

**Categories:** Maple Ridge, British Columbia, Cities in British Columbia, Lower Mainland, Populated places in Greater Vancouver, Populated places on the Fraser River

## Introduction

On 12 September 1874, fifty-odd families scattered across 33,000 acres of forest and river bottom incorporated themselves as a district municipality. It was a generous amount of paperwork for a very small number of people, and it made Maple Ridge the fifth-oldest municipality in British Columbia, behind only New Westminster, Victoria, Langley and Chilliwack. A hundred and forty years later, on 12 September 2014, the province signed off on a change of status. The district became a city - on the same calendar day, to the anniversary, as the original incorporation. By then the population was heading for 90,990, the forest had thinned into subdivisions, and the twin summits north of town were still doing what they had always done: catching the last of the light after the valley floor had gone dark.

## Land of the Moss

None of this begins in 1874. The name Katzie comes from a Hunʼqumiʼnum word meaning land of the moss, and the q̓ic̓əy̓ - the Katzie First Nation's name for themselves - hold traditional territory that takes in the entire Pitt watershed, the Alouette watershed, the Fraser itself, and the country running south toward Boundary Bay. They speak həṅq̓əmín̓əḿ, the downriver dialect of Halkomelem, still, after everything done to end it. The band office sits in neighbouring Pitt Meadows; as of February 2024 the nation numbered 563 people, 310 of them living on reserve. Maple Ridge and the Kwantlen First Nation's territory overlap here too, which is why the local museum's own signage describes the ground under the city as unceded. Two centuries of deeds and plat maps have not changed that word.

## Haney's Hundred and Forty-Five Acres

Ask anyone downtown where they are and a fair number will say Haney rather than Maple Ridge, which is a stubbornness worth explaining. Thomas Haney bought 145 acres near the river, built a house on it in 1883, and the settlement that grew around him took his name. Port Haney below it was a steamboat landing when the Fraser was the highway; today it is a heritage district with a West Coast Express commuter station where the wharf used to be. Haney's descendants lived in that 1883 house for three generations, moving out only in 1979, when the family handed it over to become a museum. The city's official downtown core has been Maple Ridge for decades. The habit of calling it Haney shows no sign of dying.

## The Golden Eyries

North of town, a double summit rises to 1,716 metres with more than a kilometre of clean prominence beneath it - the southernmost outpost of the Garibaldi Ranges, and visible from most of the western Lower Mainland on a decent day. The name is an accident. Early settlers called the feature the Golden Eyries, apparently for golden eagles nesting near the top, and the phrase wore down over time into Golden Ears. The corruption stuck because it happened to fit: the twin peaks do look like a cat's ears, and at sunset they go the colour the name promises while everything below is already in shadow. The park behind them draws hikers up trails that begin in second-growth cedar and end above the treeline; the cable-stayed bridge that carries traffic south across the Fraser to Langley took the mountains' name too.

## Standing In for Somewhere Else

Maple Ridge has spent fifty years pretending to be other places. Ridge Film Studios operates downtown out of a converted retail space, and the city's streets, farmland and river flats have doubled for small-town America in The X-Files, Smallville, Bordertown, Bird on a Wire and Percy Jackson & the Olympians: The Lightning Thief. The old Bordertown western set outlasted its own series and turned up years later in an episode of Legends of Tomorrow, a set pretending to be a place pretending to be a set. Meanwhile forestry - the thing that actually cleared this ground - remains the largest private-sector employer, Interfor among them, alongside makers of building materials, poles and yachts.

## Goalie, Outfielder, Hall of Famer

For a city this size, the export record in professional sport is absurd. Cam Neely and Andrew Ladd came out of here, and so did Champ Car driver Greg Moore. So did Larry Walker, born 1 December 1966, who wanted to be an NHL goaltender and was not going to be one; he took up baseball instead and was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 2020 on his tenth and final ballot with 76.6 percent, a hair over the threshold, in the last year he was eligible. Tyler O'Neill followed him to the majors. The town's other bet on the future has been slower: E-One Moli Energy, which has made lithium-ion cells here for years, announced a $796 million plant on 16 November 2023 that was to start production in 2028 and employ 450 people, then suspended the plan on 22 November 2024. Maple Ridge is still waiting to hear.

## Flight Context

Maple Ridge centres on 49.2167°N, 122.60°W, on the north bank of the Fraser River about 40 km east of downtown Vancouver, with a townsite elevation near 80 m spread over 267.8 km². Fly the north side of the Fraser at 2,000-3,000 ft AGL: the city fills the bench between the river and the mountain front, laid out on the long east-west lines of Highway 7 and Dewdney Trunk Road. The unmistakable landmarks are the double summit of Golden Ears at 1,716 m directly north, the white cable-stayed Golden Ears Bridge crossing the Fraser to Langley on the west side of town, and the long dark finger of Pitt Lake beyond the ridge to the northwest. Alouette Lake sits in the valley behind the peaks. CYPK Pitt Meadows lies roughly 10 km west and is one of Canada's busiest general-aviation fields - expect constant circuit and helicopter traffic. CYNJ Langley Regional is about 15 km southwest across the river, CYXX Abbotsford some 30 km southeast, and CYVR Vancouver International around 40 km west. Summer afternoons give clean visibility and the classic low-sun glow on the Golden Ears; November through February the valley fills with rain and stratus, and the peaks vanish for days at a time.
