# Secret Harbor School

A boarding school for troubled teenagers on an island with no phone line to the mainland, remembered now mainly through court records and escape attempts.

**Location:** 48.5513, -122.6941

**Categories:** washington, san-juan-islands, island, defunct-school, social-history

## Introduction

In 1974 a newspaper in Spokane covered a lawsuit brought by a young man from Michigan against a boarding school on a small island in northern Puget Sound. He alleged that he had been abused there, that he had been denied medical attention, and that staff had made him shovel raw sewage. Whether any of that was proved, the article does not say. But it recorded one detail almost in passing, as background rather than accusation, and that detail is the one that stays with you: no phone line reached the mainland. Children were being sent to Secret Harbor on Cypress Island from Chicago, from Los Angeles, from Canada, from France and the Philippines, and once they arrived there was no way to call anyone.

## Farms First

It began as Secret Harbor Farms, and the original idea was of its era and not obviously sinister: take young people who were struggling, put them somewhere quiet, give them animals to look after and work to do, and see whether structure and open air might accomplish what schools and cities had not. Plenty of institutions across North America were built on that premise. Some of them helped people.

Cypress Island offered the setting in abundance. It is one of the larger islands in the San Juan group and one of the least developed, steep and forested, with a sheltered bay on its eastern side. The school appeared in the state record early: in 1955 Secret Harbor Farms went to the Washington State Supreme Court as a defendant in a case about land access rights. Even then, the question was about who could get to the place and on what terms.

## Fifty-Two Attempts

In 1978 a judge issued a warning to the school about the number of residents trying to leave. The figure behind that warning was fifty-two escape attempts over the previous three years - roughly one every three weeks, from an island.

The individual stories have the quality of adventure fiction until you remember the ages involved. In 1985 a resident faked an injury in order to be taken off the island for treatment, then walked out of the hospital and disappeared; nobody caught up with him. In 1987 three residents got off Cypress, made it to Guemes Island, and left on the public ferry like anyone else. Getting off an island in cold water without a boat takes planning, nerve, and a considerable determination not to be where you are. Fifty-two attempts is not a discipline problem. It is a sustained message from the people who lived there.

## Sent Away

A school article from 1976 noted with evident pride that alumni had come from Chicago, Los Angeles, Canada, France, and the Philippines. It was meant as a measure of reach and reputation. Read now, it measures something else: the distance families were willing to put between themselves and a difficult adolescent, and the size of the market for doing so.

This is the shape of the whole industry that grew up around what came to be called troubled teens - programs positioned at the end of long journeys, in places where oversight was thin and visiting was hard. Isolation was not an unfortunate side effect of the setting. It was part of what was being purchased. That does not mean every day at Secret Harbor was a bad one, or that no staff member ever did good work there. It means the design had a flaw that the design itself was unlikely to surface.

## What Came Out Later

The record hardened over time. In 1990 two residents were charged with kidnapping and assaulting an employee after obtaining a shotgun and a pistol; they fled by power boat and were later arrested - an incident in which the employee was plainly the victim, and which also says something about how the place was running. The school eventually closed. The property sat unoccupied until 2005, when new owners bought it with plans for a luxury resort and spa; local resistance saw those plans off, and they did not happen.

In 2023 the Seattle Times reported lawsuits against the school alleging child abuse during the 1980s and 1990s. Those are allegations, working their way through a legal system that moves slowly on matters this old, brought by people who were children when the events they describe took place. Cypress Island has meanwhile become the kind of place people go to for quiet. A 2020 novel by Michael J Moore borrowed the name for a story about a school on an island in the San Juans - fiction, but the title tells you what the phrase has come to mean.

## Flight Context

Secret Harbor lies at 48.551N, 122.694W on the eastern side of Cypress Island, in the northern San Juan archipelago between Guemes Channel and Rosario Strait. From 2,000 to 3,500 feet Cypress is easy to identify: a steep, dark, largely undeveloped island with the notch of Secret Harbor indenting its east coast, Guemes Island and Anacortes to the southeast, Orcas Island across Rosario Strait to the west, and Lummi Island north. There is no public airfield on the island. Nearest fields are Anacortes (74S) 6 nm southeast, Skagit Regional (KBVS) to the southeast on the mainland, Orcas Island (KORS) west, NAS Whidbey Island (KNUW) south - expect military traffic there - and Bellingham (KBLI) northeast. Tidal currents run hard through Rosario Strait and the surrounding passes, summer fog is common at dawn, and wind accelerates through the channels between the islands.
