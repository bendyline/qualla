# Scia'new First Nation

The name means salmon place. In 1850 the people who held it signed away nearly everything for a stack of blankets, and they are still at the negotiating table.

**Location:** 48.3373, -123.6077

**Categories:** indigenous-nations, coast-salish, treaty-history, vancouver-island, british-columbia

## Introduction

The name is a description of a food supply. In Klallam, Beecher Bay is xʷčiyánəxʷ -- built from xʷ-, meaning location, and the root for salmon -- and it is usually rendered in English as the place of the big fish. The Saanich dialect of Northern Straits borrowed the Klallam word and made it W̱ĆIYÁNEW̱, which English-speaking clerks eventually flattened into Scia'new. Four different ancestral languages are recognized by the community here, and not all of them are still spoken aloud. But the oldest name on this bay has survived every spelling inflicted on it, and it still means the same thing: come here, there are fish.

## Seven Islands and a Bay

The Scia'new lands sit on the exposed southern tip of Vancouver Island, where the Strait of Juan de Fuca funnels weather in from the open Pacific. The holdings are a bay and a scatter of small islands: Beecher Bay itself -- spelled Becher on some charts and Beecher on others, a two-century-old typographical argument nobody has settled -- along with Fraser Island, Lamb Island, Long-neck Island, Twin Island, Village Island, and Whale Island. As of 2024 the nation numbered 274 people. Ethnohistoric research identifies the community at Beecher Bay as Klallam who moved across from the Olympic Peninsula in the middle of the nineteenth century, with territory reaching between Sooke Inlet and Albert Head. The strait that appears on modern maps as an international boundary was, for those families, simply the water between one set of relatives and another.

## Three Days in the Spring of 1850

Between the 29th of April and the 1st of May, 1850, James Douglas of the Hudson's Bay Company met with representatives of the Songhees, Esquimalt, Beecher Bay, and Sooke peoples. Over the following four years he concluded fourteen such agreements, covering roughly 927 square kilometres of Vancouver Island around Victoria, Saanich, Sooke, Nanaimo, and Port Hardy. The documents, written in English, state that the signatories surrender their land entirely and forever to the Company, while retaining their village sites and enclosed fields and the liberty to hunt over unoccupied lands and to carry on their fisheries as formerly. Payment came in blankets and pounds sterling. The chiefs who agreed to these terms marked the papers with an X. What they understood themselves to be agreeing to, and what the written text was later held to mean, have been the subject of litigation and argument ever since -- because those few surviving clauses about fishing and hunting turned out to be the most durable Indigenous rights language in British Columbia.

## The Clause That Outlasted the Company

The Hudson's Bay Company is long gone from the land business, and the colony that Douglas governed became a province. The treaty text stayed. British Columbia now acknowledges that Scia'new holds Douglas Treaty rights to fish and hunt as formerly within its historic traditional territory -- an obligation carried forward from a document signed when Victoria was a fur-trade fort of a few hundred people. That is an unusual inheritance. Most of British Columbia was never treated for at all, which is why so much of the province's modern legal landscape is built on unresolved title. Here, a handful of nineteenth-century sentences written for the convenience of a trading company have become a permanent constraint on the Crown.

## Still at the Table

A hundred and seventy years after the blankets changed hands, Scia'new is negotiating a modern treaty. It does so as a member of the Te'mexw Treaty Association, alongside the Malahat, Nanoose, Songhees, and T'Sou-ke Nations -- five small nations pooling their leverage in a process that has taken decades and is not finished. The work is unglamorous: land selection maps, fiscal chapters, governance provisions, actuarial tables. It is also the only mechanism on offer for repairing an agreement that one side could read and the other could not. Meanwhile the bay does what it has always done. Salmon run past the islands, the swell comes in off the strait, and the place keeps its name.

## Flight Context

Located near 48.34 N, 123.61 W on the southern tip of Vancouver Island, at the western approach to the Strait of Juan de Fuca. From the air, Beecher Bay is a distinct notch in an otherwise rugged, rocky coast, guarded by a cluster of small islets -- Village, Whale, Fraser, and Twin islands among them -- with the Olympic Mountains of Washington State filling the horizon to the south across the strait. Race Rocks and its black-and-white lighthouse lie a short distance east; East Sooke Regional Park's headlands are immediately west. Best viewed at 1,500-3,000 ft AGL on a clear day, when the Olympics and the strait's shipping lanes are both visible in one frame. Victoria International (CYYJ) is about 20 nm northeast; Victoria Inner Harbour Water Aerodrome (CYWH) roughly 15 nm east; William R. Fairchild International (KCLM) at Port Angeles sits about 15 nm south-southeast, on the far shore. Note the international boundary running down the middle of the strait. Expect strong westerlies funneling through the strait, sea fog in summer, and rapid onset of low stratus.
