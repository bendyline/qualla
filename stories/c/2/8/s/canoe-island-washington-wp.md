# Canoe Island (Washington)

Forty-seven acres in Upright Channel that the U.S. Army reserved for coastal guns it never installed, an outlaw hid on for a night in 1913, and a couple turned into a French immersion camp for children.

**Location:** 48.5581, -122.9261

**Categories:** Islands, San Juan Islands, Military History, Indigenous Heritage, Washington

## Introduction

In 1898 a man named I. J. Lichtenberg leased an entire island from the United States government for one dollar. The catch was written into the agreement: he had to be ready to walk off it the moment the Army wanted the ground back. Canoe Island had been set aside as a military reservation, one of seven the Army Engineers carved out of the San Juans in the 1870s for the fortification of a border that had only just stopped being disputed. The guns never came. No artillery was ever mounted, no buildings raised, no troops garrisoned - on Canoe Island or on any of the other six. A dollar turned out to be roughly the right price.

## Forty-Seven Acres in a Channel

The island sits in the middle of the archipelago, in Upright Channel between Shaw and Lopez, and it is small enough to walk across in ten minutes - forty-seven acres, topping out at 127.7 feet. Second-growth cedar, fir, hemlock and madrona cover most of it, with a few old-growth survivors mixed in, and the rocky shoreline is fringed by dense forests of bull kelp that lift and fall with the tide like something breathing. You can only reach it by boat. But you can see it constantly: Washington State Ferries running to Friday Harbor and the Lopez landing pass through the gap between the island's southern tip and Flat Point on Lopez, which means thousands of people slide past Canoe Island every summer without ever learning its name.

## Skwsa7 snexwlh

The Samish name for the island is Skwsa7 snexwlh. Canoe Island lies within waters used by Coast Salish peoples - the Lhaq'temish (Lummi), Tulalip, S'Klallam and Samish among them - and while the documentary record of exactly who lived or camped here is thin, the ground itself is not silent. There are several shell middens on the island, the accumulated remains of meals eaten over a very long time, which is the archaeological signature of a place returned to again and again. The English name turns up much later, on British Admiralty Chart 2840 drawn by Richards between 1858 and 1860, and surveys by Captain George Henry over the following years described the channel around it as a safe passage with mild tides. Two naming systems, a century of overlap, and only one of them still in common use.

## Guns That Never Came

When the boundary arbitration of 1872 finally gave the San Juans to the United States and ended the long standoff known as the Pig War, Washington moved quickly to fortify what it had won. A team of Army Engineers under General Nathaniel Michler surveyed the islands for defensible ground, and by September 1874 had designated seven large government military reservations. Canoe Island was one. The reservations were enormous relative to any plausible need, and not everyone was impressed. In 1902 the San Juan Islander argued that less than a tenth of the reserved land would ever be required, and pointed out what islanders already knew: the reserves were blocking settlement across the county. Leases like Lichtenberg's - he also held the reservation at the north end of Lopez - were the government's way of letting the land be used while keeping the option open. The option was never exercised.

## Two Weeks in 1913

The strangest episode in the island's history lasted about a night. In 1913 two men came to Orcas by skiff and caught a boat for Bellingham; when their skiff was found adrift, it held a pocketbook full of documents relating to bank robberies and other crimes in Canada, and British authorities confirmed both men were wanted. Days later one of them came back for the pocketbook. What followed was a running fight on the porch of the Orcas Hotel, an escape into the woods, a second exchange of shots that night, and then a morning departure by stolen boat in the guise of an untroubled fisherman. He ate breakfast on Shaw, stole a second boat, and that boat was found on Canoe Island - empty. A $1,500 reward produced nothing. Searchers turned up his camp on Shaw with bloody bandages in it. After two weeks the sheriff and the Canadian authorities gave up, convinced the man was still on Shaw and that the neighbours were feeding him.

## French on an Island

In 1965 Dr. Warren Austin and Florence Heath Horton Austin - Bunny, daughter of the founder of Chicago Bridge and Iron - bought Canoe Island for $95,000. Austin had loved French since childhood and had served in France during the war with the 49th Field Hospital, attached to Patton's Third Army. There was no French camp anywhere on the West Coast, and the Austins wanted one for their daughter Dorothy, so three years later they founded the Institut Francile and Canoe Island Camps with help from the Lycee Francais de Los Angeles. It began on Orcas, at the Madrona Inn in Eastsound, while an architect named Paul Gray designed the buildings going up across the water. In 1975 the whole operation moved to the island. It runs there still, a 501(c)(3) welcoming about two hundred children a summer to do archery, fencing, theatre, art and kayaking, much of it in French. An island the Army once reserved for artillery is now forty-seven acres of teenagers conjugating verbs in the rain.

## Flight Context

Located at 48.558 N, 122.926 W in the centre of the San Juan Islands, Washington, in Upright Channel between Shaw Island to the north and Lopez Island to the south. From the air it is a small, dark, wholly forested oval - 47 acres, high point 127.7 ft - with a light rocky shoreline and visible kelp beds, sitting in the gap that the Friday Harbor and Lopez ferry routes thread past Flat Point. The surrounding water is the identifying feature: Upright Channel, San Juan Channel to the west, and the scatter of Shaw, Lopez, Orcas and San Juan Islands around it. Mount Baker stands large to the east, the Olympics to the southwest, and Vancouver Island fills the northwest horizon. Recommended viewing altitude is 1,500-3,000 ft MSL to keep the channel system and the ferry tracks in frame. Friday Harbor (KFHR) is roughly 8 km northwest, Orcas Island (KORS) about 10 km north, and Lopez Island (S31) about 5 km south - all small, uncontrolled fields with seasonal traffic. There is no airstrip on Canoe Island and it is private property. Expect summer haze over the archipelago, strong tidal lines visible in the channels, and abrupt marine-layer incursions from the Strait of Juan de Fuca.
