# Shaw Island

For almost thirty years the general store and the ferry landing on this San Juan island were run by nuns - one of three Catholic orders on an island of 188 people.

**Location:** 48.5732, -122.9573

**Categories:** San Juan Islands, Populated places in San Juan County, Washington, Unincorporated communities in Washington (state), Islands

## Introduction

The Washington State Ferry from Anacortes pulls into Shaw Island, and for almost three decades the person waiting on the dock to handle the lines was a nun. The Franciscan Sisters of the Eucharist ran the island's ferry terminal and its only general store until 2004 - habit, cash register, mooring lines and all - which is the kind of detail that sounds invented and is not. Shaw is the smallest of the four San Juan Islands the ferries serve: 19.952 square kilometres of forest, rock and pasture with a year-round population of 188 at the 2020 census. It has three Catholic religious communities, one store's worth of commerce, and a school that has been running longer than any other in Washington State.

## Sqʼemenen

Long before any of that, the island was Lummi country. Shaw lies within the historical territory of the Lummi Nation, who know it as Sqʼemenen - the name given by hereditary chief Bill Tsilixw James. The United States acquired the San Juan archipelago by treaty in 1855, but the Lummi retained cultural and resource rights, fishing among them, and those rights are exercised here still. In 1841 the Wilkes Expedition wrote a different name onto the chart, honouring the United States naval officer John Shaw, and that is the one that stuck. In 2015 a San Juan County resident proposed restoring Sqʼemenen to a bay on the island that had carried a name now widely recognised as a slur against Indigenous women. The Shaw Island Historical Society countered with a different idea, and the compromise was adopted: the bay is now Reef Net Bay, named for the Coast Salish reef-net salmon fishery practised in those waters both historically and today.

## The Little Red Schoolhouse

Shaw Island School District Number 10 operates out of a one-room schoolhouse built in 1891. A second room was added at some point, which is the only concession the building has made to the twentieth century. It teaches kindergarten through eighth grade, it is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and it has been in continuous use since the year it went up - making it the longest-running school in the State of Washington. Consider what that means on an island of fewer than two hundred people: a single institution where everyone's grandparents, parents and children have sat in the same room, taught across an age range that would defeat most trained educators, still open.

## Three Orders and a University

The Franciscan Sisters were not alone. Benedictine nuns established a monastery on 150 acres here in the 1970s, and the Sisters of Mercy have kept an unofficial retreat on the island since the 1980s. Why an island this small should have attracted three separate religious communities is not documented anywhere, but it is not hard to guess: it is quiet, it is difficult to reach casually, and it is beautiful. The other large landholder is the University of Washington, which owns property across the island - most notably Cedar Rock Preserve on the south shore, a gift from Robert H. Ellis Jr. The university's stated aim for these lands is to maintain and restore native biodiversity and ecosystem function, support education and research consistent with those goals, and preserve important parts of the cultural landscape.

## Eleven and a Half Miles of Pavement

The road network is a complete inventory: 11.5 miles of asphalt and 2.37 miles of gravel, arranged as three loops through the island's interior with branches running to the ferry dock, to Shaw Island County Park, out to Neck Point, and part-way toward the private ground at Broken Point. The county park holds the best shoreline access on the island, with a long stretch of beach. There is a small library and historical museum, funded by its members. That is the island's public infrastructure, more or less entirely, and residents have written rules to keep it that way: under the Shaw Subarea Plan, adopted through Washington State's Growth Management Act, tourism businesses here are required to maintain the island's character as a small-scale, rural and agricultural community - a constraint that applies to lodging, food service, moorage and dry storage alike. Summer weekends still fill up. Nobody has built a resort.

## Flight Context

Shaw Island lies at 48.5732 N, 122.9573 W, in the middle of the San Juan Islands archipelago in northwestern Washington. Recommended viewing altitude is 2,000-4,000 ft AGL. Shaw sits at the hub of the group: Orcas Island immediately north across Harney Channel, with the long inlet of East Sound cutting deep into it and Mount Constitution as the archipelago's high point; Lopez Island south and east across Upright Channel; San Juan Island west across San Juan Channel, with Friday Harbor's boat basin clearly visible. Shaw itself is heavily forested with a scatter of cleared pasture, and the ferry landing on its north shore is the only obvious structure. Nearest airports: KFHR (Friday Harbor) about 8 km west, KORS (Orcas Island, at Eastsound) about 12 km north, and KBLI (Bellingham International) about 35 km east on the mainland. Shaw has no airfield. The islands sit in the Olympic rain shadow and are noticeably drier and sunnier than the surrounding coast, but sea fog can fill the channels quickly in late summer.
