# Rosario Strait

The channel that lost an international boundary dispute and won a secondhand name, and now carries more than five hundred oil tankers a year past the San Juan Islands.

**Location:** 48.5611, -122.7597

**Categories:** Bodies of water of Island County, Washington, Straits of San Juan County, Washington, Bodies of water of Skagit County, Washington, Straits of Washington (state), Bodies of water of Whatcom County, Washington, Pig War (1859), Salish Sea

## Introduction

The name on this strait was originally attached to a different body of water. In 1791 the Spanish explorer Francisco de Eliza christened what is now the Strait of Georgia the Gran Canal de Nuestra Senora del Rosario la Marinera. Fifty-six years later a British captain, reorganizing the Admiralty charts and determined to scrub the American names off them, shortened Eliza's grand title and moved it sideways - onto the narrower channel east of the San Juan Islands, which has been Rosario Strait ever since. The water it was meant for got named after a British king instead.

## Four Names in Fifty-Seven Years

Nobody could settle on what to call this place. In 1790 Manuel Quimper and Juan Carrasco, sailing the Princesa Real, took it for a bay and named it Boca de Fidalgo after Salvador Fidalgo. The following year Jose Maria Narvaez worked out that it was in fact a strait and adjusted the name to Canal de Fidalgo. George Vancouver came through in 1792, named the Strait of Georgia for King George III, and left this channel with nothing at all. Charles Wilkes of the United States Exploring Expedition then called it Ringgold Channel after one of his officers. In 1847 Captain Henry Kellett swept the Admiralty charts clean of what he considered pro-American naming, affirmed the British and Spanish choices, and left Rosario Strait standing where it stands today.

## The Boundary That Wasn't

The naming quarrel was a polite version of a real one. After the Oregon Treaty, the border was to run west from the 49th parallel down the main channel to the open sea, and each side had a preferred channel. The British argued for Rosario, assuming it to be the deepest route from the treaty line's terminus in the middle of the Strait of Georgia - and it is in fact the shorter shipping route. The Americans wanted Haro Strait, on the far side of the San Juan Islands, wider but somewhat longer. Between the two lay the islands themselves, and whoever won the channel won them. The dispute escalated into the confrontation remembered as the Pig War, went to arbitration, and came back for Haro. The San Juans are American, and Rosario Strait is a domestic waterway.

## Islands on Both Hands

The strait runs roughly 23 kilometres north from the Strait of Juan de Fuca to the Strait of Georgia, separating San Juan County from Skagit and Whatcom counties. The U.S. Geological Survey pins its southern end to a line from Point Colville on Lopez Island across to Rosario Head on Fidalgo Island, and its northern end to a line from Point Migley on Lummi Island out to the eastern tip of Puffin Island and on to Point Thompson on Orcas. In between, it threads a corridor with Lopez, Decatur, Blakely and Orcas on the western hand and Fidalgo, Cypress, Sinclair and Lummi on the eastern. From the water it reads less like a strait than like a street running between two rows of forested hills.

## Five Hundred Tankers

More than five hundred oil tankers move through Rosario Strait every year, bound for or coming from the Cherry Point refinery to the north and the refineries near Anacortes to the east. Add the traffic for Bellingham, Anacortes and the San Juan Islands themselves, and the channel is in essentially constant use. Ships headed for British Columbia or Alaska often choose it over the passages farther west when the tidal currents here offer a better ride - a strait shaped by argument between two empires now gets picked, day to day, on the strength of the tide table. All of that runs through a corridor a few kilometres wide, past islands whose economy is built on the water staying clean.

## Flight Context

Rosario Strait centres near 48.5611 N, 122.7597 W. Transit at 2,000-4,000 ft gives the clearest read: the channel runs north-south with Lopez, Blakely and Orcas to the west and Fidalgo, Cypress and Lummi to the east, Rosario Head marks the southeastern corner, and the Deception Pass bridge is just south of it. Mount Constitution on Orcas is the best terrain reference in the group; the Anacortes refineries and the Cherry Point facility to the northeast are unmistakable industrial landmarks. Orcas Island (KORS) is about 20 km northwest, Skagit Regional (KBVS) about 25 km east, and Friday Harbor (KFHR) lies west across the islands. Check NOTAMs for NAS Whidbey Island (KNUW) activity to the south before routing through. Fog off the Strait of Juan de Fuca drifts up this corridor readily, and low stratus can sit on the island tops while the channel itself stays open.
