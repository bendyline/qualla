# Russell Books

Three generations, two cities and more than a million used volumes - and in 2019, half a million of them carried across a Victoria street by hand.

**Location:** 48.4244, -123.3640

**Categories:** Bookstores, Victoria, British Columbia, Family Business, Literary History

## Introduction

In 2019 the staff of Russell Books moved more than half a million books across the street. Not in a truck, not by contractor: by hand, armload after armload, from the old shop to a new one with ten thousand square feet on the main floor and another eight thousand below. It is the kind of undertaking that only makes sense if you are certain the thing you are carrying is worth the trouble. Russell Books had been assembling this inventory for decades across two provinces, and it has been described as the largest used bookstore in Canada.

## The Book Nook

It started a long way from Vancouver Island. Reg Russell opened a shop in Montreal in 1962 and called it The Book Nook, a name that lasted twenty-one years before the business took his own in 1983. Used bookselling in that era was a matter of physical presence and patient accumulation: you bought collections, you sorted them, you shelved them, and you waited for the right customer to walk in. The shop worked, and it kept working through decades in which the retail landscape around it changed more or less completely. Then the city itself arrived at the door.

## Expropriated

In 1999 the Montreal location was expropriated to make room for the expansion of the Palais des congres de Montreal. A convention centre needed the ground, and the bookstore did not get to argue. Reg Russell responded by opening a smaller shop nearby called Diamond Books and ran it until he retired in 2004, when he sold it. Most of the regular stock stayed behind with the new owners, but his antiquarian collection did not. It was crated up and shipped across the country to his daughter's store in Victoria, where the rare and old material joined an inventory that had been growing on the west coast for over a decade.

## West, and Then Larger

That Victoria store had opened in 1991, with Reg Russell helping his daughter Diana and her husband Ron DePol get it established. Today it is run by his granddaughter Andrea Minter and her husband Jordan Minter, which makes three generations of one family in the same trade. The store expanded as opportunities came: it absorbed the neighbouring Fort Cafe in 2013, and when the move across the street came in 2019 the additional floor space made a satellite operation, Books on View, unnecessary, and it closed. Before that move the business held over a million volumes across its locations, and the stated ambition for the new building was a million and a quarter.

## Early to the Wire

Used bookselling looks like the most analogue business imaginable, which makes it easy to miss how quickly this particular shop understood the internet. In 1996, Russell Books became one of the first independent bookstores anywhere in the world to list its inventory on AbeBooks. Think about what that meant in 1996. A used bookstore's whole economic problem is that its stock is unique and unrepeatable; a single copy of an out-of-print title sits on a shelf waiting for the one person who wants it, and that person may live three thousand miles away and never know the book exists. Putting the catalogue online solved a problem booksellers had lived with for centuries, and a family firm in Victoria was among the first to do it.

## Why the Carrying Mattered

The LSE Review of Books, published by the London School of Economics, has rated Russell Books among the best bookstores in Victoria, which is a slightly improbable sentence and a genuinely earned one. What a store like this offers is not efficiency. Anything you can name, you can order online in a minute. What you cannot order is the book you did not know you were looking for, sitting at eye level on a shelf between two titles you have never heard of. That experience requires somebody to have bought, sorted, shelved and kept a million volumes in one building. It requires, occasionally, that somebody carry them across a street.

## Flight Context

Russell Books occupies 48.4244 N, 123.3640 W on Fort Street in downtown Victoria, British Columbia, a few blocks inland from the Inner Harbour. Best viewing altitude is 1,000 to 2,000 feet over the downtown core; the store is a street-level commercial building and is located by its surroundings rather than picked out from the air. Key visual landmarks: the Inner Harbour basin to the southwest with the Parliament Buildings and the Empress Hotel on its edge, the Johnson Street Bridge and the Upper Harbour to the west, Beacon Hill Park to the south, and the Strait of Juan de Fuca beyond with the Olympic Mountains rising across it in Washington. Victoria Inner Harbour Water Aerodrome (CYWH) is immediately southwest of the downtown grid and handles frequent scheduled floatplane traffic at low level; Victoria International (CYYJ) lies roughly 13 nm north. Controlled airspace and the nearby international border both demand attention here. Expect summer morning fog off the strait and low ceilings through the winter months.
