# Uplands

Canada's most carefully designed garden suburb, laid out by John Charles Olmsted in 1907 - and its finest feature is the seventy-six acres he left wild.

**Location:** 48.4485, -123.3051

**Categories:** Neighbourhoods, British Columbia, Victoria, Landscape Architecture, National Historic Sites

## Introduction

Drive Beach Drive through Uplands and the message is unambiguous: estate lots, manicured hedges, mansions set well back from serpentine streets, and cast-iron lamp posts topped with green globes that have been the neighbourhood's signature for over a century. Then the road reaches Uplands Park and everything designed simply stops. No flower beds, no walks, no lawn. Instead there are jagged rock crags, Garry oaks bent and stunted by the wind off Haro Strait, open heath, and the sea. In the most meticulously planned residential subdivision in Canada, the single most memorable feature is the piece its designer deliberately declined to touch. That was the point. John Charles Olmsted thought the land here was already better than anything he could impose on it, and he spent his effort figuring out how to build among it without ruining it.

## What $275,000 Bought

In 1907 three brothers - John A., Robert, and Dawson Turner - bought this stretch of northeast Oak Bay for $275,000. They were Scots by origin and cattle and horse ranchers by trade, out of Turner Valley in Alberta, and they had the unusual instinct to hire the best landscape architect on the continent rather than a surveyor with a straightedge. John Charles Olmsted, of the firm that had shaped Central Park and a generation of American cities, took the commission and worked the site between 1907 and 1908. He surveyed the ground in five-foot contour intervals so the roads could follow the land's own shape, and he mapped the existing Garry oaks and the sightlines to the water so that neither would be lost to a lot line. His firm stayed involved until 1921. Of all his subdivision work, Olmsted rated Uplands "unquestionably the best adapted to obtain the greatest amount of landscape beauty in connection with suburban development."

## Rules That Outlast Owners

A plan is only as durable as its enforcement, and Olmsted knew it. He drafted protective deed restrictions intended to hold the neighbourhood's character against the ordinary pressures of subdivision, resale, and fashion - and the municipality of Oak Bay eventually took over enforcing them. The result is a place that has changed remarkably little across four generations. The streets still curve where he curved them. The lot sizes remain generous. The lamp posts remain. Beneath all of it sits quartz diorite gneiss, with well-drained sandy loams of the Cadboro and Langford series over most of the neighbourhood and thick dark topsoil throughout, which is a large part of why the gardens here look the way they do. Good design helps. Good soil helps more than anyone admits.

## The Land They Did Not Sell

In 1946 a seventy-six-acre undeveloped tract along the seaward edge passed to the municipality as a public park, and Uplands Park has stayed wild ever since. It holds Garry oaks, rocky outcrops, open ground, and sweeping views across the strait - along with Indigenous archaeological sites, evidence that this shoreline was a used and valued place long before any Turner brother saw it. Coastal Garry oak ecosystems are now among the most reduced habitats in British Columbia, squeezed out by exactly the kind of development that surrounds this park on three sides. Uplands Park survives because the developer, having planned an entire suburb, left one corner unplanned. The Royal Victoria Yacht Club, the oldest yacht club in the province, sits within the neighbourhood's boundaries, keeping the connection to the water practical as well as scenic.

## Recognized, Eventually

For most of its existence Uplands was simply an expensive address. Formal recognition came on 19 August 2019, when the federal minister responsible for Parks Canada designated it a National Historic Site. The citation is unusually direct about why: Uplands is "an exceptional example of subdivision design and planning," Olmsted's work here ranks "among the finest works of a renowned landscape architect," and the design "changed the way suburbs were planned well into the 20th century." Parks Canada also noted that leading Canadian and British Columbian architects designed a number of the houses. On 13 October 2020, Oak Bay council voted to install the commemorative plaque at Midland Circle. It is a strange kind of monument - a plaque marking not a battle or a building but the intelligence of a street layout, which is the sort of achievement that normally goes entirely unremarked.

## Flight Context

Uplands occupies 188 hectares at 48.449 N, 123.305 W in the northeast corner of the District of Oak Bay, east of Victoria, British Columbia. From 1,500-3,000 feet MSL the neighbourhood reads unmistakably: a patch of curving, serpentine streets and heavy tree canopy set against the rectilinear grid of the surrounding suburbs, with large lots and unusually dark, continuous greenery. Uplands Park forms the open, rocky headland at its seaward edge, and the Royal Victoria Yacht Club marina sits on the Cadboro Bay shore to the north. The Oak Bay Islands and Haro Strait open to the east, with the San Juan Islands beyond and Mount Baker on the horizon in clear weather. Victoria International (CYYJ) is about 8 nm northwest at Patricia Bay, Victoria Harbour Water Aerodrome (CYWH) roughly 4 nm southwest; both are busy, and the international boundary runs down Haro Strait just east - stay well clear unless cleared. Conditions are among the mildest and driest on the coast, with best light and visibility from May through September.
