# Vancouver Film Studios

Thirty acres of East Vancouver light industry - a mattress factory, a bakery, an elevator test tower, a Costco parking lot - bought up building by building and turned into the largest studio complex in Canada.

**Location:** 49.2603, -123.0286

**Categories:** Organizations based in Vancouver, Cinema of British Columbia, Film organizations in Canada, Canadian film studios

## Introduction

A gift centre. A mattress factory. A stonemason's yard, a commercial dry cleaner, a bakery, an electronics store, a wholesale tool merchant, a cigarette company, and a tower where a crane firm tested elevators. That was the inventory of tenants on the block at Boundary Road and Grandview Highway when the McLean Group started buying it in the early 1980s - an entirely unglamorous corner of East Vancouver, chosen for cheap industrial square footage rather than for anything cinematic. Four decades later the same thirty acres hold one of Canada's largest purpose-built studio complexes, and a comic-book city, a Pennsylvania motel and an entire alternate universe have all been constructed on them.

## Bought Building by Building

The film business arrived in 1987, when the group bought Northstar International Studios - the operation that occupied what the complex still calls Building D. For the next decade the strategy was simply acquisition: more parcels, more warehouses, whatever came up for sale on the block. In 1998 the whole assemblage was renamed Vancouver Film Studios and six purpose-built sound stages were put into construction. Building G came first, finished in February 2000 on ground cleared by demolishing a courier warehouse. Buildings J and C followed, both done by July 2001; Building A opened in April 2002. Demand kept outrunning capacity, so two more stages, H and I, were ordered in December 2002 and opened sixteen months later, replacing a building on East 11th Avenue that had been knocked down to make room.

## The Ghosts in the Floorplan

Read the building list closely and the industrial estate is still visible underneath. Building B was where the Heede Crane Company built elevators; bought in 1997 and renovated in 2001, it now holds production offices and two 15,000-square-foot stages. Building J's three stages sit on what used to be a Costco parking lot. Building D, the original Northstar premises and the oldest thread in the whole operation, was finally torn down in 2012 and 2013 and rebuilt as two stages, one of them 21,000 square feet - the largest on the lot. Building X houses Pacific Backlot Services, the grip and lighting company, which means the trucks of cable, stands and lamps never have to leave the property.

## What Got Built Inside Them

Features have passed through - The Day the Earth Stood Still in 2008, If I Stay and Step Up All In in 2014, Fifty Shades of Grey in 2015 - but the economics of a studio like this one run on television. Fringe occupied stages here for five seasons and a hundred episodes. Bates Motel built the Bates house and motel for fifty. Arrow ran 167 episodes across eight years, long enough that a generation of Vancouver crew members effectively built careers inside one show. More than a hundred productions have used the lot. A feature is a sprint; a series is a mortgage payment, arriving every week for years, and it is series work that made Vancouver the production town it is.

## Hollywood North's Machine Room

The pitch has always been logistics rather than romance. Thirty acres are dedicated to production, with sound stages running from 12,500 up to 21,000 square feet and eight production offices and support buildings around them. Downtown Vancouver is fifteen minutes away; Vancouver International Airport is twenty-five. In 2006 the studio took a BC Export Award in the New Media and Entertainment category and was named one of the province's top twenty-one exporters - an odd-sounding honour until you consider what is actually being exported, which is several thousand skilled hours a week of Canadian labour, packaged as American television.

## Under New Ownership

In June 2023 the McLean Group sold the complex to Hackman Capital Partners, a Los Angeles firm that has spent the last decade assembling studio real estate across North America - the end of nearly four decades of local family ownership and a fair indication of how the industry now values physical stage space. The lot has kept building since. It counts thirteen purpose-built sound stages today, and in 2025 it took B Corp certification, a designation more often associated with organic groceries than with soundstages. The trucks still turn in off Boundary Road. The bakery and the mattress factory are long gone, but the block is still, in its way, a manufacturing district.

## Flight Context

Vancouver Film Studios occupies the block around 3500 Cornett Road at 49.2603 N, 123.0286 W, in East Vancouver just west of Boundary Road - the city line with Burnaby - and immediately south of Grandview Highway. From 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL the site reads as a tight cluster of very large, windowless flat-roofed boxes with white roofs and big paved yards, distinct from the finer-grained industrial buildings around it; the Grandview Highway and Highway 1 interchange lies just north, and the green of Central Park is 2 km south. This is controlled airspace under the Vancouver terminal area: Vancouver International (CYVR) is 13 km west-southwest, Vancouver Harbour Flight Centre (CYHC) 8 km northwest with heavy floatplane traffic, Boundary Bay (CZBB) 20 km south and Pitt Meadows (CYPK) 20 km east. Low cloud and drizzle are the default from November to March - part of why the region's production calendar leans on interiors.
