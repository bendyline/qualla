# Simon Fraser University

A university designed and built in eighteen months on the summit of a rainforest mountain - one enormous interlocking concrete building that has been quietly reinventing itself ever since.

**Location:** 49.2782, -122.9194

**Categories:** Universities, Architecture, Modernism, Burnaby, British Columbia, Education

## Introduction

Eighteen months. Ground was broken on the summit of Burnaby Mountain in the spring of 1964, and on September 9, 1965, 2,500 students walked into a finished university. Not a first building with more to come - a campus, complete, its halls and quadrangle and library already locked together under continuous roofs, 365 metres above Burrard Inlet, in second-growth rainforest. Arthur Erickson and Geoffrey Massey had won the design competition with a proposition that sounds obvious now and was radical then: don't scatter pavilions across a lawn. Build one thing. Let people cross the entire institution without stepping into the weather.

## A Single Building With a University Inside It

The result is the most uncompromising piece of modern architecture in the province and, for many visitors, the strangest campus they have ever walked. Board-formed concrete, deep covered walkways, a great glazed Convocation Mall, and at the centre the Academic Quadrangle - a hollow square of offices raised on columns around a sunken courtyard, reflecting pool included. Everything connects. Weather is something you observe rather than endure. The design has aged into honours: the Royal Architectural Institute of Canada gave it the Prix du XXe siecle in 2007, and a Lieutenant-Governor gold medal followed in 2009. Film crews have used the concrete for decades, standing in for spaceports, laboratories and future cities, because there is nowhere else in the Lower Mainland that looks so persuasively like somewhere else.

## Why the Summit

Gordon Shrum, the university's first chancellor, was offered a menu of sites and chose the hardest one: the top of Burnaby Mountain, 365 metres up, facing northwest over the inlet. The campus now spreads across 170 hectares of that summit. It is traditional Coast Salish territory - Tsleil-Waututh, Kwikwetlem, Squamish and Musqueam - and the mountain was known long before it held a library. The elevation has consequences. It snows up here while it rains downtown. The campus disappears into cloud on afternoons when Vancouver is in sunshine, and reappears above the fog on mornings when the city cannot see across the street. The main library carries the name of W. A. C. Bennett, the premier whose government created the place - a reminder that this university was an act of policy, built fast because the province decided it needed one.

## Three Semesters and a Habit of Going First

SFU ran year-round on a three-semester schedule from the beginning, which in 1965 was unusual enough to be considered a statement. Thelma Finlayson arrived as the university's first female faculty member, in biological sciences, and on her retirement in 1979 became its first professor emerita. The research reach grew outward from the mountain: SFU is a partner in TRIUMF, Canada's national particle and nuclear physics laboratory and home of the world's largest cyclotron, and in the Bamfield Marine Sciences Centre out on Vancouver Island's west coast. In 2015 it became the second Canadian university accredited by the Northwest Commission on Colleges and Universities. In 2025 it became the first in Canada to adopt open scholarship principles university-wide - a decision about who gets to read publicly funded research, made by an institution that has never much minded being first.

## The Only Canadians on the Field

For more than a decade SFU was the single strangest entry in American college sport: the only member of the NCAA based outside the United States, accepted into Division II in 2009 and competing in the Great Northwest Athletic Conference from 2011. The arrangement was always awkward, and it came apart from the edges. On April 4, 2023 the university cancelled football outright after the Lone Star Conference ended its affiliation, leaving the team without a league - a decision delivered to players who had crossed a border to play for a program that ceased to exist, and one the university confirmed in January 2025 would not be reversed. Then, in November 2025, SFU announced it was leaving the NCAA altogether for U Sports, calling the cross-border model financially and logistically unsustainable. Sixteen years of an experiment, ended.

## Pipes, and a Town on the Ridge

One thing on this mountain has never needed reinvention. Students formed a pipe band in 1966 and it was re-established in 1981 when the brothers Terry and Jack Lee were recruited to it; the Simon Fraser University Pipe Band has since won the World Pipe Band Championship in Grade One seven times - 1995, 1996, 1999, 2001, 2008, 2009, and again in 2026. A concrete campus in a coastal rainforest is, improbably, one of the great addresses in Highland piping. And the summit is no longer only a university. UniverCity, the residential community Erickson and Massey imagined in 1963 and which finally began construction in 2000, now houses roughly 3,000 people on the ridge, with shops along University High Street and an elementary school that opened in September 2010. Students, families, researchers and a world-champion pipe band, all living in the clouds above a city that mostly forgets they are up there.

## Flight Context

Simon Fraser University occupies the summit of Burnaby Mountain at 49.2782 N, 122.9194 W, roughly 15 km east of downtown Vancouver, with the campus floor at 365 metres (about 1,200 feet) above sea level. Fly it at 2,500-3,500 feet AGL, giving real clearance over terrain that rises abruptly from sea level on all sides. The mountain is a solitary forested dome, and the campus reads from the air as a long pale concrete spine laid east-west along the ridge, with the Academic Quadrangle's distinct hollow rectangle at its centre and the UniverCity blocks clustered at the western end. Burrard Inlet and the tank farms of the Westridge terminal lie directly below to the north; Indian Arm cuts north-east beyond them; Burnaby Lake and Deer Lake sit in the flat ground to the south. Nearby airports: Pitt Meadows (CYPK) about 9 nm east-southeast, Vancouver Harbour Flight Centre (CYHC) about 8 nm west, Vancouver International (CYVR) about 12 nm west-southwest, and Boundary Bay (CZBB) about 13 nm south-southwest. Note the genuine hazard here: the summit sits in cloud on a great many days when the lowlands are clear, and the ridge collects fog and low stratus. Bright winter days after a cold front give the best visibility and the sharpest contrast between grey concrete and dark second-growth forest.
