# 1965 Coquitlam massacre

In April 1965, days after members of a robbery ring began to be arrested, Vancouver police officer Leonard Hogue shot his wife and six children and then himself in their Coquitlam home, ending a double life that had run from petty theft to a $1.2 million heist.

## Introduction

No one knew anything was wrong until Leonard Hogue failed to show up for work. When officials went looking for the Vancouver police officer on April 20, 1965, they found that his wife and all six of his children were dead in the family's home in Coquitlam, British Columbia, and so was he. At about 1:30 that morning, Hogue had shot each member of his family and then himself. It was a familicide committed by a man sworn to protect the public, whose secret life had just begun to unravel.

## A Growing Family

Leonard Raymond Hogue came west from Saint Boniface, Manitoba, now part of Winnipeg, bringing his wife and two children to Vancouver. In 1956 he graduated from the Vancouver Police Department, and he was the pistol shooting champion of his class. He began as a patrolman and was later transferred to the city prison. At home the family kept growing, and by 1961 the Hogues had six children. From the outside it was an ordinary picture: a police officer, his wife and a houseful of children. That picture concealed a great deal. Two of his fellow graduates from the police class of 1956 would eventually become his partners in crime.

## The Double Life

In the early 1960s, Hogue and another police officer began stealing, taking bags of cash from Dairy Queen freezers. The crimes grew bolder, and according to writer Eve Lazarus, the group's targets came to include a sporting goods store, a Simpson Sears on Kingsway and several banks. In 1962 Hogue and two other officers robbed a bank, and on February 11, 1965, he and three other officers robbed a rail shipment of banknotes bound for destruction, stealing $1.2 million. There was a catch: the old money had been drilled with three large holes and was virtually worthless. On April 17, 1965, members of the ring were arrested after a bartender called police because they kept paying with patched bills. In the days that followed, Hogue had an accident on his way to work, later described as a suicide attempt. Soon afterward, another police officer lent him a .357 Magnum revolver.

## The Night of April 20

That revolver was the weapon Hogue used in the early hours of April 20. His wife was found in the bed, and two of the children were still in their beds. The other four children were found in different parts of the house, and investigators believed they had tried to escape. Hogue's own body lay on the master bedroom floor. Every member of the family was killed by a bullet to the head. The children bore no part in their father's crimes. Their mother, who had come west from Manitoba with him and raised six children, died in her bed. They were the real victims of the Hogue story: a mother and six children who deserve to be remembered as more than the final entry in a tale of police corruption.
