# McBarge

Built for Expo 86 to show Vancouver the future, the floating McDonald's spent forty years as a monument to plans that never happened - and in 2025 it went down at its moorings.

**Location:** 49.2930, -122.9800

**Categories:** Expo 86, Floating structures, Urban legends and landmarks, Modern history, British Columbia

## Introduction

Expo 86 asked what the next century would look like, and one of the answers was a hamburger restaurant on a barge. The Friendship 500 was 187 feet long, designed by Robert Allan Ltd. - a Vancouver naval architecture firm that normally draws tugs and workboats - and moored in False Creek as the showpiece of five McDonald's outlets built for the fair at a combined cost of $12 million. It was only the second floating McDonald's in the world, after one in St. Louis. Its whole premise was that it would sail off to a new life somewhere else when the fair ended. Forty years later, the strange part is that it did move on - twice, under tow - and never opened again.

## The Long Wait in Burrard Inlet

The restaurant served customers for a single season in 1986. Then the fair closed, the crowds went home, and the barge simply stayed where it was, empty, until 1991, when the new owner of the Expo lands made McDonald's take it away. It was towed around to Burrard Inlet and anchored there, in working water among industrial barges and the tanks of an oil refinery, where it sat for the better part of thirty years. Rust bled down the white superstructure. The windows went dark. Boaters pointed it out to visitors the way you point out a wreck, and for two generations of Vancouverites it became the city's most conspicuous piece of abandoned optimism - a ruin that had never even had the chance to be used.

## Cast as Itself

Its most successful career turned out to be in film. In 2003 Marvel Entertainment and New Line Cinema rented the barge as a shooting location for Blade: Trinity, where it played the lair of the vampire-hunting Nightstalkers. This is the fate of a great deal of derelict architecture in a city with a large film industry, and it suited the McBarge unusually well. Something built to look like the future, then left to decay for two decades, ends up looking exactly like the sort of place a screenwriter imagines outlaws living. The barge was not dressed up to play a ruin. It simply was one.

## Sturgeon's on the Fraser

Rescue plans came and went. In June 2009 the barge's owner, Gastown developer Howard Meakin, went to Mission's city council with the most ambitious of them: a waterfront development on the Fraser River called Sturgeon's on the Fraser, with the McBarge at its centre - multiple restaurants, a marina, paddlewheeler excursions, even floatplane service to Victoria and Nanaimo. By the summer of 2010 it had real local support and was waiting on council. Then the objections did their work. Concerns about floatplane noise and the environmental effects of the marina piled up, and the proposal was rejected.

## Every Plan After That

In December 2015 Meakin announced the barge would finally leave Burrard Inlet, and on the 22nd it was towed to Maple Ridge for a promised $4.5 million refit ahead of a move to a location nobody would name. In 2017, with the diving pioneer Phil Nuytten involved, the concept became a Deep Ocean Discovery Centre; a crowdfunding launch set for 21 October that year was called off because of the weather and never rescheduled. A petition asking Vancouver council to grant the barge historic place status collected 185 signatures. The social media accounts stopped posting. By 2020 the plan was a seafood restaurant; by 2021 a site had reportedly been chosen and was awaiting government approval. None of it happened.

## 26 March 2025

On that day it was reported that the McBarge had partially sunk into the Fraser River at Maple Ridge. Howard Meakin, who had spent years trying to give the thing a second life, died the following month, and Transport Canada found itself unable to establish who now owned a half-submerged barge in a working river. As of 2026 it is still there, partly under water, its future as unsettled as it has been since 1986. There is a temptation to make it a joke, and Vancouver has made plenty. The more honest reading is that it is a piece of the future that a city genuinely believed in, at a moment when Vancouver was showing itself off to the world - and that nobody, for four decades, could work out what to do with it next.

## Flight Context

The coordinates attached to this story, 49.293 N, 122.98 W, mark the long-term anchorage in Burrard Inlet north of Burnaby where the McBarge sat from 1991 until 2015, among industrial barges and refinery tanks on the inlet's south shore. The barge itself is no longer there: since December 2015 it has been at Maple Ridge on the Fraser River, and since March 2025 it has been partly submerged. Fly the inlet at 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL for the industrial waterfront, then follow the Fraser east past the Pitt River confluence for the Maple Ridge reach. The Vancouver Harbour Flight Centre (CYHC) at Coal Harbour sits at the west end of the inlet, Pitt Meadows Regional (CYPK) is the nearest field to the Fraser site, and Vancouver International (CYVR) and Abbotsford International (CYXX) bracket the valley west and east. Autumn and winter bring persistent river fog in this stretch; summer afternoons give the cleanest water surface for spotting a low white hull.
