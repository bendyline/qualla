# Gastown riots

A two-minute ultimatum in Maple Tree Square turned a marijuana smoke-in into what a Supreme Court justice would call a police riot.

**Location:** 49.2833, -123.1042

**Categories:** 1971 riots, 1971 in British Columbia, Riots and civil disorder in Canada, History of Vancouver, Civil liberties

## Introduction

The ultimatum ran two minutes. Around two thousand people had filled Maple Tree Square in Gastown on the warm evening of 7 August 1971, drawn by notices in the underground press to a smoke-in protesting Vancouver's undercover drug squad and demanding that marijuana be legalized. A false report reached the senior officer on the scene that windows were being broken. He told the crowd it had two minutes to disperse. When the two minutes were up, mounted officers rode into the square, followed by police in riot gear carrying batons the department had issued only recently. Vancouver has called it the Battle of Maple Tree Square ever since. A justice of the Supreme Court of British Columbia would call it something blunter: a police riot.

## Operation Dustpan

The evening did not come out of nowhere. For weeks, plainclothes officers had been making drug arrests across the city under a special operation, directed from City Hall, that the police called Operation Dustpan - undercover work in a downtown where the line between a dealer and a nineteen-year-old with a joint was drawn by whoever was holding the handcuffs. Mayor Tom Campbell had built a public identity on hostility to the counterculture that had settled into Vancouver's cheap old buildings, and Gastown was the heart of it: a district of Victorian brick warehouses that had been scheduled for demolition a few years earlier to clear a route for a freeway into downtown, saved by a preservation campaign, and repopulated in the meantime by young people with nowhere expensive to be. The alternative newspaper Georgia Straight promoted the gathering. Its organizers framed it as a protest against the undercover squad. The city read it as a challenge.

## Two Minutes in Maple Tree Square

Horses go through a dense crowd the way a plough goes through soil, and the square had only a few narrow outlets. People who had come to watch, including tourists browsing the shops on a summer evening, found themselves inside the dispersal rather than outside it. Officers swung batons at whoever was in reach; some in the crowd threw rocks and chunks of broken concrete back. The fighting spilled out of the square and into the surrounding streets. Numbers of people were hurt. By the end of the night seventy-nine people had been arrested and thirty-eight were charged. The photographs that ran in the papers over the following days did more damage to the police department's standing than any of the charges did to the accused.

## Unnecessary, Unwarranted and Excessive

The public outcry was loud enough that the province appointed a commission of inquiry under Supreme Court Justice Thomas Dohm. It sat for ten days and heard forty-eight witnesses. Dohm's finding on the police was unambiguous: they had used force that was unnecessary, unwarranted and excessive. It was the first time a Canadian inquiry had said so plainly of a major city force, and the phrase stuck. The report was not a vindication of the protesters, though. Dohm assigned the primary blame to two of the smoke-in's organizers, describing them in terms that read now as a judge's alarm at a politics he did not recognize - intelligent and dangerous individuals. The BC Civil Liberties Association argued the inquiry had stopped short of the real question, which was not who lit the match but why the relationship between young people and the state had become so combustible in the first place.

## The Glass Wall at Abbott and Cordova

One block west of Maple Tree Square stands the Woodward's building, a department store that opened in 1903, anchored the neighbourhood for ninety years, and went bankrupt in 1993. Its empty carcass became the Downtown Eastside's central grievance. In 2002, activists occupied it for a week demanding social housing; police cleared them out, and a tent city stayed on the sidewalk for three months, which is what finally forced the redevelopment. The rebuilt complex was substantially finished in September 2010, carrying two hundred units of non-market housing alongside the market condominiums and a Simon Fraser University arts campus. In its atrium hangs a photograph thirty feet high and fifty feet wide, printed on glass: Abbott and Cordova, 7 August 1971, made by the Vancouver artist Stan Douglas in 2008. It is not a documentary image but a meticulous restaging of that night, actors and period cars arranged on the actual corner. Gastown itself was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 2009, and Canada legalized recreational cannabis on 17 October 2018, forty-seven years after the square was cleared over it.

## Flight Context

Maple Tree Square sits at 49.2833 N, 123.1042 W, at the junction of Water, Carrall and Alexander Streets in Gastown, on the southern shore of Burrard Inlet at the eastern edge of downtown Vancouver. From the air the district reads as a distinct pocket of low brick buildings and narrow angled streets wedged between the glass towers of the downtown core to the west and the rail yards and container cranes of the Port of Vancouver to the east - the Woodward's tower with its rooftop W sign is a prominent landmark in it. Fly it at 1,500 to 3,000 feet for the street pattern; the white sails of Canada Place are half a mile northwest, and Rogers Arena and BC Place lie a mile south. This is congested, controlled airspace and requires clearance. Vancouver Harbour Water Airport (CYHC) handles constant scheduled floatplane traffic off Coal Harbour immediately to the northwest, and Vancouver International (CYVR) is about 9 nm southwest. Boundary Bay (CZBB) lies 16 nm south and Pitt Meadows (CYPK) about 15 nm east. Summer evenings, like the one in August 1971, usually bring clear air and a light westerly off the Strait; autumn through spring expect low cloud and rain, with the North Shore mountains across the inlet often obscured.
