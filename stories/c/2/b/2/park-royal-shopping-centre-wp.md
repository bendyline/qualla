# Park Royal Shopping Centre

Canada's first covered shopping mall was named after a London brewery suburb, built partly on Squamish Nation land, and has spent seventy-five years rebuilding itself.

**Location:** 49.3260, -123.1370

**Categories:** Retail and commerce, Architecture, Urban history, Indigenous land, British Columbia

## Introduction

The name came from a brewery. When the Guinness family - the Dublin brewing dynasty - set about developing the mountainside above West Vancouver as the British Properties, they also bought the flat ground at the bottom of the hill, and they called the shopping centre they built there Park Royal, after the London suburb where a Guinness brewery stood. It opened on 1 September 1950 and it was the first covered shopping mall in Canada, which in 1950 meant something close to science fiction: shops you could walk between without getting rained on, in a place where it rains about half the year. The Guinnesses held on until 1986. The mall is still here, and still rebuilding itself.

## Two Malls and a Highway Between Them

Marine Drive runs straight through the middle of the property, which is why locals speak of the North Mall and the South Mall as separate destinations rather than wings of one building. North came first, anchored at its east end by Woodward's and at its west by the Woodward's Food Floors. A 1962 expansion pushed the centre south across the road and brought in SuperValu and Eaton's; a Hudson's Bay store followed in the mid-1970s. Then the anchors began playing musical chairs. Woodward's collapsed in 1993, The Bay moved into its space, and the old Food Floor was carved up into a liquor store, a London Drugs and a video rental outlet that has since gone the way of video rental. Two intersections and a pedestrian overpass stitch the halves together.

## Whose Ground It Is

The mall sits in two jurisdictions at once. Part of it is in the District of West Vancouver; part of it is on X̱wemelch'stn, the Capilano reserve, held by the Squamish Nation. The name means roughly "fast moving water of fish," for the Capilano River running past it to the inlet. X̱wemelch'stn is the Squamish Nation's most populous reserve, with more than five hundred houses, and the commercial development concentrated on its West Vancouver side has made the community a landlord to a substantial slice of North Shore retail and port property. Shoppers crossing between the North and South malls cross a boundary most of them have never thought about.

## Whistler, Rebuilt at Sea Level

In late 2004 Park Royal opened The Village, and the design brief was unusually literal: build something that feels like Whistler Village, where people wander outdoors between shops instead of along an interior corridor. F+A Architects of Pasadena and Vancouver's Musson Cattell Mackey Partnership delivered 238,000 square feet for roughly $30 million, billed as Canada's first lifestyle centre - open-air retail aimed squarely at an affluent West Vancouver market. A bowling alley and a golf practice range were demolished to make room. Ten years later the district approved a permit to strip the older buildings of their fabric canopies, faux columns and "nautical" theme, unifying north and south in one calmer skin. Zara, H&M, Aritzia, Anthropologie and Urban Outfitters arrived with the renovation.

## The End of the Anchor Era

Department stores made this place and then left it. Simons took over the former Extra Foods space in 2015, the same year the south-side food court moved up to the second floor. A Cineplex VIP cinema opened above The Brick on 3 April 2019. And in 2025 the oldest thread finally snapped: Hudson's Bay, successor at Park Royal to Woodward's, sought creditor protection in March and closed every store in the country on 1 June. Canadian Tire bought the brand and the stripes for $30 million; nobody bought the stores. What remains at Park Royal is a different kind of tenancy - Best Buy, Staples, Simons, Sport Chek, Dollarama - spread across some 280 shops and 1.4 million square feet.

## The Bus Stops Here

For the North Shore, Park Royal is not only where you shop; it is where you change buses. The Park Royal Exchange on Marine Drive is West Vancouver's main transit hub, the western terminus of TransLink's R2 Marine Drive RapidBus and the meeting point for the district's own Blue Bus routes. The result is a shopping centre that functions as a town centre by default, in a municipality that never built a conventional downtown. Seventy-five years after the Guinnesses cleared the ground, the mall remains the place everyone on this side of the water passes through.

## Flight Context

Park Royal lies at 49.326 N, 123.137 W, on the north shore of Burrard Inlet immediately west of the Lions Gate Bridge's north landing and just east of the Capilano River mouth. It is one of the easiest targets on the North Shore from the air: a large pale roofscape and parking fields split by the dark line of Marine Drive, with the green suspension bridge and the First Narrows on one side and Stanley Park directly across the water. Fly it at 1,500 to 3,000 feet AGL. The Vancouver Harbour Flight Centre (CYHC) floatplane base at Coal Harbour is barely three kilometres southeast across the inlet, and Vancouver International (CYVR) is about 15 km south on Sea Island; Pitt Meadows (CYPK) is the nearest quiet general-aviation field to the east. The North Shore mountains rise sharply behind the mall and routinely trap cloud on their faces - expect the ridgeline to disappear long before the waterfront does.
