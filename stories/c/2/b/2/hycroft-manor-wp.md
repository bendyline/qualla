# Hycroft Manor

A thirty-room Edwardian mansion in Shaughnessy, built on a prairie land fortune, given away to wounded soldiers, and run for the last sixty years by a club of university women.

**Location:** 49.2573, -123.1366

**Categories:** Heritage buildings, Architecture, History, Vancouver, British Columbia

## Introduction

Alexander Duncan McRae made something like nine million dollars buying prairie cheaply and selling it dear - roughly five million acres of Saskatchewan, moved through a land company he ran out of Minnesota before he was forty. He spent about a hundred thousand of it on a house in Vancouver with thirty rooms in it. Then, in 1942, he gave the house away. Hycroft still stands on its terrace above Shaughnessy's curving streets, and the extraordinary thing about it is not the plasterwork or the ballroom but the sequence of people who have had the keys: a lumber baron, then convalescing soldiers, then, for more than sixty years now, a club of university women who pay for its roof with scholarship dinners.

## Nine Million Acres of Nerve

McRae was born in Glencoe, Ontario, in 1874, and got his start in Minnesota banking and insuring grain elevators - unglamorous work that taught him exactly how much the Canadian West was about to be worth. His Saskatchewan Valley Land Company assembled some five million acres and cleared roughly nine million dollars. He then moved the money west into timber. Canadian Western Lumber became, by the measure of its day, the largest lumber and wood manufacturing company in the world, cutting 175 million board feet a year by 1912. Fisheries and mining followed. The house he built with the proceeds sits in Shaughnessy, the planned enclave the Canadian Pacific Railway laid out for men exactly like him, which is why the streets there curve while the rest of Vancouver runs in a grid.

## Hooper Built It, Marega Finished It

Thomas Hooper designed the mansion, and it went up between 1909 and 1912. Inside, the sculpted plasterwork is the work of Charles Marega, who is the more interesting name on the invoice. Marega was born in Austria-Hungary in 1871, trained in Italy, studied in Vienna and Zurich, worked in South Africa, and arrived in Vancouver in October 1909 en route to California - staying, the story goes, because his wife Bertha decided the North Shore mountains looked like Switzerland. He stayed for thirty years. He carved the caryatids on the Sun Tower in 1912, the statue of George Vancouver, and finally the two stone lions crouched at the south end of the Lions Gate Bridge. He died in 1939 after teaching a sculpture class. Hycroft's ceilings are early Marega, made before the city knew who he was.

## Major General, Member, Senator

The First World War made McRae a soldier of a particular kind. He began by procuring horses - eight thousand of them - and ended as a major general directing supplies and services, work that earned him the Order of the Bath in 1917. Politics came next. He won Vancouver North in 1926, and the following year organized the Conservative Party's first modern leadership convention, a machine-building exercise that helped carry R. B. Bennett to the prime ministership in 1930. That same election McRae lost his own seat. He was appointed to the Senate instead, and sat there until he died in Ottawa on June 26, 1946. A man who had bought and sold half a province ended his career in the chamber of second thought.

## A Ballroom Full of Stretchers

In 1942, with the war grinding on and casualties coming home, McRae handed Hycroft to the federal government to be used as a hospital for wounded servicemen. Picture the transfer honestly: dressings and bed frames in rooms designed for receiving lines, men learning to walk again along a terrace built for summer parties. It was not a gesture made in a vacuum - Vancouver was short of beds and McRae was short of use for a thirty-room house - but it is still the most consequential thing that ever happened inside the building. For four years the grandest private residence in the city belonged to whoever needed it most.

## The Club That Kept the House

In 1962 the Canadian Federation of University Women bought Hycroft and renovated it as the clubhouse of the University Women's Club of Vancouver, which has held it ever since. The club now counts more than three hundred members drawn from over a hundred universities, and funds scholarships and bursaries through its Trust Fund for Education. The house is a City of Vancouver heritage property, kept up by its members and the Hycroft Heritage Preservation Foundation through concerts, lectures, themed dinners, weddings, film shoots and the annual Hycroft for the Holidays. Walk in off McRae Avenue and you find original interiors, a walled garden, a terrace with the city below it - and an institution that pays for a lumber baron's mansion by arguing, year after year, for women's education. McRae would not have predicted it. It is the best thing that could have happened to his house.

## Flight Context

Hycroft stands at 49.25733 N, 123.13664 W, at 1489 McRae Avenue in First Shaughnessy, on the rise of land south of False Creek. Fly it at 1,500-2,500 feet AGL. The neighbourhood itself is the navigational giveaway: Shaughnessy's looping crescents and oval boulevards break sharply from the relentless grid that covers the rest of Vancouver, and the mature tree canopy over them reads darker than any surrounding district. Granville Street runs along the eastern edge; VanDusen Botanical Garden lies a short distance south; the domed Bloedel Conservatory on Queen Elizabeth Park's hill sits about 2 nm southeast, and the Granville and Burrard bridges cross False Creek just north. Nearby airports: Vancouver International (CYVR) about 4 nm southwest, Vancouver Harbour Flight Centre (CYHC) about 2 nm north-northeast, Boundary Bay (CZBB) about 12 nm southeast, and Pitt Meadows (CYPK) about 16 nm east. This lies inside Vancouver's terminal control area beneath busy arrival paths, so clearance is required. Late-afternoon light in autumn, after the chestnuts and maples turn, shows the district at its best.
