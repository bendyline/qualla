# Spokane

The heart of the Inland Empire where waterfalls thunder through downtown and the Centennial Trail connects two states along the river that gave the city its name.

**Location:** 47.6740, -117.4250

**Categories:** city, washington, urban, craft-beer, trails

## Introduction

The Spokane River crashes over the falls in the heart of downtown, a rare sight - a major waterfall at the center of a city, its thunder audible from the shops and restaurants that line the streets above. This is Spokane (say 'spo-CAN'), the largest city between Seattle and Minneapolis, hub of the region locals call the Inland Empire. Named for the Salish-speaking tribe who were the original inhabitants, spelled with the 'e' added by early European trappers, Spokane anchors an economic region of 1.5 million people spanning eastern Washington and the Idaho panhandle. The wheat fields of the Palouse end here, at the valley carved by the river that still powers the city.

## Falls at the Heart

Riverfront Park occupies the islands and banks where Spokane Falls drops the river through a series of cascades. The park was born from Expo '74, the first world's fair with an environmental theme, which transformed an industrial railyard into the green space that now defines downtown. The legacy includes a landmark clock tower, a historic carousel, and views of the falls from walkways and bridges.

The Monroe Street Bridge spans the gorge below the falls, offering perspectives on the hydroelectric complex that has generated power here since the 1890s. The Centennial Trail passes through, a 37-mile paved path that follows the river from downtown Coeur d'Alene through the Spokane Valley and into Riverside State Park - one of the finest urban trail systems in the country.

## Gateway to Adventure

Spokane's location makes it a gateway to adventures in every direction. Glacier National Park lies three hours northeast. Mount Spokane rises in the Selkirk foothills 45 minutes north, offering skiing in winter and views stretching into Canada from its 5,883-foot summit. Grand Coulee Dam, the largest concrete structure and electric power producer in the United States, sits 90 miles west.

Closer to home, the region claims 33 golf courses, including Coeur d'Alene's famous floating green. The Spokane River itself offers rafting, kayaking, and tubing in summer. The climate is drier than Seattle - Spokane receives only a fraction of western Washington's rainfall - but winters bring real snow, and the city embraces the cold with local ski hills and frozen adventure.

## Craft Beer Capital

Spokane has exploded as a craft brewing destination. The Inland Northwest Ale Trail maps over 25 microbreweries, many within the city limits. Taprooms cluster downtown and in the neighborhoods, serving locally brewed beers that have won regional and national recognition.

The dining scene has kept pace. Chef-owned restaurants emphasize locally sourced ingredients, with the Palouse's agricultural bounty available year-round. The city has become known for artisan pizza - creative pies with names like the 'Honey Badger' topped with a honey glaze and four cheeses. Downtown's historic buildings house restaurants that range from casual pubs to upscale dining, many in renovated spaces that preserve the city's early 20th-century architecture.

## Events and Culture

Bloomsday draws the city's biggest crowds - on the first Sunday of May, tens of thousands run the 12-kilometer course through downtown streets. Hoopfest follows in late June, transforming the city into the world's largest 3-on-3 basketball tournament. Both events are so popular that locals advise visitors to plan around them: traffic becomes impossible and hotels book solid.

Beyond these signature events, the city offers year-round culture. Art galleries cluster downtown, hosting regular First Friday openings. The historic Davenport Hotel, magnificently restored, stands as a landmark of early 20th-century grandeur. The Centennial Trail brings cyclists, runners, and skaters through the downtown core at all hours, particularly beautiful where it passes under the Monroe Street Bridge with views of the falls above.

## Flight Context

Located at 47.67N, 117.43W in eastern Washington where the Spokane River valley meets the edge of the Palouse. Downtown is clearly visible along both sides of the river, with the falls and Riverfront Park at the center. I-90 runs east-west through the city, continuing to Coeur d'Alene 30 miles east and Seattle 280 miles west. Spokane International Airport (KGEG) is located southwest of downtown with good airline service. The rolling wheat fields of the Palouse stretch to the south; forested mountains rise to the north and east. Climate is high desert - much drier than western Washington. Hot summers, cold winters with real snow. Clear conditions common; smoke from wildfires possible in late summer.
