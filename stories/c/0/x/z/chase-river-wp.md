# Chase River

A short Vancouver Island river named for a manhunt in the snow, dammed to wash coal, and split lengthwise by a fault line.

**Location:** 49.1361, -123.9192

**Categories:** Rivers of Vancouver Island, Mid Vancouver Island, History of Vancouver Island, Rivers of British Columbia

## Introduction

Rivers are usually named for what they look like, or for whoever claimed the land, or for a word in the language that was spoken here first. Chase River is named for a manhunt. In the winter of 1852, a Scottish shepherd named Peter Brown was killed on the ground that would become Nanaimo. Word went out that the governor and his troops were coming, and fresh snow made the tracking easy. The pursuit ended at a small river running east off the flank of Mount Benson, and the river has carried that word ever since. So has the Nanaimo neighbourhood built along its lower reach.

## Nine Kilometres of Downhill

Mount Benson rises 1,023 metres about nine kilometres west of Nanaimo, the dominant shape on the city's skyline. Captain Richards of the Royal Navy named it in 1859 for a friend, Alfred Robson Benson, a Yorkshire physician then serving the Hudson's Bay Company. Chase River begins on the mountain's southwest side and runs east without much ceremony, dropping through Colliery Dam Park, threading the neighbourhoods of Harewood and Chase River, and finally spilling into the Nanaimo River estuary at the south end of Nanaimo Harbour, where the fresh water disappears into the Strait of Georgia. It is not a long river. It is, however, a river that has been asked to do a great deal of work, and it has left the marks to prove it.

## Tracks in Fresh Snow

The killing of Peter Brown in the winter of 1852-53 was the kind of event that reshapes a colony's relations for a generation. Two men were accused, one Cowichan, one Snuneymuxw. The Snuneymuxw man, hearing that the governor and an armed party were coming for him, ran. He might have succeeded in any other season. Snow had fallen fresh, and a set of prints across open ground is an argument no one wins. The chase closed at the riverbank. What happened next moved quickly, and the speed is the part that still reads strangely more than a century and a half later.

## The Seventeenth of January

The two accused were tried aboard the Hudson's Bay Company steamer Beaver on 17 January 1853, and they were hanged the same day. There was no interval for appeal, for counsel, or for anything else. The site of the gallows was on an island in Nanaimo Harbour then called Douglas Island, and the headland took its name directly from what had been done there: Execution Point. Both names were eventually softened. In 1960 Execution Point became Gallows Point, which is a euphemism only if you do not know what a gallows is. Douglas Island became Protection Island. The point still marks the eastern edge of the harbour, and most of the people who pass it have no idea what it was called first.

## Water to Wash the Coal

Nanaimo grew on coal, and coal has to be washed. In 1910 and 1911 the Western Fuel Company threw a series of dams across Chase River to guarantee the supply, creating reservoirs whose primary customer was the washing plant and whose secondary beneficiaries were the households strung along the pipeline. Before long most of South Harewood was drinking from it. The industry that built the dams is gone, but the dams outlived it, and the city eventually did the sensible thing and turned the whole waterlogged valley into Colliery Dam Park. Today the reservoirs are for swimming. The trails that follow them were once maintenance roads. It is a rare piece of industrial infrastructure that ended up making a town's summers better.

## The Abyss

There is a reason the river found this particular line down the hill. The Chase River Fault, a strike-slip fracture in the bedrock, runs straight through the park, and the river and its reservoirs sit in the groove the fault carved. Walk the park and the evidence surfaces: narrow fissures, abrupt clefts, rock faces that do not match across a gap. About a kilometre from the Upper Colliery Dam is the one locals simply call The Abyss, a chasm deep enough to draw hikers and geologists in roughly equal numbers. Most rivers make their own valleys over unimaginable spans of time. This one was handed a crack in the island and told to use it.

## Flight Context

Chase River meets salt water at 49.136°N, 123.919°W, at the south end of Nanaimo Harbour in the Nanaimo River estuary. The best visual reference is Mount Benson at 1,023 m (3,356 ft), nine kilometres west of the city and unmistakable from any direction; trace its southwest shoulder east and you have the drainage. From 3,000-4,000 ft the Colliery Dam reservoirs show as two small dark rectangles of standing water in the forest just southwest of downtown Nanaimo, and Protection Island with Gallows Point sits in the harbour immediately offshore. Nanaimo Airport (CYCD) lies south of the city; Vancouver International (CYVR) is a short hop east across the Strait of Georgia. Expect afternoon inflow winds funnelling through the strait and low stratus on the island's east coast in the cooler months.
