# Mansons Landing

A sandspit, a lagoon and a warm freshwater lake at the south end of Cortes Island, where thousands of years of Klahoose shell midden lie under a park named for a Shetland trader's store.

**Location:** 50.0667, -124.9830

**Categories:** Populated places in the Strathcona Regional District, Cortes Island, Provincial parks of British Columbia, First Nations history

## Introduction

Walk out onto the sandspit at Mansons Landing and pay attention to what is underfoot. It is not sand all the way down. A large part of the spit is midden — shell, bone, charcoal and fire-cracked rock, compacted layer on layer, the accumulated residue of meals eaten on this beach across a span of time no plaque attempts to summarize. A provincial park covers the ground now. A trading post and a post office stood nearby, and the name on every chart and ferry schedule belongs to a Shetlander who arrived in the 1880s. The ground itself keeps a considerably older record, written in broken clamshell.

## Before the Landing Had a Name

This is Klahoose territory, and has been for far longer than the written record reaches. The Klahoose used and managed Cortes Island and the waters around it well before Europeans arrived, moving through a seasonal round that answered to what was ripe and what was running. Squirrel Cove, on the island's east side, was a summer place — spring shellfish, berries, gardens planted and harvested before the return to Toba Inlet on the mainland in the fall. In the late 1890s the Klahoose relocated from Toba Inlet to Squirrel Cove permanently. The spit and lagoon at the island's southern tip were part of the same productive geography: a sheltered tidal lagoon that traps warmth and fish, a beach that yields clams, and a freshwater lake a short walk inland. That combination is why the midden is there, and why it is deep.

## Two Brothers from Shetland

Michael Manson was born at Pickigarth in the Shetland Islands in April 1857 and came to British Columbia in 1874, still a teenager. By the late 1880s he and his younger brother John, who followed him out from Shetland in 1887, were running a trading post at the south end of Cortes — buying and selling with the Klahoose and supplying the logging and fishing outfits then working their way up the coast. The store held together until 1895. The paperwork followed the commerce: the Cortez Island Post Office opened on April 1, 1893 with Michael Manson as postmaster, shut on December 1, 1898, reopened on May 1, 1904, and was finally renamed Mansons Landing Post Office on July 9, 1941. The provincial gazette got around to recognizing Mansons Landing as a steamboat landing and settlement on April 6, 1950 — fifty-seven years after the mail first started arriving. Michael himself went into politics, sitting in the Legislative Assembly for Comox from 1909 to 1916 and returning for Mackenzie in 1924, a seat he still held when he died at Bella Coola in July 1932.

## Float Houses in the Lagoon

For a stretch of the twentieth century the lagoon was a neighbourhood that floated. In the 1950s roughly fifty people lived here on float houses moored in the sheltered water, most of them logging families, with a few dwellings hauled up onto the shore. It was a practical arrangement on a coast where the work moved and the roads did not. When a show finished, a house could be towed to the next inlet behind a boat. Children rowed to school. The store at the lagoon served the whole arrangement, and the tide came and went beneath the kitchen floor twice a day. Nothing about it was quaint at the time — it was simply how you lived on a coast with more water than road — but it produced a kind of community that no longer exists anywhere on this shore.

## A Park, and a Building Worth One Dollar

Mansons Landing Provincial Park was established in 1974 to broaden the range of marine parks in the northern Gulf Islands, and it took in about a hundred hectares fronting water on two sides: Manson Bay and the saltwater lagoon on one flank, Hague Lake on the other. Hague Lake is the draw for most visitors — freshwater swimming warm enough to be genuinely pleasant, off a beach of pale sand, a rarity on a coast where the ocean rarely gets above bracing. The store at the lagoon carried on for about a year after the park arrived, then closed. Its building stood empty until roughly 1995, when BC Parks sold it to the Cortes Island Museum and Archives for one dollar. That is a fitting last transaction for a place that began as a trading post: the store itself, sold cheap, to the people whose job is remembering.

## Flight Context

Mansons Landing sits at 50.07N, 124.98W near the southern tip of Cortes Island in the northern Strait of Georgia, British Columbia. From the air the signature is a pale sandspit enclosing a shallow tidal lagoon on the island's southwest shore, with Hague Lake — a small dark freshwater lake — just inland to the east. Sutil Channel separates Cortes from Quadra Island to the west; Marina Island lies immediately southwest, and Hernando Island a few miles south. Use Smelt Bay at the island's southern point as your turning fix. Nearest airports: Campbell River (CYBL) about 20nm west, Powell River (CYPW) about 22nm southeast, and Cortes Island's own water aerodrome traffic plus regular floatplane service into Cortes Bay and Squirrel Cove. Best viewed between 1,500 and 3,000 feet at low tide, when the lagoon drains and the full shape of the spit and tidal flats shows clearly. Summer mornings here are often clear with afternoon inflow winds through Sutil Channel.
