# Twin Islands (British Columbia)

Two private islands at the mouth of Desolation Sound, separated by sixty metres of water, where a German margrave's family retreat became the one place in Canada a queen could go to be left alone.

**Location:** 50.0294, -124.9328

**Categories:** islands, british-columbia, discovery-islands, royalty, coastal-history

## Introduction

In August 1994, having opened the Commonwealth Games in Victoria, Queen Elizabeth II went missing for a few days. Not dramatically, and not far - roughly two hundred kilometres up the Strait of Georgia to a pair of forested islands at the entrance to Desolation Sound, where there was a lodge, a garden, and nobody to receive her. Prince Philip went the other direction entirely, north to Yellowknife, Iqaluit, and Rankin Inlet. She stayed at Twin Islands, which is not a public place and never has been. It belonged to family. That is the strange and quiet fact at the centre of these two small islands southeast of Cortes: a scrap of the Discovery Islands archipelago that ended up with a standing invitation from the House of Windsor, and almost nobody else.

## Sixty Metres of Water

Twin Islands are, as advertised, two. The southern island runs about a kilometre and a half across, rocky and blunt; the northern one is slightly smaller, less rocky, and more heavily forested. Between them lies a channel roughly sixty metres wide - close enough that on a still morning a voice carries easily from one shore to the other, wide enough that they were never going to be one island. Together they amount to about 281 hectares. They sit southeast of Cortes Island in the Strathcona Regional District, at the southern fringe of the Discovery Islands where that archipelago meets the north Salish Sea and the water opens into Desolation Sound. Captain Vancouver gave the sound its gloomy name in 1792. The people who boat through it now use words like turquoise and bathwater, because the sound's shallow sills trap summer heat and make it the warmest salt water on the entire coast north of Mexico.

## What the Record Does Not Hold

People lived on these islands for thousands of years before any European ship came up this coast, and almost nothing of that occupation has been written down. Middens, clam gardens, and the memory of nations whose territory this is - that is the record, and it is not the kind that Wikipedia summarises well. The written history starts late and starts elsewhere. Spanish charts from the era of the Galiano and Valdes survey of 1792 labelled these the Ulloa Islands, a name that did not survive into English usage. The renaming to something purely descriptive, Twin Islands, is characteristic of this coast: an older name in a language the mapmakers did not speak, replaced by the most obvious thing a passing surveyor could see.

## A Lodge Built at the Wrong Time

In 1936 the Americans Dick and Ethel Andrews acquired the islands and set about building a lodge on them - a sprawling place raised from logs and stone cut on site by Scandinavian craftsmen brought over from nearby Lund, its gardens laid out against rock and rainforest. It was an ambitious thing to attempt in a spot reachable only by boat, and the timing was not accidental: the Andrews were shifting capital out of an import business tied to Japan, anticipating the war they could see coming. The lodge, finished around 1938, beat the war to completion. It is still there, still standing above the beach, and for most of the last ninety years the answer to whether you could visit has been no. The islands have been private almost continuously since, most recently under the ownership of Mark Torrance.

## The Baden Connection

In the late 1950s Maximilian, Margrave of Baden, of Baden-Baden in Germany, bought Twin Islands as a summer retreat for his family. Max von Baden was Prince Philip's nephew - Philip's sister Theodora had married into the House of Baden - and that single line of family relation is what put a British monarch on a rock in the Strait of Georgia. In 1971, during a tour aboard the royal yacht Britannia, Queen Elizabeth, Prince Philip, and Princess Anne came ashore here and had a picnic beneath a flowering dogwood. Twenty-three years later the Queen came back on her own. What Twin Islands offered was not grandeur, which she had plenty of, but the rarest commodity available to a head of state: a place where the only thing scheduled was the tide.

## Flight Context

Twin Islands lie at 50.029 degrees north, 124.933 degrees west, southeast of Cortes Island at the entrance to Desolation Sound in British Columbia. A viewing altitude of 3,000 to 5,000 feet shows both islands, the narrow channel dividing them, and the wider geometry of the Discovery Islands spreading north. Navigate by Cortes Island to the northwest and the deep blue inlets of Desolation Sound beyond it; the mainland mountains rise steeply behind, still snow-streaked well into summer. The islands themselves read as a single dark green mass unless you are close enough to resolve the sixty-metre channel. Nearest airports are Campbell River (CYBL) roughly 40 kilometres southwest on Vancouver Island, Powell River (CYPW) about 35 kilometres southeast on the mainland, and Comox (CYQQ) further south. Floatplanes serve the area from Campbell River and Vancouver. Summer brings reliably calm mornings and an afternoon inflow wind up the strait; winter southeasters funnel hard through these channels.
