# West Redonda Island

The Discovery Islands island where Spanish and British surveying expeditions anchored side by side in 1792 and pooled their charts - now home to twelve winter residents, a lake reached by portage, and the summer jellyfish of Roscoe Bay.

**Location:** 50.2164, -124.8644

**Categories:** Islands, Discovery Islands, Desolation Sound, Maritime History, British Columbia

## Introduction

In the summer of 1792 two rival empires parked their ships in the same inlet and helped each other with the paperwork. The Spanish schooners of Dionisio Alcala Galiano and Cayetano Valdes lay at anchor in Lewis Channel and Teakerne Arm alongside George Vancouver's two British vessels, and for several days the four crews worked the same problem: a maze of channels and islands that no European chart could yet make sense of. They sent boat parties out in every direction and shared what came back. It was not the era's usual arrangement. The island they were tied up against got the Spanish name - Isla Redonda, round - and West Redonda Island has been quietly hosting other people's expeditions ever since.

## Not a Single Pleasing Prospect

Vancouver was less charmed by the surroundings than the collaboration might suggest. Of the great sheltered basin just south of the island he wrote that there was not a single prospect that was pleasing to the eye, and the name stuck: Desolation Sound. It is one of the more spectacular misjudgements in the naming of this coast. What Vancouver saw as desolate - steep timbered walls dropping straight into deep green water, no beaches to speak of, no obvious harvest - is now among the most sought-after cruising grounds in North America. Summer boat traffic here runs into the hundreds of vessels a day. The sound is flanked by Cortes Island and the two Redondas, and it draws people for precisely the reasons Vancouver disliked it: nothing but rock, water and trees, arranged vertically.

## A Geography Made of Gaps

West Redonda is defined more by the water around it than by anything on it. Waddington Channel separates it from East Redonda; Lewis Channel runs between it and Cortes Island to the southwest; Deer Passage opens toward Raza Island; and Pryce Channel divides it from the mainland, which here is not gentle country but the mouths of Toba Inlet and Bute Inlet, two of the deepest fjord systems on the British Columbia coast. The island's west side is bitten into by Teakerne Arm, the inlet off Lewis Channel where the surveyors anchored. Administratively all of this falls inside Electoral Area C of the Strathcona Regional District, a jurisdiction that on paper governs an area most of its own residents have never crossed.

## The Portage to Black Lake

On the eastern side, Roscoe Bay Park protects a small inlet with sheltered anchorage - the kind of hole in the rock that a cruising sailor learns about once and remembers forever. Two hundred metres of flat portage from the head of the bay brings you to Black Lake, fresh water after days of salt, with a shoreline walk of an hour or two and fishing at the end of it. The bay itself is better admired than swum in; historical sewage discharge left water-quality concerns that newer federal rules may eventually clear. What the bay does deliver, reliably and strangely, is jellyfish. Roscoe Bay is known for annual congregations of moon jellies - translucent discs drifting in their thousands beneath anchored hulls, pulsing slowly in water so still it holds them like glass.

## Twelve People in Winter

Refuge Cove is the only community on the island, and effectively the only one in Desolation Sound. In July it is a crossroads: moorage, fuel dock, groceries, ice, showers, laundry, espresso, and hundreds of boaters a day stopping to provision before disappearing back into the channels. About twenty families live there through the summer. Then the season turns, the traffic evaporates, and the population settles to roughly a dozen people. The general store and fuel dock keep winter hours that tell you everything about the scale of the place - Monday, Wednesday and Friday, one o'clock to three. Getting there at all requires intent. The usual route is a ferry to Powell River, a drive to Lund, and then about thirteen miles by boat, unless you arrive the other way, which is by float plane onto the water out front.

## Flight Context

Centred at 50.216 N, 124.864 W in the Discovery Islands of British Columbia, between Vancouver Island and the mainland. The island is a high, heavily timbered mass with almost no flat ground, and from the air it is best identified by the water around it: Desolation Sound immediately south, Lewis Channel on the southwest side toward Cortes Island, Waddington Channel on the east separating it from East Redonda, and Pryce Channel to the north against the mainland. Teakerne Arm cuts into the west side; Roscoe Bay notches the east; Refuge Cove on the southwest shore is the only settlement and shows as a cluster of docks and floats. Toba Inlet and Bute Inlet drive northeast into the Coast Mountains and make unmistakable navigational features. Recommended viewing altitude is 3,000-5,000 ft MSL for the channels, higher if you want the inlets and the icefields behind them. Nearest fields are Powell River (CYPW) roughly 40 km southeast and Campbell River (CYBL) about 50 km west; Refuge Cove itself is a working float-plane destination. Expect channel-funnelled winds, glassy mornings, and afternoon inflow up the inlets; low stratus can close the passages quickly even on otherwise clear days.
