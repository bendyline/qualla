# Halfmoon Bay, British Columbia

A scalloped bay on the Sunshine Coast named three times over, in shíshálh territory, with one of the mildest climates in Canada.

**Location:** 49.5167, -123.9170

**Categories:** british-columbia, sunshine-coast, coastal, first-nations, small-town, climate

## Introduction

The main road into town is called Redrooffs Road, and it is named after a resort that is no longer there - a cluster of cabins, every one of them roofed in red, which locals used as a landmark until the landmark itself stopped existing and only the directions survived. That is a fairly Sunshine Coast way to organize a place. Halfmoon Bay is a crescent of water on the mainland shore northwest of Sechelt, sheltered behind South Thormanby Island, with the bulk of Vancouver Island standing off beyond the Strait of Georgia to take the weather first. About 2,800 people call it home, though a good share of them only in summer.

## Three Names for One Crescent

In she shashishalhem, the language of the shíshálh Nation, this place is xwilkway. It carried that name for a very long time before anyone wrote it down.

The second name arrived in 1899 with the Priestland family, who settled here and lent the bay their surname: Priestland Bay, on charts and in correspondence, for a stretch of the early settler period. The third name is the one that stuck, and it is the least imaginative of the three - somebody looked at the shape of the water and called it Halfmoon Bay. The curve really is that clean. From a boat coming in past the Thormanby Islands, the shoreline scoops around in a single unbroken arc, and the name stops being a cliché and starts being a description.

## The Territory Around It

Halfmoon Bay lies within the shíshálh swiya - the lands and birthplace of the shíshálh Nation, a Coast Salish people whose territory covers some 515,000 hectares of the Sunshine Coast and the inlets behind it. Their language, she shashishalhem, is closest kin to Squamish, Halkomelem, and Nooksack, and elders began the work of reviving it in the 1970s, eventually producing a dictionary with a linguist from UBC.

In 1986 the shíshálh became the first Indigenous government in Canada to regain self-government under its own act of Parliament - a genuine first, decades ahead of most of the country. The Nation elects a Chief and Council every three years and runs the tems swiya Museum at its administrative complex in Sechelt. Anyone driving Highway 101 through here is driving through a territory that has been continuously governed, in one form or another, the whole time.

## Summer People, Winter People

The Sunshine Coast is not reachable by road from Vancouver - you take a ferry to get here, and that single fact shapes everything. It filters out casual traffic, keeps property turnover slow, and splits communities like Halfmoon Bay into two overlapping populations: the ones who stay through the grey months and the ones who arrive when the water warms up.

Homes scatter along Redrooffs Road and off Highway 101, tucked into the trees rather than lined up along a main street. Halfmoon Bay Elementary handles kindergarten through grade seven for 200 to 300 students; after that, teenagers ride out to Pender Harbour, Chatelech, or Elphinstone Secondary, which means a school bus route that doubles as a tour of the whole lower coast. It is a commute that would be intolerable in a city and is simply normal here.

## Zone 9b

Gardeners know Halfmoon Bay for a number: 9b on the Canadian cold hardiness map, which puts it among the mildest climates in the country. That rating is a licence. People here grow things that have no business surviving a Canadian winter - palms, hardy subtropicals, plants that would be a joke in Winnipeg - and they do it outdoors, in the ground, year after year.

The explanation is geography doing its quiet work. Vancouver Island absorbs the Pacific's worst, the Strait of Georgia moderates what gets through, and the coast range blocks the cold air that would otherwise spill down from the interior. What is left is a narrow band of rainy, frost-shy shoreline where the sea keeps the thermometer honest. Mornings arrive damp and soft. The bay holds its scallop of flat water. Somebody's Trachycarpus stands in a front yard at fifty degrees north, looking entirely out of place and thriving.

## Flight Context

Halfmoon Bay sits at 49.517N, 123.917W on the mainland shore of the Sunshine Coast, about 25 nm northwest of Vancouver across the Strait of Georgia. From 2,000 to 4,000 feet the bay's crescent is the giveaway, with South Thormanby and North Thormanby Islands standing offshore to the southwest and Highway 101 threading the forested shoreline northwest toward Pender Harbour. Sechelt lies a short hop southeast at the narrow isthmus between the strait and Sechelt Inlet. Nearest fields are Sechelt Aerodrome (CAP3) to the southeast and Powell River (CYPW) up the coast; Vancouver International (CYVR) is roughly 30 nm south-southeast, and floatplane traffic is constant along this shoreline in summer. Expect low stratus and drizzle through the wet season, good VFR conditions and glassy morning water in summer, and outflow winds funnelling out of the mainland inlets in winter cold snaps.
