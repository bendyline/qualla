# Red Lake Shootings

On March 21, 2005, a sixteen-year-old killed nine people and himself at two locations on the Red Lake Indian Reservation in northern Minnesota -- the deadliest school shooting in the United States since Columbine and still the worst in Minnesota history.

## Introduction

The Red Lake Indian Reservation sits in northern Minnesota, surrounded by boreal forest and anchored by the lake that gives it its name. It is the only reservation in the state owned and occupied entirely by members of its band -- the Red Lake Band of Chippewa Indians, a sovereign Ojibwe nation. With a population of roughly 5,000, Red Lake is remote: the nearest grocery stores are in Bemidji, more than 30 miles away. On March 21, 2005, this isolated community became the site of the deadliest school shooting in the United States since the 1999 Columbine massacre -- a tragedy that claimed ten lives, including the gunman's, and left at least nine others wounded.

## Eleven Minutes

The violence began at 2:47 p.m. when sixteen-year-old Jeff Weise shot his grandfather, Daryl Lussier Sr., a 58-year-old sergeant with the Red Lake tribal police, as he slept. He then killed his grandfather's girlfriend, 32-year-old Michelle Sigana. Weise took his grandfather's police-issue weapons -- a Glock 22 pistol and a Remington 870 shotgun -- along with a bulletproof vest, and drove the police squad car to Red Lake Senior High School, arriving at 2:49 p.m. At the main entrance, he shot and killed unarmed security guard Derrick Brun, 28. Moving through the corridors, Weise entered a study hall and killed English teacher Neva Rogers, 62, and students Alicia White, 14; Thurlene Stillday, 15; Chanelle Rosebear, 15; Chase Lussier, 15; and Dewayne Lewis, 15. After exchanging gunfire with arriving police, Weise turned the shotgun on himself at 2:58 p.m. From the first shot to the last, the entire rampage lasted eleven minutes.

## A Boy Without a Foundation

Jeff Weise was born on August 8, 1988. By the time he arrived at Red Lake Senior High School with a shotgun, nearly every pillar of his young life had collapsed. In 1997, when he was eight, his father died by suicide after a days-long standoff with Red Lake tribal police -- the same department where his grandfather served as sergeant. Two years later, his mother suffered severe brain damage in an alcohol-related car accident and was committed to a nursing home. Weise was placed in the custody of his paternal grandmother and moved from Minneapolis, where he had spent most of his childhood, to the reservation. He eventually moved in with his grandfather and Sigana, who gave him his own room. By 2004, he had attempted suicide twice. His aunts arranged hospitalization and ongoing treatment, which included counseling and a prescription for Prozac. He had recently been placed in homebound schooling for breaking school rules.

## A Reservation in Mourning

The night after the shooting, community members gathered in the high school gymnasium for a healing ceremony. They performed traditional Ojibwe ceremonies and prayed. The response from the broader nation was slower to arrive. Some Native American leaders publicly criticized what they perceived as muted attention from Washington compared to other school shootings. In April 2005, the Red Lake Band distributed 15 grants of $5,000 each from a memorial fund that had received $200,000 in donations from across the country. One grant went to Weise's own relatives, to help pay for his funeral and burial. Some objected, but a tribal spokesman explained that the family carried "a double burden" and was not eligible for state victim compensation. Security guard Derrick Brun received special recognition for his bravery, including acknowledgment from President George W. Bush. Sophomore Jeffrey May, who was wounded while trying to attack Weise, was later featured in Reader's Digest as an everyday hero.

## The Scars That Remain

On July 21, 2006, the Red Lake school district reached a settlement with the families of the victims, agreeing to pay $1,000,000 -- the maximum amount allowed by Minnesota law. Of that sum, $900,000 went immediately to 21 families, with $100,000 set aside for future distribution. Louis Jourdain, the son of Tribal Chairman Floyd "Buck" Jourdain Jr., was arrested on March 28, 2005, and charged with conspiracy to commit murder based on email exchanges with Weise related to plans for the attack. The conspiracy charge was eventually dropped, but Jourdain pleaded guilty to transmitting threatening messages through the Internet. The shooting remains the deadliest school attack in Minnesota history. A 2004 study had already found that a high proportion of Red Lake high school students had contemplated suicide. The tragedy exposed the depth of crisis facing an isolated reservation community -- limited employment, geographic remoteness, and intergenerational trauma -- while the community's response, rooted in Ojibwe tradition and collective grief, demonstrated a resilience that no headline could fully capture.

## Flight Context

Located at 48.16°N, 95.10°W in Beltrami County, northern Minnesota. The Red Lake Indian Reservation covers approximately 840,000 acres surrounding Upper and Lower Red Lake, which are visible as large bodies of water from cruising altitude. The town of Red Lake sits on the southern shore of Lower Red Lake. Nearest airport is Bemidji Regional Airport (KBJI), approximately 31 miles south. The reservation's remoteness is apparent from the air -- dense boreal forest, few roads, and the dominant presence of the lake system. Thief River Falls Regional Airport (KTVF) lies to the northwest. Overflying at 5,000-8,000 feet provides a clear view of the lake system and the scattered communities along its shores.
