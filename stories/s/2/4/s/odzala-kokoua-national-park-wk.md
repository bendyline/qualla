# Odzala-Kokoua National Park

One of Africa's oldest national parks shelters one of the world's largest concentrations of western lowland gorillas in 13,500 square kilometers of Congo Basin rainforest.

**Location:** 0.8000, 14.9000

**Categories:** national-parks, wildlife, conservation

## Introduction

One of the largest concentrations of western lowland gorillas on Earth lives here. That single fact makes Odzala-Kokoua National Park, deep in the Republic of the Congo, one of the most consequential patches of wilderness in Africa. Designated in 1935, it is among the continent's oldest protected areas, and at 13,500 square kilometers it sprawls across an expanse of equatorial rainforest larger than some European countries. Yet for most of its history, Odzala has been a park more in name than in practice, battered by civil war, ravaged by Ebola, and largely invisible to the outside world.

## A Forest of Clearings

From above, Odzala reveals itself as three distinct landscapes layered north to south. The northern reaches are mature rainforest, dense and dark, the canopy so thick that sunlight barely touches the ground. In the middle belt, marantaceae thickets dominate, creating an understory so tangled that progress on foot slows to a crawl. To the south, the forest breaks apart into a mosaic of savannah and gallery forest, open grasslands interrupted by ribbons of trees tracing the courses of streams. Scattered throughout are the bais, natural forest clearings where mineral-rich soils draw elephants, gorillas, and forest buffalo into the open. These clearings are Odzala's windows, the places where the forest's most elusive residents can actually be seen. Humidity clings to everything. Temperatures hover between 20 and 30 degrees Celsius year-round, and two wet seasons drench the park from March through May and again from September to November.

## The Gorillas' Stronghold

Odzala is home to 114 mammal species, but its gorillas define the park. Roughly 22,000 western lowland gorillas live here, constituting one of the largest single-park concentrations of a critically endangered subspecies. Researchers have habituated a few gorilla communities, meaning visitors can sit among them, close enough to hear the low rumble of a silverback's contentment or watch a juvenile tumble through the undergrowth. Chimpanzees also inhabit the forest but remain far harder to find. Thousands of forest elephants roam the park, tracked by a collaring program that aims to monitor their movements and guide protection strategies. Spotted hyenas survive here too, one of the last populations in this type of equatorial ecosystem. Three species of pangolin, including the giant pangolin, inhabit the forest floor and canopy. Above, more than 440 bird species fill the air. African grey parrots and African green pigeons gather in flocks numbering thousands, their collective calls building into a wall of sound.

## War, Disease, and Resurrection

For decades, Odzala existed largely on paper. The Congo Civil War from 1997 to 1999 shattered whatever fragile protections the park had enjoyed, and a series of Ebola outbreaks devastated both human communities and ape populations in the surrounding region. Poaching went unchecked. Tourism vanished entirely. By the time the violence subsided, the park was a protected area in which very little was actually being protected. The turning point came in 2010, when African Parks entered a management partnership with the Congolese government. Law enforcement was overhauled, anti-poaching patrols expanded, and the organization began working with the thousands of people living around the park's borders, recognizing that conservation without community engagement was conservation without a future. Poaching remains a persistent threat, but the trajectory has reversed. Wildlife populations are recovering, and the park has slowly reopened to visitors willing to make the journey.

## The Journey In

Getting to Odzala is itself an act of commitment. Driving from Brazzaville takes approximately 16 hours on roads that test both vehicle and resolve. The alternative is a two-hour charter flight from the capital, offered twice a week, that deposits visitors into a landscape most will never have imagined. Three lodges operated by the Congo Conservation Company provide accommodation within the park and its surrounding concessions. Ngaga Camp, with six rooms near the park's edge, serves as the center of gorilla viewing. Lango Camp, also six rooms, sits in dense gallery forest overlooking a bai and a winding river. Mboko Camp, the largest at twelve rooms, stretches along the bank of a river tributary. Walking and driving excursions radiate from each camp into surrounding forest. The remoteness that makes Odzala difficult to reach is the same quality that has preserved it.

## A Basin Under Watch

Odzala-Kokoua does not exist in isolation. The WWF considers it a biodiversity hotspot within TRIDOM, a tri-national conservation area spanning the Republic of the Congo, Cameroon, and Gabon. Over 4,400 plant species have been recorded across the Congo Basin, and a significant portion of that botanical diversity grows within Odzala's borders. BirdLife International has recognized the park as an Important Bird Area. The park's story is one of potential repeatedly tested by reality, a place where the sheer biological richness of equatorial Africa confronts the forces that threaten to dismantle it. From the air, the forest canopy stretches to every horizon, unbroken and ancient, concealing within its depths a concentration of life that few places on Earth can match.

## Flight Context

Located at 0.80N, 14.90E in the Republic of the Congo. The park covers 13,500 square kilometers of equatorial rainforest. From altitude, the forest canopy appears as an unbroken green carpet with occasional bai clearings visible as lighter patches. The nearest major airport is Maya-Maya Airport (FCBB) in Brazzaville, approximately 500 km to the south. Charter flights land at strips within the park. Best viewed at 15,000-25,000 feet where individual bais and the savannah-forest mosaic in the south become distinguishable.
