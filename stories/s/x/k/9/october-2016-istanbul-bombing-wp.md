# October 2016 Istanbul Bombing

On October 6, 2016, a motorcycle bomb detonated in the Yenibosna neighborhood of Istanbul, wounding ten civilians in an attack targeting a nearby police station.

**Location:** 40.9956, 28.8315

**Categories:** Istanbul history, 2016 Turkey, Terrorism, Bahçelievler, Urban violence, Memorial

## Introduction

On the afternoon of October 6, 2016, at around 3:50 p.m., a bomb concealed on a motorcycle detonated in the Yenibosna neighborhood of Istanbul's Bahçelievler district. Ten people were wounded — ten ordinary residents of a residential neighborhood, going about a Thursday afternoon. One was critically injured. The bomb had been directed at a police station a short distance away and detonated before reaching it. No one was killed, though the device was powerful enough to leave its mark on the street and on the people near it.

## A Thursday Afternoon in Yenibosna

Yenibosna is a working-class neighborhood in southwest Istanbul, far from the tourist corridors of Sultanahmet or the art galleries of Beyoğlu. It is a place of apartment blocks, local businesses, and the particular rhythm of an urban residential district — children heading to and from school, people running errands, the ordinary business of a Thursday. The bomb went off at 15:50, and the people it wounded were not in any sense participants in the political conflict it expressed. They were caught in a neighborhood street. Istanbul Governor Vasip Şahin arrived at the scene and confirmed the injuries; his initial statement reported five wounded, a figure later revised upward to ten as the full toll became clear.

## The Schools Nearby

Turkish authorities and security experts noted something about the timing that amplified the sense of what might have been: the bomb detonated roughly ten to twenty minutes before the end of the school day. Two schools stood in the immediate vicinity of the attack site. Had the explosion come later — when children were streaming out of buildings, filling the street, crowding into buses — the toll could have been catastrophic. That it came when it did, and that the bomb detonated short of its intended target, meant ten people were hurt rather than many more. The ten who were wounded, including the one critically injured, deserve to be remembered as individuals who suffered real harm, not as footnotes in a calculation about what could have been worse.

## Attribution and Aftermath

Turkish authorities attributed the attack to the PKK (Kurdistan Workers' Party), which had been engaged in a renewed armed conflict with the Turkish state since 2015. No group formally claimed responsibility at the time. On the day of the attack, Turkish police detained six individuals suspected of involvement. The primary suspect, identified as Felek Gun, was arrested in Aksaray Province while traveling toward Syria, carrying a false identification document. Five others, including Ekrem Gun and Serdar Gun, were detained alongside him. The investigation moved quickly; the arrests came within hours of the detonation.

## A City Under Pressure

The October 2016 attack in Yenibosna came during one of the most turbulent years Istanbul had experienced in a generation. Earlier in 2016, the city had suffered several devastating bombings — a suicide attack in Sultanahmet in January, a bombing near Taksim Square in March, an attack at Atatürk Airport in June that killed 42 people and wounded more than 230. The October attack was far smaller in scale than those, but it occurred in a city that was already grieving and already tense. For the people of Yenibosna, the proximity of the schools and the randomness of who happened to be on that street at that moment made the attack feel like something that could have found any of them. Memory holds what statistics cannot.
