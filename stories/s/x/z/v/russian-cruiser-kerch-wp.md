# Russian Cruiser Kerch

A Cold War warship that outlived the navy that built her, only to end her life burning at anchor in the harbor she was meant to defend.

**Location:** 44.6080, 33.5990

**Categories:** naval history, Cold War, Crimea, warships

## Introduction

She spent nearly half a century in the same stretch of the Black Sea, and in the end she never left it. The cruiser Kerch was built to hunt submarines across open ocean, a bristling product of Soviet naval ambition, yet her final voyage measured only a few miles: a slow tow across Sevastopol's inner waters to a scrapyard at Inkerman, where cutting torches took her apart piece by piece. Between the day her keel was laid and the day the last section fell, an entire empire had risen, aged, and dissolved beneath her decks.

## Built for a Colder War

Kerch began as steel in a shipyard on the Black Sea coast. Her keel was laid down on 30 April 1971 at the 61 Communards Shipyard in Nikolayev, the city now known as Mykolaiv, and she slid into the water on 21 July 1972. On 25 December 1974 she was commissioned into the Soviet Black Sea Fleet, a large anti-submarine cruiser designed to find and kill the enemy boats prowling the deep during the Cold War standoff. For seventeen years she served under the Soviet flag, part of a fleet whose reach stretched from the Mediterranean to the far corners of the world's oceans. When the Soviet Union collapsed in 1991, the ship simply changed masters, passing to the new Russian Navy without ever changing her home port.

## The Golden Eagle Class

Kerch belonged to a breed the Soviets called Project 1134B, and gave the soaring codename Berkut-B, the golden eagle. In the West they were known as the Kara class, and only a handful were ever built. What set them apart under the surface was their machinery: rather than steam, they ran on gas turbines, chosen for their efficiency and their relative quiet, and long tied to the engine works around the Nikolayev yards where Kerch herself was born. She was a submarine-hunter to her core, built around a missile-and-torpedo complex that could reach out toward an enemy boat tens of kilometers away, and she carried a helicopter on her stern deck to extend that reach still further over the horizon. For years she served as the flagship of the Black Sea Fleet, the most powerful vessel in a force with a long and storied history on these waters.

## Fire in the Harbor

The end came not in battle but at rest. On 4 November 2014, during routine servicing in Sevastopol, fire broke out aboard Kerch. Officials reported that no one was hurt and that the flames were held to the ship's aft section, but a warship gutted by fire is rarely worth saving. For a vessel that had spent forty years ready for war, it was an unremarkable, almost domestic kind of death: no torpedoes, no enemy, just smoke rising over the same harbor she had always called home. The damage sealed her fate. What the Cold War never demanded of her, an ordinary maintenance day finished in an afternoon.

## Erased from the Water

On 24 April 2020, Kerch was pulled from her dock and towed to the breakers at Inkerman, at the head of Sevastopol Bay. Her dismantling was so deliberate that anyone could follow it from space: satellite images captured on Google Earth show the cruiser shrinking through the spring and summer of that year, deck by deck, until only an empty berth remained by winter. It is a strange kind of monument, this record of a warship's disappearance logged from orbit. A vessel built to move unseen across the oceans ended her days watched from above, vanishing frame by frame into the sunlit shallows where she was born, served, and finally unmade. Every one of her sister ships in the class met a similar fate, retired and broken up as the fleets they were built for shrank and changed. The Kerch simply held on longer than most, one of the last of her kind still flying a flag, until the fire and the torches caught up with her too.

## Flight Context

The ship's final berthing and scrapping area lies around 44.61°N, 33.60°E, in the sheltered upper reaches of Sevastopol Bay near Inkerman, Crimea. From 5,000 to 7,000 feet the long, deep harbor inlet of Sevastopol cutting inland from the Black Sea is the dominant feature. Nearest airport is Sevastopol/Belbek (UKFB), with Simferopol (UKFF) about 40 miles to the northeast.
