# Gorg Blau

A mountain reservoir in Mallorca's highest country that drowned a celebrated beauty spot once painted by poets and artists.

**Location:** 39.8025, 2.8183

**Categories:** Reservoirs, Mountains, Nature, Serra de Tramuntana, Art & Culture, Mallorca

## Introduction

One of the most admired beauty spots in the Mallorcan mountains no longer exists — or rather, it lies beneath the surface of a lake. For generations, travelers on the winding road to Sóller would stop at a stone bridge where a mountain torrent met sheer cliff faces, a scene so striking that poets and painters made the journey just to see it. Then engineers dammed the valley, the water rose, and the bridge came down. What remains is Gorg Blau, an artificial reservoir cradled high in the Serra de Tramuntana between the two tallest peaks on the island.

## Between the Two Giants

Gorg Blau sits in the valley dividing Puig Major and Puig de Massanella — Mallorca's highest summit at 1,445 meters and its second at 1,364. This is the wildest, highest country on the island, all limestone ridges and deep ravines, and it forms part of the Serra de Tramuntana World Heritage Site. The creek that feeds and drains the reservoir carries two names, the Torrent del Gorg Blau and Sa Fosca, and it flows on to become a tributary of the Torrent de Pareis, the island's most famous gorge. In 2003, the Balearic Islands Government declared the watercourse a natural monument, formally protecting a landscape that had already been celebrated for more than a century.

## A Valley Turned to Water

The transformation came in stages. A small hydroelectric plant was built in 1906 in the nearby mountain district known as Cals Reis, but the dam that actually created today's reservoir came decades later, completed in 1971. When the reservoir rose, the old bridge on the Sóller road was dismantled and the route rerouted through a tunnel bored into the rock. Since 1971, the reservoir has served a purely practical purpose: together with the neighboring Cúber reservoir, it supplies drinking water to Palma and the surrounding towns. It is a strange fate for a place once prized for doing nothing but being beautiful — the mountain water that once inspired poets now runs from taps in the island's capital. The scale of the loss was never really tallied; a working reservoir simply took the valley's place.

## The Beauty That Was Lost

Before the dam, the spot where water, bridge, and cliff faces met was one of the acknowledged wonders of the Tramuntana. Its fame was not the loud kind. What drew people was a particular quality of stillness — the dark pool beneath the crags, the rush of the torrent, the play of light on stone — and that atmosphere pulled a steady procession of poets, photographers, and painters up the mountain road to record it. The valley's isolation only sharpened the effect, since reaching it meant a slow climb into the heart of the range, far from any town. Much of what we know of the original Gorg Blau now survives only in their work, made in the years before the waters closed over the valley floor.

## Quietude and Sadness

The art that Gorg Blau inspired has outlasted the place itself. The Romantic poet Pablo Piferrer, among the first to write of its beauty, found in its waters a mood of 'quietude and sadness.' The Mallorcan poet Miquel Costa i Llobera was moved to compose a poem simply titled El Gorg Blau. And the painter Joaquim Mir turned the scene into a luminous stained-glass triptych, El Gorg Blau, made around 1911 and now held in the National Art Museum of Catalonia in Barcelona. Stand at the reservoir's edge today, the mountains doubling in the still water, and you can still catch the quietude those artists chased — even if the bridge they painted lies somewhere below the surface.

## Water for a Thirsty Island

For all its beauty, Gorg Blau now does essential, unglamorous work. Cradled among the highest peaks, the reservoir catches the rain that the Serra de Tramuntana wrings from passing weather, and since 1971 it has helped slake the thirst of Palma and the towns around it. Together with the neighboring Cúber reservoir, it forms the core of the island's mountain water supply — a system that turns winter storms high in the range into drinking water far below. There is a quiet irony in the arrangement. The same isolation and grandeur that once drew poets to the valley now make it an ideal place to gather and hold the island's most basic resource.

## Flight Context

Gorg Blau lies at 39.8025°N, 2.8183°E, high in the Serra de Tramuntana between Mallorca's two greatest peaks. From the air it and the adjacent Cúber reservoir appear as a pair of deep-blue lakes ringed by grey limestone — an unmistakable landmark in an otherwise wild expanse of ridges. The clearest reference point is Puig Major just to the north, crowned by the white radar domes of its military installation and, at 1,445 m, the highest ground on the island. Keep a generous altitude, at least 6,000–7,000 ft, and expect strong mountain turbulence and fast-changing cloud. Palma (LEPA) is about 30 km to the south; Menorca (LEMH) lies far to the east.
