# Church of St. Jaume

Alcúdia's parish church, whose gilded baroque chapel was built to honor a Holy Christ said to have wept blood during a 1507 drought.

**Location:** 39.8515, 3.1205

**Categories:** Churches, Religious Sites, Baroque Architecture, History, Alcúdia, Mallorca

## Introduction

The great baroque altarpiece inside the Chapel of the Holy Christ was carved by someone who could not abide an empty surface. Every plane of it writhes with gilded ornament — the horror vacui, or 'dread of the void,' that drove Spanish baroque sculptors to fill each gap with another curl of gold. It is the show-stopping heart of the Church of St. Jaume, a Roman Catholic parish that has grown, chapel by chapel, at the edge of Alcúdia's walled old town for more than seven centuries. From the outside it looks solid and severe; step inside, and it glitters.

## Seven Centuries in the Making

The church has taken a long time to become what it is. The land for it was granted by King Jaume II of Majorca in 1298, the same year he began raising the walls that still ring Alcúdia's old town. The church sits just inside those medieval gates, so most visitors arrive at it on foot, through streets closed to cars. What stands today is largely a later reworking: the main body is neo-Gothic, and a major reconstruction of part of the building was completed in 1893. The result reads like a timeline in stone, medieval foundations carrying centuries of additions and repairs. Just beside it sits the Museu Monogràfic, where finds from the neighboring Roman city of Pollentia are displayed — a reminder that this corner of Alcúdia has drawn people together since long before the first church rose.

## A Chapel Born of Drought

The church's most remarkable space grew out of a moment of desperation. In 1507, during a drought, an image of Christ in the cave of Sant Martí on the city's outskirts was said to have sweated water and blood — a sign, the faithful believed, sent to relieve their parched fields. Devotion to this 'Holy Christ' spread through Alcúdia and the surrounding villages, and in time the town resolved to honor it with a chapel worthy of the miracle. The first stone was laid on 8 December 1675, and the work took more than two decades, finishing in 1697. Beneath a central dome, four side chapels open off the main space, and the whole was designed to draw the eye upward toward the drama overhead.

## The Gilded Altarpiece

Then came the altarpiece. Between 1699 and 1703, the sculptor Mateu Joan i Serra filled the chapel's focal wall with a spectacular work of baroque art — a composition so densely worked that art historians reach for the Latin phrase horror vacui, the fear of leaving any surface bare. Gilded figures, twisting columns, and ornament crowd every plane, catching and scattering the light that falls from the dome above. Three centuries later, in 2007, the altarpiece was carefully restored to mark the five-hundredth anniversary of the Holy Christ legend that had inspired the chapel in the first place. For many visitors, it is the single reason to step through the door.

## Saint James at the Center

The parish's main altarpiece tells a quieter story of two hands at work. It was built in stages: the stonemasonry is the work of Llorenç Ferrer i Martí, while the rest was completed by Miquel Arcas. At its center stands an image of Saint James — Sant Jaume in Catalan — the patron saint of both the church and the town of Alcúdia. That patronage still shapes the local calendar; each July, Alcúdia gives nine days to celebrating him. For a building that began as a modest parish and grew, chapel by chapel, into something far grander, it feels right that its heart still belongs to the saint whose name it has carried from the start.

## A Devotion Still Alive

The Holy Christ that inspired the chapel is not a museum piece; the devotion born in that 1507 drought still shapes Alcúdia today. Every three years the town holds the Triennial of the Holy Christ, when residents walk barefoot and in silence through the streets in a procession that traces its origin to the very miracle the chapel was built to honor. The next falls in 2028. To step into the Church of St. Jaume, then, is not simply to admire old stone and gilded wood. It is to stand at the source of a living tradition — a parish still gathering around the same story it has told for more than five hundred years.

## Flight Context

The Church of St. Jaume stands at 39.8515°N, 3.1205°E, just inside the walls of Alcúdia's old town on Mallorca's northeastern headland. From the air the individual church is hard to pick out, so navigate to the compact walled quarter between the Bay of Alcúdia and the Bay of Pollença; the Roman ruins of Pollentia lie immediately to its south. A viewing altitude of 2,500–4,000 ft frames the old town against the beach and marina. Palma (LEPA) is about 55 km to the southwest and Menorca (LEMH) roughly 40 km east across the water. Best light and calmest air come in the early morning.
