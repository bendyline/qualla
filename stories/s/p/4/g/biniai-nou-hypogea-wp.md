# Biniai Nou hypogea

Near Mao lie two rock-cut tombs holding the oldest known human remains on Menorca, and the story of the first farmers who reached the island by boat with their herds aboard.

**Location:** 39.9058, 4.2123

**Categories:** Archaeology, Prehistory, Megalithic Monuments, History, Menorca, Balearic Islands

## Introduction

The history of human Menorca begins, as far as anyone can prove, with a single bone. Near Maó, in a place called Biniai Nou, two funerary hypogea were cut into the rock in the Chalcolithic period, and from one of them archaeologists lifted a human bone that radiocarbon dating placed between 2290 and 2030 BC. It is the oldest firm date in the island's prehistory, the earliest hard evidence that people lived and died here. Everything about Menorca's long human story, every taula and talayot to come, traces back past this quiet spot in the fields.

## The Oldest Date on the Island

For an island so thick with prehistoric monuments, Biniai Nou holds a special place: it anchors the calendar. A team sponsored by the Museum of Menorca excavated the two tombs and, crucially, sent a human bone for carbon dating, which returned a result of roughly 2200 BC. That single measurement gave the island its oldest secure date. Archaeologists are careful about what this means. The bone proves people were here by then, but it does not prove they were the very first, since earlier settlers could have come and left fainter traces. What it fixes is a floor beneath the whole story, a point before which we simply cannot yet see.

## They Came by Boat, With Their Herds

The people who made Menorca a permanent home did not arrive empty-handed. The evidence suggests the first lasting settlers were already farmers and herders, and they crossed the sea carrying livestock in their boats, goats and sheep and pigs and cows that they then bred on the island. Imagine the nerve of it: loading animals into wooden craft and setting out across open Mediterranean water toward a coastline you could barely see. The tombs preserved fragments of these people's material world too, ceramic vessels, awls, and pieces of radiolarite, a hard rock they worked into tools. Farmers, herders, and seafarers all at once, they brought a whole way of life ashore.

## Two Tombs Cut in Rock

Biniai Nou's tombs share a distinctive design. Each has its burial chamber hollowed directly into the bedrock, while the entrance corridor was built up from vertical stone slabs into a megalithic façade, a hybrid of carving and construction. Hypogeum 1 presents a slightly concave front with a central entrance leading into a corridor roofed by a doubled-slab system, opening onto an oval chamber with a stone bench along the left side. Fifty meters away, a curious artificial depression known locally as a capada de moro was cut into the rock, one of those unexplained marks the ancient landscape keeps offering up. The whole tradition belongs to a family of collective tombs, from fully built dolmens like Ses Roques Llises to rock-cut chambers like these.

## Eighty-One, and a Roof That Fell

The second tomb tells a more human story. Hypogeum 2 has a concave façade and a central entrance opening into a short corridor and a circular chamber, and excavation revealed something remarkable inside: 81 individuals, laid to rest here in primary burials. These were not stray bones but a community's dead, generation upon generation returned to the same chamber. Then the roof collapsed while the tomb was still in full use, sealing the circular room and its people in a single moment of ruin. Archaeologists later restored the damaged structure. It is easy to walk past a low stone entrance in a field, but 81 lives ended and were mourned here, in a chamber their descendants kept returning to until the day the sky fell in.

## Flight Context

Biniai Nou lies at 39.9058 N, 4.2123 E in the countryside near Maó, at the eastern end of Menorca. The tombs are small ground-level features, so navigate by the coast: the great inlet of Maó harbour lies a short distance northeast, an unmistakable landmark from 2,500 to 4,000 feet. Menorca airport (LEMH) is very close, just to the south and west of the site, so expect controlled airspace and traffic on approach and departure. Palma de Mallorca (LEPA) lies west across open sea. Clear conditions between the island's frequent tramuntana winds give the best low-level views of this gently rolling eastern farmland.
