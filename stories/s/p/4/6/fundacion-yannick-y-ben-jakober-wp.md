# Fundacion Yannick y Ben Jakober

On a hillside above Alcudia, an Egyptian architect's whitewashed house guards a cistern full of watching children painted centuries ago.

**Location:** 39.8430, 3.1785

**Categories:** Art, Museums, Cultural Heritage, Architecture, Mallorca, Alcudia

## Introduction

Step down into what was once a water reservoir on the Alcudia peninsula and dozens of children are waiting for you, their faces lit against the dark. They are not real, of course. They are Old Master portraits, painted between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries, and someone had the strange and lovely idea to hang them where cool water used to be stored. This is the heart of the Fundación Yannick y Ben Jakober, a private cultural foundation on Mallorca that turns an old cistern into a gallery and an Egyptian architect's villa into a museum. Few places on the island feel less like a museum and more like a secret.

## The Children in the Reservoir

The foundation is best known for this collection: formal portraits of children spanning some three centuries of European painting, gathered and recognized as protected Historical Heritage. In their own era, children of noble families were often painted stiff and solemn, dressed as miniature adults in lace and heavy fabric, less like the children they were than the dynasties they represented. Seen together in the hushed, converted reservoir, the effect is quietly moving. Behind the finery are real sons and daughters, many of whom did not live to grow up, their likenesses now the only trace they left. The old water tank, conditioned for the purpose, gives them a stillness that a bright gallery wall never could.

## An Egyptian Architect's House

The museum building itself is a work of art with a surprising signature. Its main structure, the Sa Bassa Blanca Museum, was designed in 1978 by Hassan Fathy, the celebrated Egyptian architect who spent his career reviving traditional earthen and vernacular building and arguing that architecture belonged to ordinary people, not only the wealthy. On a Mallorcan hillside his sensibility reads as arches, thick whitewashed walls, and shaded courtyards that hold the Mediterranean heat at bay. The Heritage Commission of the Government of Mallorca recognized the achievement in 2011, declaring the building a Listed Landmark. It is a rare thing on the island: a single house that connects Cairo and the Balearics in the same silhouette.

## Why a Foundation Exists

Behind the art and the architecture is a mission. Established in 1993, the Fundación Yannick y Ben Jakober is a non-profit institution devoted primarily to preserving and restoring Spanish historical heritage, with the Balearic Islands as its home ground. It does not simply hoard beautiful objects. The foundation promotes the fine arts, especially painting and sculpture, and it runs exhibitions, educational programs, and artistic exchanges that keep the place alive rather than embalmed. A foundation is a promise across time, a legal vessel built so that private passion can outlast the people who felt it, and this one turns one couple's collecting into something the public can walk through.

## A Quiet Corner of Alcudia

The setting matters as much as the collection. The foundation sits on the peninsula northeast of Alcudia, where Mallorca narrows into headlands between the great bays of Alcudia and Pollença, far from the beach resorts that define this coast in summer. Alcudia is one of the island's oldest towns, its Roman and medieval bones still visible in the ring of walls around its old center. Against that deep backdrop, a museum that treasures the faces of long-dead children and shelters them inside an Egyptian architect's vision feels entirely at home. This is the other Mallorca, the one that rewards travelers who climb away from the sand and go looking.

## Flight Context

The foundation lies at 39.843 N, 3.1785 E on the peninsula northeast of Alcudia, on Mallorca's north coast. Recommended viewing altitude is 2,500 to 4,500 feet to take in both the sheltered Bay of Alcudia and the Bay of Pollença that flank the headland. Look for the walled old town of Alcudia inland and the wooded ridges of the Cap des Pinar peninsula reaching out to sea. Palma de Mallorca airport (LEPA) is about 55 km to the southwest; Menorca airport (LEMH) lies across open water to the east. Visibility is typically excellent in spring and autumn, with summer bringing coastal haze.
