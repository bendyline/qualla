# Joan Gamper

A Swiss athlete turned away from one Barcelona club founded his own on the opposite principle, giving FC Barcelona its identity as a club open to everyone.

**Location:** 41.3553, 2.1551

**Categories:** History, Sports, FC Barcelona, Biography, Association football

## Introduction

He was a foreigner, and the first door he knocked on shut in his face. When Hans Gamper arrived in Barcelona from Switzerland at the end of the 1890s, he asked to join a group of local football enthusiasts and was told the club wanted no outsiders. So he built his own, and he built it on the opposite idea: open to anyone, Swiss or British or Catalan, whoever wanted to play. On 29 November 1899 a small meeting at a city gymnasium gave that idea a name, Futbol Club Barcelona. Gamper, known in Catalan as Joan, became its first captain, its most prolific early scorer, and the president who would rescue it five separate times.

## The Athlete from Winterthur

Hans Max Gamper was born in Winterthur, Switzerland, on 22 November 1877, the eldest son among five children. His mother died of tuberculosis when he was eight, and the family moved to Zurich, where he later trained as a tradesman in the silk business. Football was only one of his passions. He was a serious cyclist and runner who held Swiss records over 800 and 1,600 metres in 1898, and he organized an international athletics meeting in Zurich that grew into the celebrated Weltklasse Zürich. He also played rugby, tennis, and golf. He helped found FC Zürich and captained it; his first club, Excelsior Zürich, played in the same red and blue that his Barcelona club would later adopt.

## A Club Open to Everyone

Rejected by a club that wanted no foreigners, Gamper set out to create something deliberately inclusive: an organization open to all, regardless of origin, run democratically by its own members. The founders who answered his call were a mix of Swiss, British, and Spanish enthusiasts. He did not merely administer the new club, he starred for it, scoring Barça's very first goal and piling up more than a hundred goals in just fifty-five matches. On 23 March 1902 he struck a hat-trick in a 15-0 rout to help win the Copa Macaya, the club's first trophy. The following year, in a single match, he scored nine goals in a 13-0 victory.

## Five Times President

By 1908 the club Gamper founded was on the verge of collapse, its best players retired and its finances failing. He stepped in as president, the first of five separate terms that spanned 1908 to 1925 and totalled roughly nine years at the helm. One early triumph mattered more than any trophy: in 1909 he secured the funds to build the club's own ground, the Camp de la Indústria, the stadium widely credited with letting Barça grow through the 1910s. He recruited the star forward Paulino Alcántara, appointed the manager Jack Greenwell in 1917, and presided over the club's first golden age, a haul of Catalan championships, Copa del Rey titles, and Pyrenees Cups.

## More Than a Club

On 14 June 1925 the politics that surrounded the club caught up with its founder. Barça supporters jeered the Spanish national anthem and cheered God Save the King, played by a visiting British Royal Marine band, and the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera accused Gamper of promoting Catalan nationalism. There was truth in the charge, though not as an accusation. Grateful to the region that had welcomed a young foreigner, Gamper had woven Catalan identity into the club so deeply that it became inseparable from what Barcelona means. The motto that Barça still lives by, that it is more than a club, traces directly back to his vision of football as a means of belonging.

## Barcelona Doesn't Love Me

The end was quiet and sad. The Wall Street Crash of 1929 wiped out Gamper's fortune, and, pushed away from the club he had built and worn down by depression, he confided to friends that Barcelona did not love him. He took his own life at his home on 30 July 1930, at the age of fifty-two. The city answered in a way he had not lived to see. His funeral drew an enormous crowd, and mourners carried his coffin to the cemetery on Montjuïc. In 1966 the club named a trophy in his honour, and the Joan Gamper Trophy still opens Barça's season each year, a yearly reminder of the man who gave the club its name and its soul.

## Flight Context

Gamper is buried at the Montjuïc Cemetery, around 41.355 N, 2.155 E, on the seaward slope of the Montjuïc hill south of central Barcelona. The hill itself, crowned by its castle and ringed by the terraces of the Olympic ring, is the dominant landmark and easy to identify from the air, with the commercial port spreading below it. A pass at 1,500 to 2,500 feet frames the cemetery against the harbour and the Mediterranean. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies about 5 nm southwest along the coast, Sabadell (LELL) roughly 12 nm north-northwest, and Girona (LEGE) about 48 nm northeast. The exposed hillside stays clear when inland haze builds; sea breezes can pick up over the ridge by afternoon.
