# Barcelona Museum of Contemporary Art

Richard Meier's gleaming white 'pearl' in the old Raval, whose front plaza became one of the most famous skateboarding spots on Earth.

**Location:** 41.3831, 2.1668

**Categories:** Art museums and galleries in Barcelona, Modern art museums in Spain, Richard Meier buildings, El Raval, Skateboarding spots, Art museums established in 1995

## Introduction

Before the doors open each morning, the plaza already belongs to the skateboarders. The clean marble ledges and gentle steps in front of the Barcelona Museum of Contemporary Art draw riders from across the world, and the ring of wheels on stone is the first sound many visitors hear. It is an unlikely soundtrack for a temple of high art, but MACBA has always thrived on that kind of collision. Since opening on 28 November 1995 in the Plaça dels Àngels, deep in the working-class Raval district, the museum has stood as a bright modern intruder among some of the oldest streets in the city.

## The Loudest Plaza in Barcelona

Ask a skateboarder to name the great spots of the world and MACBA's forecourt will be on the list. The smooth pavement, the low ledges, and the broad steps have made the square in front of the museum a pilgrimage site for the sport, one of the most iconic anywhere. It has appeared in skateboarding magazines and been recreated in mobile games, its geometry memorized by riders who have never set foot in Barcelona. The museum has largely made peace with this second life. By day the plaza is a stage for ollies and kickflips; by night the same white walls glow above the traffic of the Raval. Few art institutions are so thoroughly claimed by the street outside their entrance. There is a fitting logic to it, too: a museum devoted to the restless, unofficial art of the present has become the backdrop for one of the most improvised, self-taught cultures a city can host, the two feeding off the same energy without either quite acknowledging the other.

## A Pearl in El Raval

The building is the work of the American architect Richard Meier, and when it was finished local media reached for a single word: the pearl. Bright white and luminous, its southern wall largely glazed to pull daylight deep into the galleries, it sits amid the narrow lanes and aging facades of the Raval like something dropped from another century. Meier faced an unusual challenge. He was designing a home for a collection that did not yet exist, shaping galleries for artworks he could not see. The roughly 35-million-dollar building was also part of a larger civic bet, an attempt to renew a dense old neighborhood by planting a landmark of contemporary culture in its midst. The gamble drew both praise and unease: some saw a beacon lifting a long-neglected district, others a gleaming intruder that had little to do with the lives lived around it. Three decades on, the museum and the Raval have grown into an uneasy but genuine coexistence.

## A Museum Before Its Art

MACBA was conceived in the ambitious years around the 1992 Olympics, though it did not open its doors until 1995, and its early life carried a real awkwardness: a museum built before it owned much to show. Critics noted the strangeness of raising a grand contemporary art institution with almost no collection to fill it. Time answered them. What began with around 1,100 works has grown to some 5,000, concentrated on Catalan and Spanish art made after 1945, with a scattering of international voices alongside. The building's emptiness at birth turned out to be an invitation. Decade by decade, donations and acquisitions filled Meier's white rooms with the difficult, restless art the architecture had been waiting for.

## Art and Its Arguments

A museum of contemporary art is bound to make people uncomfortable, and MACBA has weathered its share of storms. In 2015 its director resigned amid a censorship controversy over a provocative installation by the Austrian artist Ines Doujak. After Catalonia's 2017 independence referendum, one collector, fearing political instability, withdrew a major long-term loan and repatriated the works to France. Yet the institution has kept evolving. Across the square, a medieval chapel in the old Convent dels Àngels serves as a second space, the Capella MACBA, for performances and installations, its entry free to all. In 2021 Elvira Dyangani Ose became director, the first Black woman to lead the museum, opening a new chapter for the pearl of the Raval.

## Flight Context

MACBA sits at 41.3831°N, 2.1668°E, in the Raval just west of La Rambla within Barcelona's old town. From altitude the white, sharp-edged building stands out against the dark, irregular rooftops of the surrounding medieval district, near the adjoining CCCB cultural center and only blocks from the harbor. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies southwest down the coast, Sabadell (LELL) inland to the north, and Girona (LEGE) up the coast to the northeast. Look for the contrast between the tight tangle of the Ciutat Vella and the straight blocks of the Eixample beginning just to the north, with the port and the Mediterranean framing the view to the south.
