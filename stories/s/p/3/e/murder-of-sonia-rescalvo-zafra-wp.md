# Murder of Sonia Rescalvo Zafra

A bandstand in Barcelona's Parc de la Ciutadella now carries the name of a trans woman whose killing forced Spain to confront the violence it had long ignored.

**Location:** 41.3892, 2.1861

**Categories:** history, human rights, barcelona, memorials, lgbtq history, social history

## Introduction

In the heart of Parc de la Ciutadella stands a small ornamental bandstand, the kind of tidy nineteenth-century pavilion that hundreds of visitors pass without a second glance. Since 2013 it has carried a name: the Glorieta de la Transsexual Sònia. It marks the spot where, in the early hours of 6 October 1991, Sonia Rescalvo Zafra was beaten to death for being a trans woman, the first killing of its kind known in Spain. The square that grew up around her memory is an insistence that she not be forgotten, and that what happened to her never be dismissed as anything other than what it was.

## Lights on the Paral·lel

Sonia Rescalvo Zafra was born in Cuenca in 1946, to Leopoldo Rescalvo and Dolores Zafra. At sixteen she left for Barcelona and found work as a showgirl in the theatres along the Avinguda del Paral·lel, the city's great avenue of music halls and cabarets, where variety shows drew crowds late into the night. For a young trans woman in mid-century Spain, still living under a regime hostile to any life like hers, the stage offered something rare: a place to be seen and applauded on her own terms. Those were the bright years, and they deserve to be remembered alongside what came after.

## Hard Years

Life narrowed as she grew older. Sonia fell on hard times, lost her housing, struggled with addiction, and turned to sex work to survive. It is a familiar and unjust trajectory for people pushed to the margins, denied steady work and stable support, and it should be read not as a moral tale but as a measure of how little her society offered her. By 1991 she was sleeping rough, sharing the shelter of the park's bandstand with a friend named Doris Romero. She was, in every respect that matters, an ordinary person trying to get through the night.

## The Bandstand

In the dark hours of 6 October 1991, six teenage skinheads set upon the two women as they slept, striking them with a broomstick and kicking them in the head with steel-toed boots. Sonia was killed. Doris was gravely injured and left unconscious. The attackers turned on three other people sleeping nearby; one of them, Miguel Pérez Barreiros, was left completely blind yet still managed to reach the police. The violence was so severe that early reports misidentified Sonia entirely, and in much of the press she was referred to in the masculine, a last indignity laid on a woman who could no longer correct the record.

## A Reckoning in Court

The killing did not go unpunished. In June 1994 six of the attackers were convicted for their part in the assault and given long prison terms, ranging from more than twenty to fifty years, while a seventh was penalized for having known of the crime and failed to report it. Spain's Supreme Court reduced the sentences in 1996, and all six have since been released. Beyond the courtroom, the case carried a heavier weight of meaning. It stands as the first documented instance in Spain of a person murdered for being transgender, a grim first that made undeniable a violence many had preferred not to name.

## Her Name on the Square

Memory took root slowly, then held fast. A plaque went up on the bandstand in 1993, and in 2013 the structure was officially renamed in Sonia's honour. For the thirtieth anniversary in 2021, the city went further, entering a 400-square-metre Plaça de Sonia Rescalvo Zafra into its official register of place names, while other towns such as Molins de Rei dedicated spaces of their own. Each year the city council and local LGBTQ organizations gather at the glorieta to remember her. Sonia Rescalvo Zafra's murder helped galvanize Spain's trans rights movement, and the quiet pavilion where she died has become a place where a whole community refuses to let her be erased. What began as an anonymous death in a public park has grown, year by year, into an act of collective memory, and a promise that the next Sonia will not be so easily forgotten.

## Flight Context

Parc de la Ciutadella lies at 41.389°N, 2.186°E, just inland of Barcelona's Port Vell and northeast of the Gothic Quarter, a rectangle of dense greenery between the old town and the Vila Olímpica. From 2,000-3,500 ft the park reads as a clear green block beside the marina, with the sail-shaped W Hotel and the beaches to the south. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) is about 8 nm southwest along the coast; Sabadell (LELL) sits inland to the northwest; Girona (LEGE) roughly 50 nm northeast. Coastal light is usually good; the park and adjacent harbour make easy visual references.
