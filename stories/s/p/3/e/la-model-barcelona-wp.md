# La Model (Barcelona)

Built as a philosopher's ideal of the reforming prison, Barcelona's La Model spent 113 years holding the city's men, including the last political prisoner executed by garrote in Spain, before becoming a place of memory.

**Location:** 41.3828, 2.1442

**Categories:** History, Prisons, Architecture, History of Barcelona, Spanish Civil War

## Introduction

The building was designed to be watched. In 1787 the philosopher Jeremy Bentham imagined a prison shaped like a wheel, cell wings radiating from a central hub so a single guard could see everyone at once, and inmates, never sure when they were observed, would come to behave as if they always were. Barcelona built its version of that idea between 1881 and 1904 and named it the Model, because it was meant to be the template for a rational, reforming age of imprisonment. For the next 113 years it held the city's men. Much of what happened inside it was anything but rational.

## A Philosopher's Blueprint

The design followed the panopticon principle directly, its galleries fanning out from a central point where a watchman could survey every wing. Josep Domenech i Estapa oversaw the construction, and when the prison opened on 9 June 1904 its official name was the Cellular Prison. The nickname came from its purpose: it was to be a model, a demonstration of the penitentiary reform of the era, and over time the informal name became the official one. The ambition was humane on paper. A well-lit, orderly, individually celled institution was supposed to correct rather than merely punish. The century that followed would test that promise against war, dictatorship, and execution.

## Emptied Twice by War

Across its long history the prison stood empty on only two occasions, both at the edges of the Spanish Civil War. The first came on 19 July 1936, the day of the failed military coup in Barcelona, when anarchists threw open the gates and released all 851 prisoners. The second came at the war's bitter end. On 23 January 1939, military intelligence marched hundreds of inmates toward the French border, where some were executed and others set free. Three days later, in the final hours before Franco's troops entered the city, the remaining prisoners were all liberated at once, a strange mix of captured Nationalists alongside militants of the anarchist CNT and the POUM, released together as the front collapsed.

## Between Ideal and Reality

The reforming promise of the Model kept colliding with the country's politics. A prison conceived as a rational, improving institution spent long stretches of its 113 years serving as an instrument of repression, most harshly under Franco's dictatorship, when its cells filled with political prisoners. Generations of Barcelona men passed through it, the guilty and the merely inconvenient, common criminals beside dissidents and strikers. Overcrowding turned the individual cells the design had prized into shared and airless spaces. The building never quite escaped the contradiction at its heart: an architecture meant to correct human beings, repeatedly turned to the older, simpler work of controlling and punishing them. To understand the prison is to hold both truths at once.

## The Last Garrote

One name has come to haunt La Model above all others. Salvador Puig Antich, a young anti-Franco militant, was held here and, on 2 March 1974, executed inside the prison by garrote, the iron collar tightened until it killed him. He was twenty-five. He was the last political prisoner put to death by that method in Spain, and his killing turned him into a lasting symbol of resistance to the dictatorship, mourned by Catalans and anarchists for decades after. His cell is preserved, as is that of Lluis Companys, the president of Catalonia once imprisoned here. In 2025 the Spanish state formally declared Puig Antich's trial illegitimate and annulled his death sentence, half a century too late for the man himself.

## From Cellblock to Memorial

The prison stayed in use for decades more, and it kept making headlines: a 1977 revolt later dramatized in the 2022 film Prison 77, and a 1984 riot during which a notorious inmate escaped. It finally closed for good on 8 June 2017, and the city chose not to demolish it. Instead La Model became a center of memory, open to the public for visits guided and unguided. There is a quiet reversal in that. A building conceived so that a hidden watcher could observe the confined is now a place where anyone may walk freely through the galleries, look into the cells, and remember the people, guilty and innocent, political and ordinary, who were held behind its walls.

## Flight Context

La Model occupies a full city block at 41.383 N, 2.144 E in the Eixample district of western Barcelona, its distinctive star-shaped, spoked footprint recognizable from directly overhead, unlike almost anything else in the surrounding grid. A pass at 1,500 to 2,500 feet frames it within the regular diamond-chamfered blocks of the Eixample, with the green rise of Montjuic to the south and the sea beyond. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies about 6 nm southwest, Sabadell (LELL) roughly 9 nm north-northwest, and Girona (LEGE) about 46 nm northeast. Clear mornings give the best light for picking out the radial cellblocks from the air; afternoon sea haze tends to soften the detail.
