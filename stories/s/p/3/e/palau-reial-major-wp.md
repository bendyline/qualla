# Palau Reial Major

The royal palace of medieval Barcelona, whose great Gothic hall is where, by tradition, the Catholic Monarchs received Columbus home from the New World.

**Location:** 41.3842, 2.1776

**Categories:** history, architecture, barcelona, landmarks, medieval history, gothic architecture

## Introduction

A broad fan of stone steps climbs from the Plaça del Rei toward a plain, high wall, and by long tradition it was here, in the spring of 1493, that Christopher Columbus knelt before Ferdinand and Isabella to report what he had found across the ocean. The hall behind that wall is the Saló del Tinell, the throne room of the Palau Reial Major, the royal palace of medieval Barcelona. Few squares in Europe pack so much history into so little space, and fewer still let you stand exactly where a new world was first announced to an old one.

## The Fan of Stone Steps

The Palau Reial Major is not one building but a knot of them, gathered around the Plaça del Rei in the Gothic Quarter. For centuries it served as the residence of the Counts of Barcelona and, later, the kings of the Crown of Aragon, the rulers whose fleets and treaties carried Catalan influence across the Mediterranean. The complex grew by accretion, each monarch adding a hall, a chapel, or a tower, until the square became a kind of stone diary of Catalan power. To cross it today is to walk through five hundred years of royal ambition compressed into a few hundred paces.

## Where a New World Was Announced

The Saló del Tinell was raised between 1359 and 1362 under King Peter IV, to the design of the architect Guillem Carbonell, and it remains one of the supreme achievements of Catalan Gothic. Six great semicircular arches leap across the room and carry the roof without a single interior wall, an audacious span for its age. Beneath the floor lies the palace's deeper past: the hall's foundations rest on 11th-century vaults, and those in turn stand over a monumental structure dating to the Visigoth era. It was into this soaring, austere chamber that the returning Columbus is said to have been received, the plainness of the stone only sharpening the drama of the moment.

## The Chapel Within Roman Walls

Beside the great hall stands the Palatine Chapel of St. Agatha, built in 1302 under King James II and designed by the architect Bertran Riquer as the royal family's private oratory. A slender octagonal tower rises from one flank, and inside, a single aisle runs to a polygonal apse beneath a timber roof. The chapel's sacristy was tucked directly into the ancient Roman walls that still gird this corner of the city, medieval devotion built against imperial defence. Its greatest treasure came later: around 1463, King Peter V commissioned the painter Jaume Huguet to create the altarpiece of the Epiphany, an outstanding work of Gothic painting that still presides over the altar.

## The Viceroy's Wing

As the centre of power drifted away from Barcelona, the palace kept evolving. Between 1549 and 1557, the architect Antoni Carbonell built the Palau del Lloctinent in a graceful late Gothic-Renaissance style, intended as the residence of the Viceroy, or Lloctinent, who governed Catalonia in the monarch's absence. From the same period rises the so-called Mirador of King Martin, a five-storey tower on a rectangular plan completed in 1555, its tiers of galleries climbing above the surrounding roofs. Together these additions marked the palace's shift from a working royal seat to an instrument of administration.

## Inquisition and Afterlife

After the 16th century the complex ceased to be a royal home, and its rooms were divided between the royal administration and the Inquisition, a colder chapter for halls that had once hosted coronations and audiences. A doorway from this era, topped by a triangular tympanum and once leading to the Royal Audience Hall, survives today at the entrance to the nearby Museu Frederic Marès. The rest of the palace endures as part of the Barcelona City History Museum, the MUHBA, where visitors can descend beneath the Plaça del Rei into the excavated streets of Roman Barcino, the city on which every later Barcelona was built. Standing in the Saló del Tinell today, beneath those leaping arches, it is easy to feel the compression of it all, Roman, Visigothic, medieval, and modern, stacked one atop another in a few metres of Catalan stone.

## Flight Context

The Palau Reial Major stands at 41.384°N, 2.178°E in the Barri Gòtic, a short block inland from the Barcelona Cathedral and roughly midway between La Rambla and the Via Laietana. It is an old-town landmark best seen below 2,500 ft or on approach, its medieval towers, including the tall Mirador of King Martin, rising above the dense grid of the Gothic Quarter near the cathedral's spires. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies about 7 nm southwest along the coast; Sabadell (LELL) is inland to the northwest; Girona (LEGE) roughly 50 nm northeast. The cathedral and Port Vell make handy visual anchors.
