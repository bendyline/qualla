# Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya

Inside a palace on Montjuïc, Barcelona keeps a thousand years of Catalan art, including Romanesque frescoes lifted whole from mountain churches.

**Location:** 41.3683, 2.1533

**Categories:** museums, art, culture, barcelona, history, landmarks

## Introduction

Step into one of the museum's dim, apse-shaped halls and a wide-eyed Christ looks straight back at you, one hand raised in benediction, painted on a curved wall that was never meant to leave a stone church in the high Pyrenees. This is the Pantocrator of Sant Climent de Taüll, a masterpiece of the 12th century, and its presence here in Barcelona is the result of one of the boldest rescue operations in the history of art. The Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya, MNAC for short, is where an entire region gathered up its past and carried it down the mountain to keep it safe.

## The Palace on the Hill

MNAC occupies the Palau Nacional, a vast Italian-style building raised on Montjuïc for the 1929 International Exposition, standing at the head of the Avinguda de la Reina Maria Cristina near Plaça Espanya. Its cascade of terraces and its great dome make it one of the most recognizable silhouettes in the city. The Palau has housed Catalonia's art collections since 1934, though the road to a single national museum was long. Declared a national museum only in 1990, it was overhauled to plans by architects Gae Aulenti and Enric Steegmann; the grand Oval Hall reopened for the 1992 Olympics, and the collections were installed gallery by gallery until the museum was formally inaugurated on 16 December 2004. Today it ranks among the largest museums in Spain.

## Christ Comes Down from the Mountains

The heart of MNAC is its Romanesque collection, considered the finest of its kind anywhere. It exists because of a scare. In 1919 an American art dealer bought the frescoes from a former monastery at Castell de Mur, intending to sell them abroad for profit, and the shock of watching Catalan heritage walk out the door set off a campaign to save the rest. Through the early 1920s, murals were carefully detached from remote Pyrenean churches and reassembled in Barcelona. The rooms unfold in chronological order across the 11th, 12th, and 13th centuries, from the Carolingian-tinged paintings of Sant Joan de Boí to the expressive apse of Sant Climent de Taüll with its commanding Christ in Majesty, tangible proof of the creative power of medieval Catalonia.

## Kingdoms in Gold

Beyond the Romanesque lies a Gothic collection that maps the reach of the Crown of Aragon, gathering works from Catalonia, Aragon, Valencia, and Majorca. Here the story is one of styles colliding and merging: French models giving way to the Italian manner of Giotto, then to the luminous realism that arrived with the influence of Jan van Eyck. The Valencian painter Lluís Dalmau produced his acclaimed Virgin of the Consellers in 1445, its faces rendered with Flemish precision, while the great Catalan master Jaume Huguet set up his dominant workshop in Barcelona. The mural paintings of the Conquest of Majorca preside over the first Gothic room, secular history rendered in the same gold-ground grandeur usually reserved for saints.

## Masters and Benefactors

Unlike Europe's royal collections, MNAC's Renaissance and Baroque holdings were built largely from Catalan soil and then enriched by private generosity. The galleries hold El Greco's fevered religious scenes, a St Paul by Velázquez, Zurbarán's serene Immaculate Conception, and Ribera's harrowing Martyrdom of Saint Bartholomew. Two great gifts transformed the museum's scope. The Cambó Bequest, assembled by the Catalan politician and patron Francesc Cambó, brought works by Titian, Rubens, Cranach, Fragonard, and Goya. Later, at the 2004 opening, the Thyssen-Bornemisza collection added a further wealth of Catalan and European painting, turning a regional museum into a window on the whole sweep of European art.

## Modernisme and the Modern Eye

The journey ends where Barcelona's own golden age begins. MNAC's modern collection celebrates modernisme and noucentisme, the movements that reshaped the city around 1900. Ramon Casas appears riding a tandem with his café partner Pere Romeu, Santiago Rusiñol and Isidre Nonell capture the light and melancholy of the age, and Marià Fortuny's Wounded Picador from 1867 anticipates it all. The story carries forward through Pablo Picasso, represented since 2007, the pioneering sculptures of Pablo Gargallo and Julio González, and the postwar abstraction of Antoni Tàpies. Woven through it, too, is Agustí Centelles's stark photographic record of the Spanish Civil War, the same conflict that once forced these very collections to be evacuated to Olot, Darnius, and Paris for safekeeping.

## Flight Context

The Palau Nacional sits at 41.368°N, 2.153°E on the northern shoulder of Montjuïc, facing Plaça Espanya. From 2,500-4,000 ft it is one of the easiest landmarks in Barcelona: a huge domed palace at the top of a broad staircase and fountain axis, flanked by the twin brick campaniles at Plaça Espanya and the old Las Arenas bullring nearby. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies about 6 nm southwest along the coast; Sabadell (LELL) is inland to the north; Girona (LEGE) roughly 55 nm northeast. Clear coastal days give sharp views of the Montjuïc complex from the west or southwest.
