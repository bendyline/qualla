# Santa Maria del Mar, Barcelona

Barcelona's cathedral of the sea, raised in just fifty-four years by the guilds and dockworkers of the Ribera, is a masterpiece of Catalan Gothic that has survived earthquake, fire, and civil war.

**Location:** 41.3836, 2.1819

**Categories:** Churches, Gothic architecture, Medieval history, Barcelona, Catalonia, Religious sites

## Introduction

The men who built this church carried it on their backs. When work began on Santa Maria del Mar in 1329, the great blocks of stone came from the royal quarry on Montjuic, and it was the bastaixos, the porters of the waterfront, who hauled them through the narrow streets of the Ribera to the building site. They were not nobles or bishops. They were laborers, and this was their church, financed and built by the common people of a mercantile quarter at the height of Catalonia's maritime power. Look at the bronze doors of the main entrance today and you will find them there, the porters, cast in metal, still shouldering their eternal load.

## A Church of the Common People

A place of worship dedicated to Saint Mary by the Sea had stood on this ground since at least 998, but the building visitors see today was begun in the fourteenth century by the canon Bernat Llull, appointed Archdean of Santa Maria in 1324. What set the project apart was its backing. Where great cathedrals usually rose on the wealth of kings and lords, Santa Maria del Mar was driven by the guilds of the Ribera, the merchants, sailors, and craftsmen who made Barcelona a Mediterranean power. The foundation stone was laid on 25 March 1329 by King Alfonso IV of Aragon, an event still marked by a tablet in Latin and Catalan on the facade. Under the architects Berenguer de Montagut, who designed the building, and Ramon Despuig, every guild of the quarter took part, and the church was completed in 1383, an astonishingly short span for a medieval building of its size.

## The Arithmetic of Heaven

Because it was raised in a single sustained campaign of just fifty-four years, Santa Maria del Mar possesses a purity and unity of style rare in large medieval buildings, and it stands as one of the finest examples of Catalan Gothic anywhere. That harmony is no accident. According to the art historian Josep Bracons, the whole church was laid out on a module of the medieval foot, about 33 centimeters. The side chapels are ten feet deep, the side aisles twice that, and the central aisle four times as wide, a full forty feet. Add it all together and the church measures one hundred medieval feet across, a width that exactly equals its maximum height. The result inside is a soaring, luminous space of slender octagonal columns set far apart, drawing the eye up and outward rather than merely upward. It is Gothic reduced to its serene essentials.

## Earthquake, Fire, and Endurance

For all its calm, the building has weathered violence. The Catalonia earthquake of 1428 caused casualties and shattered the great rose window at the west end; a new one in the Flamboyant style was finished by 1459 and glows there still. Far worse came in 1936, when anti-clerical rioters set the church ablaze at the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War. It burned for eleven days. The fire consumed the Baroque altar, the images, and much of the accumulated ornament of centuries, yet the stone skeleton endured, and some who love the church say the flames left its pure Gothic bones cleaner than before. Its two towers tell their own story of patience: the north-west tower was finished in 1496, but its twin was not completed until 1902, more than four hundred years later.

## Saints, Football, and Fame

Rebuilding after the fire dragged on for decades, and money was scarce. The damaged windows were not replaced until the 1960s, and when funds ran short a fundraising campaign was launched. Futbol Club Barcelona was asked to contribute and agreed on one condition: that the club's coat of arms appear on one of the stained glass windows. It is there to this day, the crest of a football team glowing among the saints, a very Barcelona kind of miracle. In the twenty-first century the church found a wider audience through Ildefonso Falcones's 2006 bestseller Cathedral of the Sea, which dramatizes its construction and the porters who made it possible, and which became a Netflix series in 2018. The book gave a global readership what the people of the Ribera always knew: that this is a monument built by ordinary hands, and it belongs to them.

## Flight Context

Santa Maria del Mar stands at 41.384N, 2.182E, in the Ribera district of the Ciutat Vella, close to the old harbor and the waterfront. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet the church's twin west towers and long nave rise above the tight medieval roofline between the Gothic Quarter and the marina, with the modern Port Vell and the beaches of Barceloneta just to the southeast. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) is about 12 km down the coast to the southwest, Sabadell (LELL) sits inland to the northwest, and Girona (LEGE) lies to the northeast. In clear coastal air the church reads best against the open water it was named for, the same sea whose trade paid for every stone.
