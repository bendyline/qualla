# May Days

In May 1937 the Spanish Republic turned on itself in the streets of Barcelona, as anarchists and the POUM fought former allies over a telephone exchange and the soul of a revolution.

**Location:** 41.4019, 2.1714

**Categories:** History, Spanish Civil War, Anarchism, Military history, History of Barcelona

## Introduction

In the middle of a gun battle, someone sent up sandwiches. It was May 1937, and inside Barcelona's central telephone exchange, police loyal to the Republic had seized the lower floors while anarchist workers held the upper ones, each side aiming rifles at an enemy that, less than a year before, had fought shoulder to shoulder against a fascist coup. The police on the first floor passed bocadillos up to the anarchists above them. Then grenades fell from the rooftops and tore apart the police cars in the square below. This was the May Days, the civil war that broke out inside the Spanish Civil War, and it would leave the Republic fighting itself in the streets of its own great stronghold.

## The War Within the War

By the spring of 1937, Barcelona was the beating heart of a social revolution. Since defeating the coup the previous July, the anarchists of the CNT and FAI, together with the dissident Marxists of the POUM, had run much of the city and its industry through workers' committees. The Republican government and its growing communist allies wanted that power back, arguing that the state must be rebuilt and the war won before any revolution. The gulf was captured in a single exchange on 2 May, when a minister telephoned the Catalan government from Valencia and an anarchist operator told him there was no government in Barcelona at all, only a Defense Committee.

## The Telephone Exchange

The spark came on 3 May. A force of two hundred police, led by the Catalan public order minister Eusebio Rodriguez Salas, arrived at the Telefonica exchange on Placa Catalunya intending to wrest the building from the anarchist workers who controlled it. A crowd gathered in the square. Inside, a strange truce held, since the phone lines were vital to the war effort and neither side wanted them cut, and it was during this standoff that the police sent food up to the anarchists on the floors above. But grenades from the rooftops soon blew up police vehicles outside, and word raced through the city that the government had moved against the workers. Barcelona began, once again, to build barricades.

## Barricades Again

By 4 May the city had fallen into an eerie silence broken only by rifle and machine-gun fire. Shops shuttered, streets vanished behind barricades, and anarchist groups attacked Assault Guard barracks and government buildings while government and communist militias fired back. The tragedy was that the leaders often wanted it stopped. The anarchist ministers Joan Garcia Oliver and Federica Montseny went on the radio to plead with their own followers to lay down their weapons and go back to work. On the Aragon front, militia divisions loyal to the anarchists and the POUM prepared to march on Barcelona and were turned back only when the Republican air force threatened to bomb them on the road.

## Murder in the Truce

On 5 May, President Lluis Companys brokered a fragile settlement under which the Catalan government would dissolve and reform. Yet even amid the truce the killing continued. The Italian anarchist intellectuals Camillo Berneri and Francesco Barbieri were arrested by a group of guards and murdered during their detention, two of the conflict's most mourned victims. Over the sixth and seventh the fighting ebbed; Assault Guards disarmed and arrested members of the CNT, FAI, and POUM, and by 8 May the barricades were coming down. How many died is still argued over. Contemporary reports spoke of some five hundred killed, careful later studies count closer to two hundred and twenty, and the highest estimates run past a thousand.

## The Reckoning

The May Days changed the Republic from the inside. They exposed the anarchists as unable to act with one voice, split between ministers focused on winning the war and militants determined to defend the revolution. The government and the communists moved decisively against the left they blamed for the chaos. In June the POUM was outlawed, its leaders arrested, and its most prominent figure, Andreu Nin, seized and killed. The anarchist movement never again wielded the power it had held, four anarchist ministers left the national cabinet, and communist influence surged. A young English volunteer named George Orwell watched it unfold as a POUM militiaman and set it down in Homage to Catalonia. The Republic had wounded itself, and Franco was still coming.

## Flight Context

The May Days centered on Placa Catalunya, the vast square at roughly 41.402 N, 2.171 E where the Telefonica exchange still stands at the junction of the old city and the Eixample. From the air the wide open plaza, the Rambla running south from it toward the harbour, and the regular grid fanning north together mark the ground where the fighting was fiercest. A pass at 1,500 to 2,500 feet frames the square against Montjuic to the southwest and the sea beyond the port. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies about 7 nm southwest, Sabadell (LELL) roughly 10 nm northwest, and Girona (LEGE) about 46 nm northeast. Clear coastal mornings give the sharpest view of the plaza and the streets radiating from it.
