# Tibidabo Amusement Park

One of the world's oldest working amusement parks, perched 500 meters above Barcelona, where a red airplane from 1928 still circles over the city and Walt Disney once tried to buy the automatons.

**Location:** 41.4218, 2.1200

**Categories:** Amusement parks, Barcelona, History, Tourist attractions, Catalonia, Family attractions

## Introduction

In 1957, Walt Disney climbed a mountain above Barcelona and offered to write blank cheques. He wanted the collection of antique automatons at the Tibidabo Amusement Park, a place he had come to love, and he was willing to let the owners name their own price. They said no. That a small park on a Catalan hilltop could turn down the king of theme parks tells you something about Tibidabo. Built in 1899 and opened in 1905, it is among the oldest amusement parks in the world still running and the longest-running in Spain, a working museum of fairground history that happens to sit 500 meters up, with all of Barcelona spread out beneath it.

## A Pharmacist's Mountain

The park was the dream of a pharmacist. In 1899, Salvador Andreu proposed building an amusement park atop Tibidabo, the peak of the Collserola ridge that rises behind Barcelona, complete with a funicular and a tram line to carry visitors up. Work on the Tranvia Azul and the funicular began in June 1900, and both were inaugurated by the city's cardinal, Salvador Casanas y Pages, in October 1901. The first amusement rides did not arrive until 1905, modest things at first, binoculars and mirrors, automaton displays and games. Over the next two decades the park grew bolder, adding rides like the Avio, the Ferrocarril Aereo, and the Talaia, all three of which are still in operation today. From the top level, the main entrance sits 516 meters above the sea, and the whole city falls away below.

## The Red Airplane and the Automatons

The star of Tibidabo is a small red airplane that has been flying in circles since 1928. The Avio is a replica of the aircraft that made the first commercial flight between Madrid and Barcelona in 1927, suspended from a crane and spun around by its own propeller so that riders seem to bank out over the edge of the city. For 87 years it was never once detached from its crane, until a 2017 restoration finally brought it down for new paint and lights before it was ceremoniously sent aloft again. Elsewhere the park keeps its history close. The Embruixabruixes, a suspended monorail, dates to 1915. The Museum of Automats has been part of the park since the beginning, its oldest piece a figure called The Mandolin Clown that dates from 1880, and it was this collection that Disney tried and failed to buy.

## Boom, Bust, and a Fraudster

Tibidabo has not always had an easy century. It prospered into the late 1950s, then began to slip as the little SEAT 600 put cars within reach of ordinary families, who could suddenly drive somewhere new instead of riding the funicular up the mountain. In 1987 the businessman Javier de la Rosa bought the park and poured money into new attractions from Italy and Germany, but his tenure ended in disgrace: he was charged with fraud after looting some 68 million euros from the company, which lost money through the 1990s and filed for bankruptcy in 1999. On the verge of collapse, the park's land was very nearly sold to the candy maker Chupa Chups before the Barcelona City Council stepped in and bought it for 791 million pesetas. Public ownership rescued a landmark that private ambition had almost destroyed.

## New Thrills, Old Soul

Under the city, Tibidabo has kept modernizing without shedding its past. The winter of 2008 to 2009 saw the retirement of the old Montana Rusa in favor of the Muntanya Russa, a custom Vekoma coaster that hugs the hillside so that riders get a sweeping view of Barcelona. The Giradabo ferris wheel arrived in 2014, and in July 2024 the park opened Merli, a 50-meter drop tower. But thrills carry risk, and Tibidabo learned that painfully. The Pendol, a thrill ride opened in 2006, was removed after a serious accident in 2010 took the life of a young rider, a loss later attributed to mechanical failure. It is a sober thread running through a place devoted to joy, a reminder that behind the laughter, the machines must always be worthy of the trust people place in them.

## A Balcony Over Barcelona

What makes Tibidabo unlike any other old amusement park is simply where it stands. Most parks are built on flat, cheap land at the edge of a city. This one clings to a mountaintop, so that the view from the rides is not of a parking lot but of an entire metropolis, the grid of the Eixample, the spires of the Sagrada Familia, the harbor, and the blue Mediterranean beyond. Ride the Avio out over the railing, or rise on the ferris wheel, and Barcelona opens up in a single sweep. More than a century after a pharmacist first imagined it, the park still does exactly what he intended: it lifts people up above their city and, for a moment, lets them fly.

## Flight Context

Tibidabo Amusement Park sits at 41.422N, 2.120E, atop the Tibidabo peak of the Collserola ridge, 516 meters above sea level and about 400 meters above the city below. It is one of the most conspicuous landmarks in the Barcelona skyline: from any altitude the neighboring Temple of the Sacred Heart church and the tall Torre de Collserola communications tower crown the same ridge, with the park's ferris wheel and rides just beside them. Best viewed from 3,000 to 5,000 feet, where the wooded green of Collserola contrasts sharply with the dense city grid spilling down to the sea. Barcelona-El Prat (LEBL) lies about 15 km to the southwest along the coast, Sabadell (LELL) sits just inland to the northwest beyond the ridge, and Girona (LEGE) is to the northeast. In clear weather the hilltop commands a sweeping panorama of the whole metropolitan area.
