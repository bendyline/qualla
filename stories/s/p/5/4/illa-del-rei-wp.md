# Illa del Rei

A small island in the channel into Port Mahon that has been a king's landing place, a 6th-century basilica, a British naval hospital nicknamed Bloody Island, and now a restored art gallery.

**Location:** 39.8833, 4.2833

**Categories:** History, Menorca, Islands, Naval History, Art

## Introduction

The British called it Bloody Island, and they had earned the right to. Every ship entering the long channel into Port Mahon passes this small pear-shaped rock in midstream, and for two centuries the low building on its southwestern flank was where sailors were carried to be cured or to die. Barely four hectares of stone rising fourteen metres from the sea, Illa del Rei has been a conqueror's landing place, a lost early church, a naval hospital feared and needed in equal measure, and finally something no one in its grim past could have imagined: a gallery of contemporary art.

## The King's Landing

The island's Catalan name, Illa del Rei, means the King's Island, and it commemorates a landing. Legend holds that King Alfonso III of Aragon first stepped ashore here in 1287, during the Christian reconquest of Menorca from its Muslim rulers. Before that royal arrival the rock had a humbler title, Illa dels Conills, Rabbit Island, but a conqueror deserved better than rabbits, and so it was renamed in his honour. The change is a small monument in itself: a shift from the ordinary life of an islet to the weight of a king's foot on its shore, preserved in a name that has held for more than seven hundred years.

## A Basilica Beneath the Grass

Long before Alfonso, the island had already been holy ground. Buried in its soil are the remains of an early Christian basilica from the sixth century, unsuspected until 1888, when a mosaic came to light. Its patterns echoed those of a synagogue at Hammam-Lif in North Africa, discovered only a few years earlier, hinting at the tangled artistic currents that crossed the ancient Mediterranean. Not until 1964 did systematic excavation trace the full footprint of the church, along with further buildings that may have been monastic and more mosaics underfoot. A place most people now visit for its later history turns out to have been a centre of worship when the Roman world was still dissolving into the medieval.

## Bloody Island

The building that dominates the island went up in 1711, when the British held Menorca and prized its harbour above almost any in the Mediterranean. Pierce Griffyth, an agent of the board that cared for sick and wounded sailors, oversaw its construction under orders from Admiral Sir John Jennings, and the result was a naval hospital on an island reachable only by boat. The British named it Bloody Island or, more plainly, Hospital Island. Its wards outlasted their builders by far, serving in turn the Spanish, French, British, Italian and American navies through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, sometimes doubling as a store for coal and naval supplies between the tides of the sick.

## Abandonment and the Lizard

The hospital's usefulness finally undid it. In 1964 a modern hospital opened in Mahon, and the island wards were abandoned, defeated by the simple difficulty of ferrying patients across the water. The buildings began to crumble. Recognition followed the decline: in 1979 the hospital and basilica were placed under national monument protection, and in 1993 the whole island was declared a biosphere reserve. Part of the reason was a reptile. Illa del Rei is the sole home of Podarcis lilfordi balearica, an endemic subspecies of Lilford's wall lizard that exists on this island and nowhere else, an entire lineage riding out the centuries on a rock in the harbour mouth.

## From Ruin to Gallery

The latest chapter is a revival. In 2004 a foundation was created to rescue the derelict buildings, and by 2011, the hospital's three-hundredth anniversary, the exterior of the main structure had been restored. Then the island turned an unexpected corner. A contemporary art gallery moved into the repurposed historic buildings, its grounds landscaped by the celebrated Dutch garden designer Piet Oudolf, and acclaim quickly followed: a Best Social Responsibility award from the Balearic government in 2021, a place among Architectural Digest's works of wonder, and the title of Best Art Destination in the 2022 Wallpaper Design Awards. Bloody Island had become a place people cross the water to admire rather than to survive.

## Flight Context

Located at 39.883 N, 4.283 E, an island set in the middle of the main navigable channel of Port Mahon on the northeastern side of Menorca. Menorca Airport (LEMH) lies about 3 nm to the west-southwest. From the air it is a distinct pear-shaped island in the centre of the long harbour inlet, its large former hospital building clearly visible, roughly halfway between the city of Mahon and the harbour mouth. A viewing altitude of 1,500-2,500 ft along the inlet frames the island against the surrounding water and the two shorelines. Summer visibility is excellent.
