# Camí de Cavalls

An ancient coastal path that rings the whole of Menorca, once ridden by island cavalry guarding the shore and now a 185-kilometre walking route linking its most hidden beaches.

**Location:** 39.8975, 4.2575

**Categories:** Travel, Hiking, History, Menorca, Geography

## Introduction

The name gives the game away in Catalan: cami de cavalls, the way of horses. Today walkers follow it to reach coves so remote that no road will take you there, wild half-moons of white sand and water the colour of glass. But the path was never cut for pleasure. It was cut for defence, a continuous track ringing the entire coast of Menorca so that men on horseback could patrol every inlet where an enemy might land. Follow it far enough and you circle the whole island, from the mouth of Port Mahon back to the mouth of Port Mahon.

## A Ring Around an Island

The Cami de Cavalls is now catalogued as the GR 223, part of Spain's network of long-distance footpaths, and it runs roughly a hundred and eighty-five kilometres in an unbroken loop around Menorca. Its great gift to walkers is access. The route stitches together some of the least reachable beaches on the island, calas hidden at the foot of ravines or behind headlands, places you would never find by car. The whole circuit is split into twenty stages, each between five and fourteen kilometres, and because every start and finish point can be reached by road, you can walk a single stretch or string several together. Few coastlines anywhere are so thoroughly, so deliberately traceable on foot.

## Built for Horses and Muskets

The path's purpose was military from the start. It was laid out to connect the watchtowers, fortresses and cannon positions strung along the coast, and to move troops and artillery quickly from one threatened point to the next. Just when it first appeared is less certain. The most widely accepted theory credits the French, who occupied the island during several periods, while some studies push its origins back to the fourteenth century. What is not in doubt is who walked it: soldiers mounted on the compact, jet-black Menorquin horses that still define the island's festivals. From those patrolling riders the track took its name, the way of the horses, and the name has outlasted the muskets entirely.

## From Private to Public

For much of the twentieth century the Cami de Cavalls was not truly a public path at all. Long stretches crossed private land, and for decades ordinary walkers could not use them. That changed after the island's government reached an agreement with the landowners, opening the route to everyone. The authorities then restored the whole path, an expensive undertaking carried out in stages: roughly 935,000 euros in a first phase and a further 800,000 in a second, completed in May 2011. Fences came down, waymarks went up, and a defensive relic became a right of way. The transformation gave Menorca one of the finest coastal walks in the Mediterranean, hiding in plain sight the entire time.

## Two Coasts, One Circuit

To walk the full loop is to meet two very different islands. The northern stages run along a wilder, wind-scoured shore, exposed to the Tramuntana that funnels down from the mainland, past coves like Cala Pregonda and Cala Pilar and the bleak headland of Punta Nati. Swing round to the south and the mood softens: the celebrated pale-sand calas of Turqueta and Macarella open at the foot of wooded ravines, their water shading from jade to deep blue. The path threads them all together in a single continuous line, so that a walker who keeps going long enough feels the whole character of Menorca change underfoot, coast by coast, stage by stage. Few routes anywhere let you read an island's contradictions so completely just by following its edge.

## The Path as Arena

Once opened, the loop became a stage for feats of endurance. In the spring of 2012 the adventure-racing world champion Arnau Julia circled the island three times in a single continuous effort of 82 hours and 30 minutes, once running, once by mountain bike and once by kayak. That same May, the Trail Menorca Cami de Cavalls was run for the first time, drawing 140 competitors across five races, the longest of which covered the full 185.3 kilometres of the circuit. What island cavalry once rode at a patrol's pace, ultrarunners now attack against the clock, the same coast, the same relentless ring, a very different kind of endurance.

## Flight Context

The Cami de Cavalls encircles the entire island of Menorca; the marker point is 39.898 N, 4.257 E near Port Mahon, where Stage 1 begins along the waterfront. The path traces almost the whole coastline, so from altitude it follows the interface between land and the surrounding Mediterranean. Menorca Airport (LEMH) sits centrally near the south coast, roughly 4 nm southwest of the Mahon trailhead. A viewing altitude of 2,000-4,000 ft along the shore reveals the string of coves, headlands and old watchtowers the route connects. Summer visibility is excellent; the north coast is exposed to the Tramuntana wind.
