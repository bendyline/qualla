# Es Castell

The easternmost town in Spain, founded by the British as Georgetown at the mouth of Port Mahon, where black-and-white riders still rear their horses through the summer crowds.

**Location:** 39.8803, 4.2908

**Categories:** Travel, History, Menorca, Towns, Culture

## Introduction

Every morning, the sun reaches Es Castell before it reaches anywhere else in Spain. Perched at the very mouth of Port Mahon on Menorca's eastern edge, this is the first town in the country to catch the dawn, the daylight sliding across its harbour while the rest of Spain still sleeps in shadow. It is a fitting quirk for a place that has always faced outward, toward the sea and the ships and the empires that came with them. Almost eight thousand people live here now, in a town that began as a foreign garrison and has worn several names since.

## First Light in Spain

Geography made Es Castell what it is. It sits at the seaward end of the long inlet that forms Port Mahon, guarding the approach to one of the finest natural harbours in the Mediterranean. That position is why the town exists at all, and why its history is stitched through with fortification. The town takes its very name from a castle, the Castle of San Felipe, raised to shield the island from Turkish raids in the sixteenth century. To live at the harbour mouth was to live at the island's front door, the first place a friend or an enemy would reach, and the first, each day, to see the light.

## A Town With Three Names

Es Castell has changed identity with its rulers. The main town was founded in 1771 by the British, who then held Menorca, and they called it Georgetown in honour of King George III. When the island passed to Spain, the name shifted to Villacarlos, a name still heard today. Only in 1985 did it settle on Es Castell, or El Castell in Spanish, tying itself once more to the fortress at its edge. Three names for one town, each a fossil of a different power that once prized this harbour. Few small towns carry their contested history so openly in the words people use to name their home.

## Where the Horse Is King

Come late July, the streets belong to the horses. Es Castell keeps its patron saint festival in honour of the apostle Santiago, Sant Jaume in Catalan, across the 24th, 25th and 26th, and as in every Menorcan town the horse is the undisputed star. Riders called caixers, dressed sharply in black and white, guide their decorated mounts into a dense crowd and coax them up onto their hind legs, front hooves pawing the air above the packed bodies below. This is the jaleo, a tradition equal parts nerve and spectacle. In hand, more often than not, is a glass of pomada, the island's festival drink of gin cut with lemonade.

## A Cluster of Forts

For all its festival colour, Es Castell was born a garrison, and stone soldiers still surround it. The Castle of San Felipe, raised against sixteenth-century Turkish raids and lending the modern town its name, once stood watch over the entrance to Port Mahon, the same fortress the British defended and the French besieged in their long tug-of-war for the island. Across the water rises the great fortress of La Mola, guarding the harbour mouth from the northern headland. The town itself keeps a military museum among its old barracks, a fitting institution for a place laid out by an occupying army. To walk Es Castell is to walk a defensive plan: streets set on a grid, buildings arranged for order, a whole town conceived as an instrument of control over one of the Mediterranean's most valuable harbours.

## Everyday Life at the Harbour Mouth

Between festivals, Es Castell is simply a working Menorcan town. The Me-1 expressway runs the length of the island east to west, linking the airport and the other towns to this eastern corner, and TMSA buses thread the same route. Every Tuesday morning a market fills the central square from eight until noon, and around it the ordinary infrastructure of island life carries on: bakeries, butchers, greengrocers selling produce straight from the farm. The grand strategic drama that built the town has long since faded into treaties and museums. What remains is a harbourside community that still wakes, quite literally, to the earliest light in Spain.

## Flight Context

Located at 39.880 N, 4.291 E at the eastern mouth of Port Mahon on Menorca, immediately adjacent to the island capital. Menorca Airport (LEMH) lies about 5 nm to the west-southwest. From the air Es Castell reads as a compact town wrapped around the small harbour of Cales Fonts and facing the main channel of Port Mahon, with the ruined lines of old fortifications nearby and La Mola fortress across the water to the north. A viewing altitude of 1,500-2,500 ft over the harbour mouth gives the clearest sense of its guardian position. Summer visibility is excellent; watch for the northerly Tramuntana across the exposed eastern coast.
