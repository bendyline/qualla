# Museum of Menorca

Housed in a former Franciscan convent in Mahon, the Museum of Menorca guards 200,000 objects tracing the island's past, from prehistoric taulas to a Roman emperor's bust.

**Location:** 39.8910, 4.2607

**Categories:** Museums, History, Archaeology, Menorca, Culture

## Introduction

The building has been emptied more than once. A Franciscan convent stood on this ground with roots in the fifteenth century, only to be destroyed in the Turkish attack on Mahon in 1535. Rebuilt across the following two centuries, it lost its friars again in 1835 when the state seized church property, and then served, in turn, as a house of mercy, a nautical school, a public library and a high school. Now this much-repurposed cloister holds something it was never built for: the collected memory of an entire island, some two hundred thousand objects reaching from the Bronze Age to the near present.

## A Convent Full of Time

The setting is half the story. The Museum of Menorca occupies the old Franciscan convent beside the church of Saint Francis in Mahon, a structure built between the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries and refurbished in the early nineteenth. Its heart is a square Baroque cloister, three floors of arches wrapped around an open court, where classical order meets baroque flourish. Where friars once kept the schools of grammar and philosophy on the ground floor and slept in cells above, visitors now climb through galleries of the island's past. A place designed for contemplation of eternity has been turned to the patient study of deep time, which is perhaps not so different a vocation.

## From a Handful of Roman Tombs

The institution is older than its current home. Its ancestor, the Municipal Museum of Mahon, opened on 4 November 1889 in a building opposite the town hall, the project of a local promoter named Joan Segui Rodriguez and stocked at first entirely with private gifts. The very first artifacts were fragments of Roman tombs unearthed in Mahon, donated by the museum's own curator, Pere Monjo Monjo. From that modest handful of funerary stone, a collection began to grow, passed after the founder's death into the care of the town's schoolteachers, who became the unlikely guardians of Menorca's material history.

## A Museum That Kept Moving

For much of the twentieth century the collection had no settled home, drifting from building to building as fortunes shifted. It moved to the Ateneo in 1906, folded into a new Provincial Museum of Fine Arts in 1944, then into the Can Mercadal palace in 1948. Not until 1980, when the derelict Franciscan convent was donated to the state, did the collection find a permanent address, and even then progress was slow: the government of the Balearic Islands took over management in 1984, part of the building reopened in 1986, and the full permanent galleries only opened at the close of the century. The museum's own history reads like a long search for a room large enough to hold the past.

## Objects and a Famous Digger

That patience paid off in scale. The collection now runs to roughly two hundred thousand objects, supported by a specialised library and a workspace where researchers can study the holdings directly. Its treasures span millennia, including a bust of the Roman emperor Tiberius found in Mahon. Some pieces travelled a long way to arrive. Artefacts excavated in the 1930s by Margaret Murray, the pioneering British archaeologist, were recovered from Cambridge and returned to the island. The museum has drawn on the work of institutions from Boston University to the University of Cagliari, and since 2007 its own archaeologists have been digging at Cornia Nou, a Talayotic settlement near Mahon, adding fresh finds to shelves already heavy with the island's story.

## Where the Island Keeps Itself

Today the Museum of Menorca is the leading keeper of the island's heritage, the place where the fruits of its famous prehistoric sites come to rest. The Punic jars and animal bones from feasting enclosures, the mosaics and pottery from ruined basilicas and navetas, the everyday objects of a hundred generations, all converge here. Between 2017 and 2018 the permanent exhibition was completely renovated, reopening in July 2018 with a thoroughly modern display, while temporary shows and school programmes keep the old convent busy. On an island where three-thousand-year-old towers still stand in the fields, this is the room where the pieces too fragile or too precious to leave outside are finally brought in from the weather.

## Flight Context

Located at 39.891 N, 4.261 E in the old town of Mahon, capital of Menorca, on the southern bluff above Port Mahon. Menorca Airport (LEMH) lies about 5 nm to the southwest. The museum itself, a former convent with a square cloister beside the church of Saint Francis, sits within the dense historic core and is hard to isolate from the air; the long fjord-like harbour of Mahon just to the north is the dominant landmark. A viewing altitude of 1,500-2,500 ft over the old town and harbour gives the best orientation. Summer visibility is excellent.
