# Santa Tecla Festival

Every September, Tarragona hands its streets to giants, devils, and human towers in a festival whose backbone was fixed in 1370 and whose relic arrived by sea from Antioch in 1321.

**Location:** 41.1175, 1.2528

**Categories:** Festivals and traditions, Culture, History, Catalonia, Religion, Folklore

## Introduction

The gralla shrills, the drums roll, and somewhere a tower of human beings climbs toward the balconies while a child in a helmet scrambles to its top. For a few days each September, Tarragona stops being an ordinary Catalan port and becomes a stage for its own oldest self. Devils spit sparks, wooden giants sway above the crowd, and a procession carries a saint's arm through streets scrubbed clean for the occasion. This is the Festa de Santa Tecla, and beneath its modern noise of rock concerts and fireworks lies a ceremonial structure that the city has kept, almost unchanged, for more than six centuries.

## A City in the Streets

Strip away the concerts and film screenings and the heart of Santa Tecla is the Popular Retinue, the city's own troupe of dances, bestiary figures, entremesos, spoken dances, and the castells, the celebrated human towers of Catalonia. It is a festival defined by taking possession of public space, by filling plazas and narrow lanes with bodies and sound. That distinctiveness is officially recognized twice over. The Government of Catalonia has named it a Traditional Festival of national interest, and the Spanish Government a Touristic Festival of national interest, making Santa Tecla one of only two festivals in all of Catalonia to hold both declarations at once.

## The Arm from Antioch

The festival's turning point arrived by sea. On 17 May 1321, a relic said to be the arm of the patron saint reached Tarragona from Antioch, in the East, and the city met it with a reception so overwhelming that it reset the festivity forever. For the first time, the whole population, clergy and soldiers, officials and working people, poured into the streets and claimed them together. New dances appeared, performed by the local guilds, blending pre-Christian and Christian ritual in the service of the sacred. Half a century later the shape was made permanent. On 26 July 1370, at the request of the city's consuls, archbishop Pere Clasquerí issued the Bylaws of Santa Tecla, the founding rulebook of the celebration.

## The Calendar of the Saint

Those bylaws still govern the rhythm of the days. On 22 September, vespers are sung in the cathedral, the spiritual center of everything, while guild dances wind through the city in the great parade now called the Cercavila. The following day brings the religious service and the Procession of the Holy Arm, carried under a canopy and led, as always, by the dances. The bylaws even specified the housekeeping: streets swept and decorated along the route, twenty candles bearing the city's emblem kept burning to light the relic. Around this frame grew the beloved extras, the pre-dawn Matinades with their grallers and drummers, the fireworks, and Magí de les Timbales, the Council drummer who became a festival character in his own right.

## Giants, Devils, and Human Towers

The retinue accumulated its cast over generations. Documents record the fantastic bestiary from 1381, biblical figures from 1385, and, from the second quarter of the 15th century, the roques, moving platforms like today's floats, and the entremesos. In the 19th century the morphology of the castells was defined and the human towers took their essential place. The sculptor Bernat Verderol carved the towering giants that still parade, the Moorish Giants, the Black Giants, and the Old Nanos, while the Ball de Diables, the Devils' Dance, balanced fire and thunder and became a forerunner of the modern correfoc. Even the sardana, imported from the Empordà region, joined the mix on its way to becoming a national symbol.

## Silenced and Reclaimed

The festival has been taken from Tarragona and given back more than once. In 1911 Pope Pius X abolished the feast's holiday status, turning the saint's day into an ordinary working day, until Pope Benedict XV restored it on 10 July 1917 at the mayor's request. The darkest chapter came with the Spanish Civil War of 1936 and the dictatorship that followed, when the authorities demoted Santa Tecla and elevated the smaller Sant Magí festivity to draw summer tourists, at the expense of the city's own people. Only with the return of democratic town halls did Tarragona reclaim its festival, reviving the Popular Retinue, the pyrotechnics, and the hand-rung cathedral bells as a celebration made once more of and for its inhabitants.

## Flight Context

Tarragona centers on 41.118N, 1.253E, its old city and cathedral rising above the harbor on the Costa Daurada. From 2,500 to 5,000 feet the medieval Part Alta, the cathedral, and the amphitheatre above the beach are clear anchor points for the streets where the festival unfolds. Reus (LERS) lies about 6nm west-northwest, Barcelona (LEBL) roughly 50nm northeast, and Lleida-Alguaire (LEDA) some 50nm northwest. In late September, when the festival runs, the coastal air is typically clear and warm with long visibility along the shoreline.
