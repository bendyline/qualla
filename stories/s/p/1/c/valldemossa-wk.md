# Valldemossa

A mountain village one famous guest scorned and an Austrian archduke loved so completely he bought the coastline around it.

**Location:** 39.7117, 2.6226

**Categories:** Villages, Valldemossa, Serra de Tramuntana, Mallorca, Balearic Islands, Pilgrimage Sites

## Introduction

"The food is expensive, includes too much pig, and the peasants are as unpleasant as dishonest," George Sand wrote of Valldemossa after wintering there in 1838. The village has spent nearly two centuries quietly proving her wrong. Today it is one of the most admired spots on Mallorca, a cluster of honey-colored stone houses stacked on a green mountainside, its lanes hung with flowers and its cafés full. Sand's companion, Frédéric Chopin, made immortal music here. But the visitor who truly loved Valldemossa was an Austrian archduke who liked it so much he bought the mountainside.

## A Village in the Mountains

Valldemossa sits about 20 kilometers north of Palma, tucked into a fold of the Serra de Tramuntana, the rugged limestone range that runs along Mallorca's northwest coast. Fewer than 2,000 people live here, in a village whose terraced gardens and steep stone streets climb toward the great bulk of its fourteenth-century Charterhouse. The surrounding mountains are laced with dry-stone walls, patiently built to hold soil on the slopes, and the long-distance GR 221 trail, the route of the dry stone walls, threads through the village on its way across the range. The village is small enough to cross in minutes, yet many visitors stay for hours, held by the view down the terraces toward the sea. For all its fame, Valldemossa remains small and steep and green, a place best explored on foot, one narrow lane at a time.

## Two Verdicts

Valldemossa owes much of its renown to two very different outsiders. The first was the writer George Sand, who came in the winter of 1838 with Chopin and left with a grudge, publishing a travel book so unflattering that locals still quote its insults with a kind of pride. She found the weather cruel, the food heavy and the villagers cold to a pair of unmarried foreigners, one of them coughing with tuberculosis. Her account was unfair, but it made Valldemossa famous, and the memory of Chopin composing his Preludes in a monastery cell now draws visitors from around the world. Some places are put on the map by praise. Valldemossa was put there partly by complaint.

## The Archduke Who Bought a Coastline

The second outsider adored the place. Archduke Ludwig Salvator of Austria, a Habsburg who first saw the Balearics in 1867 as a young man of about twenty, was so captivated that he made Mallorca his life. In 1872 he bought the Miramar estate near Valldemossa, drawn there partly by his study of the medieval Mallorcan mystic Ramon Llull, and over the following years he kept buying, acquiring Son Marroig near Deià and most of the land between the two villages. What he did with it was remarkable for his era. Long before conservation was a common idea, the archduke fiercely protected the coast's plants, animals and landscapes, built viewpoints and paved footpaths, and pressed to keep the mountains from being spoiled. The trail that links Valldemossa to Deià still bears his name, the Camí de s'Arxiduc, and the stretch of Tramuntana he guarded is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

## The Farm Girl Who Became a Saint

Valldemossa's most enduring figure, though, was born here long before either visitor arrived. Caterina Tomàs came into the world in the village in 1531, a farmhand's life ahead of her, and she worked the land until she left to enter a convent in Palma. There she gained a reputation for piety and visions, and after her death her fame only grew. She was beatified in 1792 and finally canonized in 1930 as Saint Catalina Tomàs, one of the patron saints of Mallorca, entombed in the Palma convent where she had taken her vows. Her birthplace in Valldemossa is now a small shrine, and every year on the 27th and 28th of July the village fills for her festival. In Valldemossa her feast eclipses even the village's other patron, Sant Bartomeu, honored a month later, a sign of how deeply the island still holds the local girl who became its saint.

## Flight Context

Valldemossa lies about 20 km north of Palma in the Serra de Tramuntana (39.71°N, 2.62°E), some 400 meters up in terraced mountain country. From the air the village shows as a compact knot of ochre roofs on a green shelf between limestone ridges, the tower of its Charterhouse standing proud at the center and the northwest coast just beyond the peaks. Palma de Mallorca Airport (LEPA) is roughly 20 km to the southeast. This is mountain terrain: plan on 3,000 to 5,000 feet, fly in the calm of morning, and expect afternoon cloud and turbulence to build over the Tramuntana.
