# Gran Hotel (Palma)

The building that brought Modernisme and electric light to Mallorca in 1903, now reborn as a Palma art center.

**Location:** 39.5718, 2.6501

**Categories:** Modernisme Architecture, Palma de Mallorca, Art Museums, Historic Buildings, Balearic Islands, Belle Époque

## Introduction

On Plaza Weyler, in the heart of Palma's old town, stands a building that seems to bloom out of the street. Its façade is a dense tapestry of brick, wrought iron and glazed ceramic, floral shapes worked into tile and metal until the whole surface appears to grow. This is the Gran Hotel, and when it opened in 1903 it was both the most modern and the most beautiful building on Mallorca. Over the century since, it has been a luxury hotel, a government office and finally an art gallery, changing its purpose again and again while holding onto its remarkable face.

## Modernisme Comes to the Island

The Gran Hotel was designed by Lluís Domènech i Montaner, one of the towering figures of Catalan Modernisme, the movement that gave Barcelona its swirling, organic architecture. Completed in 1903, it is widely regarded as the building that carried that style across the water to Mallorca. Domènech i Montaner covered the façade with sculptures and ceramics, coaxing wrought iron into curling, plant-like forms and setting floral motifs into colored tile. Where older Palma built in honey-colored stone, here was a structure that wanted to be looked at, ornamental and unashamed. The same architect gave Barcelona some of its most celebrated Modernista landmarks, and his hand is unmistakable here. For many islanders the hotel marked the moment their capital joined a wider Mediterranean conversation about beauty, modernity and the shape a city could take.

## The First Electric Light

Behind the ornament, the Gran Hotel was a machine of modern comforts. It was the first building on the island to have a telephone and an electricity supply, generating its own power on site. Hot and cold running water flowed through its rooms, central heating warmed them, and an elevator spared guests the stairs. In an age when Mallorca was still a distant island reached by ship, this was luxury on the scale of the great European capitals, and it drew the traveling elite of the belle époque. News of its comforts spread well beyond Palma, and the hotel's name became a byword for a certain kind of arrival. For the well-heeled travelers just beginning to discover Mallorca, a night at the Gran Hotel was proof that the island could offer sophistication to rival anywhere on the continent. To step inside was to step decades into the future, at least by the standards of the surrounding town.

## War and a Long Decline

The good years did not last. Battered by the upheavals of the Spanish Civil War and the Second World War, the hotel closed its doors in 1941. The following year the state acquired it, converting the elegant building into offices for the Instituto Nacional de Previsión, the national welfare institute. The conversion was brutal. Partitions carved up the first floor, and the graceful arcades and columns simply disappeared behind new walls. In the process the building lost much of its avant-garde character, the very qualities that had made it remarkable. What had been a showpiece became simply a place people worked in, its history all but forgotten by those passing on the square outside.

## A Second Life as CaixaForum

Rescue came from an unexpected direction. In 1993 the banking foundation La Caixa reopened the restored building as a CaixaForum, one of its chain of cultural venues, and the Gran Hotel became an art center rather than a place to sleep. Today it houses the Fundación la Caixa and a permanent gallery of paintings by the Catalan artist Anglada Camarasa, whose lush, color-drenched canvases suit the exuberant setting. The care taken in the restoration was formally recognized in 2003, when Spain's culture ministry declared the building a site of cultural interest, citing both its faithful revival and its architectural importance. A century after it introduced Modernisme to Mallorca, the Gran Hotel had been given back its face. Concerts, exhibitions and a busy café now fill rooms that once housed paying guests. It now anchors a stretch of the old town where visitors pause to photograph a façade most cannot name but few can ignore.

## Flight Context

The Gran Hotel stands on Plaza Weyler in Palma's historic center (39.57°N, 2.65°E), a short walk north of the great Gothic cathedral, La Seu, that dominates the waterfront. From the air the dense old town reads as a warren of narrow streets behind the seaward walls; the cathedral and the Bay of Palma are the surest landmarks, with the Gran Hotel a few hundred meters inland. Palma de Mallorca Airport (LEPA) lies about 8 km east along the bay. A city sight best seen at street level, but the old town's compact grid is a clear visual anchor from 1,500 to 3,000 feet.
