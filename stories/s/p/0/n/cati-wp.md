# Catí

A stone village on a mountain balcony in the Alt Maestrat, remembered for a coat of arms bearing a loyal dog, a lawsuit against a neighbor that ran for four centuries, and the healing springs of L'Avellà.

**Location:** 40.4708, 0.0222

**Categories:** Mountain Villages, Medieval History, Spain, Alt Maestrat, Historic Towns, Spa Towns

## Introduction

A lawsuit can outlive the people who file it, the town that fights it, even the century that gives it meaning. In 1292 the villagers of Catí took their powerful neighbor Morella to court, and they could not have guessed the quarrel would still be unsettled when their great-great-grandchildren were old. It did not end for nearly four hundred years. That kind of patience says a lot about a place, and about this stone village perched on a broad mountain balcony in the Alt Maestrat of inland Castellón.

## A Balcony in the Mountains

Catí sits at 661 meters, spread across a wide valley the locals call the Catí Corridor. It is a threshold as much as a town. Here the rugged Ports country around Morella, to which Catí belonged for centuries, gives way to the gentler Baix Maestrat, and the ridgelines around the village mark that seam in the land. Peaks with names worth saying aloud rise on the horizon: the Serra de Vallivana, the Serra d'En Celler, the Tossal de la Nevera, the Tossal de la Barbuda. This is high, dry, luminous terrain, the kind of place where the air feels thinner and the light falls harder, and where a small community learned long ago to be self-sufficient. The corridor is a natural passage in its own right, linking the fortress country around Morella in the west to the Maestrat ranges beyond, and the village grew up watching over that route through the hills.

## Forty Families and a Loyal Dog

The village has a birthday. On 25 January 1239, the nobleman Blasco d'Alagó granted the land to a man named Ramon de Bocona and forty more households to settle, a charter later confirmed by King James I of Aragon, the great conqueror of these territories. The surnames of those first neighbors are thought to have come north from the country around Lleida. Ramon de Bocona prized one virtue above all others, loyalty, and he built it into his coat of arms in the plainest way he could imagine: a dog, walking, faithful and alert. Eight centuries later the founding shield still speaks for the man who chose it, a small act of medieval self-portraiture carried down through the generations.

## Four Centuries in Court

For all its loyalty, Catí was no pushover. The knights of the village rode out on the great adventures of the age, joining the assaults on Mazalquivir, Oran, and Bougie on the North African coast aboard a ship belonging to the Bayle of Morella. Yet at home the same village was locked in a slow-motion feud with that very town. The legal dispute Catí opened against Morella in 1292 ground on through generations of judges, kings, and clerks, outlasting plagues and dynasties before it was finally settled in 1691. Few communities can claim a grievance nursed so patiently. It is a reminder that in these mountains, memory ran long and pride ran deep. The knights who sailed for the North African coast and the neighbors who sued Morella for four hundred years were, in the end, the same kind of people: loyal to their own, and immovable once their minds were made up.

## The Waters of L'Avellà

A few kilometers from the village lies L'Avellà, a hamlet gathered around a spring and a bathhouse where people have long come to take the waters. Its Casa de Banys drew visitors seeking the cool, mineral flow that rises from the mountain, and the sanctuary beside it gave the place a quiet devotional air. Beyond the springs, Catí's territory scatters into old farmsteads, the masies, with names that read like a roll call of the families who worked them across the centuries. It is a landscape built for endurance rather than spectacle, and endurance, more than anything, is what Catí has always done best.

## Flight Context

Catí lies inland at 40.47°N, 0.02°E, in the mountains of the Alt Maestrat about 30 km northwest of the Mediterranean coast. Set in a broad upland valley at 661 m, it is best viewed at 3,500–5,500 ft, with the surrounding ridges of the Serra de Vallivana and the Ports country rising around it. Castellón–Costa Azahar (LECN) sits to the south, Reus (LERS) to the northeast, and Valencia (LEVC) further down the coast. The pale cluster of the village against terraced valley floor and dark pine ridges is the clearest visual cue; expect strong thermals and bright, dry mountain light by midday.
