# Montgó Massif

A 753-metre limestone giant shaped like an elephant, guarding the Marina Alta coast with a botanical treasury found almost nowhere else on Earth.

**Location:** 38.8083, 0.1167

**Categories:** Mountains, Natural Parks, Marina Alta, Costa Blanca, Wildlife, Archaeology

## Introduction

From the streets of Xàbia, the mountain looks unmistakably like an elephant — a great domed skull, a long trunk sloping down to drink from the Mediterranean. Montgó climbs 753 metres straight out of the coastal plain, the last defiant spur of the Cordillera Prebética before the range surrenders to the sea. It stands alone between the old towns of Dénia and Xàbia, so close to the water that on a clear day a walker on its summit can make out the pale silhouette of Ibiza, floating far offshore.

## Born of a Collision

The elephant is made of stone laid down beneath a vanished sea. During the Cretaceous, roughly 70 million years ago, the slow collision of the African and European plates buckled the seabed upward and lifted Montgó into the sky. Erosion and rockfall spent the next few million years carving it into the craggy profile visible today. Look closely and the mountain reveals its layers: soft marl and marly lime on the lower slopes, hard compact limestone forming the sheer escarpments above. Those cliffs, riddled with caves and fissures, would go on to shelter everything from nesting falcons to Stone Age hunters.

## A Garden Found Nowhere Else

Botanists come to Montgó the way pilgrims come to a shrine. More than 650 plant species crowd its slopes, some growing almost nowhere else. The blue Mediterranean fan palm springs up wild here — the only palm native to Europe. Rarer still is the Carduncellus dianius, known locally as hierba santa, 'holy herb,' which grows on this massif and on the island of Ibiza and nowhere else in the world. On the shaded, humid cliffs, endemic survivors cling to cracks in the rock: the Valencian rock violet, cave scabious, and Mediterranean scrub perfumed with rosemary and rock lavender. Where the primitive Mediterranean oak once blanketed the mountain, over-exploitation thinned it long ago, yet stubborn groves persist.

## The Realm of Eight Raptors

Above the botanists wheel the hunters. Eight species of birds of prey are known to breed on Montgó — four by night and four by day. After dark the Eurasian eagle owl, scops owl, tawny owl and little owl patrol the cliffs. By day the peregrine falcon, goshawk, kestrel and sparrowhawk ride the thermals; Bonelli's eagle winters in the heights. Along the coastal edge at Cap de Sant Antoni, seabirds work the wind. Here the Audouin's gull, one of the Mediterranean's scarcest, winters among the salt-tolerant fennel and everlasting flowers that grow where almost nothing else will.

## Thirty Thousand Years of Footsteps

People have watched from these slopes for a very long time. The first hunters sheltered in the seaward caves some 30,000 years ago, living off rich fishing grounds and mild winters, and their painted marks survive in the Cova del Barranc del Migdia. In the Cova de l'Aigua, a Roman legionary carved an inscription dated to the year 238. Around the start of the 10th century, the Córdoban caliph Abd al-Rahman III thought the mountain worth a special journey, sending men to gather more than a hundred medicinal herbs from its flanks. Later generations terraced the lower slopes for Moscatel grapes, drying them into raisins and pressing the sweet mistela wine of the Marina Alta. At the very summit sit the ruins of the Casa de Biot, an Iberian settlement from the 8th century BC.

## A Mountain Worth Fighting Over

Dénia and Xàbia have long squabbled over their shared giant. One regional folktale claims the two towns tugged at Montgó so fiercely that one fell flat on its face and the other on its backside — an origin story, told with a wink, for the nicknames locals still trade. In time the rivalry cooled into stewardship. In 1987 the Generalitat Valenciana declared the mountain and the 2,150 hectares around it a protected natural park, safeguarding a coastline, a forest and a botanical treasury in a single stroke of the pen.

## Flight Context

Centered at 38.81°N, 0.12°E, Montgó rises to 753 m (about 2,470 ft) directly between Dénia and Xàbia on the Marina Alta coast. Approach from the southeast, over Xàbia, at 3,500–4,500 ft to catch the mountain's distinctive elephant-head profile; the flat coastal shelf of Les Planes and the cliffs of Cap de Sant Antoni mark its seaward end. Nearest airports are Alicante-Elche (LEAL), about 90 km southwest, and Valencia (LEVC) to the north, with Ibiza (LEIB) out to sea on the horizon. Expect mountain-induced turbulence and rapidly forming orographic cloud on the summit even when the coast below is clear.
