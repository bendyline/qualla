# Teulada, Spain

An inland Valencian hill town, founded in 1386, that kept its low skyline and terraced heritage even as foreign residents grew numerous enough to win its town hall.

**Location:** 38.7292, 0.1019

**Categories:** Historic Towns, Costa Blanca, Marina Alta, Festivals, Spanish History, Wine

## Introduction

In 1999, the party that won Teulada's town hall was made up mostly of people who could not cast a ballot in a Spanish general election. They were English, German and Dutch — foreign residents drawn to this quiet stretch of the Costa Blanca who had organised into a party of their own and swept the local vote. It was a startling result for a town founded more than six centuries earlier, and it captured something essential about modern Teulada: an old Valencian hill town remade, again and again, by the people who fall for it.

## Two Towns, One Name

Teulada sits inland, up among terraced hillsides, three kilometres from the coast road; its seaside half is Moraira, and the two are so bound together they are usually spoken of as one, Teulada-Moraira. Like its neighbour, the town made a defining choice about how to grow. While Calp to the south sprouted apartment blocks and Benidorm became a skyline of towers, Teulada's council passed laws limiting building heights and protecting the pines that cover the valley slopes down to the sea. The town stayed low, green and legible — a place that still reads as a Valencian town rather than a resort strip.

## Founded in 1386

People were here long before the town had a name. In a cave on the Cap d'Or headland, archaeologists found traces of habitation from the Upper Paleolithic, some 15,000 to 20,000 years ago. Teulada itself was founded in 1386, and through the 16th century it fortified against the Barbary corsairs who raided this coast for captives. The restored lookout tower on the Cap d'Or, raised in 1563, still keeps its watch on the headland. The long centuries of Moorish presence left their mark in the land itself — the walled agricultural terraces stepping up the hillsides, planted then as now with almonds, vines and citrus.

## The Stone Heart

The old town keeps its history in stone. The parish church of St. Catherine, begun in the final third of the 16th century, was built to double as a refuge, its heavy walls as much fortress as sanctuary in an age of coastal raids. Nearby stands the Sala de Jurats i Justícia, a 17th-century hall where the town's magistrates once dispensed justice. Scattered through the surrounding country are the hermitages — Sant Vicent Ferrer, Divina Pastora, Font Santa — small whitewashed chapels that still anchor the processions woven through the local calendar. Down on the coast, the Castle of Moraira completes the municipality's roster of monuments: a seaward fort to answer the inland church, both raised in their time against the same threat coming off the water.

## When the Foreigners Took the Town Hall

By the late 20th century, tourism and a booming construction trade had reshaped Teulada, and with them came a large European expatriate community, predominantly British, German and Dutch. Their numbers grew great enough to matter at the ballot box, and in 1999 the Moraira Party, composed chiefly of non-Spanish residents, won control of the town hall. What followed was a run of investment in local facilities, concentrated especially in the coastal district of Moraira. It was a modern echo of an old pattern on this shore: newcomers arriving by sea, and leaving their stamp on the place they found.

## Fiestas and the Sweet Grape

For all the change, Teulada holds tight to its Valencian calendar. The year turns through its festivals: Sant Vicent Ferrer, the patron saint, honoured on the Monday after Easter; the Moros i Cristians pageant in the second week of June, reenacting the old clash of Moor and Christian; and Santa Catalina on the 25th of November. Best of all is the Festa del Moscatell on the first Saturday of September, a celebration of the sweet Muscat grape whose raisins and mistela wine have flavoured this coast for generations. The town stages some of the finest firework displays on the Costa Blanca — proof that a place can guard its traditions without ever standing still.

## Flight Context

Teulada lies at 38.73°N, 0.10°E, set back about 3 km from the N-332 coast road on the Marina Alta. From 2,500–3,500 ft the compact hilltop old town reads clearly against the terraced slopes, with the Cap d'Or headland and its watchtower marking the coast at Moraira just southeast. The Penyal d'Ifac at Calp, a short way down the shore, is the standout regional landmark for orientation. Nearest airports are Alicante-Elche (LEAL), about 80 km southwest, and Valencia (LEVC) to the north; Ibiza (LEIB) lies out across the sea to the east.
