# Sant Antoni de Portmany

A two-thousand-year-old fishing village on Ibiza's western bay that reinvented itself, in a single generation, as a global capital of sunsets and all-night clubbing.

**Location:** 38.9808, 1.3006

**Categories:** Cities, Nightlife, Music, Beaches, Ibiza, Balearic Islands

## Introduction

For two thousand years, Sant Antoni de Portmany was a fishing village - boats, nets, a small church, the slow turn of the seasons on a quiet western bay. Then, in the space of a single generation, it became one of the loudest small towns in the Mediterranean. The hotels rose in the late 1950s, the package flights followed, and a place that had spent two millennia watching the western sky for weather began watching it for something else entirely: the show. Every clear evening, the whole town still turns to face the sunset.

## From Nets to Neon

Sant Antoni sits on a broad, sheltered bay on the west coast of Ibiza, and for most of its history that geography meant one thing: fish. The transformation came fast. In the late 1950s, as Spain threw open its coasts to mass tourism, hotels and resorts sprouted along the shoreline almost overnight, and the second-largest town on the island swapped its working harbour for a holiday one. Sun, cheap flights, and cheaper drinks did the rest. Within a few decades the fishing village had become a destination known across northern Europe, its old rhythms buried under a new economy that ran on arrivals and departures. What took twenty centuries to build, tourism rewrote in twenty years - and the town has been negotiating with that bargain ever since.

## The Whole Town Faces West

One thing here is beautiful beyond argument, and it happens for free. As the sun drops toward the water on the far side of the bay, crowds drift down to the rocks along the shore, drinks in hand, and go quiet. The ritual has a birthplace: Café del Mar, which opened on this waterfront in 1980. From 1991 the DJ Jose Padilla built its reputation with sunset sets of ambient, downtempo music, and from 1994 the bar lent its name to a series of chillout compilation albums that carried the Ibiza sunset sound to living rooms around the world, selling in the millions. Other bars now line the same stretch, the so-called sunset strip, each cueing its own soundtrack to the sky. But the moment they are all chasing is older than any of them - light on water, and a town that stops to watch.

## The West End After Dark

When the sky finally goes dark, a different Sant Antoni wakes up. British clubbers have called it the clubbing capital of the world, and though most of the island's giant clubs actually sit outside town, this is where the night begins, in the dense tangle of bars known as the West End. The reputation has swung hard over the years. In the 1980s the town became associated with rowdy British football crowds, and tourism suffered; by the mid-1990s it had reinvented itself again as the base camp of choice for a new generation drawn by world-famous DJs. Those visitors are overwhelmingly young and overwhelmingly British, in search of the same release and belonging that crowds have always sought after dark. The excess has a cost, too. In late 2020 the local government signalled its intent to permanently close streets where heavy drinking and anti-social behaviour had put public health and safety at risk.

## The Town Behind the Party

Step a few blocks off the strip and a quieter, year-round town appears - the one the locals keep for themselves in the daylight hours while the visitors sleep. The parish church of Sant Antoni anchors the old center, and out on the point stands Sa Punta des Moli, a restored windmill kept as a reminder of the working island that came before the resorts. So many outsiders pass through that most residents speak fluent English, a small measure of how completely the world has arrived on this bay. Sant Antoni is easy to caricature and often is - loud, sunburned, unsubtle. But it is also a real place with two thousand years behind it, still fishing, still praying, still turning, every evening, to watch the same sun go down.

## Flight Context

At 38.9808 N, 1.3006 E, Sant Antoni de Portmany occupies a wide horseshoe bay on Ibiza's west coast, about 10 km northwest of Ibiza Airport (LEIB). From 2,000-4,000 ft the town is unmistakable: a dense white waterfront wrapped around a sheltered bay open to the west, with a marina and the small offshore islet of Sa Conillera guarding its mouth - the classic sunset-facing amphitheatre. It sits under the western approaches to LEIB, so expect controlled airspace and holiday traffic. Alicante-Elche (LEAL) lies roughly 150 km east across the sea on the Spanish mainland. The signature light is at dusk, when the whole bay glows and the western horizon draws every eye ashore.
