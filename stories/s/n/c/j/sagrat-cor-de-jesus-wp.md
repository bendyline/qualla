# Sagrat Cor de Jesus

A 23-meter stone Christ on a hill above Ibiza, raised in gratitude to the villagers who hid a hunted man through the terror of the Spanish Civil War.

**Location:** 38.9412, 1.4223

**Categories:** monuments, history, Spanish Civil War, religion, Ibiza, memorials

## Introduction

This statue is a thank-you carved in stone. Twenty-three meters of pale rock, shaped into the figure of Christ, the Sacred Heart of Jesus, stand on a knoll north of Ibiza Town, and the monument exists because ordinary islanders once risked themselves to shelter a hunted man. During the chaos of the Spanish Civil War, a fugitive named Antonio Cardona Riera hid in these hills, and local people kept him alive. When the danger passed, the community raised this towering figure to remember what they had done for him, and what he owed them in return.

## A Statue Built From Gratitude

The monument is built entirely of stone and rises 23 meters above its hilltop, carved by the sculptor Antoni Sanjuan and raised in 1947. It is not a grand pilgrimage site or a famous landmark; it is a local act of remembrance made monumental. The figure faces outward over the countryside north of Ibiza Town, arms and heart offered in the traditional pose of the Sacred Heart. Most monuments of this size commemorate kings, battles, or saints. This one commemorates something quieter and rarer: a debt of gratitude that a community chose to pay in the most permanent material it had.

## The Man Called Frit

The fugitive was Antonio Cardona Riera, known on the island as Frit. His hiding came during the most dangerous stretch of the war on Ibiza. After the Nationalist military rebellion of 1936, Republican forces under Alberto Bayo retook the island; when Bayo then left for Majorca, the anarchist FAI, the Iberian Anarchist Federation, was left in control. It was in that lawless, frightening interval that Cardona went into hiding for several days in these hills. He survived only because the local population fed and concealed him, ordinary people who understood exactly what could happen to anyone caught helping a marked man.

## The Hill Called Monte Cristo

The statue stands on a small knoll in a hamlet named Monte Cristo, now shadowed by a nearby industrial estate, a few miles north of Ibiza Town. The site is famously hard to find. There is no grand approach; you climb through workaday landscape, turn onto a small lane, and arrive at a modest car park just below the monument. The effort is rewarded at the plinth, where the land falls away and Ibiza Town spreads out toward the sea below. The view is the reason the hill was chosen, a vantage from which the sheltered man's rescuers could always be seen watching over the town.

## What the Stone Remembers

Civil wars are usually remembered through their dead, their battles, and their grievances. This monument remembers a moment of mercy instead. In a season when neighbors turned on neighbors across Spain, a group of Ibizans decided to protect a single hunted man, and later decided that the deed deserved to be marked in a way that would outlast all of them. The Sacred Heart they carved is a religious image, but the story beneath it is entirely human: fear, risk, and the choice to help. More than seventy-five years on, the stone Christ still keeps watch, a memorial less to faith than to the people who acted on it.

## Flight Context

Sagrat Cor de Jesus stands at 38.941N, 1.422E, on a hilltop knoll in the Monte Cristo area roughly 3.5 miles north of Ibiza Town and about 5 nm north-northeast of Ibiza Airport (LEIB). From 1,500-2,500 feet the 23-meter statue reads as a distinct pale figure on high ground, best spotted in raking morning or late-afternoon light that throws its silhouette into relief. Use Ibiza Town and its harbor to the south as your orientation anchor, then track inland to the elevated ground behind the industrial estate. Summer visibility is typically excellent.
