# Pont Vell, Santa Eulària des Riu

An old stone bridge over Ibiza's only river, said to shelter a bottled pixie that demanded either work or food, and a man imprisoned in 1744 for claiming to own one.

**Location:** 38.9827, 1.5274

**Categories:** history, bridges, folklore, Roman, Ibiza, archaeology

## Introduction

Under the arches of this bridge, an old Ibizan legend insisted, grew a herb you could only gather at first light on the feast of Saint Juan. Pick it fast, before it vanished, seal it in a black bottle, and you would trap a pixie who could work miracles or ruin you. The Pont Vell spans the Riu de Santa Eulària, the only river on the island of Ibiza, on the western approach to the town of Santa Eulària des Riu. Modern traffic now uses the road bridge beside it; the old stone crossing carries only walkers, and its stories.

## The Only River on Ibiza

Ibiza is a dry island, and the Riu de Santa Eulària is its single true river, which makes the crossing that spans it more significant than its modest size suggests. The Pont Vell was once the only way into Santa Eulària from the west, an important strategic crossing that every traveler, trader, and army had to use. Today it stands quietly next to the modern road bridge, demoted to a footbridge while cars and trucks rumble across the newer span alongside. Stripped of its traffic, the old bridge has become something to look at rather than something to cross in a hurry.

## Roman Roots, Later Stone

The bridge's origins are considered Roman, though the story of Rome and Ibiza is unusual. The empire granted the island the status of a federated city, a civitas foederata, treating it as an ally rather than a conquered subject; Ibiza was never taken by force, and its culture drifted toward Roman ways gradually rather than under the boot. The stone structure visible today is much younger than that ancient foundation. Historians estimate the present bridge was probably built in the sixteenth or seventeenth century, a later crossing laid over a route that had already carried travelers for well over a thousand years.

## The Herb Beneath the Arches

The bridge's real fame is folkloric. Islanders told of a Familiar, an ugly, bad-tempered pixie who could be both helpful and ruinous, and whose appearance could be conjured only by a precise ritual. Beneath the Pont Vell's arches grew a flowering herb said to shelter these spirits. To capture one, you had to go under the bridge at first light on the day of the festival of Saint Juan, the twenty-fourth of June, and pick the flowers quickly, because they could appear and disappear in a glance. Sealed tightly in a black bottle, the Familiar could be kept and released whenever hard work needed doing fast and well.

## A Man and His Bottle

The bargain carried a catch. A Familiar wanted only two things, to work or to eat, and once freed it would cry out, "Soc es familiar, vull veina o menjar" - "I am a Familiar! I want work or food!" Give it no task and it would eat you out of house and home. However fanciful the tale, it collided with real power. In 1744 an unfortunate man from Santa Eulària was sent to prison by the Holy Office after he swore under oath that he owned a Familiar and knew many neighbors who kept them too. He was, in the end, a villager punished by the Inquisition for taking his island's folklore at its word.

## Flight Context

The Pont Vell sits at 38.983N, 1.527E on the western edge of Santa Eularia des Riu, on Ibiza's east coast, roughly 13 nm northeast of Ibiza Airport (LEIB). It is a small landmark, best appreciated at low altitude, 1,500-2,500 feet, in clear light. Look for the town at the mouth of the island's only river, marked from a distance by the hilltop fortress church of Puig de Missa on the bay's western side; the old and new bridges cross the riverbed just inland of the town. Summer visibility is typically excellent along this stretch of coast.
