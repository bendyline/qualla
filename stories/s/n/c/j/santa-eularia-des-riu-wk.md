# Santa Eulària des Riu

The only Ibizan town named for the island's only river, guarded by a fortress church and remembered for the fishermen who saved a shipwreck's survivors in 1913.

**Location:** 38.9838, 1.5291

**Categories:** towns, coastal, history, Ibiza, culture, Balearic Islands

## Introduction

On an island where water is scarce, one town took its name from a river. Santa Eularia des Riu, whose name literally means Santa Eularia of the River, sits on the southeastern coast of Ibiza beside the only true river the island has. It is the third largest town on Ibiza, home to about 36,000 people, and it has drawn a particular kind of visitor for generations: artists and writers looking for tranquility and anonymity rather than nightclubs. Above it all, a fortress church has kept watch over the bay since the fifteenth century.

## The Town of the River

Santa Eularia curls around a wide bay closed at its eastern end by the promontory of Punta Arabi. There the marina of Port Esportiu bustles with restaurants, shops, and bars, while the town's two beaches, clean and gently shelving, draw young families to their calm shallows. The place sits about 15 kilometers northeast of Ibiza Town, close enough to the island's engine of tourism but distinct in temperament. The streets run in a tidy grid from the Placa d'Espanya, with the pedestrianized Carrer Sant Vicent lined by tavernas and small restaurants, a town built to be walked rather than sped through.

## The Church on the Hill

The town's defining silhouette is Puig de Missa, the fifteenth-century hilltop fortress church that overlooks the entire bay from its western side. Its thick walls were not decorative. Churches on Ibiza were built to double as refuges when corsairs raided the coast, and Puig de Missa's bulk still reads as a stronghold as much as a sanctuary. Just below it stands Can Ros, a living museum that recreates how islanders lived for centuries before tourism arrived, a window into the hard, self-sufficient world that the fortress church was built to protect. At night the hillside glows with the lights of the houses that now climb its slopes.

## The Fishermen and the Wreck

In front of the town hall, an old building dating from 1795, stands a stone monument that the distant city of Palma, on Mallorca, erected in gratitude. On 17 January 1913, the steamboat Mallorca ran aground on a reef near the rocky inlet of Redona at Punta Arabi. The local fishermen of Santa Eularia put out into dangerous water and rescued the survivors. Palma raised the monument to thank and honor them, and it still faces the busy main street, Carrer Sant Jaume. It is easy to walk past, but it records a moment when ordinary islanders became, for one cold January day, the difference between drowning and rescue.

## Markets and Anonymity

Santa Eularia has long welcomed artists, writers, and wanderers seeking quiet, and its calendar keeps older rhythms alive too, from the Easter Procession to the centuries-old festival of the First Sunday in May, the Primer Diumenge de Maig. The area is also home to two of the island's most famous hippy markets. Punta Arabi, at Es Canar, spreads out every Wednesday from April through October; Las Dalias, near Sant Carles, runs every Saturday year-round and adds torchlit night markets in high summer. Between the fortress church, the fishermen's monument, and the market stalls, the town holds its many histories lightly, side by side.

## Flight Context

Santa Eularia des Riu lies at 38.984N, 1.529E on Ibiza's southeast coast, about 10 nm northeast of Ibiza Airport (LEIB) and 22 km from the field by road. From 2,000-3,500 feet the town is easy to identify: a wide bay backed by low apartment blocks, with the fortress church of Puig de Missa crowning a hill on the bay's western side and the Punta Arabi promontory closing it to the east. The mouth of the island's only river sits just west of town. Summer visibility along this coast is typically excellent; expect leisure-boat traffic around the marina.
