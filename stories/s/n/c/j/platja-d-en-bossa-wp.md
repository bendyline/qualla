# Platja d'en Bossa

Ibiza's longest beach, where a failed exhibition hall became the legendary club Space and the island's after-hours party was born almost by accident.

**Location:** 38.8930, 1.4110

**Categories:** beaches, nightlife, resorts, Ibiza, music, Balearic Islands

## Introduction

The building was supposed to host conferences. It failed at that, was sold off, and in 1987 reopened as a nightclub called Space, which went on to become one of the most famous rooms in electronic music. That reinvention says everything about Platja d'en Bossa, the resort strip south of Ibiza Town where the island's longest beach meets its loudest nightlife. By day it is 2.7 kilometers of sand and hotel towers; by night, for decades, it was where the world's biggest DJs came to play until well past dawn.

## The Longest Beach on the Island

Platja d'en Bossa runs for 2.7 kilometers, the longest stretch of beach anywhere on Ibiza, hemmed by large hotel complexes, restaurants, shops, and both low and high-rise buildings. It belongs to the municipality of Sant Josep de sa Talaia and sits roughly three kilometers south of Ibiza Town, with the island's main airport only about 3.2 kilometers away. The resort was built for the package-holiday market, drawing British, German, and Italian visitors in enormous numbers. In the summer months it becomes one of the busiest resorts on the island, a wall of sunbeds by day and a corridor of bass by night.

## A Failed Hall Becomes a Temple

Space occupied a building that had first been the island's conference and exhibition center, a venture that failed and was sold. In 1987 the new owners opened it as a nightclub, and over the following decades it earned a reputation as one of the so-called super-clubs, drawing electronic-music pilgrims from across Europe. It ran until its closure in 2016, ending a nearly thirty-year run. For a generation of clubbers, the name Space was shorthand for Ibiza itself, and the building's origin as a bland convention hall only sharpened the irony of what it became.

## The Accidental Terrace

The club's defining ritual began almost by accident. In the early 1990s, DJs inside Space started carrying their sessions out onto the open-air terrace next to the club, and so began the legendary after-hours parties that outlived the walls that housed them. The lineups over the years read like a roll call of the era's most influential selectors: Carl Cox, Paul Oakenfold, Sasha, Erick Morillo, Groove Armada, Danny Tenaglia, and Robbie Rivera all appeared here. What made the terrace special was the daylight; the party spilled past sunrise, and the sun became part of the show.

## The Sound Moves On

When Space closed, the resort's music did not. Ushuaia, another venue within Platja d'en Bossa, became a major stage for events, hosting broadcasts for BBC Radio 1 in Ibiza and helping shift the island's flagship parties into the open air of daytime pool sessions. Sankeys, too, set up here. The strip has become a place where the island's nightlife keeps reinventing its venues while keeping its habits, a corridor that has, for the better part of forty years, defined what people mean when they say they are going out in Ibiza.

## Flight Context

Platja d'en Bossa lies at 38.893N, 1.411E on Ibiza's southeast coast, only about 1.7 nm north of the runway at Ibiza Airport (LEIB) and roughly 1.5 nm south of Ibiza Town. From the air it reads as a long, straight, pale beach backed by a dense line of hotel blocks, immediately recognizable by its length and by its proximity to the airport. Because the field is so close, this stretch sits directly beneath arrival and departure paths; expect heavy commercial traffic in summer. Best viewed from 2,000-3,000 feet offshore, with the salt pans of Ses Salines just to the south.
