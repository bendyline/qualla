# Danakil Depression

A 200-kilometre valley 125 metres below sea level, where three of Earth's tectonic plates are pulling apart and a new ocean is slowly being born.

**Location:** 14.2417, 40.3000

**Categories:** geology, tectonics, volcanology, afar, ethiopia, eritrea

## Introduction

Eleven million years ago the Danakil microplate started rotating counterclockwise away from the African continent. It is still rotating. The extension between the Nubian plate and the Danakil (or Arrata) microplate, measured by GPS, runs at about 18 millimetres per year in the south of the depression and 8 in the north. The numbers are small. The result is large: a valley 200 kilometres long and 50 wide, 125 metres below sea level, where three lava-rich plates meet at a triple junction and a new ocean is being constructed in slow motion. This is the Danakil Depression, shared between Ethiopia and Eritrea, third-lowest point on the African continent, and one of only two places on Earth where a mid-ocean ridge can be studied on land.

## Triple Junction

The Afar Depression - of which the Danakil is the northern part - sits where the Red Sea spreading ridge meets the Gulf of Aden ridge and the East African Rift. All three are seafloor spreading systems. Usually they are under water. Here, because of the rate of sediment supply and the regional geology, they have emerged onto land. The only other place on Earth where you can see this is Iceland. A mantle plume - the Afar hotspot - sits slightly to the south of the Danakil Depression itself. The Erta Ale Range, the chain of basaltic volcanoes that runs along the depression's south, sits directly over this plume's upwelling. Petrological studies of the basalts show that in this part of the rift there is effectively no continental crust beneath the volcanoes anymore. The plates have separated enough that what is forming at the surface is transitional crust - not quite the normal basaltic oceanic crust yet, but close.

## A Sea That Was

In the Middle and Late Pleistocene, the Red Sea flooded into the depression at least four times during periods of high global sea level. Each flooding laid down marine sediments - halite, potash, carbonates - sometimes kilometres thick. The last such flooding ended approximately 130,000 years ago. What you see on the surface today is mostly those evaporite layers, partly covered by basaltic lava flows from the Erta Ale range and broken by tectonic faults along the northern margin. The western edge receives ephemeral rivers from the Ethiopian Plateau - they carry coarse conglomerates and build alluvial fans along the basin margin. Everywhere you look, you see a landscape that has been repeatedly flooded, baked, covered in lava, and cracked open by tectonic extension, layered together and reshaped until the record is as confused as it is clear.

## Dallol and the Hot Springs

In the northern part of the depression, two volcanoes stand out: Maraho and Dallol. The Dallol salt dome, at 125 metres below sea level, is the only volcanic crater below sea level on land. Its phreatic eruption in 1926 formed a 30-metre-diameter crater inside the dome. Around Dallol, hydrothermal springs discharge brine and acid fluids with pH values far below zero. Gaet'ale Pond nearby, with 43 percent salinity, is the saltiest body of water on Earth. The colours - green, yellow, ochre, red - come from inorganic iron chemistry, not from life. Studies published in 2019 found no microbial activity in the most extreme ponds, though nearby salt plains are rich in halophilic archaea. In 2022 the International Union of Geological Sciences included the Danakil Rift depression in its first list of 100 geological heritage sites, citing its role in demonstrating 'the ongoing birth of an ocean witnessed through tectonics and volcanism in an extreme evaporite arid environment.'

## Erta Ale, Alive

The Erta Ale Range includes multiple shield volcanoes and stratovolcanoes - Gada Ale, Alu, Dalafilla, Bora Ale, Erta Ale itself, and Ale Bagu. These are the ones that have built most of the depression's southern basaltic crust. Erta Ale, 613 metres above the surrounding plain, hosts a lava lake in its summit caldera that has been continuously active since at least 1906. This is one of perhaps five persistent lava lakes on Earth - and the longest-lasting of them. Looking down into that crater is looking at the same kind of process that is building the mid-ocean ridge below the Atlantic right now, visible because in the Danakil there is no ocean on top to hide it.

## The Salt Lakes

Beyond the volcanic terrain, the depression holds several endorheic saline lakes - bodies of water with no outlet. Lake Afdera, at 120 metres below sea level, is kept alive by thermal springs. Lake Karum and Lake Bakili sit in volcano-tectonic depressions. The Afar people cut salt from the pan at Karum in the cool hours before dawn, load it onto camel caravans, and walk it up into the highlands. The caravan trade has been going on for centuries, probably thousands of years. It will continue until either the salt runs out - unlikely, given how much the Red Sea left behind - or the new ocean finally breaks through, which is perhaps ten million years away. Either way, it will outlast most political arrangements currently in place above it.

## Flight Context

The Danakil Depression spans roughly 14-15N, 40-41E, mostly 100-125m below sea level. The Afar Region is in northeastern Ethiopia; the northern tip reaches into Eritrea. Nearest airport is Mekele (HAMK/MQX) 250 km southwest at 2,250m elevation, or Asmara (HHAS/ASM) roughly 400 km north. Surface temperatures above 45C in summer significantly affect aircraft performance. Airspace is remote with minimal ATC. Security conditions in Tigray and Afar have been sensitive since the 2020-2022 conflict. Recommended overflight altitude 8,000-10,000 ft AGL to appreciate the scale of the rift.
