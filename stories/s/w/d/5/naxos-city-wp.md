# Naxos (city)

The main town of Naxos greets arriving ferries with a giant marble doorway that frames the sea, and climbs behind it through a Venetian castle that is still a living medieval town.

**Location:** 37.1019, 25.3794

**Categories:** Naxos, Cities in ancient Greece, Greek city-states, Members of the Delian League, Populated places in Naxos (regional unit), Populated places in the ancient Aegean islands

## Introduction

Every ferry into Naxos is greeted by a door to nowhere. On a rocky islet at the harbour mouth stands the Portara - two marble jambs and a lintel, each block weighing about twenty tons, framing nothing but the Aegean sky beyond. It is the surviving fragment of a temple of Apollo begun around 530 BC and never finished, and it has become the emblem of the town the Greeks simply call Chora, 'the town.' Behind it climbs a white town of nearly 8,900 people, layered like the history that built it.

## The Door That Was Never a Temple

The tyrant Lygdamis dreamed big. Having seized power on Naxos around 546 BC with the backing of the Athenian strongman Peisistratos, he set out to give his island a temple to rival the greatest in Greece. Work began on the islet of Palatia, just off the harbour. Then around 524 BC Lygdamis was driven from power, and the temple stopped where it stood. Everything was eventually carted off or repurposed except the colossal marble gateway, too heavy and too high to move. So the Portara remained, a doorway leading only to the horizon. At sunset, when the light falls through it and the sea turns to copper, it explains in an instant why this is the most famous landmark on the island - and a quiet cousin to the unfinished giant of Apollonas in the north.

## A Castle You Can Still Walk Into

Above the harbour rises the Kastro, and it is not a ruin but a town. Founded in 1207 by the Venetian Marco Sanudo, who had just conquered Naxos and crowned himself Duke of the Archipelago, the fortress enclosed an entire medieval settlement on the old acropolis - the duke's palace, the Catholic cathedral, the Ursuline school, and the tall stone houses of the Latin lords. Remarkably, it survives largely intact, and some descendants of those Venetian families still live behind its walls. You enter through the Trani Porta, the 'Great Gate,' into lanes where family crests are still carved above the doors. The ruined Tower of Sanudo, once more than thirty metres tall, marks the heart of it. To wander the Kastro is to walk through the seven centuries when foreign dukes ruled these islands from this single hill.

## Bourgos, Grotta, and the Deep Past

Below the castle the old town divides into quarters that still wear their old roles. Bourgos, north of the Kastro, was the merchants' and artisans' district - a maze of arched passages, small squares and white Cycladic houses. South of the fortress, Nio Chorio, the 'New Village,' was where poorer farmers and labourers lived, on wider and gentler streets. And along the northern shore lies Grotta, named from the Venetian word for cave. Here the past surfaces literally: this was the site of the Mycenaean city of Naxos, and remains of it can still be seen along the coast, some of them now slipped beneath the waterline. Long before Venice, long before the Persians, people were already living and building on this exact shore.

## A Town That Keeps Being Lived In

Naxos has never been a museum-piece. After the long Venetian and Ottoman centuries, the island joined the Greek state in 1832. In the 1920s a refugee district, Prosfygika, grew to house Greeks fleeing western Turkey after the 1922 catastrophe in Asia Minor. From the 1970s, tourism drove a wave of building south of the old town, all of it kept to the white Cycladic style by local rule. Today the seafront promenade, the Paralia, fills each evening with strollers; ferries crowd the port; summer traffic snarls the lanes; and recent dry years have forced desalination plants to keep the taps running. The Portara still stands watch over all of it, framing the same sea it has framed for two and a half thousand years.

## Flight Context

Chora sits at 37.10194 N, 25.37944 E, on the northwest coast of Naxos, the largest of the Cyclades. From the air, look for the white town climbing from a busy harbour to the fortified Kastro on its hill, with the small islet of Palatia and its lone marble gate, the Portara, guarding the port entrance. Naxos Island National Airport (LGNX) lies about 3 km south of the town centre, with daily links to Athens. Recommended viewing altitude 1,500-4,000 ft for the townscape and harbour; the broad sweep of Agios Georgios beach runs south of the centre as a clear visual marker. Aegean visibility is excellent in summer, though the meltemi wind can make the approach gusty.
