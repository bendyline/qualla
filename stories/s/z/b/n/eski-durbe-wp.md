# Eski Dürbe

A silent domed tomb in Bakhchysarai keeps a secret nobody has solved: no one knows who lies inside, or even for certain when it was built.

**Location:** 44.7472, 33.8850

**Categories:** history, architecture, crimea, monument

## Introduction

Someone important is buried here. That much the architecture insists upon - the careful stonework, the domed chamber, the private courtyard reachable only from inside the tomb itself, as if the dead wished to receive mourners on their own terms. But who? After centuries and more than one restoration campaign, no one can say. Eski Dürbe, whose name means simply "old tomb" in Crimean Tatar, sits on the left bank of the river in Bakhchysarai, 180 meters southeast of the Khan's Palace, holding onto its anonymity with a stubbornness that has outlasted every scholar who came to solve it.

## The Argument Over Its Age

Even the tomb's birthday is contested. Some sources place its construction in the fourteenth century, during the era of the Golden Horde - which would make it more than a hundred years older than the famous Khan's Palace nearby, and one of the oldest monuments in the town. But in 2013, an archaeological expedition led by Emil Seidaliev set out specifically to date the structure, and its stratigraphic observations told a different story, pointing instead to the seventeenth century. Three hundred years hang in the balance between those two readings. The tomb, meanwhile, offers no help. It has never been significantly excavated, and so the question of its age remains exactly what it has always been: an open one.

## Yellow Stone Among the White

What sets Eski Dürbe apart, you can see with your own eyes. Of the ten türbe - Islamic mausolea - that survive in the region today, this is the only one built of local yellow stone rather than the white limestone used everywhere else. It wears its difference plainly, its color warmer and earthier than its pale neighbors. The building is austere, almost stern, with little in the way of decoration; its appeal lies in the quality of the masonry rather than any ornament. The structure is deliberately asymmetrical, two parts of unequal height joined together: the domed tomb itself, and a walled courtyard along its southern face that once, it is believed, held a fountain.

## A Cemetery Erased by Time

The tomb did not always stand alone. Nineteenth-century drawings and photographs from the start of the twentieth century show it ringed by a cemetery, gravestones clustered around its walls - a whole community of the dead that has since vanished from view. The private courtyard, sealed off from the outside and enterable only through the tomb, has led archaeologists to read Eski Dürbe as a family burial place, or a quiet space where the living came to sit with their losses. Whoever rested here mattered enough to be given a garden of grief and a doorway that only mourners could pass. That intention survives even where the names do not.

## Keeping the Roof On

For a monument nobody has fully studied, Eski Dürbe has been well tended. In 1927, under the then-director of the Bakhchysarai Palace-Museum, workers repaired the dome, restored lost elements of the türbe and its courtyard, and hung iron doors. Renovation returned in 1965, rebuilding fallen sections of the courtyard walls and sealing the dome with a cement screed; in the mid-1990s, sheets of galvanized iron went over the dome again. Today the tomb is protected as a monument of architectural and historical significance. Each generation, it seems, has decided the old tomb was worth saving - even without knowing whose memory, exactly, they were preserving.

## Flight Context

Eski Dürbe stands at 44.747 N, 33.885 E, in the Churuk-Su river valley of Bakhchysarai, just southeast of the Khan's Palace. From about 3,500-5,000 feet, trace the green valley cutting between limestone ridges; the palace complex is the largest cluster of structures, with the tomb close by on the river's left bank and the cave-city escarpment of Chufut-Kale rising to the east. Simferopol International (UKFF) lies roughly 20 nm northeast.
