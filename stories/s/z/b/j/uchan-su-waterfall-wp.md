# Uchan-su Waterfall

The highest waterfall in Crimea, plunging 98 meters down the forested slopes below Ai-Petri, thunderous with snowmelt in spring and named by the Crimean Tatars for the way its waters seem to fly.

**Location:** 44.4925, 34.0922

**Categories:** Waterfall, Nature, Crimea, Mountains

## Introduction

The Crimean Tatars gave this place a name that is really a description of motion: Uchan-su, flying water. Watch it in April, when the snows are melting off the peaks above Yalta, and you understand the poetry of it. The water does not so much fall as launch itself off the cliff, breaking apart in the air, scattering into mist and spray long before it reaches the rocks below. This is the tallest waterfall in Crimea, and on its loudest days it is less a stream than an event, a white ribbon flung from the mountains toward the distant blue of the Black Sea.

## Flying Water

Uchan-su drops ninety-eight meters down the southern slopes of the Crimean Mountains, making it the highest waterfall on the peninsula. It sits at an altitude of about 390 meters, roughly seven kilometers from the resort city of Yalta, positioned as if halfway up the road toward the great limestone crest of Ai-Petri. The water originates high on one of the Ai-Petri summits and tumbles down toward the shallows below. The Crimean Tatar name captures exactly what the eye sees: from a distance the fall looks less like a solid column of water than like something airborne, a spray of white suspended against the dark green of the mountain forest, forever falling and never quite landing.

## A Creature of the Seasons

Uchan-su is a waterfall of moods, and its character shifts entirely with the calendar. In spring it comes into its full power, gorged with meltwater as the winter snows dissolve off the high peaks of Ai-Petri and rush downhill. This is when the fall roars, when the spray drifts across the trail and the noise fills the whole ravine. Come in the dry heat of late summer, however, and the same cliff may show only a thin trickle, or nothing at all, the flying water grounded until the rains and snows return. That seasonality is part of its appeal: to catch Uchan-su at its thundering best is to time your visit to the mountain's own rhythms.

## The Mountain Above Yalta

The waterfall belongs to a landscape of dramatic verticals. Below lies Yalta and the warm subtropical coast; above rises Ai-Petri, the saw-toothed peak that crowns this stretch of the Crimean Mountains. Uchan-su hangs between the two, part of the forested slopes that climb from the sea to the summit. For generations of visitors, the short journey up from Yalta to the falls has been one of the classic excursions of the south coast, a chance to trade the beaches for the cool of the mountain forest. Surrounded by pine and the sound of moving water, the fall is a reminder that just uphill from the resorts lies a wilder, greener Crimea.

## Flight Context

Located at 44.493 degrees N, 34.092 degrees E, on the mountain slopes about 7 km southwest of Yalta and below the Ai-Petri massif. Best viewed from around 4,000 to 6,000 feet, where the waterfall's ravine cuts through the forested slope between the coast and the Ai-Petri crest; it is most visible in spring when flow is heaviest. The nearest major airport is Simferopol (UKFF), roughly 50 km to the north.
