# Principality of Theodoro

A tiny Greek principality in the Crimean mountains that outlived the Byzantine Empire itself, becoming the very last territorial remnant of the Roman world before it fell to the Ottomans in December 1475.

**Location:** 44.5833, 33.8000

**Categories:** History, Byzantine, Crimea, Medieval

## Introduction

When Constantinople fell to the Ottomans in 1453, most of the world assumed the Roman Empire had finally died. It had not, quite. Far away on the foothills of the Crimean Mountains, a small Greek principality still flew the double-headed eagle and still called its prince the Lord of Theodoro. For another twenty-two years this mountain state endured, a last flickering ember of an empire that traced its story back to the legendary founding of Rome in 753 BC. The Principality of Theodoro was the final chapter of Roman civilization, and it ended not on the Bosphorus but on a Crimean plateau.

## The Last Rump of Rome

Also known as Gothia, Theodoro grew up among the descendants of the Crimean Goths and the Greek-speaking Christians of the peninsula. After the Crimean coast drifted from Byzantine control in the late twelfth century, and after the Mongols swept through in 1238 and imposed tribute, real power was left largely in local hands. A Prince of Gothia is first recorded in the early fourteenth century. The name Theodoro, perhaps honoring the warrior saints Theodore Stratelates and Theodore Tiro, or perhaps a corruption of the old regional name Dory, first appears in a Greek inscription around 1361 and stuck. By the 1420s the ruler styled himself Lord of the city of Theodoro and the Maritime Region, while his own people affectionately called their homeland Theodoritsi, little Theodoro.

## A Crossroads of Peoples

Theodoro was small, but it was not simple. Its people were a mosaic of Greeks, Crimean Goths, Alans, Circassians, Bulgars, Cumans, Kipchaks, and others, most of them Orthodox Christians who had been drawn into Greek culture. Greek was the official language. The principality's art and architecture were essentially Byzantine, its churches painted with saints in the imperial style, though its mountain fortresses also bore local and Genoese touches, and its carved marble slabs mixed Byzantine, Italian, and Tatar decoration. Squeezed between the Genoese trading colonies along the coast and the Golden Horde to the north, Theodoro paid tribute to the Horde, jostled endlessly with Genoa over access to the sea, and even built its own port, Avlita, when it lost its harbors. It was a survivor's state, small and stubborn and improbably cosmopolitan.

## Dynasty and Downfall

The principality's final years turned on a family quarrel. Its ruling house, a branch of the Greek Gabras dynasty, had married into the highest Byzantine bloodlines; one princess, Maria of Gothia, became the wife of the last emperor of Trebizond. But as the Ottoman threat grew, Prince Isaac leaned toward accommodation with the sultan, and in 1475 his brother Alexander overthrew him with the backing of Stephen the Great of Moldavia. The change came too late. That autumn, having already taken the Genoese port of Caffa, the Ottoman commander Gedik Ahmet Pasha laid siege to Theodoro's mountain capital. Three hundred Wallachian soldiers helped defend it through five major assaults, but famine did what the walls could not. At the end of December 1475, the city surrendered.

## The End of Roman Civilization

The terms of surrender were not kept. According to Ottoman chroniclers, the city's leaders were carried to Constantinople and executed. Prince Alexander was beheaded, his young son forced to convert to Islam, and his wife and daughters taken into the sultan's household. One of the churches was turned into a mosque, and a prayer was said for the sultan in the place where Christian liturgy had sounded for centuries. These were real people, a ruling family and a community erased in a matter of weeks. Yet something of Theodoro lingered: a Greek inscription shows that as late as 1503 the survivors still spoke Greek beneath the plateau. With the fall of Theodoro, the very last political remnant of the Roman Empire vanished from the earth, more than two millennia after Rome began.

## Flight Context

Centered on its capital at roughly 44.583 degrees N, 33.800 degrees E, on the Mangup plateau in the Crimean Mountains east of Sevastopol. Best appreciated from about 4,000 to 6,000 feet, where the flat-topped fortress mountain that served as the principality's capital rises above the wooded foothills. The nearest major airport is Simferopol (UKFF), about 45 km to the northeast.
