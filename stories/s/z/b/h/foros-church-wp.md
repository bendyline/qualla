# Foros Church

A jewel-box Orthodox church perched on a 400-meter Crimean cliff, built by a Moscow tea trader in gratitude for a tsar's survival.

**Location:** 44.4044, 33.7884

**Categories:** Churches, Crimea, Architecture, Landmarks

## Introduction

It seems to float. The Church of Christ's Resurrection stands alone on the lip of a 400-meter cliff near Crimea's southernmost tip, its domes catching the sun with nothing behind them but sky and the far blue plate of the Black Sea. Travelers on the road to the Baidarsky Pass round a bend and there it is, impossibly placed, a small ornate church balanced on the edge of the world. It was built not for a village or a monastery but as a private act of thanks, one wealthy man's answer to a disaster that very nearly changed the course of an empire.

## A Thank-Offering in Stone

The church owes its existence to a train crash. In 1888 the imperial train of Emperor Alexander III derailed catastrophically at Borki, and the royal family survived, according to the story that spread afterward, because the tsar himself braced the collapsing roof of the dining car long enough for his family to escape. The near-miss shook the empire, and across Russia the deliverance was marked with prayers, chapels, and monuments. Here on the Crimean coast, a Moscow tea trader named Alexander Kuznetsov, who owned land above the village of Foros, chose to commemorate the tsar's survival with a church of his own, and he chose to put it where no one could possibly ignore it. Perched on the edge of a 400-meter drop, the building turned a moment of imperial fortune into a permanent landmark, visible for miles along the shore and seemingly balanced between earth and air. It was piety and spectacle at once, a private gift made deliberately public by the sheer audacity of its placement.

## A Beautiful Contradiction

The man Kuznetsov hired was Nikolai Chagin, a celebrated architect from Vilnius, and what Chagin produced defies easy description. The church is a deliberate blend of styles that arguably should not agree with one another: the exuberant Baroque associated with the great imperial architect Rastrelli, the patterned nationalism of Russian Revival, and the domes and arches of Byzantine Revival, all combined in a single small structure. One early observer called the mixture bizarre, though by no means unattractive, and that verdict still fits. It should be a muddle. Instead, set against the vastness of sea and cliff, the little building's busy silhouette reads as pure delight, a piece of jewelry set on the edge of a precipice. It was consecrated on 4 October 1892, in a ceremony attended by Konstantin Pobedonostsev, one of the most powerful figures in the Russian church and state.

## Weddings on the Edge

The church's dramatic setting has given it a second life as one of the most sought-after wedding locations on the coast, and its guest lists have run to the famous. In July 2003 a metropolitan of the Orthodox Church married the politician Viktor Medvedchuk and the television host Oksana Marchenko within its walls, and the actress Anastasia Zavorotnyuk and the figure skater Peter Tchernyshev also chose to wed here. It is easy to see the appeal. To exchange vows in a church suspended between mountain and sea, with the Black Sea filling the whole horizon beyond the domes, is to borrow a little of the building's own theatrical grandeur, to make a private promise on the most public and beautiful stage the coast can offer. The church has not had an easy history. It weathered the upheavals of the twentieth century, was returned to the Orthodox Church, and went through several restoration campaigns to bring back its color and its gilding. Yet through all of it the essential image has never changed: a small, improbable, jewel-bright building on the lip of a precipice, still keeping its centuries-old vigil over the sea, a monument to one family's survival that has come to feel like a promise of endurance itself.

## Flight Context

Foros Church sits at roughly 44.404 N, 33.788 E, on a cliff above the village of Foros near the southern tip of Crimea. Best viewed from low altitude in clear air; its isolated cliff-edge position and clustered domes make it a striking and unmistakable landmark against the coastline, with the Baidarsky Pass and coastal mountains rising behind. Nearest major field is Simferopol International (UKFF), well to the northeast.
