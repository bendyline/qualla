# Yusupov Palace (Crimea)

A romantic seaside palace above Yalta that passed from the fabulously wealthy Yusupov family to Joseph Stalin, who made it his favorite retreat during the world-shaping Yalta Conference.

**Location:** 44.4325, 34.0894

**Categories:** Palace, History, Crimea, Architecture

## Introduction

In the autumn of 1913, in a palace tucked among cypress and exotic gardens above the Black Sea, a young prince named Felix Yusupov celebrated his engagement to the tsar's only niece. Three years later he would help murder Grigori Rasputin. But here, in the family's Crimean retreat at Koreiz, the future still looked golden. The palace his parents had built was barely four years old, its Renaissance and Roman lines fresh against the sea, its cellars stocked with wine and its terraces heavy with the scent of imported blooms. It was a house made for a world that was about to vanish.

## A Palace for the Richest Family in Russia

The Yusupov Palace was completed in 1909 for Prince Felix Yusupov-Soumorokov-Elston and his wife, Princess Zinaida Yusupova, sole heiress to what was reputed to be the largest private fortune in Imperial Russia. To design it, they hired Nikolay Krasnov, the same architect who would later shape the imperial Livadia Palace just along the coast. Krasnov gave them something between a villa and a fortress of leisure, its style blending Renaissance Revival and Roman Revival. Around it he laid out a romantic park planted with exotic species, and beneath it lay a wine cellar first established by the vintner Prince Lev Galitzine in the nineteenth century. For a family whose wealth was measured in palaces and art collections, the Koreiz house was a private paradise on the warm southern shore.

## The Prince Who Killed Rasputin

The younger Felix Yusupov, whose engagement was celebrated in these rooms in 1913, married Princess Irina, the emperor's only niece, in February 1914. Within a few years the golden life of the Russian aristocracy would collapse entirely. In December 1916, Felix became one of the conspirators who assassinated Grigori Rasputin, the mystic whose grip on the imperial family had scandalized the court. The killing took place far away, at the family's Moika Palace in St. Petersburg, but its roots ran through this same charmed and doomed generation. When the Russian Revolution came in 1917, the Yusupovs' Crimean idyll ended. The palace was nationalized, and the family that had built it scattered into exile abroad.

## Stalin's Favorite Dacha

Under the new order, the palace found an unlikely resident. It became Joseph Stalin's favorite dacha, a private retreat he returned to over the years. In February 1945, with the Second World War in its final months, the eyes of the world turned to this stretch of Crimean coast for the Yalta Conference, the meeting where Stalin, Roosevelt, and Churchill carved out the shape of the postwar world. The formal sessions were held nearby at Livadia, but Stalin made his base here in the old Yusupov house. The rooms where a young prince had once toasted his engagement now hosted the Soviet leader as he negotiated the fate of nations. Few buildings hold such a sharp collision of Old World glamour and twentieth-century power.

## A Guarded House Above the Sea

The palace has remained tied to power ever since. From 1991 to 2014 it was held by the President of Ukraine. Following the events of 2014, it was designated for the use of the President of the Russian Federation, becoming a special state complex once again closed to the wider public. That guarded status is fitting for a house that has spent its whole existence at the intersection of privilege and history. Set among its exotic gardens above the shimmer of the Black Sea, the Yusupov Palace still keeps its secrets, a beautiful survivor of a vanished aristocratic Crimea and a silent witness to one of the most consequential meetings of the modern age.

## Flight Context

Located at 44.4325 degrees N, 34.0894 degrees E, in the coastal village of Koreiz just west of Yalta. Best viewed from around 3,000 to 5,000 feet, where the palace and its wooded park stand out against the Black Sea shoreline, with the crest of Ai-Petri rising behind. The nearest major airport is Simferopol (UKFF), about 55 km to the north.
