# Gaspra

A white-named spa town on the Crimean coast that gave the world an asteroid, sheltered Leo Tolstoy in his final years, and raised one of the most influential Crimean Tatar thinkers of the modern age.

**Location:** 44.4361, 34.1106

**Categories:** Town, History, Crimea, Coast

## Introduction

There is an asteroid, 200 million kilometers from here, that carries this small town's name. It orbits between Mars and Jupiter, a battered chunk of rock called 951 Gaspra, and it borrowed its name from a modest spa town on the warm southern shore of Crimea. Down here on Earth, Gaspra is a place of palaces and vineyards, of a name that simply means white, taken from the Greek word aspro. It has hosted a Russian literary giant and produced a Crimean Tatar visionary, and for all its quiet size, its reach has stretched from the Black Sea coast clear into the asteroid belt.

## The White Town by the Sea

Gaspra sits on the Black Sea coast just west of Yalta, an urban-type settlement in the Yalta area with the gentle, humid Cfb climate that made this coast a favorite of holidaymakers. Its name comes from Greek, aspro, meaning white, a legacy of the medieval Greek colonization of this shore. The site's history runs deeper still: the Roman fort of Charax, the largest Roman military base in Crimea, once stood on the nearby ridge of Ai-Todor. But Gaspra as a settlement is younger, known by name only since the mid-eighteenth century. After the Crimean Khanate was absorbed into the Russian Empire in 1783, the lands here were parceled out to Russian aristocrats, including members of the imperial family, who dotted the coast with their private palaces.

## Tolstoy's Last Coast

In 1901 and 1902, the aging Leo Tolstoy came to Gaspra to convalesce. The author of War and Peace and Anna Karenina, by then a towering and controversial figure, spent months on this coast as his health faltered, drawn like so many others to the mild Crimean air. Nearby rose one of Crimea's most famous silhouettes: Swallow's Nest, the tiny castle perched on the cliff edge of Cape Ai-Todor, built in 1911 and 1912 for the Baku oil baron Baron Steingel and nicknamed the castle of love. Between the ancient Roman ruins, the fairy-tale castle, and the great novelist's sickbed, Gaspra gathered an unlikely density of stories along its short stretch of shore.

## The Educator Who Awakened a People

Gaspra's most significant namesake was Ismail Gasprinski, the Crimean Tatar intellectual, educator, and publisher whose very surname traces to his father's ancestral village here, though Gasprinski himself was born nearby in Avcıköy, outside Bakhchysarai. Working in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, Gasprinski became one of the most influential reformers in the Turkic-speaking Muslim world, championing modern education and a shared cultural awakening. The Crimean Tatars were long the heart of this land. Census records tell the story of a changing population: in 1897 the great majority of local residents were Muslim, described in the era's language as Mohammedans, and as late as the first Soviet census of 1926, Crimean Tatars still made up the clear majority of Gaspra's inhabitants. Gasprinski's legacy remains one of the town's proudest claims.

## War, Resort, and Renewal

The twentieth century pressed hard on Gaspra. During the German-Soviet war, the town lay under Nazi occupation from November 1941 to April 1944, a grim interruption to coastal life. Yet Gaspra recovered, and the 1960s and 1970s became its golden age as a resort, when sanatoriums and holidaymakers filled the shore and the town effectively merged with neighboring Koreiz and the resort strip of Miskhor. Today Gaspra is a place layered with time, Roman stone, imperial palaces, a poet's retreat, a reformer's birthplace, all gathered on a sunlit coast whose name means white and whose fame, improbably, reaches all the way to the stars.

## Flight Context

Located at 44.436 degrees N, 34.111 degrees E, on the Black Sea coast just west of Yalta near Cape Ai-Todor. Best viewed from around 3,000 to 5,000 feet, where the coastal town, the Ai-Todor headland, and the tiny Swallow's Nest castle on its cliff are all visible below. The nearest major airport is Simferopol (UKFF), roughly 55 km to the north.
