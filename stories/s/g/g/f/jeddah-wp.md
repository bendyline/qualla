# Jeddah

The city motto translates as Jeddah is different, and the claim holds: on a coast that is supposed to be Saudi Arabia, here is a Red Sea port with coral-stone houses, Henry Moore sculptures in the roundabouts, and a fountain 312 meters high.

**Location:** 21.5400, 39.1700

**Categories:** Cities in Saudi Arabia, Red Sea ports, World Heritage Sites in Saudi Arabia, Hejaz

## Introduction

Jeddah Ghair, the city motto says in Arabic: Jeddah is different. You notice it first in the food, where fish and seafood dominate rather than the inland cuisine of lamb and rice. You notice it in the architecture of Al-Balad, the old town where coral-stone merchant houses lean against each other with wooden mashrabiya screens carved into their facades. You notice it in the roundabouts of the new city, where Henry Moore, Joan Miró, Alexander Calder, and Jean Arp sculptures sit where statues of people would be forbidden. Jeddah has been the gateway to Mecca for almost 1,400 years, but gateway is the wrong word for what it became: an open-faced port city on a kingdom known for its closedness.

## The Port of the Pilgrim

In 647 AD, the third caliph Uthman ibn Affan designated Jeddah as the port for Mecca, replacing the older port of Al Shoaib. From that moment on, Jeddah's job was to receive the world's Muslims. Pilgrims came on sailing dhows from India and East Africa, from Southeast Asia and the Levant, from wherever the monsoon winds could carry them. The Yemeni Quda'a tribe may have founded the settlement as a fishing village around 115 BC, after the collapse of the Marib Dam scattered them northward. The folk belief is that Jeddah takes its name from jaddah, Arabic for grandmother, and locates the Tomb of Eve here, the grandmother of humanity. The Moroccan traveler Ibn Battuta, who passed through around 1330, wrote the name as Jiddah. T. E. Lawrence spelled it three different ways on the first page of Revolt in the Desert.

## The City That Built a Wall

When Vasco da Gama rounded the Cape in 1497 and pushed into the Indian Ocean, terror followed him. The Portuguese attacked Muslim pilgrim fleets from India to the Red Sea. The Sultan of Egypt, Al-Ashraf Qansuh al-Ghawri, assembled a fleet of 50 vessels under a governor called Hussein the Kurd. Forced labor built Jeddah's first city wall as a harbor of refuge from the Portuguese. After the Ottomans conquered Egypt in 1517, they rebuilt the walls in stone, with six watchtowers, six gates, and a small garrison castle called the Qishla of Jeddah. The Portuguese tried to attack again in 1541 and were repelled. Parts of those walls still survive in Al-Balad. So does the Christian cemetery where Portuguese soldiers were buried, tucked inside the old city, referred to locally as the Christian Graves.

## Al-Balad and the Coral Houses

Inside the old walls, Jeddah was divided into haras, districts named for their directions and residents: Harrat Al-Mathloum in the northeast, Harrat Al-Sham in the north, Harrat Al-Yemen in the south, Harrat Al-Bahar on the seafront. Its most famous building is Bait Nasseef, built in 1881 for Omar Nasseef Efendi, governor of Jeddah, and later the royal residence of King Abdulaziz after he took the city. The houses are built of blocks of coral hauled from the Red Sea, plastered over, then faced with the wooden lattice screens that let breezes through while keeping prying eyes out. The Al-Shafi'i mosque has a minaret dating to the 13th century. The Mosque of Uthman bin Affan is called the Ebony Mosque for its two ebony pillars; Ibn Battuta and Ibn Jubayr both mentioned it in their writings. A 1982 fire destroyed some of the old town, but enough survived that UNESCO granted World Heritage status in 2014, and in 2019 Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman ordered the restoration of 50 historical buildings.

## Oil, Art, and Open Air

Jeddah lost its royal role when Riyadh became the capital of unified Saudi Arabia, but its commercial importance grew. During the oil boom of the late 1970s, Mayor Mohamed Said Farsi led an unusual civic project: importing modern sculpture for the city's public spaces. Islamic tradition discourages depictions of living creatures, but abstract and object sculpture flourished. Today, drivers in Jeddah pass works by Henry Moore, César Baldaccini, Calder, Miró, Hans Arp, and Victor Vasarely, along with a giant geometry set, a huge block of concrete pierced by protruding cars, and Aref Rayess's monumental Swords of God at the Tahlia Street roundabout. King Fahd's Fountain, commissioned in the 1980s, shoots water 312 meters into the air, the highest water jet in the world according to Guinness. You can see it from most of the city.

## Cost of a Modern City

Jeddah today has 3.75 million people, ranks second in Saudi Arabia after Riyadh, hosts the administrative headquarters of the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, and serves as the country's commercial center. But the modernization has had costs. In October 2021, Saudi authorities initiated a large-scale demolition and eviction program for the Jeddah Central Project, part of Vision 2030. The demolitions affected 558,000 people in more than 60 neighborhoods. Amnesty International documented cases of residents notified 24 hours before eviction, others between one and six weeks. Official media characterized the neighborhoods as crime-ridden and populated by undocumented immigrants. In January 2022, a compensation scheme was announced covering 47 percent of the evicted. For hundreds of thousands of poorer Jeddawis, many of them foreign workers and their families, the modern city that rose around them was not for them.

## Flight Context

Jeddah lies on the Red Sea coast of Saudi Arabia at 21.5°N, 39.2°E, in the Hejaz region. King Abdulaziz International Airport (OEJN) is the primary gateway, about 20 km north of the city center. Jeddah is the principal airport serving Mecca, 65 km east. Terrain is flat coastal plain rising to the Hijaz Mountains to the east. Summer temperatures routinely exceed 40°C; high humidity from the Red Sea makes afternoon operations uncomfortable. The city is home to the 250-meter Jeddah TV Tower and the Jeddah Tower currently under construction 20 km north.
