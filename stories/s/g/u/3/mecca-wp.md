# Mecca

In a narrow valley 70 kilometers inland from the Red Sea, the holiest city in Islam is also one of the ten most-visited cities in the world, triply populated for one week each year by pilgrims from every nation on Earth.

**Location:** 21.4228, 39.8258

**Categories:** Cities in Saudi Arabia, Holy cities, Mecca Province, Hajj, Hejaz

## Introduction

In 2023, Mecca received more than 10.8 million international visitors. That placed it among the ten most-visited cities on the planet, alongside Dubai and Paris and Istanbul. Unlike those cities, Mecca has no cathedrals to tour, no museums curated for foreigners, and no casual afternoon strolls for the merely curious: non-Muslims are prohibited from entering. Everyone who comes is here for one reason. Some come for the lesser pilgrimage, the Umrah, which can be performed any time of the year. Others come for the Hajj, the fifth pillar of Islam, performed by millions simultaneously in the twelfth month of the Islamic calendar. They come from Indonesia and Nigeria and Morocco and the United States, and for one week each year, the population of this valley triples.

## A Valley Between Hills

The city lies at 277 meters above sea level, in what locals call the Hollow of Mecca: a narrow corridor between hills where several ancient valleys meet. The valley of al-Taneem, the valley of Bakkah, the valley of Abqar. The mountains around the city rise to 1,000 meters. Just beyond the eastern edge stands Jabal al-Nour, at 642 meters the highest point within the city, crowned by the Cave of Hira where Muhammad is believed to have received the first revelations of the Quran. The climate is hot desert, with summer afternoons consistently breaking 40°C and winter nights dropping to 19. Rain is rare and dramatic when it comes: al-Kurdi counted 89 floods up to 1965, the worst in 1942. Dams have since been built to manage the runoff, but the geography itself still funnels water into the mosque precinct when storms hit.

## The Year of the Elephant

In roughly 570 CE, according to Islamic tradition, an Ethiopian-Christian commander named Abraha marched on Mecca from Yemen with an army that included one or more war elephants. His intention was to destroy the Kaaba and redirect Arabian pilgrimage to a cathedral he had built at Sana'a. When Abraha sent a messenger inviting the Quraysh to negotiate, Abd al-Muttalib (Muhammad's grandfather) reportedly replied: The Owner of this House is its Defender, and I am sure he will save it. The lead elephant, known as Mahmud, is said to have stopped at Mecca's boundary and refused to advance. Some historians theorize that a smallpox epidemic swept Abraha's army. According to the 105th Surah of the Quran, Al-Fil, Allah sent a dark cloud of small birds carrying stones that broke the Ethiopian army like eaten straw. Muhammad was born that year, or so Islamic tradition holds.

## Quraysh and Caravans

By the 6th century, Mecca was a prosperous caravan city. Its Quraysh tribe, skilled traders, had built alliances with nomadic Bedouins who brought leather, livestock, and metals from the surrounding mountains. Caravans carried these goods north to Syria and Iraq, and returned with money, grain, and wine. The Byzantines' Red Sea shipping routes were being disrupted by piracy. The Sasanian overland routes through the Persian Gulf were threatened by war. Mecca, though surrounded by barren land, had the Zamzam Well and sat at the crossroads of safer caravan routes. Historian Patricia Crone has argued that Meccan trade was less grand than Islamic sources suggest (skins, hides, woolens, clarified butter, camels, possibly destined for the Roman army), but Glen Bowersock and others disagree. Whatever the volume, by the 6th century Mecca had become the binding center of Arabian trade, and the tribes of the region had a stake in protecting it.

## Conquest and Concrete

In 628, Muhammad attempted to enter Mecca for pilgrimage and was blocked by the Quraysh. The resulting Treaty of Hudaybiya was a 10-year ceasefire, broken after two years when Meccan allies attacked Muslim allies. Muhammad and his forces, now 10,000 strong, marched into Mecca in 630 and took the city without resistance. The pagan idols were destroyed. The Kaaba was rededicated to Allah alone. Mecca was declared the holiest site in Islam. In modern times, the city was captured by Ibn Saud and his forces in 1924, becoming part of what would become Saudi Arabia. Under Saudi rule, about 95 percent of Mecca's historic buildings (many over a thousand years old) have been demolished since 1985. Fewer than 20 structures from Muhammad's lifetime reportedly remain. The rationale: hotels, parking, infrastructure for the Hajj. The result: a skyline dominated by the 601-meter Makkah Royal Clock Tower, which in 2012 became one of the tallest buildings in the world, looming directly over the Great Mosque.

## Incidents and Expansion

Crowd disasters have been a tragic constant of modern Mecca. On 2 July 1990, a ventilation failure in a crowded pedestrian tunnel killed 1,426 pilgrims. On 24 September 2015, 769 pilgrims (by official count; far more by independent estimates) died in a crush at Mina. On 20 November 1979, extremist insurgents seized the Grand Mosque; several hundred people died in the two-week siege. Mecca has no airport of its own because of security concerns. It is served by King Abdulaziz International in Jeddah, about 70 kilometers west, and by the newer Haramain High Speed Railway, running at 300 kilometers per hour, connecting Mecca and Medina via Jeddah. On 2 August 2027, a total solar eclipse will pass over Mecca, darkening the city for five minutes and eight seconds. For once, the pilgrims will look up.

## Flight Context

Mecca lies at 21.43°N, 39.83°E in a mountain valley 70 km inland from Jeddah on the Red Sea. Elevation 277 m with surrounding peaks reaching 1,000 m. No airport at Mecca; the city is served by King Abdulaziz International Airport (OEJN) in Jeddah, 70-75 km west, and Taif International (OETF), 120 km southeast. Mecca's airspace and the city itself are restricted to Muslims. Recommended cruising altitude over the region is 12,000 ft or higher. The Abraj Al Bait's 601-m clock tower is the dominant visual landmark.
