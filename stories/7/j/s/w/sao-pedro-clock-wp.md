# São Pedro Clock

Salvador demolished the São Pedro church in 1913 to push an avenue through; three years later the city's shop and office clerks paid for a cast-iron clock to stand where it had been.

**Location:** -12.9811, -38.5146

**Categories:** Individual clocks, Monuments and memorials in Brazil, Buildings and structures in Salvador, Bahia, Historic Centre of Salvador

## Introduction

The church is gone and the square still has its name. São Pedro was pulled down in 1913 so that Avenida Sete de Setembro could run through the spot where it stood, and today São Pedro survives as a stretch of Salvador's historic centre that everybody treats as a small neighbourhood despite there being no parish left to define it. What holds the Largo de São Pedro now is a clock — six and a half metres of cast iron and pink granite, four atlantes shouldering a lamp above the dials, running since 1916 because the city's commercial employees decided the corner needed one.

## An Avenue Through a Church

The demolition was progress, or was sold as such. Governor J. J. Seabra was remaking central Salvador in the manner of the era — new avenues driven straight through colonial fabric, as Rio de Janeiro had done to its own downtown a decade earlier — and the São Pedro church sat in the path of Avenida Sete de Setembro. In 1913 it came down. A parish that had organised the surrounding streets for generations disappeared from the map in a matter of weeks, leaving behind a place-name and a habit of speech. Ask in Salvador where São Pedro is and you will be given directions, not a history lesson.

## Paid For by the Clerks

The replacement did not come from the state. The Associação dos Empregados no Comércio da Bahia — the association of employees in Bahian commerce — took the initiative and put up a public clock on the vacated ground, beside the monument to the Baron of Rio Branco. It was inaugurated on 15 November 1916, the anniversary of the Proclamation of the Republic, under the state government of Antônio Muniz de Aragão. The choice of donor and date says most of what needs saying. Shop and office workers, in a city where the working day was increasingly measured to the minute and few people owned a watch, paid for the instrument that measured it, and unveiled it on a republican holiday.

## Cast in Paris

The sculpture was made by the Italian artist Pasquale De Chirico and finished in Paris in 1914; the movements came from the Paris clockmaking house of Henry Lepaute. Two years then passed between the completed work in France and the unveiling in Bahia. The techniques were casting and hewn stone, the materials cast iron and rose granite, and the whole assembly stands 6.5 metres from pavement to lamp. Look up at the dial for the small anomaly: the four is written IIII rather than IV, the old clockmakers' four, a convention that has outlived every explanation offered for it.

## The Street It Stands On

Avenida Sete de Setembro turned out to matter more than the church it displaced. Running from the Farol da Barra at the tip of the peninsula to Praça Castro Alves in the historic centre, Avenida Sete — nobody uses the full name — became the city's main artery and its ceremonial one. Salvador's parades take this route, carnival included, which means that for a few days every year the clock in the Largo de São Pedro presides over a river of people moving past it in both directions, keeping perfectly accurate time for a crowd that has stopped caring what time it is.

## Keeping It Running

In 1999 a vehicle hit the monument, which forced the first major overhaul of the clock's life. Since that repair one man, the clockmaker Wilson Ribeiro, has been responsible for keeping it going. When the city rebuilt Avenida Sete, the Fundação Gregório de Mattos took the clock in hand again: rust ground off, mechanism repaired, paint renewed, glass replaced, the work completed on 3 September 2015. The monument turned a hundred the following November. A further restoration, this time adding automation, was finished by 30 April 2024. The clock has now stood in the Largo de São Pedro considerably longer than the church whose name it keeps — a hundred and ten years of telling a busy commercial street what time it is, from a plinth put up by the people who most needed to know.

## Flight Context

Located at 12.981 S, 38.515 W in the Largo de São Pedro on Avenida Sete de Setembro, central Salvador — about 1.8 km southwest of the Pelourinho and 22 km southwest of Deputado Luís Eduardo Magalhães International Airport (SBSV / SSA). The monument itself is far too small to pick out from the air; the navigational landmark is Avenida Sete de Setembro, the long avenue running down the spine of the peninsula from the historic centre toward Campo Grande and Barra, with the Bay of All Saints on one side and the Atlantic on the other. Recommended viewing altitude 1,500-2,500 ft AGL over the peninsula. VFR conditions are the norm year-round; expect afternoon showers between April and July and glare off both coasts near midday.
