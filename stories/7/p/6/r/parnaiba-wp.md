# Parnaíba

In the 18th century, Parnaíba's warehouses shipped carnauba wax to Germany and cotton to England - and for a time the port city mattered more than Piauí's capital.

**Location:** -2.9050, -41.7769

**Categories:** city, colonial, port, delta

## Introduction

Leonardo de Sá claimed the land in 1699 and the name came later, borrowed either from a São Paulo parish or from a Tupi phrase meaning "great unnavigable river." Both origins fit. For two centuries, Parnaíba was Piauí's window to the Atlantic - a trading post whose warehouses at Porto das Barcas filled with carnauba wax, cotton, leather, and babassu oil bound for Portugal, Spain, England, and Germany. For a while the coastal city eclipsed Oeiras, the landlocked capital, in both wealth and influence.

## Charqueada on the Igaraçu

Development began in earnest around 1761, when the Portuguese pioneer Domingos Dias da Silva founded Porto das Barcas and established a charqueada - a salt-beef operation that cured hides and meat for export. A year later, Colonel João Pereira Caldas moved the provincial government seat here, renaming the settlement São João da Parnaíba. The 18th-century warehouses that still stand at Porto das Barcas, just off the bridge to Ilha Grande de Santa Isabel, were the engine of that prosperity. They are remarkable survivors: a direct architectural thread from the era when Piauí's economy ran through these docks rather than through Teresina.

## The Only Open-Sea Delta in the Americas

The Parnaíba River runs 1,485 kilometers before splitting into five arms at its mouth. Unlike most deltas, which push into protected bays, Parnaíba's opens directly into the Atlantic - one of only a handful of open-sea river deltas in the world. Seventy-three islands rise from the brackish mix of river and ocean, their dunes and mangroves sheltering monkeys, ibis, and caimans. The city itself sits on the southernmost arm, the Igaraçu, on reclaimed coastal plain only a few meters above sea level. The 313,800-hectare Delta do Parnaíba Environmental Protection Area, established in 1996, now safeguards the ecosystem.

## Carnauba Wax and the Rota das Emoções

Parnaíba still exports what the region has always exported: carnauba wax, babassu and coconut oils, cashews, cotton, and leather. Jaborandi leaves, harvested here, supply a global pharmaceutical market. But tourism is the newer engine. The city anchors the Rota das Emoções - the "route of emotions" - a tourist circuit that links Lençóis Maranhenses, the Parnaíba Delta, and Jericoacoara. In 2011 the city posted a 229 percent growth rate, the fastest in Brazil that year. Today the population is more than 169,000, second in Piauí only to Teresina.

## Pedra do Sal and Portinho

Fifteen kilometers north, on Ilha Grande de Santa Isabel, Pedra do Sal beach splits its personality down the middle. One side stays calm for fishing and families; the other catches consistent wind that draws surfers, kite surfers, and hang gliders. An 18-megawatt wind farm rises from the dunes, turning the same Atlantic air into electricity. Portinho Lagoon, the other landmark of the region, changes color with the light - blue in cloudcover, green under sun - and is slowly being chased southward by advancing dunes that may one day swallow it. The lagoon is loved and watched; no one knows how many decades it has left.

## The Estádio and the Shark

Football arrived with the English. Casa Inglesa, a British trading firm, built Estádio Internacional in the 1920s with an architectural style borrowed directly from English stadiums - a curiosity in Brazil, and the home ground of the Parnahybano Championship. The stadium was later renamed Petrônio Portela. Parnahyba Sport Club, founded in 1913 and the oldest in Piauí, plays under the nickname Tubarão do Litoral - the Coastal Shark - a reminder that even in a city 15 kilometers from the beach, the Atlantic is never far away.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 2.90°S, 41.78°W. Parnaíba-Prefeito Dr. João Silva Filho International Airport (ICAO SBPB) has a 2,500-meter runway and is served by Azul (weekly flights from Campinas) and Voepass (Fortaleza and Teresina). From altitude the five-armed delta of the Parnaíba River is unmistakable, dividing the states of Piauí and Maranhão. Teresina lies 366 km inland to the southwest. Best visibility runs July through December; heavy rains from January through June.
