# Gruta de Ubajara

A nine-chambered limestone cave in Brazil's smallest national park, reached by a cable car that drops off the edge of the Ibiapaba escarpment in three minutes.

**Location:** -3.8343, -40.8999

**Categories:** Caves of Brazil, Landforms of Ceará, Tourist attractions in Ceará, National Parks, Geology

## Introduction

Every centimetre of crystal inside the Gruta de Ubajara took roughly three years to form. Run that arithmetic across a stalactite the length of your arm and you arrive at a couple of centuries of dripping water, which is a useful thing to hold in your head while standing in the dark under the Serra da Ibiapaba. The cave sits in the flank of the escarpment in western Ceará, a few hundred metres below the plateau edge, and it is the reason a 6,288-hectare patch of forest and thorn scrub — the smallest of Brazil's national parks — exists at all.

## Nine Rooms in the Limestone

The cave runs to somewhere between 1,100 and 1,200 metres of passage, arranged as a sequence of nine halls, and reaches roughly 75 metres below the level of its entrance. An underground river moves through it. Visitors see a fraction of that: about 450 metres of gallery, lit, with a vertical descent of some 35 metres over the course of the walk. The halls are named after what people decided their formations resembled, which is the universal convention in show caves and never gets less endearing. Bats live here, as they do in every warm limestone cavity in the tropics, and their presence is part of why the deeper sections stay closed.

## Off the Edge in Three Minutes

The famous approach is the cable car. It runs about 550 metres from the plateau rim down the face of the escarpment to the cave mouth, and the trip takes three minutes — long enough to register that the forest canopy below is a very long way below, short enough that there is no time to reconsider. It is operated by the state tourism department and has spent stretches out of service, at one point for two years running, which is worth checking before committing to a plan built around it. The alternative is to walk. The Trilha dos Cafundós descends the same escarpment on foot, with a credentialed guide, twelve kilometres round trip and about seven hours door to door, including roughly 500 metres inside the cave. Departures go at nine and ten in the morning. Nobody who has done both describes them as equivalent experiences.

## Looking for Silver

The cave has been known since the early eighteenth century, when Portuguese colonists came through the region prospecting for minerals — silver above all. They found very little. A persistent local explanation holds that part of the cave is not natural at all, but the residue of those excavations, widened by two centuries of weather since. The geology argues otherwise, but the story is durable, and it says something about how much effort was thrown at this escarpment for so little return. Other caves honeycomb the same limestone and stay closed to visitors: the Gruta do Urso Fóssil, which is paleontologically important; the Gruta do Morcego Branco, the white bat's cave; the Gruta de Cima; and the Gruta do Pendurado.

## The Park That Shrank and Grew Back

Ubajara National Park was created on 30 April 1959 by federal decree 45,954, covering 4,000 hectares. In April 1973 it was cut down to 563 hectares — a reduction of more than eighty-five percent — and it stayed that way for nearly thirty years before a December 2002 expansion restored it to roughly 6,288 hectares. Even now it is the smallest national park in Brazil. It occupies land in the municipalities of Ubajara, Tianguá and Frecheirinha, sits between about 720 and 840 metres above sea level, and is administered by ICMBio. Immediately around it lies the Serra da Ibiapaba Environmental Protection Area, an altogether different order of thing at more than 1.59 million hectares.

## Three Climates on One Slope

The escarpment does something useful to weather. Moist air rising against the Ibiapaba wall drops around 1,440 millimetres of rain a year on the high ground, and the result is that a single short slope carries humid forest at the top, transitional forest in the middle, and caatinga — the semi-arid thorn scrub of the northeast — at the bottom. Temperatures run between 16 and 28 degrees, averaging around 22, which by the standards of interior Ceará is remarkably mild. Surveys have counted 41 mammal species, 127 birds, 16 lizards, 17 snakes and 16 amphibians in that compressed space. Among them are the oncilla and the cougar, the red-handed howler monkeys of Maranhão, and two small frogs, *Adelophryne baturitensis* and *Adelophryne maranguapensis*, that exist in only a handful of Ceará's forest fragments.

## Flight Context

The Gruta de Ubajara lies at 3.83°S, 40.90°W in western Ceará, roughly 320 km from Fortaleza. The defining feature from the air is the Serra da Ibiapaba itself — a long, straight north-south escarpment separating the Ceará lowlands to the east from the Piauí plateau to the west, with the plateau surface around 720-840 metres and the cave set into the eastern face below the rim. Approaching from the east at 4,000-6,000 feet, the wall appears abruptly; the town of Ubajara sits on the plateau just back from the edge, with Tianguá to the north. Nearest airports are Fortaleza's Pinto Martins (SBFZ), about 320 km east, and Teresina's Senador Petrônio Portella (SBTE), roughly 280 km southwest. Orographic cloud forms readily along the escarpment face, especially in the February-to-May wet season; mornings in the dry half of the year offer the cleanest view of the scarp and the forested slope beneath it.
