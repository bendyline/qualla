# Banco Central Burglary at Fortaleza

In August 2005 a crew tunneled eighty meters under downtown Fortaleza and carried three and a half tonnes of banknotes out of the Central Bank vault - and then the money started killing them.

**Location:** -3.7339, -38.5226

**Categories:** 2005 crimes in Brazil, Bank burglaries, Fortaleza, Crime history

## Introduction

At 8:20 on the morning of Monday 8 August 2005, staff opening the Banco Central branch in downtown Fortaleza found a hole in the floor of the vault. Underneath it was a tunnel: eighty meters long, lined with plastic sheeting, shored with timber, wired for lighting and ventilation and even air conditioning, running out under Rua 25 de Marco to the back of a small business that claimed to sell natural and synthetic lawn turf. The vault was three and a half tonnes lighter than it had been on Friday evening. Whoever had emptied it had a forty-four-hour head start, and by then they were long gone.

## The Grass Company

The house at Rua 25 de Marco 1071, in the center of Fortaleza, had been rented some months earlier by a man registered as Jorge Luiz da Silva, using false documents. The declared business was landscaping - natural and synthetic grass. It sat one block from the Banco Central building. A turf company is an unusually convenient front for an operation whose main product is dirt, and roughly thirty tonnes of excavated earth left the property without anyone thinking twice about it. Neighbors saw a small firm going about its work. Underneath the floor, a crew was digging toward the vault of the Central Bank.

## Eighty Meters

The tunnel was a piece of engineering, not a scrape in the ground. It ran eighty meters at a depth of about four meters, seventy centimeters wide - narrow enough that the diggers worked lying down or crouched. Wooden beams held the roof. Tarpaulin and plastic sheeting kept the walls from shedding. There were lights strung the length of it, a ventilation system, and air conditioning to make the heat survivable. At the far end came the hard part: a floor slab of steel-reinforced concrete a meter and ten centimeters thick, which the crew cut through without setting off the alarms. Investigators later put the cost of building the tunnel at around half a million reais. It was, by any measure, an investment.

## Three and a Half Tonnes

What came out was cash - about 164 million reais, something on the order of 75 million US dollars at the time, all of it in fifty-real notes. Paper money in that quantity stops being an abstraction and becomes freight. Three and a half tonnes of it. Enough to fill six and a half one-cubic-meter containers. Stacked note on note, it would have stood nearly thirty-three meters high. The thieves worked between Friday evening on 5 August and midday on Saturday the 6th, hauled the load out through the tunnel, and left the branch looking untouched from the inside. Nobody came to work again until Monday.

## The Guard, the Mayor, and the Engineer

The case that unraveled afterward was less about criminal genius than about who had been paid. Deucimar Neves Queiroz, a former Banco Central security guard, supplied the layout of the vault, the cameras and the alarms, and received two million reais for it. Antonio Argeu Nunes Vieira, a former mayor of Boa Viagem, put a hundred thousand reais into financing the dig and took four million out. Antonio Jussivan Alves dos Santos, known as Alemao, was named by police as the mastermind; arrested in 2008 and sentenced to 49 years and two months, he was shot during an escape attempt in 2017. Moises Teixeira da Silva - Tatuzao, the big armadillo - dug, was arrested in 2009, and saw his sentence cut from seventeen years to two. Marcos Rogerio Machado de Morais, called Cabecudo, the tunnel's engineer, escaped in 2011 and stayed free for twelve years before being recaptured in Sorocaba, Sao Paulo, in 2023.

## What the Money Did to Them

The part of the story that does not play as caper is what happened next. Money in that quantity drew predators, and the people who had taken it became targets. Luis Fernando Ribeiro - Fernandinho - identified by the Federal Police as the operation's first financier and one of its leaders, was kidnapped in October 2005 and killed at Camanducaia, in Minas Gerais, after his family paid a two-million-real ransom. Others connected to the burglary were seized in the same wave. These were not cinematic deaths; they were abductions and murders that left families to bury people and pay for nothing. Of the thirty-six eventually charged, twenty-seven were arrested, and only about twenty million reais was recovered in the immediate aftermath, with later tallies putting recoveries near thirty-two million against well over a hundred arrests as the investigation ground on for years.

## The Hole in the Floor

Most of the money was never found. The branch went on operating, the tunnel was filled, and the case settled into Brazilian folklore, retold ever since, most widely in the Netflix documentary 3 Tonelada$: Assalto ao Banco Central - three tonnes, the title says, understating it slightly. The Federal Police credit the investigation with heading off later attempts on other vaults by the same methods. What lingers is the arithmetic of that first Monday morning: a bank that had lost a fortune, a tunnel nobody had noticed being dug a block away, and forty-four hours during which no one in Fortaleza knew anything had happened at all.

## Flight Context

The Banco Central branch stands at 3.73 degrees south, 38.52 degrees west, in the Centro district of Fortaleza, a few blocks inland from the Praia de Iracema waterfront. Best viewing altitude is 2,500-4,000 feet to pick out the downtown block grid; the bank is a squat modern block near the Jose de Alencar theater district and the Dragao do Mar cultural center, with Rua 25 de Marco running one block away. Orientation landmarks from the air: the Beira Mar seafront curving northeast toward Mucuripe port, the Dragao do Mar planetarium dome, and the pale sweep of Praia de Iracema. Nearest airport is Pinto Martins International (SBFZ / FOR), about 6 km south of the center. Weather is tropical and humid year-round; the wet season runs roughly February to May with heavy afternoon convection, while July to December offers steady visibility and strong easterly trades.
