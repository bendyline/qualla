# Tutóia

The guidebooks call it a stopover between the dunes and the delta - which is a strange way to describe a municipality of 53,000 people and more than eighty islands.

**Location:** -2.7619, -42.2739

**Categories:** town, delta, gateway, brazil

## Introduction

The travel guides give Tutóia about four lines, and most of them are bus times. It is, they explain, a place you pass through between Lençóis Maranhenses and Parnaíba — where you get off a bus and onto a pickup truck. That is a fair description of what a traveller does here and a poor description of what the place is: a municipality of roughly 53,000 people spread across 1,566 square kilometres of coastal plain, mangrove and dune, holding more than eighty islands and one bank of the great delta at the mouth of the Parnaíba. Tutóia is a stopover the way an airport is a hallway.

## Vila Viçosa, Briefly

The Tremembé were here first, along this whole stretch of shifting sand and brackish channel. Colonial administration arrived in 1758, when the settlement was raised to the status of a vila and given the tidy Portuguese name Vila Viçosa — a rebranding that failed, since the older name simply outlasted it. In 1901 the seat moved to Porto Salinas. The municipality in its present form dates from 29 March 1938. Three names, three centuries, one location that kept being renamed by people who had come to it recently and would not be the last word on it.

## Four Theories and a Beautiful One

Where the name comes from is genuinely unsettled. The most colourful theory credits Phoenician traders in search of saltpetre, deriving Tutóia from a supposed "Turtoia" — a claim that tells you a great deal about nineteenth-century Brazilian romanticism and rather little about the town. The likelier explanations are indigenous. In Tremembé, the name is read as "sheet of sand" or "great expanse of dunes," which describes the ground exactly. In Tupi-Guarani it is glossed as "good water." And there is a fourth reading, from "Totói," translated as an exclamation: what beauty. Take your pick. The competing etymologies happen to name the three things the place actually consists of — sand, fresh water and a view worth an exclamation.

## Coconut, Cashew, and the Rural Majority

Tutóia is the second-largest coconut producer in Maranhão and ninth in cashew, on a municipal economy that reached about 414 million reais in 2019. Agriculture is only a fraction of that figure on paper, but it structures the place: at the 2010 census roughly two-thirds of the population lived in rural areas, at a density of about 34 people per square kilometre. The rhythm is set by the climate, which is generous and predictable — rain from January through June, dry from July through December, average temperatures above 27 degrees Celsius, and 1,200 to 1,600 millimetres of rain a year. The town itself sits about twenty metres above the sea, high enough to be dry and low enough to be part of the water.

## The Water Door

East of town the coast dissolves. The Parnaíba, which forms the border between Maranhão and Piauí for its entire 1,400-kilometre run down from the Chapada das Mangabeiras, breaks into five arms and scatters into a maze of islands, channels, mangrove and dune before reaching the Atlantic. Tutóia is one of the gateways into that maze, and the whole municipality lies within the Delta do Parnaíba Environmental Protection Area, created by federal decree on 28 August 1996 across 313,800 hectares and ten municipalities in three states. The protected habitats are a checklist of northeastern Brazil's transition zones: coastal caatinga, restinga, mangrove and flood plains full of carnaúba palms. Past the last street the municipality stops being a shoreline and becomes an archipelago — bays, tidal flats that empty twice a day, and islands by the dozen.

## Getting Out, Which Is the Point

Several buses a day run east to Parnaíba, a three-hour trip, the last one leaving at four in the afternoon. One a day goes west to São Luís. For Paulino Neves and the road to the dunes, the transport is a 4x4 pickup, an hour and a half, departing mostly to meet the buses coming in from Parnaíba — with one dependable run at ten in the morning. There used to be a scheduled boat across to Parnaíba, which would have been the more sensible way to travel; it is gone, though a motor launch can be chartered for up to four people. Accommodation is a handful of simple pousadas and a campsite outside town. There is, as the guidebook puts it with admirable honesty, a beach of sorts within walking distance.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 2.76°S, 42.27°W, on the Maranhão side of the Parnaíba delta. The nearest commercial airport is Parnaíba (ICAO SBPB), about 60 km east-southeast; Marechal Cunha Machado International (ICAO SBSL) at São Luís is roughly 300 km west. Recommended viewing altitude 2,000-4,000 feet: from there the coast east of Tutóia reads as a braid of tidal channels, mangrove islands and pale dune fields, with open Atlantic to the north. Westward the sand grows continuous toward Paulino Neves and the Pequenos Lençóis. Best conditions July through December; the January-June rains bring low cloud and reduced visibility over the delta.
