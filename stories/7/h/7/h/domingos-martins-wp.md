# Domingos Martins

A mountain municipality named for a revolutionary executed by the Portuguese, now home to Pomeranian communities, German-style architecture, and a granite peak tinted blue by lichen.

**Location:** -20.3628, -40.6589

**Categories:** Municipalities in Espirito Santo

## Introduction

On June 12, 1817, a man named Domingos Jose Martins was executed in Salvador for treason. He had fought in the Pernambucan revolt that year, part of a wave of independence movements that would eventually tear Brazil loose from Portugal, but he did not live to see it. More than a century later, on December 20, 1921, a town in the mountains of Espirito Santo changed its name from Campinho to Domingos Martins. The choice was not accidental. Martins had been born in Espirito Santo, near Itapemirim, in 1781, and the state was claiming one of its own.

## From Campinho to Domingos Martins

The town was founded in 1893 under the name Campinho as a secondary district within the colony of Santa Isabel, but it outgrew its parent and eventually took the role of capital for itself. The renaming in 1921 placed the town within a national narrative about independence and sacrifice, drawing a line between the Pernambucan rebels of 1817 and the Brazilian citizens of the early Republic. Today the municipality subdivides into five districts: Capital (Sede in Portuguese), Arace, Santa Isabel, Paraju, and Melgaco, spanning a total area of 1,225.327 square kilometers. The municipal seat sits roughly 50 kilometers by road west of the state capital, Vitoria, at 542 meters above sea level.

## A Church With a Tower

The Lutheran-Confessional Evangelical Church in the town square, Praca Arthur Gerhardt, was founded on May 20, 1866. What set it apart was its bell tower, inaugurated in 1887 at a time when Imperial Brazilian law allowed only Catholic churches to have them. The Pomeranian and German Lutheran congregations of the mountain interior built one anyway, and the church survived to become a listed heritage building and a symbol of a religious pluralism that Brazilian law only later fully permitted. The church itself remains active. In the plaza surrounding it, festivals continue to mark the immigrant year: the Sommerfest, Pommerfest, Festa do Vinho, Blumenfest, and the harvest festival still called Erntedankfest in deference to the Pomeranians.

## A Language Without a Homeland

The most distinctive feature of Domingos Martins is not its architecture or its festivals but its language. Pomeranian immigrants from a region now divided between Germany and Poland settled these mountain valleys, and generations of their descendants still speak East Pomeranian, a dialect that linguists believe is extinct in Europe. In Brazil, in hard-to-reach Atlantic forest hollows, it survived. That survival has made the Pomeranian communities of these mountains a subject of linguistic study. The Germanic architecture and cuisine that also persisted are visible to any visitor; the language requires listening.

## Pedra Azul

Within the municipality lies the Pedra Azul State Park, whose central feature is a massive granite rock jutting 1,822 meters above sea level. The blue tinge in the stone, which gives the formation and the park their name, comes not from the mineral itself but from the species of lichen that cover it. When sunlight strikes the lichen-covered surface at certain angles, the rock appears blue. At other times it shows other shades as the light shifts through the day. The surrounding mountain region rises to 2,000 meters, sheltering fragments of subtropical rainforest that elsewhere in the region have been cleared for coffee and cattle.

## The Green City

Domingos Martins markets itself as the Green City, and the nickname reflects an accident of geography that became an asset. Where other parts of Espirito Santo saw most of their Atlantic forest cleared during colonization and the 20th century, the mountainous terrain here made large-scale clearing difficult. Dense subtropical rainforest still frames the town on multiple sides. Tourism and agriculture both draw on this advantage: visitors come for the cool air and the cold winter nights that few other parts of the tropical state offer, and farms in the surrounding valleys produce flowers, strawberries, coffee, and vegetables. A soft drink factory called Refrigerantes Coroa has its headquarters in town, alongside small biscuit factories producing German-inspired recipes. With 35,416 people at the 2022 census, a density of about 29 per square kilometer, it still feels rural even as tourism grows.

## Flight Context

Domingos Martins sits at 20.36 degrees S, 40.66 degrees W in central Espirito Santo at 542 meters elevation. Pedra Azul rises 1,822 meters above sea level nearby and is the principal visual landmark, recognizable by its exposed granite surface. Vitoria/Eurico Salles Airport (SBVT / VIX) lies about 40 kilometers east-northeast in a straight line, roughly 50 km by road along the BR-262 corridor, and serves regular flights from Rio (SBGL, SBRJ), Sao Paulo (SBSP, SBGR), Brasilia (SBBR), and other capitals. The surrounding terrain is rugged Atlantic forest, rising from coastal plains to peaks of 2,000 meters.
