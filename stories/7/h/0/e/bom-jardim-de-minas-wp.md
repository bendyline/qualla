# Bom Jardim de Minas

A mountain town whose founder lost his son, married into a neighbor's family, and planted the garden that gave the place its name.

**Location:** -21.9469, -44.1908

**Categories:** Municipalities in Minas Gerais, Mantiqueira Mountains, Highland towns

## Introduction

In 1770 a man named Manoel Arriaga de Oliveira brought his wife and six children to the high country of southern Minas Gerais and tried to start a colony. Indigenous people who lived in the region attacked. One of his sons was killed. Grief-stricken, Arriaga abandoned the colony and moved his remaining family to the banks of a small stream called Milho Branco, where he cleared land and built a farmhouse. Years later Captain Antonio Correia de Lacerda arrived with his family. The two men joined forces. The families intermarried. Their combined farm became known for a well-maintained garden near the main house, and when the village eventually grew up around it, the name came naturally: Fazenda do Bom Jardim. The Good Garden. That is how towns begin in colonial Brazil - with a death, a retreat, a garden, and eventually a city.

## Naming and Renaming

Brazilian municipalities accumulate names the way some rivers accumulate tributaries. The chapel in the village was built at Captain Lacerda's request and dedicated to the Lord Good Jesus of the Good Garden - Senhor Bom Jesus do Bom Jardim. In 1856 provincial Law 761 of May 2 made the village a formal district, first subject to Aiuruoca and by 1864 to the municipality of Turvo. State Law 843 of 1923 shortened the name to just Bom Jardim. In 1930 Turvo itself was renamed Andrelandia. In 1938 Decree-State Law 148 finally made Bom Jardim its own municipality, combining its headquarters district with Taboao. In 1943 the name was expanded again to Bom Jardim de Minas to distinguish it from other Bom Jardins, and the new district of Arantina was added. In 1962 Arantina broke away to become its own municipality. The town settled down, finally, with two districts and a name it could keep.

## 1,200 Meters of Mountain Air

Bom Jardim de Minas sits at roughly 1,200 meters in the Mantiqueira Mountains of southern Minas Gerais, on the banks of the Rio Grande. The surrounding topography has been called a sea of hills - the average municipal elevation hovers near 1,100 meters, rising to the 1,733-meter peak of the Serra da Bandeira in the Taboao district. The main paved road in or out is the BR-267, which runs east toward the Juiz de Fora region. The climate is mesothermal highland tropical, with cool dry winters and warmer wet summers. July, the coldest month, averages between 13 and 16 Celsius with overnight lows that can hit freezing. The mountain winter is gentle but genuine. The wet springs that pour down from the Taboao hills feed several waterfalls that the town now promotes as tourist attractions.

## A Festival Town

Population 6,459. The numbers make Bom Jardim small, but it compensates with festivals. Carnival is celebrated with more energy than a town this size should be able to muster. The agricultural exhibition in May brings farmers and livestock together from across the southern highlands. In August the Feast in Honour of Jesus Christ fills the town with processions, food stalls, and music. These events, combined with the waterfalls and the mountain scenery, have made tourism an increasingly important part of the local economy - visitors come up from Juiz de Fora and further afield to hike the trails, swim at the falls, and eat the mineiro cuisine that has made the region famous.

## Water at the Headwaters

The Rio do Peixe, one of the larger rivers in the southern Minas region, starts in the hills of the Taboao district. Many springs flow from these high points, which is why the area has so many waterfalls - the same water that powered small farms for generations now draws weekenders with waterproof cameras. The town lies in the watershed of the Rio Grande, the river that eventually becomes the Parana and flows all the way to the River Plate between Argentina and Uruguay. From 1,200 meters up in the Mantiqueira, every stream that trickles off the Serra da Bandeira is starting a journey toward the Atlantic via the second-longest river system in South America. Bom Jardim de Minas is a headwaters town, one of thousands of small Brazilian settlements that sit where major water begins.

## The Garden That Stayed

The original garden is long gone. The Arriaga and Lacerda families are dispersed into the town's genealogy. But the name has outlasted everything - the indigenous people who were here first, the colonizers who displaced them, the captaincies and empires and republics, the renamings, the splits, the reconfigurations of Brazilian administrative geography. Bom Jardim de Minas remains what it was always quietly trying to become: a small town in the mountains, with a church, a main square, a few waterfalls, and a name that honors an ornamental garden that once grew beside a farmhouse. It is 281 kilometers from Belo Horizonte. It takes effort to reach. Those who make the effort find the mountain air cool, the hills green, and the festival calendar reliably full.
