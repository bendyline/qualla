# Agulhas Negras Peak

The fifth-highest mountain in Brazil, a jagged cluster of dark stone on the Rio-Minas Gerais border whose identity keeps shifting as the surveyors recalculate.

**Location:** -22.3797, -44.6611

**Categories:** Mountains of Brazil, Highest points of Brazilian states, Landforms of Minas Gerais, Landforms of Rio de Janeiro (state), Mantiqueira Mountains

## Introduction

For most of the twentieth century, Brazilians thought Agulhas Negras was the tallest mountain in their country. It was not, and it never had been, but the surveyors took their time getting it right. The name means Black Needles, because the summit is a cluster of sharp dark stones that crowns the rounded back of a much larger massif called Itatiaia, a Tupi word meaning stone with many sharp points. At 2,791 meters on the border of Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais states, Agulhas Negras is now the fifth-highest mountain in Brazil, the highest point in Rio state, the third-highest in Minas, and the second-highest peak in the Mantiqueira Mountains. Each of those rankings is the product of a correction.

## The Long Demotion

First, the title of tallest in Brazil went south - literally. Pico da Bandeira, in the Caparao range between Minas Gerais and Espirito Santo, turned out to be taller at 2,891 meters. Then, in 1965, surveyors confirmed that Pico da Neblina - a sharp granite horn on the Venezuelan border in the far north, where clouds wrap the summit nearly year-round and Brazil meets the Amazon - was taller still. Pico da Neblina currently measures 2,995 meters. Agulhas Negras got quietly bumped again. Even the consolation prize - highest in the Mantiqueira range - held only until 2001, when a GPS measurement found that nearby Pedra da Mina was 2,798 meters, about seven meters taller. A joint Brazilian Army-IBGE expedition confirmed it. The surveyors went on to remeasure Agulhas Negras itself, raising it from 2,787 to 2,791.55 meters, and in 2016 a more precise calculation based on a new Brazilian geoid settled on 2,790.94 meters - a difference of 61 centimeters, or about two feet, from the previous figure.

## Itatiaia and the Tupi Names

Every peak in this part of Brazil carries at least two names, the Portuguese and the Tupi. The massif as a whole is Itatiaia, and its national park - Brazil's first, created 1937 - preserves 30,000 hectares of Mantiqueira forest around the summit ridges. The highest rock, which forms the true summit of Agulhas Negras, is called Itatiaiacu - aacu being a Tupi suffix for big - or simply Acu among the generation of Brazilian mountaineers who climbed here in the 1940s and 50s. The climb is not long by mountaineering standards. But the rock itself is the attraction: a jumble of dark volcanic towers, spires, and chimneys that look like something out of a different range entirely, dropped onto the smooth green back of the Mantiqueira. You climb the last stretch by scrambling over boulders and wedging up chimneys, your hands on stone that is 60 million years old.

## A Road That Reaches the Sky

The access to Agulhas Negras passes through one of the more remarkable driving routes in Brazil. From the BR-354 federal highway, a gravel road called the BR-485 climbs off the Garganta do Registro pass and into Itatiaia National Park's north entrance at 2,460 meters. A short spur off the main road, leading to an Eletrobras Furnas communications tower, reaches 2,662 meters - the highest point any ordinary passenger car can reach in Brazil. The Abrigo Reboucas, a mountain shelter at about 2,350 meters, is the staging point for climbs of the peak. From Agulhas Negras on a clear day, you can see back down the length of the Paraiba Valley toward Resende, a flat industrial town whose residents might, at the right moment from the right angle on the Via Dutra highway between Sao Paulo and Rio, glimpse the jagged teeth of Agulhas Negras rising twenty-five kilometers to their northwest. Most of the time, lower ranges hide it.

## The Rock That Keeps Its Altitude

What is remarkable about Agulhas Negras is not that it has been demoted repeatedly. What is remarkable is how little the mountain itself has changed through all that demotion. The rock is the same rock. The needles are the same needles. The 60-million-year-old alkaline intrusions that formed this massif were uplifted and then exposed as the softer country rock around them eroded away, leaving these hard dark peaks standing above the Mantiqueira's green ridges. Agulhas Negras has been watching the Paraiba Valley for millions of years. The surveyors' revisions are a twentieth-century problem. On the summit, the wind moves through the needles and makes a sound that climbers in the 1930s heard and that climbers today still hear - a low whistle between the towers of stone, at the highest point in the state of Rio de Janeiro, which is the one ranking the surveyors have not been able to take away.

## Flight Context

Agulhas Negras Peak stands at 22.38 S, 44.66 W on the border of Rio de Janeiro and Minas Gerais states, at 2,790.94 m (9,157 ft). The peak sits within Itatiaia National Park along the Mantiqueira range, roughly 170 km west of Rio and 380 km east-northeast of Sao Paulo. Visible from the Via Dutra highway (BR-116) near Resende when conditions are clear. Nearest airports: Resende (SDRS) to the south and Sao Jose dos Campos (SBSJ) to the west. Maintain safe altitude - high terrain here reaches nearly 3,000 m and afternoon orographic cloud is common. The jagged dark summit rocks contrast sharply with the surrounding rounded green ridges of the Mantiqueira, providing an unmistakable visual signature.
