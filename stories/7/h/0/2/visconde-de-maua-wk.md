# Visconde de Mauá

Three mountain villages - one for restaurants, one for waterfalls, one that names them all - stitched into the highest folds of the Serra da Mantiqueira.

**Location:** -22.3317, -44.5394

**Categories:** Mountain destinations, Serra da Mantiqueira, Tourism

## Introduction

The locals divide the region into three villages, each with its own personality. Vila de Visconde de Mauá is where you arrive and find your bearings. Vila de Maringá is where you eat - the whole village is essentially restaurants, one after another, most specializing in trout or pine nuts. Vila da Maromba is where the waterfalls are. Hikers follow trails that drain out of Itatiaia National Park, each path eventually depositing you beside a plunge pool cold enough to stop your heart for a second. Winter temperatures can drop below freezing. This is Brazil, but not the Brazil on the postcards.

## Mountain Pass, Mountain Cold

Visconde de Mauá sits at the top of the Serra da Mantiqueira, geographically isolated at roughly 1,200 meters elevation. The Mantiqueira is the inland range that defines the boundary between Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, and Minas Gerais states - all three borders converge near the Mauá region. In winter, from June to August, temperatures can range from below freezing up to 13 degrees Celsius. Summer brings mild afternoons and frequent evening rains, with the thermometer ranging from 8 to 27. This is tropical climate at altitude, more akin to parts of the southern Brazilian highlands than to the coastal humidity of Rio. You pack for cold nights, even in January.

## Three Villages and Their Jobs

Vila de Maringá specializes in food. Stroll down its main street and you find one restaurant after another, many of them in converted pousadas, almost all serving the regional specialties: trout raised in cold mountain streams, and pine nuts from the araucária trees. The araucária - Brazil's distinctive umbrella-shaped conifer - gives the higher elevations their unusual silhouette, and its edible nuts appear in regional desserts and savory dishes. Vila da Maromba is the base for waterfall hunting. Trails leave directly from town toward cascades like the Cachoeira do Escorrega, where the smoothed rock face allows visitors to slide down into a pool at the bottom. The original Vila de Visconde de Mauá holds the accommodation and the infrastructure - banks are absent, but pousadas abound.

## Getting There Is the Point

The main access point is BR-116 / Rodovia Presidente Dutra at kilometer 311, between the cities of Resende and Itatiaia. From there, some 30 kilometers of winding road climb into the mountains. The upper stretch from Capelinha to Mauá was long unpaved gravel, until the state paved it as an 'estrada-parque' in 2011. From Rio de Janeiro the total trip is roughly 200 kilometers; from São Paulo, 300 kilometers. The nearest international airports are Rio's Galeão (GIG) and São Paulo's Guarulhos (GRU). Buses run from Rio via Cidade do Aço in about three hours, and from São Paulo via Cometa to Resende in about four hours, with a local bus onward to Mauá taking another hour. Some valley roads beyond the villages are still dirt, so check conditions after heavy rain.

## Waterfalls of Itatiaia

The trails and cascades around Mauá flow out of Itatiaia National Park, Brazil's first national park, established in 1937. The park encloses some of the highest peaks in southeastern Brazil, including the Pico das Agulhas Negras - Black Needles Peak - rising to 2,791 meters just west of the Mauá villages. Water collected in these high peaks cascades down through the Mantiqueira, feeding the streams that trout farms depend on and the waterfalls that tourists come to see. The Alcantilado Waterfalls are among the most visited - a series of cold pools linked by cascades descending through forested slopes. Horseback riding is popular on the mountain trails, and pousadas arrange guided rides.

## Penedo, the Finnish Side Trip

On the way back down to the Dutra highway, travelers often stop in Penedo, just three kilometers off BR-116. Penedo is a curious footnote in Brazilian immigration history: a Finnish colony, founded in 1929 by a group of Finnish immigrants seeking to establish a utopian agricultural community. The colony itself struggled, but the village kept its Finnish accent - saunas, wooden architecture, Finnish-themed restaurants, and an annual Finnish celebration. The juxtaposition is classic Mauá-region: European immigrant history layered into Brazilian mountain geography. The viscount of Mauá never came. The Finns came and stayed. The Germans, in the region's other villages, did the same. And now the hippies have been here for fifty years. Every wave left something behind in the valleys.

## Flight Context

The Visconde de Mauá area sits at 22.33°S, 44.54°W at 1,200 meters elevation in the Serra da Mantiqueira, close to the tri-state border of Rio de Janeiro, São Paulo, and Minas Gerais. From cruising altitude, look for the dramatic mountain relief and the Pico das Agulhas Negras at 2,791 meters to the west - one of the highest peaks in southeastern Brazil. Nearest international airports are Rio/Galeão (SBGL) about 145 km east-southeast and São Paulo/Guarulhos (SBGR) about 235 km southwest (road distances roughly 200 and 300 km). Best viewed mid-morning when the mountain valleys have cleared of fog.
