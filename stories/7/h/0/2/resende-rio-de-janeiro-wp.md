# Resende, Rio de Janeiro

A Brazilian town where army cadets train, trucks are assembled, uranium is enriched, and the country's first national park begins.

**Location:** -22.4689, -44.4469

**Categories:** Municipalities in Rio de Janeiro (state), Military academies of Brazil, Automotive industry in Brazil, Mantiqueira Mountains

## Introduction

In one Brazilian municipality, all of these things happen. Army cadets train to become officers. Volkswagen trucks roll off an assembly line. Nuclear fuel is enriched for power reactors. The first federal national park in Brazil protects mountain forests. Nissan builds cars. Michelin makes tires. The largest theater in Latin America seats 2,884. Resende, population 132,312, sits in a narrow strip of Rio de Janeiro state that borders both São Paulo and Minas Gerais, and it seems to have absorbed responsibilities from all three neighbors.

## Layers of Naming

The Puri people called the place Timburibá. When Portuguese-descended settlers founded a town here in the middle of the 18th century, they gave it the kind of ornate colonial name the era preferred: Nossa Senhora da Conceição do Campo Alegre da Paraíba Nova, 'Our Lady of the Conception of the Joyful Field of New Paraíba.' On September 29, 1801, an act of the Portuguese colonial administration renamed it Resende, honoring General José Luís de Castro, the 13th vice-regent of Brazil and second Count of Resende. Three names for one settlement, each reflecting a different moment in who held authority. Resende is the oldest town in the Paraíba do Sul valley in Rio de Janeiro state, and the territorial geography still shows that priority: it shares borders with São Paulo and Minas Gerais and sits on the Rodovia Presidente Dutra, the crucial Rio-São Paulo highway that is arguably the most important road in the country.

## The Academy of Black Needles

The Academia Militar das Agulhas Negras, AMAN, takes its name from a nearby rock formation, Pico das Agulhas Negras, the 'Black Needles Peak,' which at 2,791 meters is one of the highest points in Brazil. The academy trains officers for the Brazilian Army. Its campus in Resende is substantial, and local sources describe it as the second-largest military complex in the world. Every year, new cohorts of cadets arrive to begin their four-year training, and generations of Brazilian military leaders have passed through the academy's gates. Around the academy has grown a town accustomed to the rhythms of military life: parade days, uniform shops, the occasional crack of distant rifle practice from the training ranges.

## Industry on the Dutra

Resende's industrial strip sits along the Dutra highway, the artery that connects Brazil's two largest cities. MAN Latin America, formerly Volkswagen Trucks and Buses, runs its main plant here, the largest truck and bus factory in Brazil. Nissan do Brasil assembles passenger vehicles. PSA Peugeot Citroën builds cars. Michelin makes tires. And on the outskirts, in more secure facilities, the Indústrias Nucleares of Brasil operates the country's Nuclear Fuel Factory, the only facility in Brazil capable of enriching uranium. The combination is unusual: a municipality where you could buy a truck, a passenger car, and enriched uranium, in ascending order of legal difficulty. Resende contains, as well, the headquarters of TV Rio Sul, an affiliate of the Globo television network.

## The First National Park

Before all this industry arrived, Resende was already known for its geography. The Mantiqueira Mountains rise along the municipality's western edge, separating Rio de Janeiro state from Minas Gerais. The Paraíba do Sul River runs through the valley. In 1937, President Getúlio Vargas created Brazil's first national park, Parque Nacional do Itatiaia, covering parts of the Mantiqueira that include Pico das Agulhas Negras itself. The park is famous for its subtropical highland forests, waterfalls, and the rare experience of real cold, even occasional snow, in a Brazilian protected area. Resende also contains the 6.7-hectare Rio Pombo Nature Park, the 5,760-hectare Serrinha do Alambari Environmental Protection Area, and part of the 8,036-hectare Pedra Selada State Park. The tourist attractions cluster in the mountains: Visconde de Mauá, Penedo (the latter actually in neighboring Itatiaia municipality), Engenheiro Passos, the waterfall called Cachoeira da Fumaça.

## What a City Holds

Resende's Academy Theater, the municipal performing arts venue, is reported locally as the largest theater in Latin America by seating capacity, with 2,884 seats. The city's Estádio Municipal do Trabalhador holds 10,000 for matches of Resende Futebol Clube, which won the 2007 Campeonato Carioca Second Division. The municipality covers 1,094 square kilometers, making it one of the largest in the Rio-São Paulo corridor, and the space accommodates the remarkable variety of activities it hosts. Notable residents and natives include the volleyball player Tande, the beach volleyball players Adriana Samuel and Maria Elisa Antonelli, and the politicians Carlos and Flávio Bolsonaro. Resende Airport serves the municipality for regional and private flights, while the Dutra highway handles the heavy traffic between Rio and São Paulo. What the town demonstrates, above all, is how Brazilian federalism works on the ground: a single municipality hosting military, nuclear, industrial, and conservation functions that would be distributed across half a dozen towns in most other countries.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 22.47°S, 44.45°W. Recommended viewing altitude 5,000-8,000 ft AGL to see the Paraíba do Sul valley with the Mantiqueira Mountains rising to the west. Pico das Agulhas Negras (2,791 m / 9,157 ft) is a major landmark. The Rodovia Presidente Dutra is visible cutting east-west through the valley. Nearest airport: Resende Airport (SDRS, REZ). Larger airports include Rio de Janeiro-Galeão (SBGL, GIG) about 170 km east and São Paulo-Guarulhos (SBGR, GRU) about 240 km southwest.
