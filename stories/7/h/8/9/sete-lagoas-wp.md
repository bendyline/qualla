# Sete Lagoas

A Minas Gerais city named for seven lakes that now number seventeen, where pig iron furnaces glow and a 1959 football club once held its own against Real Madrid.

**Location:** -19.4658, -44.2469

**Categories:** Municipalities in Minas Gerais, Populated places established in 1867, Industrial cities, Football culture

## Introduction

The name is a promise and a miscount. Sete Lagoas means Seven Lagoons in Portuguese - and while seven official lakes are enshrined in a 1989 municipal law, there are actually seventeen in the municipality and ten within the urban perimeter alone. Some were dug, others joined the city as it expanded around them. The count has never quite matched the name. But no one is changing it now.

## A Goldmine Named for Lakes

Colonists pushed into Minas Gerais around 1700, chasing gold, gems, and diamonds. They did not all find fortune, but the search left its marks on the landscape and the language. The Santa Luzia quarter of Sete Lagoas is still unofficially called o garimpo - the goldmine. Evidence of pre-Columbian habitation turns up in the nearby Gruta Rei do Mato, the Cave of the Forest King, suggesting the area drew human life long before Portuguese prospectors. The name Sete Lagoas was used for various regional administrations between 1780 and 1867, but the municipality itself was formally founded in 1867, stitched together from settlements that had grown around the water.

## A City Built for Iron

At 761 meters above sea level, 70 kilometers from the state capital Belo Horizonte, Sete Lagoas sits at a useful altitude and a useful crossroads. The BR-040 motorway links it to both Belo Horizonte and the national capital Brasília, and the city has used that position well. Twenty-three factories produce pig iron, part of a heavy industry cluster that has long anchored the regional economy. In 2001 an Iveco-Fiat plant opened to produce trucks and vans, another sign of how completely Sete Lagoas leans into manufacturing. Climate helps: average temperature of 20.9 degrees Celsius, cool July mornings averaging 17.5, February highs around 23. The chill records tell another story - 0.8 degrees in July 1926, the lowest ever recorded at the municipal station.

## The Night Bela Vista Played Real Madrid

Sete Lagoas has four football clubs of note, and one with a story that has thinned with retelling but not lost its improbability. In 1959, Bela Vista Futebol Clube - a club from a working Minas Gerais city most foreign fans had never heard of - toured Europe. The tour was troubled by money problems and logistical chaos, but the club managed to step onto the pitch at the Santiago Bernabéu and hold its own in a highlight-reel performance against Real Madrid. The stadium was then at the beginning of its legend, Madrid was then the dominant force in European football, and Bela Vista was then a provincial Brazilian side on a shoestring tour. The details get blurrier with each telling, but the fact of the fixture remains.

## The Alligator's Arena

The second great football club of the city, Democrata, plays at the Arena do Jacaré - Alligator Arena, named after the team's mascot. Its official name is Joaquim Henrique Nogueira Stadium. For decades, the Clássico dos Sertões - the Countryside's Derby - pitted Democrata against Bela Vista, and the rivalry was sharpened by the fact that the two old home grounds sat just one block apart. Democrata's old arena has since been demolished; Bela Vista's original home still stands. When the Belo Horizonte giants Atlético Mineiro, Cruzeiro, and América-MG all needed somewhere to play during the 2010 and 2011 reforms of Mineirão and Independência stadiums, the Arena do Jacaré took them in. For two seasons, Brazilian top-flight football moved to Sete Lagoas.

## Galopé and Muchacho

Food in Minas Gerais is serious business, and Sete Lagoas has two dishes that exist almost nowhere else. Galopé combines chicken - galo - with pork feet - pé de porco - into something hearty and regional. Muchacho starts with a similar chicken-and-pork base but adds cheese, which in Minas is nearly a guarantee. These are peasant dishes that outlasted the era that made them, recipes that travel poorly because they are tied to the place that named them. Food, like football, like the seven-that-became-seventeen lakes, is how Sete Lagoas tells itself who it is.

## Flight Context

Located at 19.47°S, 44.25°W, 70 km north of Belo Horizonte in central Minas Gerais. Recommended viewing altitude 5,000-8,000 ft on approach to Confins. Visual landmarks: the seven official lakes scattered through the urban grid; the BR-040 corridor running south to Belo Horizonte. Nearest airports: Belo Horizonte Confins (SBCF) 50 km southeast, Belo Horizonte Pampulha (SBBH) 60 km southeast. Industrial plumes from pig-iron furnaces and the Iveco-Fiat plant are visible south of the city.
