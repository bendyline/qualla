# Belo Horizonte International Airport

Named for a president who never took office, built for traffic that took 22 years to arrive, now Minas Gerais's main air gateway.

**Location:** -19.6239, -43.9714

**Categories:** Airports in Minas Gerais, International airports of Brazil, Transport in Belo Horizonte

## Introduction

Tancredo Neves was elected president of Brazil in January 1985 and never took office. He fell ill on the eve of his inauguration, underwent emergency surgery, and died on April 21 of that year - the first civilian chosen to lead Brazil after two decades of military rule, and a man who never got to lead. The country grieved. Streets, schools, and institutions were renamed for him. On September 2, 1986, the federal government renamed Confins International Airport after Tancredo de Almeida Neves. The airport had opened two years earlier. It would take another twenty-two years for traffic to reach the million-passenger mark its planners had projected by 1990.

## A Field Too Far

The problem was always the distance. Confins sits 44 kilometers north of downtown Belo Horizonte - too far for a quick taxi ride, too far to feel convenient. When Infraero inaugurated the airport in 1984, the old airport at Pampulha was running at 120 percent of its 1.3-million-passenger capacity, and the new airport seemed destined to inherit everything. Instead, airlines and passengers stayed at Pampulha. A taxi to Confins cost about forty dollars. Buses were infrequent. The new terminal echoed. For most of two decades it handled a fraction of its capacity while Pampulha overflowed. The two airports shared a city and a market that stubbornly refused to rebalance them. Someone was going to have to force the issue.

## The March 2005 Decision

In March 2005 the government of Minas Gerais, backed by federal aviation authorities, restricted Pampulha to aircraft carrying no more than 50 passengers. One hundred and thirty flights transferred overnight from the old airport to Confins. Annual passengers at Confins jumped from 350,000 to about three million in that year alone. The airport that had been waiting for its moment since 1984 finally got it - not through organic growth but through regulatory force. The MG-10 highway, part of a larger project called Linha Verde, was improved to cut travel time from the city. An industrial airport project followed, offering tax exemptions to businesses willing to set up near the terminal. Confins began to look like the airport Infraero had always meant it to be.

## Concession and Expansion

On November 22, 2013, the Brazilian government auctioned operating rights for the airport. The winning consortium was Aerobrasil, branded as BH Airport, combining the Brazilian investment company CCR with 75 percent and the Swiss operator Flughafen Zurich AG with 25 percent. Infraero retained 49 percent under the concession terms. The new operators came in ahead of the 2014 FIFA World Cup, when Belo Horizonte would host six matches. A R$342.3 million investment plan had been announced in 2009 covering parking, runway extension, apron expansion, and terminal renovation. By 2016 the runway had been lengthened to 3,600 meters. Terminal 2 opened in December 2016, raising capacity to 22 million passengers per year. The long wait for Confins to become a real international gateway had finally ended.

## Gol, LATAM, Azul

Gol Airlines chose Confins as the location of its main maintenance facilities - a vote of confidence from one of Brazil's two largest carriers. Azul Brazilian Airlines, based in Campinas, built an extensive hub here, connecting Belo Horizonte to destinations across Brazil and, in the years since 2020, to Orlando, Fort Lauderdale, and seasonally to Argentine ski destinations like Bariloche and Mendoza. LATAM, the Chilean-Brazilian giant, expanded its routes including direct service to Porto Alegre starting in 2025. The airport code SBCF is now stamped on tickets from dozens of destinations. What was once an underused concrete expanse has become a genuine hub, serving roughly 10 million passengers in recent years and functioning as the air gateway to Minas Gerais's mining economy, its historical cities, and its sprawling metropolitan core.

## The Name Outlasting the Man

Tancredo Neves governed Minas Gerais in the 1980s before winning the 1985 presidential election. He was the compromise candidate who united civilian opposition to the military regime, a veteran politician who had served as prime minister under Joao Goulart in the early 1960s. His death on the eve of power turned him into something between a martyr and a lost possibility - Brazil might have had him in the chair, democratically, at the moment democracy returned. Instead it had his vice president, Jose Sarney, and it had his name attached to the airport that opened just before he should have taken office. The planes taxi past gate signs that carry his memory. Most travelers never learn why. A few remember. The airport serves the city either way - 44 kilometers north, waiting for each arrival, connected by the Linha Verde highway that gets a little faster every year.

## Flight Context

ICAO SBCF, IATA CNF. Coordinates 19.62 S, 43.97 W, elevation 827 meters, about 44 km north of downtown Belo Horizonte in the municipality of Confins. Single runway 16/34 at 3,600 meters with a planned parallel runway. Visible landmarks include the Belo Horizonte urban area to the south and the Serra do Curral range. Pampulha Airport (SBBH) lies closer to the city center and handles regional traffic. Weather is typically clear with afternoon thunderstorms during the October to March rainy season.
