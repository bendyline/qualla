# Belo Horizonte

Brazil's first planned modern city, laid out on graph paper in 1897 to replace a baroque capital that had run out of gold.

**Location:** -19.9211, -43.9389

**Categories:** Cities in Minas Gerais, Brazilian state capitals, Planned cities, UNESCO heritage

## Introduction

Ouro Preto ran out of gold. That was the fundamental problem Belo Horizonte was built to solve. By the 1890s the old capital of Minas Gerais was a baroque masterpiece whose economic moment had passed, its churches and cobblestones still beautiful but its mines exhausted and its republican intellectuals angry. When Brazil became a republic in 1889, the young government decided to start over. In 1893 Governor Afonso Pena selected a small village called Curral del Rey - the King's Corral, an old bandeirante farm - as the site. The urbanist Aarao Reis drew up a grid on paper that deliberately echoed Washington, D.C. On December 12, 1897, the new capital was inaugurated. The first planned modern city in Brazil opened, half-finished, waiting to see if anyone would come.

## The Grid That Broke Its Walls

Aarao Reis laid out perpendicular streets in the center, cut diagonally by avenues named after Brazilian states and indigenous tribes, and encircled the whole plan with the Avenida do Contorno. Workers were to live outside this ring - the trendy neighborhood called Funcionarios was reserved for government employees, and the plan assumed class would be legible on the map. It held for about a decade. By the 1920s the city had spilled past the Contorno, and by the end of the twentieth century it had absorbed 34 surrounding municipalities into what is now Greater Belo Horizonte, the third-largest metropolitan area in Brazil with six million people. The grid still structures the core. The rest of the city improvised around it, building favelas on the hillsides and industrial districts to the west in Contagem and Betim.

## Niemeyer's First Commission

In the 1940s a young mayor named Juscelino Kubitschek met a young architect named Oscar Niemeyer and handed him a lake. The Pampulha Complex - a Niemeyer-designed neighborhood around a large artificial lake to the city's north - became one of the first full-scale expressions of Brazilian modernist architecture. The Church of Sao Francisco de Assis, with its concrete parabolic vaults and Candido Portinari's tile mural of the Stations of the Cross, was so radical the Catholic Church refused to consecrate it for fourteen years. The casino closed in 1946 when gambling was banned nationally, reopening as the Pampulha Art Museum. The yacht club and ballroom joined the complex, which became a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2016. Kubitschek became president and commissioned Niemeyer to design Brasilia. The working relationship began on the shore of Pampulha.

## Right to Food

In 1993, under mayor Patrus Ananias de Souza, Belo Horizonte launched a public policy that quietly made it famous among urban planners worldwide. The city declared that citizens had a right to food - not charity, not welfare, but a right. Farmers' markets were set up across neighborhoods for direct sales, bypassing middlemen. Market prices were surveyed and posted publicly so consumers could compare. Participatory budgeting drew ordinary residents into municipal decisions. Infant mortality fell by half in a decade. In 2009 the city received the first Future Policy Award from the World Future Council, an international jury that included Vandana Shiva and Youssou N'Dour. The Vila Viva program urbanized favelas while keeping families in place, using 80 percent local labor. None of this made international news at the time. Cities quietly learned from BH instead.

## Metal, Music, Modernism

The Clube da Esquina - the Corner Club - started on a BH street corner in the mid-1960s and became one of the most influential movements in Brazilian music. Milton Nascimento emerged from it. So did Lo Borges, Beto Guedes, Toninho Horta, and the songwriter Fernando Brant, whose lyrics Nascimento set to music that crossed borders. The city also became Brazil's capital of heavy metal. Sepultura, the world's best-known Brazilian metal band, formed here. Sarcofago, one of the founding acts of black metal globally, came out of the BH scene in the 1980s. The contrasts keep going: the Palacio das Artes designed by Niemeyer houses theaters and galleries, the Circuito Cultural Praca da Liberdade ties together eleven museums around the former governor's palace, and the puppet theater group Giramundo has operated since 1970 with its own dedicated museum. Music, metal, marionettes, modernism - BH holds them all.

## The Pampulha Question

The city is still built on contradictions. It has the architectural ambition of Niemeyer's modernism and the daily reality of traffic-choked avenues. It has one of Brazil's best urban food security records and persistent favela poverty on its hillsides. It has a metro line that opened in the 1970s and, nearly fifty years later, still has only one line - Line 2 and Line 3 are promised but have been promised for decades. It has a tropical savanna climate cooled by its 852-meter elevation and a rainy season that floods parts of the low-lying city every year. It is Brazil's sixth-largest city and often its most overlooked, shadowed by Sao Paulo to the south and Rio to the southeast. The local answer, as always, is to go to the bar and argue about it. Belo Horizonte is used to being underestimated.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 19.93 S, 43.94 W at 852 meters elevation in central Minas Gerais. Served by Confins International Airport (SBCF) 44 km north for international and major domestic flights and Pampulha Airport (SBBH) in the city for regional traffic under 50 passengers. Highest point is Serra do Curral at 1,538 meters. Visible landmarks include Pampulha Lake with Niemeyer's modernist complex to the north, the Curral Ridge to the south, and the Mineirao football stadium.
