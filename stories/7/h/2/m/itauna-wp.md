# Itaúna

An Iron Quadrangle town 80 km from Belo Horizonte with roots in colonial sesmarias and bandeira expeditions, founded on a chapel donation in 1765 and now home to over 100,000 people.

**Location:** -20.0750, -44.5758

**Categories:** Municipalities in Minas Gerais

## Introduction

In 1675, a Portuguese-Brazilian named Lourenço Castanho Taques came up the São João River with an armed expedition and set up camp on its banks, fighting with the Cataguases people who already lived there. That camp was the first recorded European presence in what would become Itaúna. Ninety years later, in October 1765, a landowner named Manoel Pinto de Madureira donated the land for a chapel dedicated to Sant'Ana, and the Archdiocese of Mariana formally recorded the gift on 14 October. Historians today argue about who exactly was the first settler - Antônio Gonçalves da Guia, Manuel Pinto, Sergeant-Major Gabriel da Silva Pereira - but they agree that Madureira's chapel donation marks the town's beginning. Many a Brazilian town like this one can trace itself back to a piece of paper filed with a bishop.

## Bandeiras and Sesmarias

The colonial history of Itaúna is a story of land grants and armed expeditions. The *bandeiras* were privately-organized expeditions launched from São Paulo during the 17th century, nominally searching for gold and silver but often for indigenous people to enslave. The first bandeirante to reach this area was Lourenço Castanho Taques in 1675; the second was Manoel de Borba Gato, who arrived in 1710 after the War of the Emboabas and received a *sesmaria* - a Portuguese land grant - that covered the São João Valley. Before Borba Gato, the territory had passed through a remarkable chain of owners. Antônio Guedes de Brito - who sat on the interim junta that governed colonial Brazil from Bahia in the late 1670s - accumulated 160 leagues of land along the right bank of the São Francisco River between 1652 and 1684, a stretch that reached from Bahia down into what is now Minas Gerais. His daughter D. Isabel Maria Guedes de Brito inherited the territory. On 24 September 1739 a new sesmaria charter was granted to Manoel Pinheiro Diniz, who became the sixth and final major colonial landowner before the settlement proper began.

## A Chapel, a Donation, a Town

The foundational moment of Itaúna is a religious one. Manoel Pinto de Madureira - whose name appears sometimes incorrectly recorded as Manoel Pinto Moreira - donated the land for a chapel dedicated to Sant'Ana on 11 October 1765. The Archdiocese of Mariana recorded the donation three days later. The settlement that grew around the chapel took the name Santana do São João Acima - Santana of the Upper São João - and it remained under the ecclesiastical jurisdiction of Mariana for decades. The chapel itself was the town's center. Miguel Augusto Gonçalves de Souza, the local historian whose *História de Itaúna* is the principal source for the town's early years, describes Madureira as "undoubtedly the builder, in the broadest sense of the word, of the Chapel of Sant'Ana, and above all, its donor, the foundational core of the new settlement." Madureira had also tried - unsuccessfully - to find gold in the Jacuba Stream, a tributary of the São João.

## A Literary Twist

The history of Itaúna intersects with one of the founding works of Brazilian literature. The Arcadian epic poem *Caramuru*, written by José de Santa Rita Durão and published in 1781, tells the story of Diogo Álvares - a shipwrecked Portuguese sailor who lived among the Tupinambá people in 16th-century Bahia - and Catarina Paraguaçu, the indigenous woman he married. They are historical figures. Their daughter Apolônia Álvares is also documented. What connects this 16th-century story to 18th-century Itaúna is that Antônio Guedes de Brito, the Bahian nobleman who once owned the territory, was a descendant of Apolônia Álvares. Through this lineage, the vast land holdings that eventually became parts of modern Minas Gerais trace back to one of the earliest recorded Portuguese-Indigenous marriages of colonial Brazil - a thread connecting literature, genealogy, and land that runs straight through Itaúna's chain of ownership.

## Mining to Agriculture

The 18th and early 19th centuries saw Minas Gerais transition from a gold-mining economy to an agricultural one. As the mines gave out, rural activities took over, and Santana do São João Acima - never a significant mining town - settled into a steady agricultural rhythm. Livestock and some mining drove early settlement; by the early 19th century, agriculture was the dominant activity. For seventy-six years the settlement was administratively subordinate to Pitangui, a larger municipality to the northwest. On 3 April 1839 the Provincial Assembly created the Parish of Espírito Santo de Itapecerica, and Santana came under its jurisdiction for two years. On 7 April 1841 Santana itself was elevated to the category of Parish, which shifted migration flows and accelerated its growth. In 1850 the first primary school opened. The 1872 census counted 4,259 inhabitants, of whom 711 were enslaved - about 16.69 percent of the population - a proportion that reflects the labor economy of 19th-century interior Brazil. On 23 December 1874 the settlement became a district under Pará de Minas. On 17 February 1877 its post office opened. These are the visible markers of a town becoming itself, slowly and officially.

## The Two Chapels

One of the stranger features of Itaúna's history is that the town's two oldest chapels exchanged names. The original chapel - dedicated to Sant'Ana by Madureira's 1765 donation - sat on a hilltop. A second, smaller chapel dedicated to Our Lady of the Rosary was built in the 1840s by enslaved people working in their free time, expanded under the supervision of early vicars, and finished with interior paintings in 1875 by the Sabará artist Pedro Campos. The expansion cost the life of the carpenter João Júlio César Silvino, who died working on the church's interior roofing - likely the first recorded occupational accident in the city's history. Midway through that work, in 1853, the two chapels swapped names: the Rosário chapel in what is now the city center became the Chapel of Santana, and the original hilltop chapel became the Rosário Chapel. Names had been attached to locations; the names moved. Today Itaúna is a municipality of 97,669 people at the 2022 census (an estimated 103,000 by 2025), counted in the Iron Quadrangle and about 80 kilometers by road from Belo Horizonte, in the industrial and agricultural heart of central Minas Gerais. It has a university, municipal and state schools, and a history built from sesmarias, chapels, and the patient accumulation of parish records.

## Flight Context

Located at 20.07°S, 44.58°W in central Minas Gerais, about 70 km west-southwest of Belo Horizonte in a straight line (about 80 km by road). The city sits in the transitional zone between the iron-mining district and the agricultural uplands. Nearest airport: Belo Horizonte Confins (SBCF) about 80 km northeast. Best flown in dry winter months (May-September). Recommended viewing altitude: 5,000-8,000 ft to see the city's layout and the surrounding hills.
