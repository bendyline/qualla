# Carlos Drummond de Andrade Airport

Named for one of the great poets of the 20th century, Pampulha Airport served Belo Horizonte for 86 years before being retired to general aviation in 2019.

**Location:** -19.8519, -43.9506

**Categories:** Airports in Minas Gerais, Airports established in 1933, Transport in Belo Horizonte, Buildings named for writers, Carlos Drummond de Andrade

## Introduction

Carlos Drummond de Andrade was a poet, not a pilot. Born in the iron-mining town of Itabira in Minas Gerais in 1902, he became one of the most important voices in Portuguese-language poetry of the twentieth century - a writer whose work insisted on the dignity and strangeness of ordinary life. He died in 1987, and seventeen years later, on December 16, 2004, the airport that serves his home state capital was given his name. Pampulha Airport - formally now Pampulha-Carlos Drummond de Andrade Airport - sits in the northern Pampulha neighborhood of Belo Horizonte, 8 kilometers from downtown. It opened in 1933 as a support field for Brazilian Air Force flights between Rio de Janeiro and Fortaleza. For most of its life it was the airport of Belo Horizonte, the place travelers arrived when they came to see one of Brazil's most architecturally distinguished cities. In 2019 it stepped back. No scheduled flights operate here now. The old runway, extended from 1,500 to 2,505 meters over the decades, serves general aviation, maintenance, and flight schools. The poet's name remains.

## An Airport for a Growing City

In 1933, when Pampulha opened, Belo Horizonte was still a relatively young planned city - its grid laid out in 1897 to replace Ouro Preto as the capital of Minas Gerais. The airport's first commercial operator was Panair do Brasil, which in 1936 won a concession to fly between Rio de Janeiro and Belo Horizonte. Pampulha grew alongside the city. In 1943 the runway was extended to 1,500 meters by 45 wide. In 1953, to 1,700. In 1961, to 2,505 - enough to accommodate the jets that were about to change commercial aviation. By then, the district around the airport had itself been transformed. Juscelino Kubitschek, later president of Brazil and architect of Brasilia, had been mayor of Belo Horizonte in the 1940s and had commissioned Oscar Niemeyer to design a modernist cultural complex around the artificial Pampulha Lake just north of the airport. The curves that Niemeyer made famous in Brasilia - the undulating concrete of the Sao Francisco de Assis Church, the dance hall, the yacht club - were first drawn here, minutes from the runway.

## The Airport That Became Too Small

By the 1980s, Pampulha was struggling under its own success. Too many flights, too many passengers, not enough room to grow. The solution was Tancredo Neves International Airport in the neighboring municipality of Confins, which opened in 1984. But Confins was far - 40 kilometers from downtown Belo Horizonte - and airlines kept choosing Pampulha anyway. The result was that Pampulha stayed overcrowded and Confins stayed underused. In March 2005, the state government, backed by federal agencies, did something drastic. It restricted Pampulha to aircraft carrying up to 50 passengers. Larger flights had no choice but to move. In the following months, Confins filled up and Pampulha was reinvented as a regional and general aviation hub. For the 2014 FIFA World Cup, in which Belo Horizonte was a venue city - famously the scene of Germany's 7-1 defeat of Brazil in the semifinal - Pampulha received an 8.4 million reais upgrade: a new control tower, hangar improvements, an enlarged apron. The stadium capacity had a deadline. The airport met it.

## The Poet on the Runway

When Pampulha was renamed for Drummond in 2004, it joined a Brazilian tradition of naming airports for writers and artists - Rio's Tom Jobim (Galeao), Sao Paulo's Congonhas. Drummond, who had lived in Rio de Janeiro for most of his adult life as a civil servant, had never stopped writing about his home region. His poem Jose - with the famous refrain E agora, Jose, And now, Jose - became a kind of national koan. The Sentimento do Mundo, his 1940 collection, and A Rosa do Povo from 1945 are foundational Portuguese-language works. He was not a celebratory poet. He was a poet of hesitation, irony, the small human thing that will not yield to grand narratives. Putting his name on a runway has a certain quiet wit. He would probably have appreciated it. Probably. One never knows with Drummond - his poems rarely resolve, and his posthumous opinions are not on record.

## Since the Scheduled Flights Left

On June 30, 2019, Pampulha closed to scheduled commercial operations. From that day forward, it has been dedicated to general aviation, aircraft maintenance, and flight schools. Confins now handles all of Belo Horizonte's commercial traffic. The airport's operator also changed. From 1973 to 2020 it was run by Infraero, the federal airport authority. On June 17, 2020, administration was transferred to the Government of the State of Minas Gerais. The transition ended on December 31, 2020. On October 5, 2021, CCR - the Brazilian infrastructure group - won a 30-year concession to operate Pampulha. The airport has not been without incident. On April 20, 2021, a Learjet 35A overran the runway on landing, breaking through a perimeter fence; one pilot died. On March 6, 2024, a Federal Police Cessna 208 Grand Caravan lost engine power on takeoff from runway 31 and crashed back onto the field; two of the three aboard were killed. These are the reminders that general aviation carries its own risks - small planes, small margins, professionals who know all of this and fly anyway. Today Pampulha is quieter than it was. The Niemeyer buildings still ring the lake. Belo Horizonte still grows. The poet's name still hangs over the terminal.

## Flight Context

Pampulha-Carlos Drummond de Andrade Airport (SBBH / PLU) is at 19.85 S, 43.95 W, 8 km north of downtown Belo Horizonte in the Pampulha district of Minas Gerais. Elevation approximately 790 m (2,590 ft); single runway 13/31 at 2,505 m (8,218 ft). Dedicated to general aviation, aircraft maintenance, and flight schools since June 2019 - no scheduled commercial flights. For commercial service, use Tancredo Neves International (SBCF / CNF) at Confins, 40 km north. Pampulha's proximity to the Niemeyer complex at Pampulha Lake makes it a scenic approach. Watch for CCR-operated traffic patterns and active flight school activity. Afternoon thunderstorms common in summer.
