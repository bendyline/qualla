# Rádio Inconfidência

Belo Horizonte's public radio station has broadcast tango, soccer, saudade ballads, and Brazilian rock to Minas Gerais since 1936.

**Location:** -19.8997, -44.0544

**Categories:** Radio stations in Brazil, Mass media in Belo Horizonte, Public broadcasting, Brazilian radio networks

## Introduction

On the night of September 3, 1936, a voice came through the radios of Belo Horizonte for the first time announcing itself as Rádio Inconfidência. Tango music, a genre then sweeping across South America from the Buenos Aires cafés, became part of its early programming. The name chose itself: inconfidência, the Portuguese word for the 18th-century Minas Gerais conspiracy against Portuguese rule, which remains the most celebrated episode in the state's political identity. For ninety years the station has carried that name on the air, broadcasting across a territory roughly the size of France from a studio in the state capital.

## The Signal

Rádio Inconfidência broadcasts on 100.9 MHz FM and has long broadcast on 880 kHz AM, covering Belo Horizonte and the central portion of Minas Gerais; the AM signal has repeatedly faced state plans to switch it off. For listeners farther away, two shortwave frequencies, 6010 kHz and 15190 kHz, still carry the signal well beyond Brazil, logged by listeners abroad as recently as 2026. In the age of the internet those shortwave numbers feel archaic, but shortwave was exactly the technology that made the station A Voz de Minas para toda a América - 'the voice of Minas for all of America,' as one of its slogans promised. The AM and FM stations sometimes simulcast and sometimes run independent programming. The station is owned by the Minas Gerais Communication Company, the state's public broadcaster, which gives it a role closer to the BBC or NPR than to a commercial outlet.

## The Early Decades

In 1936, radio in Brazil was still a novelty. Rádio Inconfidência became one of the first stations in the state and built its early identity around live music, tango, and saudade ballads, songs of that distinctively Portuguese-language melancholy longing. The station also covered soccer. Brazilian football was rising to dominance; the station's broadcasts of national matches, Copa América games, and early World Cup coverage drew listeners who had no other way to follow their teams. It operated under early Brazilian radio call signs, moving from PRI-3 to PRK-1 and PRK-2 through the 1940s. The 1950s were a peak decade. By then, radio was the dominant medium in Brazil, and Inconfidência had become a cultural fixture of Belo Horizonte.

## Brasileiríssima and the Long Nights

In the 1980s, the station launched what became one of its best-loved programs: Brasileiríssima, broadcast in the late-night hours from midnight until 3 or 4 AM. The name is a superlative, 'most Brazilian,' and the playlist delivered on the claim, weaving together samba, bossa nova, MPB, and older styles into something like a national song archive on the radio. The slogan was Sempre Brasileiríssima, 'always most Brazilian.' Night owls developed the kind of loyalty to the program that public radio can inspire when it does its job well; it has since left the late-night slot. An FM station at 100.9 was added in 1979, giving the broadcast better audio quality for music programming. The 50th anniversary came in 1986, the 70th in 2006.

## The Lineup

The schedule, as it settled by the late 2000s, mixed public-service broadcasting with cultural programming. Mornings brought talk radio; afternoons mixed music and segments; evenings carried news. On Mondays at 9 PM, classical music took over. Weekends belonged to soccer, with state, national, Copa Libertadores, and Copa América matches broadcast from 4 PM to 8 PM, and broadcasts specifically covering Atlético Mineiro and Cruzeiro, the two great clubs of the state. Tango music from the 1920s through the 1960s filled the evening until 10 PM. At midnight, Memória Nacional took the air for three hours of historic Brazilian songs. Rock Brasileiro ran on weekends, 10 to 11 PM, a programming block added in mid-2009.

## The Voices

Its on-air voices have included Cristiano Batista, Déborah Rajão, Tina Gonçalves, Everton Gontijo, Ricardo Parreiras, Reny Parzewsky, Paulo Azeredo, and José Augusto Toscano. Public radio in Brazil, as elsewhere, depends on a particular kind of announcer: someone whose voice audiences invite into their kitchens and cars and workshops over years, who becomes a quiet presence in ordinary life. That is the relationship Inconfidência has cultivated. From Belo Horizonte the signal reaches out across the mountainous interior of Minas Gerais, into small towns where the station may still be the most reliable daily connection to the wider world. The logo features a transmitting signal alongside the first letter of the name, a simple graphic that captures what the station actually does. It transmits.
