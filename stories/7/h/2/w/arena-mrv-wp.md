# Arena MRV

Atletico Mineiro's new 44,892-seat stadium on the northwestern edge of Belo Horizonte, built through a pandemic and inaugurated in 2023 with a Paulinho goal against Santos.

**Location:** -19.9306, -44.0161

**Categories:** Football venues in Minas Gerais, Sports venues in Belo Horizonte, Clube Atletico Mineiro, Football in Belo Horizonte, Sports venues completed in 2023, 2023 establishments in Brazil

## Introduction

On April 20, 2020, a bulldozer broke ground on an empty lot in Belo Horizonte's California neighborhood. The country was locking down for COVID-19. Ten masked people watched the digger roll across the cleared earth. Clube Atletico Mineiro - the Galo, the Rooster, one of the three big clubs of Belo Horizonte - had been trying to build its own stadium for decades, had seen the land finally donated by Rubens Menin of MRV Engenharia, had sold off half of a shopping mall to raise the money, and had now, against every obvious obstacle, begun. Three years later, in April 2023, they cut the ribbon on a 44,892-seat stadium. On August 27 of that year, Atletico beat Santos 2-0 in the Campeonato Brasileiro, Paulinho scored the arena's first competitive goal, and Atletico had what every Brazilian club eventually chases: a home it actually owns.

## The Vote That Started It

The decision to build came on a September evening in 2017, at a meeting of Atletico's Deliberative Council in the club's Lourdes headquarters. Of 337 councilors voting, 325 said yes. The room included Ricardo Guimaraes, Afonso Paulino, and Ziza Valadades - former club presidents who had each, in their turn, tried and failed to get a stadium project off the ground. Alexandre Kalil was there too, a former Atletico president now serving as mayor of Belo Horizonte. Rubens Menin, the construction-company billionaire whose MRV Engenharia was Atletico's main sponsor, rose during the meeting to announce that he would donate the land where the stadium would go - a 60-million-real plot in the California neighborhood of the city's northwest. It was the kind of moment that changes a club's trajectory. Atletico had spent decades sharing Belo Horizonte's Mineirao stadium with crosstown rival Cruzeiro, paying rent and sharing sightlines. Now it would have a place of its own.

## Financing a Stadium by Selling a Mall

A stadium is an expensive object. The initial estimate of 410 million reals had to come from somewhere. Atletico owned 50.1 percent of the Diamond Mall shopping center, and it sold that stake for 250 million reals. MRV paid 60 million reals for the naming rights over ten years. The club pre-sold club seats and luxury suites for another 100 million, with Banco BMG guaranteeing 60 percent of that figure. The math added up, barely. Construction of a stadium in twenty-first-century Brazil also requires something other than money: it requires paperwork. The project passed select committees on Legislation and Justice, Environment, Public Administration, and Budget and Public Finance. The municipal assembly then approved the bill unanimously. In April 2019, the municipal environmental council gave the preliminary license - with fifty-five conditions attached.

## The Bird and the Bike Path

One of those conditions concerned a small bird called the capetinho-do-oco-de-pau, a hole-nesting species at risk of extinction that had been documented in the vicinity of the proposed site. The club had to develop a program to protect it. Another condition required bike paths linking the stadium to the Eldorado Metro Station. A third required Atletico to create a not-for-profit called the Instituto Galo to reinforce the social-interest character of the project. A fourth required a health care station and a day care center on the esplanade. The most significant environmental condition, though, was the forest compensation. Atletico had to conserve a patch of native vegetation more than twice the size of what the stadium would clear, somewhere in the same watershed. The club chose a plot inside Serra do Gandarela National Park in the municipality of Rio Acima, bought the land, and donated it to the Chico Mendes Institute for Biodiversity Conservation. Both sites - the stadium land in California and the conserved land in Rio Acima - drain to the Sao Francisco River, so the ecological accounting worked.

## Building Through a Pandemic

Racional Engenharia won the construction bid. The April 20, 2020 groundbreaking was small and masked. The country would spend the following year in a particularly grim run of COVID-19 cases, and construction sites across Brazil had to work out how to operate through it. Atletico's did: by March 2021, with the stadium still two and a half years from opening, the schedule was already eighteen percent complete. Designed by architect Bernardo Farkasvolgyi, the stadium would occupy 56,000 square meters, with about 35 percent of the total site given to the arena itself, 30 percent to the esplanade, 23 percent preserved as green area, and the remainder to gardens and paved access. The acoustic engineering was unusual for a football stadium: perforated tiles and insulating layers that would let the arena host major concerts without losing sound to the open sky, while keeping shows inside the bowl rather than spilling onto the esplanade.

## The First Goal

On April 15, 2023, about 9,000 people came to the inauguration. On July 16, former Atletico players - the Legends of the Galo - played a commemorative match with Dada Maravilha making the ceremonial kick-off. Dada, a striker from the 1970s and one of the great characters of Brazilian football, had waited his whole career for an Atletico stadium. He was 77 years old. Six weeks later, on August 27, the real thing happened. Atletico beat Santos 2-0 in the Campeonato Brasileiro Serie A. The first competitive goal at Arena MRV came from Paulinho, a striker born in July 2000, two generations after Dada. Behind him the stands held 44,892 seats - the sixth-largest private stadium in Brazil, the second-largest football stadium in Belo Horizonte after the Mineirao. The long project was, finally, not a project. It was a place.
