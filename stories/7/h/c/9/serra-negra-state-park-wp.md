# Serra Negra State Park

A mountainous refuge of Atlantic Forest, rocky fields and cerrado, where springs that feed three Brazilian river systems begin their journey.

**Location:** -18.0450, -42.8119

**Categories:** State parks of Brazil, Protected areas established in 1998, Protected areas of Minas Gerais, Atlantic Forest, Espinhaço Mountains

## Introduction

Water is the reason this park exists. In the dry Upper Jequitinhonha region of Minas Gerais - a place where rain is fickle and rivers matter more than gold - the mountains of Serra Negra act as a sponge, catching what clouds deliver and releasing it slowly into the streams below. Streams that feed the Jequitinhonha River, the Araçuaí River, and tributaries of the Doce River rise in this 13,654-hectare wilderness. Without these mountains, the valleys beyond would drink far less.

## A Range Sculpted Into Biosphere

The park sits atop the Espinhaço Mountains, the ancient spine of eastern Brazil that UNESCO recognized as one of the world's biosphere reserves in 2005. An old transmission tower stands on one of the region's highest points, at 1,581 meters - once the perch of TV Leste, now a lookout where the entire mountain region unfolds in folds of green and rust. From here the peaks run in all directions, each ridge hiding its own hollow of forest, its own waterfall catching the light. The park lies 33 kilometers from the town of Itamarandiba and 513 kilometers by road from Belo Horizonte, far enough from everything to feel like a different country entirely.

## Where Three Ecosystems Meet

Serra Negra protects one of the more unusual transitional zones in Brazil - an overlap where Atlantic Forest, cerrado savanna, and rocky highlands touch and blend. Cedro, braúna, ipê, peroba, and jacaranda grow in the lowland forests. Higher up, stands of canela-de-ema, a strange tree-like plant of the Vellozia family with bristling leaves, reach over three meters tall on the sandy soils. Several grasses are found nowhere else, and ferns, orchids, and velozias show high levels of endemism. Maned wolves pad through the cerrado at dusk. Pumas, ocelots, howler monkeys and titi monkeys live here too, while pacas, capybaras, and coatis share the watercourses. The park is an island of the original, mostly surrounded by eucalyptus plantations and cattle pasture.

## A Park Born of Crisis

The decree came in 1998 - number 39.907, signed on the 22nd of September - creating Serra Negra as a direct response to the social and ecological pressures eroding the Upper Jequitinhonha. Charcoal production had been stripping the slopes, and the springs the region depends on needed protection. The region counted among the poorest in Minas Gerais, and conservation planners hoped the park would seed a tourism economy. In 2007, a dry-season fire swept through much of the protected area. The vegetation has been recovering since, slowly, the way these landscapes do. In 2013 the state Public Ministry went to court charging that the forestry institute had left the park without a management plan, adequate facilities or staff, and with its land tenure unresolved. The work of protection, it turns out, is never finished.

## For the Few Who Make It Here

Visiting Serra Negra requires planning. The perimeter is marked but not fenced, and infrastructure is sparse. In the late 2010s one part of the park was open daily from 8:30 to 17:30, with visits arranged in advance through the administration; check with the state forestry institute (IEF) before going. Those who come find waterfalls tumbling through moss-dark ravines, orchids, lookouts and caves. Volunteer fire brigades have been trained, and environmental education has reached schools and communities around the park. Signs, gazebos, and ecological trails thread through the more accessible areas. It is a park that rewards patience - and one where the visitor remains a rare species.

## The Shape From Above

From cruising altitude, the park reads as a dark irregular shape against the patchwork of eucalyptus plantations and pastures that have otherwise reshaped the Minas Gerais uplands. The Espinhaço range runs roughly north to south, and Serra Negra marks one of its more remote stretches. In the rainy months the headwater streams braid silver against the darker forest. In the dry season, the vegetation loses its gloss but gains a kind of austere clarity - you can see the bones of the mountains, the pale cliffs where quartzite breaks through, the red dirt roads that lead toward the town of Itamarandiba, some 20 kilometers to the north-northwest.

## Flight Context

Located at 18.05°S, 42.81°W in the Espinhaço Mountains of northeastern Minas Gerais. Recommended viewing altitude 8,000-12,000 ft. Visual landmarks: the high point at 1,581 m with its old transmission tower is conspicuous; headwater streams of the Jequitinhonha and Araçuaí rivers radiate outward. Nearest airports: Diamantina (SNDT) about 90 km west-southwest, Governador Valadares (SBGV) about 130 km southeast, Montes Claros (SBMK) about 185 km northwest, Belo Horizonte Confins (SBCF) about 215 km southwest. Clear dry-season mornings (May-September) offer best visibility.
