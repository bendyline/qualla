# Attempted Assassination of Jair Bolsonaro

On September 6, 2018, on Halfeld Street in Juiz de Fora, Minas Gerais, Adelio Bispo de Oliveira drove a knife into Jair Bolsonaro's abdomen as supporters carried the presidential candidate on their shoulders.

**Location:** -21.7607, -43.3469

**Categories:** 2018 Brazilian general election, 2018 crimes in Brazil, Failed assassination attempts in South America, Jair Bolsonaro, Stabbing attacks in 2018, Knife attacks in South America, Attacks in Brazil

## Introduction

It was mid-afternoon on Halfeld Street, the main commercial strip in downtown Juiz de Fora, Minas Gerais. Jair Bolsonaro, a federal deputy running for president of Brazil, was riding on the shoulders of his supporters through a dense crowd when a 40-year-old man named Adelio Bispo de Oliveira drove a knife into his abdomen. Bolsonaro grimaced and clutched his abdomen, and his security detail got him out of the crowd. He was rushed to the Santa Casa de Misericordia, Juiz de Fora's central hospital, where surgeons would find three perforations in his small intestine, a wound to his large intestine, and an injured abdominal vein that had cost him about 40 percent of his blood volume. The attack would upend a campaign and an election all at once. The assailant was arrested on the spot, would be declared not criminally responsible by reason of mental illness, and would be held indefinitely in a maximum security facility in Campo Grande.

## Halfeld Street, Mid-Afternoon

Bolsonaro's campaign schedule that Thursday had him arriving in Juiz de Fora by eleven in the morning. Lunch with business leaders. A stop at the Ascomcer Hospital. A rally in front of City Hall. The candidate was being escorted by Federal Police, as all candidates were during the 2018 campaign. What the cameras caught was the moment he flinched on the shoulders of the supporters carrying him, his hand going to his abdomen. Bolsonaro said afterward that at first he felt only a blow, and that once he realized what had happened, 'the pain was unbearable.' By what was reported as a matter of centimeters, the knife had missed the larger veins and arteries nearby, according to the hospital's technical director. It still did grave damage. He needed four bags of blood. He was moved the next day to the Albert Einstein Israelite Hospital in Sao Paulo for continued care.

## Adelio Bispo de Oliveira

The man arrested on Halfeld Street had a biography that seemed to explain everything and nothing. Adelio Bispo de Oliveira was forty years old, a former evangelical missionary, and a member of the Socialism and Liberty Party (PSOL) from 2007 to 2014, though the party condemned the attack and investigators found no evidence of any party role in it. He was already being prosecuted for a 2013 bodily injury charge. He told investigators he had attacked Bolsonaro at the behest of God. His Facebook page railed against the political class generally, against then-President Michel Temer, and against Freemasonry, which he believed was running a global conspiracy. A niece told reporters he had distanced himself from his family and held, in her words, troubled ideas. Data from his cell phone showed he had been tracking Bolsonaro's movements through Juiz de Fora for hours, photographing the hotel where the businessmen's lunch was held, City Hall, and other stops on the candidate's schedule. On July 5, 2018 - two months before the attack - he had turned up at a shooting club called Clube de Tiro 38 in Sao Jose, Santa Catarina, after being told it was frequented by two of Bolsonaro's sons. Across two inquiries the Federal Police would analyze terabytes of files, hundreds of hours of video, more than 6,000 messages and some 40,000 emails. Their conclusion: he acted alone.

## Twenty-Three Days in the Hospital

Recovery took most of September. Bolsonaro spent the first days in intensive care, waiting for his intestines to resume function. The first medical bulletin came on September 9 - abdominal condition improving, patient walking briefly. By September 11 he was taking food orally for the first time. On September 12 he was rushed back to surgery to clear adhesions that had blocked his intestines. By September 16 he was out of the ICU. September 19 brought oral liquids; September 22 brought discharge from the semi-intensive unit; September 29 brought the ride home from the hospital. He kept campaigning from his hospital bed through social media, and after discharge stayed mostly at home - skipping the TV Globo debate on October 4, citing medical advice, while TV Record aired an interview he had recorded earlier that day in the same time slot. Kept off the streets, he still led the race. On October 7 he finished first in the first round with 46.03 percent of the valid votes. On October 28 he defeated Fernando Haddad of the Workers' Party with 55.13 percent of the valid votes and became president-elect of Brazil. In April and May of 2025, he would spend about three weeks in the DF Star hospital in Brasilia after another surgery for an intestinal obstruction linked to the original injury - a reminder that a wound of that depth never fully heals.

## The Sentence and the Museum

On June 14, 2019, federal judge Bruno Savino handed down Adelio Bispo de Oliveira's sentence. The defendant's lack of legal responsibility due to mental illness had been proven, Savino wrote, so he was exempt from conventional criminal punishment. His pre-trial detention was converted into indefinite internment. He was sent to the Federal Maximum Security Penitentiary in Campo Grande because, Savino added, sending him into the regular prison system would put his life at concrete risk. Savino also noted that Adelio had indicated he still intended to kill Bolsonaro. The sentence left him acquitted but held indefinitely under a security measure, subject to periodic psychiatric review, because the court had concluded he was too ill to be punished and too dangerous to be released. The knife, a 30-centimeter blade, was sent by court order to the Federal Police's criminal museum in Brasilia for its historical value; as of 2023 it was still kept there in a sealed envelope while the museum decided whether to display it.

## Conspiracies and the Long Shadow

The attack never stopped being argued over. Bolsonaro supporters created a documentary - A Facada no Mito - suggesting the attacker had accomplices, possibly on Bolsonaro's own security team. Bolsonaro opponents created a different documentary - Bolsonaro e Adelio - suggesting the whole thing had been staged because there seemed to be too little blood at the scene. Investigators found no support for either. The Federal Police would reiterate their finding as recently as June 11, 2024 - Adelio acted alone - a conclusion Bolsonaro said he did not accept. Along the way, people with no connection to the attack had their names dragged into conspiracy theories: Jean Wyllys, the former PSOL federal deputy, who won damages from several politicians who had linked him to it; Livia Gomes Terra, a bank worker and trade unionist from Juiz de Fora falsely accused of handing over the knife, whose accuser was convicted of criminal slander and who said she developed post-traumatic stress disorder from the harassment that followed; and people in photographs who were misidentified as Adelio. The knife wound healed, slowly, with repeated complications. The political wound it opened - in a country where political violence had been present for decades but rarely this visible, this televised, this immediately turned into content - has not closed. Bolsonaro won the 2018 election and served one term. His recovery, his scar, and his near-death in Juiz de Fora remained part of his political identity in the years that followed.

## Flight Context

Located at 21.76 S, 43.35 W in Juiz de Fora, a city of about 540,000 (2022 census) in southeastern Minas Gerais state, Brazil. The attack took place on Halfeld Street, a pedestrian commercial thoroughfare in the central downtown. The Santa Casa de Misericordia hospital, where Bolsonaro received emergency surgery, is in central Juiz de Fora. Nearest airport: Juiz de Fora/Francisco de Assis (SBJF), approximately 5 km southwest of the city center, with the Zona da Mata Regional Airport (SBZM) at Goiana farther out to the northwest. The city sits at about 680 m elevation in hill country along the BR-040 highway, between Rio de Janeiro (about 180 km south by road) and Belo Horizonte (about 260 km north-northwest by road). Best viewed at 3,000-5,000 ft AGL to appreciate the urban layout within a bowl of rolling terrain. Frequent fog in the lower valleys at dawn, particularly in winter months (June-August).
