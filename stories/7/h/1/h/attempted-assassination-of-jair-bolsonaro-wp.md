# Attempted Assassination of Jair Bolsonaro

On September 6, 2018, on Halfeld Street in Juiz de Fora, Minas Gerais, Adelio Bispo de Oliveira drove a knife into Jair Bolsonaro's abdomen as supporters carried the presidential candidate on their shoulders.

**Location:** -21.7607, -43.3469

**Categories:** 2018 Brazilian general election, 2018 crimes in Brazil, Failed assassination attempts in South America, Jair Bolsonaro, Stabbing attacks in 2018, Knife attacks in South America, Attacks in Brazil

## Introduction

It was a little after 3:30 in the afternoon on Halfeld Street, the main commercial strip in downtown Juiz de Fora, Minas Gerais. Jair Bolsonaro, a federal deputy running for president of Brazil, was riding on the shoulders of his supporters through a dense crowd when a 40-year-old man named Adelio Bispo de Oliveira drove a knife into his abdomen. Bolsonaro lurched backward. The crowd recoiled, then surged. Security pulled the candidate out. He was rushed to the Santa Casa de Misericordia, Juiz de Fora's central hospital, where surgeons would find three perforations in his small intestine and a gash in a major abdominal vein that had emptied out between forty and fifty percent of his blood volume. The attack would change Brazilian politics, a recovery, and an election all at once. The assailant was arrested on the spot, would be declared not criminally responsible by reason of mental illness, and would be held indefinitely in a maximum security facility in Campo Grande.

## Halfeld Street, 3:30 PM

Bolsonaro's campaign schedule that Thursday had him arriving in Juiz de Fora by eleven in the morning. Lunch with business leaders. A stop at the Ascomcer Hospital. A rally in front of City Hall. The candidate was being escorted by Federal Police, as all candidates were during the 2018 campaign. What the cameras caught was the moment his body rocked forward on the shoulders of the supporters carrying him, his hand flying to his lower abdomen, his mouth opening in pain. Bolsonaro would describe later the sensation of being punched, not stabbed, until he looked down at his hand. The knife had entered at an angle that missed - by a margin the hospital director later called a matter of centimeters - the larger abdominal vessels that would have killed him within minutes. It still did catastrophic damage. He needed four transfusions of whole blood during the initial surgery. He was moved the next day to the Albert Einstein Israelite Hospital in Sao Paulo for continued care.

## Adelio Bispo de Oliveira

The man arrested on Halfeld Street had a biography that, to investigators, seemed to explain everything and nothing. Adelio Bispo de Oliveira was forty years old, a former evangelical missionary, and a member of the Socialism and Liberty Party (PSOL) from 2007 to 2014, though he had not been an active member for years. He was already being prosecuted for a 2013 bodily injury charge. He told investigators he had attacked Bolsonaro at the behest of God. His Facebook page railed against the political class generally, against then-President Michel Temer, and against Freemasonry, which he believed was running a global conspiracy. A niece told reporters he had distanced himself from his family and held, in her words, troubled ideas. Data from his cell phone showed he had been tracking Bolsonaro's movements through Juiz de Fora for hours, photographing the hotel where the businessmen's lunch was held, City Hall, and the rally location. On July 5, 2018 - two months before the attack - he had turned up at a shooting club called Clube de Tiro 38 in Sao Jose, Santa Catarina, a club frequented by two of Bolsonaro's sons. The Federal Police would eventually analyze two terabytes of files, 350 hours of video, 600 documents, and 1,200 photographs taken from his phone and devices. Their conclusion: he acted alone.

## Forty Days in the Hospital

Recovery took most of September. Bolsonaro spent the first days in intensive care, breathing oxygen, receiving transfusions, waiting for his intestines to resume function. The first medical bulletin came on September 9 - abdominal condition improving, patient walking briefly. By September 11 he was taking food orally for the first time. On September 12 he was rushed back to surgery to clear adhesions that had blocked his intestines. By September 16 he was out of the ICU. September 19 brought oral liquids; September 22 brought discharge from the semi-intensive unit; September 29 brought the ride home from the hospital. In between, he had run a presidential campaign from a hospital bed - declining to appear at a TV Globo debate, giving his first post-attack interview to TV Record from his home, speaking to voters through social media. The campaign strategy, forced by the injury, turned out to be electorally successful. On October 7 he won the first round of voting with 46.03 percent. On October 28 he defeated Fernando Haddad of the Workers' Party with 55.13 percent of the valid votes and became president-elect of Brazil. In April and May of 2025, he would spend 17 days in the DF Star clinic in Brasilia for another surgery related to intestinal obstruction from the original injury - a reminder that a wound of that depth never fully heals.

## The Sentence and the Museum

On June 14, 2019, federal judge Bruno Savino handed down Adelio Bispo de Oliveira's sentence. The defendant's lack of legal responsibility due to mental illness had been proven, Savino wrote, so he was exempt from conventional criminal punishment. His pre-trial detention was converted into indefinite internment. He was sent to the Federal Maximum Security Penitentiary in Campo Grande because, Savino added, transferring him to the regular prison system carried a concrete risk that other inmates would kill him. Savino also noted that Adelio had indicated he still intended to kill Bolsonaro. The sentence made him something Brazilian law rarely produces: a defendant held indefinitely, with no trial end date, because the state had concluded he was too ill to be punished and too dangerous to be released. The knife he had used was collected by the Federal Police and placed on display at the corporation's museum in Brasilia, where it is occasionally shown to visiting officials.

## Conspiracies and the Long Shadow

The attack never stopped being argued over. Bolsonaro supporters created a documentary - A Facada no Mito - suggesting the attacker had accomplices, possibly on Bolsonaro's own security team. Bolsonaro opponents created a different documentary - Bolsonaro e Adelio - suggesting the whole thing had been staged because there seemed to be too little blood at the scene. Both theories were wrong. The Federal Police would reiterate their finding as recently as June 11, 2024 - Adelio acted alone. Along the way, dozens of innocent people had their names dragged into conspiracy theories and won defamation judgments: Jean Wyllys, the former PSOL federal deputy living in Barcelona; Livia Gomes Terra, a PT trade unionist falsely accused of supplying the knife, who developed post-traumatic stress disorder from the harassment that followed; people in photographs who were misidentified as Adelio; pastors and politicians who had endorsed false claims and were ordered to pay damages. The knife wound healed, slowly, with repeated complications. The political wound it opened - in a country where political violence had been present for decades but rarely this visible, this televised, this immediately turned into content - has not closed. Bolsonaro won the 2018 election and served one term. His recovery, his scar, and his near-death in Juiz de Fora remained part of his political identity for the rest of his public life.

## Flight Context

Located at 21.76 S, 43.35 W in Juiz de Fora, a city of roughly 570,000 in southeastern Minas Gerais state, Brazil. The attack took place on Halfeld Street, a pedestrian commercial thoroughfare in the central downtown. The Santa Casa de Misericordia hospital, where Bolsonaro received emergency surgery, is in central Juiz de Fora. Nearest airport: Juiz de Fora/Francisco de Assis (SBJF), approximately 5 km southwest of the city center, and Juiz de Fora Regional (SDJF) slightly farther out.
