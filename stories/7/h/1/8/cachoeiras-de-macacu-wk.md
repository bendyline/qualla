# Cachoeiras de Macacu

A municipality 90 kilometers by road northeast of Rio where the Serra do Mar crashes down into lowland, the Macacu River runs toward Guanabara Bay, and locals call their home the Land of Crystal Waters.

**Location:** -22.4628, -42.6528

**Categories:** Rio de Janeiro state, Southeast Brazil, Atlantic Forest, Municipalities of Brazil

## Introduction

The name tells you everything and almost nothing. Cachoeiras de Macacu means Macacu Waterfalls, and the macacu is a tree once tapped for ink - a detail that preserves a working landscape from before anyone thought to name it for tourism. Drive about 90 kilometers northeast from Rio de Janeiro, the last stretch on the RJ-116, past the last subdivisions where the metropolis finally thins out, and you hit the wall of the Serra do Mar. The sprawl stops at the foot of it. Beyond lie waterfalls with names like Tenebroso and Furna da Onça - the Gloomy One, the Lair of the Jaguar - in a municipality of about 57,000 people most Brazilians from outside Rio state have never heard of.

## Two Halves of a Municipality

Cachoeiras is split by geography into two places that happen to share a name. The southern half lies in the lowland at about 54 meters elevation, flat country where the Macacu River rolls through and where the urban core has grown around the highway. The northern half climbs into the mountains, the rugged front range of the Serra do Mar that separates the coastal plain from the interior. The Macacu is one of the main rivers feeding Guanabara Bay, the big brackish estuary that Rio de Janeiro wraps around, which means rain that falls on these peaks eventually runs past Sugarloaf on its way out to sea. Local rivers - the Bengala, the Branco, the Guapiaçu - thread the landscape, and the APA Macacu Environmental Protection Area was created to protect the Macacu basin.

## Land of Crystal Waters

The town's nickname is not marketing. The waterfalls are the reason people come. Tenebroso, Sete Quedas, Barba, Samambaia, Chapadão, Furna da Onça - each one a pocket of cold mountain water tucked into a fold of the Serra do Mar. The access usually requires a hike through Atlantic Forest, a biome that once blanketed Brazil's Atlantic coast from Rio Grande do Norte to Rio Grande do Sul and now survives only in fragments. Cachoeiras holds some fine ones. Trekkers, climbers, and rappellers come here too. The town sits within reach of the Reserva Ecológica de Guapiaçu - REGUA - a private reserve, built on a farm the Locke family has owned since 1907, where Nicholas Locke and partners such as Britain's World Land Trust have spent a quarter-century restoring Atlantic Forest.

## The Reach of the Metropolis

The story of Cachoeiras in the twenty-first century is the story of Rio pressing outward. The RJ-116 highway is the vector. A van line from the Central do Brasil area and buses of the 1001 company from Rio's long-distance bus station (around R$45 in 2023) connect downtown Rio directly to Cachoeiras de Macacu - a commuter rail of the road, really, carrying workers, students, and weekenders. The urban expansion stopped at the foot of the mountain. The Serra do Mar draws the line. South of the mountain range, Cachoeiras is becoming another suburb of Grande Rio, with its traffic and its strip development along the RJ-116. North of it, the waterfalls keep cold.

## The Bay Below

From above, the relationship between Cachoeiras and Rio makes sudden visual sense. The Macacu River carries water collected in the Serra do Mar out through the lowland, joining smaller rivers, arriving finally at Guanabara Bay, the stage where Rio has performed for more than 450 years. Guanabara is famously polluted now, but its watershed begins in forests like the ones Cachoeiras still holds. What happens upstream shapes what happens downstream - so Cachoeiras de Macacu matters not just as a getaway from Rio but as one of the places the metropolis's water comes from: the Macacu and Guapiaçu feed the Imunana-Laranjal system that supplies Niterói and São Gonçalo across the bay. The APA Macacu designation, the ecological reserves, the regulation of development in the upper basin - all of it is a slow-motion attempt to keep a working watershed alive in the shadow of a 12-million-person metropolitan area.

## A Day Trip and a Departure Point

Most visitors come for a weekend, to hike to a waterfall and swim in water that, by the standards of a humid coastal summer, is shockingly cold. Simple pousadas and a couple of in-town hotels handle the overnight crowd. The highway that delivered you can take you elsewhere: 80 kilometers southwest to Niterói and the ferry back across the bay to Rio; 110 kilometers to Saquarema, known as Brazil's surfing capital; 170 kilometers to Búzios, Cabo Frio, and Arraial do Cabo, where turquoise water comes ashore against white sand. Cachoeiras, in this sense, is hinge country - a pause in the drive between the hard edges of the metropolis and the beaches where most people are actually going.

## Flight Context

Located at 22.46 S, 42.65 W in Rio de Janeiro state, roughly 73 km northeast of downtown Rio in a straight line (about 90 km by road). Elevation in the urban core is about 54 m, rising sharply to the north into the Serra do Mar, which provides the dominant visual terrain. Nearby airports: Rio de Janeiro Galeão (SBGL) and Santos Dumont (SBRJ), both about 72 km to the southwest. Best viewed at 3,000-5,000 ft to appreciate the contrast between lowland urban expansion and the forested mountain wall. Afternoon clouds commonly stack on the Serra do Mar during the wet season (November-March).
