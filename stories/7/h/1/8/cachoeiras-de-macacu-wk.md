# Cachoeiras de Macacu

A municipality 90 kilometers east of Rio where the Serra do Mar crashes down into lowland, the Macacu River runs toward Guanabara Bay, and locals call their home the Land of Crystal Waters.

**Location:** -22.4628, -42.6528

**Categories:** Rio de Janeiro state, Southeast Brazil, Atlantic Forest, Municipalities of Brazil

## Introduction

The name tells you everything and almost nothing. Cachoeiras de Macacu means Macacu Waterfalls, and the macacu is a tree once tapped for ink - a detail that preserves a working landscape from before anyone thought to name it for tourism. Drive 90 kilometers east of Rio de Janeiro on the RJ-116, past the last subdivisions where the metropolis finally thins out, and you hit the wall of the Serra do Mar. The highway stops at the foot of it. Beyond lie waterfalls with names like Tenebroso and Furna da Onca - the Gloomy One, the Lair of the Jaguar - in a municipality of 59,000 people most Brazilians from outside Rio state have never heard of.

## Two Halves of a Municipality

Cachoeiras is split by geography into two places that happen to share a name. The southern half lies in the lowland at about 54 meters elevation, flat country where the Macacu River rolls through and where the urban core has grown around the highway. The northern half climbs into the mountains, the rugged front range of the Serra do Mar that separates the coastal plain from the interior. The Macacu is the largest river feeding Guanabara Bay, the big brackish estuary that Rio de Janeiro wraps around, which means every raindrop that falls on the northern peaks eventually runs past Sugarloaf and Copacabana on its way out to sea. Local tributaries - the Bengala, the Branco, the Guapiacu - thread the landscape, and the whole region rolls under the protection of the APA Macacu Environmental Protection Area.

## Land of Crystal Waters

The town's nickname is not marketing. The waterfalls are the reason people come. Tenebroso, Sete Quedas, Barba, Samambaia, Chapadao, Furna da Onca - each one a pocket of cold mountain water tucked into a fold of the Serra do Mar. The access usually requires a hike through Atlantic Forest, a biome that once blanketed Brazil's coast from the Amazon mouth to the Uruguayan border and now survives only in fragments. Cachoeiras holds some of the best fragments. Trekkers, climbers, and rappellers come here for pitches that big wall climbers in Rio treat as an in-town warm up. The town sits within reach of the Reserva Ecologica de Guapiacu - REGUA - a private reserve where British and Brazilian conservationists have spent decades restoring forest on abandoned ranchland.

## The Reach of the Metropolis

The story of Cachoeiras in the twenty-first century is the story of Rio pressing outward. The RJ-116 highway is the vector. Bus lines and van services from Central do Brasil station connect downtown Rio to Cachoeiras de Macacu in under two hours for around R$45 on the 1001 bus company line - a commuter rail of the road, really, carrying workers, students, weekenders, and people who gave up on Rio's rents for a cheaper life in the lowland half of the municipality. The urban expansion stopped, as the locals put it, at the foot of the mountain. The Serra do Mar draws the line. South of the mountain range, Cachoeiras is becoming another suburb of Grande Rio, with its traffic and its strip development along the RJ-116. North of it, the waterfalls keep cold.

## The Bay Below

From above, the relationship between Cachoeiras and Rio makes sudden visual sense. The Macacu River carries water collected in the Serra do Mar out through the lowland, joining smaller rivers, arriving finally at Guanabara Bay, the stage where Rio has performed for 450 years. Guanabara is famously polluted now, but its watershed begins in forests like the ones Cachoeiras still holds. What happens upstream shapes what happens downstream - so Cachoeiras de Macacu matters not just as a getaway from Rio but as one of the places Rio's water comes from. The APA Macacu designation, the ecological reserves, the regulation of development in the upper basin - all of it is a slow-motion attempt to keep a working watershed alive in the shadow of a 12-million-person metropolitan area.

## A Day Trip and a Departure Point

Most visitors come for a weekend, to hike to a waterfall and swim in water that, by the standards of a humid coastal summer, is shockingly cold. Simple pousadas and a couple of in-town hotels handle the overnight crowd. The highway that delivered you can take you elsewhere: 80 kilometers south to Niteroi and the ferry back across the bay to Rio; 110 kilometers east to Saquarema, which calls itself Brazil's surfing capital; 170 kilometers further to Buzios, Cabo Frio, and Arraial do Cabo, where the continental shelf drops away and turquoise water comes ashore against white sand. Cachoeiras, in this sense, is hinge country - a pause in the drive between the hard edges of the metropolis and the beaches where most people are actually going.

## Flight Context

Located at 22.46 S, 42.65 W in Rio de Janeiro state, roughly 90 km east-northeast of downtown Rio. Elevation in the urban core is about 54 m, rising sharply to the north into the Serra do Mar, which provides the dominant visual terrain. Nearby airports: Rio de Janeiro Galeao (SBGL) and Santos Dumont (SBRJ), both within 70 km. Best viewed at 3,000-5,000 ft to appreciate the contrast between lowland urban expansion and the forested mountain wall. Afternoon clouds commonly stack on the Serra do Mar during the wet season (November-March).
