# Central Rio de Janeiro Atlantic Forest Mosaic

A patchwork of 29 protected areas stitching together the last fragments of one of Earth's most threatened rainforests.

**Location:** -22.4140, -42.6140

**Categories:** Protected area mosaics of Brazil, Protected areas of Rio de Janeiro (state), 2006 establishments in Brazil, Protected areas of the Atlantic Forest

## Introduction

Fly inland from Copacabana and the story flips. The same Atlantic that carved Rio's famous beaches once lapped an unbroken wall of rainforest stretching the entire length of Brazil - a green cathedral of some 1.3 million square kilometers that botanists now consider one of the planet's most biodiverse and most endangered ecosystems. About 88 percent of it is gone, bulldozed for sugar, coffee, cattle, and cities. What survives in the hills east of Rio is a shattered mirror. Since 2006 Brazilian conservationists have been trying to put the pieces back together.

## A Mosaic, Not a Park

Most protected landscapes are single reserves with fixed borders. The Central Rio de Janeiro Atlantic Forest Mosaic is something newer and stranger: 29 separate conservation units, totaling nearly 296,000 hectares, managed as a single interconnected system. The idea came out of a December 2005 project to create three linked mosaics along the Serra do Mar Ecological Corridor - the spine of mountains running parallel to Brazil's southeast coast. Conservation International, the Global Environment Facility, the MacArthur Foundation, the World Bank, and the Government of Japan all put money behind it. The Ministry of the Environment formally recognized the mosaic on December 11, 2006. By 2010, the original 22 units had grown to 29 and a strategic plan coordinated them all.

## From Sea Level to the Clouds

The mosaic climbs from the mudflats of Guanabara Bay to 2,316 meters at Tres Picos State Park in a single geographic sweep. Mangroves root in the coastal meanders, fed by sediment the rivers carry down from the mountains - a concentration of them lives in the Guapimirim Environmental Protection Area and the Guanabara Ecological Station. Walk inland and the mangroves give way to dense alluvial rainforest, then lowland and submontane forest in the Bacia do Rio Macacu and Guapi-Guapiacu protection areas. Higher still, the trees thin into montane and high montane forest, and finally alpine meadows near the peaks. From bay to summit, the mosaic spans five distinct vegetation zones.

## Fourteen Municipalities, One Ecosystem

The mosaic sprawls across fourteen municipalities - a mouthful of Portuguese place names that rarely appear in travel articles, among them Cachoeiras de Macacu, Duque de Caxias, Guapimirim, Mage, Nova Friburgo, Nova Iguacu, Petropolis, and Teresopolis. Some are sleepy hill towns founded by Swiss colonists. Others are industrial suburbs of Rio with the better part of a million residents. The mosaic's genius is that it ignores the political lines that divide them, letting one ecological logic run across the whole landscape.

## What Was and What Remains

Before Portuguese ships arrived in 1500, dense lowland rainforest was the dominant vegetation here, followed by submontane and montane rainforest. Semideciduous montane forest and alpine meadows were the rarest types even then. Five hundred years of clearing for sugarcane, coffee plantations, cattle pastures, and metropolitan sprawl have reduced the Atlantic Forest to roughly 12 percent of its original extent, mostly in fragments. Within this mosaic specifically, 511 surviving patches of forest and mangrove cover 177,557 hectares. Those numbers hide the damage they describe - 511 pieces, where once there was one. Reconnecting even a few of them is the real work.

## Why It Still Matters

The Atlantic Forest holds species found nowhere else: golden lion tamarins, woolly spider monkeys, maned three-toed sloths, and hundreds of bird and amphibian species biologists are still describing. About seven in ten Brazilians live within the biome, and its watersheds feed their cities' water supplies. The forests stabilize slopes that would otherwise collapse onto the towns below in the heavy rains, as they did catastrophically in Petropolis in 2022 and Teresopolis in 2011. The mosaic is not scenery. It is infrastructure - ecological, hydrological, and civic. The effort to link 29 fragmented units into one functioning system is a bet that fragmentation is not destiny, and that a forest cut to pieces can still be stitched back toward whole.

## Flight Context

Centered near 22.41 degrees S, 42.61 degrees W, the mosaic covers a broad arc inland and east of Rio de Janeiro. Best scale for viewing is 6,000 to 10,000 feet AGL where the contrast between dark intact forest and surrounding farmland or suburbs reads sharply. Look for the jagged peaks of Serra dos Orgaos north of Guanabara Bay - the Tres Picos (Three Peaks) profile marks the highest point in the mosaic at 2,316 meters. Rio/Galeao (SBGL) lies about 40-45 nm southwest of the center point; Rio/Santos Dumont (SBRJ) is slightly further. Approach from the coast to appreciate the sea-to-cloud forest gradient.
