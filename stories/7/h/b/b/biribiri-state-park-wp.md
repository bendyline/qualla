# Biribiri State Park

A ghost village of 1,200 workers sits inside a 16,999-hectare park of waterfalls, maned wolves, and a bell cast in the factory that fed them.

**Location:** -18.1454, -43.6189

**Categories:** State parks of Brazil, Cerrado, Industrial heritage, Espinhaco Mosaic

## Introduction

The clock on the village church came from the Portuguese royal family. The bell was cast in the factory itself, made by the same workers who lived in the small valley town where, at the factory's peak, 1,200 people spun textiles. When the mill closed in 1973, the village emptied. The church stayed. The houses still stand, and today some of them hold small restaurants and rooms for visitors. Vila de Biribiri is not a ruin. It is a village still owned, still maintained, simply unoccupied by the work that built it. Walking its stone paths, you are crossing an abandoned industrial town surrounded by 170 square kilometers of cerrado wilderness, in a state park that ranks among the most visited in Minas Gerais.

## The Bishop's Factory

Joao Antonio dos Santos, Bishop of Diamantina, founded the Estamparia textile mill in 1876 in what was then a fazenda in the Serra do Espinhaco. It was one of the early textile factories of Minas Gerais. Water from the Biribiri River drove the machinery through hydraulic wheels, belts and pulleys. Workers and their families lived in houses built around the factory and its church. For nearly a hundred years the mill ran, the river turned, the looms clacked. Then the factory closed in 1973, and the village emptied out. Estamparia SA still owns the factory and the church. The village has been protected as heritage by the state's IEPHA since 1994, and its buildings remain in remarkably good condition. Two restaurants now serve the day-trippers who come out from Diamantina, about 15 kilometers away.

## Cerrado on the Espinhaco

Biribiri State Park covers 16,999 hectares in the Serra do Espinhaco, the quartzite mountain range that gave Diamantina its diamonds, and so its name. Altitudes range from 720 meters in the north to 1,480 meters in the south - enough vertical relief to hold rocky high grasslands, gallery forest, and the tough shrub-and-grass cerrado, one of South America's most threatened biomes. Vegetation here includes Vellozia squamata of the rocky fields, the native pequi tree called Caryocar brasiliense whose fruit shows up in mineiro cuisine, jacaranda, pau-santo, mangaba - the region's dry-country flora. The wettest month is December. Temperatures average 18 Celsius through the year. It is cerrado at its most rugged and accessible.

## Wolves and Caracaras

The maned wolf - Chrysocyon brachyurus, a tall russet predator that looks like a fox on stilts - lives in the park alongside cougars and crested caracaras. The giant anteater walks here, probing termite mounds with its long tongue. The rock cavy, a small rodent shaped like a guinea pig, scrambles over quartzite ledges. Three bird species draw specialists to the park: the lesser nothura, a partridge-like ground bird; the helmeted manakin with its red crown; and the hyacinth visorbearer, a hummingbird found only in Minas Gerais that flashes iridescent green, blue and violet over the rocky fields.

## Waterfalls and the Slave Trail

About six kilometers from the entrance, the Cachoeira do Sentinela drops into a pool visitors can swim in. Roughly six kilometers further on is the Cachoeira dos Cristais, reachable by car. The Caminho dos Escravos - the Slave Trail - runs twenty kilometers from Diamantina toward the old district of Mendanha, much of it through the interior of the park: an eighteenth-century route for the region's diamond trade, stretches of it paved in stone by enslaved Africans. The name itself is a marker of who built the infrastructure that made colonial Minas Gerais rich - people forced into the work. Other pools called Agua Limpa and Estudante provide swimming spots. As of 2016 the park was open every day and admission was free; a state plan to hand visitor services to a private concessionaire, debated through 2026, could change that. May through July are cold and dry, the best hiking months.

## The Park That Exists Because It Does

Biribiri State Park was created by decree 39.909 of September 22, 1998, named after the Fazenda Biribiri. It joined the Espinhaco Mosaic of conservation units in 2010. And like many Brazilian state parks, it has struggled with enforcement - in April 2013 the Minas Gerais Public Ministry charged that the State Forestry Institute was failing to plan for, staff, or equip Biribiri and other conservation units. In the mid-2010s a single caretaker worked from a shack at the entrance, and infrastructure was minimal. Go in the dry months. Hire a guide. The hyacinth visorbearer does not advertise itself.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 18.15 S, 43.62 W in the Serra do Espinhaco of northern Minas Gerais, near the historic diamond city of Diamantina, about 290 km by road north of Belo Horizonte. Elevations range from 720 to 1,480 meters. Nearest airports are Diamantina (SNDT) about 10 km south-southwest and Belo Horizonte-Confins (SBCF) about 170 km south-southwest. Visible landmarks include the Jequitinhonha River basin, the quartzite ridges of the Espinhaco range, and the abandoned village of Vila de Biribiri with its church spire.
