# Catimbau National Park

A semi-arid park in Pernambuco that turns from brown to green with the first rains, and holds more than 2,000 caves and cave drawings that may be as much as 6,000 years old.

**Location:** -8.6167, -37.1500

**Categories:** national-park, archaeological-site, cave-art, caatinga, brazil, pernambuco

## Introduction

When the first rains of the wet season fall, the brown hillsides of Catimbau turn lush and green with young plants. When the rains stop and the dry season returns, the green fades back to brown, and cactus and thorny brush take over the slopes. Visitors often take this semi-arid corner of Pernambuco, in northeastern Brazil, for desert, but the place refuses to hold one shape for long. Beneath the shifting surface lies what is often called the second-largest archaeological site in Brazil: a park with more than 2,000 caves, and cave drawings that may be as much as 6,000 years old.

## Feast or Famine

The park belongs to the caatinga, a biome whose plants have adapted to the "feast or famine" rainfall of the region. The adaptation clearly works. More than 600 plant species have been identified in Catimbau, including many kinds of cactus, and the park sits in the transition between two vegetation zones, the agreste and the sertão, which makes a mosaic of landscapes with high biodiversity.

The wildlife is varied too. More than 190 species of birds have been recorded here. Deer move through the scrub, along with many small species of rodents, and the dry, rocky hills are home to a number of endemic reptiles, including small lizards that thrive in the desert-like terrain.

## Carved by Dry Wind

Stone is the park's other great artist. Red and gray sandstone formations have been carved by driving dry winds over millennia, and the hills and canyon walls are perforated by caves - some deep, though most are quite shallow.

Brazil created the park as part of its national parks system in 2002, on December 13 of that year. It covers 62,300 hectares, or about 154,000 acres, spread across three municipalities: Buíque, Ibimirim and Tupanatinga. Two agencies share the work of looking after it. ICMBio administers the park's natural resources, as it does for other national parks, while the archaeological site is overseen by IPHAN, the National Institute of Historic and Artistic Heritage.

## Pictures in the Rock

Catimbau has been designated a National Archaeological Heritage Site. The park holds more than 2,000 caves, and dozens of cataloged archaeological sites hold prehistoric rock paintings. At Pedra da Concha, the cave art is thought to be several thousand years old, and people have lived in this area for about 6,000 years.

People are part of this land as well. The park lands were the traditional ancestral home of the Kapinawá, an Indigenous people of northeastern Brazil. Kapinawá Indigenous land adjoins and overlaps the park, and Kapinawá families live both inside it and around it today. They say the park's rules restrict building and access to their sacred sites.

## Bring Water

Getting there takes some effort, which suits the place. The nearest large airport with international commercial service is in Recife, and the park lies 290 km west of that city by highway BR-232 as far as Arcoverde. From Arcoverde, you turn south toward Buíque and watch for the sign to the park entrance. Travelers without a car can take one of the several daily buses from Recife to Arcoverde, a trip of four to five hours, and finish the last stretch of roughly 40 km to Vila do Catimbau by taxi, which takes close to an hour.

There is no park entry fee. At the entrance, guides can - and should - be hired, at about R$250 a day for a group of up to seven at last report (October 2025), and it pays to negotiate how long the guide will stay and for how much. There are no restaurants within the park, only in small towns outside it, so food needs planning, and water needs carrying: about 3 liters per person per day. Hiking and mountain biking are the ways to see the formations, the caves and the vast, changeable landscape. Come when the hills are green and Catimbau will look like a different park from the one the dry season leaves behind. Afterward, Recife and Olinda lie back toward the coast, and farther afield sits Chapada Diamantina National Park.

## Flight Context

Catimbau National Park is centered near 8.62°S, 37.15°W in Pernambuco state, about 250 km west of Recife in a straight line (290 km by road via BR-232 to Arcoverde). The nearest large airport with international commercial service is Recife/Guararapes-Gilberto Freyre International (SBRF). The park covers 62,300 hectares of semi-arid caatinga, so look for a broad expanse of scrubby terrain broken by red and gray sandstone hills and canyon walls. Around 3,000 feet above ground level gives a good view of the formations, and low-angle morning or late-afternoon light brings out their relief. The season changes the look of the whole park: green after the rains, brown in the dry season.
