# Aracaju

Sergipe's capital did not grow into its shape — an engineer drew it as a chessboard in 1855, and the grid still runs straight down to the river.

**Location:** -10.9167, -37.0500

**Categories:** Aracaju, Sergipe, Cities in Brazil, Planned cities, Northeast Region, Brazil, Coastal cities

## Introduction

Most Brazilian cities happened. Aracaju was drawn. On 17 March 1855 the president of Sergipe province, Inácio Joaquim Barbosa, moved the capital out of colonial São Cristóvão and onto a sandy plain at the mouth of the Sergipe River, and the engineer Sebastião José Basílio Pirro laid the new city out in strict geometry — a grid so regular that contemporaries compared it to a chessboard, with every street running down to the water. It made Aracaju the second purpose-planned state capital in Brazil, three years behind Teresina. Walk the centre today and the plan is still doing its job: you can stand at almost any downtown corner, look one way and see the river.

## The Argument Was About Ships

São Cristóvão had been the capital since the sixteenth century and had every claim of seniority. What it did not have was water deep enough for the ships that mattered. Sitting inland, it could be reached by river craft but not by ocean-going vessels, and by the 1850s Sergipe's sugar needed to leave by sea. Barbosa's decision was commercial before it was anything else.

It was also unpopular. Moving a capital away from its churches, its notables and its three centuries of habit is not the sort of thing a provincial president does without making enemies, and the new site had problems of its own. Early twentieth-century accounts describe the sandbar at the river entrance as exceptionally dangerous, with the port frequented only by coasting vessels of light draught. The city that was founded to be a proper harbour spent decades explaining why it was not quite one.

## Cashews of the Parrots

The name is older than the plan. Aracaju comes from the Tupi-Guarani ará acaiú — ará for parrots, acaiú for the cashew fruit — the cashew of the parrots, which is a fair description of a coastal plain where cashew trees grow in sand and flocks come for the fruit. A village called Santo Antônio de Aracaju was recorded here in 1699, founded by an Indigenous Brazilian remembered as João Mulato.

Sand is still the ground truth of the place. The city sits on a low sandy plain, and there are dunes inside the municipal limits. The Sergipe River forms the eastern edge; the Poxim runs through the middle. Water is never more than a few blocks away in any direction.

## The Orla

Nobody claims Atalaia is the most beautiful beach in the Northeast. What it is, for six kilometres and about nine from downtown, is one of the best-organised urban waterfronts in Brazil. A major refurbishment of the beachfront avenue turned it into a night-time promenade: plastic chairs pushed into the sand near Avenida Rotary for beer and green coconut, restaurants and bars by the dozen, clusters of bronze statues, a go-kart track, a large playground.

The Oceanário de Aracaju sits along the same strip, run in partnership with Projeto Tamar, Brazil's long-running sea-turtle conservation programme. Order crab. It is the dish here, usually attacked with a small wooden mallet and a great deal of napkin, and the fish arrives fried or as moqueca.

## A Capital of Modest Size

The 2022 census put Aracaju at 602,757 people, which makes it the least populous of the Northeast's capitals and the thirty-third largest municipality in Brazil. Greater Aracaju, taking in Nossa Senhora do Socorro, Barra dos Coqueiros and old São Cristóvão, reaches 932,210.

Since 24 September 2006 the Ponte Construtor João Alves has carried traffic across the Sergipe River to Barra dos Coqueiros, opening the northern beaches to the city in a way ferries never did. Downtown holds the Mercado Municipal Antônio Franco and its neighbours, where the sertão arrives in sacks — rapadura, dried meat, cashews, clay figures. The Museu da Gente Sergipana, meaning roughly the Museum of the Sergipe People, does the state's culture as sound and projection rather than glass cases.

The climate is hot and humid year round, averaging 26 degrees Celsius with about 1,695 millimetres of rain. The economy runs on services, industry and petroleum: Sergipe's offshore and onshore oil is a substantial part of why the capital's GDP reached around 16.4 billion reais in 2020.

## Flight Context

Aracaju lies at 10.92 degrees south, 37.05 degrees west, on the Atlantic coast of Brazil's Northeast, at the mouth of the Sergipe River. Terrain is effectively flat and at sea level, so the visual cues are all water and pattern: the Sergipe River estuary and its sandbar to the east, the Poxim threading through the built-up area, the six-kilometre arc of Atalaia beach to the south, and the Ponte Construtor João Alves reaching north to Barra dos Coqueiros. From 3,000 to 6,000 feet the 1855 street grid is unmistakable — a rectangular lattice in the old centre that breaks into irregular modern suburbs at the edges. Santa Maria Airport (SBAR/AJU) sits 12 kilometres from downtown at 7 metres elevation with a single 2,200-metre asphalt runway, 12/30. Alternates are Maceió (SBMO) roughly 200 kilometres northeast along the coast and Salvador (SBSV) to the southwest. Expect coastal haze, humid air and a persistent onshore breeze; convective build-ups are common in the wetter months from April to July.
