# Tambaba

A kilometre of sand in Conde, Paraíba — the first beach in Brazil where naturism was permitted by municipal law, and the first place in the Southern Hemisphere to host the world naturist congress.

**Location:** -7.3658, -34.7972

**Categories:** Nude beaches, Beaches of Brazil, Landforms of Paraíba, Naturism in Brazil

## Introduction

In 2008 a one-kilometre beach in the Brazilian state of Paraíba beat the Calabria region of Italy by 137 votes to 99 and won the right to host the 31st World Congress of the International Naturist Federation. It was the first time the congress had come to Latin America, or to the Southern Hemisphere at all. The beach was Tambaba, in the municipality of Conde, and it had earned the invitation the way institutions usually do: by going first, and by writing the rules down.

## The First Beach With a Law

Tambaba is the first beach in Brazil where naturism was permitted by municipal law, and that distinction carries more weight than it might sound. Naturist beaches elsewhere in the country tend to run on custom, local tolerance and a certain amount of hoping nobody complains. Tambaba runs on a statute. The Federação Brasileira de Naturismo counts it among a small group of officially recognised naturist beaches nationally, and it is the one the others cite as precedent. The name comes from Tupi and refers to shell remains — the debris of shellfish, which is a fair description of what the tide leaves along the waterline.

## The Rules Are the Institution

Naturism here is administered by an association, Tambaba Nua, and it comes with conditions that are posted, enforced and not open to negotiation. Complete nudity is required to enter the naturist sector — partial dress is not an option, and neither is spectating from the edge. Filming and photography are prohibited without authorisation. Sexual behaviour of any kind is entirely forbidden. So are drugs. The cumulative effect of that list is to make the place ordinary, which is precisely the intention. What happens at Tambaba is a regulated practice with a governing body, a code of conduct and a membership culture behind it, closer in spirit to a swimming club than to anything the word nudist tends to summon up.

## Two Beaches on One Shoreline

The beach is divided. On one side are the naturists; on the other, everyone who prefers to keep their clothes on. The two sectors share the same sand, the same warm Atlantic and the same line of cliffs behind. Tambaba is short — about a kilometre, set between Arapuca to the north and Barra do Graú to the south, in the Jacumã district of Conde on the southern Paraíba coast, roughly thirty kilometres from João Pessoa. The land behind the beach rises only about five metres. UOL Viagens has put Tambaba on a list of the twenty most beautiful beaches in Brazil, which in this country is a fiercely contested category.

## The Road Changed Everything

For years the last stretch of road in was unpaved, and that did as much to regulate visitor numbers as any bylaw could. It ended when the Ministro Abelardo Jurema highway, the PB-008, was completed and the route from João Pessoa was surfaced end to end. Meanwhile the expanded BR-101 running Recife–João Pessoa–Natal brought the whole southern Paraíba coast — Tabatinga, Praia Bela, Coqueirinho — within comfortable reach of three state capitals, and tourism has become one of the more important things the Paraíba economy does. A 150-hectare development, the Tambaba Country Club Resort, is closely tied to the beach. Getting here is easier than it has ever been. The rules at the top of the sand have not moved.

## Flight Context

Tambaba lies at 7.366 degrees south, 34.797 degrees west, on the Atlantic coast of Paraíba about 30 kilometres south of João Pessoa and roughly 115 kilometres north of Recife. Ground elevation is about 5 metres, with low vegetated cliffs immediately behind the sand. From 1,500 to 3,000 feet AGL it appears as a short bright crescent, barely a kilometre long, notched into an otherwise continuous line of reddish cliff and coconut plantation, with Arapuca beach immediately north and Barra do Graú south. The PB-008 highway runs parallel a short distance inland, joining the BR-101 corridor to the west. Nearest field is João Pessoa–Presidente Castro Pinto International (SBJP/JPA), about 45 kilometres north; Recife/Guararapes (SBRF/REC) is the southern alternate. Steady southeasterly trades, generally good visibility from October to December, heavier showers and lower cloud March to July.
