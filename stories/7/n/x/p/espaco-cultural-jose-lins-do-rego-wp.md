# Espaço Cultural José Lins do Rego

Sérgio Bernardes wrapped a planetarium, a comics library, two theatres and a novelist's sewing machine under a single steel roof called the White Wing.

**Location:** -7.1221, -34.8453

**Categories:** Entertainment venues in Brazil, Cultural centers in Brazil, Modernist architecture, Planetaria, João Pessoa, Paraíba

## Introduction

In a display case in João Pessoa there is a sewing machine. It belonged to the Rego Cavalcanti family, and on it was sewn the layette for a baby born at Engenho Corredor, in the Paraíba municipality of Pilar, on 3 June 1901. The baby grew up to be José Lins do Rego, who put the sugar plantations of the Northeast into Brazilian literature more completely than anyone before or since. The machine now sits near the middle of a 22,978-square-metre steel-and-concrete complex that carries his name — one of the largest cultural buildings in Paraíba, and one of the stranger pieces of modern architecture in the Brazilian Northeast.

## The Boy from the Engenho

Rego's cane cycle runs to five novels — Menino de Engenho in 1932, Doidinho in 1933, Bangüê in 1934, O Moleque Ricardo in 1935 and Usina in 1936 — with Fogo Morto arriving in 1943 as the summation. Together they trace the collapse of the old engenho, the family mill with its big house and its chapel, as the industrial usina swallowed it. He wrote from inside that world, raised on a family sugar estate whose social order had been built on slavery and was still populated by the people slavery had made. His books do not look away from it: the senzala stands in them as plainly as the casa-grande. One critic remarked that this universe would never again exist except in José Lins do Rego's novels. He took his law degree at Recife, moved to Rio de Janeiro, was elected to Chair 25 of the Academia Brasileira de Letras on 15 September 1955, and died in Rio on 12 September 1957, aged 56.

## Sérgio Bernardes Builds in Steel

The Espaço Cultural opened in April 1982, under the government of Tarcísio de Miranda Burity, on the site of the old Paraíba Aeroclub. Its architect was Sérgio Bernardes — not, as people sometimes assume of any large modernist gesture in this city, Oscar Niemeyer, whose Cabo Branco station arrived twenty-six years later on the cliffs east of here. Bernardes worked in steel, and this was the first significant metal-framed structure in the Paraíba capital. It occupies a sloping block roughly 350 metres by 140, under a single great roof the project called Asa Branca, the white wing. Free plan, horizontal openings, inside running into outside: the modernist vocabulary is all present and entirely unapologetic. At the time it was proposed the scheme was thought controversial, mostly on the grounds of sheer size.

## Six Thousand Stars and a Dinosaur Footprint

The planetarium here was the first in the Northeast. Inside a dome twelve and a half metres across, a Zeiss SpaceMaster projects six thousand stars and seventy-eight constellations, along with the solar system, nebulae and galaxies, and the sky as seen from any point on Earth in any season. Down the way is the Estação Ciência, inaugurated on 30 March 1990 during Burity's second government and, at the time, only the second science station in the country. Its seven hundred square metres hold working laboratories in physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics and astronomy. They also hold whale and human skeletons, Paraíba minerals, replicas of the rock inscriptions at Pedra do Ingá, and casts of the dinosaur tracks from the Vale dos Dinossauros at Sousa — the deep past of the state, indoors and to scale.

## Books, Comics, and a Music School Like No Other

The Juarez da Gama Batista Library is the largest public library in Paraíba: more than a hundred thousand volumes, three hundred readers at a time across open tables and thirty-six study booths, and roughly six hundred visitors a day. A few doors along, the Gibiteca Henfil — a comics library that grew out of a Federal University of Paraíba extension project — holds thousands of Brazilian and foreign comic books and hundreds of fanzines, with complete runs going back to the 1980s. The Anthenor Navarro State Music School, founded by the Paraíba maestro Gazzi de Sá in 1931 and housed in the complex since 1983, teaches more than 1,700 students, some of them six months old. And the Escola Especial de Música Juarez Johnson is the first music school in Brazil created exclusively for students with disabilities, staffed by musicians working alongside a speech therapist, an occupational therapist, a psychologist, a physiotherapist and a nurse.

## What the Building Keeps

The José Lins do Rego Museum opened on 19 March 1985. It holds more than five thousand catalogued volumes from the writer's own library, along with letters, manuscripts, photographs, canvases, commendations and the personal objects — the sewing machine among them. Around it sit the rest of the tenants: the Paulo Pontes Theatre, seating 660 and still the room where the state's most important productions are staged; the arena theatre formally named for Leonardo Nóbrega, the Paraíba theatre-maker who died in 1997; the Archidy Picado Gallery underground with its 130 works; the José Siqueira music research centre, founded in 1987 by the musicologist Domingos de Azevedo Ribeiro; the Cine Bangüê, 160 seats, showing what the commercial screens will not; and a covered square that can take fifteen thousand people. It is open every day from eight in the morning until ten at night, and it is a great deal of building for one city — which was rather the objection in 1982, and is now the reason it works.

## Flight Context

The Espaço Cultural José Lins do Rego stands at 7.122 degrees south, 34.845 degrees west, in the Tambauzinho district of João Pessoa, on the corridor between the historic centre and the beaches, a short distance off Avenida Epitácio Pessoa. Site elevation is roughly 40 metres. The complex is easy to identify from the air: a single very large low roof spanning a sloping block about 350 by 140 metres, conspicuously flat and pale against a dense grid of mid-rise apartment blocks, with the planetarium dome as a distinct circular element. Best seen from 1,500 to 2,500 feet AGL. João Pessoa–Presidente Castro Pinto International (SBJP/JPA), in Santa Rita, lies about 12 kilometres west of downtown; Recife/Guararapes (SBRF/REC) is roughly 115 kilometres south. Tropical monsoon climate — expect heavy rain and low cloud from March to July, with October to December the clearest months.
