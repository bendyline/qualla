# Ponta do Seixas

A survey in 1941 settled it by 1,683 metres: the easternmost corner of the continental Americas is not a cliff or a cape but an ordinary Paraiba beach.

**Location:** -7.1553, -34.7931

**Categories:** Landforms of Paraíba, Extreme points of Brazil, Headlands of Brazil, João Pessoa, Paraíba

## Introduction

Ponta de Pedras, up the coast in Pernambuco, had a reasonable claim on the title. In 1941 Brazilian naval officers ended the argument the way such arguments end — with instruments and arithmetic. Measured properly, a low spit of pale sand southeast of João Pessoa pushed farther into the Atlantic than any other point on the mainland of the Americas, and it did so by roughly 1,683 metres. That spit is Ponta do Seixas. The corner of a double continent turns out to be a wide, bright beach with vendors working the sand.

## The Margin Was 1,683 Metres

Continental extremes are usually decided by geology. This one was decided by surveying. At roughly 7°09' south and 34°48' west, Ponta do Seixas lies about eight kilometres southeast of João Pessoa, the capital of Paraíba, on the coastal highway that runs north toward the port of Cabedelo. Nothing about the place announces itself. The shoreline simply runs out of east to give, then bends away southward. What the 1941 survey established was that it bends a little later here than anywhere else on the mainland — that the tip of the Americas is not a headland you have to climb but a strip of beach you can walk barefoot.

## The Fine Print on "Easternmost"

The claim needs its qualifier, because three different superlatives get tangled here. Count the islands, and the easternmost point of the Americas is Nordostrundingen, on the northeastern shoulder of Greenland at 11°29' west — a frozen headland above 81 degrees north, more than twenty degrees of longitude ahead of Paraíba. Ask instead for the easternmost point of Brazilian territory, and the answer is the islands of Trindade and Martim Vaz, far out in the South Atlantic. Ponta do Seixas holds the title that remains, and it is the one people actually mean: the easternmost point of the continental Americas, the last of the mainland before the ocean takes over.

## Behind the Sand, the Tabuleiros

The land steps up abruptly behind the beach. These flat-topped tables of sedimentary rock are called tabuleiros, and they shape the whole Paraíba coast — a green shelf ending in a bright wall above the sand. Rain is generous on this eastern edge, which is why the tabuleiros stay green while the interior of the Northeast bakes under the same sun. Follow the highway north and it delivers you to Cabedelo, where the Paraíba River meets the sea. Go south and the white beaches simply keep going, kilometre after kilometre, with the ocean always on your left.

## The Lighthouse Beside the Point

About 800 metres north stands the structure everyone photographs instead. The Cabo Branco lighthouse, opened in 1972, is a triangular concrete tower eighteen metres tall, painted white with a horizontal black band, its three winglike projections spreading 3.5 metres above the floor — a shape meant to suggest a sisal plant, the crop that once underwrote the state's economy. Its focal plane sits forty-six metres above the sea, and the light is a real working aid to navigation: a white flash every ten seconds, visible some 27 nautical miles out to sea. It stands on a cliff the Atlantic is steadily eating.

## Two Places, One Postcard

Visitors routinely collapse Cabo Branco and Ponta do Seixas into a single destination, and foreigners do it almost every time. They are separate beaches in separate official neighbourhoods, and only one of them is the continental corner — the sandy one, to the south, without the famous tower. The confusion is understandable and mostly harmless. From the cliff at Cabo Branco the whole João Pessoa coastline unrolls to the north; walk down and south to the point itself and the view narrows to sand, surf, and open water. The record-holder is the plainer of the two, which is often how geography works.

## Flight Context

7.155°S, 34.793°W, at sea level on the Paraíba coast. Best viewed on a low coastal run at 1,500–3,000 ft AGL, tracking south from Cabedelo. The unmistakable navigation feature is the white triangular Cabo Branco tower on its cliff about 800 m north of the point; south of it the coastline turns sharply from an east-facing shore to a south-facing one, and that hinge is Ponta do Seixas. Nearest airport is Presidente Castro Pinto International (SBJP) at João Pessoa, a few minutes to the northwest; Campina Grande (SBKG) lies inland. Humid tropical coast: expect scattered cumulus and haze in the wet season, best light and visibility in the early morning.
