# Kahal Zur Israel Synagogue

The first synagogue built anywhere in the Americas stood on a Recife street the locals called Rua dos Judeus, and when the colony that protected it fell, its congregation scattered into the founding stories of Jewish communities across two continents.

**Location:** -8.0613, -34.8713

**Categories:** jewish-history, religious-sites, colonial-history, museums, brazil

## Introduction

The Dutch garrison holding Recife in January 1654 was beaten and knew it, but it would not sign the surrender until the Portuguese put one guarantee in writing: the Jews of the city were to be allowed to leave unharmed. It was an unusual clause to argue over while a city was changing hands, and it says a great deal about what the previous eighteen years had been. The congregation it protected had worshipped for eighteen years in a house on a street the neighbours called Rua dos Judeus - the Street of the Jews - and it was the first Jewish congregation in the Americas to have a building of its own.

## The Street of the Jews

Kahal Zur Israel - the Congregation Rock of Israel - was established in 1636, six years into Dutch rule, at what is now 197 Rua do Bom Jesus in Recife's old port district. Its founders were Sephardic Jews from Portugal and Spain who had fled forced conversion, and they were joined by New Christians already living in the colony, families baptised under duress who had kept what they could of their practice in private and who may well have helped build the place. One of the men behind it had an unusual résumé. Moses Cohen Henriques had led a Jewish contingent to the island of Itamaracá in 1630 and settled from there in Recife; around 1636, retiring from a career privateering for the Dutch, he helped his brother Abraham Cohen found the synagogue. It was the first synagogue erected anywhere in the Americas.

## The First Rabbi in the New World

In 1642 Moses Cohen Henriques invited Isaac Aboab da Fonseca out from Amsterdam, and Recife acquired what was probably the first appointed rabbi in the Americas - a distinction he shares with his colleague Moses Raphael de Aguilar. By then the congregation had a synagogue, a mikveh and a yeshiva, among the earliest in the New World. Aboab served twelve years, through the sugar boom and then through nine years of war in which the Jews of Recife fought alongside the Dutch, urged on from the other side by a Jesuit who complained that they kept open synagogues to the scandal of Christianity. He went back to Amsterdam after 1654 and became chief rabbi of its Portuguese community. In 1656 he was one of the scholars who excommunicated Baruch Spinoza; later he pressed for the great Esnoga, the Portuguese Synagogue that opened on the Amsterdam canals in 1675. A rabbi who had preached in a house on a tropical street ended his career in the most magnificent Sephardic building in Europe.

## What the Surrender Bought

When the capitulation took effect, the community dissolved outward along the sea lanes it had always used. Aboab and most of his congregation went back to Amsterdam. Others did not. In September 1654, twenty-three refugees from Dutch Brazil reached the small Dutch port of New Amsterdam, on the island of Manhattan. Director Peter Stuyvesant tried to turn them away. He did not get his way: in 1655 the colony's Jews were granted official permission to settle. They were not allowed a public synagogue for generations, but their congregation - Shearith Israel - endures as the oldest Jewish congregation in the United States. The chain of causation is often told too tightly, as though the twenty-three sailed straight from Recife harbour to Manhattan; the documented fact is simply that they were refugees out of Dutch Brazil, arriving in a month when Dutch Brazil had ceased to exist.

## The Mikveh Under the Floor

The original building survived a remarkably long time for something whose community had gone. It stood into the early twentieth century before being torn down, and the address slipped into ordinary commercial use: by the late 1900s the site carried a two-storey house with a pair of shops on the ground floor. Archaeologists settled the question of what lay underneath. Excavation confirmed the location and uncovered elements of the seventeenth-century structure, including the mikveh, the ritual bath, cut down into the ground. In 2001 the decision was taken to open a museum there. The building that went up was designed to echo the synagogues that Sephardic Jews from Spain and Portugal built in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and inside it holds a Torah and a bema - and, left open to view, the excavated stonework of the original.

## Twenty Thousand People on a Cobbled Street

Recife has four synagogues today, and Kahal Zur Israel is not the one most families attend on an ordinary Friday. It is the one they get married in. Weddings and b'nei mitzvah are held here specifically for what the address means - proof of a Jewish presence in Brazil older than almost anything else in the country's colonial record. Every November the street outside fills for a Jewish festival of dance, film and food, from gefilte fish to fluden, drawing something like twenty thousand people to the synagogue and to Rua do Bom Jesus. The old name has not been erased, either; the street is still commonly called Rua dos Judeus, which is how a small congregation gets remembered by a city that outlived its empire.

## Flight Context

The synagogue museum sits at 8.0613 degrees south, 34.8713 degrees west, in Recife Antigo - the narrow island of the old port, between the Beberibe channel and the Atlantic. Rua do Bom Jesus runs a single short block, so from the air the marker is the island itself: a spit of low colonial rooftops with the sandstone reef line and the harbour breakwater just to the east. Recife/Guararapes-Gilberto Freyre International (SBRF) is roughly five nautical miles south-southwest, and departures off runway 18 pass within sight of the old town. Best light for the pastel façades of Rua do Bom Jesus is early morning; afternoon convective cloud is common through the March-to-July rainy season.
