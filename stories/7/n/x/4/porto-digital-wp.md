# Porto Digital

Pernambuco privatised its electricity company and put the money into a technology park inside Recife's derelict old port quarter - a regeneration bet that took twenty-five years to pay out, and did.

**Location:** -8.0583, -34.8711

**Categories:** brazil, pernambuco, technology, urban-regeneration, economy

## Introduction

Pernambuco sold its electricity company and spent part of the proceeds on a bet. The privatisation of CELPE supplied most of the seed capital - an initial R$33 million - for a scheme launched in Recife in 2000 with two objectives that had no obvious relationship to each other: stop the state's computing graduates leaving for São Paulo, and do something about the rotting historic quarter down by the harbour. A quarter of a century later both have happened. Porto Digital is not a business park on a ring road. It occupies restored buildings on the island where the city began.

## Why the Old Port

Bairro do Recife - Recife Antigo, the island wedged between the harbour and the rivers - holds the oldest urban fabric in the city, and by the 1990s a lot of it was empty. The colonial and nineteenth-century buildings around Rua do Bom Jesus had lost their commercial reason to exist when the port's business moved elsewhere. Putting software companies into them answered two questions with one budget line: young firms got central floorspace they could afford, and buildings that would otherwise have been left to collapse acquired tenants with an interest in the roof. Most urban regeneration schemes promise exactly this. The difference here is that somebody kept count.

## The Classroom Next Door

The park's flagship is CESAR, the Recife Center for Advanced Studies and Systems, an R&D institute and incubator that a federal assessment in 2004 rated the most innovative in Brazil. Its supply line is academic. The Center of Informatics at the Federal University of Pernambuco produces the graduates, and the wall between the two is thin enough that Sergio Cavalcante has served as CESAR's chief executive while teaching at CIn. The university also runs a course called Projetão, in which students build a product rather than write about one; some of those products walked out of the classroom as companies. In Loco Media, previously UBee, turned a student indoor-positioning project into a mobile advertising network. Fiat Chrysler sited a research operation here.

## The Arithmetic of Incentives

Neither city hall nor Brasília writes cheques straight into the park. They discount taxes instead. Firms inside the zone get up to 60 per cent off the ISS, the municipal services levy, and the federal Lei de Informática - laws 8.248/91, 10.176/01 and 11.077/04 - reduces IPI for companies developing hardware and automation. In a country with one of the heaviest tax burdens in the world, that margin is not decoration; it is what makes the sums close. There are labour-side incentives too, cutting the paperwork and cost of hiring. Running the whole apparatus is a non-profit, the Porto Digital Management Unit, steered by an eighteen-member council drawn from resident firms, companies with ties to the park, the state government, UFPE and Recife City Hall, with a permanent staff whose backgrounds include urban planning as well as software.

## Games, Music, Design

In 2013 the park widened its remit past information technology into the creative economy: electronic games, audiovisual production, music, design. That suited a city with a long record of exporting culture more successfully than it exported code. The same year, Recife hosted the 30th World Conference of the International Association of Science Parks and Areas of Innovation, of which Porto Digital is a member. It had already been assessed as the largest science park in Brazil by AT Kearney in 2005, and it took ANPROTEC's national prize for best Brazilian science park in 2007, 2011 and 2015. The strategy is written down in ten-year plans - the first of them broke into forty projects across six areas, from attracting investment to internationalisation to environmental sustainability.

## Counting

The trajectory is documented, and it is not a straight line. In 2014 the zone housed 500 entrepreneurs and 240 institutions, with another 150 related companies elsewhere in Recife. By 2020 it reported more than 330 technology companies employing over 11,000 people. That deadline matters, because the first strategic plan had set a target of 20,000 professionals and 400 innovative enterprises by 2020 - so on the day the clock ran out, employment stood at roughly half the goal. The park got there late rather than never. By the end of 2025 more than 540 companies, research institutes, incubators, accelerators and investment funds were operating inside Porto Digital, employing over 24,000 people and turning over R$7.4 billion. Press releases usually quote only the last of those figures. The interesting part is the five years of shortfall in the middle.

## Flight Context

Porto Digital is centred at 8.058 degrees south, 34.871 degrees west, on Recife Antigo - the low island between the Port of Recife and the Capibaribe, essentially at sea level. From the air the district reads as a dense grid of nineteenth-century blocks with the harbour basin and its breakwater on the seaward side, the reef line just beyond, and Marco Zero square and the Malakoff Tower as the clearest landmarks. Recommended viewing altitude 1,500 to 3,000 feet to resolve individual buildings and the bridges linking the island to Santo Antônio and Boa Vista; 6,000 feet for the district in the context of the wider metropolitan area. Recife/Guararapes-Gilberto Freyre International (SBRF) lies about ten kilometres south behind Boa Viagem beach; João Pessoa (SBJP) is the alternate up the coast. Best visibility runs September to December; June is the wettest month with the lowest ceilings.
