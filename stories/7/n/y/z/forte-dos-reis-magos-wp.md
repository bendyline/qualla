# Forte dos Reis Magos

The star-shaped fort on the Potengi sandbar is older than Natal itself — begun on Epiphany 1598, finished in stone by a royal engineer, and Dutch for twenty-one years.

**Location:** -5.7564, -35.1947

**Categories:** history, military, fortification, colonial, brazil, rio-grande-do-norte

## Introduction

The fort is older than the city it defends. On 6 January 1598 — Epiphany, the feast of the Three Wise Men — Portuguese soldiers drove the first stakes of an earthwork into the sand at the mouth of the Potengi River, and the date handed the place its name: Reis Magos, the Magi. Natal would not exist for another twenty-three months, until a mass on Christmas Day 1599 founded a town upriver and gave it the Portuguese word for Christmas. The fort came first. Everything in Natal is downstream of it, literally and otherwise.

## A Plan Drawn Before Anyone Saw the River

The design arrived from Europe ahead of the soldiers. Father Gaspar de Samperes, a Jesuit who had trained under the Italian military architect Giovanni Battista Antonelli, drew the plan in Portugal in 1597 — a fortification for a river bar he had never seen. On 25 December 1597 a squadron entered the mouth of what is now the Potengi with orders to secure the coast against Indigenous resistance and French corsairs. Jerônimo de Albuquerque Maranhão supervised the first construction: packed earth on a reef at the bar, thrown up fast. Portugal was fitting a lock to a door it had not yet opened. The town followed, and took the Christmas name. The fort kept Epiphany, and keeps it still.

## Frias da Mesquita's Star

Earth does not survive long on a tidal bar. By 1608 the works needed rebuilding, and in 1614 the job passed to Francisco de Frias da Mesquita, chief engineer to the Portuguese Crown, who raised the fort again in masonry and had the interior buildings finished by 1628. What he produced is a textbook of its moment — a star polygon with its reentrant angle facing north, bastions and tenailles stacked over every approach, roughly 64 metres along its longest axis and some 240 metres around the perimeter. Contemporaries set it beside Fort Jesus at Mombasa, whose proportions were laid out to the Vitruvian ideals of the human body. Reis Magos is the plainer sibling: the same geometry, far less ornament, built by men with less money and more hurry.

## Castelo Ceulen

On 12 December 1633 a Dutch force under Admiral Jan Cornelisz Lichthart took the bar. The Portuguese captain-major, Pero Mendes de Gouveia, was wounded in the fighting. The Dutch renamed the fort Castelo Ceulen and the town New Amsterdam, and in 1638 Johan Maurits van Nassau authorised repairs to the stonework — an occupier maintaining someone else's masterpiece. Twenty-one years passed under that flag. When Recife capitulated in 1654 the Dutch position across the northeast folded, and in February of that year Portuguese troops simply walked back in. The Netherlands conceded the territory formally in 1661, under the Treaty of The Hague. The fort had changed hands twice and never changed shape.

## The Prisoner of April

On 29 March 1817, André de Albuquerque Maranhão — a wealthy sugar mill owner — seized power in Natal in the name of the Pernambucan Insurrection and declared a provisional government. It never found popular support. When the revolt collapsed in Pernambuco, his collapsed with it. He was overthrown and wounded on 25 April, carried to the Fortress of the Three Wise Men, and died there the next day. The square at the heart of Natal's old town, the point from which every distance in the city is measured, has borne his name since 1888. The fort that began as a lock on a river had by then become a place to put people the state wanted out of the way.

## Causeway, Bridge, Craft Shop

The fort still sits offshore on its reef, reached by a causeway that runs out from the beach — a short walk that feels much longer when the wind is up and the Atlantic is working on both sides of you. IPHAN listed it as national heritage on 13 May 1949, and it is open to the public today, with a snack bar and a craft stall tucked inside walls raised to repel a Dutch fleet. Overhead, the Newton Navarro Bridge has carried traffic between Santos Reis and Redinha since 2007, its deck arcing over the same channel the Portuguese came up in 1597. From the bridge the fort reads exactly as its engineer intended: a hard white star lying flat on the water, pointed at everything approaching.

## Flight Context

Position 5°45'23"S, 35°11'41"W, at the mouth of the Potengi River on the northeast corner of Natal. Best viewed from 1,500–2,500 feet on a north–south run along the coast; the star plan is unmistakable from directly overhead and largely invisible from the beach. Navigate to the Newton Navarro Bridge — the fort lies immediately seaward of its eastern approach, on the right bank of the river bar. Greater Natal International Airport (SBSG) is 30 km west-northwest at São Gonçalo do Amarante; Natal Air Force Base (SBNT) lies south at Parnamirim. Morning light off the Atlantic is harsh; late afternoon rakes the bastions and shows the geometry best. Trade winds are steady and onshore almost year-round; the wettest months are March through July.
