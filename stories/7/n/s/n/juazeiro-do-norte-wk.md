# Juazeiro do Norte

A city in Ceará, Brazil, built around the memory of Padre Cícero and now one of the largest pilgrimage sites in South America, where hundreds of thousands of people arrive for the Romaria at the end of October.

**Location:** -7.2128, -39.3150

**Categories:** city, pilgrimage, religion, brazil, ceara, padre-cicero

## Introduction

In Juazeiro do Norte, you can buy Padre Cícero in a size that fits in your palm. There must be hundreds of tiny shops loaded with religious trinkets, and one of the things they sell is a two-inch Padre Cícero. A few kilometers north of the center, on a hill called the Horto, the same priest stands 27 meters tall in concrete, a figure visible across almost the entire municipality. Between those two scales runs the story of a city in the dry interior of Ceará, in northeastern Brazil, that grew up around one man and has become one of the largest pilgrimage sites in South America.

## The Priest from Crato

Cícero Romão Batista was born on March 24, 1844, in Crato, the neighboring town that today forms one corner of a larger urban area with Juazeiro. He came to Juazeiro as a priest in 1872, and the settlement began to grow rapidly. A chapel to Our Lady of Sorrows, Nossa Senhora das Dores, had stood there since 1827, and the devotion it started still draws pilgrims each September. Most of the city's sights still revolve around Padre Cícero.

## March 1, 1889

On the night of March 1, 1889, a woman named Maria de Araújo received communion from Padre Cícero and could not swallow the host. Devotees believed it had turned to blood, and the same thing was said to happen again on later occasions. Maria was a beata, a woman recognized as holy by popular devotion, and the news turned Juazeiro into a destination for believers.

The Church was not convinced. Bishop Joaquim José Vieira launched two inquiries. The first commission called the event supernatural, but a second concluded there had been no miracle, and Rome confirmed the bishop's decision. Maria de Araújo spent her final years cloistered, out of public view, and died on January 17, 1914. Padre Cícero was accused of heresy and suspended, although he was never formally excommunicated. The pilgrims kept coming regardless.

## A Town Becomes a City

On July 22, 1911, Juazeiro separated from Crato and became a municipality in its own right, and Padre Cícero became its first mayor. He died in Juazeiro on July 20, 1934, at the age of 90, and is buried in the Chapel of Our Lady of Perpetual Help. In 2022 the Catholic Church opened the process toward his beatification, and he now carries the title of Servant of God.

The city he left behind has outgrown its origins. Juazeiro forms a larger urban area, nicknamed CraJuBa, with neighboring Crato and Barbalha, and together they contain nearly 500,000 people, with the smaller towns of the Cariri region adding more. The 2022 census counted 286,120 residents in Juazeiro itself.

## The Romaria

For most of the year, Juazeiro is a busy regional city. In the last days of October, the weight shifts. Hundreds of thousands of people, by some estimates as many as 500,000, come to town for the Romaria, a religious festival that runs into early November.

Everything orbits Praça Padre Cícero. A great number of buses pass the western corner of the square, where a terminal-like structure stands, and the Horto bus leaves from there. Dozens and dozens of pousadas crowd the center, particularly near the square, with the cheapest on a strip of Rua São José one block away. A few simple open-air spots serve drinks on the square itself.

On the Horto, the concrete statue of Padre Cícero has stood since 1969, together with other dedications to him. The bus station lies 3 km from the center, Fortaleza is eight to ten hours away by bus, and the city has its own airport, named Orlando Bezerra de Menezes. Before leaving, there is the matter of the two-inch Padre Cícero. Why not buy one?

## Flight Context

Juazeiro do Norte lies at 7.21°S, 39.31°W in southern Ceará, in the Cariri region, at an elevation of about 377 m (1,240 ft). The city is served by Orlando Bezerra de Menezes Airport (SBJU). Juazeiro runs together with neighboring Crato and Barbalha (CraJuBa), so expect a continuous built-up area rather than a single isolated town. The best landmark is the Horto hill, a few kilometers north-northwest of the center, where the 27-meter concrete statue of Padre Cícero stands. Around 2,500 to 3,500 feet above ground level gives a good overview of the city and the hill. The Romaria in the last days of October brings hundreds of thousands of people to town.
