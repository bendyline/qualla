# Kalemie

Capital of Tanganyika Province, founded in 1891 as an anti-slavery outpost under Captain Alphonse Jacques, renamed from Albertville in 1971, and still the junction where rail, road and 673 kilometres of lake meet.

**Location:** -5.9128, 29.1906

**Categories:** Kalemie, Populated places in Tanganyika Province, Populated places on Lake Tanganyika, Cities in the Democratic Republic of the Congo

## Introduction

On 30 December 1891, a Belgian officer named Alphonse Jacques drove the last stake into the shoreline of Lake Tanganyika and declared the military post of Albertville open. He was there to fight the Arab slave trade that controlled the western shore. A month earlier his expedition had relieved a French soldier named Joubert whose garrison of two hundred men, armed with a miscellaneous collection of chassepots and Remingtons and muzzle-loaders without matching cartridges, had been holding out in the mountains to the north. That outpost became Albertville. Albertville became Kalemie. And the story of the town has never fully shaken loose from the arguments of its founding.

## Missions, Slavers, and a Besieged Post

The White Fathers, Charles Lavigerie's Society of Missionaries of Africa, had established Catholic stations at both ends of Lake Tanganyika between 1886 and 1891. Three of them were abandoned under attacks by the slavers Tippu Tip and Rumaliza, who by 1891 controlled almost the entire western shore apart from a defended pocket around Mpala held by Léopold Louis Joubert. Captain Jacques, financed by the Belgian Anti-Slavery Society, arrived to relieve him. On 30 December 1891 Jacques founded Albertville south of the Lukuga River. Within months Sergeant Alexis Vrithoff was dead, killed on 5 April 1892 defending the outpost. Rumaliza's forces besieged Albertville from 16 August 1892 until 1 January 1893, when a relief column sent from Brussels broke the siege. The slavers retreated. The enslaved people whose communities had been raided for decades did not, in any straightforward sense, go home - the Arab slave trade would take years more to end, and the Belgian regime that replaced it would impose its own brutalities on the Congolese who had just been liberated from captivity.

## World War and Railway

The old Albertville slowly emptied as the garrison shifted north of the Lukuga to the post of M'Toa - the site of present-day Kalemie. In 1914, as the First World War engulfed East Africa, Albertville became a base for Belgo-Congolese forces of the Force Publique during the East African campaign. The railway reached town in 1915. The port was built in 1916, and the coal works at Greinerville opened the same year. Trains could now roll from the Lualaba River at Kabalo down through Albertville to the lake, where lake steamers would carry freight and troops on to Kigoma and the Tanzanian Central Railway to Dar es Salaam. It was one of the strangest logistics chains on any front of the war: a train, a steamer, a train. At the end of 1940 a second war brought a second base - first South African, then British - to manage forces deploying to Kenya and Abyssinia.

## Independence, Rebellion, and a New Name

The Congo became independent in 1960. Katanga seceded eleven days later. In August 1964, during the Simba Rebellion, mercenaries under the South African soldier Mike Hoare attacked Albertville as part of operations to put down the uprising. The town survived, barely. In 1971, under Mobutu Sese Seko's Zairianisation policy, Albertville became Kalemie. The same policy renamed the country to Zaire, replaced Christian names with Congolese ones, and banned Western-style dress in favour of the Mao-style abacost tunic. Most of those measures faded in the 1980s. The new name did not. Today Kalemie is the capital of Tanganyika Province and home to the University of Kalemie, which keeps the largest library in the region.

## A Port Waiting for Its Next Chapter

Kalemie Port was designed to link the Great Lakes rail line to Kigoma and, through Kigoma, to the Indian Ocean at Dar es Salaam. Its three mobile cranes would give it a respectable capacity on two shifts a day. The cranes no longer work. Lake silt has built up around the wharf until most vessels cannot reach it. The railway west of town is, in the bureaucratic language of logistics assessments, very degraded. The power line from the Bendera hydroelectric plant carries the town's entire electrical grid on one thin thread. And yet trains are again running between Kalemie and Kindu, as of December 2024; boat services still cross the lake; pineapples still grow in the hills; the 2005 earthquake that destroyed dozens of houses is becoming a story told by grandparents. This is a town that has been written off and rebuilt several times. It is currently in the rebuilding phase.

## Flight Context

Located at 5.91 degrees south, 29.19 degrees east, on the western shore of Lake Tanganyika. Kalemie Airport (FZRF) handles regular FlyCAA service from Kinshasa (FZAA) and Lubumbashi (FZQA). From 8,000-12,000 feet AGL the Lukuga River is clearly visible draining west out of the lake, the railway line curves in from the northwest through Nyunzu, and the distinctive grid of colonial-era Albertville is still legible south of the river. Afternoon thunderstorms are common November through April; dry season June-September offers clearest views of the 673 km lake to the east.
