# Mpanda

Capital of Tanzania's newest region, Mpanda juggles four ethnic groups, five denominations, rice exports, and a railhead at the end of the line from Tabora.

**Location:** -6.3436, 31.0694

**Categories:** Katavi Region, Districts of Katavi Region, Constituencies of Tanzania, Regional capitals in Tanzania, Bende people, Pimbwe people, Tongwe people, Konongo people

## Introduction

The cathedral in Mpanda has pictorial windows. For a town that was until recently considered a backwater, this is a point of civic pride. When the Catholic Church created the Diocese of Mpanda in October 2000, the new cathedral got windows that tell stories in colored glass. Six months later, in June 2010, the Anglican Diocese of Lake Rukwa set up its headquarters in the same town. There is a Moravian church nearby, a sect whose Tanzanian presence dates to German mission work in the 1890s. There are mosques serving the Muslim population spread across the urban area. Mpanda, a frontier town of about 204,000 people in the far west of Tanzania, turns out to be more spiritually populous than it first appears.

## Capital of the Newest Region

Tanzania redrew its map in 2012, splitting the enormous Rukwa Region into two new ones. The northern half became Katavi Region, and Mpanda became its administrative headquarters. The town is simultaneously the capital of Katavi Region, the seat of Mpanda District, and one of the region's four districts in its own right. If that sounds bureaucratically redundant, it also reflects something real: in the far west of Tanzania, there are not that many places big enough to host a government office. Mpanda is the one that is, and so it carries the weight of several administrative layers at once. The postcode, for what it is worth, is 50100.

## Rice, Maize, and the Scent of Gold

Economically, Mpanda does what rural Tanzanian towns do: it marshals the countryside's production. Farmers from surrounding villages bring in rice and maize for marketing and transshipment. Buyers from Dar es Salaam and beyond send trucks in to carry the bags east. The Katavi Region is also on every mineral prospector's watch list lately, especially for gold, which has drawn speculative interest and some small-scale mining. Mpanda is the staging point for all of it. It is also the staging point for Katavi National Park, 35 kilometers south at Sitalike, a park famous for hippopotamus populations that stack up in the dry-season pools in numbers that look almost impossible.

## Where the Roads Are Not

The map shows roads into Mpanda from Sumbawanga, Tabora, and Kigoma. The map does not show that all of them are unsealed. In February and March, at the peak of the wet season, the dirt turns to something that can swallow a truck. The Tanzanian government has been working on sealing the road north from Sumbawanga, and progress is real if slow. Sumry runs two daily buses to Sumbawanga - 240 kilometers in five hours, one continuing to Mbeya. Several lines serve Tabora, a nine-hour slog over 380 kilometers. Local minibuses head to Sitalike, to Usevya, and down to the fishing villages of Karema and Ikola on Lake Tanganyika. The train from Tabora takes 12 to 15 hours. In 2012, the government upgraded Mpanda airport to a two-kilometer sealed runway, and commercial flights began shortly after.

## The People Who Live Here

Katavi is home to the Bende, Pimbwe, Tongwe, and Konongo peoples, whose ancestral lands make up the region. Mpanda itself draws on all of them, along with migrants from elsewhere in Tanzania drawn by the mineral prospects or the rural economy. The new dioceses reflect this pluralism more honestly than a single mission church would. On Sunday mornings, the Catholic cathedral fills. So does the Moravian church. So do the Lutheran services, the African Inland Church, the Tanzania Assemblies of God. The muezzin calls from several directions. Mpanda is not a city yet, not quite, but it is no longer only a frontier outpost either. It is something in between: the capital of a region that has just finished deciding what to call itself.

## Flight Context

Mpanda is at 6.34°S, 31.07°E in western Tanzania. Mpanda Airport (HTMP) has a 2-km sealed runway with Air Tanzania service. Recommended viewing altitude 5,000-8,000 feet MSL. Katavi National Park is 35 km south - look for the dry-season hippo concentrations in the Katuma River pools. Lake Tanganyika is visible to the west at higher altitudes; the fishing villages of Karema and Ikola sit on its shore. Terrain is mostly rolling miombo woodland below 1,200 m. Nearest alternates: Tabora (HTTB) 380 km east, Kigoma (HTKA) to the northwest. Weather note: February-March wet season produces heavy afternoon storms; visibility best June-October.
