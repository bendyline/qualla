# Don Som

The second largest of the Four Thousand Islands has seven thousand residents, twelve villages and one guesthouse - and once sheltered a French painter who came for three months and stayed forty years.

**Location:** 14.0439, 105.8800

**Categories:** Islands of the Mekong River, River islands of Laos, Si Phan Don, Champasak Province

## Introduction

Marc Leguay arrived in Indochina in December 1935 on a three-month contract. He was twenty-five, trained as a painter in France, fresh from exhibitions in Perpignan and across the Spanish border, where he had met Salvador Dali. The three months ended. Leguay did not leave. When French authorities next picked up his trail, in 1937, he was living among the Mekong islands of Khong district in Lower Laos - and by Don Som's own account, on this island. He would stay in the country for four decades, and the drawings he made there ended up in the pockets of everyone who used Lao money or posted a Lao letter.

## The Painter Who Never Went Home

Leguay set up an ecole d'arts lao among the islands and began painting the life around him: river, fields, water buffalo, the particular light of the lower Mekong. In 1946 he married Nang Sengdeuane Soulivong, from a prominent Khong family; she modelled for a number of his pictures. The following year the couple moved to Vientiane, where he taught drawing at the lycee until 1976. The commissions that made his name were small and everywhere at once. In 1945 he designed a 100-piastre banknote for the Indochinese states. From 1951 onward he produced 55 Laotian postage stamps, many engraved by Jean Pheulpin - meaning that for a generation, the image of Laos circulating around the world was a Frenchman's rendering of villages very much like the ones on Don Som. After the political change of 1975 he crossed into rural Thailand, wanting only to live quietly. He stopped painting. He died there in 2001, at ninety-one.

## Thirteen Kilometres, Twelve Villages

The island runs about 13 kilometres from north to south and reaches three kilometres across at its widest, which makes it the second largest landmass in the Si Phan Don archipelago - beaten only by Don Khong, immediately upstream. Roughly 7,000 people live on it, distributed among twelve villages whose names read like a slow walk down the length of the island: Ban Hang Som, Ban Veunsom, Ban Kengkoum, Ban Thapao, Ban Thapo, Ban Deua Tai, Ban Deua, Ban Sala, Ban Muang, Ban Thamakheb, Ban Thakham and Ban Don Som. The economy is agriculture and fishing, in roughly that order, and it has been for a very long time.

## One Guesthouse

Here is the statistic that defines Don Som better than any other. Its immediate neighbours - Don Khong to the north, Don Det and Don Khon to the south - have absorbed the great majority of tourism in southern Laos, complete with riverside bungalows, tubing, banana pancakes and the whole apparatus. Don Som, the second biggest island in the group, sitting directly between them, has one guesthouse, in the village of Ban Thamakheb. Visitors are few. The reasons are unglamorous: no famous waterfall, no French railway bridge, no dolphin pool, no established boat run from the mainland pier at Ban Nakasang. What that has preserved is an island where the daily rhythm still belongs to the people who live there rather than to the people passing through.

## A River That Rearranges Itself

Life on any of these islands is negotiated annually with the Mekong. In the monsoon months the river swells until roughly half the islands of Si Phan Don vanish beneath it, channels merge, and the archipelago briefly becomes something closer to a lake. Then the water falls away and gives the sandbars back. Rice goes in on ground the flood has already fertilised; fishing follows the river's own calendar, keyed to the migrations that push up from the Cambodian reaches below. It is a way of living that assumes the landscape will change shape every year, and builds around that assumption instead of fighting it. On an island with one guesthouse and no through-traffic, that assumption still governs almost everything.

## Flight Context

Don Som lies in the Mekong at approximately 14.044 degrees north, 105.880 degrees east, in Khong District, Champasak Province, southern Laos. From the air it reads as a long, narrow, north-south island roughly 13 kilometres in length, set between the much larger Don Khong upstream and the tourist islands of Don Det and Don Khon downstream, within the braided expanse of the Si Phan Don archipelago. Look for cultivated fields and village clusters strung along its length, with almost no visible tourist development. Pakse International Airport (VLPS) lies about 120 kilometres north and is the nearest airport with scheduled service. Best viewed between 3,000 and 8,000 feet. In the November-to-March dry season the island stands out sharply against pale sandbars; in the wet season the surrounding channels flood and its outline softens considerably.
