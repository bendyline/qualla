# Sacred Heart Cathedral, Buôn Ma Thuột

A thatched chapel raised for fifty people in 1934 grew into the mother church of a diocese that now counts close to half a million Catholics across the southern Central Highlands.

**Location:** 12.6806, 108.0451

**Categories:** Roman Catholic cathedrals in Vietnam, Buôn Ma Thuột, Religious Architecture, Central Highlands, 20th-century Roman Catholic church buildings in Vietnam

## Introduction

In March 1937 the mission station at Ban Mê Thuột was raised to a parish and given its first resident priest, Phêrô Nguyễn Đắc Cẩn. He lasted about a year. The highlands handed him malaria and then typhoid, and he had to be taken back to the bishop's house to recover; the parish went four years without a priest of its own. That is the ground the Sacred Heart Cathedral stands on — not metaphorically but literally. The site at 2 Phan Chu Trinh is the same patch of red basalt earth where, on 15 August 1934, someone put up a chapel with a thatched roof and mud walls for a congregation of roughly fifty people.

## Ninety Years of Not Getting There

The Catholic Church had been trying to reach these uplands since 1842, when the apostolic vicar of Cochinchina, Stêphanô Théodore Cuénot, sent two priests and a catechist named Micae Cuông toward the Central Highlands. They failed. Between 1842 and 1846 further parties tried routes through Quảng Nam and Quảng Ngãi, and every attempt turned back. Not until 1847 did a Paris Foreign Missions priest, Father Fontaine, find a way through; he lived and worked among the M'nông near buôn Yeng Drôm, in the country between Bản Đôn and Đăk Mil, and is counted the first missionary in Đắk Lắk. The obstacle was not hostility so much as fever. Malaria made the plateau lethal for outsiders, and it stayed that way for generations. When Pope Pius XI erected the Diocese of Kon Tum in January 1932, its new bishop, M. Jannin Phước, did not reach Buôn Ma Thuột until 29 January 1934.

## One Year to Build

The Daughters of Charity of Saint Vincent de Paul arrived in 1953 and opened a girls' school and an orphanage. Then came 1954 and the partition of the country, and with it a wave of Catholics displaced from the north; the little chapel was enlarged and re-roofed in sheet metal, and the congregation swelled from a few dozen households to about four thousand people. Something larger was needed. In September 1956 the parish received Father Gioan Baotixita Trần Thanh Ngoạn, who set about raising the money and the labour from his own parishioners. Construction took exactly one year, and the church was dedicated on the second Sunday after Easter in 1959. It is a cross in plan — forty-five metres long, twelve wide, with transept arms twelve metres square — enclosing some 828 square metres apart from the sanctuary, and seating twelve hundred.

## Qui Dei Benignitate

On 22 June 1967 Pope Paul VI issued the bull Qui Dei Benignitate and cut a new diocese out of the territories of Đà Lạt and Kon Tum, covering the provinces of Đắk Lắk, Quảng Đức and Phước Long. Sacred Heart became its cathedral. The numbers at the founding were slight against the geography: fifty-five priests and thirty-three parishes for 56,719 Catholics scattered over 21,723 square kilometres of forest, plateau and plantation holding 290,800 people. Phêrô Nguyễn Huy Mai was consecrated its first bishop and led it for twenty-three years. One small stubbornness survives from that moment. The city's official name is Buôn Ma Thuột, and has been for decades, but the diocese has never stopped calling itself Ban Mê Thuột — the older spelling, fixed in Latin as Banmethuotensis and left alone ever since.

## Who Fills the Pews

The cathedral parish itself counts more than eleven thousand people, of whom 10,579 are Kinh and 521 are Ê Đê — a reminder that in the highlands the church has always been a meeting of two very different congregations under one roof. Across the diocese as a whole the balance shifts. Its 24,474 square kilometres are home to Ê Đê, M'nông and S'tiêng communities, each with its own language, and by 2024 the diocese counted eight deaneries, 118 parishes, 77 independent sub-parish communities, 281 priests, more than a thousand religious, and roughly 481,000 Catholics. The patrons are the Vietnamese Martyrs, and the feast falls on 22 June, the anniversary of the diocese's own creation. Bishop Gioan Baotixita Nguyễn Huy Bắc has led it since 2024.

## Schoolrooms in the Shadow

Ask what a cathedral does in a place like this and the answer is mostly education. The De La Salle Brothers arrived in 1959, the year the church opened, and ran the town's first secondary school from the 1959–60 academic year until 1975; today they keep a boarding house for boys on Nguyễn Văn Trỗi Street. The Daughters of Charity, whose orphanage and girls' school stood where a kindergarten and a secondary school stand now, run a kindergarten and free classes for children who would otherwise not attend. The Queen of Peace sisters, the diocese's own congregation, work outward from Buôn Ma Thuột into the village mission points with teaching, nursing and relief. The Paulist sisters came in 1975 and opened Vi Nhân, a school for children with disabilities. The building is the landmark; the institutions around it are what most people in the neighbourhood actually touch.

## Flight Context

The cathedral sits at 12.681°N, 108.045°E, at 2 Phan Chu Trinh in central Buôn Ma Thuột, roughly 500 metres above sea level on the Đắk Lắk plateau. Its cruciform footprint and pale tower make it one of the more legible structures in the old city core from 1,500 to 3,000 feet AGL; it lies only a few hundred metres from Đình Lạc Giao, so both can be picked up on a single pass. Buôn Ma Thuột Airport (VVBM) is immediately east of the city, with Pleiku (VVPK) the nearest alternate to the north. Expect clean visibility in the November-to-April dry season and convective cloud with reduced slant visibility through the southwest monsoon.
