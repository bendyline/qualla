# Chi Phat

A commune deep in the Cardamoms that rebuilt its economy on the premise that a living forest pays better than a cleared one.

**Location:** 11.3290, 103.5225

**Categories:** Cardamom Mountains, Ecotourism, Koh Kong province, Communes of Cambodia

## Introduction

Chi Phat makes its visitors a proposition, and the proposition is not really about scenery. Sleep in a family's spare room, hire a village guide, rent a village boat, and the forest around this commune becomes worth more to the people who live in it standing up than cut down. That sentence is easy to write and enormously hard to build. Chi Phat has been building it since 2008, in four villages reachable only by a twenty-kilometre boat ride up a brown river, in a range where the alternative livelihoods - logging, snaring, burning a hillside to plant it - are all still perfectly available to anyone who wants them.

## Twenty Kilometres Upriver

You arrive by giving up your seat on the highway. National Route 48 runs from Phnom Penh toward the Thai border, and somewhere along it, at a village called Andoung Tuek, you ask the driver to stop - a request worth making twice, because the bus will not stop otherwise. From there the road is finished and the Preak Piphot takes over. The boat goes twenty kilometres upstream through mangrove that gradually gives way to forested slopes, the channel narrowing, the engine noise flattening against walls of green. Somewhere in that hour the Cardamom Mountains stop being a name on a map. Chi Phat appears as a scatter of stilted houses on the bank, and the last stretch of the journey has already made the argument the village wants to make.

## The Arithmetic of a Guide's Wage

The community-based ecotourism project launched here in 2008, with technical help from Wildlife Alliance in setting up the planning and management structure. Its logic is unsentimental. A hunted animal pays once; a guided visitor who wants to see that animal pays repeatedly, and keeps paying as long as the animal is alive to be seen. So the project set out to give households a reason to stop hunting in the Cardamoms and stop clearing hillsides for a season of rice. The wider landscape backs it up: Wildlife Alliance's forest protection programme covers 1.4 million hectares of Cardamom rainforest from twelve ranger stations, and no elephant poaching has been recorded across that area since 2006. Chi Phat is the part of the strategy that runs on consent rather than enforcement.

## A Rotation, Not a Race

The detail that reveals how carefully this was designed is the booking system. Homestays and guesthouses take visitors in rotation, so that families earn in turn rather than competing to capture the trade - no household with the best-located house cornering the market, no neighbour left with an empty room and a grievance. Fees are collected centrally and distributed back through the community. It is a deliberate brake on the thing that usually breaks village tourism, which is not a shortage of tourists but the resentment that arrives with uneven money. Around three thousand people live in the commune's four villages - Chi Phat, Komlot, Chom-Sla and T'k La'o - and most of them still make their living primarily from farming and fishing. Tourism is a supplement, and it was built to stay one.

## What the Trails Are For

Guides run treks, mountain-bike routes and kayak trips out of the CBET office, equipment and food included. The trails lead to waterfalls - O'Mallu among them - and into forest that holds the Cardamoms' full roster of secretive animals, most of which you will not see. That is worth saying plainly. This is not a place engineered to deliver wildlife on schedule. What it delivers is the sound of it: gibbons calling across a valley at first light, insects rising to a wall of noise at dusk, rain arriving on the canopy several seconds before it reaches you. The forest around Chi Phat is still here largely because a few hundred families decided it was worth more intact, and the trip upriver is, in the end, a way of joining that arithmetic.

## Flight Context

Chi Phat sits at 11.33°N, 103.52°E on the Preak Piphot, roughly twenty kilometres upstream from Andoung Tuek on National Route 48, in the southern Cardamom Mountains of Koh Kong province. Best viewed from 4,000-8,000 ft, where the river's meanders through mangrove and into forested hills are clearly traceable and the commune reads as a small clearing on the north bank. Terrain rises steeply inland to the Southern Cardamom ridges - there is no airstrip and no road access from the east. Nearest airports: Sihanouk International (VDSV/KOS) to the southeast, Dara Sakor International (VDDS/DSY) on the coast to the west, and Phnom Penh International (VDPP/PNH). Weather: heavy southwest monsoon rain from roughly May to October, with the windward Cardamom slopes among the wettest terrain in Cambodia; morning valley fog and afternoon convective cloud are routine, and river levels rise fast after rain.
