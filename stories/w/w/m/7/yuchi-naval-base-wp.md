# Yuchi Naval Base

Home port of China's first aircraft carrier Liaoning, this sprawling naval base south of Qingdao underwent massive expansion starting in 2012 and anchors the PLAN's North Sea Fleet.

**Location:** 35.7236, 119.9967

**Categories:** military, naval, infrastructure, defense

## Introduction

When the aircraft carrier Liaoning arrived at its new home port on February 27, 2013, it sailed into a harbor that had been preparing for this moment for years. Yuchi Naval Base, formally known as Navy Comprehensive Support Base Qingdao, sits on the Yellow Sea coast south of Qingdao's main urban area, and it had been undergoing a dramatic expansion since early 2012 to accommodate China's first carrier battle group. The base that was founded on September 9, 1950, as a modest Cold War installation had become one of the most strategically important naval facilities in the western Pacific.

## Built for Carriers

The infrastructure at Yuchi speaks to the scale of modern carrier operations. The base is protected by multiple breakwaters that shield the harbor from the Yellow Sea's storms and swells. Four mooring piers stretch over 1,000 meters in length, and a fifth measures approximately 820 meters, providing berths for the carrier and its escort vessels. The harbor is divided into two main areas, with the carrier battle group occupying the eastern section. Deep-draft access channels allow the Liaoning and its accompanying destroyers and frigates to enter and exit without restriction. Satellite imagery, which first confirmed the base's expansion, shows a facility that grew rapidly from a regional naval station into a complex capable of supporting blue-water operations.

## North Sea Fleet Headquarters

Yuchi Naval Base is subordinate to the People's Liberation Army Navy's North Sea Fleet, the force responsible for defending China's maritime interests in the Yellow Sea and Bohai Sea. The North Sea Fleet has historically been the PLAN's most equipment-heavy command, and the arrival of the Liaoning carrier group cemented that status. The base hosts not only the carrier but a constellation of escort and support vessels, including guided-missile destroyers, frigates, and supply ships that together form one of China's two operational carrier battle groups. The fleet's area of responsibility includes some of the most contested waters in East Asia, where Chinese, Japanese, and Korean maritime interests intersect.

## From Coastline to Blue Water

For decades, the People's Liberation Army Navy was a coastal defense force, its mission limited to protecting China's shores and nearby waters. Yuchi Naval Base embodies the transition from that defensive posture to a navy capable of projecting power across open ocean. The expansion that began in 2012 was not simply about building longer piers. It represented a fundamental shift in how China conceives of its naval role, from a force that operates within the first island chain to one that aspires to operate beyond it. The carrier battle group based at Yuchi has conducted exercises in the western Pacific and transited through international straits, each deployment extending the operational reach of a navy that barely possessed ocean-going capability a generation ago.

## Strategic Geography

The base's location within the West Coast New Area, south of Qingdao's main urban center, offers both logistical advantages and strategic depth. Qingdao itself is one of China's most important port cities, with extensive shipyard facilities and a long naval history dating to the German colonial period. The Yellow Sea provides relatively sheltered waters for training and working up carrier operations before venturing into the open Pacific. Yet the base also faces strategic constraints: the Yellow Sea is shallow and enclosed, and any carrier group departing Yuchi must pass through choke points monitored by neighboring navies. These are the calculations that define modern naval strategy, and they are written into the geography around every pier at this sprawling installation.
