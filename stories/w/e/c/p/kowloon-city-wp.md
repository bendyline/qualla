# Kowloon City

Kowloon City has been a place of refuge, resistance, and reinvention for two thousand years — from Song dynasty emperors fleeing the Mongols to the Thai community that made it Hong Kong's Little Thailand.

**Location:** 22.3306, 114.1900

**Categories:** Kowloon City, New Kowloon, Thai Towns, Hong Kong history

## Introduction

For nearly three decades, aircraft landed at Kai Tak Airport by descending steeply over the rooftops of Kowloon City at what felt like arm's length. Pilots lined up on a checkerboard painted on a hillside, banked sharply over the densely packed neighborhoods below, and touched down on a runway that jutted into the harbor. The whole approach was famous for its drama, and for the height restrictions it imposed — no building in Kowloon City could rise above a certain level without threatening the flight path. When Kai Tak closed in 1998 and flights moved to Chek Lap Kok, the height restrictions lifted, and the skyline began to change. But the neighborhood's history runs far deeper than any airport.

## Pearl, Salt, and Empire

The history of Kowloon City reaches back to the Qin dynasty — 221 to 206 BCE — when the area was already known for pearl production. By the Song dynasty, from 960 to 1279, it had become part of an official salt yard called Kwun Fu Cheung, administered by Chinese officials. Toward the end of that same Song dynasty, as Mongol forces pressed southward through China, two young emperors — Zhao Bing and Duanzong — sought refuge in the area now marked by Sung Wong Toi Garden. The name of the garden translates roughly as the Terrace of the Sung Kings, commemorating that desperate flight. Temples and relics dating back some eight hundred years remain in the neighborhood, physical evidence of the era when Kowloon City sheltered the last flickers of a dying dynasty.

## The Walled City and Its Shadow

The neighborhood that became Kowloon City also contains the footprint of the original Kowloon Walled City, a fortification erected during the Qing dynasty that later became one of the most densely populated places on earth. After its demolition in 1993 and 1994, the site was transformed into Kowloon Walled City Park, which opened in 1995. The park preserves the old yamen — the government office at the heart of the original enclosure — along with gardens and exhibit halls that interpret the city's extraordinary history. The shadow of that walled city still shapes how people understand this neighborhood: dense, layered, capable of absorbing contradictions and continuing regardless.

## Little Thailand in the Heart of Kowloon

Walk down the main streets of Kowloon City today and the smell announces the neighborhood before the signs do. Lemongrass, fish sauce, dried chilies, and something frying in a wok. By 2016, approximately 33 percent of all Thai people living in Hong Kong resided in Kowloon City. The concentration of Thai restaurants, grocery stores, and businesses was dense enough that the area became widely known as Hong Kong's Little Thailand. It is also famous for Chiuchow-style braised dishes — the slow-cooked, soy-fragrant specialty of families from the Chaoshan area of Guangdong Province, who settled here in large numbers. The result is a neighborhood that functions as a genuine food crossroads: traditional Hong Kong-style restaurants alongside Southeast Asian kitchens, local wet markets alongside imported Thai ingredients.

## The City Rebuilt, Again

Kai Tak's closure triggered a transformation that the neighborhood is still working through. Without the flight path overhead, developers began stacking towers where low buildings had stood for decades. The old tong lau — the narrow, multi-story shophouses that defined the streetscape — gave way to residential skyscrapers. The pace of gentrification accelerated in the 2010s and early 2020s. Zoning authorities flagged some of the new high-rise developments as undesirable and proposed height restrictions for new construction, but the pressure for density in a land-scarce city is relentless. The Thai businesses that gave Kowloon City its distinctive character faced threats from redevelopment proposals in 2022. The neighborhood is not disappearing, but it is changing — as it has changed before, from pearl fishery to salt yard to walled city to food paradise, always finding the next form.

## Flight Context

Kowloon City is located at approximately 22.331°N, 114.19°E on the Kowloon Peninsula, Hong Kong. From the air, the area is easily recognizable as a dense urban district sitting north of the former Kai Tak Airport site — now redeveloped into the Kai Tak district, featuring the Kai Tak Runway Park on the old tarmac spit and the Kai Tak Sports Park (opened March 2025) on the former apron area to the northwest. The runway spit into Kowloon Bay is a prominent and distinctive landmark visible from altitude. Sung Wong Toi Garden and the Kowloon Walled City Park are both visible in the district. The nearest airport is Hong Kong International Airport (VHHH) on Lantau Island, approximately 30 km to the west. Recommended viewing altitude is 3,000–5,000 feet for a clear overview of the Kowloon urban fabric, harbor, and the surrounding hills.
