# Qianshan National Park

A mountainous park in Liaoning where Buddhist and Taoist temples share peaks shaped, according to legend, by a goddess who dropped a stone while mending the sky.

**Location:** 41.0264, 123.1361

**Categories:** national-parks, religious-sites, natural-landmarks, china

## Introduction

According to legend, the goddess Nuwa was mending a broken sky when she dropped a stone. It shattered the earth below into a thousand peaks shaped like lotus blossoms. The Chinese took that myth literally when they named this place: Qianshan means "Thousand Mountains," an abbreviation of its full name, Thousand Lotus Flower Mountains. The reality is only slightly less dramatic. Tucked into Liaoning Province about 17 kilometers southeast of the industrial city of Anshan, Qianshan National Park packs 44 square kilometers of forested ridgelines, temple complexes, and one enormous natural Buddha into a landscape that has drawn pilgrims and poets since the Tang dynasty.

## Where Buddha and Laozi Share a Mountain

Qianshan is one of the rare places in China where Buddhist and Taoist practitioners have worshipped side by side for centuries. Temples, monasteries, and nunneries from both traditions dot the peaks and valleys, their incense smoke mingling in the same mountain air. The religious history stretches back to the Tang dynasty and was expanded during the Ming and Qing eras, when new shrines and hermitages were carved into the hillsides. The turmoil of the twentieth century left some buildings damaged and the site largely abandoned, but restoration efforts have since brought the complex back to life. New pagodas and temples now stand alongside ancient ones, and the annual Qianshan Great Buddha Festival draws visitors every June.

## The Giant Who Was Always There

Among Qianshan's most remarkable features is a natural rock formation that resembles the Maitreya Buddha, standing 70 meters high and 46 meters wide. Unlike the carved colossal Buddhas found elsewhere in Asia, this one was shaped entirely by geological forces. Several temples have been built on surrounding peaks to overlook the formation, including the Pagoda of Maitreya and the Great Buddha Temple. The park claims it as the largest naturally occurring image of Maitreya Buddha in the world. Whether that claim holds up to scrutiny, the sheer scale of the rock formation against the forested mountainside is undeniable.

## An Ancient Forest, Still Standing

Forests blanket 95 percent of the park's terrain, and at least ten thousand of its pine trees are estimated to be over a century old. The canopy shelters rare flora and a wealth of plants used in traditional Chinese medicine, while more than a hundred bird species inhabit the slopes, including the uncommon black-headed stork. At its highest point, the park reaches 708 meters, modest by Chinese standards but steep enough to leave visitors breathless on the stone footpaths that climb through the trees. Motor vehicles are banned inside the park. Visitors walk or ride electric carts along paved paths that wind past stelae honoring the dead, small shrines, and pagodas half-hidden in the undergrowth.

## The Climb That Cable Cars Cannot Finish

Three cable car routes connect to several of Qianshan's scenic peaks, but none of them reaches the summit. The park seems to insist that the final stretch of any ascent must be earned on foot. The footpaths climb steeply through thick forest, past stone monuments and small temples tucked into cliff faces. The effort rewards with views across the Qianshan Mountains, a range that extends from the Changbai Mountains on the China-North Korea border westward to Liaoyang and south to the port city of Dalian. On clear days the peaks seem to fold into one another endlessly, and the park's nickname, "The Northeast Pearl," begins to feel less like a boast and more like a description.

## Flight Context

Located at 41.03°N, 123.14°E in Liaoning Province, about 17 km southeast of Anshan. The park's forested peaks and ridgelines are visible at medium altitudes. Nearest major airport is Shenyang Taoxian International (ZYTX), approximately 100 km to the north. Dalian Zhoushuizi International (ZYTL) is roughly 300 km to the south. The Qianshan mountain range runs visibly from northeast to southwest across the landscape.
