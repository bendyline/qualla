# Zhangjiakou

Beijing's Northern Door -- a city at the junction of the Great Wall and the Mongolian steppe where camel caravans once carried tea to Russia and Olympic skiers now carve through Chongli powder.

**Location:** 40.8108, 114.8811

**Categories:** cities, Great Wall of China, Olympics, trade routes

## Introduction

In October 1909, a train completed the first journey on the Beijing-Zhangjiakou Railway, the first line solely designed and built by Chinese engineers. The chief engineer, Zhan Tianyou, had solved a seemingly impossible problem: how to push a railroad through mountain passes that climbed too steeply for the locomotives of the era. His solution -- a switchback system at the Qinglongqiao station -- became a point of national pride. More than a century later, a high-speed train covers the same route in 47 minutes. But Zhangjiakou's story stretches back far further than the age of rail, to a time when this mountain city served as the threshold between civilizations.

## Where Chinese History Begins

According to Chinese tradition, around 2500 BC the legendary ancestors Huangdi, Yandi, and Chiyou lived in the area around nearby Zhuolu Town. The Battle of Zhuolu and the Battle of Banquan, fought here, amalgamated different tribes into the single Huaxia people -- the foundation narrative of Chinese civilization itself. Whether mythic or historical, these stories anchor Zhangjiakou to the deepest layers of Chinese identity. By 106 BC, the Western Han dynasty had organized the region into prefectures, and by 48 AD the Eastern Han was conducting regular exchanges with the Wuhuan peoples at markets established in local strongholds. The city has always been a meeting point.

## The Camel Road to Russia

From 1727, Zhangjiakou served as a critical station on the Kyakhta trade route between Russia and China. Each early autumn, long lines of camels would arrive from every direction to collect tea chests for the journey to Kyakhta, across the Gobi Desert. Each caravan typically made three crossings during the winter months. By the 19th century, the city's population had reached 70,000 to 100,000, and Russian merchants maintained permanent residences and warehouses just outside the Great Wall gate of Dajingmen. The 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica noted the city's unusual houses, "mostly roofed with earth and become covered with green-sward," giving the settlement an almost subterranean appearance.

## Beijing's Northern Door

The nickname is not poetic license. Zhangjiakou sits precisely where the Mongolian Plateau descends to the North China Plain, surrounded by mountains on three sides, with the Qingshui River dividing the city into its Qiaodong and Qiaoxi districts. Whoever controlled this corridor controlled access to Beijing -- a fact that made the city a military priority for every dynasty. During the Japanese occupation beginning in 1937, Kalgan (as it was then known to the West) became the capital of the puppet state of Mengjiang. During the Chinese Civil War, the 35th Army was surrounded and defeated at nearby Xinbao'an, a loss that led to the peaceful surrender of Beijing. In the early 1960s, at the height of Sino-Soviet tensions, Zhangjiakou was considered one of the most strategically important cities in China.

## From Mountain Fortress to Olympic Stage

In 2022, Zhangjiakou hosted freestyle skiing, Nordic skiing, and snowboarding events for the Winter Olympics. The venues in the Chongli District's Taizicheng village represented a transformation that had been building for years -- the ski resort earned over 1.54 billion yuan in tourism during the 2015-16 snow season alone. But the Olympics were only the most visible expression of a broader reinvention. The city boasts the freshest air and least PM 2.5 pollution of any Chinese city north of the Yellow River. It holds 4.6 percent of China's wind energy resources and ranks second nationally in solar energy use. Forest coverage reaches 37 percent, earning the title of National Forest City. The high-speed railway that opened in December 2019 shrank the journey from Beijing to 47 minutes, turning a once-remote frontier outpost into a weekend destination.

## Flight Context

Located at 40.81N, 114.88E in northwestern Hebei Province. The city sits in a mountain basin where the Mongolian Plateau meets the North China Plain. The Great Wall's Dajingmen gate is visible at the city's western edge. Zhangjiakou Ningyuan Airport (ZBZJ) serves the city with flights to major Chinese cities. The 2022 Winter Olympic venues at Chongli District are visible northeast of the city center. Recommended altitude 5,000-8,000 feet AGL to appreciate the dramatic terrain transition.
