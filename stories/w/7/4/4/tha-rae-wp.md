# Tha Rae

On the north shore of Thailand's largest natural northeastern lake sits a village of two thousand Catholic households that has paraded two hundred illuminated stars down the highway every Christmas Eve for more than a century.

**Location:** 17.2607, 104.1881

**Categories:** Villages, Religious sites, Thailand, Isan, Catholic heritage, Festivals

## Introduction

Every 24 December, a stretch of highway in northeastern Thailand fills up with stars. Some are the size of a car door. Others ride on flatbed trucks wired to portable generators, spinning and blinking as they roll, and there are around two hundred of them in a given year. They move out of Tha Rae and down the road toward the rest of Mueang Sakon Nakhon district, throwing red and gold light across rice paddies in a country where Catholics number well under one percent of the population. The procession has run on that night, in that village, for over a hundred years.

## Two Hundred Stars

The Tha Rae Star Procession stretches across five days, roughly 21 to 25 December, but Christmas Eve is when the road belongs to it. The star is the point: not the manger, not the tree, but the Star of Bethlehem, the sign that something had happened in the world. Villagers build the frames themselves, wire them, argue about them, and drive them slowly past neighbours who come out to watch. Father Joseph Surasak Phongphit, speaking of the tradition, described the stars as representing "the high spirit of sharing goodness to each other." It is not a tourist invention retrofitted onto a quiet town. Nothing else quite like it exists, and it grew out of the specific circumstances of a specific settlement — a Catholic village that has spent well over a century being the exception in its own province and decided, at some point, to be loud about it once a year.

## The Crossing of 1884

Nong Han Lake covers 125.2 square kilometres, the largest natural lake in Isan, and it is shallow: an average depth of about 1.9 metres, ten at the deepest, with margins that go dry in the hot season. In November 1884, a French missionary named Xavier Guego led a group of Catholic families out of downtown Sakon Nakhon and across that water in improvised boats. They landed on a wooded stretch of the northern shore and stayed. The founding party was small — by one account about ten Vietnamese Christian households and ten Isan households, a little over twenty in all. Those families were minorities twice over, Catholic in a Buddhist kingdom and, for the Vietnamese among them, immigrants building a life in a language not their own. The village they started now numbers roughly 2,200 households.

## A Cathedral on the North Shore

The church they founded is now the Cathedral of St Michael the Archangel, and it is not a modest parish building. It is the seat of the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Thare and Nonseng, which traces itself to the Vicariate Apostolic of Laos established on 4 May 1899 with its seat at Ban Nonseng in Nakhon Phanom Province. The archdiocese's co-cathedral, St Anne, stands in Nakhon Phanom itself, an hour and a half east on the Mekong. In December 2021 the Tha Rae cathedral received a new marble statue of St Michael, carved by a Tuscan workshop and modelled on the figure that stands atop Castel Sant'Angelo in Rome — a fairly direct statement, from a lakeside village in Isan, about which tradition it belongs to.

## Shuttered Fronts on the Main Street

Walk the main street and the architecture tells you who built it. Old colonial-era shophouses still stand along the road, their proportions and shuttered upper storeys borrowing from French, Vietnamese and Chinese models at once — the visual record of a community assembled from more than one place. Locals treat Tha Rae as the old-town quarter of Sakon Nakhon, which is a slightly odd thing for a separate sub-district 21 kilometres from the provincial capital to be, but the buildings support the claim. Sakon Nakhon Airport sits 14 kilometres away, close enough that arriving passengers pass through the district without necessarily noticing they have crossed into it.

## Rice, Fish, and the Fruit Season

Tha Rae is administered as a sub-district municipality and divided into eight administrative villages, most of them close enough together to blur, though a few sit a kilometre out on their own. The land is a rural-urban fringe, mostly lowland running down to the lake. Rice is the work; when rice is thin, there is freshwater fishing on Nong Han and there is fruit. Around 14,000 people live in the district, roughly 95 percent of them Christian, which makes this the largest Catholic community in Thailand. For eleven months of the year that is an unremarkable fact about a farming town. Then December arrives, the generators come out, and the stars go up.

## Flight Context

Tha Rae sits at 17.2607°N, 104.1881°E on the northern shore of Nong Han Lake in Sakon Nakhon Province, northeastern Thailand. The lake is the navigational key: a broad, pale, shallow sheet of water roughly 125 square kilometres in area, unmistakable against the surrounding paddy at any altitude above 3,000 ft AGL, with the town strung along its north edge. Sakon Nakhon Airport (SNO / VTUI, 2,600 m runway, field elevation 529 ft) lies about 14 km southwest; Nakhon Phanom Airport (KOP / VTUW) is roughly 55 km east on the Mekong. Terrain is flat and forgiving. Best light is early morning in the cool season (November to February), when haze is lowest; from March onward agricultural burning across Isan can cut visibility sharply, and the southwest monsoon brings convective build-ups from May through September. In the dry season the lake's shallow margins retreat, exposing wide mud flats that change the shoreline's shape noticeably from month to month.
