# Nakhon Phanom

A quiet riverfront town where a Vietnamese exile once kept a garden, an American air war was run by computer, and burning boats still float down the Mekong once a year.

**Location:** 17.4069, 104.7808

**Categories:** Isan, Cities in Thailand, Populated places on the Mekong River, Thailand-Laos border crossings

## Introduction

Somewhere between 1928 and 1931, a Vietnamese exile in his late thirties lived in Ban Nachok, a village a few kilometres west of Nakhon Phanom, among Vietnamese families who had already made this stretch of the Mekong home. He was organising, quietly, under a name that was not the one history settled on. Four decades later, American aircraft were taking off from a runway near that same village to attack the supply network the world had named after him. The house is still there. So is the runway. They are perhaps fifteen minutes apart by road, and the town between them has spent a very long time being a small place with an outsized view of other people's wars.

## The House on the Road to the Airfield

Ho Chi Minh's preserved home and the museum built beside it draw a steady flow of Vietnamese visitors, which makes Nakhon Phanom one of the few Thai towns where the tour buses arrive from Hanoi rather than Bangkok. The Vietnamese presence here is much older and broader than one famous resident. Families settled across the river through the colonial decades and the wars that followed, and the town's population today is a genuine mix - Thai, Thai-Lao, Thai-Vietnamese, Thai-Chinese - with Isan the language you actually hear in the market. In 1960 the Vietnamese community raised a clock tower on the riverfront as a public thank-you to the town that had taken them in. It still stands on the Mekong walking street, lit in colours at night, with a weekend night market running past its base.

## The War Run by Computer

The US airfield west of town, built by Navy Seabees in 1962 and opened for flying the following June, became the home of the 56th Air Commando Wing in April 1967 - redesignated the 56th Special Operations Wing in August 1968. It flew propeller aircraft in a jet age: A-1 Skyraiders, A-26 Invaders, C-123s, AC-119 gunships, and the Nail forward air controllers who found targets on the Ho Chi Minh Trail roughly 48 km east. Rescue squadrons launched from here to pull downed crews out of Laos and North Vietnam. The strangest tenant was Task Force Alpha, which ran the signal-processing centre for Operation Igloo White - air-dropped electronic sensors feeding a room full of machines that tried to read a jungle trail as data. The Son Tay prison raid staged through here in November 1970. The last USAF units left on 1 October 1975.

## Boats Made of Fire

At the end of Buddhist Lent, communities across the province build scaffolds of bamboo, mount them on boats, and hang them with tin cans of kerosene stuffed with rags. After dark the wicks are lit and the boats are pushed onto the Mekong, and the bamboo frames resolve into blazing outlines - temples, dragons, the king's portrait - drifting downstream past thousands of people standing on the bank. Lai Ruea Fai happens one night a year. The riverfront, which for the other 364 evenings is a place where people walk slowly and eat grilled chicken, becomes the best seat in Thailand.

## Two Towns, One River

Thakhek, capital of Laos's Khammouane province, sits directly across the water; from the Nakhon Phanom promenade you are looking at another country's provincial capital over a few hundred metres of current. The Third Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge, opened on 11 November 2011 about 10 km north of town, made the crossing routine - though not on foot, since walking the bridge is not permitted and the international bus runs eight times a day with about ninety minutes of paperwork at each end. Twice a month the border market fills with Lao traders, and among the usual Thai market goods you will find forest produce brought down from the Khammouane karst: roots, wild honey, mushrooms, orchids.

## Skylabs and Sticky Rice

Around town you travel by skylab, the northeast's three-wheeled motorcycle taxi, or you rent a bicycle and take the waterfront cycleway, which runs roughly 12 km north of the city - flat, quiet, scenic and merciless in the afternoon heat. The food argument here is settled: sticky rice, grilled chicken and som tam, at their national best. What Nakhon Phanom does not have is a nightlife, or English menus, or much sense that it needs either. Dinner is a stall on Fuangnakhom Street from four in the afternoon, on a road closed to traffic, with the river going dark beyond it.

## Flight Context

Nakhon Phanom sits at 17.41°N, 104.78°E on the Mekong's west bank, with Thakhek, Laos directly opposite. The Third Thai-Lao Friendship Bridge crosses about 10 km north of the town centre and is the clearest visual fix; a 3 km lake lies immediately west of the city. Nakhon Phanom Airport (VTUW/KOP) is a short hop west of downtown, with Sakon Nakhon (VTUI/SNO) about 90 km west and Savannakhet, Laos (VLSK/ZVK) to the south. Recommended viewing altitude 2,500-5,000 ft AGL to pick out the riverfront promenade and bridge. Tropical savanna climate: dry and clear from November to February, hottest in April with daily highs near 35°C, and monsoon rain with convective build-ups from May through October.
