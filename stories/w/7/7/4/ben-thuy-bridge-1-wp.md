# Bến Thủy Bridge 1

For generations, crossing the Lam River between Nghệ An and Hà Tĩnh meant queuing for one of three ferries; in 1990, 630 metres of steel and concrete ended the wait.

**Location:** 18.6465, 105.7084

**Categories:** Road bridges in Vietnam, Bridges completed in 1990, Bridges in Vietnam, Nghệ An province, Hà Tĩnh province

## Introduction

Three ferries. That was the entire capacity for moving people, motorbikes, trucks and freight across the Cả River between two provinces whose main arteries both ran straight at the water. Everything on Vietnam's north-south highway had to stop at Bến Thủy and wait its turn, or take the pontoon bridge when one was in place. The queues at the landing were part of the region's texture — hours lost as routine, a bottleneck so old that nobody under sixty had known the crossing any other way. Ending it took four years, five hundred workers and a dam built inside the river.

## Draining the River

Construction started in 1986, part of a wider push to unblock the region's infrastructure and its economy. More than 500 people worked on the project, and many of them did not commute to it — they relocated, moving into quarters built on the construction site and living beside the work for the duration.

The hardest problem was the middle of the channel, where the piers had to go and the water was deepest. The solution was to build steel sheeting out into the river, seal it, and pump the enclosed section dry so crews could work on the exposed bed. Into that dry pocket went P300 concrete for the central pillars — a Vietnamese grade specification, poured to a standard set for a bridge that would have to carry the whole country's traffic on one deck. Then the sheeting came out and the river came back.

## What They Built

The finished structure runs 630.5 metres over thirteen spans, and it is not one bridge so much as four techniques stitched together. A prestressed reinforced concrete T-beam carries the leading span. Steel truss girders take the main channel, the deep-water stretch where the pumped-out cofferdam had stood. Two simple beams of 53 metres each and three continuous girder spans make up the rest.

It opened to traffic on 19 May 1990, the anniversary of Hồ Chí Minh's birth. When it opened it was the second-longest bridge in Vietnam, and the first permanent crossing of the Cả River in the region's history. Nghệ An and Hà Tĩnh, which had been fifteen minutes and an indefinite wait apart, were suddenly one drive.

## Proof by Closure

The bridge's importance became clearest when it stopped working. Between 2009 and 2010 a series of serious motor accidents raised real concerns about the structure's integrity, and engineers began closing it without warning to run structural tests. Each closure backed up traffic into the thousands of vehicles.

There is a bleak logic to that. A bridge that nobody misses is a bridge nobody needed. Twenty years after opening, Bến Thủy was carrying so much of Vietnam's north-south freight that taking it out of service for an afternoon produced queues visible from the air. The jams were not an argument against the bridge; they were the strongest possible argument for a second one. In 2010 the government announced exactly that.

## Handing Over

Bến Thủy Bridge 2 opened in September 2012, a box girder structure 996 metres long, built alongside its predecessor to take the through traffic off it. National Route 1 now runs across the newer bridge, bypassing Vinh, and the 1990 span has become part of the old road — local journeys, commuters between the two banks, the kind of traffic it was originally sized for.

It got its first major overhaul in 2014, closing for three months. That is a striking number: twenty-four years of continuous service before the first significant repair, on a bridge that had absorbed far more load than anyone specified in 1986. What began as the fix for a three-ferry bottleneck now works as the local alternative to the road that replaced it — a demotion that is really a kind of retirement, and better earned than most.

## Flight Context

The bridge crosses the Cả (Lam) River at 18.6465°N, 105.7084°E, linking Vinh in Nghệ An with Hà Tĩnh province on the south bank. It is easy to identify from the air as one of a pair: Bến Thủy 1's steel truss over the main channel sits beside the longer, cleaner box-girder line of Bến Thủy 2, which carries National Route 1. Dũng Quyết mountain rises immediately north of the crossing and makes a reliable pointer; the Hồng Lĩnh ridge fills the horizon 15 km to the south. Vinh International Airport (VVVH) is about 10 km north. Low-level flight below 2,000 ft shows the span composition clearly, but the river valley holds haze and low stratus through the winter months, and afternoon visibility in the dry season is often reduced by dust off the coastal plain.
