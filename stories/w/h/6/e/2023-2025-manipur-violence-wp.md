# 2023-2025 Manipur Violence

Beginning in May 2023, ethnic violence between the Meitei majority and Kuki-Zo tribal minority tore through Manipur, displacing tens of thousands and exposing deep fractures in India's northeastern frontier.

**Location:** 24.6000, 93.8000

**Categories:** 2023-2025 Manipur violence, Ethnic conflicts in Manipur, 2020s in Manipur, Human rights in India

## Introduction

On the evening of 3 May 2023, the Imphal Valley burned. In Churachandpur, Meitei settlements were attacked. In Imphal city, Kuki-Zo neighborhoods were set alight. By the time dawn-to-dusk curfews were imposed across eight districts, roughly 1,700 houses had been destroyed and the state of Manipur had fractured along ethnic lines so completely that, two years later, the wounds had not closed. What began as a dispute over tribal status classifications escalated into one of the worst episodes of communal violence in modern Indian history -- a crisis that displaced over 46,000 people, killed dozens in its first weeks alone, and drew condemnation from the United Nations, India's Supreme Court, and citizens around the world.

## The Faultline Beneath the Valley

Manipur's geography mirrors its ethnic divisions. The Meitei, who are predominantly Hindu and make up the majority of the state's population, inhabit the fertile Imphal Valley. The Kuki-Zo and Naga peoples, many of them Christian, live in the surrounding hills. Tensions over land, resources, and political representation have simmered for decades, but the immediate trigger came from a Manipur High Court recommendation that the Meitei be added to the Scheduled Tribes list -- a classification that would grant them access to protections and land rights in the hill districts traditionally reserved for tribal communities. For the Kuki-Zo, the prospect was existential: it threatened to open their ancestral hill territories to settlement by the valley's more numerous and politically dominant population. Protests erupted, met by counter-protests. On 3 May, stone-throwing at Torbung escalated into full-scale communal violence.

## A State Torn in Two

The violence spread with terrifying speed. Residences, churches, and temples were burned across Churachandpur, Kangpokpi, Tengnoupal, and the Imphal Valley. The government issued shoot-on-sight orders on 4 May. Nearly 10,000 army, paramilitary, and Central Armed Police Forces deployed into the state, eventually growing to 126 army columns and 62 paramilitary companies. Despite this massive military presence, the violence kept recurring. On 29 May, five more people were killed. On 14 June, at least eleven were shot in a fresh outbreak so severe that doctors said many bodies were difficult to identify. Armed groups on both sides -- the Meitei organizations Arambai Tenggol and Meitei Leepun, and Kuki militant groups operating under ceasefire agreements with the government -- were implicated in attacks on each other's villages. Weapons looted from state armories circulated freely.

## Victims in the Crossfire

Reuters reported that during the first week alone, 77 Kukis and 10 Meiteis were killed. By 14 May, the government tally stood at 73 dead, 243 injured, and over 26,000 people sheltered in 178 relief camps. The human cost went beyond statistics. A viral video that emerged in July 2023 showed two Kuki women being paraded naked by a mob -- an assault that had occurred on 4 May but went unreported for over two months because the government had shut down internet access across the state. One of the women was allegedly gang-raped; her father and teenage brother were killed by the mob trying to protect her. Despite a complaint being filed, police took no action until the video surfaced publicly. A 21-year-old Kuki youth, arrested for a social media post critical of the chief minister, was beaten to death on an Imphal street while supposedly in police custody.

## When the Courts Intervened

India's Supreme Court called the police investigations "tardy" and declared that there had been an "absolute breakdown of law and order and machinery of the State." On 7 August 2023, the court took suo moto cognizance and formed a three-member committee led by retired Justice Gita Mittal to oversee relief and rehabilitation. Former Mumbai Police Commissioner Dattatray Padsalgikar was appointed to supervise the investigations. The court expressed shock that it had taken 14 days to register even a preliminary report on the assault captured in the viral video. In March 2025, six Supreme Court judges visited relief camps still housing displaced families. The UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, Volker Turk, urged Indian authorities to address the root causes of the violence. Human rights defender Babloo Loitongbam saw his home vandalized and received threats from Meitei organizations for speaking out. The UN Human Rights office publicly called for his protection.

## An Unfinished Reckoning

Prime Minister Narendra Modi did not publicly address the crisis until 20 July 2023 -- nearly three months after the violence began -- and only after the video of the two women went viral. His silence drew sharp criticism. All ten Kuki-Zo members of the state legislature, including ruling party representatives, accused the state government of tacitly supporting the violence and said that living under a Meitei-majority administration would be "as good as death" for their community. Olympic medallist Mary Kom, a Manipur native, tweeted an appeal for help. Eleven international and national award-winning athletes from the state threatened to return their awards if the state's territorial integrity was compromised. The political fallout continued into 2025, with coalition partners withdrawing support from the state government. For the tens of thousands who remained displaced, the crisis was not a political talking point but a daily reality of relief camps, lost homes, and a fractured state where neighbors had become enemies.

## Flight Context

Located at 24.60°N, 93.80°E in the Imphal Valley of Manipur, northeast India. The valley is a distinctive flat basin surrounded by forested hills, clearly visible from altitude. Imphal Airport (VEIM/IMF) is the primary airport. The Imphal Valley floor sits at roughly 790 meters elevation, with surrounding hills rising to 1,500-2,500 meters. The geographic divide between valley (Meitei areas) and hills (tribal areas) is strikingly visible from the air. Best viewed at 15,000-20,000 feet to see the full valley-to-hills transition.
