# Sihanoukville

Cambodia built a city from nothing in five years to reach its own sea, and has spent every decade since arguing over who gets to use it.

**Location:** 10.6333, 103.5000

**Categories:** Cities in Cambodia, Provincial capitals in Cambodia, Port cities, Gulf of Thailand, Planned cities, Vietnam War sites

## Introduction

Cambodia has always had a coastline. For most of its recorded history it could not use one. Trade moved down the Mekong, and the Mekong reached the sea through Vietnamese territory, which meant that a country with several hundred kilometres of shore was functionally landlocked by somebody else's river. Kampot was the only international seaport, eight days from the capital by oxcart. Then, in June 1955, with independence two years old, crews began cutting a deep-water port out of a stretch of empty shore called Kompong Som — and a city had to be invented to serve it.

## A City Ordered Into Existence

The port cost twelve million US dollars, most of it French money, and was finished in late 1959; a French minister, Louis Jacquinot, inaugurated it in April 1960. The city arrived on paper first. A 1959 urban plan laid out a settlement for 55,000 people, with cycle paths and green space, zoning for the port and the railway, a town centre for municipal offices and business, and a tourist strip reserved along the southern beaches. There was no bourgeois colonial quarter to inherit, as in Kampot or Phnom Penh, because there had been nothing here to colonise. What went up instead was new: reinforced concrete buildings by the architects of Cambodia's brief modernist decade, sited on a peninsula whose highest ground barely clears fifteen metres. The Southern Line railway, 264 kilometres of it, was laid from here to Phnom Penh between 1960 and 1969.

## The Last Place Out

The plan lasted about ten years. During the Vietnam War the port served both sides in sequence — supplying the National Front for the Liberation of South Vietnam, then, after the 1970 coup brought Lon Nol to power, the United States. It was the last place the US Army evacuated, days before the Khmer Rouge took the country in April 1975. Barely a month later it became a battlefield. On 12 May 1975 the Khmer Rouge seized the American container ship SS Mayaguez; the rescue operation that followed played out over three days on and around Koh Tang island offshore, with American air strikes hitting the port, the Ream naval base, the airfield, the rail yard and the refinery. Three Marines were left behind on Koh Tang in the withdrawal and later executed. Their names are the last ones carved into the Vietnam Veterans Memorial in Washington.

## Boomtown

Recovery came slowly, then all at once. Container traffic through the port climbed from 255,000 units in 2012 to over 640,000 in 2020, and the highway corridor to Phnom Penh now carries around three-quarters of Cambodia's trade. The Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone, built with Chinese backing, held 174 factories and more than 30,000 workers by March 2020. A Chinese-built expressway to the capital opened in October 2022. Alongside the industry came casinos — more than a hundred of them planned by 2019, aimed squarely at mainland Chinese gamblers who could not legally gamble at home. By that year, Chinese nationals were reported to own the large majority of the city's businesses, and estimates put the Chinese resident population near 78,000. The building went up fast and not always well: in 2019 a partly constructed building collapsed in the city, killing twenty-eight people and injuring twenty-six.

## After the Ban

Cambodia banned online gambling in 2019, and then the pandemic emptied what was left. The casinos stood, half-finished towers stood, and something else moved into them. Compounds across the city were turned to online fraud — the long-con investment swindles known as pig butchering — staffed not by employees but by people trafficked in from across Asia on the promise of ordinary jobs, then held behind guarded walls and made to run scripts under threat of violence. The UN Human Rights Office has documented hundreds of thousands trapped this way in Cambodia and Myanmar. Raids began in 2022 and enforcement has come in bursts since. In October 2025, prosecutors announced the seizure of roughly 127,000 bitcoin, then worth over fourteen billion dollars — the largest cryptocurrency forfeiture on record — in an action against a Cambodian conglomerate accused of running such compounds. In January 2026, after further arrests, Cambodian authorities released thousands of workers; more than 2,750 Indonesians alone went to their embassy asking to go home.

## The City Underneath

None of that is the whole city, and it is easy to forget there is one. The 2019 census counted about 73,000 residents, roughly 67,000 of them in the urban core. Almost nobody's family has been here more than three generations — this population is assembled from the Cambodian diaspora, from the wreckage of the Pol Pot years, and from Vietnamese, Chinese, Cham, Thai and Korean communities that came for the port. There is no public transport; you move by tuk-tuk or by moto-dup, and the drivers navigate by landmark because most streets have no useful names. Wat Leu sits on the hill above the bay, dedicated to the Buddhist patriarch Chhoun Nath, who died before the Khmer Rouge came. From up there the port, the beaches and the towers all fit in one view.

## Flight Context

Sihanoukville sits at 10.6333°N, 103.5000°E on the tip of a low peninsula, elevation about 15 m, with roughly 196 km² of city area. Best viewed from 3,000 to 5,000 ft AGL — the peninsula shape, the Autonomous Port on the north shore, and the beach chain running south from Victory through Independence and Ochheuteal to Otres are all legible at that height. Koh Puos lies 800 m off Victory Beach, linked by road bridge; Koh Rong is 25 km west. Sihanouk International (KOS/VDSV, elev. 40 ft, 3,300 m runway) is 20 km east in Ream commune, near National Highway 4. Tropical monsoon: wet season April to November with humidity often above 90 percent and afternoon convection; December to March is dry, hot and often hazy. Tropical cyclones rarely reach this coast.
