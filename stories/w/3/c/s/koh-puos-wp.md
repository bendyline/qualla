# Koh Puos

The bridge to Snake Island was finished in 2011; the city it was meant to serve is still being argued over.

**Location:** 10.6264, 103.4805

**Categories:** Islands, Cambodia, Coastal, Development and real estate, Bridges

## Introduction

Most places build the town first and the bridge afterwards. Koh Puos did it the other way round. In 2011 a causeway of prestressed concrete reached out from the Sihanoukville waterfront and touched down on a wooded island barely half a kilometre offshore — a serious piece of engineering, built to carry traffic to a luxury resort city that had not yet been built and, more than a decade later, is still being redrawn.

## Elbow Island

The island's shape gave it its first European name. French colonial charts called it Île Coudée — Elbow Island — for the bend in its outline, and the description holds up from the air. It runs about 1.7 kilometres long and between 700 metres and 1.4 kilometres wide, covering roughly 1.1 square kilometres of forest and rock in the Gulf of Thailand. In Khmer it is Koh Puos, which English renders as Snake Island, and property brochures have at various points preferred the softer Morakot. It sits about 630 metres from the Sihanoukville shoreline, some 800 metres out from Victory Beach, close enough that the whole island reads as part of the town's seafront rather than something separate from it. Administratively it belongs to Sangkat 3 of Mittakpheap District — an island with a municipal address.

## The Concession

In 2006 the Cambodian government granted a long-term concession over the island to the Koh Puos (Cambodia) Investment Group, a Russian-backed venture with a large-scale plan: most of the island turned into a luxury residential and resort complex, an entire settlement conjured onto land that had never held a town. Cambodia's coastline was being parcelled out in exactly this way through the 2000s, island by island, on concessions measured in decades. Koh Puos was among the most visible of them, because you could stand on Victory Beach and see the whole thing at once.

## Ahead of Schedule, Behind Schedule

The bridge went up on time and the city did not follow. What followed instead was a long sequence of revisions — altered plans, new blueprints, fresh applications to the Council for the Development of Cambodia, each cycle consuming months and then years. The scheme has been redesigned more than once and delayed repeatedly. Anyone who has watched a large speculative development stall will recognise the pattern: the expensive, permanent, unmissable piece of infrastructure gets finished first, because it is the part that proves the project is real, and then the part it was built to serve slows to a crawl.

## A Coast Full of Islands

Koh Puos is the nearest of a scatter that runs west and south from Sihanoukville, and the contrast between them is the whole story of this coastline. Koh Ta Kiev and Koh Russei lie within a short boat ride, low and forested. Koh Rong and Koh Rong Sanloem sit twenty to thirty kilometres out, big enough to hold villages and long white beaches, and they took the backpacker traffic that once filled Sihanoukville's guesthouses. East of the city, Ream National Park protects a stretch of mangrove and estuary where boats push up channels through the roots. Most of them have at some point been drawn into a concession or a resort masterplan. Koh Puos simply got its bridge before the others got their arguments.

## The Boom That Went Somewhere Else

Meanwhile the mainland transformed without waiting. Through the 2010s Sihanoukville absorbed an extraordinary wave of Chinese investment; by 2019 more than 90 percent of businesses in the city were Chinese-owned, and over a hundred casinos were planned for a town that had been a backpacker beach stop a decade earlier. Then Cambodia banned online gambling in 2019, the pandemic closed the rest, and the boom left behind a skyline of unfinished concrete towers. Koh Puos sat 800 metres offshore through all of it, connected by a bridge, forested, waiting. It remains one of the more honest views in Cambodia: a beautifully engineered road to a promise still under negotiation.

## Flight Context

Koh Puos lies at 10.6264°N, 103.48054°E, immediately off the north-western tip of Sihanoukville in the Gulf of Thailand. From 2,000 to 4,000 feet the causeway is the giveaway — a thin bright line running roughly 900 metres from the mainland shore near Victory Beach to the island's inner edge, with the elbow-shaped, densely wooded landmass beyond it. The Sihanoukville port cranes are about 3 km south-east; Koh Rong and Koh Rong Sanloem sit 20-30 km west across open water. Sihanouk International Airport (VDSV / KOS) is roughly 20 km east-south-east. Water clarity and colour are best in the dry season from November to April; the south-west monsoon between May and October brings low cloud, reduced visibility and a choppy sea state that flattens the island's outline against the water.
