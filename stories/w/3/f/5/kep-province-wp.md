# Kep province

Cambodia's smallest province holds the ruins of its most glamorous resort - and the truth about how those ruins were made is not the one usually told.

**Location:** 10.4833, 104.3000

**Categories:** Kep province, Provinces of Cambodia, Gulf of Thailand, States and territories established in 2008

## Introduction

The story everyone tells about Kep is that the Khmer Rouge blew up the villas. It is a tidy story, and it is wrong. What actually happened to Cambodia's grandest seaside architecture is harder to hear. After 1975 people who had nothing took the houses apart by hand - fittings, window frames, roofing, wiring, anything that could be carried - and moved it over the border into Vietnam to trade for rice and cash. The mansions of Kep-sur-Mer did not fall to ideology. They were dismantled piece by piece by neighbours who needed to eat, and the roofless concrete shells standing in the palm scrub today are the record of that hunger.

## Three Hundred and Thirty-Six Square Kilometres

Kep is the smallest province in Cambodia and the least populous - 336 square kilometres, fewer than fifty thousand people at the 2024 census. It is also one of the newest, carved out of Kampot province by royal decree on 22 December 2008. Kampot still wraps it almost completely: the only edge Kep has that is not Kampot is 16 kilometres of coastline facing the Gulf of Thailand, with the Vietnamese island of Phú Quốc out on the horizon. Twenty kilometres to the west lies Kampot town, twenty to the east lies Vietnam. Inside that small compass are one district, one municipality, five communes, a national park, a mountain range with a violent past, and thirteen islands. Not much land, then, but an unusual amount of history per hectare.

## Kep-sur-Mer

The French built it, in the administrative sense and very nearly the literal one. Kep belonged to the Circonscription Résidentielle de Kampot, and in 1908 it was established as a seaside station and given the name Kep-sur-Mer. For the next six decades it was where the colonial administration and then the Cambodian elite went to be seen - the most prestigious beach town in the country, right up until the early 1970s, when the war arrived and the whole idea stopped making sense. The coastline itself was never the point. Kep's shore is mangrove marsh and black rock, nothing like the white sand of Sihanoukville; the beach that visitors walk today was widened and lightened with sand trucked in from Sihanoukville. What Kep had instead was position, sea breeze, and the willingness of a lot of money to build there.

## Concrete That Knew What It Was Doing

The buildings that made Kep extraordinary went up after the French left, between 1953 and 1970. Vann Molyvann and Lu Ban Hap, the leading figures of what became known as New Khmer Architecture, took the vocabulary of the modern movement - Bauhaus, Le Corbusier, Richard Neutra - and rebuilt it for a climate of monsoon and glare, folding in the forms of traditional Khmer architecture. Norodom Sihanouk championed the style as the look of an independent Cambodia, and he practised what he promoted: he kept a villa in Kep and an island offshore, the Île des Ambassadeurs, where he entertained a steady procession of guests. Those villas were among the finest modernist houses anywhere in Asia. Most of them are now open to the sky, and they are still worth the trip.

## Thirteen Islands, One Rabbit

East of the peninsula the water is dotted with a tiny archipelago of thirteen islands and islets, and their names are a small dictionary of rural Cambodia: Egret, Chili, Mango, Wild Mango, Millstone, Dry Fish, Dry Island, and Koh Angkrong, named for the Ziziphus cambodiana tree. The famous one is Koh Tonsay, Rabbit Island, two square kilometres of white sand about 4.5 kilometres offshore. Its name has at least two origin stories. In one, Prince Sakor Reach fled here with exhausted troops who spread out across the island - Koh Rumsay, then Ormsay, then Ornsay, then Tonsay. Older residents who grew up in Kep before 1975 remember it as Koh Antai instead, a word with no obvious meaning left in it. Under Sihanouk's Sangkum Reastr Niyum the island served as a penal settlement, where the men sent to be rehabilitated were also expected to defend the place. Their horse-cart paths and thatched motels have long since gone to weather and war.

## Rice, Salt and Limestone

Kep's economy is still mostly what it always was: farming, fishing, salt pans and, increasingly, visitors. In 2008 some 92 percent of the population worked in agriculture, three-quarters of that rice. The province has no university and, as recently as 2008, no hospital - five clinics, and in the dry season of that year 78 percent of families were drinking from unsafe sources. Landmine and unexploded-ordnance casualties still appeared in the 2009 provincial survey. Against that, the schools multiply: Don Bosco's technical school, the Kep Gardens Association, the Ecole pour Tous, the Kep International School. Inland, the 11.52-square-kilometre Kep National Park, established in 1993, offers an eight-kilometre trail circling the hills behind town, and at Phnom Sar Sear three low limestone rises - the tallest around 40 metres - hold caves that locals have used for far longer than anyone has been counting tourists.

## Flight Context

Kep province occupies the coast around 10.483°N, 104.30°E in southern Cambodia, wedged between Kampot province and the Vietnamese border. The whole province fits comfortably in one view from 3,000-5,000 ft: a triangular peninsula pushing southwest into Kep Bay, forested hills rising behind the town, salt pans and rice paddy inland, and thirteen small islands scattered east and south over shallow water. Koh Tonsay, the largest and whitest-beached, lies about 4.5 km offshore. Useful visual references are the Kep White Horse Monument where National Road 33A branches off the coast road, the Phnom Voar range inland to the north, and the Bokor plateau of Kampot province rising to the northwest. Phú Quốc is visible to the south on a clear day. Nearest airports: Sihanouk International (VDSV) about 40 nm west, Phnom Penh International (VDPP) about 71 nm north, Phú Quốc International (VVPQ) about 27 nm south. Kampot has a 579 m grass strip (KMT) 23 km northwest of Kampot town. Southwest monsoon from May to October brings low cloud, haze and heavy afternoon convection; November to April gives the clean horizons.
