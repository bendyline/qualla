# Nguyễn Trung Trực Temple

Fishermen hid an executed rebel's memory inside a shrine to the whale spirit; a century and a half later, tens of thousands come to eat free meals in his courtyard.

**Location:** 10.0117, 105.0794

**Categories:** Vietnam, Temples, Colonial resistance, Mekong Delta, Festivals

## Introduction

The French could not catch Nguyễn Trung Trực, so they took his mother. He came in. On 27 October 1868 they beheaded him in the market at Rạch Giá, in front of the town he had captured four months earlier; he was thirty years old. The sentence attributed to him is one Vietnamese schoolchildren still learn: when the Westerners have pulled up every blade of grass in this country, only then will there be no Vietnamese left to fight them. The French had wanted a public ending. What they got was a shrine — though it took the people of Rạch Giá some care and some cunning to build one.

## Hidden Behind the Whale

Openly venerating an executed rebel under colonial administration was not a realistic option. Venerating a whale was. Along Vietnam's southern coast, fishing communities have long honoured the whale spirit — Nam Hải Đại Tướng Quân, the Great General of the Southern Sea — as a protector of fishermen, and a simple shrine to him already stood near the Kiên River, built by local fishermen out of wood and thatch. Within a few years of the execution, sometime in the late 1860s or early 1870s, the people of Rạch Giá began quietly directing their offerings there to a second figure. The whale god provided the cover. The rebel received the incense. It is one of the most elegant acts of concealment in the region's history, and it worked long enough to outlast the empire it was hiding from.

## Nhật Tảo

Born in 1838 in Tân An, in what is now Long An province, Nguyễn Trung Trực began life as Nguyễn Văn Lịch and made his name on the water. On 10 December 1861 his men attacked the French lorcha L'Espérance on the Nhật Tảo canal and burned her, killing around twenty French sailors and Vietnamese auxiliaries while losing four of their own — a small engagement by the standards of the conquest, and an enormous one by the standards of a resistance that had very little to celebrate. In mid-1868 he took the French fort at Rạch Giá outright, killing the French-installed provincial chief and some thirty defenders. Then came the hostage-taking, the surrender, and the market square. Among the figures honoured on the temple altars beside him is Lâm Quang Ky, one of the men who fought under him.

## Built by Subscription

The thatch gave way to something permanent by degrees. Repairs in 1881 enlarged the site, and a full reconstruction ran from 20 December 1964 to 24 February 1970 — more than five years of work, designed by the architect Nguyễn Văn Lợi and paid for entirely by community contributions, with no state money involved. The result follows the 'tam' plan, three parallel elements named for the Chinese character 三: a main hall flanked by eastern and western wings. The three-gate entrance carries a tiled roof crowned with two dragons contesting a pearl, and couplets drawn from the poet Huỳnh Mẫn Đạt frame the opening. Inside, the roof curves under dragon and chrysanthemum motifs, and the columns are wrapped in carved dragons coiling upward toward the beams.

## A Grave, a Statue, a Certificate

The pieces arrived slowly, each one a small assertion. In 1986 a tomb was built in the courtyard — a plain rectangular cement structure with a stele reading Anh hùng Nguyễn Trung Trực, 1838–1868, hero. In 1988 the Ministry of Culture and Information designated the whole site a national historical and cultural relic. A traditional medicine clinic opened on the grounds in 1989 and has been giving away free consultations and remedies ever since, more than three decades of them. In 2000 a bronze statue of him was moved here from the market where he died. An exhibition room displays objects from his campaigns. Nine temples in the region honour him; this is the oldest and the largest of them.

## Three Days in the Eighth Month

On the twenty-sixth to twenty-eighth of the eighth lunar month — occasionally stretching to a fourth day — Rạch Giá fills up. The festival marking his death includes ritual observance, reenactments of his battles, folk games, martial arts displays and lion dances, and it is famous less for the ceremony than for the food: volunteers cook and serve free vegetarian meals to tens of thousands of people, for days, at no charge to anyone. In 2023 the festival was inscribed as national intangible cultural heritage. Vietnamese, Khmer and Chinese families in the delta all come, which is part of why the event is spoken of as a gathering of the region's communities rather than a monument to a single ethnicity. The man was executed to make a point. The point was not the one the French intended.

## Flight Context

The temple stands at 10.0117°N, 105.0794°E in Rạch Giá, close to the waterfront and only about a kilometre from Rạch Giá Airport (VVRG / VKG), whose single 1,500 m runway sits at 7 ft elevation just south of the site. From a low circuit at 1,500–2,500 ft the tam quan gate and the three-part temple layout are picked out by their tiled roofs against the surrounding streets, with the Kiên River and the Gulf of Thailand immediately west. Phú Quốc International (VVPQ) lies roughly 110 km west-northwest. If you time a visit for the eighth lunar month, expect the surrounding streets to be impassable — the festival draws crowds in the tens of thousands.
