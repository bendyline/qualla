# Angkor Borei Museum

A one-room district museum in a pagoda yard holds the material of Funan - and lends its sculpture to Cleveland, because the city it stands in was a capital before Angkor existed.

**Location:** 10.9942, 104.9778

**Categories:** Archaeological museums in Cambodia, Funan, Chenla, Takéo province, Khmer sculpture

## Introduction

In November 2021 the Cleveland Museum of Art opened an exhibition built around a sandstone Krishna, roughly 1,500 years old, shown lifting Mount Govardhan on one raised arm. The sculpture had been found at Phnom Da, a granite hill in southern Cambodia, and two Cambodian institutions lent works to stand beside it: the National Museum of Cambodia in Phnom Penh, and the Angkor Borei Museum - a small provincial building inside a pagoda compound, in a district town of flooded rice fields that most maps of Cambodia do not label. The disproportion is only apparent. Angkor Borei was a capital while northern Europe was still in the Iron Age, and the museum sits directly on top of the evidence.

## City of Cities

អង្គរបុរី translates, plainly, as city of cities. The settlement was a major centre of Funan - the earliest state in mainland Southeast Asia, running from roughly the first century CE to the seventh - and quite possibly its capital. Scholars have proposed it as the Sinae Metropolis that Ptolemy recorded as the furthest known city to the east, though that identification stays argued. What is not argued is the plumbing: a network of ancient canals connected Angkor Borei inland to the great port of Óc Eo on the coast, now in Vietnam, and the two places together formed the working heart of Funan. Chinese envoys of the Eastern Wu, Kang Tai and Zhu Ying, visited in the mid-third century and wrote the accounts that remain the backbone of what anyone knows about the kingdom. Roman, Persian, Indian and Greek objects have come out of the ground in this delta.

## Digging in a Living Town

Serious excavation began in 1995 and continued in 1996, with a return season in 1999 and 2000, under the Lower Mekong Archaeological Project - a collaboration between the University of Hawaiʻi and the Royal University of Fine Arts examining social and political development across the millennium from about 500 BC to AD 500. The Vat Komnou cemetery alone yielded some sixty burials dating between 200 BC and AD 400. Angkor Borei also holds the earliest dated Khmer inscriptions and what may be the first tradition of Khmer stone sculpture, which makes it the place where the Khmer written and carved record actually starts. The archaeology is complicated by the fact that people still live here. Ground gets bulldozed, gardened and built on, and the project has had to work around looting and the trade in trafficked Khmer antiquities that has shadowed Cambodian archaeology for decades.

## What Is in the Cases

The museum opened to the public in 1999, funded with help from the European Union and inaugurated in front of representatives from Cambodia's Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts, its Ministry of Tourism, the EU, the University of Hawaiʻi and PRASAC - two aims at once, protecting excavated material and giving Takéo province a reason for anyone to visit. Inside are objects of stone, fired clay and copper spanning the Funan and Chenla periods: sculptures of Vishnu and Shiva, ceramics, inscriptions, jewellery, utensils, Neolithic pieces, and excavated material around 2,500 years old. Most of the monumental statuary from Phnom Da - the sixth-century temple three kilometres to the southeast, the oldest surviving Khmer stone temple - went long ago to the National Museum in Phnom Penh. What remains here is the everyday record, which is in some ways the harder thing to assemble.

## Going Out and Coming Back

Revealing Krishna: Journey to Cambodia's Sacred Mountain ran in Cleveland from 14 November 2021 to 30 January 2022, reuniting sculpture from Phnom Da that had been scattered across collections for generations. For a district museum to lend into an exhibition of that scale is not a small thing; it places Angkor Borei inside the international conversation about where Khmer objects belong and who gets to display them, rather than leaving it as the anonymous findspot on someone else's label. That conversation has money behind it now. In March 2020 the United States government committed $4.5 million to Cambodian cultural heritage work, and the Ministry of Culture and Fine Arts directed part of it into improving the Angkor Borei displays and building education programmes around them.

## Getting There Is the Exhibit

Reaching the museum means an hour by boat from Takéo town for much of the year, through country that is half water: rice standing in shallow flood, fish traps, herds of ducks, and the wooden cargo boats that work up from Vietnam. Angkor Borei and Phnom Da went onto UNESCO's tentative World Heritage list in 1992, and Cambodia renewed the nomination in 2020. The site has not yet been inscribed. Meanwhile a small building in a wat yard holds two thousand years of a city that mattered before Angkor was thought of, and the town goes on living on top of it.

## Flight Context

The museum sits at 10.994°N, 104.978°E in Angkor Borei town, about 20 km east of Doun Kaev and roughly 35 km northwest of Châu Đốc across the Vietnamese border. From 2,000-4,000 ft the district is a flat green-and-silver plain at around 5-10 m elevation, laced with straight channels - some of them following alignments two thousand years old - and the single reliable landmark is Phnom Da, an isolated granite hill roughly 100 m high about 3 km southeast of the town, carrying a distinctive square laterite-and-sandstone tower. In the June-October wet season, standing water covers most of the surrounding fields and the town reads as an island. Nearest fields: Techo International (VDTI/KTI) about 30 nm north-northwest, the former Phnom Penh International (VDPP) about 45 nm north, and Cần Thơ International (VVCT) about 60 nm southeast in Vietnam. Note that the international border runs immediately southeast of the site. Clearest conditions November through February.
