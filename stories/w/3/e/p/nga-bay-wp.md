# Ngã Bảy

Seven canals meet here in a single wide star of brown water - and when the government renamed the town in 2005, the people who lived on that water made it change its mind.

**Location:** 9.8164, 105.8197

**Categories:** Cần Thơ, Mekong Delta, Markets, Canals, Cities in Vietnam

## Introduction

Government Decree 98/2005/ND-CP, issued on 26 July 2005, carved a new town out of Phụng Hiệp district and christened it Tân Hiệp. The residents were not consulted and did not care for the result. For ninety years the place had answered to Ngã Bảy - the Seven-Way Intersection - named for the junction where seven canals converge into one wide brown star of water, and for the floating market that grew up on top of it. The objections were loud enough to register. In late 2006 the town was formally renamed Ngã Bảy. As administrative history goes this is a footnote, and it is also the rarest sort of civic victory: a name handed back to the people who actually use it.

## What the Dredges Made

Nothing about this junction is natural. The Mekong Delta's waterways were reorganised wholesale under French rule by steam dredges that cut geometrically straight channels across the floodplain - the same programme that in 1908 opened a 33 km canal linking Sóc Trăng to Phụng Hiệp. Where several of those cuts happened to intersect, engineering produced something no river would have: seven navigable channels radiating from a single point. Cái Côn, Lái Hiếu, Mang Cá and Xẻo Môn are among the arms; sources differ on how the remaining three should be named, which is its own comment on how much this waterscape has been redrawn. From the air the pattern looks almost heraldic. From a sampan at water level it looks like seven different ways to leave, which is precisely what made the town's fortune.

## Three Hundred Boats Before Breakfast

The market formed in 1915, once the canals were finished, and it became the largest waterway trading hub in southern Vietnam. On a good morning more than three hundred boats gathered at the junction, hulls nudging hulls, wholesalers and retailers working from the decks. Delta traders advertise by the cây bẹo, a tall bamboo pole raised above the deck with a sample of the cargo lashed to it - a pumpkin, a string of onions, a bundle of cassava - so a buyer can read a whole market from a distance without a word spoken. Delta fruit moved out from here into the rest of the country and beyond. The scene generated its own folklore: songs and poems about vendors calling from the water, an image that came to stand for the delta itself in the Vietnamese imagination.

## The Market That Was Moved

In 2002 the authorities relocated it. The stated reasons were congestion and pollution, both genuine problems at a junction handling that much traffic, and the market was shifted about three kilometres away to Ba Ngàn. It did not take. Traders lost the customers who had known where to find them, tourists lost the seven-canal setting that was the entire point, and the new site never developed the character of the old one. Disappointment was widespread and vocal. By late 2006 - the same season the town won back its name - the market had returned to its original water. It has never fully recovered its former scale, and regional authorities have since run a series of initiatives to revive it as a heritage and tourism landmark.

## A City on Two Kinds of Road

Ngã Bảy grew into the largest city in Hậu Giang province, with a population of 101,192 at the 2019 count - bigger than Vị Thanh, the provincial capital it answered to. The geography explains the imbalance. Vị Thanh is about 40 km away in a straight line but more than 70 km by road, while Ngã Bảy sits directly on National Road 1A, thirty kilometres from Cần Thơ in one direction and thirty from Sóc Trăng in the other. A town that spent its first century oriented toward water spent its second oriented toward asphalt, and it happens to be well placed on both.

## Absorbed

On 12 June 2025, Resolution 202/2025/QH15 merged Hậu Giang and Sóc Trăng provinces into Cần Thơ municipality and abolished Vietnam's entire district tier. Ngã Bảy ceased to be a provincial city; its four wards and two communes were reorganised as wards of Cần Thơ. The canals, needless to say, took no notice. The junction still braids seven channels together, boats still work the water at first light, and the name that residents fought for in 2006 is now attached to a ward rather than a city - which, in a place that has been renamed and reorganised as often as this one, counts as continuity.

## Flight Context

Positioned at 9.8164°N, 105.8197°E in the central Mekong Delta. The navigational landmark is unmistakable and unique in the region: seven straight canals radiating from a single junction, best read from 2,500-4,000 ft where the geometry resolves but individual boats are still visible. National Road 1A runs through the town on a Cần Thơ-Sóc Trăng axis. Cần Thơ International (VVCT/VCA, elev. 10 ft, 3,000 m asphalt runway 06/24) lies about 17 nm north-northwest and is the practical base for any low-level look at the delta. Surrounding terrain is essentially at sea level, so obstacles are limited to towers and the occasional pagoda spire. Dry-season mornings from December to March give the cleanest light on the water; monsoon months from May to October bring low cloud and heavy afternoon convection.
