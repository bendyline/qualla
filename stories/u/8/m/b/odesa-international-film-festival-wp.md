# Odesa International Film Festival

Each July, silent classics flicker across the Potemkin Stairs to a live orchestra, in the city whose most famous staircase was made immortal by a silent film.

**Location:** 46.5000, 30.7000

**Categories:** cinema, festivals, culture, Odesa

## Introduction

One July evening in 2010, thousands of people climbed onto the Potemkin Stairs, sat down on the stone, and watched Sergei Eisenstein's Battleship Potemkin projected across the open air while a live symphony orchestra played beneath the screen. It was a perfect loop of history: the very staircase that Eisenstein had made famous, in the very city, showing the very film. The Odesa International Film Festival had been born, and it had found its signature image in a single night.

## Cinema on the Stairs

That open-air screening became the festival's most beloved tradition. In the years that followed, the Potemkin Stairs hosted silent masterpieces one evening each summer, always with a full orchestra. In 2011, Fritz Lang's Metropolis drew more than two thousand viewers to the steps. In 2012, Charlie Chaplin's City Lights was presented in person by his daughter, the actress Geraldine Chaplin, who stood before the crowd to introduce her father's work. Later years brought Murnau's Sunrise, Hitchcock's Blackmail, and Dziga Vertov's Man with a Movie Camera, each washed across the great descent to the sea. There is something fitting about it. A staircase that owes its worldwide fame to cinema had become, once a year, a cinema itself, the largest and most beautiful screening room the city could offer. The festival added a second open-air venue too, first along a nearby descent and later at the Green Theatre in Shevchenko Park, so that on warm nights the whole city seemed to be watching films beneath the stars.

## The Golden Duke

The first festival ran from 16 to 24 July 2010 and drew more than forty thousand people; the next year that number climbed past seventy thousand. Its top prize is the Golden Duke, a statuette named for the nineteenth-century governor whose monument still stands at the head of the Potemkin Stairs. The opening and closing ceremonies unfold at the Odesa Opera and Ballet Theatre, one of the most admired buildings in Ukraine, where the red carpet is rolled out beneath a lavishly decorated facade. Over the years the festival has honored a remarkable roll of guests: Isabelle Huppert and Agnieszka Holland in 2017, Jacqueline Bisset in 2018, and for its tenth anniversary, the director Mike Leigh and the legendary French actress Catherine Deneuve.

## A Festival in Exile

For a city built around a great port, a summer film festival was more than entertainment; it was a way of facing outward, of gathering the world onto the seafront each July. That gathering has not always been possible in Odesa itself. With the Russo-Ukrainian War underway, the festival could not take place at home. In 2022, the Karlovy Vary International Film Festival in the Czech Republic screened parts of the program, and a companion edition was held in Pristina, Kosovo. In 2023 the festival relocated to Chernivtsi, in Ukraine's far west. The Golden Duke was still awarded, the films were still shown, and the tradition endured far from the stairs that gave it its name, waiting for the evening when the orchestra can return to the seafront.

## Flight Context

The Odesa International Film Festival is centered on the city's seafront at roughly 46.50 N, 30.70 E, anchored by the Potemkin Stairs and the Opera and Ballet Theatre near Prymorskyi Boulevard. From about 2,000 feet, the grand staircase and the harbor below make the festival's heart easy to locate along the coast. The nearest airport is Odesa International (ICAO: UKOO), about 8 km southwest of the center.
