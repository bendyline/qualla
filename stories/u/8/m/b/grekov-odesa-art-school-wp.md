# Grekov Odesa Art School

Ukraine's oldest art school, where a young Wassily Kandinsky once studied before he taught the world to see without objects at all.

**Location:** 46.4878, 30.7333

**Categories:** Art, Education, Odesa, History

## Introduction

Before Wassily Kandinsky painted the first purely abstract canvases and changed what a painting could even be, he was a student in Odesa. This is the school that taught him, the oldest art school in Ukraine, opened in 1865 as a modest drawing school with no building of its own. Its roll of former pupils reads like a syllabus of Eastern European art: Kandinsky, the Futurist David Burliuk, the portraitist Leonid Pasternak, and dozens more who passed through these rooms before scattering their influence across the twentieth century.

## Built on Generosity Alone

For its first twenty years, the school had brilliance but no roof to call its own. Founded on May 30, 1865, under a headmaster named Frederick Malman, who drafted its rules, it lived on donations from the wealthiest names in Odesa and the wider Russian Empire. Its patrons were a who's-who of the era: members of the Vorontsov and Tolstoy families, the city's governor-general and its mayor, foreign consuls, and celebrated architects. A vice president of the society, Franz Morandi, drew on his ties to the Milan Academy of Fine Arts to secure the school's first plaster casts, prints, and models. Only in 1883 was a cornerstone laid outside Preobrazhenska Street, and in 1885 the school moved at last into the premises it still occupies today, a home earned through two decades of patronage.

## The Grand Duke's School

For a quarter century, the school carried royal weight. Grand Duke Vladimir Alexandrovich served as its sponsor for 25 years until his death in 1909, and until 1917 the institution bore his name. Revolution rewrote that identity, as it rewrote so much in Odesa. In 1924 it became the Polytechnic College of Fine Arts, training muralists, printers, and potters; in 1930 it was elevated to an Art Institute, only to be returned to secondary-school status in 1934. Then, in 1965, on its hundredth anniversary, it took the name it holds now, honoring Mitrofan Grekov, a battle painter and one of its own former pupils. Through empire, revolution, and Soviet reorganization, the school kept teaching, absorbing each new name like a fresh coat of paint over an unchanged frame.

## A Living Studio

The school still hums with the work of hands. Today the Grekov Odesa Art School keeps a firmly fine-art focus across four departments: painting, sculpture, decorative arts including ceramics and batik, and design. In 2015 it enrolled 259 students, taught in intimate groups of six to ten through a four-year full-time course, closer to a workshop than a lecture hall. Its history has not been gentle. A 1993 fire destroyed an assembly hall and the library, and later years brought reports of crumbling academic buildings. Yet the impulse that founded it endures: to hand young artists the tools, the casts, the light of a studio, and let them find their own way of seeing. From these walls came a man who painted color as pure feeling. It is worth remembering that even Kandinsky started with a pencil and a sheet of paper here.

## Flight Context

Located at 46.49 degrees N, 30.73 degrees E, in the historic center of Odesa near Preobrazhenska Street. The nearest airport is Odesa International (UKOO), roughly 8 km southwest. The dense nineteenth-century street grid and nearby cathedral square help orient a low pass; the compact campus is best appreciated at street level, though the old town reads clearly from a circling altitude of 2,000 to 3,000 feet in clear weather.
