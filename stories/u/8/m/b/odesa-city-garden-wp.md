# Odesa City Garden

The oldest park in Odesa, planted by the city founder's brother and given away because he could not afford to keep it, now the leafy heart of the old town.

**Location:** 46.4847, 30.7350

**Categories:** Parks, Odesa, History, Culture

## Introduction

One man planted this garden, and then he gave it away. In 1803, Felix de Ribas, brother of the admiral who founded Odesa, laid out a private green space in the middle of the raw new city, the first park it would ever have. He owned much of this quarter, but the upkeep outran his purse, and on November 10, 1806, he handed the whole territory over to the city. More than two centuries later, the Odesa City Garden still occupies the same block off Derybasivska Street, the oldest park in town and one of its most beloved gathering places.

## A Gift the City Kept

The garden's founding is a small parable about ambition and generosity. Felix de Ribas belonged to the family at the very origin of Odesa; his brother Jose is remembered as the city's builder, and the main promenade beside the garden carries the family name. Felix's contribution was quieter but no less lasting. Having created the park in 1803, he found he could not sustain the expense of maintaining it, and rather than let it wither, he deeded the land to the growing city in 1806. It was an act of foresight as much as necessity. The young Odesa, all dust and construction, gained a permanent patch of shade and green at its very center, and it has held onto that gift ever since, tending the garden through every era that followed.

## Stages Among the Trees

A park that survives two hundred years accumulates layers. The Odesa City Garden gathered its share in the middle of the twentieth century, when the pavilion designed by the architect A. A. Gentsler went up in 1943, followed by the summer theatre of the Odesa orchestra, the work of R. A. Vladimirskaya, in 1949. Sculptures and monuments were placed among the paths over the years, giving the greenery a scattering of stone company. The most recent refresh came in 2007, when the pavilion, the facades of the surrounding buildings, and a music fountain were all renovated, tuning the old park for a new century. Music and performance have long been part of its character, the garden serving as an open-air stage set in the middle of the city.

## The City's Living Room

This is a place best measured in seasons rather than facts. In spring the beds come into flower and the holly greens against the paths; in autumn the leaves turn and drift across the alleys; in winter the fountain sleeps under a dusting of snow, and the restaurants along the edge glow warm against the cold. On a fine evening a band might play while an older couple dances, unhurried, among people simply out for a stroll. Set just off Derybasivska Street, ringed by the ornate facades of the old town, the City Garden is where Odesa comes to slow down, to sit under a tree, and to remember that a single well-placed park, given freely more than two centuries ago, can keep on giving to a city for generations.

## Flight Context

Located at 46.485 degrees N, 30.735 degrees E, in the heart of central Odesa just off the Derybasivska Street promenade. The nearest airport is Odesa International (UKOO), about 8 km to the southwest. From a low circling altitude of 2,000 to 3,000 feet in clear weather, look for the compact green square of the garden set within the dense nineteenth-century street grid, a short distance inland from the Black Sea harbor.
