# Statue of Darth Vader

The Odesa monument where Vladimir Lenin was transformed, cape and helmet and all, into the world's first statue of Darth Vader.

**Location:** 46.4699, 30.6730

**Categories:** Art and Monuments, Modern History, Cities, Popular Culture

## Introduction

For decades, a statue of Vladimir Lenin stood outside a factory in Odesa, one of thousands of near-identical figures that watched over the Soviet Union. Then, in 2015, a sculptor climbed up to it with titanium alloy and a plan. He reshaped Lenin's long coat into a flowing cape, lowered a helmet over the familiar bald head, and placed a lightsaber hilt in the outstretched fist. When he finished, the founder of the Soviet state had become Darth Vader, Lord of the Sith. The artist called it the world's first monument to the Star Wars villain, and he was almost certainly right.

## A Law and a Loophole

The transformation was born of necessity. In 2015 Ukraine passed decommunization laws that called for the removal of hundreds of Soviet-era monuments, and Lenin statues came down across the country by the score. Odesa's Lenin, standing on the grounds of the now-defunct Pressmash plant, was slated for the same fate. But rather than simply topple it, the city took an inventive path: it commissioned the artist Alexander Milov to alter the figure so that it complied with the new law while sparing the sculpture from the scrapyard. It was a peculiarly Odesan solution, equal parts pragmatism, irreverence, and creative mischief, turning a mandate for destruction into an act of reinvention.

## From Bronze Age to Space Age

The original Lenin was made of plaster, and years of weather had worn it down until it needed a protective composite shell of an undisclosed formula just to survive. Milov's additions, the cape, the helmet, the lightsaber, were crafted from titanium alloy and fixed in place with a specialized adhesive. Local rumor gleefully embellished the technical details, claiming the glue was the very kind used to build spacecraft and had been supplied by NASA. Whether or not that was true hardly mattered. The story suited the transformation perfectly: an old symbol of one century's ideology, refitted with the materials and mythology of the space age, standing guard over a very different Odesa.

## The Dark Lord Logs On

The new statue quickly gathered its own legend. In a final touch that felt entirely in keeping with the spirit of the project, the monument was outfitted to double as a Wi-Fi hotspot, so that anyone standing in Darth Vader's shadow could pick up a signal. News of the makeover raced around the world in 2015, drawing coverage from the BBC to The Guardian, and turning an obscure factory monument into an international curiosity. What had been an anonymous piece of Soviet statuary became, overnight, a place people sought out on purpose, camera in hand.

## A City That Laughs at History

There is a deeper resonance to the joke. Odesa has long prided itself on its humor, its refusal to take power too seriously, its instinct to answer heavy history with a wink. Transforming a monument to Lenin into a movie villain is exactly the kind of gesture the city has always relished, mocking and affectionate and defiant all at once. The statue does not pretend the past did not happen; Lenin is still there, beneath the cape and the helmet. But Odesa chose to reshape that past rather than erase it, and in doing so made something entirely its own, a monument that is impossible to imagine standing anywhere else on Earth.

## Flight Context

The statue stands in an industrial area on the western side of Odesa at 46.470°N, 30.673°E, on the former Pressmash plant grounds. The site is best appreciated from low altitude in clear conditions, using the surrounding factory blocks and the nearby harbor to the east for orientation. The nearest airport is Odesa International (UKOO), about 2.5 nm to the south.
