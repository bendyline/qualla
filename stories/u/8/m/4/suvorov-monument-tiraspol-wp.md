# Suvorov Monument

The bronze horseman on Tiraspol's central square honors the general who founded the city in 1792, and he has ridden across its banknotes and stamps ever since.

**Location:** 46.8366, 29.6111

**Categories:** Monument, Transnistria, History, Art

## Introduction

The city put its founder on horseback at the center of everything. On the main square of Tiraspol rises a bronze equestrian statue of Alexander Suvorov, the Russian generalissimo credited with establishing the city itself, and from that small hill he has become one of the most recognizable emblems of the whole unrecognized republic. Look closely and you will find him everywhere: cast in bronze on the square, printed across the first banknotes ever issued here, engraved on postage stamps. In a place still searching for symbols of its own, this eighteenth-century commander on his rearing horse has become a fixed point, the figure to whom the town traces its very beginning.

## The General Who Founded a City

Tiraspol regards Alexander Suvorov as its founder, and the claim rests on a specific act. In 1792, on his instructions, the Tiraspol fortress was laid out on the left bank of the Dniester as part of a defensive chain known as the Dniester line, established on land recently taken from the Ottoman Empire. From that fortress the city grew. To commemorate the connection, the monument was erected in 1979, the work of the sculptors Vladimir and Valentin Artamonov together with the architects Y. Druzhinin and Y. Chistyakov. Placed atop a small rise on Suvorov Square, the main square of the capital, it earned the Yevgeny Vuchetich Gold Medal for its artistry. According to Alla Melnichuk, director of the Tiraspol United Museum, it is considered one of the finest artistic monuments to Suvorov anywhere in the territory of the former Soviet Union.

## A Face on the Money

Few statues have been reproduced as faithfully as this one. When the unrecognized Pridnestrovian Moldavian Republic began issuing its own currency, the Suvorov monument rode straight onto the bills. It appeared on the first banknotes of 1993, in denominations of 50, 100, 200, 500, and 1,000 rubles, then on the 5,000-ruble note of 1995, and again on the 500,000-ruble note of 1997, a spread of values that traced the region's turbulent early economy. The image migrated to stamps as well: a 1997 postage stamp bore the monument, and in 2013 a new stamp titled 'Monument to Suvorov in Tiraspol' was issued. To live here is to handle the general's likeness constantly, passing it in change and pressing it onto envelopes, a piece of public sculpture folded into the smallest transactions of daily life.

## Keeping the Horseman Standing

A monument this central demands upkeep, and the Suvorov statue has needed its share. At some point a major overhaul of the pedestal was carried out after cracks were discovered in it, the kind of structural attention that a heavy bronze rider on a raised base inevitably requires over the decades. In 2013 there were plans to restore the monument's outer surface, which had suffered from an earlier cleaning meant to strip away dust and pollution. These are the quiet labors of preservation, the periodic care that keeps a beloved landmark from crumbling into neglect. For a symbol that appears on the currency and stamps of an entire self-declared state, the stakes of that maintenance are more than aesthetic; the horseman on the square is, in a real sense, the face the place shows the world.

## Flight Context

The Suvorov Monument stands on Suvorov Square in central Tiraspol at roughly 46.84 N, 29.61 E, near the left bank of the Dniester in Transnistria. It is best seen at street level, but from low altitude the open central square and the Dniester River are the clearest guides. Transnistria has no airport; the nearest is Chisinau International (LUKK) in Moldova, about 70 km to the west.
