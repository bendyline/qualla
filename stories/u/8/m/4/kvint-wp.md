# KVINT

The 1897 Tiraspol brandy house so beloved that it appears on the local banknote, its cognac has traveled to the Vatican and into orbit.

**Location:** 46.8418, 29.6266

**Categories:** Winery, Transnistria, Industry, History

## Introduction

The bottle is labeled 'Made in Moldova,' but everyone in Tiraspol knows better. KVINT's brandy has been poured for the Vatican and carried into space, and its factory is printed on the 5-ruble banknote that residents pass hand to hand every day, and yet on paper the country it represents does not officially exist. That contradiction is the whole story of KVINT, a distillery that turns grapes grown along the Dniester into a source of local pride far larger than its size, and that has kept its stills running through more upheavals than most nations survive.

## A Century and More of Distilling

The name is an acronym, unpacked from Russian as 'cognacs, wines, and beverages of Tiraspol,' and the enterprise behind it is genuinely old. KVINT was founded in 1897, when this land lay within the Russian Empire, and for its first four decades it produced only vodka. Brandy came in 1938, and it has been the house's signature ever since. Through empire, Soviet rule, war, and the emergence of an unrecognized breakaway state, the distillery never stopped, which makes it the oldest still-operating commercial enterprise in the region. Locals regard it as something close to a national treasure, a symbol of a place that has few other symbols of its own to claim. To put a factory on your money is to say, in effect, this is who we are.

## Grapes on the Left Bank

Most of Transnistria stretches east of the Dniester River, and it is here, on that left bank, that KVINT's vineyards grow. The mainstay is the Cognac grape variety, the raw material for the brandies known locally as divin, but the company also cultivates a range of classic European varieties: Malbec, Ancellotta, Saperavi, Viognier, and Syrah. The annual output is considerable, roughly ten million litres of alcoholic drinks, shipped not only to Russia and Ukraine but as far afield as Italy and China. In 2014, the enterprise brought in about fifty million dollars, a sum that amounted to nearly five percent of the entire GDP of Transnistria. For a distillery, that is an outsized economic footprint, the kind of role usually reserved for a national industry rather than a single brand of brandy.

## Made in a Country That Isn't

The 'Made in Moldova' on every label is not a marketing choice but a matter of survival in international trade. Because Transnistria is not recognized as a sovereign country, its exports must carry the name of a recognized state of origin, and so KVINT's bottles sail out under Moldova's name, whatever their makers might prefer. The company was privatized in 2006 and belongs to Sheriff, the conglomerate whose reach across Transnistrian life, from supermarkets to a famous football club, is hard to overstate. For visitors, the KVINT factory in Tiraspol is an attraction in its own right, a working monument to endurance where you can taste a brandy that has, improbably, been to the Vatican and beyond the sky, all from a country that most of the world's maps decline to draw.

## Flight Context

The KVINT distillery stands in Tiraspol at roughly 46.84 N, 29.63 E, on the left bank of the Dniester River in Transnistria. From low altitude the river and the compact grid of Tiraspol are the primary landmarks, with vineyards spreading across the surrounding left-bank plain. Transnistria has no airport of its own; the nearest is Chisinau International (LUKK) in Moldova, about 70 km to the west.
