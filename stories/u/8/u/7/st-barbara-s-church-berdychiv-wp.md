# St. Barbara's Church, Berdychiv

In this Berdychiv church a famous French novelist married the great love of his life, a future giant of English literature was baptized, and the Soviet state turned the sanctuary into a children's gymnasium.

**Location:** 49.8933, 28.5885

**Categories:** Religious Sites, History, Ukraine, Literary History

## Introduction

In the spring of 1850, the French novelist Honoré de Balzac, ailing and near the end of a long courtship, stood at the altar of a Catholic church in the provincial Ukrainian town of Berdychiv and finally married Countess Ewelina Hańska, the Polish noblewoman he had loved and written to for nearly two decades. The church was St. Barbara's. Balzac would be dead within months, but the wedding stitched this modest parish church permanently into literary history, and it was only one of the surprising moments this building has witnessed. For a place so far from the great capitals, St. Barbara's has an unusual habit of standing where famous lives turn.

## A Countess and Her Patron Saint

The church carries the memory of an even older foundation. Its name preserves that of a local Carmelite monastery established in 1630, and the present building rose on that ground in 1759, raised by Countess Barbara Tyszkiewicz. She was the daughter and heiress of a wealthy Lithuanian magnate whose vast dowry included the town of Berdychiv itself, and she dedicated the new church to her own patron, Saint Barbara. The first structure was of wood. Later, under Tsarist Russian rule, that original was pulled down and replaced by the elegant stone edifice completed in 1826. Today St. Barbara's is recognized as a Ukrainian national architectural monument and remains one of the most instantly recognizable buildings in the city.

## Where Literature Was Baptized

Balzac's wedding is the church's most celebrated moment, but not its only literary one. Just seven years later, in 1857, an infant was carried to the same basilica to be baptized. His name was Józef Teodor Konrad Korzeniowski, and the world would come to know him as Joseph Conrad, one of the greatest novelists ever to write in the English language, the author of Heart of Darkness and Lord Jim. That two writers of such stature should be bound to a single parish church in a middling Ukrainian town says something about Berdychiv in its heyday, a Catholic outpost and busy crossroads where the Polish-Lithuanian and Russian worlds met, and where the currents of European culture ran surprisingly deep.

## Suppression and Survival

The twentieth century was unkind to St. Barbara's, as it was to churches across the Soviet Union. When the Communist state suppressed religious institutions throughout the country, Berdychiv's basilica was no exception. Stripped of its sacred purpose, the building was put to a use that would have astonished its founders: it became a People's Sports College for Children, its nave given over to young athletes rather than worshippers. For decades the church served the state instead of the faith. Then, with the loosening grip of the Soviet system, its fortunes turned again. In 1993, St. Barbara's was reconsecrated by the Catholic Church and returned to the purpose for which the countess had built it, a place of worship once more.

## A Building That Keeps Its Stories

There is something fitting in a church named for Saint Barbara, a figure long invoked for protection against sudden calamity, having weathered so many upheavals and come through. Its walls have held a novelist's late-life happiness and a future master's christening, have echoed with hymns and then with the shouts of children at exercise, and have survived to hear prayer again. For visitors to Berdychiv, St. Barbara's is more than a handsome monument on the skyline. It is a small, stubborn record of how much history one building can absorb, and how, against long odds, it can be handed back to the people who first meant it as a place to gather and to pray.

## Flight Context

St. Barbara's stands at 49.89°N, 28.59°E, in the city of Berdychiv, Zhytomyr Oblast, central Ukraine. From about 3,000 feet, look for the church's tower rising among the town's older streets, not far from the larger fortified Carmelite monastery. The nearest airport is Zhytomyr (UKKV) to the north.
