# Shevchenko National Reserve

A hillside sanctuary above the Dnieper at Kaniv, built around the grave of Ukraine's national poet, that draws pilgrims by the hundred thousand every year.

**Location:** 49.7331, 31.5147

**Categories:** Culture, Ukraine, Memorials, Museums

## Introduction

Some countries keep their poets in libraries; Ukraine keeps one on a hill above a great river, and goes to visit him. The Shevchenko National Reserve, spread across forty-five hectares near the town of Kaniv, exists for a single purpose: to hold and honor the grave of Taras Shevchenko, the poet and artist whose verse gave modern Ukraine much of its voice. More than a hundred thousand people climb here every year, from across the country and beyond, to stand at the resting place of a man who spent his life longing for the free homeland he wrote into being on the page.

## The Poet Comes Home

Shevchenko died in 1861, and the reserve grew from the way his people chose to remember him. He was buried that May on a rise near Kaniv above the Dnieper, on the very river country he had loved and written about, and the site became a place of quiet pilgrimage almost at once. In 1884 the first folk museum dedicated to him was built beside the grave, and a tall cast-iron cross-monument was raised over it. The tomb's standing grew with the nation's. In 1918 the government of the newly declared Ukrainian State formally recognized it as national property, an act that turned a beloved grave into a national shrine.

## A Reserve for Memory

Formal protection followed in the 1920s. In 1925 the hill and its surroundings were recognized as a reserve and granted national status, taking the name it still carries. Decades later, in 1994, a decree of the President of Ukraine reaffirmed its standing among the country's national cultural institutions. Today the reserve encompasses eight cultural heritage sites gathered around the poet's grave, and it borders the wild forested slopes of the neighboring Kaniv Nature Reserve, so that memory and nature share the same stretch of the Dnieper's bank. It is at once a museum, a monument, and a living place of gathering, tended by generations of custodians and directors who have kept the poet's flame.

## Why They Come

To understand the crowds, you have to understand what Shevchenko means. Born a serf, he rose to become the great poetic voice of Ukraine, and his writing has carried the language and spirit of the country through centuries of pressure to erase both. Coming to his grave is, for many Ukrainians, less a tourist stop than an act of belonging, a way of standing with everything he stood for. The reserve preserves his memory in objects and rooms as well as in the landscape, including recreations of the poet's own chamber. In a nation that has often had to fight to remain itself, this hillside above the Dnieper has become one of the places where that identity is renewed, one visitor at a time.

## The Setting Above the River

Half the power of the place is the place itself. The reserve occupies a high bank of the Dnieper near Kaniv, where the land rises above the wide gleam of the river and the wooded slopes fall away toward the water. That Shevchenko rests here, rather than in a city cemetery, is the whole point: he loved this river country and wrote it into the Ukrainian imagination, and the grave repays that love by keeping him in view of the Dnieper forever. Because the reserve borders the Kaniv Nature Reserve, the visitor moves easily from monument to forest, from the works of memory to the living oak and birdsong of the bluffs. It is a landscape that does the poet's grieving and celebrating for him, season after season, and it helps explain why the hundred thousand keep coming.

## Flight Context

The reserve sits at about 49.73 degrees north, 31.51 degrees east, on the high right bank of the Dnieper just outside Kaniv in Cherkasy Oblast, central Ukraine. From altitude, look for the broad sweep of the Dnieper and the wooded bluffs above Kaniv, where the poet's monument crowns the hill. The nearest major airfields are Cherkasy downstream and Bila Tserkva to the west.
