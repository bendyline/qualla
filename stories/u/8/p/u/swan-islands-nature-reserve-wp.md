# Swan Islands Nature Reserve

A cluster of barely-there Crimean islets, nowhere higher than a two-story building, where tens of thousands of birds gather each winter.

**Location:** 45.8750, 33.5417

**Categories:** Nature & Wildlife, Coastal & Islands, Nature Reserves, Black Sea

## Introduction

In the depth of a Crimean winter, when much of the northern hemisphere's birdlife has fled the cold, this scatter of low islets fills with wings. More than twenty-five hundred swans settle on the shallow waters, joined by tens of thousands of ducks and thousands of geese, coots, and gulls, until the sky and the sea seem made of birds. The Swan Islands rise barely two meters above the Black Sea, so flat and so slight that from a distance they scarcely register as land at all. Yet for the creatures that travel the great migratory routes, this almost-invisible place is one of the most important addresses on the map.

## Land That Barely Is

The reserve's geography is an exercise in the barely-there. The islands stretch about eight kilometers along Crimea's northwestern coast, but their combined land area is a mere fifty-two hectares, and their highest points reach only two meters above the sea. What matters here is not the land but the water around it. The protected shallows extend some thirty kilometers along the coast and cover thousands of hectares, with tens of thousands more designated as a surrounding wildlife sanctuary. It is a place defined by shallowness and openness, a warm continental climate of hot summers and cold winters, where the boundary between land and sea is so gentle that a rising tide can seem to erase the islands entirely.

## A Census of Wings

The numbers are staggering. Over the years, biologists have recorded 255 species of birds within the reserve, but it is the winter gatherings that astonish. Counts have tallied somewhere between ten and thirty thousand ducks in a single season, among them mallard, teal, and merganser, alongside up to two thousand geese, more than a thousand gulls, thousands of coots, and the swans that give the islands their name, both mute and whooper varieties. To stand at the edge of these shallows in winter would be to witness one of the great concentrations of waterbird life in the Black Sea region, a natural spectacle unfolding on land that hardly clears the surface of the water.

## Guarded, Not Given

The Swan Islands are protected in the strictest way a landscape can be. Established in 1949 as a sub-unit of the larger Crimean Nature Reserve, they are designated a strict nature reserve, meaning their purpose is the protection of nature and scientific study, nothing more. There are no facilities to build, no crowds to admit. Mass recreation is forbidden, and so are hunting and fishing. It is a place set aside not for people but from them, held in reserve for the swans and the geese and the quarter-thousand species that have learned to trust these low, exposed islets. In a region crowded with human history, the Swan Islands are a rare deliberate emptiness, kept blank so that the birds may keep coming.

## Flight Context

The Swan Islands Nature Reserve lies at about 45.88°N, 33.54°E off Crimea's low northwestern coast. From altitude the islets are hard to distinguish, reading as faint sand-and-shoal streaks within a broad shelf of pale shallow water rather than as solid land. Saky air base (ICAO UKFI) sits to the south-southeast along the coast; Simferopol (UKFF) lies inland to the southeast.
