# Black Sea Biosphere Reserve

Ukraine's largest biosphere reserve, a wetland world of shifting bays and sandbars where more than 300 kinds of birds gather and roughly half of all Black Sea fish species have been recorded.

**Location:** 46.2965, 31.9186

**Categories:** nature, wildlife, wetlands, Ukraine

## Introduction

Come here in migration season and the sky does the talking. Along the low, watery edge of the northern Black Sea coast, where the land frays into bays, islands, and sandbars across the Kherson and Mykolaiv regions, the Black Sea Biosphere Reserve gathers birds by the multitude. More than 300 bird species have been recorded within its bounds, part of a living inventory that tallies roughly 3,500 species in all. This is not scenery in the postcard sense; it is one of the most biologically crowded stretches of coast in Ukraine, a shallow, sun-struck mosaic of wetland where the Gulf of Tendra and Yahorlyk Bay blur the line between sea and shore.

## A Reserve Assembled Piece by Piece

The reserve did not spring up whole; it was gathered over generations. Its roots reach back to 14 July 1927, when it was created as part of the Trans-Maritime Preserve by a decree of the Ukrainian SSR's government. In 1933 it became an independent research institution, and through the following decades it grew by accretion. In 1973 the Danube floodplains, the Kinski islands in Yahorlyk Bay, and the shallow reaches of the Gulf of Tendra were folded in; the neighboring Yahorlyk reserve joined the year after. The Danube portion later split off on its own, and in 1983 the modern Black Sea Biosphere Reserve took shape. In December 1984 it was accepted into the World Network of Biosphere Reserves, and its wetlands were entered on the international Ramsar list, formal recognition of a wildness that had been protected, in one form or another, for over half a century.

## The Census of the Living

The numbers here reward a slow read. Insects dominate the roll, with some 2,200 species catalogued, joined by 168 kinds of arachnids and 65 of mollusks. The vertebrates run to 462 species, and among them the birds are the headline act at 304. The reptiles, nine species strong, make this one of the most reptile-rich reserves in the country. Out in the reserve's marine waters, researchers have found 83 species of fish over the years, close to half of every fish species known in the entire Black Sea. Fifty land mammals and three marine ones round out the tally. Taken together, it is a portrait of abundance layered into a landscape that, from a distance, can look like little more than pale water and paler sand.

## A Laboratory of Shallow Water

This is a working scientific place as much as a wild one. The reserve operates under the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine, which means the birds and bays here are not merely protected but studied, their populations tracked across decades of patient fieldwork. Its wetlands, entered on the international Ramsar list, are exactly the kind of shallow, brackish habitat that migratory birds depend on, and the geography obliges: the Gulf of Tendra and Yahorlyk Bay spread out in a maze of low islands, sandbars, and sun-warmed shallows where waterbirds can feed and rest. Even the reptiles thrive here, with nine species making this one of the most reptile-rich reserves in Ukraine. It is an easy landscape to underestimate from a distance, all pale water and paler sand, yet it functions as a finely balanced nursery for life along the whole northern Black Sea coast.

## Fire Along the Water's Edge

Even a place this quiet was not spared the wider upheaval. In March 2022, during the Russian invasion of Ukraine, fighting in the area set off several fires across the reserve, scarring habitat that had taken a century to protect. For a sanctuary whose entire purpose is to shelter the fragile and the migratory, the damage was a hard reckoning, a reminder that wilderness sits within the reach of human conflict. Yet the reserve endures on the map and in its mission, still counting its birds and its fish, still guarding the shallow bays where so much life converges. The great flocks that cross this coast do not read borders; they answer only to the season, and each year they return to the water that has always drawn them.

## Flight Context

Anchored near 46.2965 N, 31.9186 E along the northern Black Sea coast, spanning the Kherson and Mykolaiv regions. From 3,000 to 5,000 feet the braided pattern of bays, shallow lagoons, and the sandbars of the Gulf of Tendra and Yahorlyk Bay stands out against open water. Kherson International Airport (UKOH) lies to the northeast.
