# St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery

The blue-and-gold monastery blazing above the Dnieper is not quite as old as it looks: Soviet authorities dynamited the original in the 1930s, and Kyiv rebuilt it from the ground up.

**Location:** 50.4556, 30.5228

**Categories:** monastery, history, architecture, Kyiv

## Introduction

The golden domes you see today are barely older than living memory. Stand in the square before St. Michael's, watch the sunlight ignite the row of gilded cupolas against sky-blue walls, and you are looking at a resurrection. The medieval monastery that once stood on this spot, perhaps the very first church in the Rus' world to have its dome sheathed in gold, was blown up by Soviet authorities in the 1930s and reduced to a field of sports pitches. That it stands again, gleaming above the Dnieper northeast of St. Sophia, is a story of destruction and stubborn return that runs the full length of Kyiv's history.

## The First Golden Dome

The monastery traces its origins to the turn of the 12th century, when the Kievan Rus' prince Sviatopolk II founded a stone cathedral here, likely completed between 1108 and 1113, the year Sviatopolk himself was buried inside. He dedicated it to Saint Michael the Archangel, patron of victories in war, and the church may have been raised to mark a triumph over the Polovtsian nomads of the steppe. Its dome was probably the first in all of Kievan Rus' to be covered in gold, and it is from this dazzling crown that the monastery took its name. In the centuries that followed it became a burial place for princes and, by tradition, a home for the relics of Saint Barbara, drawing pilgrims to its glowing sanctuary.

## Raids, Rebuilding, and a Baroque Face

Standing at the heart of a much-fought-over city, St. Michael's absorbed blow after blow. It was looted during the Mongol invasion of 1240, when Kyiv fell, and afterward fell into long disuse. It was damaged again in 1482, during a raid on the city by the Crimean Khan Meñli I Giray, and abandoned once more, only to re-emerge by the end of the 15th century, about the time the epithet Golden-Domed came into common use. Through the religious turmoil of the 17th century, when much of Kyiv's Orthodox clergy went over to the Greek Catholic Uniate Church, St. Michael's held firmly to its Orthodox faith even after a Polish king tried to hand it to the Uniates. In the 18th century the complex was transformed, its exterior reworked in the flowing Ukrainian Baroque style while the interior kept its ancient Byzantine bones. A new refectory church rose between 1713 and 1715, built partly from the salvaged bricks of an older Kyiv church that fire had destroyed.

## Destroyed, and Raised Again

The 20th century nearly ended the story. After the First World War, Bolshevik shelling tore a hole in the cathedral and destroyed one of the arches beneath its central dome. In 1922 the Soviet authorities closed the monastery; its refectory became a students' dormitory, and other buildings housed institutions as prosaic as a driver-training school. Then, in the 1930s, came demolition. The great cathedral, one of the oldest in the city, was dynamited, its site cleared and paved over. Some of its precious medieval mosaics and frescoes were pried from the walls and scattered to distant museums. Yet Kyiv did not forget. After Ukraine's independence in 1991, the monastery was rebuilt on its old foundations, and the reconstructed cathedral reopened in 1999. Since December 2018, it has served as the headquarters of the Orthodox Church of Ukraine, its golden domes once again presiding over the city.

## A Faithful Copy

Because the monastery you see is a modern reconstruction, it carries a peculiar poignancy. The builders worked to reproduce the lost baroque cathedral faithfully, from the sky-blue walls to the crown of gilded cupolas, so that the skyline of Kyiv's Upper Town could be made whole again. Fragments of the original survive as reminders of what was destroyed, and over the years some looted pieces have made their way back toward home. The rebuilt bell tower once more rings out over Mykhailivska Square, and the monastery has resumed its old role at the heart of Kyiv's religious life. There is something defiant in the whole enterprise. A regime tore this place down to erase it; a free country rebuilt it precisely so that it could not be erased. The golden domes, new but faithful, are an answer written in gold leaf against the sky.

## Flight Context

St. Michael's stands at 50.4556 N, 30.5228 E on Kyiv's Upper Town bluff, just northeast of St. Sophia and overlooking the Podil quarter and the Dnieper. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet the row of gold domes on sky-blue walls is unmistakable, paired with St. Sophia across Sofiivska and Mykhailivska squares. Nearest airports are Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) to the southwest and Boryspil International (UKBB) to the east.
