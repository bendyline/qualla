# Monument to Prince Volodymyr

Kyiv's oldest monument raises a bronze cross above the Dnipro, honoring the prince who baptized the Rus more than a thousand years ago.

**Location:** 50.4564, 30.5263

**Categories:** monument, history, ukraine, kyiv

## Introduction

High on the bluff above the Dnipro, a bronze prince holds a cross toward the sky. He has stood there since 1853, and he is the oldest sculptural monument in Kyiv, one of the images by which the whole city is recognized. This is Volodymyr the Great, the ruler who around the year 988 converted his pagan people to Christianity, cast in metal at the very moment of that world-changing choice. From the water, from the bridges, from the far left bank, the silhouette on Volodymyrska Hill has anchored the skyline for well over a century and a half. Few cities keep the memory of a single decision so visibly, and so high. For generations of Kyivans, this bronze figure with his lifted cross has simply been part of the sky over the river.

## A Prince Cast in Bronze

The monument was built to be seen from far off, and it is composed with care. The bronze figure of the Baptizer of the Rus stands 4.4 meters tall, wrapped in a coat, a great cross raised in his right hand and the grand prince's cap held in his left. He rises atop a 16-meter pedestal shaped like an octagonal chapel in a pseudo-Byzantine style, set on a square base, its brick core sheathed in cast-iron plates, so that the whole reaches 20.4 meters into the air. The design passed through two hands: it was begun by the sculptor Vasily Demut-Malinovsky and completed by Peter Clodt von Jürgensburg, the celebrated horse sculptor, in 1853. Reliefs wrap the base like pages of a story. One high-relief panel depicts the Christianization of Kievan Rus, freezing in metal the very event the monument commemorates; other panels carry the emblem of the Order of Saint Volodymyr and an image of the Archangel Michael, the city's ancient protector. The whole composition reads at a distance as a chapel that has grown a figure at its summit.

## The Hill That Bears His Name

Everything about the setting reinforces the story. The statue crowns Volodymyrska Hill, a steep green terrace on the right bank, its landscaped parkland dropping away in switchbacks toward the river below. The whole quarter carries the prince's name, threaded through the streets and stairways of old Kyiv, because it was Volodymyr who first bound this city to the faith and the wider world of Byzantium. The same hill is served by the century-old funicular that climbs from the riverside district of Podil, delivering visitors almost to the monument's feet. Nearby, other memorials extend the same thread, including a lower monument to the city's Magdeburg rights, sometimes called the Saint Volodymyr Lower Monument. To climb the hill and stand beneath the cross is to look out over the same great river down which, a thousand years ago, the trade and the ideas of a medieval capital once flowed.

## A Symbol Worth Guarding

Over time the figure became a shorthand for Kyiv itself. When independent Ukraine issued its early karbovanets banknotes between 1993 and 1995, the Monument to Prince Volodymyr appeared on them, and it has since featured on commemorative stamps and on a jubilee fifty-hryvnia coin. Even the Soviet Union once struck the monument onto a commemorative twenty-five-ruble coin, a rare acknowledgment of a Christian prince from an officially atheist state. The statue has been cared for as a treasure, restored in 1953 a century after its unveiling, its long survival marked on a memorial placard at its base. During the 2022 invasion, it was carefully wrapped in protective covering, the raised cross swaddled against the possibility of harm, the way a city shields something it cannot bear to lose. The prince who once remade Kyiv still watches over it, and the city, in its turn, watches over him.

## Flight Context

The monument crowns Volodymyrska Hill on Kyiv's right bank at 50.4564 N, 30.5263 E, overlooking the Dnipro. From 2,000 to 4,000 feet the tall cross-bearing statue, the wooded terraces of the hill, and the river's broad channel are clear landmarks, with the gold domes of Saint Michael's and Saint Sophia just inland. Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) is the nearest airport; Boryspil International (UKBB) lies east across the river.
