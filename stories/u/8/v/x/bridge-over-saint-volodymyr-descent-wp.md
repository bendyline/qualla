# Bridge over Saint Volodymyr Descent

Kyiv's glass footbridge was opened by two heavyweight champions jumping on its transparent floor, and it has since become a symbol of a city that refuses to fall.

**Location:** 50.4552, 30.5281

**Categories:** architecture, landmark, ukraine, kyiv

## Introduction

To open the bridge, the mayor of Kyiv and his brother jumped up and down on the glass. Vitali and Volodymyr Klitschko, two of the most feared heavyweight boxers of their generation, bounced together on a transparent panel suspended over a busy street to prove the thing would hold. The crowd loved it. Kyivans took to calling it the Klitschko Glass Bridge, though its official name is more sober: the pedestrian and cycling bridge over Saint Volodymyr Descent. Slung 210 meters between two hilltop parks, it lets you walk on air above the traffic, the wooded slope of the right bank falling away toward the Dnipro on one side and the golden city rising on the other. It is at once a piece of ordinary civic infrastructure and, as events would prove, something the city came to love far more fiercely than anyone expected.

## A Bridge Ninety Years in the Making

The idea is older than it looks. The Ukrainian architect Vasyl Krychevsky first dreamed of a span linking these two parks back in 1934, when Kyiv had just regained its status as capital and the city was imagining a grand government center. His viaduct won praise but never rose. The notion resurfaced in 2006, then again in 2013, drifting through architecture contests that drew entries from the Netherlands, Britain, Switzerland, Spain, and beyond, only to stall each time in disagreement. Not until 2019 did steel finally meet air. Andrii Myrhorodskyi's winning design threaded straight spans of high-tensile steel across the descent, and crews from Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Lviv, and half a dozen other cities worked around the clock to finish it in five months. The mayor said the effort had united Ukrainians. He was, it turned out, foreshadowing something.

## Glass That Learns to Break

A transparent bridge is an invitation to test it, and Kyivans obliged from the very first night. Panels cracked almost immediately, most of them the outer protective layer, damaged by people jumping to see what would happen. Over the years the glass would crack again and again, sometimes from vandals, sometimes from the ordinary violence of temperature swings and the bridge's own gentle sway. One man from Lutsk cracked an information sign to test its strength and drew a five-year sentence for hooliganism. Engineers were called in more than once, and each time the verdict was the same: the structure itself was sound, meeting every standard for a pedestrian bridge. The glass was designed in layers, so that the surface could fracture without the span ever giving way. It was, in retrospect, an oddly fitting design for what the city was about to endure.

## To Withstand Is Not to Fall

On 10 October 2022, during a wave of missile strikes on Kyiv, a Russian cruise missile fell beside one of the bridge's piers. The span survived with only minor damage. Within days repairs were underway, and by December it reopened, its eighteen floor panels and one hundred forty-three side panels replaced. In the meantime, the bridge had become something more than a place to cross. Klitschko launched a merchandise line bearing its image and the words "To withstand is not to fall." Fragments of its shattered glass were handed out at a congress in Barcelona and sold at charity auction to fund a Kyiv rifle battalion, each shard inscribed with a promise that the bridge, the city, and the country would all endure. The following spring, after the largest drone attack the war had yet seen, Kyivans woke to find a single cracked railing on the bridge, and could not say for certain whether the blast had done it. A structure built to crack on the surface and hold underneath had, without quite meaning to, become a portrait of the people walking across it.

## Flight Context

The glass footbridge crosses Saint Volodymyr Descent at 50.4552 N, 30.5281 E, on the wooded right bank just above the Dnipro. From 2,000 to 4,000 feet you can pick out the green ridge of Volodymyr Hill and the river's broad sweep, with the gold domes of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra downstream. Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) is the closest airport; Boryspil International (UKBB) lies farther east.
