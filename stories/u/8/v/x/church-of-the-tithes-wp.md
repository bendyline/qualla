# Church of the Tithes

Kyiv's first stone church, built by the prince who converted the Rus, has been raised, ruined by Mongols, rebuilt, and demolished again, its foundations still contested a thousand years on.

**Location:** 50.4578, 30.5175

**Categories:** history, religion, ukraine, kyiv

## Introduction

The church took its name from a promise about money. When Grand Prince Vladimir, called Volodymyr the Great, finished Kyiv's first stone church around the year 996, he pledged one-tenth of his revenues to its upkeep, and so it became known forever as the Church of the Tithes. This was no small building and no small gesture. Volodymyr had recently converted the pagan Rus to Christianity, and here, on the high right bank above the Dnipro, he raised the mother church of a new faith for a new realm. He and his wife Anna, sister of the Byzantine emperor, were buried within its walls, so that the church held not only the faith of the realm but the bones of the rulers who had brought it.

## Where a Kingdom Learned to Pray in Stone

Everything about the church announced a shift. Kyiv had been a place of wooden halls and old gods, and now Byzantine and local craftsmen worked together to erect something permanent, its foundations laid where two Christian martyrs were said to have died. Built between 989 and 996, it became the spiritual anchor of Kyivan Rus and a mausoleum for its rulers. Fire damaged it badly in 1017, and Yaroslav the Wise rebuilt and rededicated it in 1039, then in 1044 gathered the bones of earlier princes, Yaropolk and Oleg among them, to be baptized and interred beneath its floor. To be buried here was to be written into the founding story of a people. The saints Olga and Volodymyr, the Byzantine emperor's sister Anna, even relics attributed to Pope Clement, all rested in this ground at the heart of the young capital. For two and a half centuries it stood as the mother church of a Christian realm, the place where a new faith had first put down roots in stone.

## The Weight of a Roof

In December 1240 the Mongol armies of Batu Khan reached Kyiv and laid siege to it. As the city fell, terrified residents crowded into the Church of the Tithes, seeking refuge in the strongest building they knew. Chroniclers record that the sheer weight of the people who climbed onto its vaults for a last stand brought the ancient structure crashing down, burying those inside. The first stone church of the Rus ended as a tomb for its own citizens, its collapse a grim mirror of the city's own fall. For centuries afterward only rubble marked the spot, until, in the early nineteenth century, a bishop had the site excavated and a new church raised over the ruins between 1828 and 1842, designed in the Russian Revival style that did not much resemble the vanished original. It was an homage to a building no one living had ever seen.

## Foundations Still Fought Over

That second church did not last either. In 1935 Soviet authorities demolished it, part of a broad campaign against religion, leaving the old foundations exposed once more beside the National Museum of History of Ukraine. Ever since, the empty footprint has drawn arguments rather than answers. Some want the medieval church rebuilt for its enormous place in Slavic history; others point out that no reliable drawing of the original survives, so any reconstruction would be invention rather than restoration. In 2005 a Ukrainian president even signed a decree to rebuild it, with tens of millions of hryvnias promised, yet the plan stalled amid the dispute. A wooden chapel put up on the ruins in the 2000s became its own legal battleground, ordered dismantled by a Kyiv court and finally taken down in 2024. In a fitting coda, the company that removed it handed its fee to the Ukrainian armed forces. A thousand years on, the ground where Kyiv first prayed in stone still refuses to settle.

## Flight Context

The site sits on the historic Old Kyiv Hill at 50.4578 N, 30.5175 E, beside the National Museum of History. From 2,500 to 4,500 feet the wooded right bank and the Dnipro's wide channel frame the view, with the golden domes of St. Sophia and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra as nearby landmarks. Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) is the closest airport; Boryspil International (UKBB) lies to the east.
