# Battle of Kiev (1918)

In February 1918, as diplomats bargained over Ukraine's future at Brest-Litovsk, Red Guards stormed Kyiv and held it for twenty violent days before losing it just as fast.

**Location:** 50.4500, 30.5233

**Categories:** Military history, Kyiv, Ukrainian history, Russian Civil War

## Introduction

The city changed hands while men in a distant town argued over its fate. In the first days of February 1918, as delegates negotiated the future of Eastern Europe at Brest-Litovsk, Bolshevik Red Guards from Petrograd and Moscow closed in on Kyiv, the newly declared capital of an independent Ukraine. The young republic was barely months old. Its defenders were few, some of them students and cadets, and the guns of the advancing columns grew louder each day. On the ninth of February the city fell. What followed was twenty days that Kyivans would not forget, and that would end almost as abruptly as they began.

## A Capital in the Crosshairs

The operation had a clear aim: to install Soviet power across Ukraine, one city at a time. Through the winter of 1917 and 1918, Red forces had already taken Kharkiv, Poltava, and the city then called Yekaterinoslav, now Dnipro. Kyiv was next on the list. The advance was commanded by Mikhail Muravyov, and it moved on the capital from the direction of Bakhmach and Lubny. Inside the city the government declared a state of siege and named Mykhailo Kovenko to organize its defense, but the situation was grim. The garrison had been worn down by months of upheaval and swayed by Bolshevik agitation, and many Ukrainian units were depleted or declared themselves neutral, unwilling to fight. A capital was about to be defended by a fraction of the men it needed.

## The Arsenal and the Defenders

As the Red columns neared, Bolshevik workers inside the city rose up at the Arsenal factory, a rebellion that took seven days of hard fighting to put down and that pulled scarce Ukrainian troops away from the front just when they were needed most. Those who did take up the city's defense were a patchwork of the willing: Symon Petliura's Haidamaka Host, the disciplined Sich Riflemen under Yevhen Konovalets, Free Cossack formations, and a battalion of military cadets, teenagers and young men barely trained for war. Against the numbers arrayed against them, courage could only buy time. On the eighth of February the Ukrainian government was forced to abandon Kyiv and withdraw westward toward Zhytomyr, carrying the young republic with it into exile.

## Twenty Days

On the ninth of February, Muravyov took the city, and the occupation turned brutal. For twenty days his forces carried out reprisals against the population, a wave of killings that fell hardest on those suspected of loyalty to Ukraine or to the old order, and that swept up ordinary people caught in the machinery of terror. Historians count these among the darker episodes of the Russian Civil War, a reminder that behind the maps and troop columns were families in a frightened city, hearing knocks at the door in the night. The people who suffered were not statistics in an order of battle. They were residents of a capital that had, for one brief and hopeful winter, believed it was building something of its own.

## As Quickly Lost

The Bolshevik hold proved astonishingly brief. On the very day Kyiv fell, Ukraine's Central Rada signed a separate peace with the Central Powers at Brest-Litovsk, inviting German and Austro-Hungarian forces to help drive the Red Guards out. When word reached the occupiers, panic spread through their ranks, and they began fleeing eastward in disarray, abandoning the capital without even token resistance. Ukrainian forces under Petliura, alongside German and Austro-Hungarian troops, re-entered Kyiv on the first of March, and two days later the Bolshevik government formally recognized Ukraine's independence. The Soviets had ruled the city for just twenty days. The battle would not be the last to sweep over Kyiv in those turbulent years, but it left a lasting memory of how swiftly a capital could be taken, and how much it could cost while it was held.
