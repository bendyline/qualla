# Battle of Kiev (1943)

Two years and two months after the city fell, Soviet and Czechoslovak troops fought their way back into Kyiv in a single night, timing its liberation to the anniversary of revolution.

**Location:** 50.4500, 30.5233

**Categories:** history, world war ii, ukraine, military

## Introduction

At 6:50 on the morning of 6 November 1943, Kyiv was free again. The city had endured two years of German occupation since the catastrophe of 1941, and now the tanks that rolled through its western suburbs in the dark belonged to the other side. First into the city center were not Soviet troops at all but the 1st Czechoslovak Independent Brigade, exiles fighting to reclaim someone else's capital on their way toward reclaiming their own. The timing was no accident. Stalin had wanted Kyiv taken by 7 November, the anniversary of the Bolshevik Revolution, and the Red Army delivered with a day to spare. Symbolism and strategy had converged on a single dark morning, and the capital that had fallen so catastrophically two years before was, at last, back in Soviet hands.

## Crossing the Great River

The second battle for Kyiv was one act in a far larger drama: the Battle of the Dnieper, where the Red Army threw itself at the wide, cold river the Germans had fortified as their wall in the east. General Nikolai Vatutin's First Ukrainian Front had established bridgeheads on the far bank and now drove for the city itself. The German Fourth Panzer Army under Hermann Hoth reeled under the assault, its divisions taking heavy losses in the opening stages. Reinforcements arrived, including the freshly raised 25th Panzer Division, but it had received only emergency individual training and lacked entire categories of equipment. Committed over the protests of Heinz Guderian, the inspector of panzer troops, it became the first German panzer division on the Eastern Front to fail even its opening attack. In ten days the Soviets pushed 150 kilometers west, took Zhitomir, and tore a hundred-kilometer gap between the German army groups, a wound in the front that the Germans would spend the rest of the year trying to close.

## The Ground Fights Back

Then the war slowed to the rhythm of the land. The Soviet advance stalled outside Zhitomir when, in one of the war's stranger interludes, the troopers of a Guards cavalry corps stumbled onto the German army's alcohol stocks and stopped to loot them. Momentum lost is hard to regain. The Germans seized the moment: Hermann Balck's panzer corps drove north and west and retook Zhitomir, touching off a huge tank battle that ground on until the autumn mud swallowed both armies whole. When December's frost finally hardened the roads, the Germans lunged again, trying to encircle Soviet armies north of the city. Vatutin begged Stavka for reserves and got them. The Red Army absorbed the blow, turned, and by late December both sides had fought themselves to exhaustion. The battle for Kyiv was over, and the city stayed in Soviet hands.

## A Capital Reclaimed

The Soviets had not achieved everything they wanted. They failed to sever the German rail link to Army Group Center or to trap Army Group South, and their exhausted armies could push no further for a time. But they had done the thing that mattered most to a nation that had lost so much: they had broken the Dnieper line and taken back Kyiv, the third-largest city in the Soviet Union. For the people who had lived through two years of occupation, the arithmetic of tank losses meant little. What mattered was that the ravine at Babyn Yar, where so many had been murdered, and the ruined heart of the city now lay behind their own lines. Within weeks the offensive rolled onward, and by 3 January 1944 the front had been pushed back to the old Polish border of 1939. The long road home had turned decisively westward, and it would not turn back.

## Flight Context

Kyiv lies at 50.45 N, 30.5233 E. The 1943 fighting swept the right bank and the country westward toward Zhitomir and Fastiv. From 6,000 to 9,000 feet the Dnieper's broad channel and the wooded bluffs of the right bank read clearly, with the golden domes of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra a natural landmark. Boryspil International (UKBB) lies east of the city; Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) sits closer to the center.
