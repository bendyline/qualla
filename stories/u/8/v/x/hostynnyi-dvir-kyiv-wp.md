# Hostynnyi Dvir

For two centuries this neoclassical trade hall has anchored Kyiv's old merchant quarter, its name meaning both a house of goods and a house of welcome, and its fate fought over to this day.

**Location:** 50.4639, 30.5181

**Categories:** architecture, history, ukraine, kyiv

## Introduction

The building has two names in two languages, and the difference tells you something about the place. In Russian, Hostynnyi Dvir means a court of goods, a marketplace, pure commerce. In Ukrainian, the same words shade toward a court of welcome, a place of hospitality. Both fit. Standing on Kontraktova Ploshcha, the old Contracts Square in Kyiv's riverside Podil district, this long neoclassical hall has been a house of trade and a gathering place for over two hundred years, drawing merchants, buyers, and, more recently, the citizens who fought to save it. Its columns have watched the square through empire, war, and revolution, and its survival has never once been guaranteed.

## Built Against the Flames

Fire haunted this building from the start. Podil was a district of wooden houses, and it burned often. When the old merchant house grew too small for Kyiv's swelling trade, and then a blaze destroyed the market entirely in November 1808, the merchants petitioned to build in stone. They hired the Swiss-Italian neoclassical architect Luigi Rusca, whose two-storey design was approved in 1809. Almost nothing went smoothly. A mistake in the foundation cracked the walls before they were even finished. Then, in July 1811, another fire tore through Podil for three days, and construction stopped with only one floor built. Rather than wait, they roofed the single storey and opened for business in 1813, unfinished but working. It would be reconstructed and enlarged over the following decades, gaining the second floor Rusca had always intended. The design that finally completed it, by the architect Andrey Melensky, followed the grand empire style of the age, and later still the ground floor was lowered to make yet more room for trade. The building, in other words, was never quite finished; it simply kept adapting to the commerce that swirled around it.

## The Hall That Would Not Fall

The twentieth century was unkind to it. Soviet-era renovations stripped away much of the original facade. During World War II the hall was abandoned, and a 1947 city master plan marked it outright for demolition, yet somehow it survived and kept trading. When the metro was tunneled beneath Podil in the 1970s, the ground shifted and the old walls sank again. Restorers hesitated to touch a structure in such fragile condition, until architects Valentyna Shevchenko and Yurii Losytskyi found Rusca's original drawings in the archive and rebuilt from the source. In 1979 the merchant court was granted the protection of an architectural landmark. Even a music video by a People's Artist of Ukraine was filmed inside its halls in the 1990s, a sign of how firmly the building had lodged in the city's affections. Through fire, war, sinking earth, and neglect, the hall on Contracts Square simply refused to disappear.

## A Battle for the Court

In the 2010s the fight over Hostynnyi Dvir turned into a public struggle. A developer leased the building, promising care but planning a shopping mall with a glass roof and a third floor, a scheme that stripped the hall of its heritage protection in 2011. The architect who had led the earlier, faithful restoration publicly criticized the new plans. Activists from the Save the Old Kyiv and Right on City movements occupied the courtyard in protest, calling their vigil an alternative celebration of Kyiv Day. Then, on a February night in 2013, the roof caught fire and burned for six hours, gutting the building. Many believed the developer was behind the blaze. Riot police brutally cleared the protesters, arresting some, and demolition crews moved into the courtyard itself. But later that year Ukraine's Revolution of Dignity erupted, the work halted, and the courts ultimately canceled the reconstruction and declared the developer's ownership illegal. The old welcome court had once more outlasted those who would remake it.

## Flight Context

Hostynnyi Dvir stands on Kontraktova Ploshcha in the Podil district at 50.4639 N, 30.5181 E, close to the Dnipro on Kyiv's lower right bank. From 2,000 to 4,000 feet the river, the domed churches of Podil, and the funicular climbing the hill above make good landmarks. Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) is the nearest airport; Boryspil International (UKBB) lies to the east.
