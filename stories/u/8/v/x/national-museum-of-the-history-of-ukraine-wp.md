# National Museum of the History of Ukraine

On the hill where Kyiv itself was born, a museum of 800,000 objects tells a country's story, including the long saga of how those objects were carried off in wartime and fought back home.

**Location:** 50.4583, 30.5161

**Categories:** museum, history, archaeology, Kyiv

## Introduction

The museum stands on Starokyivska Hill, the ancient rise where Kyiv itself is said to have begun, so the building looks out over the very ground its exhibits describe. Inside are roughly 800,000 objects and, on any given day, some 22,000 on display: a mammoth-tusk bracelet carved in the Stone Age, Scythian swords worked in gold, coins stamped with the trident of Prince Volodymyr, Cossack banners, a metropolitan bishop's gilded carriage. The oldest tools here are around a million years old. Yet the most dramatic thread running through this collection is not how the objects were made. It is how many times they were carried away, and how they kept finding their way home.

## Born of a City's Curiosity

The museum opened its first exhibition in 1899, in a building still under construction, funded not by a state but by the people of Kyiv themselves. The archaeologist Vikentiy Khvoyka led its founding department, and Mykola Biliashivsky, its first director from 1902, shaped the collections that followed. In 1904 it opened formally under the sprawling imperial title of the Emperor Nicholas II Kyiv Art, Industry and Science Museum, kept afloat by wealthy patrons like the Tereshchenko and Khanenko families, who financed digs and donated coins and artifacts year after year. From the start this was a museum that belonged to its city. That sense of shared ownership would matter enormously in the century of upheaval to come.

## A Century of Renaming

Few institutions have been relabeled so often. After the Bolsheviks nationalized it in 1919 it became the First State Museum; in 1924, the Taras Shevchenko Ukrainian Historical Museum; later the Central Historical Museum, run out of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, the ancient monastery that Soviet authorities turned into a hub of antireligious propaganda. In 1934 the collection was evicted from its original home; its art wing split off to become what is now the National Art Museum of Ukraine. The present building on Volodymyrska Street, a handsome 1930s work by architect Joseph Karakis, was designed as an art school and only became the museum's home in 1944. Each new name marked a shift in who controlled the past and how it was meant to be told.

## What the War Took

The Second World War nearly emptied the place. As Nazi Germany invaded, much of the collection was evacuated east to Ufa, in Bashkortostan, while some remained behind in occupied Kyiv. There the Reichsleiter Rosenberg Taskforce took charge, and its staff packed the holdings at the Lavra to ship them to Germany. Some of the most valuable artifacts were hauled west in 1941 and 1943 and ended up trapped in a German castle; after the war they passed through American hands at the Munich Central Collecting Point before finally returning to Kyiv in 1947. Many unique pieces were lost for good. In the years that followed, the museum rebuilt itself the hard way, sending expeditions to former front lines and excavation sites to fill the empty cases with new discoveries.

## The Golden Treasury

The museum's most breathtaking holdings live in a branch inside the Lavra, the Museum of Historical Treasures of Ukraine, known simply as the golden treasury. More than 56,000 objects in gold, silver, and gemstones span from the Bronze Age to modern times, and its crown jewel is the Golden Pectoral from Tovsta Mohyla, a fourth-century-BC Scythian breastplate whose tiny cast figures of herders, cattle, and lions are among the finest metalwork of the ancient world. The struggle over such treasures continues. In 2023, after years in a Dutch courtroom, the museum took custody of 565 Scythian and Sarmatian artifacts that had been on loan in Amsterdam when Crimea was annexed in 2014; the museum says it will hold them until Crimea's return. A year later, hundreds more smuggled objects, intercepted at Estonia's border, came home to these halls.

## Flight Context

The museum sits at 50.4583 N, 30.5161 E on Starokyivska Hill in Kyiv's historic upper town, a short walk from the golden domes of St. Sophia and St. Michael's. From 2,500 to 3,500 feet the compact hilltop cluster of cathedrals and museum buildings reads clearly against the Dnieper valley to the east. Nearest airports are Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) to the southwest and Boryspil International (UKBB) to the east.
