# Hryshko National Botanical Garden

Every spring the lilacs bloom and Kyiv comes to breathe them in, at a garden where two ancient monasteries stand among thirteen thousand kinds of plant.

**Location:** 50.4127, 30.5622

**Categories:** Nature, Gardens, Science, Kyiv

## Introduction

For a few weeks at the end of every spring, the lilacs of the Hryshko garden do something to Kyiv. They bloom in great banked drifts of purple and white, and the city arrives to walk among them, cameras up, the scent hanging thick in the warm air. It is the most beloved moment in the calendar of a garden that holds thirteen thousand kinds of plant. Founded in 1936 and spread across 120 hectares of the Dnieper's high bank, the M. M. Hryshko National Botanical Garden is not just a collection but a landscape, and folded into its slopes are two working monasteries older than almost anything growing there.

## The World in Miniature

The garden is arranged as a kind of map of a continent. Its grounds are divided into floristic zones that recreate whole regions in miniature, the Ukrainian Carpathians, the plains of Ukraine, Crimea, the Caucasus, Central Asia, the Altai and Western Siberia, the Far East, with the terrain of each shaped to suit the plants that belong there. Walk a few hundred meters and the geography shifts beneath you. Beyond the outdoor zones, the greenhouses shelter a trove of tropical and subtropical rarities, including more than 350 species of orchid. Peonies, roses, magnolias, and stands of conifer and honey locust fill the open ground. It is, above all, a scientist's garden, run by the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine and named for the botanist Mykola Hryshko, born in Poltava, whose life's work was the study and cultivation of exactly this kind of living diversity.

## Monasteries Among the Roses

Two monasteries occupy the garden's grounds, and their presence gives the place a depth no plant collection could supply on its own. The Vydubychi Monastery and the Trinity Monastery of St. Jonas stand among the beds and slopes, their domes rising over the greenery. Vydubychi in particular is bound to the oldest layers of Kyiv's history, an ancient foundation on the riverbank below the Berestov heights. To descend the garden's main stairs toward the river with monastery domes catching the light is to feel the two halves of the place at once: the patient, scientific work of naming and growing the world's flora, and the far older human impulse to build something sacred on a beautiful hill above a great river.

## A Garden Between Nations

The garden keeps making room for the wider world in small, deliberate ways. In 2012, with help from the Korean government, a traditional Korean garden modeled on the historic Changdeok grounds was planted here, created to mark twenty years of diplomatic relations between Ukraine and the Republic of Korea. It is a fitting gesture for a place already built to gather distant landscapes into one hillside. Among the plantings stands an older transplant of a different kind, a kurgan stele, one of the weathered stone figures that ancient peoples raised over burial mounds across the steppe. Living collections and long-dead monuments, Korean pavilions and Carpathian slopes, share the same ground, all of it drawing the same crowds each spring to the lilacs that made the garden famous.

## A Garden of Seasons

The garden changes character with the calendar, and Kyiv follows it through the year. Spring belongs to the lilacs and the flowering magnolias, when the crowds are thickest and the air is sweetest. Summer fills the beds with peonies and roses and turns the wooded slopes deep green, a place to escape the heat of the city below. Autumn sets the conifers against the turning leaves and thins the visitors to those who come for quiet. Even winter has its pull, the greenhouses holding their tropical warmth and their orchids while snow settles over the outdoor zones. As a research institution of the National Academy of Sciences, the garden is always at work behind the display, studying, propagating, and conserving plants, but for the people of Kyiv it is something simpler and just as vital: 120 hectares of living green on the river's edge, a place to walk and breathe in every season, anchored by the lilacs that draw the whole city out each spring.

## Flight Context

The Hryshko National Botanical Garden lies at 50.4127 N, 30.5623 E on the high right bank of the Dnieper in Kyiv's Pecherskyi District. From about 2,000 feet AGL, look for a large hillside of terraced greenery and open lawns dropping toward the river, with monastery domes among the trees, just south of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. The river bluff and the Vydubychi domes aid orientation. Nearest airport is Kyiv International (Zhuliany), ICAO UKKK, west of the city; Boryspil International, UKBB, lies to the east.
