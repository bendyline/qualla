# Museum of Historical Treasures of Ukraine

Inside a monastery on the Dnipro hills glitters one of the world's great hoards of Scythian gold, crowned by a 2,400-year-old pectoral pulled from a burial mound.

**Location:** 50.4357, 30.5578

**Categories:** Museums, Kyiv, Archaeology, Scythian gold

## Introduction

Somewhere in the third section of a golden collar, a young goat nibbles at a branch while a griffin sinks its claws into a horse. The whole scene is the size of a dinner plate and more than two thousand years old, and it was buried in the earth of the steppe until 1971. Today it hangs in a vault inside the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, the ancient cave monastery above the Dnipro, where a museum keeps some of the finest gold the ancient world ever left behind. The building announces nothing. The treasure inside announces everything.

## The Golden Chamber

The museum began with a bureaucratic order and a very good idea. In 1963 the Soviet government created a branch of Ukraine's state historical museum devoted solely to objects of precious metal and gemstone, and called it the Golden Chamber. Its founding collection was assembled by gathering treasures from twenty separate museums across the republic and bringing them under one roof. The logic was practical, since gold demands guards and vaults that ordinary collections do not, but the effect was transformative. Suddenly the scattered brilliance of thousands of years of Ukrainian soil sat in a single set of rooms. The institution has since been renamed the Treasury of the National Museum of the History of Ukraine, but Kyivans still speak of the gold, and of one object above all.

## The Pectoral

In 1971 the Ukrainian archaeologist Borys Mozolevskyi was excavating a burial mound called Tovsta Mohyla in the Dnipro region when he found a golden pectoral, a broad ceremonial collar that had once lain against the chest of a Scythian ruler. It is solid gold, roughly thirty centimeters across, and weighs more than a kilogram. Three tiers of tiny figures curve across it. The top band shows scenes of daily life; the middle blooms with plants and flowers; the lowest seethes with beasts locked in combat, griffins tearing at horses. Scholars read the three registers as the Scythian world entire, from the hearth to the wild to the cosmos beyond. The single kurgan yielded over six hundred gold objects, among the richest finds of its age anywhere on Earth.

## Riders Who Left No Cities

The Scythians who made these things were nomads, mounted horse peoples who ranged the Pontic steppe north of the Black Sea between roughly the seventh and fourth centuries BC. They built no marble temples and wrote no histories of their own, so almost everything we know of them comes from Greek accounts and from what they carried into their graves. The museum's cases hold that legacy in miniature: weapons, horse bridles, drinking vessels, and jewelry worked with astonishing delicacy for a people so often imagined as only warriors. The gold shows a different picture, of artisans and storytellers, of a culture that pressed its entire cosmology onto a collar no wider than a man's shoulders.

## What Gold Is For

There is something fitting about keeping this collection inside the Lavra, where monks once carved chapels into the hillside and gilded icons by candlelight. Two very different civilizations, separated by more than a thousand years, met on the same green slopes above the Dnipro to make the same wager, that gold could hold meaning against time. For the Scythian ruler it was insignia carried into the dark of a burial mound. For the museum it is inheritance, gathered and guarded. Visitors stand before the pectoral and lean close, tracing the goat and the griffin with their eyes, and for a moment the two thousand years between them and the maker seem very thin indeed.

## Flight Context

The Treasury sits at 50.4357 N, 30.5578 E within the grounds of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra on the right bank of the Dnipro. From 2,000 to 3,000 feet AGL, orient on the Lavra's cluster of gold-domed churches and its tall Great Lavra Bell Tower; the museum is among the reserve's buildings on the hillside. Nearest airports are Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) about 8 km west and Boryspil International (UKBB) roughly 30 km east.
