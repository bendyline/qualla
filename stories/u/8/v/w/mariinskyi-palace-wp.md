# Mariinskyi Palace

An empress ordered this turquoise-and-white Baroque palace built in 1744 but never set foot inside it; centuries later it became the ceremonial residence of Ukraine's president, and briefly an Oscar-winning film set.

**Location:** 50.4483, 30.5375

**Categories:** Architecture, History, Baroque, Kyiv

## Introduction

The empress who ordered this palace never got to enjoy it. In 1744, Elizaveta Petrovna of Russia commanded a residence be built on the high right bank of the Dnipro in Kyiv, and she gave the commission to Bartolomeo Rastrelli, the most celebrated architect in the empire, the man behind its grandest Baroque palaces. His student Ivan Michurin and a team of others finished the work in 1752, but Elizabeth never made the journey to see it, remaining on the throne until her death in 1762 without ever setting foot inside. The turquoise-and-white palace she summoned stands to this day, its columns and flourishes pure Elizabethan Baroque, and its life has run far beyond anything she could have imagined, from imperial guest house to the ceremonial home of Ukraine's president.

## Rastrelli's Baroque Jewel

The palace is a small masterpiece of a particular moment in taste. Elizabethan Baroque, named for the empress who loved it, was all ornament and color, gilded detail against pale blue and white, and Rastrelli was its supreme practitioner. The first senior member of the imperial family to actually stay here was Elizabeth's niece-in-law, Catherine the Great, who visited Kyiv in 1787. Through the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries it served as the residence of the region's governors-general, a seat of imperial administration overlooking the river. Then fire nearly finished it. In the early 1800s a series of blazes gutted the palace, and it stood abandoned and ruined for close to half a century, a grand shell above the Dnipro, waiting for someone to care enough to bring it back.

## The Empress's Namesake

Its rescue came in 1870, when Emperor Alexander II ordered the palace rebuilt. The architect Konstantin Mayevsky worked from old drawings and watercolors to restore what the fires had destroyed, and the reborn palace took a new name, honoring the reigning empress, Maria Alexandrovna. It became the Mariinskyi Palace, and at Maria's wish a large park was laid out along its southern side, the green setting it still enjoys. Until 1917 the palace received visiting members of the imperial family, a place of ceremony and rest above the river. The twentieth century then dragged it through upheaval, headquarters for a revolutionary committee during the civil war, an agricultural school, a museum, badly damaged again in the Second World War and restored at the end of the 1940s, with another major restoration completed in the early 1980s.

## A Living Residence

Today the Mariinskyi Palace is the official residence of the president of Ukraine, adjoining the neoclassical parliament building, the Verkhovna Rada. Long-running reconstruction work began in 2007, and the palace continues its ceremonial role at the very center of Ukrainian public life. In 2022 its grandest room took on new meaning: the White Hall, where the country's leaders receive foreign heads of state, was named the White Hall of Heroes of Ukraine, the place where those honored with the nation's highest title now receive it. It is a fitting turn for a building that has always been about ceremony and reception, though the ceremonies now belong to an independent nation rather than an empire, and the heroes honored beneath its gilded ceilings are its own.

## The Palace on Screen

The Mariinskyi Palace has one unexpectedly joyful claim to fame. In August 2021, the palace grounds hosted the filming of the music video for Naatu Naatu, a exuberant dance number from the Indian Telugu-language epic RRR. The sight of the film's stars dancing before a Ukrainian presidential palace traveled around the world, and the song went on to a remarkable haul of honors, winning the 2023 Golden Globe for Best Original Song and then the Academy Award for Best Original Song. A Baroque residence commissioned by a Russian empress, restored by an emperor, and now serving a Ukrainian president had become, for a few unforgettable minutes, the backdrop to one of the most celebrated dance sequences in recent film. Few palaces have worn so many roles with such apparent ease.

## Flight Context

The Mariinskyi Palace stands at 50.4483 N, 30.5375 E on the Dnipro's high right bank in Kyiv's Pecherskyi District, beside the domed Verkhovna Rada parliament building and its surrounding park. From about 1,500 feet AGL, look for the turquoise-and-white Baroque palace with its formal gardens, just above the river bluff and near the golden domes of the Lavra to the south. Nearest airport is Kyiv International (Zhuliany), ICAO UKKK, west of the city; Boryspil International, UKBB, lies to the east.
