# Holosiiv National Nature Park

A genuine wild forest, home to a hundred kinds of bird and forty-four mammals, survives inside the southern edge of a capital city of three million people.

**Location:** 50.3800, 30.5059

**Categories:** Nature, Parks, Wildlife, Kyiv

## Introduction

You can stand among hornbeams older than most of the buildings in Kyiv and forget, for a moment, that a capital city surrounds you on every side. That is the peculiar magic of the Holosiiv National Nature Park: a real forest, not a manicured park, breathing away inside the southern reaches of a metropolis. Foxes and forty-three other mammals move through it. A hundred species of bird nest in it. Roughly forty-five hundred hectares of woodland, lakes, and wetland persist here as a stubborn remnant of the forest-steppe that once rolled across all of central Ukraine, now hemmed in by neighborhoods with names like Demiivka and Teremky pressing at its edges.

## A Monastery's Old Domain

The name Holosiiv first appears in writing in 1541, when this stretch of woods belonged to the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, the great cave monastery to the north. By 1617 a small homestead, a khutir, stood among the trees. For centuries the forest was monastic land, quiet and worked at its margins, until the Soviet campaigns against religion swept the monks away and turned their holdings to new uses. An agrarian institute rose on former monastic ground, growing eventually into today's National University of Life and Environmental Sciences. In the 1950s and 60s came an exhibition center and a city park; later, research institutes of the National Academy of Sciences settled at the woodland's edge. Layer by layer, the city arrived, and yet the forest at the core held on, protected at last as a national park.

## Life in the Numbers

The park keeps an extraordinary ledger of living things. Its flora runs to 650 species of vascular plants, 118 kinds of moss, and more than sixty fungi. Twenty-four of those plants are listed in the Red Book of Ukraine, the national roll of species at risk. The animal count is just as dense: 190 species of insect, 181 vertebrates in all, including 100 kinds of bird and ten species of amphibian threading through the ponds. Bats find refuge here under international protection, as do migratory birds tracked across borders by conservation agreements. To walk the park's ecological trails is to move through a genuinely functioning ecosystem, monitored by scientists precisely because it is rare, a pocket of biodiversity that a growing city has, remarkably, chosen to leave alone.

## Caves, Domes, and Bunkers

History hides among the trees in unexpected forms. Near the park stand the Holy Trinity Monastery, set in the Kytaiv hollow with ancient caves cut into the hillsides, and the Holy Protection Monastery, founded in the seventeenth century and reborn in our own. The Main Astronomical Observatory of the National Academy of Sciences occupies buildings raised in the 1840s, its telescopes turned skyward from within the woods. Strangest of all are the concrete relics of the Kyiv fortified region, an eighty-five-kilometer defensive line built in the early 1930s, its pillboxes half-swallowed now by forest. A handful of these bunkers sit inside the park itself, and in the southern sector they were fitted into the earthworks of the ancient Serpent's Wall, a rampart older than the city's memory. Deep time and modern war lie side by side beneath the canopy.

## A Forest That Held Its Ground

The park is not one continuous woodland but a scatter of forests across the southern city, disconnected patches that together make up its roughly forty-five hundred hectares. That fragmentation is the mark of how the city grew around them, filling the gaps between the trees rather than clearing them. In 2014 the protected area was greatly enlarged, with thousands of hectares of the Sviatoshyn forest added by presidential decree, an acknowledgment that this green inheritance was worth defending on paper as well as in fact. For the people of Kyiv, Holosiiv is where the city stops. Its ecological trails lead in minutes from apartment blocks and tram lines into hornbeam groves where the traffic fades and birdsong takes over, a genuine forest reachable on an ordinary afternoon. That a capital of millions still holds this much wild ground at its edge is not an accident but a choice, renewed each time the city decides, again, to let the forest stand.

## Flight Context

Holosiiv National Nature Park lies at roughly 50.38 N, 30.51 E across the southern districts of Kyiv. From cruising altitude in clear weather it reads as a broad, irregular block of dark green forest interrupting the city grid, threaded with small ponds and lakes and bounded by the E40 route toward Feofaniia. The white domes of the astronomical observatory are a useful marker. Nearest airport is Kyiv International (Zhuliany), ICAO UKKK, a few kilometers north; Boryspil International, UKBB, lies to the east.
