# Monument to the Founders of Kyiv

A sculptor gave his dead daughter's face to a legendary princess, and set her sailing forever down the Dnipro at the spot where a city was born.

**Location:** 50.4291, 30.5687

**Categories:** Monuments, Kyiv, Ukrainian history, Sculpture

## Introduction

Four figures crowd into a small boat that never moves, riding a frozen crest of granite waves above the Dnipro. Three brothers huddle near the stern, clutching spears and a bow. At the bow stands their sister Lybid, arms flung wide to the river and the wind. She is looking at the water the way you look at something you are about to leave forever. The sculptor Vasyl Borodai gave her the face of his own daughter, who had died at thirty-one, and so the woman greeting the horizon here is at once a founding legend and a father's grief cast in copper.

## The Siblings Who Named a City

Ukrainian legend tells of four siblings who settled the wooded hills above the Dnipro: the brothers Kyi, Shchek, and Khoryv, and their sister Lybid. The eldest, Kyi, gave the city its name. Whether they ever lived, no one can say, but for fifteen centuries Kyivans have told their story as an origin myth, a way of saying that this place was chosen, not stumbled upon. When the Soviet city marked what it called the 1,500th anniversary of Kyiv in 1982, it commissioned Borodai to raise the four founders in bronze-toned relief on the Dnipro's bank, where the water still bends past the very slopes the legend describes. The boat points downstream, as if the siblings had just arrived and were deciding where to land.

## Copper Skin Over a Concrete Heart

The monument looks like weathered metal, but its heart is reinforced concrete. That compromise was political. After the expense of the 1980 Moscow Olympics, the Central Committee of the Soviet Communist Party forbade the use of costly nonferrous metals, so Borodai sheathed a concrete armature in thin copper leaf. It fooled the eye for nearly thirty years. Then, one evening in 2010, the rear of the sculpture simply gave way. Two of the brothers collapsed. Inside, the hidden steel frame had rusted through, rotted by decades of river damp that the design had never accounted for. What Kyivans had taken for solid bronze turned out to be a hollow shell held up by corroding bones, a reminder of how much of a monument's endurance is invisible.

## Rebuilt in a Hundred Days

The city moved fast. Officials briefly floated replacing the original with a bronze copy, then thought better of erasing the beloved thing itself. Three months and five days after the collapse, the restored monument was unveiled in a solemn ceremony timed to Kyiv Day, its middle rebuilt with waterproof concrete and a drainage system finally threaded through to carry off the water that had killed it. Engineers predicted it would now stand for at least a century. Two years later Kyiv chose the founders' boat as the emblem for its role hosting the UEFA Euro 2012 football tournament, and in 2022, as war reached the city, workers wrapped the sculpture in sandbags and plywood, sheltering the legendary founders as they might shelter their own.

## A Daughter at the Bow

Return to Lybid, and to the private sorrow hidden in plain sight. Borodai modelled her serene, outward-gazing face on his daughter, lost at thirty-one, so that every visitor who lingers at the riverbank is quietly meeting a young woman the sculptor could not save. There is a second, entirely different monument to the same four founders on Maidan Nezalezhnosti in the city center, unveiled in 2001, but it is this one on the Dnipro that Kyivans call a symbol of their city. Perhaps because a symbol works best when it carries something true underneath the myth. Here the myth is fifteen centuries old, and the truth is one father's love, and the two ride the same small boat down the same wide river.

## Flight Context

The monument stands at 50.4292 N, 30.5687 E on the western bank of the Dnipro in Kyiv's Pecherskyi District, near Navodnytskyi Park. Best appreciated from a low, slow pass at 1,000 to 2,000 feet AGL in clear light; look for the copper boat on its granite wave-pedestal beside the broad river, with the city's Pechersk hills and gold-domed monasteries rising just inland. Nearest airports are Kyiv Zhuliany (UKKK) about 8 km southwest and Boryspil International (UKBB) about 30 km east.
