# Maksym Zalizniak Oak

A single oak in the Kholodnyi Yar forest has stood for more than eleven centuries - old enough to have shaded the rebel whose name it now carries.

**Location:** 49.1106, 32.2669

**Categories:** Natural monuments, Trees, Ukrainian history, Cossack heritage

## Introduction

It was already ancient when the rebels gathered beneath it. Near the village of Buda, in the depths of the Kholodnyi Yar forest, a single oak has been growing for more than 1,100 years - putting on rings while empires rose and fell around it, and reaching a girth no arms could hope to close around. Its trunk measures nine meters in circumference. It climbs some thirty meters into the canopy. And it carries the name of Maksym Zalizniak, a Cossack who, tradition holds, mustered his fighters in this forest before one of the great uprisings of the eighteenth century.

## Older Than the Kingdoms Around It

Eleven centuries is a difficult span to hold in the mind. When this acorn first split and sent up its shoot, there was no Ukraine on any map, no Moscow, no Ottoman court in Constantinople - the medieval world was still assembling itself. The tree has outlived all of it. Judged one of the oldest and largest trees in Ukraine, and among the venerable giants of Europe, it took third prize in the national "Tree of the Year" competition in 2010, a modest honor for something that has quietly witnessed the whole recorded history of these lands. To stand beneath its crown is to feel the ordinary scale of a human life reduced to something very small.

## The Rebel in the Forest

The oak is named for Maksym Zalizniak, a leader of the Koliivshchyna - the great haidamak rebellion that swept the Right-Bank lands in 1768. The haidamaks were bands of rebels, often peasants and Cossacks, who rose against the landlords and the powers that ruled them, and Kholodnyi Yar was among their strongholds. Legend places Zalizniak and his men in the shade of this very tree as they prepared to march. Whether or not the story is exact, the pairing feels right: an uprising that came out of the deep forest, remembered by the deepest-rooted thing in it. The rebellion was crushed, as such risings usually were, and Zalizniak's own fate was harsh - but the tree kept his name, and the name kept the memory alive.

## Still Growing

The remarkable thing is that the oak is not a ruin or a relic behind a rope. It still leafs out each spring. It still grows. Now protected as a natural monument within the Chyhyryn national preserve, it draws visitors who come simply to stand in the presence of so much time, in a forest already thick with mounds and legends. Around it the ancient woodland closes in, the same dim green shelter that hid rebels and refugees for centuries. The oak asks nothing and explains nothing. It simply endures, as it has for over a thousand years, adding another ring while the people who come to see it try to take its measure.

## Flight Context

The Maksym Zalizniak Oak stands at 49.111N, 32.267E, near Buda village within the Kholodnyi Yar forest of Cherkasy Oblast, central Ukraine. The tree itself is far too small to spot from altitude; navigate instead by the dark oak forest of Kholodnyi Yar, with the town of Chyhyryn to the east near the Dnieper. The nearest airport is Cherkasy (UKKE).
