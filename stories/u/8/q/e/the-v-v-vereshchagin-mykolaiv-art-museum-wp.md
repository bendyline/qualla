# The V. V. Vereshchagin Mykolaiv Art Museum

An art museum founded as a memorial to a painter who spent his life showing war without glory, its own collection twice built up and once carried off.

**Location:** 46.9732, 31.9860

**Categories:** Museum, Art, History, Ukraine

## Introduction

The museum began as an act of remembrance. In 1914, a group of local art lovers in Mykolaiv resolved to create a monument to Vasily Vereshchagin, the celebrated Russian painter who had made his name depicting the reality of war stripped of its heroism and pageantry. They named the new museum for him, and its first works arrived from the Academy of Arts, from the imperial museum in the capital, and from the painter's own widow, Lydia Vasilyevna, who sent pieces from his hand. From that memorial impulse grew one of southern Ukraine's notable art collections, a place whose story mirrors the turbulent century it was born into.

## A Collection Carried Off

By the eve of the Second World War the collection had grown to nearly a thousand works, and then war took almost all of it. During the occupation the museum was plundered, its holdings scattered, and only after Mykolaiv was liberated could the slow work of restoration begin. In 1945 the museum resumed its life in a small former estate, and the rebuilding of a lost collection became a shared national effort. Works flowed in from museums in Kyiv and Odessa, and the holdings recovered and grew. Among the treasures are pieces once owned by Vereshchagin himself and paintings by artists such as the great seascape master Ivan Aivazovsky, whose luminous depictions of the Black Sea feel especially at home in a city built beside its waters.

## Rooting the Museum in Its Region

Through the 1970s and 1980s the museum deliberately turned toward the art of its own region. The local artists' organization sought out and acquired the earliest works of painters connected to the area, and over time the museum assembled what became the fullest collection anywhere of works by contemporary Mykolaiv artists. In 1986 it moved into a new and more spacious home, arranging its holdings across upper floors given over to painting, sculpture, decorative arts, and graphic work, with a separate hall reserved for Western European art. What had begun as a memorial to a single painter had become a living institution documenting the artistic life of a whole community, generation by generation.

## Honored for What It Saved

The museum's devotion to the artist it was named for did not go unrecognized. In 2002 the Russian Academy of Arts, together with a foundation dedicated to preserving Vereshchagin's legacy, awarded it a gold medal for its work restoring the painter's canvases and promoting his art. In 2009 the Ukrainian Academy of Arts added a silver medal, honoring the museum's contribution to art, to the aesthetic education of the young, and marking the ninety-fifth anniversary of its founding. Those awards speak to something quietly heroic in the museum's history: a collection twice assembled, once looted and rebuilt, kept alive across a violent century by people who believed that a painter's vision, and a region's art, were worth preserving.

## Flight Context

The museum stands in central Mykolaiv at about 46.97 degrees north, 31.99 degrees east, within the city grid near the Southern Bug. From the air the surrounding old-town streets and riverfront serve as the main reference points. Nearest airport is Mykolaiv International (UKON) to the north; Kherson (UKOH) and Odesa International (UKOO) lie further along the Black Sea coast.
