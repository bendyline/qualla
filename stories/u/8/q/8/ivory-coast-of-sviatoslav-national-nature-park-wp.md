# Ivory Coast of Sviatoslav National Nature Park

Where two great rivers meet the Black Sea, freshwater and salt lie side by side across a wild ribbon of sand, pine, and steppe named for a warrior-prince of old.

**Location:** 46.5000, 31.6667

**Categories:** Nature, Coastal, National Park, Ukraine

## Introduction

Walk the sandy paths near the tip of the Kinburn Spit and you can taste the difference in the air. To the north, the water is fresh, poured out by the combined Dnieper and Southern Bug as they finish their long journey across Ukraine. To the south lies the salt of the open Black Sea. Between them stretches a low, wild threshold of shifting sand, pine woods, and open steppe that carries an unexpectedly poetic name: the Ivory Coast of Sviatoslav. This is a park built not around a mountain or a monument but around an edge, the exact place where river becomes sea, and where the dry grasslands of the steppe give way to marsh, bay, and beach.

## A Meeting of Waters

The park sits in the Black Sea Lowland, at the farthest northern reach of the western Black Sea, on the very ground where the Dnieper and the Southern Bug enter the sea together. That double river mouth defines everything here. North of the Kinburn Spit the estuary runs fresh, fed by the rivers; south of it the bay and sea run salt. The strangest result is the water itself. The area is famous for its many lakes, some fresh and filled by rain, others salty and quietly recharged by seepage from the sea, sometimes only a short walk apart. The land lies about 150 kilometers west of the narrow bridge to Crimea, in the Pontic-Caspian steppe, a vast grassland belt that once carried nomads and horsemen from the edge of Asia deep into Europe.

## Sand, Pine, and Grass

The dominant character of the Kinburn Spit is sandy steppe, a landscape that can look austere until you learn to read it. Amid the grasses stand groves of pine and oak, ribbons of meadow, and patches of marsh where water-loving and salt-loving plants sort themselves out by ground and season. The climate is continental and sharply seasonal, with genuinely hot summers, at least one month averaging above 22 degrees Celsius, and cold, exposed winters. To the south lies Yahorlyk Bay, a shallow arm of the Black Sea recognized as a wetland of international importance under the Ramsar Convention, where migrating birds find shelter on the boundary between the steppe and the sea. Just along the coast, the neighboring Black Sea Biosphere Reserve continues the same protected mosaic of habitats.

## A Quiet, Fragile Wild

This was never a park of visitor centers and paved overlooks. In peacetime it remained largely undeveloped, and hikers on the ecological trails were told plainly to bring their own food and water, because little was provided out on the sand. Boat excursions once crossed the estuary and bay, and rangers led guided walks for groups who wanted to understand what they were seeing. Since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, much of the spit has been front-line or occupied ground, scarred by fires, and park staff have kept up monitoring work with satellite imagery from Ukrainian-controlled territory rather than leading tours on-site. The wildness is real, and so is its fragility, sharpened now by war. Managing such a scattered, open landscape was already difficult work, and illegal poaching and fishing had long put pressure on the very species the park exists to shelter. To visit, when it is safe to do so again, will mean stepping into a threshold country, spare and salt-scented, that asks to be treated with care.

## Flight Context

The park centers on roughly 46.50 degrees north, 31.67 degrees east, spanning the Kinburn Peninsula and Spit, the Dnieper-Bug Estuary to the north, and shallow Yahorlyk Bay to the south. From the air the pale sandbar of the spit, its dark pine groves, and the mirror-flat lakes stand out clearly against the surrounding water. Nearest airports are Kherson (UKOH) to the east and Mykolaiv International (UKON) to the north; Odesa International (UKOO) lies further along the coast to the west.
