# Ukrainian Cruiser Ukraina

A guided-missile cruiser nearly finished but never commissioned, moored for over three decades at a bankrupt shipyard as one country after another declined to claim her.

**Location:** 46.9753, 32.0036

**Categories:** Ukraine, Naval, History, Ships

## Introduction

She has floated at the same wharf in Mykolaiv for more than thirty years, and in all that time she has never once put to sea under her own power. A small skeleton crew still boards her, and shore cables keep a few of her compartments lit, so that at night the derelict shipyard around her shows one improbable ribbon of light across a warship that was never finished. Laid down in 1983 as Admiral Flota Lobov and launched in 1990, just months before the Soviet Union that ordered her ceased to exist, she is a vessel outlived by the empire, the navy, and even the name she was born with.

## A Ship Between Countries

The cruiser's fate has always turned on paperwork rather than combat. When the Soviet Union dissolved, ownership passed first toward Russia, then to newly independent Ukraine, and with the country came a new name, Ukraina. Budget shortfalls froze the work in the early 1990s with the hull already most of the way complete. In 1997 Ukraine declared it had no need for her and put her up for sale; Russia would not buy, and China and India, approached in turn, showed no interest. For a warship of her size and reported ninety-five percent completeness, no home could be found. In 2010 the Ukrainian parliament went so far as to strip her of the name Ukraina, leaving her a great gray hull without a country's word painted on her stern.

## The Suitors Who Never Came

For two decades she was courted and abandoned by turns. In 2010, after talks between the two presidents, Russia and Ukraine announced they would finish her together, but the deal dissolved into arguments over who should pay; by 2011 Russia would take her only free of charge, and Ukraine's defense minister refused to scrap a warship so nearly done. In 2018 Brazil expressed interest in acquiring and modernizing her for its own navy. None of it came to anything. By 2017 the state was spending roughly two hundred and twenty-five thousand dollars a month simply to keep her maintained and afloat, a running bill for a ship that produced nothing but the cost of her own survival.

## The Fate of a Sister

Ukraina belongs to the same Slava class as the cruiser Moskva, and that kinship has shaped the last proposals for her. After the Moskva sank, officials including a Ukrainian defense minister and the governor of Mykolaiv Oblast suggested that the unfinished Ukraina be preserved instead as a museum ship, a monument rather than a warship. Whether she becomes an exhibit, a scrapyard's final tally, or simply remains where she is, her story is unusual among great vessels: a ship remembered not for any voyage or battle, but for the long, patient decades she spent tied to a single pier, waiting for a decision that never quite arrived.

## Flight Context

The cruiser lies moored at the Mykolayiv Shipyard at 46.98°N, 32.00°E, on the Ingul River in the north of Mykolaiv. From roughly 5,000 to 8,000 feet, look for a single large gray warship berthed among the yard's cranes and the river's twin channels. Ukrainian airspace is closed to civil aviation; the nearest operating airport is Chișinău (ICAO LUKK), with Odesa (UKOO) the closest Ukrainian field.
