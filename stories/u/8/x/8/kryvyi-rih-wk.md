# Kryvyi Rih

One of the longest cities in Europe stretches for 126 kilometers along a seam of iron - a hard-working, sprawling place where trams run for free and the horizon glows at night.

**Location:** 47.9100, 33.3900

**Categories:** Cities, Industry, Ukraine, Travel

## Introduction

The name means Crooked Horn, and the shape of the city takes the idea literally. Kryvyi Rih does not gather itself into a tidy center the way most cities do; instead it stretches, a ribbon of streets and mines and mills that runs for 126 kilometers from its northern end to its southern one, following the ore in the ground beneath it. Home to some 660,000 people in central Ukraine's Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, it is a place built entirely around what lies underneath - iron, and the work of pulling it up - and to travel its length is to cross what feels less like a city than a country of its own.

## A City Drawn Out Thin

Most cities you can pace across in an afternoon. Kryvyi Rih you measure in hours. Its extraordinary length - among the longest of any city in Europe - is the direct result of geology: the settlement grew along the narrow iron-ore basin rather than spreading in a circle, so mines, districts, and mills line up one after another down the valley of the Inhulets and Saksahan rivers. The result is a place with many centers and many railway stations, where getting from one end to the other is a genuine journey. It also means the city never quite lets you forget why it exists. The mine headframes and industrial silhouettes are never far from view, punctuating the long horizontal sweep of the town.

## Getting Around the Long Way

For a city this stretched out, moving through it is its own small adventure, and Kryvyi Rih has built an unusually rich transit web to manage the distances - buses, trams, trolleybuses, and swarms of the shared minibuses known as marshrutkas, threading routes up and down its length. In a striking gesture for riders, the buses, trams, and trolleybuses run free of charge; only the marshrutkas ask a modest fare. There is even a fast tram system, part light rail and part metro, running partly underground to speed passengers along the spine of the city. Walking works well in the pedestrianized central streets and along the quaysides, but to grasp the true scale of Kryvyi Rih, you ride.

## The Price of Iron

This is a working city, and it wears the work openly. Steel is its principal industry, and the mining and processing that feed it come at a cost the city cannot hide: Kryvyi Rih records the highest emissions of air pollutants of anywhere in Ukraine. That honesty is part of the place's character - it does not pretend to be a resort. Yet there are softer notes threaded through the industry. A botanical garden offers green relief; the riverside quays invite a slower pace; and beyond the city limits, the surrounding region rewards anyone willing to drive out and explore. Kryvyi Rih is not a city that flatters its visitors. It is a city that shows you exactly what it is - and there is a bracing kind of pride in that.

## Flight Context

Kryvyi Rih is centered near 47.91N, 33.39E in central Ukraine, but its 126-km length means it sprawls far to the north and south along the ore ridge. From altitude the giveaways are the enormous open-pit iron mines, tailing ponds, and the industrial complexes strung down the valley of the Inhulets and Saksahan rivers. The nearest airport is Kryvyi Rih International (UKDR). Note that Ukrainian airspace is closed to civil aviation during the war.
