# Djurs Sommerland

A hotelier and a music agent opened an amusement park in a Djursland field in 1981; their sons now run the most-visited summer park in the Nordic countries.

**Location:** 56.4248, 10.5499

**Categories:** Amusement parks in Denmark, Tourist attractions in the Central Denmark Region, Amusement parks opened in 1981, Roller coasters, Djursland, Water parks

## Introduction

A hotelier and a music agent walk into a field. It sounds like the opening of a joke, and in 1981 it more or less was: Børge Godsk Jensen ran hotels, Ole B. Nielsen booked bands, and between them they decided that what Djursland needed was an amusement park. They built it just north of Nimtofte, a village twenty-three kilometres west of Grenaa and thirty-six east of Randers, in farm country with no obvious claim on anyone's holiday plans. In 2025 the place recorded 885,000 visitors. Nielsen's sons Henrik and Michael run it now, and it has expanded in some form nearly every year of its existence.

## The Year Piraten Arrived

For its first quarter-century Djurs Sommerland was a good regional family park - not a destination people crossed the country for. That changed in 2008. Piraten, an Intamin steel coaster, opened in a new pirate-themed section: 755 metres of track, thirty-one metres above the ground at its highest point, and a top speed of ninety kilometres per hour. It was the fastest roller coaster in Denmark, and it stayed the fastest for fourteen years, until Fønix opened at Fårup Sommerland in 2022 and took both the height and speed records north to Jutland's west coast. The press attention Piraten generated did something the park's steady incremental growth never had. It made a field near Nimtofte a place tourists deliberately drove to.

## Building a Coaster Every Few Years

The pattern since has been relentless. Thor's Hammer, a Gerstlauer bobsled, opened back in 2002 - sixteen metres tall, 480 metres long, sixty kilometres per hour. Then Skatteøen in 2011, a Mack water coaster with a twenty-eight metre drop and a seventy kilometre per hour finish, billed as the largest water coaster in the Nordic region. Juvelen followed in 2013: an Intamin motorbike coaster, a full kilometre of track, eighty-five kilometres per hour, and Denmark's only double-launch ride. Den Vilde Hønsejagt, a Zierer family coaster themed on chasing hens, arrived in 2015. DrageKongen in 2017 and Jungle Rally in 2018 anchored a new area. The T-Rex Family Coaster came with Dinosaurland in 2022. Alongside them sit Tigeren, an Intamin Gyro Swing forty-five metres high that swings riders to a hundred kilometres per hour and was named Thrill Ride of the Year at the Park World Excellence Awards in 2019, and El Grito, a sixty-metre Funtime drop tower that opened in 2024.

## Nine Worlds on Thirty-Three Hectares

The park covers 330,000 square metres, divided into themed areas that are all included in a single admission price - a policy that matters more than it sounds when the alternative is a family of five paying per ride. Sommerland is the 1981 original, rebuilt many times over. Bondegårdsland, opened in 2015, is eleven farm-themed rides pitched at the youngest visitors, with a merry-go-round of barnyard animals and a ride that turns milk into milkshake. Westernland has a replica frontier town. Mexicoland holds Juvelen and El Grito and a mini-golf course. Piratland dates from 2008. Vikingeland tucks Thor's Hammer and a pirate-ship swing into the back of the site. Wild Asia opened in 2019 around DrageKongen and Jungle Rally, absorbing Long Cun Expedition, a 370-metre log flume that has been running since 1991. Dinosaurland came in 2022.

## Water Since 1985

Vandland opened in 1985 as the first water park of its kind in Denmark, and it remains inside the standard ticket rather than behind a second gate. The Honolulu Bay wave pool, the Waikiki Surf School with six slides side by side, the enclosed Black Hole tube, the two-person halfpipe called The Wave and the Wild River tube run all operate on posted height limits rather than age rules, which is the park's general philosophy: build for the shortest person who can safely ride. The wet rides continue outside the water park. Rio Grande Rafting, opened in 1998, sends six-passenger rafts down a 500-metre course through tunnels and water effects. Piratfisken, from 2009, is a gentler 150-metre flume ending in a five-metre drop.

## The Short Danish Summer

Everything here is compressed into a season. The park opens around May and closes in mid-October, with Halloween days extending the tail, and every capital investment has to earn back its cost in roughly five months of Danish weather. That constraint explains the park's obsession with families rather than thrill-seekers: teenagers come once, families come annually for a decade. The 2025 figure of 885,000 broke the previous record of 878,000 set in 2022, making Djurs Sommerland the most visited summer park in the Nordic countries and the fourth largest amusement park in Denmark. Not bad for two men, a field, and a village of a few hundred people.

## Flight Context

56.425°N, 10.550°E, in central Djursland just north of Nimtofte. Best viewed at 1,500-3,000 ft AGL. From the air the park is an unmistakable anomaly: a dense patch of bright colour, white water surfaces and looping steel structures set in an otherwise uniform grid of Danish farmland, with the wave pool and flume channels reading as blue geometry and Piraten's thirty-one-metre lift hill throwing the longest shadow. Approach from the coast at Grenaa and track roughly 23 km west. Aarhus Airport at Tirstrup (EKAH) lies about 11 nm south-southwest - expect commercial traffic on the approach corridors. Anholt (EKAT) is far offshore to the northeast. The site is visually distinct in any light but flattest at midday; late afternoon sun picks out the coaster structures best. Note the park is only active May to mid-October.
