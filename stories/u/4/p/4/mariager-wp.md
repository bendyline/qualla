# Mariager

A cobbled town of 2,650 on Mariager Fjord that calls itself the City of Roses, sits on a salt dome, and produced the architect who gave Copenhagen its grandest façades.

**Location:** 56.6500, 9.9750

**Categories:** Cities and towns in the North Jutland Region, Mariagerfjord Municipality, Market towns, Fjords, Salt mining, Heritage railways

## Introduction

Mariager is a town of 2,650 people that gave Copenhagen its royal theatre, its national gallery, its most famous hotel and the elephants that hold up the Carlsberg gate. Vilhelm Dahlerup was born here in 1836, in a small fjord town on the southern shore of Mariager Fjord, and left to become the most productive Danish architect of the nineteenth century. He was not the only one. For a place this size - a few cobbled streets of half-timbered houses on a slope in Kronjylland, Crown Jutland - Mariager has an unreasonable export record in people who go on to shape things elsewhere.

## Maria's Field, By Papal Decree

The town owes its name and its existence to a monastery. Bridgettines settled on the hill above the fjord ferry crossing in the 1420s, and when Pope Eugene IV formally confirmed the house in 1446, the name Mariager - Maria's Field - entered the record. A settlement grew downhill from the cloister, feeding it, serving it, and burying its dead in its churchyard. Formal recognition took much longer. On 23 May 1592, a royal charter granted the inhabitants market town status with, in the language of the document, the same privileges as other market towns in the realm, and particularly the same as Hobro up the fjord. The high whitewashed church from the 1400s still looks down over the roofs, the last quarter of a building that was once one of the largest in Denmark.

## The Salt Under the Streets

Mariager sits on salt. Deep beneath the town lies a deposit that has shaped local industry more than the fjord traffic ever did, and the Akzo Nobel salt works remains one of the area's major employers. Down at the harbour, Danmarks Saltcenter turns that geology into an afternoon out: a museum of salt extraction, a mine you can walk into, and a saturated bathing lake the staff nickname the Dead Sea, where visitors float without effort and come out feeling faintly ridiculous. The town's older industries were coarser - lime kilns and brick factories working the same underground inheritance. Modern Mariager balances these with DISA Danmark and with Urtekram, Denmark's oldest organic food company.

## The Boy Who Rebuilt Copenhagen

Dahlerup's list is absurd for one lifetime. The Royal Danish Theatre, opened 1874. Hotel D'Angleterre, 1873 to 1875. Statens Museum for Kunst, 1890 to 1891. The Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek, 1891 to 1897. The Elephant Gate and Tower at Carlsberg, 1900 to 1901. If you have photographed a grand Copenhagen façade, there is a fair chance a man from Mariager designed it. Philip Smidth, born here in 1855, followed him into the same Historicist style and was similarly prolific. Budtz Müller, born 1837, became one of Denmark's pioneering photographers. Earlier still, Mariane Bournonville left in the 1780s to dance with the Royal Danish Ballet, joining the company in 1784 at sixteen and dying in 1797 at twenty-nine. Peter Nansen, the novelist and publisher, ran the other direction - born elsewhere in 1861, he died in Mariager in 1918.

## Nineteen Kilometres of 1950

The Mariager-Fårup-Viborg railway opened on 1 July 1927 and closed on 31 March 1966, one casualty among the hundreds of Danish branch lines dismantled in that decade. Mariager refused the loss. On 2 May 1970, the town's mayor Ib Frederiksen reopened nineteen kilometres of it as the Mariager-Handest Veteranjernbane, and it has been running ever since as a working museum - staff in period uniform, rolling stock preserved to the standards of a 1950s Danish private railway. It is not a ride around a park. It is a real branch line to Handest, operated as though the closure notice never arrived.

## Roses, Water, and Only Teardrops

Rosernes By, the City of Roses, is the town's motto, and in summer it is enforced with real conviction along the cobbled lanes. Down at the water, the tourist boat Svanen works the fjord in season between Hobro, Mariager and Hadsund, which remains the most pleasant way to understand why a monastery chose this slope in the first place: the fjord is long, narrow and improbably calm, more lake than sea. And in May 2013, a singer who grew up here won the Eurovision Song Contest. Emmelie de Forest, born in Randers in 1993 and raised in Mariager, took the final on 18 May with 281 points and a barefoot performance of "Only Teardrops." The town of 2,650 added another name to a list it has been quietly building for four centuries.

## Flight Context

56.650°N, 9.975°E, on the south shore of Mariager Fjord in eastern Himmerland. Best viewed at 1,500-3,000 ft AGL. The fjord itself is the navigation feature: a long, narrow, sharply defined inlet running roughly northeast from Hobro to the Kattegat at Hadsund, with steep wooded slopes on both banks - unmistakable from altitude and unlike any other Danish waterway of its length. Mariager appears as a compact cluster on the southern bank, the whitewashed church on its hillside the brightest object in the town. Randers Airfield (EKRD) lies about 13 nm southwest, Aalborg (EKYT) about 22 nm north, and Aarhus at Tirstrup (EKAH) roughly 33 nm south-southeast. Morning light along the fjord axis is exceptional; low-lying fog forms readily over the water in autumn.
