# Hospital of the Holy Ghost, Aalborg

Founded in 1431 to shelter the sick and the poor, Aalborg Kloster has never stopped looking after people — through the Reformation, three centuries of schoolboys, and a teenage resistance cell.

**Location:** 57.0489, 9.9186

**Categories:** Christian monasteries in Denmark, History of Aalborg, Hospitals in Denmark, Listed buildings and structures in Aalborg Municipality, Danish resistance movement

## Introduction

In 1431 a wealthy woman named Maren Hemmingsdatter gave away a large house, the land around it and enough money to keep the place running, on condition that it care for the sick, the old and the poor. Nearly six hundred years later people still live in those buildings and are still looked after there. Almost nothing else in Denmark can say that. The Hospital of the Holy Ghost served as Aalborg's hospital from 1431 until 1953, and the four brick ranges around its garden are both the oldest buildings in north Jutland and the oldest social institution in the country. The word most often attached to it is kloster, monastery, though there has been no religious community here since the 1530s. The name outlived the monks; the job outlived everything.

## A Gift and a Fire

What Hemmingsdatter founded was a Helligåndshus, a House of the Holy Ghost, a type common in Denmark at the time: a charitable establishment with a religious character, taking in whoever could not look after themselves. It burned down in 1434, three years after it opened, and the present buildings went up to replace it. They took most of a century to finish. By 1500 the complex had reached its full shape, four ranges enclosing a quadrangular garden, with a large church on the south side and separate wings for the male and female religious, all of it in late Brick Gothic — the deep red, sparely ornamented Baltic manner that gives northern Denmark its particular hardness of surface. Walk into the courtyard today and you are standing in a room that was completed while Columbus was still looking for a sponsor.

## Under the Order

On 20 August 1451 the house formally joined the Order of the Holy Ghost, a hospitaller order founded at Montpellier in France to care for the sick, the aged and orphans, and answering to the mother house of Santo Spirito in Sassia in Rome. Aalborg took on a specialism that few wanted: the care of people with leprosy, who at that date were shut out of ordinary society almost everywhere in Europe and who were received here as patients rather than as a problem. It was a double house, with both men and women in religious life, and it rose high in the order's esteem — the prior of Aalborg served as the grand master's deputy for all of Scandinavia. Money came from an estate scattered across northern Jutland: farms, mills, fishing rights, even the house's own brickworks. When that fell short, the hospital sent out licensed gatherers to ask the countryside for food, cloth, coin or goods on behalf of the sick poor.

## The Reformation Changed the Sign, Not the Work

The Reformation dissolved the establishment in 1536, and the town promptly reinstated it. Aalborg still needed a hospital, so the city simply secularised what it had: most of the religious gave up their status and carried on doing exactly the same work, and the lay staff the order had always employed were not affected at all. Care continued without a break until 1953. The church was a different matter. Post-Reformation Aalborg found itself with three large churches and no monastic bodies to maintain them, and the burghers refused the expense, so the hospital church and Vor Frue were both condemned, leaving Budolfi as the city church. The hospital's tower outlasted its nave by centuries and came down only in 1880. The emptied buildings found other uses in the meantime: from 1540 until 1814, for well over two and a half centuries, Aalborg Cathedral School taught its Latin here.

## Eight Boys in the Cloister

A few days before Christmas in 1941, eight teenagers from that same cathedral school formed a group in these buildings and called it the Churchill Club. Knud Pedersen, the leader, was sixteen at the time of their arrest; he and his brother Jens lived in the vicarage inside the monastery grounds, which gave the group quiet rooms nobody thought to search and a plausible reason to be coming and going at odd hours. They had watched Denmark surrender to Germany in a single morning in April 1940. Over the following months they carried out around twenty-five acts of sabotage on bicycles — destroying German vehicles, stealing weapons, hitting the Fuchs construction office in February 1942 and the Aalborg railway yard on 2 May. They were arrested four days later, on 6 May 1942, and sentenced to between eighteen months and three years. They are widely counted as Denmark's first organised resistance group, and the point that stuck with the country afterwards was their ages.

## Still Open

The hospital function ended in 1953, at which point the institution renamed itself Aalborg Kloster and kept going. The ranges now hold independent apartments for elderly residents, along with meeting and exhibition rooms, and there is a chapel where clergy from Budolfi Cathedral hold services. Governance has stayed deliberately public: a board comprising the Bishop of Aalborg, a representative of the North Jutland Region, the mayor, the Chief of Police and two further church representatives, under a warden appointed by the crown. The house is entitled to fly the split flag, the swallow-tailed Dannebrog otherwise reserved to the state, which is a small piece of ceremony carrying a large claim — that a charity founded by one woman in 1431 has never stopped being a public trust.

## Flight Context

Aalborg Kloster stands at 57.0489 N, 9.9186 E, on Latinergyden in the old town, about 150 metres northwest of Budolfi Church and 600 metres south of the Limfjord. From the air look for the closed quadrangle: four red-brick ranges around a green courtyard, an unmistakably medieval footprint dropped into a grid of 19th and 20th century streets, immediately west of the cathedral spire. Recommended altitude for the old-town cluster is 1,200 to 2,000 feet AGL. Aalborg Airport (EKYT) lies 6 km northwest across the fjord; Sindal (EKSN) is 55 km north, Aarhus (EKAH) 100 km south. The courtyard is easiest to pick out near midday when the sun reaches the enclosed garden; in low winter light the ranges read as a single dark block.
