# Fru Haugans Hotel

Northern Norway's oldest hotel, running on the same riverside street since 1794 and held for five generations by a succession of women who each inherited it from the last.

**Location:** 65.8357, 13.1906

**Categories:** Historic hotels, Norway, Nordland, Helgeland, Architecture, Family business, Hospitality

## Introduction

During the German occupation, the woman running the hotel on Sjøgata had its flagpole cut down. Mosjøen had become the occupiers' northern headquarters in Helgeland, and Eli Haugan Jenssen-Hals had decided there would be no swastika raised over the house her mother had spent thirty years building into the best address in the region. It is a small act, easily lost in the history of the war, and it says most of what needs saying about Fru Haugans Hotel: a business that stayed in the hands of one family for 138 years, passed almost entirely through the hands of women, in a town of ten thousand people beside a river in Nordland.

## Royal Consent, 1794

The hotel counts its age from a piece of paper. In 1794 a skipper named Erik Sjursen obtained royal consent to operate a guesthouse and trading post at the mouth of the Vefsna — a licence that bundled together the right to trade, to keep a shop, to sell spirits and to put travellers up for the night. In eighteenth-century Helgeland those functions were inseparable. The coast ran on boats, the boats needed somewhere to land and do business, and the people doing business needed beds. What Sjursen founded was less a hotel in the modern sense than the commercial hinge of a stretch of coastline, and it has been operating on the same ground ever since. That continuity is why Fru Haugans is described, consistently and by every Norwegian source that bothers to check, as the oldest hotel in Northern Norway.

## The Woman the Hotel Is Named For

Ellen Schistad Haugan took over in 1885, and the establishment she acquired was not thriving. What she did with it was the ordinary miracle of hospitality done properly: she built a reputation for food and for service so solid that the place became the reason people stopped in Mosjøen rather than merely a consequence of having stopped. The hotel does not carry the name of its founder or of any of the merchants who held it in between. It carries hers — Fru Haugan, Mrs Haugan — because the town decided that was whose house it was. She ran it until her death in 1914.

## Five Generations, All of Them Women

Ellen's youngest daughter, Eli Haugan Jenssen-Hals, took over in 1914 and held the hotel for nearly half a century. She expanded it substantially in the 1930s, adding some forty rooms, and she was the one who dealt with the flagpole. When she died in 1962, her niece Eli Marie Jürgensen stepped into the leading role. That niece's niece, Bjørg Jürgensen Johannessen, moved to Mosjøen to lead the hotel in 1991 and became its owner in 1997; after Bjørg's death in 2004 her husband, Alf Gilroy Johannessen, kept the hotel running for another decade before the fifth generation, Ellen Løvold Strand, took over in 2014. The family sold the hotel in 2023, when the Glastad family bought it and HEIMR Collection took over its operation, ending 138 years in the hands of one family. No other hotel in Norway stayed with one family for so long. The succession was remarkable for its length and more remarkable for its shape — daughter, niece, niece, daughter, an inheritance passed through women at nearly every step.

## Elistuen and the River Garden

The buildings stand on Sjøgata, the preserved timber street running along the Vefsna in the town centre, which means the hotel is not a monument parked among ordinary shops but one of the working elements of a protected historic district. Elistuen, the oldest wooden section, houses a restaurant and bar. Some guest rooms are furnished with historic Norwegian pieces approved by the Directorate for Cultural Heritage, and a small museum keeps artefacts from the original trading post. Behind the buildings, a formal garden in the renaissance style runs down toward the water. Successive enlargements have brought the hotel to 76 rooms with a conference centre and several restaurants, and it has a durable reputation for traditional Norwegian cooking. Mosjøen's railway station and bus terminal are a short walk away; the airport at Kjærstad is a few minutes south.

## Flight Context

Fru Haugans Hotel sits at 65.836°N, 13.191°E in central Mosjøen, on the Sjøgata waterfront where the Vefsna river meets the Vefsnfjord. From the air the target is the district rather than the building: look for the tight line of timber warehouses and piers along the river's western bank at the head of the fjord, with the 814-metre Øyfjellet rising immediately west of town. Mosjøen Airport, Kjærstad (ENMS) lies about 7 km south and is the obvious arrival; Sandnessjøen Airport, Stokka (ENST) is 71 km west by road and Mo i Rana Airport, Røssvoll (ENRA) lies north. Mosjøen sits at the head of a narrow fjord flanked by mountains — approaches are valley work, and the town takes heavy autumn and early-winter precipitation with snow cover typically from late November into mid-April.
