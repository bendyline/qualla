# Vuoggatjålme

Sweden's official cold record, minus 52.6 degrees, was written down at this lakeside outpost on the Silver Road by an observer who half-suspected his thermometer had broken.

**Location:** 66.5750, 16.3478

**Categories:** Populated places in Arjeplog Municipality, Weather records, Swedish Lapland, Arctic Circle, Mining history

## Introduction

The note that went south from Vuoggatjålme on 2 February 1966 reads almost like a confession. Never before had the observer seen so low a temperature, he wrote; he had nearly assumed the thermometer was faulty, but unfortunately it had gone past 50 below in several of the upper mountain valleys, and he did not wish for a repeat. The figure on the instrument was minus 52.6 degrees Celsius. Six decades on, nothing measured at an official Swedish weather station has beaten it, and this small cluster of buildings on a lake shore in the Arjeplog mountains, a shade north of the Arctic Circle, still holds the coldest entry in the country's record book.

## Minus Fifty-Two Point Six

Two mornings later, on 4 February, the same station logged minus 52.1 degrees. Whatever settled over these valleys that week was not a single freak reading from a broken glass tube. Even so, the record carries an asterisk. SMHI, Sweden's meteorological and hydrological institute, points out that the three-hourly observations from Vuoggatjålme do not sit comfortably alongside the reported minimum, and that somewhere in the week's paperwork at least one value appears to have been written down wrong. The record stands regardless, because the alternative is shakier. Sweden's cold mark previously belonged to Malgovik, where a schoolhouse thermometer read minus 53 degrees on 13 December 1941. Inspectors from SMHI examined that instrument and accepted the observation, but it was a private thermometer with an uncertainty too wide to carry a national record. Malgovik now survives as a footnote beneath Vuoggatjålme's official number.

## Where the Cold Sits Down

Minus 52.6 is not the temperature this place lives at. The 1991-2020 normals for Vuoggatjålme, taken at 500 metres above sea level, give an annual mean of minus 0.1 degrees, a mean daily maximum of 4.0 degrees, a mean daily minimum of minus 4.8, and 610.6 millimetres of precipitation a year. On paper it is a cool place, not an impossible one. What the mountains add is geometry. Under clear skies, over deep snow, with no wind to interfere, chilled air grows dense and slides downhill until it pools in the valley floors, and once it has pooled nothing stirs it out again. The observer's own note is the best evidence of how local that effect is. The cold he recorded was not a Vuoggatjålme phenomenon; it was a mountain-valley one, breaking 50 below in several upper valleys at the same hour.

## Silver, and Who Carried It

National road 95 runs past the turn-off, and its nickname explains why a road exists in this country at all. Silvervägen, the Silver Road, is named for ore that came off Nasafjäll, a mountain on the Norwegian border some 40 kilometres west, where silver was worked from 1635 to 1659 and again from 1770 to 1810. A Sami man named Peder Olofsson found the deposit. The Swedish crown then ran the first phase on conscripted labour: young men from the Bothnian coast were sent up to the workings, while Sami families were pressed into hauling ore and supplies with their own reindeer and sleds, moving loads in relays between staging points down the mountains for little or no payment. Many left for Norway rather than continue. The mine yielded roughly 860 kilograms of silver and 250 tonnes of lead in that first period before a Norwegian force under Preben von Ahnen crossed the mountains in August 1659, plundered the site and burned it.

## A Record Nobody Wants to Break

Vuoggatjålme today is a tourist facility rather than a village, a set of buildings on the shore of Vuoggatjålmjaure just above the northern tip of Sädvvájávrre, with char and trout in the water and the Arctic Circle running almost through the property. Head south on the 95 and you reach Jäkkvik, then Hornavan, at 221 metres the deepest lake in Sweden, then Arjeplog itself. Head north and the road climbs past Mierkenis to the Norwegian border. Guests come for the fishing, the snow and the particular satisfaction of standing in a place whose entire claim on the map is a number nobody has managed to beat. That is unlikely to change soon. Northern Sweden's really cold days are growing scarcer, and the winter mornings that made minus 52.6 possible in the first place are becoming harder to come by.

## Flight Context

Located at 66.575N, 16.348E in the mountains of Arjeplog Municipality, Norrbotten County, at roughly 500 metres elevation and just north of the Arctic Circle. The site sits on the shore of Vuoggatjålmjaure, immediately above the northern tip of the long lake Sädvvájávrre; the pale ribbon of national road 95 (Silvervägen) running northwest toward the Norwegian border is the clearest navigation feature. Terrain rises steeply to the west toward Nasafjäll on the border. Recommended viewing altitude 4,000-7,000 ft AGL for the lake chain and road corridor. Nearest airports: Bodø (ENBO) roughly 65 nm west-northwest across the border in Norway, Arvidsjaur (ESNX) about 90 nm southeast, Gällivare (ESNG) about 110 nm northeast. Expect rapid mountain weather changes, low cloud on the border ridges, and in deep winter extreme surface cold with strong temperature inversions in the valleys.
