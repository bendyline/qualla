# Freetown Christiania

For fifty years the residents of Christiania governed themselves on a strip of old Copenhagen rampart - and in 2024 they tore up their own most famous street to take it back.

**Location:** 55.6736, 12.5997

**Categories:** Anarchist intentional communities, Counterculture communities, Copenhagen, Car-free zones in Europe, Denmark

## Introduction

On 6 April 2024, the people who live in Christiania took shovels and sledgehammers to Pusher Street and pulled it apart. Anyone who wanted to watch was welcome, and anyone who wanted a cobblestone was welcome to take one home. This was not a police raid; the police had tried that for decades. It was a decision made by residents who had spent years being shot at, blamed and used, and who had concluded that the street their community was famous for had stopped belonging to them. The cannabis trade at Christiania was never the point of the place. Getting rid of it was the residents' own work.

## September 1971

The Badsmandsstraede Barracks had been standing empty for a while when people started climbing the fence. The land itself is older than any of it: Christian IV laid out Christianshavn and its ramparts in 1617 by reclaiming the shallows between Copenhagen and Amager, and Christian V had the defences reinforced between 1682 and 1692 into a complete ring. Those earthworks are still there, and are reckoned among the finest surviving 17th-century defence works anywhere. On 26 September 1971, Jacob Ludvigsen - a provo and journalist who published a paper called Hovedbladet, widely read by the young - declared the free town open. He had already run a piece under the headline "Civilians conquered the 'forbidden city' of the military". A constitution followed on 13 November. Between 850 and 1,000 people live there now, on former barracks and rampart, without cars.

## The Junk Blockade

Christiania's reputation abroad has always been the cannabis, which obscures the harder history. In one year at the end of the 1970s, ten residents died of overdoses. The community's response was not to call anyone. In 1979 they organised the Junk Blockade, patrolling their own streets for forty days and forty nights until hard drugs were driven out, and hard drugs have been unwelcome ever since. The 1980s brought a different threat: motorcycle gangs fighting for control of the drug market, with the Bullshit Motorcycle Club beating off a Hells Angels chapter to take sole control by 1984. In 1987, after police found a dismembered body under the floorboards of a Bullshit bike shop inside Christiania, the community, the police and the Angels between them cleared the club out. Biker colours have been banned in the Freetown from that day on.

## Fifty Years in Court

The Danish state took Christiania to law on 1 April 1976, and the Supreme Court ruled in 1978 that the area should be cleared. Nobody cleared it. In 1989 Parliament passed a law recognising the place. In 2004 a new Act reasserted the state's ownership; the Eastern High Court upheld it in 2009 and the Supreme Court confirmed it in February 2011. What followed was the strangest turn in the whole story. In June 2011, after negotiations bitter enough to see the area sealed off, residents agreed to set up a fund and buy the ground beneath them. People who had spent forty years arguing that land should not be owned made their first payment in July 2012 and became legal landowners of about 19 of the original 84 acres. In July 2013 the Christiania Law was repealed, and ordinary Danish law took over.

## Pusher Street

The open cannabis market ran in the middle of the settlement, on a lane the Christiania council once labelled the Green Light District, and for years the authorities let it be. The argument for tolerance was containment: keep the trade in one visible place and it spreads less, and users are less likely to be pushed toward harder things. The argument against was that a law is a law. Neither side got what it wanted. In April 2005 a gang shooting killed a man and wounded three. In April 2009 a hand grenade thrown into the seating at Cafe Nemoland blew away part of a 22-year-old's jaw. On 31 August 2016 a man carrying cannabis earnings shot two police officers and a civilian, one officer critically, before dying in a shootout in Kastrup. At a community meeting the residents voted to pull down the Pusher Street stalls, and did it the next day. Sales fell by an estimated 75 per cent - and then came back.

## Neighbours Under Siege

By August 2023 residents were blocking the entrances to Pusher Street with concrete barriers and asking friends of the neighbourhood to stop buying. The money, they pointed out, was not financing Christiania; it was financing gangs who neither lived there nor respected the place's rules. On 26 August 2023 two masked gunmen opened fire, killing a 30-year-old man and injuring four more. Afterward the residents said plainly what had been true for years: they could not get the gangs off the street alone. The 2024 excavation was the result of that admission, carried out with the state rather than against it, and tied to a proposal to build public housing on remaining land in exchange for help. Around half a million people visit Christiania every year, which makes it one of Copenhagen's busiest attractions. Very few of them are there to see the thing that actually holds it together - roughly a thousand people running a town by consensus, badly outmatched, and still at it.

## Flight Context

Christiania occupies 55.6736 N, 12.5997 E, on the eastern edge of Christianshavn on Amager, immediately southeast of central Copenhagen. From 1,500-3,000 ft it is one of the most legible features in the city: a wedge of dense green and low, irregular self-built houses set inside the star-points of Christianshavn's preserved 17th-century ramparts, with the moat drawing a clear dark outline around it. The gold-and-black corkscrew spire of the Church of Our Saviour stands just to the west as a fix. Copenhagen Airport (EKCH) lies 6 km southeast on the same island - approaches to runway 22L pass close by - and Roskilde (EKRK) is 32 km west. Terrain is flat and reclaimed; nothing here rises above the church spires. Best light is late afternoon in summer, when the moat and the harbour behind it go copper.
