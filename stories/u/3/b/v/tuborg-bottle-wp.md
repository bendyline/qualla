# Tuborg Bottle

A 26-metre beer bottle on the Copenhagen coast road, built in 1888 as an advertisement, fitted with a water-powered lift, and still standing long after the brewery behind it moved away.

**Location:** 55.7233, 12.5783

**Categories:** Novelty architecture, Buildings and structures in Gentofte Municipality, Industrial heritage, Copenhagen, 1888 establishments in Denmark

## Introduction

One of the first passenger lifts in Denmark did not run on electricity. It ran on tap water — pressure piped from the Copenhagen Waterworks and translated by a system of stamps and pulleys into a slow climb up the inside of a beer bottle. The bottle stood 26 metres tall. It was made of painted canvas stretched over a frame, it was the work of a theatrical scene painter, and in the summer of 1888 it was one of the most talked-about objects in Copenhagen.

## An Argument, Built at Scale

The Nordic Exhibition of 1888 filled the open ground of Halmtorv, the square that Copenhagen City Hall and Rådhuspladsen would later occupy. Every serious Danish manufacturer wanted a pavilion there. Tuborg Breweries built a bottle instead — architect Viggo Klein designed the frame, and Carl Lund, a painter of theatrical scenery, gave it its skin and its label. The choice of shape was not whimsy. Tuborg was the first Danish brewery to sell beer in bottles as an alternative to the traditional barrel, and bottled beer in 1888 still needed explaining. A building you could see from half a kilometre away, shaped like the thing being sold, explained it faster than any pamphlet.

## Up Through the Neck

Visitors did not simply admire the bottle. They rode it. The hydraulic lift carried them from the base up to an observation platform, where the reward was a view over an exhibition ground and a city that had almost no tall buildings to compete with. The mechanism was ingenious and fragile in equal measure, dependent on the mains pressure of a municipal waterworks, and it did not survive the century of alterations that followed. A spiral staircase replaced it. The climb is now made on foot, or not at all.

## Five Øre, Refunded in Beer

When the exhibition closed in 1889 the bottle was dismantled and moved north to Hellerup, to the corner of Strandvejen and Carolinevej beside Tuborg's own headquarters. There it acquired a business model of unusual elegance. Admission to the tower cost five øre — and the five øre came straight back to you if you sat down afterwards in the adjacent restaurant and beer garden and ordered a glass. For decades it was the natural stop on the coast road, a place where a drive up Strandvejen paused for a view and a pilsner. The arrangement finally collapsed in 1925, undone not by the public but by the competition: other Strandvejen restaurateurs objected that they were being undercut by the brewery that supplied them. The pavilion limped on as a car repair shop and showroom before being demolished in 1934.

## The Label Turns Green

The bottle has kept its silhouette and changed almost everything else. The original label was for Red Tuborg, the brewery's lager; as pilsner overtook it in popularity the paintwork was reissued in green, and green is what the structure has worn ever since. The canvas cladding that Carl Lund painted is long gone, replaced by glassfibre. A renovation in 2003 brought the whole thing back into good order. In the same year, Denmark's heritage authority considered listing it and declined, on the grounds that so much of the original fabric had been replaced that the architectural and cultural-historical case for formal protection no longer held. Gentofte Municipality takes a warmer view: it regards the bottle as unconditionally worth preserving, and nothing can be altered or removed without the municipality's consent.

## The Brewery Leaves, the Bottle Stays

Tuborg merged with Carlsberg in 1969, and by 1979 most of the beer was being brewed in Jutland. The Hellerup works were wound down and the waterfront they occupied became Tuborg Havn — apartments, offices, marina. In 1988 the bottle went back to City Hall Square for a spell, a hundred years after it first stood on that ground, before returning to Hellerup. It is now the last conspicuous thing on the site that is still recognisably a brewery's, an advertisement that outlived the product line, the restaurant, the lift, the label colour and the factory itself. Novelty architecture rarely ages into landmark status. This one did.

## Flight Context

55.7233°N, 12.5783°E, on the Øresund shore at Hellerup roughly 6 km north of central Copenhagen. Best seen low and slow — 1,000-1,500 ft AGL following the coastline north from Copenhagen along Strandvejen. The bottle is small in aviation terms at 26 metres, so use the Tuborg Havn marina basin and the modern waterfront development as your fix, with Copenhagen's spires astern and the Swedish coast across the Sound to the east. Copenhagen Kastrup (EKCH) lies about 15 km south; Roskilde (EKRK) is 35 km southwest. Coastal haze and low stratus off the Sound are common in spring and autumn.
