# Otto von Guericke University Magdeburg

Three separate colleges were folded into one university in 1993 and named after a seventeenth-century mayor who made his reputation proving that nothing was something.

**Location:** 52.1403, 11.6432

**Categories:** Universities and colleges in Saxony-Anhalt, Otto von Guericke University Magdeburg, Educational institutions established in 1993, Buildings and structures in Magdeburg, Research institutes in Germany

## Introduction

In 1965 a twenty-year-old Kenyan named Raila Odinga arrived in Magdeburg on a scholarship. He studied mechanical engineering at the city's Technische Hochschule, took a master's degree in 1970, went home, and decades later served as prime minister of Kenya. The college that taught him no longer carries that name. In 1993 it was folded together with Magdeburg's teacher-training college and its medical academy into a single new institution, and the result was named for a seventeenth-century mayor who had spent his spare time proving that nothing was something. Otto von Guericke University Magdeburg is barely thirty years old, one of the youngest in Germany, with about 13,000 students across nine faculties — and three campuses, because it inherited three schools.

## Three Schools, One Name

Universities are usually founded once. This one was assembled. The Technical University, the University of Education and the Medical Academy had each existed separately in Magdeburg, and the 1993 merger put them under one roof without actually putting them in one place — Universitätsplatz, Universitätsklinikum and Zschokkestraße are still three distinct campuses across the city, and the geography of the institution is a map of what it used to be. The engineering DNA of the oldest component still shows in the faculty structure. Four of the nine faculties are engineering or applied science: mechanical engineering, process and systems engineering, electrical engineering and information technology, and computer science, sitting alongside mathematics, natural sciences, medicine, economics and management, and a single combined faculty for humanities, social sciences and education.

## The Man on the Letterhead

Otto von Guericke lived from 1602 to 1686 and served as mayor of Magdeburg from 1646 until he stood down in 1678, which is a long time to run a city that had been almost erased in his own lifetime. He is remembered instead for what he did away from the town hall. Around 1650 he built an air pump, and having built it he went looking for a way to make its results undeniable. The answer was two copper hemispheres fitted together with the air drawn out from between them, and sixteen horses, eight harnessed to each side, unable to pull them apart. It is one of the great pieces of scientific theatre, and it makes an unusually apt patron for an engineering university: a demonstration that the point of an experiment is to be visible.

## What They Work On

The declared research priorities read like a list of things a mid-sized industrial region needs from its university — process engineering, biosystems technology, automotive research, digital engineering, renewable energies — with two outliers that give the place its distinctive weight. One is neuroscience, tied closely to the medical faculty and the university hospital. The other is preventive medicine. The university publishes two international journals in-house, the Journal of Automata, Languages and Combinatorics in theoretical computer science and Technische Mechanik in analytical and numerical mechanics, which tells you where the depth is. In the Times Higher Education world rankings for 2024 it placed in the 601–800 band globally and 46–48 within Germany: not a name that opens doors on its own, and considerably better than a thirty-year-old institution has any obvious right to be.

## Passports

Odinga was not an anomaly. Magdeburg has been taking in students from a long way off for sixty years, and the structures around them are unusually developed for a university this size. IKUS, an intercultural association for international students, runs a rolling calendar of festivals with students organising the day for their own country. MIPS — Magdeburg International PhD Students — exists specifically for foreign doctoral researchers, and does the unglamorous work of explaining scholarships, job openings, the German language and the city itself to people who have just landed. The Studentenwerk runs the canteens and more than ten halls of residence on or beside the campuses. Among those who came and left again: Nguyễn Thiện Nhân, who took a doctorate in cybernetics here and became Vietnam's deputy prime minister and education minister, and Rumiana Jeleva, who earned a PhD in sociology and went on to be Bulgaria's foreign minister.

## Where They End Up

The alumni and faculty list has a consistent shape: people who did the work here and made their reputations somewhere larger. Claudia Maria Buch became vice-president of the Bundesbank. Birgitta Wolff went on to preside over Goethe University Frankfurt. Axel Ockenfels, an economist and Leibniz Prize winner, is among the best-known names attached to the place. Dagmar Schipanski moved from physics into politics. Others stayed: Bernhard Sabel heads the Institute of Medical Psychology, and Henning Scheich held a professorship in physiology on the medical faculty. Then there are the ones who defy categorisation entirely — Antje Buschschulte, a world champion swimmer who also finished a doctorate in biology, and Johannes Mallow, who won the World Memory Championships. Since 2022 the university has belonged to EU Green, a network of nine European universities, which is one more way a young institution goes looking for weight it cannot yet supply on its own.

## Flight Context

The main campus sits at 52.1403°N, 11.6432°E, on Universitätsplatz about 2 km north of Magdeburg Cathedral and under a kilometre back from the west bank of the Elbe. From 1,000–2,000 ft AGL it reads as a distinct cluster of flat-roofed institutional blocks and open quadrangles, noticeably more regular than the mixed housing around it, with the university hospital forming a second and larger complex further north. Use the Elbe and the cathedral towers to orient, then follow the river north. Magdeburg–Cochstedt (EDBC) lies about 40 km southwest, Leipzig/Halle (EDDP) roughly 90 km south-southeast, and Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) about 130 km east. The Börde plain west of the city is almost featureless farmland, so the river is the only reliable line feature for miles — worth remembering in the haze that settles here in high summer.
