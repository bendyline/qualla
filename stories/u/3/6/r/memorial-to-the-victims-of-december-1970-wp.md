# Memorial to the Victims of December 1970

An eleven-metre bronze angel on Szczecin's Solidarity Square, standing on a boat that rises out of the pavement - and at its foot, fifteen names, fifteen ages and fifteen trades.

**Location:** 53.4283, 14.5583

**Categories:** Monuments and memorials in Szczecin, Old Town, Szczecin, 2005 sculptures, Polish 1970 protests, Solidarity (Polish trade union), Outdoor sculptures in Poland

## Introduction

Julian Swiecicki was fifty-nine and a locksmith. Jadwiga Kowalczyk was sixteen and still at school. Their names are two of the fifteen cut into a granite slab in the middle of Szczecin, at the foot of an eleven-metre bronze angel with its wings held straight out and a crown of thorns in its hands. Read the whole list and what stands out is how ordinary it is - a driver, two carpenters, a teacher, a glazier, a storeman, a bricklayer, a turner, a technician, two more locksmiths, three students and a twenty-year-old soldier. That is who was killed in this city in December 1970, and the monument's insistence on giving each of them a trade is the point of the thing.

## Black Thursday

The government announced steep increases in the price of food and everyday goods on 12 December 1970, a fortnight before Christmas, and the Baltic coast went up. Gdansk and Gdynia moved first; Szczecin followed on 17 December, when workers walked out of the Adolf Warski Shipyard and a crowd of around ten thousand marched into the city centre. They reached the voivodeship party committee building, forced their way in, wrecked it and set it alight. The response was live ammunition. Security forces opened fire on the crowds, and tanks of the 12th Mechanized Division swept the streets with machine-gun bursts to break them up. Some of the dead had been in the march. Others had not been anywhere near it, which is what happens when a city is fired on. By the time the coastal protests were over, more than forty people had been killed and over a thousand wounded, and some three thousand arrested.

## Fifteen Names

The second slab carries them all, each with an age and a job: Eugeniusz Blazewicz, 22, driver; Jadwiga Kowalczyk, 16, student; Daniel Kucma, 24, carpenter; Roman Kuzak, 23, teacher; Stanislaw Nadratowski, 20, soldier; Henryk Perkowski, 20, glazier; Edward Prysak, 42, carpenter; Zbigniew Semczyszyn, 23, storeman; Michal Skipor, 19, student; Stefan Stawicki, 16, student; Waldemar Szuminski, 22, bricklayer; Julian Swiecicki, 59, locksmith; Zygmunt Toczek, 23, turner; Wojciech Woznicki, 21, locksmith; Janusz Wrzodak, 27, technician. Polish accounts usually put the Szczecin dead at sixteen; fifteen are named here. Nadratowski's inclusion complicates any tidy version of the week - a conscript in the army that was doing the shooting, dead at twenty like the glazier standing somewhere on the other side. The youngest were school-age. The oldest had been working with his hands for four decades. For twenty years the state's position was that none of this had happened in any way worth recording, and the families kept the details themselves.

## What the Angel Holds

Czeslaw Dzwigaj's sculpture stands eleven metres of bronze, wings extended horizontally rather than raised, giving the figure the shape of a cross when seen head-on. In its hands is a crown of thorns whose twisted form spells out Grudzien 1970 - December 1970. Underneath, a boat pushes up out of the ground as though surfacing, which in a shipyard city needs no explanation. People call it the Angel of Freedom. Four stone slabs stand vertically in the pavement around it. The first, front left, is addressed to the victims of the pacification of the Coast carried out by the communist authorities in December 1970 in Szczecin, and ends: they fought for their daily bread and died so that you could live a decent life. The third, at the back, carries the city's coat of arms and a dedication from the inhabitants of Szczecin, dated 17 December 2005 - the thirty-fifth anniversary of the shooting, four months after the monument itself went up.

## Who Paid For It

The fourth slab is a receipt, and an unusually specific one. It records that the monument came about at the initiative of the Social Association of December '70 and January '71, through the efforts of the mayor of Szczecin and the city councillors of the 2002-2006 term, with the Szczecin and Kamien Metropolitan Curia and Polish Americans from New York City and St. Louis, Missouri. The cornerstone was laid on 17 December 2003, the thirty-third anniversary. The unveiling came on 28 August 2005, timed to the anniversary of the August Agreements - the 1980 accords that legalised independent trade unions and gave Solidarity its name. That choice of date says something about how the events are understood here. December 1970 is not treated as a self-contained tragedy but as the beginning of a sequence that ran through the January 1971 strike, when Edward Gierek came to the Warski yard in person to bargain with workers who had stopped believing him, and did not finish until 1989.

## Solidarity Square

The square the angel stands on is called Plac Solidarnosci, in the Old Town a short walk from the Ducal Castle and the terraces above the Oder. It is a working city square, crossed all day by people who are not thinking about 1970, which is roughly what a memorial in a living city has to accept. The shipyard that produced the marchers of 17 December is largely gone now, its cranes thinned out along the waterfront to the north. What has lasted is the list. Anyone who stops at the second slab gets fifteen full names, fifteen ages and fifteen occupations, in a country where for two decades the official record of that week consisted of nothing at all.

## Flight Context

The monument stands at 53.4283 degrees north, 14.5583 degrees east, on Solidarity Square in Szczecin's Old Town, roughly 400 metres west of the Oder. Best viewed at 1,000-2,500 ft AGL; the angel itself is 11 metres tall and reads as a bright bronze point rather than a mass, so use the surroundings to find it. The navigational anchors are close together: the rebuilt Renaissance bulk of the Ducal Castle on its hill just north-east, the short pyramid roof of St. James' Cathedral a few hundred metres south, the Chrobry Embankment terraces on the river bluff, and the Oder itself curving north into the braided channels below the city. Nearest airports: Szczecin-Dabie (EPSD), a grass aeroclub field about 7 km east across the river, Szczecin-Goleniow Solidarnosc (EPSC) about 30 km north-east, and Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) about 140 km south-west. Low winter cloud off the Baltic is routine.
