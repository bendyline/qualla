# European University Viadrina

Brandenburg's first university opened in 1506, shipped its library upriver to Breslau in 1811, and came back in 1991 on a border that had just stopped being one.

**Location:** 52.3422, 14.5539

**Categories:** universities, germany, brandenburg, history, education, border-towns

## Introduction

The foundation stone of Brandenburg's first university went into the ground in 1498, on the site of a synagogue the town had demolished. Eight years later the Alma Mater Viadrina opened, and within twelve months more than nine hundred students had enrolled from across Germany, Poland, Sweden, Norway and Denmark. It ran for three centuries, closed in 1811, and stayed closed for a hundred and eighty years. The institution that carries the name today reopened in 1991 on a border that had just stopped functioning as one, with a partner university on the Polish bank of the Oder - which makes the Viadrina one of the few universities in Europe that can name two founding dates and mean both of them.

## Viadrus, a River God Nobody Prayed To

The Latin word Viadrina means belonging to, or situated at, the Oder, and it derives from Viadrus, a presumed river god of that river. Presumed is doing a great deal of work in that sentence. No ancient name for the Oder is actually documented; the earliest recorded form is Oddera, in the Dagome iudex of 991, a document describing the realm of Prince Mieszko I of Poland. The Latin coinage seems to have come from a local scholar, Jodocus Willich (c. 1486-1552), and it turns up in Sebastian Münster's Cosmographia in 1544, by which time the town itself was known in Latin as Francofortum ad Viadrum. A university named after an invented deity of a real river is a fitting start for a place whose entire business would become the crossing of that river.

## Tetzel Takes His Doctorate

Elector Joachim I Nestor founded the university on 26 April 1506 with the permission of Pope Julius II, and the theologian Konrad Wimpina (c. 1465-1531) became its founding prorector. Four faculties opened - law, theology, philosophy, medicine - and the main building, the Collegienhaus, was finished in 1507. In 1518 Johann Tetzel obtained his doctorate here: the indulgence preacher whose sales the previous autumn had provoked Martin Luther into writing. One of the university's early chancellors, Bishop Georg von Blumenthal (1490-1550), fought the Reformation hard and did his utmost to expel Lutherans, Willich among them. He lost. The Viadrina turned to Lutheran orthodoxy anyway. Nearly five centuries later, in 2018, a direct descendant of his brother, Julia von Blumenthal, was appointed president of the refounded university.

## Admitted Against the Faculty's Wishes

The Great Elector Frederick William opened the university to Jewish students in the seventeenth century, over fierce resistance from his own teaching staff - an instruction from above that the professors could obstruct but not overturn. The same ruler had a botanical garden laid out beside the premises while the damage of the Thirty Years' War was still being repaired. An anatomical theatre went up in 1684 at the urging of the physician Bernhardus Albinus (1653-1721), and a complete renovation followed in 1693/94. Not every appointment went smoothly. The eminent jurist Johann Jakob Moser arrived in 1736 to head the law faculty and was gone within three years, his liberal convictions unwelcome to King Frederick William I.

## The Library Went Upriver by Boat

The Prussian Reforms of the Napoleonic era did what wars and reformations had not. In 1811 the university was moved to Breslau, present-day Wrocław, and merged with the Leopoldina, founded there in 1702 by the Habsburg emperor Leopold I, so that Prussia could field something able to compete with the brand-new University of Berlin. The merger included the books: more than 28,000 volumes, the second-largest collection in Prussia after the state and university library at Königsberg, loaded onto boats and sent up the Oder. Some professors took posts in Berlin instead. The Collegienhaus became a schoolhouse, came through the Second World War barely damaged, sheltered refugees from the former eastern territories, then stood empty until it was controversially demolished in the early 1960s. The arch of its main entrance was kept as a monument. It is all there is.

## Four in Ten

Refounded in 1991, the Viadrina now runs three faculties - cultural studies, law, and economics and business - for a student body of roughly four thousand, which makes it one of the smallest universities in Germany. Around four in ten of those students come from abroad, most of them Polish, a far higher proportion than at most German institutions. Directly across the water in Słubice stands the Collegium Polonicum, operated jointly with Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań. The historian Eduard Mühle has been president since April 2023. As for the alumni of the older institution, the list is difficult to beat for a provincial university on a frontier: Ulrich von Hutten, Thomas Müntzer, Carl Philipp Emanuel Bach, Wilhelm and Alexander von Humboldt, and Heinrich von Kleist, who was born a few streets away.

## Flight Context

The Viadrina campus sits at 52.34 N, 14.55 E in the centre of Frankfurt (Oder), directly on the German-Polish border about 80 km east of Berlin. The main building occupies the former Regierungspräsidium in the city core, a block or two back from the river. From the air the navigation feature is the Oder itself - a broad, straightened watercourse carrying the international boundary, with Frankfurt on the west bank and Słubice on the east, joined by the city bridge; the Collegium Polonicum stands on the Polish side within sight of the campus. Best read from 2,500-4,000 ft, when the twin urban footprints, the bridge and the tower of St Mary's church all resolve together. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) is the nearest major airport, roughly 80 km west; Eisenhüttenstadt (EDAE) lies about 25 km south; Zielona Góra-Babimost (EPZG) about 85 km south-east on the Polish side. Airspace here is cross-border - brief both German and Polish notices. Summer haze and winter stratus over the river valley are the usual visibility constraints.
