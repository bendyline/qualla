# Görlitz Department Store

The soaring Art Nouveau atrium that plays the lobby of The Grand Budapest Hotel is not a set - it is a real 1913 Kaufhaus in a Saxon border town that Hollywood nicknamed Görliwood.

**Location:** 51.1528, 14.9876

**Categories:** architecture, art-nouveau, film-locations, history, germany, saxony

## Introduction

Wes Anderson did not build that lobby. It is the single hardest thing to believe about *The Grand Budapest Hotel* - that the glowing, symmetrical, tier-upon-tier atrium at the centre of the film is a genuine room in a genuine building, standing empty in a Saxon border town when the location scouts found it. The Görlitz department store opened in 1913 and closed in 2009, and in the gap between those two facts it became one of the best-preserved Art Nouveau department stores anywhere in Europe, largely because nobody ever got around to modernising it.

## The Department Store at the Ostrich

An inn called the Goldener Strauß - the Golden Ostrich - stood on this corner as far back as 1717, and grew into a hotel. The Jewish merchant Louis Friedländer, watching Berlin, wanted something like the great Wertheim store on Leipziger Platz for himself. The Potsdam architect Carl Schmanns drew up plans for what the paperwork actually called "the department store at the ostrich." Down came the hotel in 1912; up went a steel skeleton dressed in an Art Nouveau facade modelled squarely on the Berlin original. Nine months of construction later, on 30 September 1913, the doors opened. Whether or not the name amused the shoppers, the ostrich stuck around in the building's paperwork long after the inn was rubble.

## Four Germanys, One Building

Rudolph Karstadt AG bought the store in 1929, and the Karstadt owners were pushed out after the Second World War. In 1950 the German Democratic Republic took it over and handed operations to the state Handelsorganisation, which folded it into a chain of GDR department stores; in 1984 the HO began carefully restoring the historic facade and interior rooms, an unfashionable act of preservation that is a large part of why the building looks as it does today. Reunification returned it to Karstadt. Then came the corporate churn: into the Karstadt Kompakt division, sold on 1 October 2005 to the British investment group Dawnay Day, rebranded Hertie on 1 March 2007, and on 20 May 2009 slated for closure. Chancellor Angela Merkel announced that June that it had been pulled out of the Hertie insolvency to be sold on its own. The tills fell silent on 15 August 2009. Until they did, every sign in the store was written in German and Polish, and you could pay in euros or złoty - the border is a short walk east.

## Görliwood

Görlitz came through the war almost untouched, which left it with something close to four thousand listed buildings and very little money to keep them up. Then, beginning in 1995, an anonymous benefactor started giving the city roughly half a million a year, on two conditions: that the money go only to facades and monuments, and that nobody ever learn who was sending it. Locals called it the Altstadtmillion. It ran for more than two decades and it worked. What emerged was an unusually intact German town that film crews began treating as a standing set - hence the nickname Görliwood - and it was on one of those scouting trips that Anderson found the vacant store. His team filled in the open galleries of the atrium to read as hotel corridors, with rooms opening off each tier. Ralph Fiennes, Tilda Swinton and Willem Dafoe spent the ten weeks of shooting in early 2013 at the Hotel Börse next door.

## The Long Intermission

In June 2013, months after the crew packed up, an investor bought the building meaning to trade from it again - a Kaufhaus der Oberlausitz, a KaDeO to answer Berlin's KaDeWe. The reopening was announced for 2016, then autumn 2017, then not at all, as the true scale of the restoration became clear. In November 2020 the project stalled outright over two neighbouring buildings the owner wanted demolished for delivery access and the landmark preservation office insisted must stand. The argument has continued through the years since, with preservation authorities and the owner still testing how much original substance a working twenty-first-century store is allowed to cost. Meanwhile the empty building has been broken into and vandalised more than once, a citizens' initiative has campaigned to rescue it, and there was a stretch around 2012 when the entire retail offering of this vast Jugendstil palace was a single beauty shop. The lobby that millions of people have watched on screen is still, for now, waiting for customers.

## Flight Context

The store stands at 51.1528 N, 14.9876 E on Demianplatz in central Görlitz, easternmost city in Germany, hard against the Polish border in Saxony's Upper Lusatia. From 2,000-3,000 ft AGL the old town is unmistakable: a tight grid of red pantile roofs and church towers on the western bank of the Lusatian Neisse, with the modern Polish town of Zgorzelec facing it across the water and the river itself marking the international frontier. The department store sits at the northwestern edge of the historic core beside the Frauenkirche; the twin spires of the Peterskirche above the river make the better navigation fix. The Karkonosze range rises about 40 km south and is the nearest terrain of consequence. Nearest airports are Dresden (EDDC) roughly 85 km west and Wrocław (EPWR) roughly 140 km east. Morning fog forms readily in the Neisse valley; clear afternoons in late spring show the roofscape at its best.
