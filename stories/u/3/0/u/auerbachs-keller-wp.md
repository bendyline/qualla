# Auerbachs Keller

A Leipzig wine cellar where a student named Goethe drank beneath two paintings of a magician riding a barrel out the door - and then wrote the scene into Faust.

**Location:** 51.3394, 12.3754

**Categories:** restaurant, literary history, goethe, leipzig, germany, folklore

## Introduction

Two panels painted on wood in 1625 hung in a Leipzig wine vault, and between them they told a short story. In the first, a magician sits drinking with students. In the second, he is riding a wine barrel out through the doorway, which is not a thing a barrel does without help. A law student named Johann Wolfgang von Goethe drank in that cellar in the 1760s and looked at those pictures often enough that they lodged. Decades later he put the room into Faust, and Auerbachs Keller became the first stop Mephistopheles makes with the soul he has just bought.

## Doctor Auerbach's Vault

There was already wine being poured on this spot in 1438, which is the earliest documentary mention. The man who gave the place its name arrived a century later. Heinrich Stromer (1482-1542) was a professor of medicine, rector of Leipzig University, a city councillor, and personal physician to a remarkable spread of princes - Duke George of Saxony, and the Hohenzollern brothers Joachim I of Brandenburg and Albert of Mainz. Everyone called him Doctor Auerbach after the Bavarian town he came from, in the Upper Palatinate. Around 1530 he built a trade fair complex above the vault, Auerbachs Hof, and reopened the wine cellar underneath it. The bar took his nickname and kept it for the next five hundred years. Leipzig was already one of Europe's great fair cities; merchants came for the trading and stayed for the wine, and by the 16th century this was one of the most important cellars in town.

## The Man on the Barrel

The legend was here before the poet was. Johann Georg Faust was a real man - an itinerant magician and astrologer of the early 1500s whose reputation for dealing with the devil outran him everywhere he went and hardened, after his death, into a story told across Germany. Somebody attached that story to this cellar, and by 1625 a painter had put it on the wall: Faust at the table with a crowd of students, and Faust astride a barrel in the doorway, going out under his own impossible power. The story was popular enough to be staged as a travelling puppet show, Dr. Faust, which turned up at street fairs and which Goethe knew from childhood. So when he sat down here as a student, he was drinking inside a legend that already had furniture and a cast.

## The Favourite Wine Bar

Goethe studied at Leipzig University from 1765 to 1768, and he later called Auerbachs Keller his favourite wine bar. What he did with it in Faust I is not exactly a tribute. In the scene titled Auerbach's Cellar in Leipzig, Mephistopheles brings Faust to a table of drinkers whose idea of a good evening is bad songs and worse jokes. He bores holes in the tabletop, conjures whatever wine each man asks for, and then lets the illusion collapse; the drinkers, seeing grapes where their neighbours' noses are, come close to slicing one another's faces off. It is a portrait of student Leipzig written by a man who had been a student in Leipzig, and the affection in it is entirely ironic. It also made the cellar famous in every language into which Faust was translated, which is most of them.

## Under the Mädlerpassage

The medieval buildings above the vault came down between 1912 and 1914 to make way for the Mädlerpassage, the glass-roofed shopping arcade that now stands at Grimmaische Strasse 2, a few steps east of the market square. The cellar was rebuilt and enlarged in the process and reopened on 22 February 1913. On that same day two bronze groups by the sculptor Mathieu Molitor were set at the entrance - Mephisto and Faust on one side, the bewitched students on the other, the scene lifted straight from the play and planted at the door of the room that inspired it. Five historic dining rooms survive underground: the Fasskeller, or barrel cellar, the Lutherzimmer, the Goethezimmer, Alt-Leipzig, and the Grosser Keller added in 1913, with the Mephisto Bar on the floor above. Since reunification the whole arcade has been comprehensively restored. Auerbachs Keller is the second-oldest restaurant in Leipzig, and a great many of the people who come down its stairs have never read a line of the play that made it famous.

## Flight Context

Auerbachs Keller lies beneath the Mädlerpassage at 51.339°N, 12.375°E, in the compact historic core of Leipzig just east of the Markt - underground, so there is nothing to see from the air but plenty to navigate by. Use the heavy stone tower of the Neues Rathaus and the steep dark roof and single spire of the Thomaskirche a few hundred metres west; the ring road traces the line of the vanished city walls in a clear oval around the whole old town. Leipzig/Halle (EDDP) is about 15 km northwest, with Leipzig-Altenburg (EDAC) further south. Flat terrain in every direction, best light for picking out the old town in the low sun of late afternoon.
