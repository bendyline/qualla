# Richard Wagner Memorial

Wagner stands short and unimposing on a marble pedestal in his birthplace, with the older composer behind him as a black bronze shadow.

**Location:** 51.3425, 12.3704

**Categories:** leipzig, germany, memorial, music, wagner, art

## Introduction

Stephan Balkenhol made the bronze figure 1.8 meters tall, only slightly taller than Wagner himself, who stood 1.66 meters. The composer is shown young, in everyday clothes, not posed, not pointing, not draped in operatic significance. Behind him rises a four-meter-tall black bronze cutout of his older silhouette like a shadow he could never quite outwalk. The whole memorial sits at the foot of a marble pedestal designed a century earlier by Max Klinger, with three Rhinemaidens carved naked across the front. It was unveiled in Leipzig on May 22, 2013, the composer's 200th birthday, and the public had opinions. Some applauded. Others did not. Either response was, in a way, exactly what Balkenhol wanted, because Wagner without ambivalence is not really Wagner.

## The pedestal that waited a century

Max Klinger was Leipzig's most famous artist when the city asked him in 1903 to design a Wagner monument. By 1911 he had a plan: a two-meter marble cube carved with figures from Wagner's operas, topped by a five-meter standing statue of the composer. The cornerstone was laid on Wagner's hundredth birthday in 1913. Then the First World War broke out, the marble blocks Klinger had ordered from Laas in South Tyrol stayed half-finished in their quarry, and Klinger died in 1920 before he could complete the work. Only the pedestal eventually arrived in Leipzig, in 1924. The city set it up in the southern Palmengarten, where it stood for more than eighty years. Locals nicknamed it the porn cube because of the naked Rhinemaidens. The actual statue was never made.

## The Nazi monument that almost happened

In 1932, with Wagner's fiftieth death anniversary approaching, Leipzig started over. The sculptor Emil Hipp won a new design competition. On March 6, 1934, Adolf Hitler personally laid the cornerstone of what was now branded the Richard Wagner National Monument near the Elster basin. Hitler was a Wagner obsessive; the music had been the soundtrack of his political imagination since his Vienna years. Hipp worked on the friezes and figures until 1944, but the war ended before they could be installed in Leipzig. After 1945 the city refused to take delivery, even though it had paid for the work, because the project's Nazi associations had become impossible to scrub off. Hipp's pieces went into storage. The park around them, the Richard-Wagner-Hain, kept its name. The monument never came.

## Balkenhol's reply

By the early 2000s a group called the Wagner Memorial Association set about completing the unfinished Klinger pedestal at last. Nine artists submitted designs. In 2011 the jury chose Stephan Balkenhol, a sculptor from Kassel known for refusing the heroic gesture. Balkenhol funded most of the work himself by selling twenty-five small bronze replicas of the monument. He rebuilt the staircase that Klinger had originally specified, set the marble pedestal at the top, and placed his short bronze Wagner on it, with the older composer behind in silhouette. Balkenhol called the design a paraphrase of Klinger. He also called it deliberately anti-monumental. Wagner gets a human dimension here, no exaggeration, no pathos, just a short man on a pedestal, with the weight of his later self looming behind. Behind that, today, stands an office building once used by the Stasi.

## The shadow behind the figure

Wagner is one of the most consequential composers in Western music. He invented the leitmotif. He pushed the orchestra to sizes that had not been imagined. The Ring cycle, Tristan und Isolde, Parsifal, his work redrew what opera could be and dragged late Romanticism into something darker and stranger. He was also, by his own writing, a virulent anti-Semite. His 1850 essay Das Judenthum in der Musik attacked Jewish composers on essentially racial grounds and was reissued under his own name in 1869. Hitler considered him the spiritual godfather of the Nazi project; the Bayreuth Festival under Winifred Wagner became a regular Nazi pilgrimage site. None of this disqualifies the music, which is what it is. None of it is erased by the music either. Balkenhol's monument leaves the question open. The young Wagner stands forward in color. The shadow he became stands behind in black.

## Flight Context

The memorial sits at 51.34 N, 12.37 E on the Goerdelerring on the northwest edge of Leipzig's old town center, part of the Promenadenring that loops the historic core. Leipzig/Halle Airport (EDDP) is 18 km north. The memorial itself is too small to see from altitude, but it sits within the green ring road that is highly visible from the air, encircling the dense Leipzig city center.
