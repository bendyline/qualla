# Bogensee

A small forest lake north of Berlin that the city cannot give away, because on its shore stand Joseph Goebbels's country house and the Stalinist campus East Germany built to train its youth cadres.

**Location:** 52.7730, 13.5308

**Categories:** Lakes of Brandenburg, Wandlitz, Barnim, Joseph Goebbels, Nazi architecture, East Germany, Havel basin

## Introduction

You could walk around the Bogensee in half an hour. It is a small oval of meltwater on the Barnim plateau north of Berlin, roughly three hundred metres long and a hundred and eighty across, nowhere deeper than about three metres, fed by spring-fed meadows along its western edge. There are no houses on the bank, no beach, no path along the water. Anglers take carp, pike, perch and tench out of it. The lake belongs to the state of Berlin, which is where the trouble begins, because Berlin also owns what stands a few hundred metres up the slope: the country house it built for Joseph Goebbels, and behind it a Stalinist campus where East Germany trained its youth cadres. The city has spent years trying to give the whole thing away.

## A Puddle Left by the Ice

The lake is a relic of the last glaciation, one of thousands of shallow basins the retreating ice scraped into the sandy Barnim upland and then filled. It sits inside the Barnim Nature Park, between the villages of Wandlitz, Prenden and Klosterfelde, about fifteen kilometres beyond the Berlin city boundary. The surrounding land came to the city in 1919, when Berlin bought up the estates of the Lanke manor, and that purchase is the reason a lake in Brandenburg is the property of a city in another state. Ecologically it is unremarkable and, precisely because nothing was ever built on its banks, quietly intact: reeds, alder carr, a nature trail with information boards, and forest in every direction.

## A Birthday Present of 496 Hectares

In 1936 the Berlin city administration made Goebbels a present for his thirty-ninth birthday: lifetime use of the Bogensee and 496 hectares of forest around it. A blockhouse came first. Then, in 1939, the architects Juergen and Heinrich Schweitzer, working with Hugo Constantin Bartels, put up a main house of 1,600 square metres on a natural stone base - thirty private rooms, forty service rooms, a hundred-square-metre cinema, sixty telephones, its own waterworks and treatment plant, air conditioning, a guest house with quarters for the SS, and from 1944 a bunker of its own. UFA, the film company his ministry controlled, contributed 1.5 million Reichsmarks toward the cost. Film stars came out for the weekend: Zarah Leander, Emil Jannings, Heinz Ruehmann. The house has since acquired a tabloid reputation as the place Goebbels entertained actresses whose careers depended on him. The plainer description is that the man in charge of Nazi propaganda had a private cinema in the woods, paid for by the industry he controlled.

## Eighteen Language Booths

The Soviet Military Administration took the property in 1945, and in April 1946 the Free German Youth opened a school there; in 1950 it was named for Wilhelm Pieck. From 1951 Hermann Henselmann, later East Berlin's chief architect, designed a lecture building and dormitories in the heavy classical manner the state then favoured, and a second phase after 1980 added a sports hall and more accommodation. The main auditorium was fitted with the second-largest simultaneous-interpretation system in East Germany: eighteen language booths and five hundred and sixty seats, because by the 1970s the students being trained here came from Chile, Greece, Grenada and Denmark as well as from the socialist bloc, and West German communists came too. For forty-four years this quiet lake in the pines was where the East German youth movement made its functionaries. The school closed in 1990.

## Free to a Good Home

After that, tenants came and went - an international social work association from 1991 to 1999, then Berlin's forestry administration - and since about 2005 most of the buildings have stood empty and deteriorating. They are listed monuments, so knocking them down is neither simple nor cheap; the demolition figure quoted publicly runs to around fifty million euros, while simply keeping the site standing costs the city on the order of a quarter of a million euros a year. In May 2024 Berlin's finance senator, Stefan Evers, offered the property to anyone prepared to take on its restoration, for nothing. Applicants are vetted, because the one outcome the city will not tolerate is the complex passing to the far right. Earlier that year a false story circulated on social media claiming that Volodymyr Zelensky had bought the place; he had not. A forest school still runs in one of the side buildings, taking children out among the trees, which is the only part of the estate doing anything useful at all.

## Flight Context

The Bogensee lies at 52.773 N, 13.531 E in the Barnim Nature Park, about 15 km north of the Berlin city boundary and 45 km north of Berlin Brandenburg Airport. From 1,500-2,500 ft AGL it reads as a small dark oval in continuous pine and mixed forest, with the pale rectangular blocks of the Henselmann campus and the low roof of the Goebbels house immediately northwest of the shore - a distinctive cluster of large buildings in a landscape with nothing else built in it. Wandlitz and its lakes lie a few kilometres west, the Autobahn A11 runs east. Eberswalde-Finow (EDAV) is about 12 km northeast, Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) about 45 km south, and Schoenhagen (EDAZ) about 60 km southwest.
