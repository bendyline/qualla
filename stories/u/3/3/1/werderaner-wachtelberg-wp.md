# Werderaner Wachtelberg

The vines on this glacial hill above the Havel froze to death in the winter of 1955/56 and stayed dead for nearly thirty years - until a Brandenburg town decided to plant 17,200 of them again.

**Location:** 52.3697, 12.9333

**Categories:** Vineyards of Germany, Hills of Brandenburg, Wine, Brandenburg, Agriculture, Germany

## Introduction

The winter of 1955/56 killed the last of them. Frost had been finishing off Brandenburg vineyards on and off for centuries - the freeze of 1739/40 held on into June - but this one ended the argument, and the vines on the Wachtelberg above Werder died and were not replaced. The hill sat bare for close to thirty years. What makes that unremarkable-sounding fact worth a story is where the hill is: 52 degrees and 22 minutes north, well beyond the latitude at which European wine-growing is supposed to make any sense. Somebody planted grapes here anyway, in the Middle Ages. And in 1984, somebody decided to do it again.

## A Moraine With a Microclimate

The Wachtelberg - quail's mountain - is a 60-metre rise on the east bank of the Havel, and it is there because of ice. The ridge is what remains of a terminal moraine dumped during the Weichselian glaciation of the last ice age, a pile of debris left behind when the ice sheet finally stopped advancing. The soil it left is slightly argillaceous sand, and sandy ground makes mild wines with low acidity. The other gift is water. Lakes surround the hill on almost every side - the Schwielowsee, the Großer Zernsee, the Glindower See - and that much standing water holds heat, which is why the ground here often measures slightly warmer than the German average. Precipitation is modest, 519 mm in a year. Extremes are rare. It is not the Moselle, but it is not nothing.

## The Monks Brought It North

Viticulture spread west to east across Europe through the Middle Ages, carried along with Frankish and German expansion and with the Church. Wine was needed for communion, so monasteries sponsored it, and around the Havel the Cistercians were decisive. That is the origin of the Werder vineyards. By the end of the thirteenth century the wine of the Margraviate of Brandenburg was among the region's more important exports to eastern and northern Europe, and the Hohenzollerns, arriving from Franconia, made it part of the court - drunk in the Great Elector's residence until his death. Elector Johann Georg founded the first Wine Master Order of Brandenburg in 1598, and it was said around that time that the Havel might one day have vineyards like the Moselle's. The Wachtelberg is the oldest wine-growing location outside the island town itself.

## Seventeen Thousand Two Hundred Vines

The replanting began in 1984, when the town of Werder decided to bring 4.7 hectares of fallow hillside back into use and reconnect with a tradition it had let lapse. Between 1984 and 1987, 17,200 grafted Müller-Thurgau vines went into the ground, sourced from Hungary and from Saxony, held up by 3,500 concrete posts. It did not go smoothly. Downy mildew hit the young plantings, and selling Brandenburg wine turned out to be its own problem. In January 1996 an agricultural consultant, Dr M. Lindicke, took over as tenant. Trucking grapes to Saxony-Anhalt for pressing cost real quality, so he built a winery of his own at the Plessower Eck on the B1; it has run since 2012, and the fruit is now processed the same day it comes off the hill.

## Registered as 11.1.5.-017

In December 1991 an institute at Bad Kreuznach assessed the site and found it schutzwürdig - worth protecting. The Wachtelberg was affiliated to the Saale-Unstrut wine region as a großlagenfreie Einzellage, a single vineyard site belonging to no larger collective, recognised by the EU and filed under the registration number 11.1.5.-017. For three decades that made it one of the most northerly registered locations for QbA quality wine in Germany, a distinction that survived until the German wine law was revised in 2021. What grows there now is a working spread rather than a monoculture: Müller-Thurgau on 2.46 hectares, Regent on 1.82, Dornfelder on 0.68, Sauvignon Blanc on 0.44, Saphira on 0.25, plus a 0.3-hectare parcel of 1,000 Solaris vines added by 2019, and teaching rows planted for the Bundesgartenschau in Potsdam where dozens of red and white varieties are grown side by side for people to look at.

## Flight Context

The Wachtelberg lies at 52.370 N, 12.933 E, a 60-metre moraine ridge on the east bank of the Havel immediately southeast of Werder's island town. From 1,500-3,000 ft the hill is the only relief in an otherwise table-flat landscape, and the vineyard reads clearly in the growing season as a striped green rectangle of trellis rows on the south-facing slope, distinct from the surrounding orchard blocks. The Großer Plessower See sits directly south, the Havel and Großer Zernsee north and west, the Schwielowsee to the east - the ring of water is the best navigational cue. Potsdam is 9 km east, Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) 39 km east-southeast, and Schönhagen (EDAZ) about 25 km southeast. Berlin's controlled airspace lies east of Potsdam. Best light for the slope is late afternoon, when the terraced rows throw shadow and the hill separates from the flat ground around it.
