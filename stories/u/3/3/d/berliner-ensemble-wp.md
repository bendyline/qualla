# Berliner Ensemble

Brecht's greatest hit premiered in this theatre in 1928. He did not get the keys to it for another twenty-six years.

**Location:** 52.5217, 13.3861

**Categories:** Berlin, Theatre, Literature, Cold War, Culture, Germany

## Introduction

On 31 August 1928, a neo-baroque house on a bend of the Spree staged the first performance of Die Dreigroschenoper. Bertolt Brecht was thirty, the theatre was thirty-five, and neither of them had any claim on the other. The Threepenny Opera made him famous, and then the decade turned and he spent fifteen years in exile — Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Santa Monica, a chair in front of the House Un-American Activities Committee. When he came back to Berlin in 1948, the Theater am Schiffbauerdamm had been standing through the whole thing. It took him another six years to move in.

## A Company Without a House

Helene Weigel and Brecht founded the Berliner Ensemble in January 1949 in East Berlin. Weigel was the actress; she was also, from the beginning, the one who ran the thing. Their first public production, Mother Courage and Her Children, opened on 11 January 1949 at the Deutsches Theater, where Wolfgang Langhoff took the new company in as a guest.

They stayed a guest for five years. Not until 19 March 1954 did the ensemble play in a house of its own — Heinrich Seeling's Theater am Schiffbauerdamm, opened in 1892 as the Neues Theater, the same stage where Threepenny had gone up twenty-six years earlier. Brecht wrote no new plays for the company he founded. He remounted old ones instead, directing The Caucasian Chalk Circle and, with Erich Engel, Life of Galileo. He had two more years to work. He died in 1956; Weigel ran the ensemble until her own death in 1971.

## Eight Hundred Photographs

What made the Berliner Ensemble a method rather than a repertory was the rehearsal process, which ran for months where other companies took weeks, and the paperwork that came out of it. Every production was recorded in a Modellbuch — a preview album holding six to eight hundred photographs of the staging, moment by moment. Hainer Hill was among the photographers. The idea was that a production could be transmitted, argued with, and rebuilt somewhere else by people who had never seen it.

Brecht's collaborators are the reason the archive is worth anything: the designers Caspar Neher and Karl von Appen, the composers Paul Dessau and Hanns Eisler, the dramaturge Elisabeth Hauptmann. His students Benno Besson, Egon Monk, Peter Palitzsch and Manfred Wekwerth were handed his unstaged plays to direct. After his death the ensemble gave first performances to The Resistible Rise of Arturo Ui, Schweik in the Second World War and The Visions of Simone Machard.

## Five Directors, One Theatre

Ruth Berghaus took over in 1971 and opened the repertoire out to the rest of European drama; Manfred Wekwerth followed in 1977. Then the country the ensemble had been founded in stopped existing.

In 1992 the Berlin Senate tried to solve the succession by appointing five artistic directors at once: Peter Zadek, Peter Palitzsch, Heiner Müller, Fritz Marquardt and Matthias Langhoff. The theatre was converted from a state institution into a limited company, privatised in 1993, and kept on public subsidy. The five-headed arrangement collapsed by 1995, leaving Müller alone. The work did not suffer for it. Müller's staging of Arturo Ui, with Martin Wuttke in the title role, became one of the most successful productions in the company's history. Müller died in December 1995. Robert Wilson premiered Brecht's The Flight across the Ocean here in 1998 for the centenary of his birth, and on 30 April 1999 the curtain came down on Müller's Die Bauern with the ensemble's future still unsettled.

## Ernst Busch's Building

Claus Peymann arrived from the Burgtheater in Vienna with a reputation for provocation and a full order book, and reopened the renovated theatre in January 2000. He held the job until 2017, when Oliver Reese took over for the 2017/18 season and still has it.

The house is worth reading through its actors as much as its directors. Ernst Busch, the singer who made Brecht and Eisler's songs into something the audience could carry home. Therese Giehse, who had created Mother Courage in Zurich during the war. Curt Bois, Erwin Geschonneck, Gisela May, Angelica Domröse, Jürgen Holtz. And Weigel herself, whose Mother Courage — the mouth open, no sound coming out of it — is the image most people picture when they picture Brecht at all.

## Two Kilometres North

Brecht and Weigel spent their last years at Chausseestrasse 125, about two kilometres north of the theatre. Next door, at number 126, is the entrance to the Dorotheenstadt cemetery, where Hegel and Fichte are buried. Brecht wrote his last plays with a philosophers' graveyard on the other side of the wall.

He is buried there now, and Weigel with him. The theatre they built between them still programmes Brecht, still programmes Müller, and still puts on the work that quarrels with both — a three-part inheritance the company has summarised for years in a single line: Brecht, Müller, Shakespeare.

## Flight Context

The Theater am Schiffbauerdamm stands at 52.522°N 13.386°E, on the north bank of the Spree at the Schiffbauerdamm, immediately west of Friedrichstrasse station. From 2,000–3,000 ft AGL the reference chain is short and unambiguous: find the Fernsehturm at Alexanderplatz, run about 2 km west along the Spree past Museum Island, and the river makes a distinct kink where the Friedrichstrasse rail bridge crosses it. The theatre is the pitched-roof block on the north bank just west of that bridge, with the Reichstag and its glass dome another 900 m west and the Hauptbahnhof roof beyond. Brecht's last flat and the Dorotheenstadt cemetery lie about 2 km north-northwest on Chausseestrasse, an unusual patch of green in an otherwise solid grid. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) is roughly 20 km south-southeast; Strausberg (EDAY) is the nearest general-aviation field east, Schönhagen (EDAZ) southwest. The government quarter's restricted airspace begins immediately west of here and the whole area sits under the Berlin CTR and TMA — transit under clearance. Low stratus is common from November through February; long summer evenings give the best light along the river.
