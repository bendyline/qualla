# St. Michael's Church, Berlin

A Venetian-inspired garrison church on the Engelbecken that lost its roof in one night of 1945, was cut in half by the Berlin Wall, and now has a garden where its nave used to be.

**Location:** 52.5072, 13.4194

**Categories:** Berlin, Churches, Mitte, World War II, Berlin Wall, Architecture

## Introduction

You go in through the side. The eastern transept door is the main entrance now, because the church stops halfway: walk toward where the nave should be and there is sky, and under the sky a garden. St Michael's lost its roof on the night of 3 February 1945, when over 950 American aircraft came over the Luisenstadt and left almost nothing of the quarter standing. The outer walls held, and so did the domed tower and the Annunciation mosaic above the portal. What burned out was the middle — the organ, the furnishings, the roof — and eight decades later the middle is still open to the weather. From the front door you can now look through the portal window straight up at the dome, a view its architect never drew.

## Venice, Reconstructed in Brandenburg Brick

August Soller spent five months travelling through Italy in 1845 and came home with the church already half-decided. The filigree angels across the front are lifted from San Giorgio Maggiore; the plan, with its three apses and long body, owes most to San Salvador. Frederick William IV — a Protestant king approving only the second Catholic church built in Berlin since the Reformation — wanted it primarily as a garrison church, somewhere for Catholic soldiers to go that would take the pressure off St Hedwig's. Soller dropped his original twin Gothic towers, which left the building without a silhouette, and solved that with a domed tower over the crossing rising more than fifty-six metres. The foundation stone went down on 14 July 1851 with the king and his family watching and several thousand Berliners lining the banks of the Engelbecken. Building ran ten years. The Bishop of Breslau consecrated it on 28 October 1861, in the presence of King William I of Prussia.

## The Michaelites

A garrison area for 3,000 Catholic soldiers was set up straight after the consecration, but the district filled in fast around it and the church became something else. The parish had 6,000 members when it was founded in 1888 and nearly 20,000 Catholics by 1900 — they called themselves Michaelites — packed into the tenements of a quarter that had been open wasteland when the foundation stone was laid. It was a hard place. Unemployment brought large protests and rioting on 26 February 1892, and parishioners organised a relief society in response. Marist sisters arrived from Breslau in 1888 and opened the Marienstift in 1909: a kindergarten, mobile nursing, rooms for domestic servants, all of it running until 1995. Maximilian Kaller, who ran the parish's social work from 1917 to 1926, later stood against the Nazis. So did Bernhard Lichtenberg, provost of St Hedwig's Cathedral a few streets away — the church St Michael's had originally been built to take pressure off. After the November 1938 pogrom Lichtenberg prayed publicly for persecuted Jews at Vespers until his arrest on 23 October 1941; he died on 5 November 1943 while being transported to Dachau.

## The Swans Win

The Luisenstadt Canal was filled in during 1926, and the city proposed turning the Engelbecken — the Angels' Pool, named for the church's patron — into a public swimming bath. Catholic Berlin was furious. With the Centre Party's help the plan was blocked in the Prussian Landtag, and the basin became a pond with swans on it and green space around. It is a small municipal squabble that turned out to matter enormously: the pond and its park preserved the long open sightline south, so that the church could still be seen the way Soller intended it, water in front and dome behind.

## A Line Through the Parish

Services moved into the Marienstift after the raid; under Franz Kusche the apse, sacristy and transept were rebuilt and worship returned to the church in 1953, followed by three new bells in 1957 and a Sauer organ in 1960. Then the Wall went up in 1961, and it ran along the old canal. St Michael's stood on the eastern side. West Berlin's half of the parish built its own church on Waldemarstraße, designed by Rudolf Schwarz in the last year of his life and intended to serve one day as a parish hall once the city was whole again. The two halves grew apart — the western congregation young and Kreuzberg-facing by the 1980s, the eastern one keeping to the older liturgy. From Oranienplatz, no one could see the church at all for twenty-nine years, so the artist Yadegar Asisi painted its missing lower half onto the western face of the Wall itself, trompe-l'œil, to make the concrete look transparent. Segments of that painting were bought at auction in Monte Carlo in 1990 and installed in the Vatican Gardens in 1994.

## Roofless, Listed, Still Open

The building was listed in 1978 and its copper dome renewed between 1978 and 1980. After the Wall fell the bell tower was refurbished and the statue of Michael — a replica of a figure by the sculptor August Kiß — went back up between 1991 and 1993; the Annunciation mosaic was restored in 1999. The parish itself did not survive. By 2003 it was down to 800 members, and on 31 October that year the archbishop merged it into the parish of St Hedwig's Cathedral. Two years later a plan to fill the roofless nave with a Centre Against Expulsions was announced and then cancelled within the month, for lack of agreement in the congregation. So the nave stays a garden, the transept stays a church, and the view across the Engelbecken — closed off in 1961, reopened in 1990 — is once again the one Soller drew.

## Flight Context

St Michael's stands at 52.507°N, 13.419°E on Michaelkirchplatz, on the Mitte side of the boundary with Kreuzberg. The navigation cue is water: the Engelbecken, a rectangular pond in a formal park, sits immediately south of the church, with the Spree curving past 400 m to the north-east. The copper dome over the crossing rises more than 56 m and reads green against red brick; the roofless nave shows from above as an open rectangle of garden attached to an intact transept, which makes the building unmistakable once you have seen it. From 1,500–2,500 ft the line of the former Luisenstadt Canal — and with it the line of the Berlin Wall — is legible as a strip of park running south-west from the church. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) is about 17 km south-south-east; Schönhagen (EDAZ) roughly 37 km south-west. Mitte lies inside EDDB controlled airspace. Haze and low cloud are common November to February.
