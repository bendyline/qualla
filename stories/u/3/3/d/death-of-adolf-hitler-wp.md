# Death of Adolf Hitler

Hitler shot himself in a bunker study on 30 April 1945; the only part of his body ever recovered was his teeth, and the confusion Stalin deliberately sowed about the rest kept a fringe industry busy for eighty years.

**Location:** 52.5125, 13.3810

**Categories:** World War II, History of Berlin, 1945 in Germany, Forensic history, Cold War

## Introduction

By the last days of April 1945, Adolf Hitler no longer trusted the poison he intended to kill himself with. Heinrich Himmler had supplied the cyanide capsules, and on 28 April a BBC bulletin told the bunker that Himmler had been quietly offering the Western Allies a surrender. If the man was a traitor, what was in the ampoules? On the afternoon of 29 April, Hitler ordered his physician to test one on Blondi, his German shepherd. The dog died instantly. The next afternoon Hitler went into his study with Eva Braun, and the argument about what happened in that room has never entirely stopped.

## The Arithmetic Runs Out

He had moved into the Führerbunker on 16 January and stayed as the map shrank around him. Soviet artillery reached Berlin for the first time on 20 April, his fifty-sixth birthday, and Red Army tanks were at the outskirts by the evening of the twenty-first. When a counterattack he had ordered from an SS detachment under Felix Steiner failed to materialise, Hitler collapsed into a tirade against his generals and said aloud, for the first time, that the war was lost and that he would shoot himself in Berlin. Göring's telegram asking to assume leadership was read as a coup and answered with an arrest order. Himmler's negotiations produced the same result, and his SS liaison in the bunker, Hermann Fegelein, was shot for desertion. Shortly after midnight on 29 April, in a map room, Hitler married Eva Braun. Then he dictated his last will and testament to his secretary Traudl Junge and signed it at four in the morning.

## The Study

Around half past two on the morning of 30 April, roughly twenty people, most of them women who worked in the bunker, lined up in a corridor to say goodbye. He went down the row shaking hands. Later that morning General Helmuth Weidling, commanding the city's defence, told him the garrison would be out of ammunition by nightfall; permission for a breakout came at about one o'clock. Hitler ate lunch, made a second round of farewells, and at about half past two went into his private study with Braun. His adjutant Otto Günsche stood outside the door. When Heinz Linge, his valet, and Martin Bormann entered some time afterwards, they found the two of them on a sofa, Hitler slumped to his right with blood running from his temple and pooling on the rug, a 7.65 mm Walther pistol on the floor at his feet. Braun had no visible wound. Her body smelled of bitter almonds; his smelled of gunpowder.

## Petrol in the Chancellery Garden

His instructions had been explicit, and they were followed within minutes. The bodies were carried up the emergency stairs into the Reich Chancellery garden while Soviet shells came down across the grounds. The first attempt to light the petrol failed, so Linge fetched a thick roll of papers, Bormann set them alight and threw them onto the corpses, and the men standing in the bunker doorway raised their arms. SS guards brought more fuel through the afternoon. Somewhere around half past six, what was left — described by witnesses as somewhere between charred bone and ash that fell apart when touched — was covered over in a shallow bomb crater. The blood-soaked rug was burned separately. Shelling and incendiary fire continued over the garden until 2 May, which is why nobody went back to look.

## A Jawbone and Some Teeth

General Hans Krebs carried the news to Vasily Chuikov just before four on the morning of 1 May, hoping to trade it for a ceasefire. Stalin was told minutes later and did not believe it. He ordered SMERSH, the Red Army's counterintelligence arm, to produce a corpse. What they found in the soil in early May were dental remains, and those were enough: on 11 May, Käthe Heusermann, who had assisted Hitler's dentist Hugo Blaschke, and the technician Fritz Echtmann identified the bridgework from memory. Their reward for being right was years in Soviet prisons. The teeth remain the only part of Hitler's body ever confirmed to have been recovered. In 2017 the French forensic pathologist Philippe Charlier examined them in the Russian archives and found them in perfect agreement with X-rays taken in 1944, down to plant fibres in the tartar consistent with a vegetarian diet.

## The Fog Stalin Made

On 5 June 1945 the Soviets announced that the body had been examined and that Hitler had died of cyanide. Four days later they said they had not identified any body and he had probably escaped. Asked in July how Hitler died, Stalin replied that he was living in Spain or Argentina. This was not incompetence but policy, and it worked. British intelligence sent Hugh Trevor-Roper to establish the facts; his 1947 book settled the question for historians and for nobody else. The FBI and CIA chased survival claims into the mid-1950s, gave credence to none, and kept the files classified for decades, which fed the theories rather than starving them. A West German court in Berchtesgaden interviewed forty-two witnesses separately over four years and issued a death certificate on 25 February 1956, with fifteen hundred pages attached. A skull fragment the Soviets long believed was Hitler's was DNA-tested in 2009 and turned out to be a woman's. In 2025, blood from the study sofa was matched to a living paternal relative and confirmed as his. The evidence has been consistent for eighty years. The stories persist anyway, which was rather the point.

## Flight Context

The Führerbunker site lies at 52.5125°N, 13.381°E, beneath an unremarkable car park off In den Ministergärten in Mitte, marked only by an information board. From the air it is easiest found by triangulation: the Brandenburg Gate sits about 400 m northwest, the grey grid of the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe about 200 m north, and Potsdamer Platz's cluster of towers roughly 500 m southwest. The green rectangle of the Tiergarten is the largest landmark west of the whole area. Nothing of the bunker is visible; view from 1,500–3,000 ft for context rather than detail. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) lies about 24 km southeast.
