# My God, Help Me to Survive This Deadly Love

Dmitri Vrubel painted two ageing communists kissing on the east side of the Berlin Wall, signed away every right to the image, and watched it become one of the most reproduced paintings on earth.

**Location:** 52.5079, 13.4352

**Categories:** Berlin Wall, Street art, Cold War, History of Berlin, Public art, Political satire

## Introduction

Dmitri Vrubel wanted permission to paint on the eastern face of the Berlin Wall, so he asked the East German Ministry of National Defence, which informed him that the Wall was not its responsibility. What he found instead was a young Scottish woman selling permits of dubious provenance. He signed her contract, which surrendered every right he had to whatever he painted, and then he painted Leonid Brezhnev kissing Erich Honecker on the mouth. The image went around the world. Vrubel owned none of it.

## Seven October, 1979

The kiss was not invented. It happened in East Berlin on 7 October 1979, during the celebrations for the German Democratic Republic's thirtieth anniversary, and the French photographer Régis Bossu was standing in the right place. Brezhnev had come to mark the occasion and to seal a deal: two days earlier the two states had signed a ten-year agreement of mutual support, under which East Germany would send ships, machinery and chemical equipment east while the Soviet Union sent back fuel and nuclear technology. The socialist fraternal kiss was ordinary diplomatic choreography in that world, a greeting exchanged between men who ran police states and understood each other as brothers. Bossu's photograph made it look like something else entirely. It was reprinted everywhere, and the reason is not hard to identify: the two faces are absolutely committed, and absolutely unaware of how they look.

## The Side Nobody Could Reach

For twenty-eight years, Berlin Wall graffiti was a purely Western art form. The painted side was the side you could walk up to. The eastern face belonged to the death strip, patrolled and floodlit, and no citizen of the GDR was getting close enough to it with a spray can. That changed in November 1989, and by 1990 a stretch of the eastern face 1,316 metres long in Friedrichshain had become the largest open-air gallery in the world, with more than a hundred artists from twenty-one countries working along it. Vrubel, a Russian painter who had moved to Berlin at thirty, took a section of it and reproduced Bossu's photograph at monumental scale. Above and below the figures he lettered the line that gave the work its name, in Russian and in German: my God, help me to survive this deadly love.

## A Perfect Fit

Critics complained at the time that it was hardly a painting at all — just a photograph copied larger. Later assessments were kinder; the historians Anthony Read and David Fisher called it 'particularly striking, with a sharp, satirical edge.' Vrubel himself explained the logic in a 2014 interview with disarming plainness. 'In this painting, there's one German and one Russian,' he said, 'and the Berlin Wall is about the same thing but in reverse: here, there's total love, while the Berlin Wall separates two worlds — it was a perfect fit.' He had been hoping for a 'wow' from passers-by. He did not expect what he got, which was the most photographed section of concrete in Germany and a phrase that people who have never heard of Honecker can still finish.

## Erased and Repainted

Weather is unkind to acrylic on concrete. By 2009 the East Side Gallery had faded and crumbled badly enough that Berlin decided to strip the surviving works and invite the original artists back to redo them in more durable paint. In March the mural was scrubbed off the Wall entirely, which produced a small international outcry and headlines about the kiss of death. Vrubel was commissioned to paint it again, was paid three thousand euros, and gave the money to a social art project in Marzahn. That June he and Régis Bossu finally met, and were photographed on 16 June 2009 holding reproductions of the photograph and the painting made from it. Vrubel died in Berlin on 13 August 2022, aged sixty-two, of complications from COVID-19.

## The Pose Keeps Coming Back

An image that legible does not stay put. In 2016 a mural in Vilnius put Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump into the same embrace under the title Make Everything Great Again; the same year, in Bristol, a wall carried Trump and Boris Johnson mid-kiss in the weeks before the Brexit referendum. Neither is subtle, and neither needs to be — the grammar was established on a slab of the Berlin Wall, and every version since borrows it. The original still stands in Friedrichshain, on the Spree side of Mühlenstraße, where the tour groups stop and someone always tries to photograph it without anyone else in the frame. Nobody manages.

## Flight Context

The mural sits at 52.5079°N, 13.4352°E, on the East Side Gallery along Mühlenstraße in Friedrichshain, on the north bank of the Spree between the Oberbaumbrücke and Ostbahnhof. From the air, look for the double-tower Oberbaum Bridge crossing the Spree and follow the river northwest — the gallery is the long pale line running parallel to the water. The Mercedes-Benz Arena's grey drum sits immediately across the street. Low and slow works best here: 1,000–2,500 ft on a Spree-following track. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) lies about 17 km southeast. The Fernsehturm, 3 km northwest, is the all-weather reference point for central Berlin.
