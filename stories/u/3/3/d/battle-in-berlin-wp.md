# Battle in Berlin

Once the Red Army crossed Berlin's outer ring on 23 April 1945, the war became a street-by-street struggle through the German capital, fought by exhausted Soviet veterans and by Hitler Youth boys handed Panzerfausts.

**Location:** 52.5186, 13.3761

**Categories:** Battles and operations of World War II, 1945 in Germany, History of Berlin, Urban warfare

## Introduction

On 23 April 1945, Soviet ground forces pushed through the outer suburbs and the war crossed Berlin's last imaginary line. The Battle of Berlin, the strategic operation, had effectively been decided days earlier on the Seelow Heights and in the encirclement at Halbe. What was left was the Battle in Berlin, the battle inside the city: nine days of close-quarters fighting through streets that the soldiers from the steppes had never seen and the residents could no longer recognise. By 27 April, Berlin was completely cut off. By 30 April, Hitler was dead in his bunker. By 2 May, General Helmuth Weidling had surrendered the Berlin Defence Area to Vasily Chuikov, commander of the Soviet 8th Guards Army. In between, an entire generation of conscripts and adolescents fought a battle most of them knew was already lost.

## Boys with Panzerfausts

The German garrison inside the city was a desperate composite. Regular Wehrmacht units had been ground down before they reached the suburbs; what remained was reinforced by Volkssturm, the civilian militia drafted in the war's last months and armed sometimes with single-shot weapons from the previous century, and by Hitler Youth. The youngest of the Hitler Youth defenders were fourteen, some even younger, given Panzerfaust anti-tank weapons and told to wait in stairwells and basements for Soviet tanks. Many died in the first ambush they tried. Others were killed by the second wave of Soviet infantry that followed every armoured push. They had been raised inside a regime that idolised them; they were now being expended by it. To frame them as combatants in any meaningful sense is to grant the Nazi state a moral legitimacy it never had. They were children put in front of tanks by adults who knew the war was over.

## The streets and the U-Bahn

Soviet doctrine for taking a fortified city was straightforward and brutal. Storm groups built around tanks, sappers and submachine-gunners would clear a street, building by building. Air defence guns, lowered to fire horizontally, would break holes in walls. Sewers and the U-Bahn became their own theatre of combat: SS units used the tunnels to outflank Soviet infantry; Soviet sappers flooded sections of the U-Bahn, drowning soldiers and the civilians who had taken shelter in them, the precise number of dead in the tunnels never satisfactorily established. Berliners themselves, hundreds of thousands of them, were in cellars without water and electricity, listening to the city collapse above their heads. The wounded waited in basements that had been converted to first-aid stations; the dead were sometimes buried in courtyards because the streets were too dangerous.

## The bunker and the surrender

Two weeks of the operation came down to a few hundred metres in the centre. Soviet troops fought through the Tiergarten and toward the Reichstag, which fell in pieces over the night of 30 April, the same day Hitler killed himself in the Führerbunker. Joseph Goebbels and his wife Magda murdered their six children in the bunker on 1 May before taking their own lives. General Weidling had assumed command of the Berlin defence on 23 April; on 2 May he ordered the city's garrison to lay down arms and surrendered to Lieutenant-General Vasily Chuikov of the 8th Guards Army, part of Marshal Zhukov's 1st Belorussian Front. The fighting did not end neatly. Some German units kept pushing west, hoping to surrender to American forces near the Elbe instead of to the Soviets they had been told to fear. Many of them died in the attempt.

## The cost

There is no honest way to write about the days after the surrender without naming what happened. Soviet troops, many of them hardened by the appalling violence the German army and SS had inflicted on Soviet civilians and prisoners during the long advance west, carried out mass sexual violence against German women and girls in Berlin on a scale that the historian Antony Beevor and the diarist Marta Hillers, among others, have documented. Estimates of the number of women raped in Berlin in those weeks run from tens of thousands to over a hundred thousand. The survivors, like the children pushed into the streets to face tanks, were victims of decisions they had no part in making. Berlin's civilian dead during the entire battle are estimated at around 125,000. The city above them, the city the Hitler Youth had been told to defend, was largely rubble. The war that began with German conscripts marching into Poland and Soviet territory ended with German conscripts and Soviet conscripts dying together in a German capital that no longer existed.

## Flight Context

The Battle in Berlin centred on Mitte at 52.519°N, 13.376°E, with the Reichstag, Brandenburg Gate and Reich Chancellery all within a few hundred metres of each other. The Tiergarten makes a green rectangle west of the centre. EDDB (Berlin Brandenburg) lies about 24 km southeast; Tegel (EDDT) closed in November 2020. The Spree River and the long axis of Unter den Linden running west to the Brandenburg Gate are the obvious visual landmarks from cruising altitude.
