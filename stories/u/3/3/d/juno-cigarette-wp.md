# Juno (cigarette)

A Berlin cigarette that was round when everything else was flat, sold on a rhyme that outlived it by decades, and whose corporate history runs straight through the Nazi seizure of a Jewish family's factory in Pankow.

**Location:** 52.5124, 13.4187

**Categories:** Berlin, Industry, German history, Advertising, Cold War

## Introduction

Aus gutem Grund ist Juno rund. There is a good reason Juno is round. The line was registered as a trademark on 26 April 1934 and it did what the best advertising does — it detached itself from the product and lodged in the language, where it is still occasionally quoted by Germans who could not tell you what it was selling. It was selling a cigarette, made in Berlin, and the good reason was purely commercial. In the 1890s the fashionable cigarette was the flat oriental kind, oval in cross-section. The Berliner Cigarettenfabrik Josetti wanted something a customer could identify by touch in a jacket pocket, so in 1896 it rolled its new Virginia blend thick and round and told everyone so: Dick und rund, said the packs of the Gründerzeit. The shape was the whole idea.

## Hamburger Straße 8

The firm behind it began in 1888, when Oskar Josetti started making cigarettes at Hamburger Straße 8 in Berlin. He lasted four years before emigrating to the United States and selling up; from 1895 the business traded as Cigarettenfabrik Josetti, Inh. Meier & Peters oHG, and the demand for the new round cigarette pushed it through a series of rented premises — Rosenthaler Straße 40, the Zeughausmarkt from 1896 to 1902, a small plant on Wusterhauser Straße from 1910. The name "Josetti Juno" was entered in the German trademark register on 9 February 1898 under number 34169. It is still on the books. Berlin at the time was full of cigarette makers; the largest of them, Manoli, employed two hundred people and counted as serious industry in the German Empire.

## Rungestraße 22–24

Ownership moved faster than the product did. In 1905 the Dresden firm Jasmatzi AG took over Josetti; during the First World War, a state-backed campaign financed by Deutsche Bank bought foreign shareholdings back out of German tobacco. Then came Reemtsma of Hamburg, which after the hyperinflation of 1923–24 built an interest group out of its rivals and, from 1928, consolidated them into a single company employing 14,000 people by 1935. Reemtsma took control of Manoli in 1924 and moved Josetti's production into Manoli's building complex at Rungestraße 22–24 in Berlin-Mitte, a block behind the Spree. On 18 February 1928 it registered its own trademark rights to Josetti Juno. Manoli's own production was shut down in 1930 and the company liquidated in 1935 — a brand kept alive on paper only, which was a normal way for a competitor to end.

## What Was Taken in 1938

The consolidation did not stay commercial. Up in Pankow stood the cigarette works of Josef Garbáty-Rosenthal, who had come from Lida in the Russian Empire, started rolling cigarettes in 1879 and founded his firm in 1881; his family were Jews, and after 1933 that fact was made to matter. In 1938 the Garbáty property was forcibly sold — to the Koerfer group of Cologne and to Reemtsma. The family got out to the United States in 1939. Josef Garbáty-Rosenthal himself stayed in Berlin and died there on 29 June 1939, in his late eighties. What the paperwork called a purchase was a theft with a contract stapled to it, and the round cigarette's parent company was one of the beneficiaries. Pankow named the square outside the old works Garbátyplatz in 2000.

## Two Junos

War finished the Berlin plant. Air raids destroyed it and the cigarette vanished from the market at the end of 1943. Then partition did something odder: it split the brand in half. The East ended up holding Josetti's company records — the firm continued as VEB Josetti — and "Ost-Juno" was put back into production, flat and oval, later folded into VEB Tabak Nordhausen. Reemtsma in Hamburg started a "West Juno," thick and round, on 5 March 1951. The GDR eventually added the Format 100 Juno Filter, a longer cigarette whose extra centimetres were meant to demonstrate, in the hand of an ordinary smoker, the superiority of the socialist system. For years, two cigarettes carrying the same name and opposite cross-sections were sold on either side of the same city.

## Past Tense

The advertising outlasted both. Juno had run on Berlin buses in the 1920s under the slogan Berlin raucht Juno; in 1951 Just Scheu wrote and composed a jingle for the western version, and Bully Buhlan recorded it with the Erich Börschel orchestra on a shellac advertising disc. The West Juno was cut back to the filter version in the 1970s, withdrawn for poor sales, and pushed back into the German market again in 1983. It limped along another three decades. In May 2016 Reemtsma announced it was dropping Juno altogether, wanting to concentrate on its large brands and unwilling to redesign the packaging for the EU's new picture health warnings. German papers wrote the brand's obituary in the only way available to them, by putting the rhyme into the past tense: Aus gutem Grund war Juno rund.

## Flight Context

The story's Berlin anchor is Rungestraße 22–24 in Mitte, at roughly 52.5124°N, 13.4187°E, on the south bank of the Spree between Jannowitzbrücke and the Köpenicker Straße corridor — about 1.5 km east-southeast of Alexanderplatz. From the air the landmark is the river itself: the Spree makes a broad eastward sweep here, with the elevated U-Bahn viaduct and Jannowitzbrücke station on the near bank and the tall slab of the television tower standing just to the northwest as the unmissable reference. The Garbáty works lie about 6 km north in Pankow, near the Schönhauser Allee axis. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) is roughly 18 km south-southeast; Strausberg (EDAY) about 28 km east-northeast and Schönhagen (EDAZ) about 45 km southwest. This is central Berlin airspace with a TV tower topping 360 m nearby, so 2,000 to 3,000 feet is both the useful and the sensible band. Haze off the plain often flattens the light over Mitte in summer; winter mornings give the sharpest read on the riverfront blocks.
