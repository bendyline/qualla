# Hansa Tonstudio

A chamber-music hall built by a property owners' association in 1913 ended up 150 metres from the Berlin Wall, and became the room where David Bowie sang "Heroes".

**Location:** 52.5063, 13.3772

**Categories:** Music, Recording studios, Berlin, Cold War, Historic buildings, Popular culture

## Introduction

A landlords' association built it. In 1910 the Real Estate Association of Berlin and its Suburbs bought a plot at Köthener Strasse 38 and put up an office block with a 266-square-metre concert room at its centre; the chairman opened the building in October 1913, and someone drafting the tender documents had already given the room its name. Meistersaal. Master hall. It hosted chamber music and small gatherings, acquired a reputation in the Berlin art scene of the 1920s, came through the war, and then found itself in the strangest position a concert hall has ever occupied - roughly 150 metres from the Berlin Wall, at the dead edge of a divided city. David Bowie called it the big hall by the wall.

## Named for a Trading League

In 1962 the brothers Peter and Thomas Meisel, of the music publishing house Edition Meisel & Co., founded Hansa Musik Produktion and the Hansa Records label. They took the name from the Hanseatic League, the medieval trading network of the northern seas: a merchant's name for a music business. From 1965 they rented recording facilities from Ariola Records inside the Köthener Strasse building. They built a studio at their own offices in 1969, opened Hansa Studio I on Nestorstrasse in the Halensee district in 1973, and in 1975 bought the Köthener Strasse building outright from Ariola. The Meistersaal, with its high ornate ceiling and its acoustics designed for orchestras rather than microphones, became Hansa Studio II. Whatever a room built in 1913 was supposed to do, this one turned out to be very good at something nobody had invented yet.

## The Big Hall by the Wall

In 1976 David Bowie and Iggy Pop came to Berlin, finished Low and The Idiot at Hansa's studios, and then made "Heroes" and Lust for Life there with Tony Visconti producing and Brian Eno in the room. The story attached to the title track has Bowie at a studio window, looking out toward the Wall, watching Visconti kiss the backing vocalist Antonia Maass - two lovers by the wall, the image that anchors the whole song. Whether it happened quite that way is one of rock's pleasantly unresolvable arguments. What is not in question is that the record's sound belongs to the hall. Visconti put three microphones on Bowie at nine inches, twenty feet and fifty feet, and gated the two distant ones so they opened only when he sang loudly enough. Sing quietly and you get a man in a booth. Push, and the room arrives.

## Hansa Blue

When Hansa Studio 1 closed at the end of the 1970s its equipment was stripped out and used to build a second room inside the Köthener Strasse building, which opened in 1980. The studio also bought three custom Solid State Logic SL 4000 E consoles finished in a shade engineers still call Hansa blue. Siouxsie and the Banshees, Wire and The Psychedelic Furs recorded there, and the address acquired a second identity as the home of post-punk and synthpop. In 1983 Depeche Mode arrived with Daniel Miller and Gareth Jones to mix Construction Time Again, then came back for the single that broke them internationally, "People Are People," and the album Some Great Reward. Martin Gore recorded his vocal for "Somebody" standing in the Meistersaal with no clothes on. Black Celebration followed in 1986.

## Still Booked

U2 opened the Achtung Baby sessions at Hansa in late 1990, weeks after German reunification had erased the border outside, before decamping to a rented house near Dublin to finish. Since then the building has quietly rearranged itself around the music. Film production companies took offices in what had been Studio 3, and Emil Berliner Studios moved in there in 2010; the old Hansa mixing room is now the permanent base of the Swedish producer and engineer Michael Ilbert. The Meistersaal itself has been fully restored and works as a recording hall, a concert venue and an event space. The credits keep stacking up - Manic Street Preachers, R.E.M., In Flames, Herbert Grönemeyer, Chvrches, Pet Shop Boys, Parcels, Blood Incantation in 2024, Biffy Clyro in 2025. The Wall is gone and the neighbourhood is glass towers. The room is well over a century old and still working.

## Flight Context

52.5063°N, 13.3772°E, at Köthener Strasse 38, a block south of Potsdamer Platz in central Berlin. From the air, find the Potsdamer Platz towers first; Hansa is the low, ornate stone block immediately south-southwest of them, with the Landwehr Canal running a short distance further south and the green expanse of the Tiergarten to the north. The line of the former Berlin Wall passed between the studio and the Tiergarten, and the modern street grid still shows the seam where the death strip was cleared. This is dense Class C/D airspace over the German capital - VFR transit normally requires clearance and there are overflight restrictions near the government quarter, so plan a routing rather than a loiter. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) lies roughly 11 nm southeast. At 2,000-3,000 ft the whole geography of the divided city reads at once: Potsdamer Platz, the Wall's scar, and a recording studio that spent thirty years pointing at it.
