# Marx-Engels Forum

A bronze Marx and Engels in the heart of East Berlin, sculpted by Ludwig Engelhardt in 1986 and now uprooted by the U-Bahn extension that runs beneath them.

**Location:** 52.5183, 13.4042

**Categories:** berlin, germany, monuments, gdr-history, communism, public-art, cold-war

## Introduction

Marx is sitting. Engels is standing. They are slightly larger than life and cast in dark bronze, and Marx's lap is polished smooth from forty years of tourists climbing onto it for photographs. The Berlin sculptor Ludwig Engelhardt deliberately put them at eye level, accessible, touchable, with no plinth between them and the people who would come to look. His Culture Minister Hans-Joachim Hoffmann said in 1986 that the point was that visitors would be able to talk with Marx and Engels rather than look up their nostrils. It was a remarkable thing for a state minister to say about state monuments. Three years later, the state that commissioned the statues was gone.

## The Quarter That Used to Be Here

Before the Second World War, the strip of land between the Spree and Alexanderplatz was the Heilige-Geist-Viertel, the Holy Ghost Quarter, a dense huddle of medieval streets and the city's main post office. Allied bombing in 1944 and 1945 leveled most of it. The East German government cleared what was left and let the ground sit empty for thirty years. When planning began in 1977, the GDR decided to fill the gap with a green park between the Palast der Republik and the Fernsehturm, and to put Marx and Engels at its center. The Nikolaiviertel next door was being rebuilt as a sanitized medieval quarter. The Forum was the new ideological one.

## Engelhardt's Reading

Engelhardt had been working on the figures since the late 1970s. What he produced is more melancholy than triumphalist. Marx looks tired. Engels looks patient. Behind them stand stainless steel reliefs by Werner Stotzer and a bronze frieze by Margret Middell showing scenes from the German workers' movement. Critics in the West read the seated, weary Marx as an unintentional epitaph for the system that commissioned him. Whatever Engelhardt intended, the inauguration on 4 April 1986 fell within a window of about three and a half years before the Berlin Wall came down. The statues survived 1989 because Berliners argued for keeping them. They had become part of the cityscape that people walked through every day, and removing them felt too much like erasing a chapter rather than turning the page.

## Lives in the Frame

It is easy now to think of the GDR as a museum exhibit, but the people who attended the unveiling in 1986 were living their lives. They had jobs, families, weekend dachas in Brandenburg, opinions about the local football team. The Forum was where they took their children on Sunday afternoons. After 1990, some of those families lost everything they had built; others discovered Stasi files about themselves; many felt a quieter loss, the disappearance of a country that had also been home. The German word for that feeling, Ostalgie, gets mocked in headlines, but it is real and it is honest. Standing at the Forum today, you are looking at one of the few public reminders of forty years of lived experience for around sixteen million people.

## Moved for the U-Bahn

In 2010, work began on the extension of the U5 line under central Berlin. The construction site swallowed the Forum, and Marx and Engels were lifted from their pedestal and shifted about thirty meters east, where they now stand in a temporary configuration overlooking the river. The reliefs and frieze went into storage. The U5 extension opened in December 2020, but the question of what the Forum should look like next remains undecided. Some Berliners want the medieval quarter rebuilt, as Mayor Klaus Wowereit once floated. Others want the GDR park preserved as a deliberate act of remembering. The statues themselves are mute on the matter, gazing toward the reconstructed Berlin Palace across the Spree, where the Palace of the Republic used to stand.

## What You See Now

Visit on a clear summer afternoon and you will find a small, slightly forlorn green space with a bronze pair facing east. There will probably be a tourist sitting on Marx's knee, arms slung casually across his shoulder, while a friend takes the photograph. There will be Berliners cutting through on their way to the Rotes Rathaus or the Nikolaiviertel, paying the statues no particular attention. The Fernsehturm rises behind the trees. The reconstructed dome of the Berlin Cathedral catches the light across the river. The Forum is a place caught between three eras, none of which has fully decided what to do with it.

## Flight Context

Located at 52.518 N, 13.404 E in Berlin's Mitte district, on the eastern bank of the Spree directly opposite the reconstructed Berlin Palace. The Fernsehturm at 368 meters dominates the immediate skyline a few blocks east. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) is the nearest airport at about 18 km southeast. Best viewed at low altitude alongside the Spree island; the Forum itself is small and reads as a green clearing among the surrounding masonry.
