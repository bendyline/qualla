# AquaDom

For nineteen years a glass elevator carried visitors up through a million litres of seawater in a Berlin hotel lobby — until one freezing December morning when the tank let go.

**Location:** 52.5197, 13.4028

**Categories:** berlin, engineering, disasters, architecture, modern-history, germany

## Introduction

The elevator ran up the inside of the fish tank. Visitors stepped into a glass cylinder in a hotel lobby on Karl-Liebknecht-Strasse and rose slowly through a million litres of warm saltwater, close enough to the acrylic that one passing fish could fill the whole window. Around them some 1,500 tropical fish from more than a hundred species circled a column of water fourteen metres deep. The AquaDom — mixed Latin and German for 'water dome,' though its makers preferred the grander 'water cathedral' — stood twenty-five metres from the lobby floor to its top and held the Guinness World Record for the largest cylindrical aquarium on Earth. It opened on 2 December 2003. It lasted nineteen years.

## Forty-One Panels, Bonded on Site

Building it was less architecture than chemistry. The cylinder was fabricated from 41 acrylic panels — 26 for the outer wall, 15 for the inner tube carrying the elevator — and those panels could not simply be bolted together, because a bolted seam is a leak waiting to happen. They were bonded on site into what amounted to one continuous piece of plastic. Reynolds Polymer Technology supplied the material, International Concept Management built the cylinder, and Sergei Tchoban drew the architecture. The finished project cost about €12.8 million.

The engineering numbers were reassuringly heavy. The outer acrylic wall measured 22 centimetres thick at the bottom, where water pressure is greatest, tapering to 18 centimetres at the top. The tank itself stood 16 metres tall and 11.5 metres across, resting on a nine-metre foundation that lifted the whole assembly to its full height in the DomAquarée atrium.

## Divers in the Morning

Somebody had to go in. A team of scuba divers worked the tank every day, feeding the collection and scrubbing the acrylic from the inside, and the routine became an attraction in its own right — the divers were as watchable as the fish. The water was held at 26 °C, a permanent tropical summer maintained in a city that spends a good part of the year below freezing.

In 2020 the aquarium was refurbished and upgraded. The entire column was drained and the fish were moved down to a breeding facility in the basement while the work went on, then brought back up when it was finished. At the time it looked like unremarkable maintenance on a well-run machine.

## 16 December 2022

The tank let go at around a quarter to six in the morning, in December darkness, with Berlin outside at roughly minus nine degrees. Local seismographs registered it. A million litres of 26-degree saltwater went out through the lobby and into Karl-Liebknecht-Strasse, taking the interior with it, reaching neighbouring businesses including a Lindt chocolate shop before draining away into the storm sewers. Two people were taken to hospital with injuries. Most of the 1,500 fish were killed.

Berlin's Technisches Hilfswerk mounted a full-scale deployment and worked the site for twelve hours. Onlookers described the wrecked lobby and atrium as looking like a battlefield. Down in the basement, the power failure that came with the flood put hundreds of smaller fish in the breeding tanks at risk; those were reached in time and saved. Officials afterwards made the point everyone had already arrived at independently: at almost any other hour of the day, that lobby would have had people standing in it.

## No Conclusive Cause

Police found no evidence of foul play. The early working theory pointed to material fatigue in the acrylic, possibly aggravated by the temperature gradient across the tank wall that night — some 35 degrees between the water inside and the air outside. Investigators spent ten months on the question. On 24 October 2023 prosecutors closed the case: the experts could not establish a conclusive cause, and none has been established since.

The hotel stayed shut for two years and reopened on 12 February 2025. Where the cylinder had stood, the lobby now holds a 24-metre vertical garden — roughly 2,000 plants across 22 species, a green column climbing through the same atrium void the aquarium once filled. Same shape, same theatre, nothing that needs containing.

## Flight Context

Coordinates 52.5197°N, 13.4028°E, in the heart of Berlin-Mitte on Karl-Liebknecht-Strasse, immediately east of Berlin Cathedral and the Museum Island bend in the Spree. From the air the DomAquarée block reads as a low modern quarter wedged between the cathedral dome and the television tower at Alexanderplatz, which stands about 700 m northeast and is the single most useful landmark in the city. Best viewing altitude is 1,500–3,000 ft AGL, with the Spree's loop around Museum Island making the approach unambiguous from any direction. Berlin Brandenburg (EDDB) is roughly 25 km southeast; Strausberg (EDAY) and Schönhagen (EDAZ) serve general aviation east and southwest respectively. The Mitte core lies under Berlin's controlled airspace — coordinate before descending. Clear cold winter mornings, of the kind that may have contributed to the collapse, also give the sharpest visibility of the year over the city.
