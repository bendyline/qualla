# Altes Museum

Karl Friedrich Schinkel's 1830 neoclassical temple of art on Berlin's Museum Island opened the European royal collection to the public, and built a Pantheon at its centre to do it.

**Location:** 52.5194, 13.3989

**Categories:** Altes Museum, Antikensammlung Berlin, Museum Island, Karl Friedrich Schinkel buildings, Neoclassical architecture in Germany

## Introduction

Eighteen Ionic columns face the Lustgarten in a single uninterrupted row, and behind them, hidden from the street, sits a Pantheon. Karl Friedrich Schinkel's Altes Museum, completed in 1830, took the great circular hall of imperial Rome and used it as the heart of a building whose stated purpose was nothing less than to give ordinary citizens of Berlin the same access to art that kings had once jealously guarded. Frederick William III of Prussia paid for the building. Schinkel designed it. Crown Prince Frederick William, the future king, sketched some early ideas himself, including a portico he wanted modelled directly on classical antiquity. The result became the founding work of the museum that would later be called the Old Museum, only because, in 1845, a New Museum opened next door.

## A republic of looking

The museum was a political object before it was an aesthetic one. By the 1820s the German bourgeoisie was wealthy, educated and increasingly impatient with the idea that art and learning belonged to courts. Frederick William III, no radical himself, leaned into the Humboldtian conviction that an educated citizenry was a stronger citizenry, and he commissioned Schinkel to design a public building for the royal art collection. Schinkel placed it at the north end of the Lustgarten, opposite the Berlin Palace to the south, the Zeughaus arsenal to the west and the Berlin Cathedral to the east. The arrangement was deliberate: worldly power, military power, divine power, and now, balancing them, science and art for the people. The vocabulary was monarchical; the message was new.

## Schinkel's Pantheon

Walk up the long stair, past the eighteen Ionic columns, and the building opens into a domed rotunda the architect modelled directly on the Pantheon in Rome. Light falls from a coffered dome onto a ring of two stories of columns, with classical statuary placed around the perimeter as if the visitor had walked into a small temple of the gods. Schinkel wrote that the rotunda was meant to prepare the visitor for serious contemplation, a kind of secular nave. The trick worked. The eye lifts; the city outside dampens; you become, briefly, the kind of person the Enlightenment wanted to make of you. Outside, two great equestrian bronzes flank the steps, and a single granite bowl, almost seven metres across and cut from one boulder hauled from a Brandenburg moraine, sits in the Lustgarten as a kind of overture.

## Building Museum Island

In 1841 Frederick William IV decreed that the entire northern tip of the Spree island be turned into a 'sanctuary for art and science', and one by one the buildings followed: Friedrich August Stüler's Neues Museum in 1855, Johann Heinrich Strack's Nationalgalerie in 1876, the Bode Museum in 1904, and finally Alfred Messel and Ludwig Hoffmann's Pergamonmuseum in 1930. Together they form Museum Island, listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999 in recognition not just of the architecture but of the social experiment the Altes Museum had begun. Julius Carl Raschdorff's bombastic neo-Renaissance rebuild of the Berlin Cathedral between 1894 and 1905 broke the original classical balance Schinkel had so carefully set up; the new dome dwarfs the museum's quiet portico. You can still feel the lost proportions if you stand in the middle of the Lustgarten and look around.

## Damage, return, antiquity

The building has not had a quiet life. In the 1930s Albert Speer used it as a backdrop for Nazi rallies in the redesigned Lustgarten. Late in the Second World War a fuel-truck explosion in front of the museum gutted much of the interior; the frescoes Schinkel and Peter Cornelius had designed for the vestibule and the back wall of the portico were largely lost. Restoration work stretched across decades on both sides of the Berlin Wall. Since 1998 the Altes Museum has housed the Antikensammlung's Greek collection, including the treasury, on the ground floor, with Etruscan and Roman material on the upper floor. Visitors today walk through galleries of black-figure vases, marble portraits, gold wreaths, and bronze helmets in the building Schinkel imagined as a temple to the idea that everyone deserves to see them.

## Flight Context

The Altes Museum stands on Museum Island in central Berlin at 52.519°N, 13.399°E, on the north end of the Spree island opposite the Berlin Cathedral and the rebuilt Berlin Palace (Humboldt Forum). From the air the island is unmistakable, a long narrow strip in the Spree threaded by bridges. EDDB (Berlin Brandenburg) lies roughly 24 km southeast; Tegel (EDDT) closed in November 2020. The TV tower at Alexanderplatz, a few hundred metres east, makes a useful visual landmark.
