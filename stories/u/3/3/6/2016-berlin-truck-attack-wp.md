# 2016 Berlin truck attack

On a Monday evening in December 2016, a hijacked truck tore through Berlin's Breitscheidplatz Christmas market, killing thirteen people who had come for mulled wine and shopping in the shadow of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.

**Location:** 52.5053, 13.3344

**Categories:** 2016 in Berlin, Charlottenburg, Christmas in Germany, December 2016 in Germany, Terrorism in Germany

## Introduction

Łukasz Urban was thirty-seven years old, a Polish lorry driver from the village of Banie, and on the afternoon of 19 December 2016 he was sitting in the cab of a black Scania he had driven from Turin. He had a delivery of steel for a Berlin construction site and a trip home to look forward to. Sometime between 4:30 and 5:30 p.m., a man named Anis Amri shot him once in the head with a small-caliber pistol and rolled the body into the passenger seat. Three hours later, Amri drove the same truck along Hardenbergstraße and into the Christmas market at Breitscheidplatz, in the shadow of the broken spire of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church. He covered about fifty metres before the truck's automatic emergency brake stopped it, level with the church. Eleven people died in the market that night. Urban became the twelfth victim. A first responder, gravely injured by a falling beam, became the thirteenth in 2021.

## Eleven names from the market

They had come for what Berliners come to a Weihnachtsmarkt for: glühwein in heavy ceramic mugs, candied almonds, the smell of grilled sausages, the wooden stalls strung with lights. Fabrizia Di Lorenzo was thirty-one, an Italian working in Berlin in logistics. Dalia Elyakim, sixty, was an Israeli tourist visiting with her husband Rami, who survived but was severely injured. Anna and Georgiy Bagratuni were a married couple. Sebastian Berlin, Nada Cizmar, Christoph Herrlich, Klaus Jacob, Angelika Klösters, Dorit Krebs and Peter Völker rounded out the eleven killed in the few seconds it took the truck to grind through the stalls. Forty-eight more were injured, many with broken bones from the wooden stalls collapsing under the impact. The market reopened a few days later behind concrete bollards; everyone agreed nothing about it was the same.

## Łukasz Urban

It took some time to understand what had happened to Urban. Early reports speculated he might have grabbed the wheel and forced the truck to stop, paying for the moment with his life. The autopsy made the truth crueler and simpler: he had been killed hours before the attack, and the truck had been stopped not by his hand but by a German engineering safety system. His cousin Ariel Żurawski, who owned the trucking company, told reporters he could not imagine Łukasz being part of any attack and quickly proved he had been right. A British truck driver named Dave Duncan started a GoFundMe for Urban's family that collected more than €110,000 in four days. A petition urging that Urban receive Germany's Federal Cross of Merit gathered thousands of signatures. President Andrzej Duda attended his funeral in Banie on 30 December. The Polish government covered the costs and arranged a state pension for his family. The German government refused to compensate Żurawski for the destroyed truck.

## A man the system kept losing

Anis Amri was twenty-four. Born in Tataouine, Tunisia in 1992, he had drifted across the Mediterranean in a refugee raft in 2011 and through prisons in Sicily, where, his family later said, he was radicalised. He arrived in Germany in 2015, applied for asylum in 2016, used at least fourteen aliases and three claimed nationalities. The Federal Criminal Police had warned in March 2016 that he was planning a suicide attack and recommended immediate deportation; North Rhine-Westphalia ruled he could not be removed. Tunisian authorities initially denied he was their citizen; the documents that would have allowed his deportation arrived in Germany after the attack. Three weeks before Breitscheidplatz, Moroccan intelligence sent another warning. After the attack the State Criminal Police Office of Berlin was investigated for obstruction of justice over manipulations of his file. He fled west through the Netherlands, Belgium and France. On 23 December at three in the morning, two Italian officers on a routine patrol in Sesto San Giovanni near Milan asked him for ID. He drew his pistol, wounded one of them in the shoulder, and the other shot him dead.

## The Goldener Riss

Where the truck came to rest, just below the steps of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church, the city installed a memorial called the Goldener Riss, a 'Golden Crack', a jagged seam of bronze running across the cobblestones into the church floor with the names of the dead inlaid alongside. The Berliner Fussilet-Moscheeverein, which Amri had visited, was banned in February 2017. The German Bundestag passed new surveillance laws in March. The Christmas market reopened with concrete barriers and steel bollards that have since become standard at every European holiday market. On the fourth anniversary in 2020, survivors and families gathered at the broken spire and the Archbishop of Berlin Heiner Koch told them, in his prayers, that the city 'was not the same as before'. None of the security measures could undo any of it. Eleven people went out for mulled wine and never came home; one driver was killed for a vehicle; a first responder died years later from the injuries he sustained trying to help.

## Flight Context

Breitscheidplatz lies in the heart of former West Berlin at 52.505°N, 13.334°E, a few hundred metres south of Bahnhof Zoo and the western end of the Kurfürstendamm. The broken spire of the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church is the unmistakable visual marker. EDDB (Berlin Brandenburg) lies about 24 km southeast of central Berlin; the older Tegel airport (EDDT) closed in November 2020. From cruising altitude, the green expanse of the Tiergarten just to the east makes the City West district easy to find.
