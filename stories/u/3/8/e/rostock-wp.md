# Rostock

Germany's largest Baltic port has been a Hanseatic merchant city since the thirteenth century, with brick Gothic spires, a 1419 university, and a recent past it is still working through.

**Location:** 54.0833, 12.1333

**Categories:** germany, baltic, hanseatic, ports, cities, history

## Introduction

The herring gulls give it away. Walk Rostock's Kropeliner Strasse pedestrian street and they are everywhere overhead, screaming the way they do in any serious port town on the Baltic. This is not a city pretending to be near the sea. The Warnow River cuts north through the heart of it and opens out at Warnemunde into the Bay of Mecklenburg, where ferries leave for Sweden and Finland and Denmark on schedules nobody fusses about. Rostock has been a Hanseatic merchant port since 1218 and a university town since 1419, when the city's burghers founded what is now the third-oldest university in continuous operation in Germany. The brick Gothic spires of the old town rise above tile roofs that have burned and been rebuilt across eight hundred years.

## The brick Gothic city

Rostock is one of the great cities of the Baltic Brick Gothic tradition, where medieval merchants built churches and warehouses out of the only material plentiful around the North German Plain: red clay fired into brick. St. Mary's Church on the Ziegenmarkt is the masterpiece. Begun in the thirteenth century and reshaped at the end of the fourteenth into a cross-shaped basilica, it holds an astronomical clock erected in 1472 by Hans Duringer that still works. The Town Hall on the Neuer Markt began as a thirteenth-century brick building and acquired a baroque facade in the eighteenth century, the layered city in a single building. Six original gable houses on the square survived an Allied air raid in 1942 that destroyed everything else around them. The Nikolaikirche, oldest church in the city, was bombed nearly flat in the war and rebuilt as a concert hall.

## The university and the Nobel laureates

Rostock University opened in 1419 with a charter from Pope Martin V. Tycho Brahe studied here in the sixteenth century before he lost part of his nose in a duel and went on to become the most important pre-telescopic astronomer in Europe. Albrecht Kossel, born in Rostock in 1853, won the 1910 Nobel Prize in Physiology for working out the chemical composition of nucleic acids, the foundational chemistry behind what would later be understood as DNA. Hans von Ohain, who built one of the first jet engines, worked at the Heinkel aircraft factory in Warnemunde during the 1930s. The Heinkel factory itself is a complicated piece of Rostock's history. It made night fighters for the Luftwaffe, used forced labor, and was bombed heavily by the Allies. The university now teaches more than 14,000 students.

## Warnemunde and the open Baltic

The Warnow opens into the Baltic at Warnemunde, twelve kilometers north of central Rostock, where the river meets the sea between two long jetties. The fishermen's quarter survives as a strip of low gabled houses along the Alter Strom. The lighthouse from 1898 still works. The beach is wide and white and runs east toward Markgrafenheide. Warnemunde is now also Germany's biggest cruise terminal, taking more cruise tourists than any other port in the country. The headquarters of AIDA Cruises sits in modernist office buildings near the docks. Every August the Hanse Sail festival brings in more than a hundred sailing ships and over 1.5 million visitors to remind everyone that the Baltic is still, for many people, a working sea.

## The 1992 riots, and what came after

Rostock's recent past includes a week in late August 1992 that the city has been working through ever since. In the Lichtenhagen district, a mob of several hundred locals, joined by hundreds more from elsewhere in eastern Germany, attacked a high-rise apartment building housing Vietnamese contract workers and Romani asylum seekers. The mob threw firebombs. Bystanders applauded. Police withdrew at one point and left the building burning with people trapped inside. No one was killed, by something between luck and the building's concrete construction, but the Lichtenhagen riots became the most serious xenophobic violence in postwar Germany and forced a national reckoning. The city has since installed memorials, funded reconciliation programs, and let the events into its public history rather than scrubbing them out. Joachim Gauck, born in Rostock in 1940, became Germany's federal president from 2012 to 2017 and made memory and reckoning central themes of his tenure.

## Flight Context

Rostock sits at 54.08 N, 12.13 E on the Warnow River about 12 km inland from the Baltic coast. Rostock-Laage Airport (ETNL) is 28 km southeast. The brick Gothic spires of St. Mary's, St. Peter's, and the Nikolaikirche rise above the old town and are visible from low altitude in clear weather, with the Warnow's wide channel curving north through the city to the Baltic at Warnemunde.
