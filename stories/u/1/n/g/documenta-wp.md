# Documenta

Every five years Kassel hosts one of the most consequential exhibitions in contemporary art - founded in 1955 to put back on German walls the modern art the Nazi state had driven out of them.

**Location:** 51.3136, 9.4975

**Categories:** Contemporary art exhibitions, Documenta, Museums and galleries in Germany, Recurring events established in 1955, Postwar German culture

## Introduction

The first documenta was not really about contemporary art at all. In the summer of 1955 Arnold Bode, an artist, teacher and curator from Kassel, filled the Fridericianum with Fauvism, Expressionism, Cubism, Blauer Reiter, Futurism and Pittura Metafisica. The building had burnt out under Allied bombing a decade earlier and had only been patched up; Bode draped the raw interior in black and translucent white plastic sheeting to control the daylight falling on the pictures. The point was not novelty. Every movement on those walls had been condemned by the German state as "degenerate art" and stripped from the country's museums. Bode was putting back what had been taken away. Around 130,000 people came to see it.

## Putting Back What Was Taken

The show was conceived as a sideshow. It ran as an accompaniment to the Bundesgartenschau, the federal horticultural exhibition then being held in Kassel, and nobody expected it to outlive the flowers. The name was invented for the occasion: a documenta, a documentation of the modern art German audiences had been prevented from seeing. Those close to Bode spread the story that the Latin documentum could be pulled apart into docere, to teach, and mens, intellect - which suited the mission well enough that the etymology hardly needed to be true. Each edition since has commissioned its own visual identity, and most have kept to the all-lowercase typography that came out of the Bauhaus. One rule has held from the beginning: documenta sells nothing. It is not a fair. Whatever else it is arguing about, it is not arguing about price.

## Every Five Years, a New Argument

The focus shifted from retrospective to contemporary, and the map widened from Europe to the Americas, Africa and Asia. The fourth documenta, in 1968, was the first to turn a profit, with pop, minimal and kinetic art. In 1972 Harald Szeemann took the theme "Questioning Reality - Pictorial Worlds Today" and used it to redefine what could be called art at all, devoting a large section to Adolf Wölfli, the Swiss outsider artist then almost unknown, while Joseph Beuys performed repeatedly under the banner of his Organization for Direct Democracy. The 1987 edition pulled design into the category of art. In 1997 Catherine David became the first woman and the first non-German-speaker to direct the exhibition. Five years later Okwui Enwezor, the first non-European director, staged platforms in Vienna, Berlin, New Delhi, St Lucia and Lagos before Kassel even opened, building Documenta11 around migration, urbanisation and the postcolonial.

## What the City Kept

Some works never left. Joseph Beuys began 7000 Eichen at documenta 7 in 1982 - seven thousand oaks, each planted beside a basalt stone, distributed across Kassel over years. Walter De Maria's Vertikaler Erdkilometer went into the ground on Friedrichsplatz in 1977, along with Haus-Rucker-Co's Rahmenbau, Horst H. Baumann's Laserscape Kassel and Anatol Herzfeld's Traumschiff Tante Olga from the same edition. Claes Oldenburg left a giant pickaxe, the Spitzhacke, in 1982; Jonathan Borofsky's Man walking to the sky and Thomas Schütte's Fremde both date from 1992. Bode also understood that an exhibition this size generates a paper trail worth keeping, and founded a documenta archive in 1961. It now holds the organisation's files alongside expanding video and image collections and the bequests of Bode himself and the artist Harry Kramer.

## Bigger Than the City

In 1992 documenta drew more than half a million visitors for the first time. The 2002 edition brought 650,000 people to Kassel - more than triple the town's own population. In 2007 there were 754,000 paying visitors, over a third of them from abroad. In 2012 the count reached 904,992. That scale carries costs. documenta 14 split itself between Athens and Kassel in 2017, and critics argued the institution's framing of Athens as a site of resistance and novelty amounted to a patronising fascination with crisis. The budget ran to 37 million euros spread across 2013 to 2018, and overspending in Athens opened a liquidity gap of 7 million. Documenta's shareholders - the city of Kassel and the state of Hesse - stepped in with emergency loan guarantees worth 8 million euros.

## 2022, and What Comes Next

documenta fifteen, curated by the Indonesian collective ruangrupa, opened in June 2022 and was overtaken within days. A large banner by the Indonesian collective Taring Padi, People's Justice, made in 2002, included figures widely identified as antisemitic caricature; it was covered over and then dismantled on 21 June. Two further disputes followed, over the participation of the Palestinian collective Question of Funding and over a 1988 brochure by Burhan Karkoutly, Presence des Femmes, which documenta asked to be withdrawn until it could be "appropriately contextualised." The artist Hito Steyerl pulled her own work in protest. Sabine Schormann, the director general, resigned in July. In November 2024 documenta named Naomi Beckwith, deputy director and chief curator of the Guggenheim, to lead the sixteenth edition in 2027 - an exhibition founded to repair German culture's relationship with art, still arguing, seventy years on, about what that repair requires.

## Flight Context

Documenta is centred on the Fridericianum at 51.314N, 9.497E, on Friedrichsplatz in the middle of Kassel, northern Hesse, with the Fulda river running along the eastern edge of the old city. From the air at 2,000-4,000 ft the anchor points are easy: the large open rectangle of Friedrichsplatz with its long neoclassical museum block on the west side, and the Karlsaue, the big baroque park running southeast toward the river, where the temporary exhibition pavilions have been built. The unmistakable regional landmark is the Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe on the wooded hill west of the city, with the Hercules monument and its cascade aligned straight at the city centre - follow that axis east and it delivers you to Friedrichsplatz. Kassel-Calden (EDVK) lies just northwest of the city; Hanover (EDDV) and Frankfurt (EDDF) are the larger alternatives north and south. Terrain is hilly on all sides, so expect ridge cloud in poor weather.
