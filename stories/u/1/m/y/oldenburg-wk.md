# Oldenburg

A traveller's Oldenburg: pedestrian Old Town, free buses on Saturdays, cheap rental bikes, and a horse market that sells no horses.

**Location:** 53.1439, 8.2139

**Categories:** travel, cities, germany, lower-saxony, cycling

## Introduction

On Saturdays, between 4 a.m. and 4 a.m. the following morning, every bus in Oldenburg is free. The policy runs through 2025 and 2026, a quiet experiment in northern German municipal politics that has the side effect of being one of the friendliest welcomes a tourist arriving on a budget will find anywhere in Lower Saxony. Add the cycle paths that thread the city centre and reach all the way to the next towns, and a rental bike for six and a half euros a day, and you start to understand the local pace. This is a town for arriving slowly and staying late.

## Where to Aim For

Oldenburg sits between Bremen and the Dutch border, roughly the halfway point of the long flat run from the Weser to the Ems. The closest airports are Bremen (about an hour by tram and train, around twelve euros), Hamburg (two hours and ten minutes, thirty-six euros), and Amsterdam Schiphol, which is geographically reasonable but logistically painful since the Friesen bridge damage of 2015. The simplest international approach is still Bremen Airport, tram 6 to Bremen Hauptbahnhof, change for an Oldenburg train. For drivers, four motorways meet the city: A1, A7, A27, and the European E22. FlixBus runs cheap direct services from Hamburg and from Amsterdam Sloterdijk, terminating at the north side of the ZOB.

## The Pedestrianised Heart

The Altstadt, the old centre, is mostly closed to cars. This is the part of Oldenburg that locals call simply die Innenstadt, and it is where you spend most of an afternoon: pedestrian shopping streets fanning out from the Lange Strasse, cafes spilling into the squares, the spire of the Lambertikirche orienting you when the side alleys begin to play tricks. St Lambert's is the city's main church, seat of the Evangelical Lutheran Church in Oldenburg, with a circular neoclassical interior that surprises visitors expecting Gothic. From the church you can reach the Schloss and its gardens in less than ten minutes on foot, which is true of essentially everywhere downtown.

## Sleeping Off Lunch in the Schlossgarten

The Schloss, the Renaissance and Baroque palace begun in 1607, dominates the south end of the centre, now housing the State Museum for Art and Cultural History. South of it stretches the Schlossgarten, sixteen hectares of English landscape garden begun in 1814, with rhododendrons among the oldest in Germany and curved paths designed in the manner of Humphry Repton. Bring sandwiches. The Pferdemarkt, the horse market, hosts a weekday market that sells fresh bread, vegetables, and meat, but, as locals like to point out to visiting friends, no horses. The name is older than the trade that replaced it.

## Getting Around Like a Local

Oldenburg prides itself on cycling, and the visitor evidence is everywhere: bike racks crowding every corner, separate cycle tracks beside most main roads, and signed routes leading out of town to Bad Zwischenahn, Rastede, and beyond. Rentals are easy. Vosgerau Am Damm asks six and a half euros a day, Fahrradzentrum Oldenburg around ten, and Swapfiets does a monthly subscription at 17.50 euros that makes sense if you are staying longer. The bicycle self-help workshop at Carl von Ossietzky University also rents out bikes by the month. Local buses, run by VWG, charge 2.90 euros per single ride, or 7.60 for a day pass, but again, Saturdays are free.

## Onward

Bremen lies thirty to forty minutes east by train, two services an hour, and it is the obvious onward step, a free city with its own UNESCO-listed market square and the Town Musicians of Bremen. Delmenhorst is closer still. Groningen, across the Dutch border and Oldenburg's twin city since 1989, sits a couple of hours west, though as of writing the rail link remains broken by the Friesen bridge and you will need a train-bus combination. From Oldenburg you can also reach the North Sea coast in well under an hour by car: Wilhelmshaven for the harbour, Dangast for the mudflats, or further inland the polders and dikes of the Wesermarsch toward Stadland.

## Flight Context

Oldenburg lies at 53.14 degrees N, 8.21 degrees E. From cruise altitude look for the autobahn ring (A28, A29, A293) circling the historic core, with the Hunte snaking through the centre and the Küstenkanal continuing west toward the Ems. Bremen Airport (EDDW) is the practical arrival, 50 kilometres east. The grass-and-canal landscape around Oldenburg is unmistakable: 80 percent grassland, intersected by dead-straight drainage ditches, dotted with red-roof farmsteads.
