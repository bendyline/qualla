# Kaiserkeller

A St. Pauli basement with a stage made of planks on beer crates, where two Liverpool bands played themselves into shape in 1960 and then broke it on a bet.

**Location:** 53.5512, 9.9579

**Categories:** Music venues, Beatles history, Nightlife, Hamburg, Popular culture

## Introduction

The stage was planks laid across beer crates, which tells you roughly what the Kaiserkeller thought of the bands standing on it in the autumn of 1960. Two Liverpool groups were alternating there, five or six ninety-minute sets a day between them, and out of boredom and mutual competitive exhaustion they made a bet: whoever broke the stage first would win. They worked at it for days. A crack finally appeared. Then Rory Storm climbed onto the upright piano and jumped.

## Koschmider's Arrangement

Bruno Koschmider opened the Kaiserkeller at Große Freiheit 36 in St. Pauli on 14 October 1959: a basement with room for around 550 people, a few steps off the Reeperbahn. The Liverpool connection arrived almost by accident. A Caribbean steel band that had played Allan Williams's Jacaranda club in Liverpool took work in Hamburg and wrote home enthusing about the scene, so Williams got in touch with Koschmider and offered himself as a booking agent. Koschmider had already brought over Derry and the Seniors, who did well, and asked for more groups. Williams's first choice was Rory Storm and the Hurricanes, but they were committed to a Butlins holiday-camp season and turned him down; so did Gerry and the Pacemakers. Williams sent the band that was left. The Beatles reached Hamburg on 17 August 1960 and played their first night at Koschmider's smaller club, the Indra, the same evening.

## Six or Seven Hours a Night

The terms were punishing and they were also the making of the group. Six or seven hours a night, seven nights a week, in front of a drinking crowd that had to be held or it would leave. They slept in small, dirty rooms behind the screen of the Bambi Kino. When the Indra shut on 4 October 1960 after complaints about the noise, they moved straight down the street to the bigger Kaiserkeller. You cannot rehearse your way to that kind of playing; you can only survive it. The band that came back to Liverpool at the end of the year had spent more hours on a stage than most groups manage in years.

## The Bet

Rory Storm and the Hurricanes finished at Butlins and reached Hamburg on 1 October 1960, replacing Derry and the Seniors and having negotiated better money than either the Seniors or the Beatles were getting. Their drummer was Ringo Starr. The two Liverpool bands alternated sets in the same basement night after night, which is how the wager over the stage came about. When Storm finally came down on it, his guitarist Johnny 'Guitar' Byrne remembered the planks cracking loudly and folding into a V around him; Storm vanished into the hole and the amplifiers and Starr's cymbals slid in after him. Koschmider was not amused, and replaced the live music with a jukebox. Both bands walked across the road to Harold's café for breakfast. Koschmider's doormen followed them with coshes and beat them. The club's own bouncer, Horst Fascher — a boxer whose career had ended after he unintentionally killed a sailor in a street fight — later became a friend of the Beatles and looked after them.

## Between Sets

The Kaiserkeller also delivered the other thing Hamburg gave the Beatles: an audience of art students. Klaus Voormann wandered in one night, watched the Hurricanes and the Beatles, and came back the next day with the photographer Astrid Kirchherr and their friend Jürgen Vollmer. Between sets, Kirchherr met the band's bass player, Stuart Sutcliffe, and the two began seeing each other soon afterwards. Voormann had been Kirchherr's partner at the time; across further visits to the club that relationship turned platonic, and they stayed close friends. Sutcliffe left the band in the summer of 1961 and remained in Hamburg to study art. He died there on 10 April 1962 of a brain haemorrhage. He was twenty-one.

## Breaking the Contract

In late October 1960 the Beatles broke with Koschmider and moved to the Top Ten Club, run by Peter Eckhorn, which opened on 31 October. Koschmider took it badly and took his revenge through the authorities. George Harrison, seventeen and working nights in violation of the rules, was reported and deported on 21 November. Paul McCartney and Pete Best were arrested over a fire lit in the Bambi Kino storeroom and deported on 1 December; John Lennon's work permit went shortly after. The group was back in Liverpool by early December, and back at the Top Ten by the following March. The Kaiserkeller is still there, still on Große Freiheit, operating today as an alternative rock club within the Große Freiheit 36 venue — a basement that has never entirely stopped being loud.

## Flight Context

Große Freiheit 36 lies at 53.5512 N, 9.9579 E, in St. Pauli on Hamburg's north bank, about 400 m inland from the Elbe and one short block north of the Reeperbahn. From 2,000–2,500 ft AGL the district reads as a bright, dense grid between the green Wallanlagen park to the northeast and the river to the south; the St. Pauli Landungsbrücken piers and the Old Elbe Tunnel rotunda mark the waterfront directly below, and the copper spire of the Michel stands about 1.5 km east. Hamburg Airport / Helmut Schmidt (EDDH) is roughly 9 km north-northeast; Hamburg-Finkenwerder (EDHI) sits across the Elbe to the southwest; Lübeck (EDHL) is about 60 km northeast. Central Hamburg is controlled airspace. Best visibility over the harbour usually comes on a clear northwesterly behind a front.
