# DESY

A campus of low halls in western Hamburg sitting on kilometres of buried accelerator tunnel, where the gluon was found, the ribosome was decoded, and X-ray lasers were invented.

**Location:** 53.5758, 9.8794

**Categories:** Science, Physics, Research facilities, Hamburg, Particle accelerators

## Introduction

The gluon was first seen in a Hamburg suburb. In 1979, four experiments running on a newly built ring under Bahrenfeld - TASSO, JADE, MARK-J and PLUTO - picked out the messenger particle of the strong force, the thing that holds every atomic nucleus in the universe together. There is nothing to look at on the surface. DESY is a campus of low halls and workshops in the west of Hamburg, and almost everything that matters about it is buried in tunnels under the streets around it.

## A Ring Named After Itself

Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron was founded on 18 December 1959, and its first machine gave the whole institution its name. The DESY synchrotron, a 300-metre ring, came on line in 1964 and ran particle-physics experiments until 1978. It was never switched off. Like almost every accelerator on the site, it was demoted rather than retired - rebuilt and upgraded, then put to work as a pre-accelerator and a test beam facility feeding higher-energy machines further down the chain. That is the pattern here. New rings were not built to replace old ones but stacked on top of them, each generation feeding the next, so the campus works like a set of Russian dolls made of magnets.

## DORIS, PETRA, HERA

The DORIS storage ring, 289 metres around, collided electrons with positrons from 1974. In 1987 its ARGUS experiment became the first to observe large mixing of B mesons - matter and antimatter caught behaving differently. PETRA followed in 1978, 2,304 metres around, and delivered the gluon a year later. Then came HERA in 1992: a 6,336-metre ring, DESY's largest and the biggest research instrument Germany has ever built, and the only facility in the world where electrons or positrons could be slammed into protons. Its four experiments - H1, ZEUS, HERMES and HERA-B - spent fifteen years mapping the inside of the proton before it shut down in 2007. The tunnel did not go to waste either. It now houses ALPS II, which reuses HERA's superconducting dipole magnets to hunt for extremely light particles.

## When the Byproduct Took Over

Bend a beam of electrons and it sheds light. For particle physicists that synchrotron radiation was a nuisance; by the 1960s other researchers had realised it was the brightest X-ray source they would ever get their hands on. DESY built HASYLAB for them in 1980, with stations on DORIS. It was there, between 1986 and 2004, that the Israeli biochemist Ada Yonath ran the experiments that let her work out the structure of the ribosome - the Nobel Prize in Chemistry followed in 2009. Photon science is now half of what DESY does. PETRA III has delivered hard X-rays of very high brilliance to more than forty experimental stations since 2009, and each year over 3,000 researchers from more than forty countries come to Hamburg to use the light: chemists, structural biologists, materials scientists, geologists, even conservators studying cultural heritage.

## Niobium at Two Kelvin

In the early 1990s DESY committed to a technology that has since spread worldwide: accelerating cavities made of niobium, cooled with liquid helium to about 2 K, roughly minus 271 degrees Celsius, where the metal loses all electrical resistance. The test rig built for it became FLASH, a 315-metre free-electron laser that in 2000 and 2001 produced the world's first laser flashes in the vacuum ultraviolet and soft X-ray range. The full-scale version followed. Between 2009 and 2016 an international consortium led by DESY built the European XFEL, whose core is a 1.7-kilometre superconducting linear accelerator running at 17.5 GeV - the most powerful machine of its kind. Twelve European countries hold shares in it; DESY operates the accelerator on their behalf.

## South Pole, Zeuthen, Bahrenfeld

DESY's reach runs well past its fence line. It is the second-largest partner in the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, a detector frozen into the ice at the South Pole, and it works on the gamma-ray telescopes MAGIC, H.E.S.S. and VERITAS and the planned Cherenkov Telescope Array. It contributes to ATLAS and CMS at the Large Hadron Collider and to Belle II in Japan. A second site at Zeuthen near Berlin, absorbed from the East German Academy of Sciences on 1 January 1992, builds the electron sources. Around 3,000 staff from more than sixty nations work across the two campuses on an annual budget of roughly 359 million euros. Beate Heinemann has chaired the Directorate since 1 April 2025. The next machine, PETRA IV, is already on the drawing board.

## Flight Context

DESY occupies 53.5758 N, 9.8794 E in the Bahrenfeld quarter of Hamburg's Altona district, on the western side of the city. There is little to identify from altitude: the accelerators are underground, and the surface reads as a tight cluster of long low halls, workshops and car parks in an otherwise residential and allotment-covered stretch of west Hamburg. Hamburg Airport (EDDH) lies about 9 km north-east; Hamburg Finkenwerder (EDHI) is roughly 5 km south-south-west across the Elbe. Fly it at 1,500-2,500 ft AGL with the river on your left and the Kohlbrand crossing and container terminals filling the frame to the south-east. Marine layer and haze are common year-round; visibility is usually best behind a cold front.
