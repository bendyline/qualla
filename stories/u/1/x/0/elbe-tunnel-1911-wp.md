# Elbe Tunnel (1911)

A 426-metre tiled tube under the Elbe, reached by lift, decorated with ceramic fish and rats, and still carrying Hamburg across its river more than a century after it opened.

**Location:** 53.5440, 9.9665

**Categories:** Engineering, Tunnels, Hamburg, Transport, Historic landmarks

## Introduction

You do not walk down into the Old Elbe Tunnel. You get into a lift - and until recently, so did the car behind you. Four hundred and twenty-six metres of tiled tube run under the Norderelbe from the St. Pauli Landungsbrucken to the shipyards on Steinwerder, twenty-four metres below the surface, and the only way in at either end is a shaft with lift cars big enough to swallow a delivery van. In 1911 this was a technical sensation. In 2026 it is still the most peculiar river crossing in Germany.

## Compressed Air and the Bends

Construction started on 22 July 1907 under the contractor Philipp Holzmann, with Otto Stockhausen as chief engineer working to a design by Ludwig Wendemuth. The problem was obvious from the first spadeful: the tunnel sat below the water table of a tidal river. The solution was the one used for the piers of the Brooklyn Bridge - hold the water out with compressed air and dig inside the pressurised chamber. It worked, and it hurt. Men who came back to normal pressure too quickly suffered what was then called caisson disease, and is now called decompression sickness. Of the roughly 4,400 workers on the job, more than 600 had mild symptoms, 74 were severely affected, and three died of it. The tunnel opened to pedestrians on 7 September 1911 and to vehicles that November.

## Why Anyone Bothered

Hamburg's port was among the busiest in the world, and every morning tens of thousands of men had to get from the city side of the Elbe to the docks and shipyards on the far bank. Before 1911 that meant ferries, weather, tides and queues. Two tubes under the river, running around the clock, changed the working day for an entire class of the city. The scale is domestic by modern standards - each tube is a shade under five metres across inside - but it was never meant to move a motorway's worth of traffic. It was meant to move a shift.

## Fish, Crabs and an Old Boot

The walls are not merely tiled; they are decorated. Set into the glazed ceramic are relief panels modelled by the sculptor Hermann Perl, showing the life of the Elbe - plaice, sturgeon, crabs, mussels. Then, tucked among them, the jokes: a discarded boot with rats around it, and other bits of river junk rendered with the same care as the fish. Somebody in 1911 decided that a tunnel full of dockworkers deserved wit as well as ornament. The entrance buildings got the same treatment. Raabe and Wohlecke designed them, and the northern one at the Landungsbrucken still wears its copper-clad dome over tuff stone; the southern one was rebuilt with a flat roof after wartime damage.

## Concerts, Marathons and The Odessa File

A 426-metre acoustic tube under a river turns out to be good for things nobody planned. There are art exhibitions down there, and the Elbtunnel-Marathon runs its laps in the tubes. In May 2019, to mark the reopening of the eastern tube, the Hamburg University of Music and Drama and the Hamburg Port Authority staged four performances of a symphony written for the space, directed by Professor Georg Hajdu: 144 musicians spread through both tubes, each reading their part off a tablet fed in real time by a server, while the audience walked among them and heard a different piece depending on where they stood. Film crews found it earlier. The tunnel appears in The Odessa File, The American Friend, Matchless and Secret Agent Fireball, and in the video for Scooter's One (Always Hardcore).

## Still Down There

The Hamburg Port Authority runs the tunnel, which has been a listed monument since 2003 and was designated a Historic Landmark of Engineering in Germany on its hundredth birthday in 2011. Both tubes have been through long restorations - the eastern reopened in April 2019, the western in May 2026 - and motor vehicles have been shut out since June 2019. Pedestrians and cyclists still have it around the clock, and hundreds of thousands of them use it every year, most just crossing the river, some going down purely to ride the lift and look at the fish.

## Flight Context

The northern entrance stands at 53.5440 N, 9.9665 E at the St. Pauli Landungsbrucken, on the north bank of the Norderelbe; the tunnel runs south under the river to Steinwerder. From the air, find the long floating pontoon pier of the Landungsbrucken with its clock tower, and the domed entrance rotunda sits at its western end - the tunnel line runs from there to the shipyard cranes directly across the water. The Elbphilharmonie is about a kilometre east, the Speicherstadt just beyond it. Hamburg Airport (EDDH) is roughly 10 km north-north-east; Hamburg Finkenwerder (EDHI) about 8 km west, downriver past the container terminals. Fly the river at 1,000-1,500 ft AGL. Haze and low cloud off the North Sea are routine; harbour traffic is the visual interest in any weather.
