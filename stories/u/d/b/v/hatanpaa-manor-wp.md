# Hatanpää Manor

Tampere exists because a manor owner on Lake Pyhäjärvi talked a visiting king into founding a city on land he happened to own.

**Location:** 61.4833, 23.7508

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Tampere, Manor houses in Finland, Tourist attractions in Tampere, Gardens in Finland

## Introduction

In 1775 King Gustav III of Sweden came north on a tour of his Finnish provinces and stopped at Hatanpää, a manor on a peninsula in Lake Pyhäjärvi. His host was Hans Henrik Boije, who sat on the committee then deciding where a new town should be built. Over the course of that visit the king issued the order founding Tampere at the Tammerkoski rapids — on land Boije owned. Two years later Boije sold that land to the crown. A year after that he sold Hatanpää as well and walked away from both. Finland's third-largest city began, in other words, with a well-placed dinner invitation.

## The Guest Who Founded a City

Boije's recommendation was not disinterested, and nobody at the time pretended otherwise. He held the Tammerkoski manor, he was on the planning committee, and he argued for his own rapids. The argument was also correct: the Tammerkoski drops eighteen metres between two large lakes, Näsijärvi above and Pyhäjärvi below, and that fall would power the cotton and linen mills that made the city's fortune in the following century. Gustav III's 1775 order established Tampere as a market town; full town rights followed on 1 October 1779. Boije sold Tammerkoski to the crown in 1777 for the new settlement to be laid out on, then sold Hatanpää itself in 1778. He died in 1781, before a single mill wheel turned.

## Thirty Gardeners in a Cold Country

The manor had been assembled in the 1690s out of three separate farms on the Hatanpää peninsula, and Boije (1716–1781) spent much of his tenure turning it into something that looked transplanted from England. He laid out a landscape park in the English manner — winding paths, contrived views, water used as a mirror rather than a canal — and at its height as many as thirty gardeners maintained it. That is an extraordinary number this far north, where the growing season runs barely five months and everything ornamental has to survive a winter that reliably freezes the lake solid. The park was less a garden than an argument: that a Finnish lakeshore could carry the same manners as a Home Counties estate, if you threw enough labour at it.

## The Bailiff Who Left It to the Children

Boije sold Hatanpää in 1778 to Gabriel Ahlman (1737–1799), a former crown bailiff who had spent six years collecting the king's taxes across the district. Ahlman held the manor until his death, and then did something his neighbours did not expect. His will — drafted with help from the scholar Henrik Gabriel Porthan — split the income from his considerable estate in two. Half went to the elementary education of poor children in the Tampere region; the other half to draining land and supporting the tenants who worked it. The first Ahlman schools opened at Pirkkala and Messukylä in 1811, twelve years after his death, and eventually numbered six. The agricultural school his bequest founded in 1904 is still teaching today.

## A House That Outlived Its Fields

The building visitors see now is the fourth act, not the first: a pale, symmetrical main house completed in 1885 to designs by Sebastian Gripenberg (1850–1925), an architect who would later serve as a senator and a bank director and put his name to more than sixty buildings. A villa from the same late-nineteenth-century decades stands nearby. Then the estate itself disappeared. In 1913 the city of Tampere bought the whole 1,459 hectares for two and a half million markka, and the manor's fields and forests became streets, housing blocks and factory yards. The buildings were taken into hospital use from 1916, and in the 1930s Bertel Strömmer's Hatanpää Hospital rose alongside them. The manor kept its park and lost its reason for existing.

## Roses on the Headland

What survived is now the point. The manor park on the peninsula remains, and since the 1970s the headland beside it has carried an arboretum, a tree-species collection and a rose garden — a botanical inheritance that stretches Boije's ambitions three centuries forward, with the city's parks department in place of thirty gardeners. The main building itself was given a careful going-over in 2016 at a cost of roughly 1.1 million euros. Facade colours were matched as closely as possible to the originals, the wooden windows regained the outer frames that had been swapped out in the 1990s, damaged plasterwork was repaired, and new lighting was fitted so the house reads against the winter dark. It now serves as a restaurant and function venue, looking out over water it no longer owns.

## Flight Context

Hatanpää Manor stands at 61.4833°N, 23.7508°E on a peninsula pushing into Lake Pyhäjärvi, about 2 km south of central Tampere. From the air the setting is unmistakable: a wooded green tongue of land surrounded on three sides by water, with the arboretum's darker plantings visible against the lighter parkland. Näsinneula tower, about 2.5 km almost due north, gives the initial fix; follow the Tammerkoski channel south from it to Pyhäjärvi and the peninsula is immediately to the east. Best viewed from 1,500–2,500 ft AGL. Tampere–Pirkkala (EFTP) lies 6 nm southwest, elevation 390 ft, runway 06/24. Autumn colour peaks in late September; Pyhäjärvi normally freezes from December to April, which flattens the whole scene into white and makes the park's tree structure easiest to read.
