# Alexander Church

Tampere spent eighty years asking for a church of its own, and when permission finally came it was conditional on one country pastor's death.

**Location:** 61.4972, 23.7490

**Categories:** Buildings and structures in Tampere, Tourist attractions in Tampere, Churches completed in 1881, 19th-century churches in Finland, Tampere

## Introduction

Tampere had to wait for a man to die. In 1869 the Senate of Finland finally agreed that the town could break away from the rural parish of Messukylä and run its own church, but it attached two conditions. Tampere had to build a church, which was expensive and straightforward. And the pastorate of Messukylä had to fall vacant. The pastor was Josef Grönberg, installed in 1861, and he lived to be nearly ninety. He died in 1903. The separation went through in 1904, twenty-three years after the new church had been finished and consecrated without him.

## Eighty Years of Asking

The townspeople had been petitioning since the 1820s, and their objection was practical as much as spiritual. Messukylä's old greystone church stood outside the town, and Tampere's residents were paying to maintain a building most of them rarely entered. The Senate refused. The cathedral chapter in Turku refused again in 1847 and once more in 1853. When the answer finally came back yes in 1869, the conditions attached to it made clear that nobody in authority felt any urgency. Grönberg, for his part, simply kept living, and every year he did so was another year in which a booming industrial town of thousands remained, on paper, the congregation of a country parish.

## Named for the Emperor

Theodor Decker designed the church, and it rose in brick between 1880 and 1881, consecrated at Advent of that second year as the main church of the new Tampere city parish. It was named for Alexander II, Emperor of Russia and Grand Duke of Finland, which was then an autonomous grand duchy inside his empire. The building reaches for a continental cathedral and arrives somewhere in a late, eclectic Gothic Revival. Large round and circular windows dominate the façade; the pillars standing proud of the walls carry no load whatsoever and are pure decoration. Inside are three aisles beneath a wooden ceiling, upper galleries resting on brick piers, and seating for 1,430 — an enormous room for a town that had spent sixty years being told it did not need one at all.

## The Woman Who Painted the Altar

Two years after the consecration, in 1883, Alexandra Frosterus-Såltin delivered the altarpiece, The Transfiguration of Christ. She was then in her mid-forties, and altarpieces were her trade: she painted around seventy of them between 1877 and 1915, of which roughly fifty survive. Her training had been a sequence of workarounds. From the age of fourteen she studied privately in Turku under Robert Wilhelm Ekman, then went to Düsseldorf, where the academy would not admit women and she worked instead with Otto Mengelberg, and later painted from live models in a Paris studio. Widowed, with three children to support, she made church commissions her livelihood. The picture above Tampere's altar was, among other things, how a family got fed.

## November 1937

The church came close to being lost altogether. On the night of 12 November 1937 and into the following day, fire broke out — traced to painting materials igniting on their own — and it very nearly destroyed the building. The timing made it worse: the church was midway through its first major renovation, so the flames took work that was almost finished. The repairs were carried through regardless and completed in December 1938. The following year the organ factory at Kangasala installed the instrument still in use, an electro-pneumatic organ of fifty-six voices with three extensions. A building that had taken eight decades of argument to authorise was rebuilt, from near ruin, in a little over twelve months.

## The Park of the First Dead

The church stands inside Pyynikki Church Park, which was Tampere's first cemetery. Burials began in 1785 and ended in the 1880s, once the new Kalevankangas cemetery opened in 1880. Fredrik Sacklén, the town's first mayor, lies here, together with ironworks managers and factory founders who built the place around them. Accounts also place a mass grave beside the church from the civil war of 1918, holding Reds executed after White forces took the city that April — though some researchers dispute that the burials happened on this ground. Wäinö Aaltonen, whose bronzes stand on the Hämeensilta a few streets east, set a sculpture called Suru, Sorrow, here in 1963. Finland's thickest Norway spruce grows in the same soil. Inside, the ecumenical St Thomas Mass comes round about every three weeks.

## Flight Context

The Alexander Church is at 61.4972°N, 23.7490°E, in the Kaakinmaa district of central Tampere, on the western edge of the Hämeenpuisto boulevard where it meets Pirkankatu. From the air the church tower rises from a distinct dark-green square of mature parkland — Pyynikki Church Park — set against the long straight double avenue of Hämeenpuisto running north to south between Näsijärvi and Pyhäjärvi. The wooded Pyynikki ridge lies immediately southwest. A viewing altitude of 1,500–2,500 feet AGL frames the church, the park and the boulevard axis together. Tampere–Pirkkala Airport (EFTP) is roughly 12 km southwest and Helsinki–Vantaa (EFHK) about 145 km southeast. Low winter sun picks out the tower against the ridge; from June the park canopy largely hides the building from directly overhead.
