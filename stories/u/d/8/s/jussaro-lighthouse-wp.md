# Jussarö Lighthouse

A concrete tower on a bare skerry in the Gulf of Finland, built in 1922 to be the first Finnish lighthouse that never needed a keeper at all.

**Location:** 59.7902, 23.5497

**Categories:** lighthouse, finland, gulf-of-finland, maritime, archipelago, mining, ghost-town, national-park

## Introduction

A compass is only as honest as the rock beneath it. Off the outer edge of the Raseborg archipelago, in the western Gulf of Finland, the iron locked inside the island of Jussarö pulls magnetized needles away from true. Sailors closing this coast in haze had one instrument they could rely on, and here it quietly lied to them. That is the problem the tower four kilometres to the south exists to answer. The name is its own small confusion: everyone says Jussarö Lighthouse, but the lighthouse is not on Jussarö. It stands on Sundharu, a skerry so bare that the tower is essentially the whole island — twenty-five metres of white reinforced concrete banded in red, flashing once every twelve seconds over water that has swallowed more ships than anyone bothered to count.

## The Rock That Lied to Compasses

The first attempt stood on Jussarö itself: a round brick tower seventeen and a half metres tall, whitewashed with a red band around its waist, completed in 1881. Around it grew the small society that a manned light required — quarters for the keeper and his three attendants, and housing for the maritime pilots and their families who worked the same fairways. The light turned white and threw two red flashes every half minute. It should have been enough. It was not. Water seeped into the structure, and the tower's position gave poor warning of the shoals it was meant to mark, on a stretch of coast where the ore beneath a ship's keel was already confusing the helmsman's compass. Within four decades the Finnish lighthouse service concluded that the answer was not to repair the tower but to move the light entirely.

## A Light With Nobody In It

The new tower was lit on 20 November 1922, and it broke with everything that had come before it. Sundharu was designed from the drawing board to run unmanned — the first lighthouse in Finland conceived that way rather than automated later as an afterthought. There were no keeper's quarters because there was no keeper. The light burned acetylene through an AGA flasher, fed from a tank large enough that a tender needed to call only about once a month, and the old light on Jussarö was extinguished the same year. A trade that had defined the coast for generations simply never reached this rock. Modernization since has been incremental and unsentimental: in 1984 the gas was replaced by a wind generator, and in 1999 a solar panel was added. The focal plane sits thirty metres above the sea, and the flash carries roughly thirty kilometres on a clear night.

## Iron, and the Men Made to Dig It

The magnetism was never a mystery to the people living here, because they were mining its cause. Ore was found on Jussarö in 1834 and immediately claimed for the crown, and the labour problem was solved the way authorities of that era often solved it: forty-five men convicted of vagrancy were shipped out from Viapori to work the pit. Poverty had made them criminals, and the sentence made them miners. Later the workforce was drawn from the prisons, swelling to around ninety-five men in summer and halving when the ice came, twelve hours a day at the rock face. The ore proved poor, the mine faltered and closed in the late 1850s, and the island went quiet for most of a century. It reopened in the 1950s under Oy Vuoksenniska Ab, shipping its first concentrate to the Koverhar smelter in 1961, and shut for good in 1967 when the ore ran thin and the costs did not.

## Finland's Only Ghost Town

What the company left behind was a village with nobody in it — mine buildings, housing, the infrastructure of a working island, all of it abandoned in place. Finland has exactly one ghost town, and this is it. For decades the emptiness had a second life as a training ground: soldiers used the derelict buildings to practise urban combat until 2005, clearing the same stairwells that miners' families had climbed. The Border Guard kept a coast guard station and a customs point for visiting boats here until November 2011. Only then did the island finish the long transition from industry to weapons to wilderness, and even now the ruins remain, roofless and photogenic, the most honest monument the mine will ever get.

## What the Light Sees Now

Jussarö today is mostly a nature destination. Its western part lies inside Ekenäs Archipelago National Park, established in 1989 across fifty-two square kilometres of skerries and open water, a park that drew 53,600 visitors in 2024 and can be reached only by boat. There is a guest harbour on the northern shore, a café, and a nature trail that runs out past the mine workings. Landing near the most important bird islands is forbidden from the first of April to the seventeenth of July, when eiders and terns and gulls have the archipelago to themselves. Four kilometres south of all that, the concrete tower keeps working. It has now stood through a century of Finnish history without a single person living inside it — which was, from the beginning, exactly the point.

## Flight Context

The lighthouse stands at 59.790 N, 23.550 E on the Sundharu skerry, about 4 km south of Jussarö island and roughly 25 km south of Ekenäs (Tammisaari) on the Finnish south coast. From the air it reads as a solitary white shaft on bare grey rock with nothing else around it — the easiest kind of target to spot over open water. Jussarö itself is unmistakable to the north: an island scarred with mine workings and abandoned buildings, its western half forested national park. Nearest airports: Hanko-Täktom (EFHN) 35 km west, Helsinki-Malmi area fields and Nummela (EFNU) to the north, Helsinki-Vantaa (EFHK) 85 km northeast, Turku (EFTU) 100 km northwest. Recommended viewing altitude 1,500-3,000 ft. Gulf of Finland haze and sea fog are common in spring when warm air moves over cold water; winter brings drift ice that can lock the whole archipelago solid.
