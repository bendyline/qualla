# Monastyrskyi Island

A granite-and-sand island in the middle of the Dnieper where Stone Age hunters left their tools, legend placed a lost Byzantine monastery, and a golden-domed church now anchors Dnipro's favorite park.

**Location:** 48.4663, 35.0743

**Categories:** river island, park, Dnipro, history

## Introduction

People have been coming to this island for a very long time, some of them before history had a way to record it. Monastyrskyi Island sits in the Dnieper River in the heart of Dnipro, a low ribbon of land that is bare granite at its western end and dissolves into a sandy spit in the east. Archaeologists digging here in 1961 turned up stone tools left by hunters of the deep prehistoric past, evidence that people camped on this river island in the Stone Age. Its name, though, comes not from those earliest visitors but from a story that no one has ever quite been able to prove, and no one has quite been willing to give up.

## The Monastery That May Never Have Been

The island is named for a monastery, and the monastery may be a legend. According to a tradition recorded by later chroniclers, Byzantine monks arrived here in the ninth century, having traveled up from Constantinople, and founded a religious community on the island. Folklore piles further wonders onto the tale: that the Kievan princess Olga sheltered here from a storm on her way south, that Vladimir the Great paused within the monastery's walls en route to Chersonesos. The monastery, so the story goes, endured until 1240, when the Mongol invasion under Batu Khan swept it away along with so much else. No archaeological trace of that first monastery has been found, which is why careful accounts call it an unconfirmed belief. But the name held, and centuries later, in 1747, Ukrainian Cossacks did build a monastery on the island, giving the legend a foundation in fact.

## A Church of White Stone

The island's gem today is younger than it looks. In 1999 the Orthodox Church of St. Nicholas rose on the northern end, built of white stone and crowned with a golden dome that catches the light and can be seen from points across the city. It stands where the old stories placed the vanished monastery, a modern echo of an ancient claim, and it has become one of Dnipro's recognizable symbols. The approach is a small pleasure of its own: an elegant pedestrian bridge arcs from the city to the island's northern tip, a landmark that locals prize almost as much as the church it leads to. Cross it on a summer evening and you join a river of strollers headed for the water's edge.

## The City's Green Retreat

Much of Monastyrskyi Island belongs to Taras Shevchenko Park, Dnipro's beloved green heart, and the island is where the city comes to breathe. Granite outcrops give way to grass and to that sandy eastern spit, a natural beach on the great river. The park honors Ukraine's national poet, Taras Shevchenko, with a monument unveiled in the late 1950s. The island itself carried a Soviet-era name for decades before it was renamed Monastyrskyi to comply with Ukraine's decommunization laws, a small act of restoring an older identity to the land. Today it draws families, sunbathers, wedding parties, and anyone wanting the sound of moving water, all on an island whose full history reaches back further than anyone can say.

## Flight Context

Monastyrskyi Island lies at about 48.466 N, 35.074 E, mid-channel in the Dnieper within central Dnipro. From 1,500 to 3,000 feet AGL the island is unmistakable, ringed by water with the golden dome of St. Nicholas Church at its north end and the slender pedestrian bridge linking it to the right bank. Nearest airport is Dnipro International (UKDD), southeast of the city.
