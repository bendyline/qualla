# Dnipro

A Ukrainian city closed to the outside world for decades, where Soviet rockets were built in secret beside a river that has carried three different names for the same place.

**Location:** 48.4675, 35.0400

**Categories:** Cities, Ukraine, Industrial Heritage, Dnieper River

## Introduction

For most of the Cold War, you could not come here. Dnipro was a closed city, off-limits to foreigners without special permission until the 1990s, because behind its ordinary streets rose one of the Soviet Union's most secret enterprises: a factory that turned out ballistic missiles. The same plants that made screws and vacuum cleaners also built rockets, and the world was not meant to see it. Today the gates are open, and what you find is a great industrial metropolis spread along both banks of the Dnieper River, layered with the architecture of every era it has passed through, still very much a working city.

## A City of Three Names

The place has changed its name as often as it has changed hands. It was founded as Yekaterinoslav, "the glory of Catherine," for the empress Catherine the Great, and it grew outward in rings from a single point, the Transfiguration Cathedral, taking on the look of a typical European city in brick and stone. In 1926 it became Dnipropetrovsk, a name that fused the river with a Bolshevik official, Grigoriy Petrovsky. Then, after the Euromaidan Revolution and a 2015 law banning communist symbols, the city shed the second half of its name in 2016 to become simply Dnipro, for the river alone. Even now the choice of name carries weight; most of the world uses Dnipro, the city's official name. Few places wear their history quite so openly, right there in what you call them.

## Layers in Stone

Walk the long central avenue, Dmytra Yavornytskoho Prospekt, and you read the twentieth century in its buildings. The pre-revolutionary city survives in neo-classical facades and flourishes of art nouveau, in the grand main building of the National Mining University from 1899, in the old city Duma and the Astoriya. Then the Bolsheviks came, and monuments were pulled down and imperial emblems chiseled away; a statue of Catherine gave way to one of a Russian scholar. The Second World War shattered much of the center, and what rose in its place was Stalinist social realism, monumental and severe, which is why so much of the avenue looks the way it does. The railway station lost its ornament and was rebuilt in the heavy new style. The result is a cityscape where every era argues with the last.

## River and Rockets

Dnipro sits on flat, open land at the meeting of the Dnieper and the Samara rivers, and that flatness let it sprawl across two hundred years into the metropolis it is now. The great river runs through its heart, wide and slow, with a green promenade stretching for kilometers along the water and Monastic Island rising midstream. After the 1950s the city's industry deepened; the Yuzhne missile and rocket works joined a forest of metallurgical and heavy-manufacturing plants, and Dnipro became one of the most important manufacturing centers in the entire Soviet Union. That industry left its marks, in pollution and in the endless prefabricated apartment blocks of the residential districts. Yet the river endures as the city's finest feature, best walked in late spring or in autumn, when the trees along its banks turn gold.

## The Working City

For all its scale, Dnipro is an easy city to move through, threaded by dozens of bus routes, trams and trolleybuses, and a short metro line. Its tables carry the hearty food of the region, borscht and cutlets, olivier salad heavy with mayonnaise, plov and street-corner shawarma. A local custom called drinking na lavochke, on a bench, sends people down to the riverside or into the center to sit outdoors with a drink and watch the evening go by. Beyond the city lie its neighbors, each with its own story: industrial Kamianske, and Gulyaipole, once the largest village in Ukraine and the capital of the anarchist Makhno's short-lived realm during the Civil War. Dnipro has always been a place of work and reinvention, and it wears that identity without apology.

## Flight Context

Located near 48.4675 N, 35.04 E, spread across both banks of the Dnieper at its confluence with the Samara River in eastern Ukraine. From the air the broad river, the long riverside promenade, and Monastic Island midstream are the defining landmarks. Note that Ukrainian airspace is currently closed to civil aviation. The city's airport is Dnipropetrovsk International (UKDD).
