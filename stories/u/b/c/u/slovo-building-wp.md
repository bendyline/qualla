# Slovo Building

A Kharkiv apartment block built in the shape of a letter to house Ukraine's finest writers became, within a few short years, a trap - and its residents entered history as the Executed Renaissance.

**Location:** 50.0117, 36.2342

**Categories:** history, literature, memorial, Ukraine

## Introduction

Seen from above, the building spells a letter - the Cyrillic С, first letter of the word slovo, meaning "word." That was the point. In the late 1920s, when Kharkiv was the capital of Soviet Ukraine and its finest writers were living in offices and improvised rooms - some storing their manuscripts in pots so mice would not eat the paper - a plan took shape to give the country's literary elite a home worthy of them. The satirist Ostap Vyshnya championed the idea, and when the money ran short, he traveled to Moscow in February 1929 and asked Stalin directly. Stalin granted the funds that same day, on one condition: the writers would pay it back within fifteen years. Most of them would not live long enough to try.

## A House Built for Words

For its moment, the Slovo Building was a marvel. Five storeys high, it held sixty-six apartments of three to five rooms each - genuine luxury in the interwar Soviet Union, where a single room was a common family's lot. It was built of the best available materials, with a solarium and a shower on the roof. Into it moved the leading lights of a remarkable flowering of Ukrainian culture: poets like Pavlo Tychyna and Volodymyr Sosiura, the prose stylist Mykola Khvylovy, the visionary theatre director Les Kurbas, the playwright Mykola Kulish, novelists, dramatists, painters. Ukrainization had drawn them to the capital, and here they were gathered in one place to write and argue and create. For a few short years, the building was exactly what it was meant to be - a house of the word.

## The Trap Springs Shut

The very feature that made the building convenient made it deadly. The authorities had approved it in part because gathering the intellectuals under one roof made them easy to watch. Telephones were tapped; neighbors were pressured to inform on neighbors. To live in the Slovo Building was to live under constant surveillance, never sure who might report you or when the knock would come. The actress Halyna Mnevska was among the first taken, arrested in January 1931 after she refused to denounce her husband, the writer Klym Polishchuk. She was sentenced to five years and never allowed to return to Ukraine; Polishchuk himself was shot in 1937. And all of this unfolded as the Holodomor, the man-made famine that killed millions of Ukrainians, ravaged the country beyond the building's walls.

## The Executed Renaissance

What happened to the residents of the Slovo Building has a name: the Executed Renaissance, the deliberate destruction of a generation of Ukrainian cultural figures between 1934 and 1938. The roll of the building's inhabitants reads like a casualty list. Mykola Khvylovy took his own life in 1933, in despair at what was coming. Then came the shootings. Les Kurbas, Mykola Kulish, the novelist Valerian Pidmohylny, and many others were executed in 1937, a great number of them killed together at Sandarmokh, a forest clearing in Karelia in the Russian north where the secret police carried out mass executions. The poet Mike Johansen was shot that same year in an NKVD prison in Kyiv. These were not statistics. They were among the most gifted writers and artists their nation had produced, silenced in their prime because of the language they wrote in and the ideas they held.

## The Word Endures

The building still stands in Kharkiv's Shevchenkivskyi District, and it has become a place of memory rather than of fear. A plaque bearing the names of its famous residents was mounted in 2003, replacing an earlier one that had been destroyed, and in 2019 the Slovo Building was entered onto Ukraine's register of national monuments. Since 2017, the ProSlovo project has reconstructed the building's story in painstaking detail - three-dimensional models of each apartment, photographs of each resident, memoirs and maps - so that the people who lived and died here are known as individuals, not erased into a footnote. Films have been made about it. The tragedy of the Slovo Building is that a house built to celebrate the word became the instrument of silencing it. Its endurance is that the words, and the names, survived after all.

## Flight Context

The Slovo Building stands at 50.01°N, 36.23°E in Kharkiv's Shevchenkivskyi District, north of the city center on a quiet residential street; its distinctive C-shaped footprint is visible from directly overhead. From 2,500-4,000 feet the surrounding grid and the nearby expanse of Freedom Square orient you. Nearest airport is Kharkiv International (UKHH).
