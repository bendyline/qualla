# Taigan Safari Park

In the Crimean foothills, dozens of African lions roam an open reserve built on the grounds of a former military site, in what is billed as Europe's largest lion breeding park.

**Location:** 45.0360, 34.5637

**Categories:** Wildlife, Crimea, Attractions, Animals

## Introduction

The sound reaches you before anything else does: at dawn and dusk, the roar of lions rolls across the Crimean foothills near the Taigan Reservoir, a sound wholly out of place in this landscape of vineyards and pale limestone hills. Yet here they are, some sixty African lions, along with tigers and other animals, ranging over more than thirty hectares of open ground. Taigan Safari Park bills itself as the largest breeding ground for lions and other big predators in Europe, an audacious thing to build in the dry uplands of inland Crimea, and its prides have made it one of the most talked-about, and most controversial, animal attractions in the region.

## A Pride in the Foothills

What sets Taigan apart is scale and openness. Rather than caging its cats in small enclosures, the park lets large numbers of lions live together across a wide expanse of Crimean foothills, so that visitors view them ranging in something closer to a pride than a zoo exhibit. The animals occupy land beside the Taigan Reservoir, the water and the surrounding hills giving the reserve a genuinely wild backdrop. Beyond the lions and tigers, the park holds a broad collection of other animals. For visitors, the appeal has always been the sheer number of big cats in one place and the chance to watch them behave more or less as lions do, sprawled in the sun, patrolling their ground, and filling the evening air with that unmistakable roar.

## Built on Old Ground

The park opened on an unlikely site. In 2006 a businessman who already owned the Yalta zoo known as "Fairy Tale" bought property on the grounds of a former military research institution and set about turning it into a safari park, acquiring rights to some thirty-five hectares of surrounding land. The process proved difficult, tangled in bureaucracy and local disputes, and the conflict grew serious enough that the park was closed to visitors in 2015 before reopening the following April. Converting a fenced military compound into a home for large predators was no small undertaking, and the reserve grew steadily around that unusual foundation. Taigan drew international attention for its close-contact experiences, and over the years it became a fixture of foreign news reports and viral videos, its lions filmed everywhere from safari vehicles to walking tours through the enclosures.

## Controversy and Consequence

That closeness carried real danger, and Taigan's story is inseparable from the controversies around it. The park's signature "Walk with the Lions," which allowed visitors direct contact with adult big cats, was the subject of alarm after a serious mauling incident in 2018, and after years of dispute with local authorities the walks were finally banned in 2019. The park's founder was later convicted of negligence by a Crimean court in 2022 following an incident in which a tiger injured a small child, and he served several months before his release. Through these upheavals Taigan stayed open, its prides continuing to draw the curious. It remains a place that provokes strong reactions, admiration for the spectacle of so many lions in one landscape, and unease about the risks of getting close to them.

## Flight Context

Taigan lies at 45.04°N, 34.56°E in the Bilohirsk district of inland Crimea, on foothills beside the Taigan Reservoir. From about 2,500 to 3,000 feet, look for the reservoir's water and the broad fenced expanse of the park against the surrounding farmland; the white cliff of Aq Qaya rises nearby to the northeast. The nearest major airport is Simferopol (URFF), a short distance to the west.
