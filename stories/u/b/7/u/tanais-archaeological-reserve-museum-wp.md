# Tanais Archaeological Reserve Museum

At the edge of the Don delta stands Russia's first archaeological open-air reserve, built around a Greek trading city that guarded the far frontier of the ancient world.

**Location:** 47.2690, 39.3350

**Categories:** Archaeology, Ancient History, Open-Air Museums, Rostov Oblast

## Introduction

Walk the low stone lines here and you are walking the northeastern rim of the classical world, the place where the Greek map ran out and the steppe began. Tanais Archaeological Reserve Museum spreads across the ruins of a city that once sat at the mouth of the Don, where the great river spills into the Sea of Azov, and for the ancient Greeks this was about as far from home as commerce reached. It is Russia's first archaeological reserve and one of its largest open-air museums, an entire vanished town rather than a single monument, its walls and streets and towers half-recovered from the earth.

## The Last Greek City

Greek colonists founded Tanais in the early third century BC, naming it for the river the ancients called the Tanais, the modern Don. It rose at the boundary between two worlds. To the south lay the Black Sea and the sailing routes home; to the north and east stretched the Great Steppe, the endless grassland of nomadic peoples. For nearly eight centuries Tanais brokered between them, a trading city where Greek goods met steppe wares and the politics of the northern Black Sea region were argued out. Its peak came in the second and third centuries AD, when it grew into a genuine center of the region. Then the currents that carried it began to fail. By the fifth century the city was abandoned, left to the grass and the silt of the delta.

## A Site That Waited

Tanais was luckier than most ancient cities in one respect: no later town buried it. Newer settlements grew up nearby, but the site itself stayed clear, its ruins sleeping just under the surface. In the early nineteenth century scholars recognized the place for what it was, and in 1853 the first excavations began, a dig that would carry on into the twentieth century and beyond. The reserve founded on those ruins became the very first archaeological museum-reserve in Russia. Since 1993 the Lower-Don expedition has worked here as an international effort, joining Russian archaeologists with the German Archaeological Institute and the University of Warsaw. In February 2009 the site was put forward as a candidate for the UNESCO World Heritage List.

## Walking the Ruins

This is a museum you experience in the open air, on foot, under the wide delta sky. Reconstructed huts and towers stand among the excavated remains, giving shape to what the eye would otherwise struggle to fill in. Sections of the old city wall survive, including a stretch that runs down toward the sea, and streets thread past the necropolis where the people of Tanais laid their dead. The wind off the Azov moves through it all the way it has for two thousand years. To stand here is to feel the strange geometry of the ancient world, in which a Greek city could sit this far east, this deep into the steppe, trading and enduring at the very edge of the known.

## Flight Context

Located at 47.269 N, 39.335 W, near the village of Nedvigovka at the mouth of the Don where the river meets the Sea of Azov, roughly 30 km west of Rostov-on-Don. From the air the braided channels of the Don delta and the open Gulf of Taganrog are the key landmarks. Best viewed at low to moderate altitude in clear weather. Nearest airport is Platov International (URRR) at Rostov-on-Don.
