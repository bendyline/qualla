# Depaldo Stairs

A dying Greek merchant left his fortune to build a grand staircase down to the sea - and its clever optical trick would later make Odessa's Potemkin Stairs famous.

**Location:** 47.2131, 38.9381

**Categories:** Historic architecture, Landmarks, Sea of Azov, Taganrog

## Introduction

A staircase is a humble thing, but this one carries a secret in its geometry. Descend the Depaldo Stone Steps in Taganrog toward the shimmer of the Sea of Azov, and the flights seem to hold a perfectly even width the whole way down. Look back up, though, and the illusion reveals itself: the stairs are narrower at the top than the bottom, deliberately tapered so that from the crest the descent appears to plunge grandly and evenly toward the water. It is a trick of perspective, quietly ingenious - and years after these steps were built in 1823, the very same technique would give a far more famous staircase in Odessa its dramatic power.

## A Merchant's Last Wish

The stairway exists because of a bequest. Gerasim Depaldo, a wealthy Greek merchant of Taganrog, left instructions and funding in his will for a grand public stairway connecting the town on its hill to the seashore below. The design fell to Francesco Boffo, the prolific architect whose classical hand shaped so much of the northern Black Sea coast. Completed in 1823, the steps ran from the crossroads of Greek Street down to the Azov foreshore - today's Pushkin Embankment - a generous civic gift bearing the donor's name. For decades they were simply the Depaldo Stairs, a monument to one man's wish that his adopted city have a beautiful way to walk down to the sea.

## The Blueprint for Odessa

In imperial Russia, the Depaldo Stairs were famous across the south, and they were more than merely handsome. Boffo built into them an optical refinement - the gentle narrowing toward the top that makes the whole flight read as uniform and monumental when seen from above. The idea proved so effective that the same principle reappeared in Boffo's later, grander work: the great staircase in Odessa completed around 1841, now celebrated worldwide as the Potemkin Stairs and immortalized in the cinema of Sergei Eisenstein. Taganrog's steps, in other words, were the quiet prototype. The trick of perspective that thrills visitors to Odessa was first tested here, on a modest hillside above the Azov, years before.

## Marking Time by the Sun

The stairs came with a companion that made the spot a genuine landmark. At the top, near the entrance, stands a sundial - the first in southern Russia - its gnomon casting the hour across a stone face for passersby to read. Old photographs from the early 1900s show it presiding over Greek Street, a stone cube marking both the head of the descent and the passage of the day. Together the staircase and the sundial turned this corner of Taganrog into a place of small daily rituals: a glance at the time, a walk down to the shore, the same steps under generations of feet. Function and beauty met here in a way that felt entirely of its cultured, Greek-flavored merchant town.

## Rebuilt, Stone by Stone

Time and war took their toll. During the Second World War the old steps were heavily damaged, and a partial reconstruction in the 1970s was left unfinished. Only in 2005 were the stairs fully restored - though at a cost dear to purists, for the reconstruction replaced all the original historic stones with new ones. What remains, then, is the design rather than the exact fabric: the same clever proportions, the same sweep toward the sea, rebuilt for a new century. The Depaldo Stairs still do what a dying merchant wished them to do nearly two hundred years ago - carry the people of Taganrog gracefully down the hill to the water's edge, past the sundial that has watched them come and go.

## Flight Context

The Depaldo Stairs descend the Taganrog waterfront near 47.21°N, 38.94°E, best appreciated from low altitude, where the stairway line links the old-town bluff to the Pushkin Embankment along the Sea of Azov. Orient on Taganrog's cape and its shoreline. The nearest major airport is Rostov-on-Don's Platov International (URRP), about 40 kilometers to the northeast.
