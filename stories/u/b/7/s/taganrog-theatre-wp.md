# Taganrog Theatre

The oldest theater in southern Russia lit the imagination of a schoolboy named Chekhov, who slipped into its gallery to watch operettas and left, in time, one of the world's great playwrights.

**Location:** 47.2161, 38.9286

**Categories:** Theater, Anton Chekhov, History, Taganrog

## Introduction

A thirteen-year-old boy sits high in the gallery of a provincial theater in October 1873, watching an Offenbach operetta called Elena the Beautiful, and something ignites. The boy is Anton Chekhov, born in this town in 1860, and the Taganrog Theatre is where he first fell helplessly in love with the stage. He would return again and again, hoarding coins for tickets, drinking in the vaudevilles and Italian operas and popular comedies. Long before he wrote a word for the theater, this room taught him what a theater could do, and today it bears his name.

## Opera on the Azov

The Taganrog Theatre was founded in 1827 by the governor Alexander Dunaev, making it the oldest theater in southern Russia. It grew alongside a cosmopolitan port whose Greek and Italian merchants brought their tastes with them, and from 1861 Italian opera performed here regularly, an unlikely thing on the shore of a shallow Russian sea. In 1874 the municipality bought the theater outright by purchasing its shares. Into this world of arias and footlights came the young Chekhov, and the enchantment never left him. The comic timing and human absurdity he absorbed in that gallery would surface decades later in plays that changed the course of modern drama, from The Seagull to The Cherry Orchard.

## The Playwright's House

The town never forgot whose imagination it had kindled. In 1944 the Soviet government formally named the theater after Anton Pavlovich Chekhov, and in 1977 it received the Order of the Badge of Honour for its service to the theatrical art. Since 1980 it has hosted a festival called In the Birthplace of Anton Chekhov, drawing companies from across Russia and far beyond, from Italy and Spain to Japan and Australia. Somewhere in the hall, preserved, is the seat that was Chekhov's favorite, a small shrine to the boy who once could barely afford to be there. To sit in this theater is to sit where a master of the human comedy learned his craft, from the audience's side.

## A Living Stage

The Chekhov theater has never settled into being a museum. In 2002 and 2010 its company traveled to Badenweiler in Germany, the spa town where Chekhov died, to stage his Proposal and The Wood Demon on foreign boards. It has carried productions to festivals across the wider Black Sea world and mounted ambitious international collaborations, including a Shakespeare project bringing together actors from a dozen countries. On 29 January 2010, during celebrations of the 150th anniversary of Chekhov's birth, the Russian president Dmitry Medvedev came to watch a rehearsal here. Nearly two centuries after its founding, the little theater where a schoolboy first looked down from the gallery is still doing exactly what enchanted him: raising the curtain, dimming the lights, and beginning again.

## Flight Context

The theater stands at 47.2161°N, 38.9286°E in central Taganrog, near the Sea of Azov. From above, the old-town grid running down toward the harbor is the guiding landmark. Nearest airports are Taganrog Yuzhny (URRT) and Platov International at Rostov-on-Don (URRP), roughly 90 km east.
