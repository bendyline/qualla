# Taganrog Lighthouse

For nearly a century a stone tower threw its beam thirty-five kilometers across the Sea of Azov to guide ships home, until age brought it down and only a memory remained.

**Location:** 47.2022, 38.9459

**Categories:** History, Lighthouse, Maritime, Taganrog

## Introduction

There is a stretch of the Taganrog waterfront where a lighthouse should be, near the bronze figure of Peter the Great, and where sailors for nearly a century looked for a familiar light. It is gone now. The stone tower that once threw its beam thirty-five kilometers out over the Sea of Azov was pulled down in 1970, too far decayed to save, and in its place stands only a slender metal mast with a lamp. But for the vessels feeling their way through the sea's treacherous shallows, that vanished tower was for generations the difference between safe mooring and a grounding on the shoals.

## A Light Rebuilt

Taganrog's first lighthouse was raised in 1770, then vanished from the record for reasons no one recorded. The tower that people remember came later. In 1877 the naval department secured land for it, on the single condition that the public keep free access to the garden that would surround it, and the stone lighthouse rose in 1878 under Admiral Nikolay Andreevich Arkas, chief commander of the Black Sea Fleet. A plaque on its wall named him. Twenty-two meters tall, cylindrical, tapering gently upward with meter-thick walls of stone, it wore two observation platforms near its glazed crown and a dome topped by a sphere and a weather vane. Set on high ground, its lantern stood some seventy meters above the sea.

## Through Darkness and Back

The lighthouse kept pace with its age. On 9 March 1909 its kerosene lamp gave way to electric light, powered by a substation built in the garden below, and its garden also held a small weather station serving the port. Then came the war that dimmed it. In the autumn of 1941, as enemy forces occupied Taganrog, the lighthouse went dark, its beam extinguished for the duration. Only after the city's liberation in August 1943 did the light return, sweeping once more across the water. In that long silence and its eventual return, the tower shared the fate of the town around it, shut down under occupation and rekindled with freedom, a small beacon of the city's endurance.

## The Beam That Remains

By August 1970 the old tower had fallen into critical condition, and it was taken down. Of the graceful ensemble that once surrounded it, only a few outbuildings survive: the housing that sheltered naval officers and the little guardhouse at the gate. The functional lamp on its metal mast still marks the harbor, but the landmark, the stone sentinel that generations of Azov sailors steered toward, exists now only in photographs and memory. Standing on the Taganrog embankment today, looking out toward Peter the Great's chosen anchorage, you can imagine the beam that once reached far into the dark, calling the fishing boats and freighters home across a shallow, unforgiving sea.

## Flight Context

The former lighthouse stood at 47.2022°N, 38.9459°E on the Taganrog waterfront near the Peter the Great monument, on the Sea of Azov. From the air the harbor and the headland reaching into the water are the guiding landmarks. Nearest airports are Taganrog Yuzhny (URRT) and Platov International at Rostov-on-Don (URRP), roughly 90 km east.
