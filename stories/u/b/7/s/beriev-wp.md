# Beriev

For nearly a century, a design bureau on the shore of the Sea of Azov has built aircraft that blur the line between airplane and boat.

**Location:** 47.1990, 38.8450

**Categories:** Aviation, Engineering, Industry, Taganrog

## Introduction

Most aircraft factories sit beside runways. This one sits beside a sea. Since the 1930s, the Beriev design bureau has worked on the edge of the shallow, forgiving waters of the Sea of Azov in Taganrog, building machines that take off from waves as readily as from tarmac. Amphibians and flying boats are a stubborn specialty - awkward, heavy, and endlessly demanding to engineer - and few organizations anywhere have pursued them as doggedly as Beriev. The result is a place where the runway ends at the water, and the aircraft simply keep going.

## The Man Who Loved Water Wings

The bureau bears the name of Georgy Mikhailovich Beriev, who founded it in Taganrog in 1934 as OKB-49. From the beginning its obsession was the seaplane - the marriage of aircraft and boat that lets a machine operate where there are no airfields at all. That focus never really wavered. Over the decades the bureau designed and produced more than twenty different models for civilian and military use, from small flying boats to enormous jet-powered amphibians. Building an aircraft that must also be seaworthy is a peculiarly hard problem: the hull has to slice through water without tearing itself apart, then lift cleanly into the air. Beriev made a specialty of solving it, again and again, on the shore of the Azov.

## Evacuated and Reborn

The bureau's history is bound up with the century's upheavals. In 1942, as war swept toward the region, the entire operation was moved east to Krasnoyarsk in Siberia to keep it beyond the reach of the fighting - a wholesale relocation of engineers, tooling, and knowledge across thousands of kilometers of a country at war. When the danger passed, it came home; by 1945 Beriev was back in Taganrog, resuming its work on the coast. The postwar years brought bolder designs, including experiments most bureaus never attempted: jet-powered flying boats, and even a prototype wing-in-ground-effect craft meant to skim just above the surface. In November 1989, Beriev became the only defense enterprise to win the Soviet government's Prize for Quality.

## Machines That Break Records

The bureau's ambitions run to the extreme. Its A-40 Albatross, developed in the 1980s, is the largest amphibious aircraft in the world - a jet flying boat of genuinely startling scale. Pilots flying Beriev seaplanes have set an astonishing 228 world aviation records, each recognized and registered by the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale, the global authority that certifies such feats. Some of the bureau's concepts read almost like science fiction: a proposed super-heavy cargo amphibian, the Be-2500 Neptun, was drawn up with a planned maximum takeoff weight of 2,500 metric tons, which would make it one of the heaviest aircraft ever conceived. Not all of these designs left the drafting table, but they reveal an engineering culture unafraid of enormous, difficult ideas.

## Wings Against the Fire

Beriev's most visible modern product answers a peaceful and urgent need. The Be-200 Altair, a large multipurpose amphibian first flown in 1998, can skim across a lake or the sea at speed, scoop water into its tanks in seconds, and drop it on a wildfire - then wheel around to do it again. Aircraft like these have fought fires across several countries, turning the bureau's decades of seaplane expertise toward saving forests and towns. Today Beriev employs some three thousand specialists and continues to develop amphibious aircraft on the same Azov shore where Georgy Beriev began. In a world that mostly forgot the flying boat, this one corner of Taganrog never did.

## Flight Context

The Beriev facility lies on the Taganrog waterfront near 47.20°N, 38.84°E, best seen from low altitude, where its slipways and apron meet the shallow Sea of Azov. Orient on the port city's cape and its adjacent airfield. The nearest major airport is Rostov-on-Don's Platov International (URRP), about 45 kilometers to the northeast.
