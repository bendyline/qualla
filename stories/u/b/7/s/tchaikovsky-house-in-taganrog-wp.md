# Tchaikovsky House in Taganrog

A merchant's mansion where Russia's most famous composer stayed with his sea-captain brother, and which Chekhov confessed he longed to own.

**Location:** 47.2125, 38.9382

**Categories:** Historic Houses, Music History, Museums, Taganrog

## Introduction

"If I were rich, I would certainly buy the house where Ippolit Tchaikovsky used to live." Anton Chekhov wrote that to his cousin in 1895, and the confession lingers, because Chekhov was a son of Taganrog and knew exactly which house he meant. It stands at 56 Grecheskaya Street, a two-story merchant's mansion built in the early 1870s, its facade turned toward the streets where the playwright had grown up. Ippolit was not the Tchaikovsky the world remembers. But his more famous brother came to this door, and the walls have kept both names.

## A Captain's Rented Rooms

The mansion belonged to a merchant named Sarandino, one of the Greek trading families who had made Taganrog a prosperous Sea of Azov port. From 1883 until 1894 he rented it to Ippolit Ilyich Tchaikovsky, a captain of the first rank who represented the Russian Society of Steam Navigation and Trade in the city. Ippolit was one of five Tchaikovsky brothers, a career navy man who had served on frigates across the Black and Baltic Seas before becoming a steamship captain, and for a time he ran the naval school in Taganrog. The long absences of a life at sea had a way of settling into melancholy. But he loved music, and he loved his family, and into these rented rooms he brought both.

## When Pyotr Came to Stay

Three times the composer came south to visit his brother here: in 1886, in 1888, and again in 1890. By then Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky was famous across Europe, the man behind Swan Lake and the symphonies that would outlive them all, and yet in Taganrog he was simply a brother arriving by train. It is easy to picture the evenings that a household like Ippolit's would have kept, the family gathered, music somewhere in the rooms. Ippolit later acquired a mechanical orchestrina fitted with cylinders that played several of his brother's own compositions, a small machine that let the absent composer keep filling the house long after the visits ended.

## Earthquake and Afterlife

Buildings outlast the people who fill them, but not always intact. In 1927 the Crimean earthquakes rattled the whole region, and the mansion on Grecheskaya Street lost its left wing to the damage. It stood on, diminished, through the decades that followed. In 1975 the music department of the city's Chekhov Library and a concert hall moved in, and the memorial room where Pyotr Tchaikovsky had stayed became a small museum devoted to the Tchaikovsky family and their ties to Chekhov. It opened on the first of May, 1976. Two neighbors of nineteenth-century Taganrog, a composer's visits and a playwright's longing, meet under one roof that neither of them owned.

## Flight Context

Located at 47.2125 N, 38.9382 W, in downtown Taganrog on the Sea of Azov. The mansion is a small target best appreciated on the ground, but from the air the low peninsula city and the wide gray water of the Gulf of Taganrog orient you. Best viewed at low altitude in clear conditions. Nearest airport is Taganrog Yuzhny (URRT); Platov International near Rostov-on-Don (URRR) lies about 100 km east.
