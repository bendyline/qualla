# Garibaldi Monument in Taganrog

A slender obelisk near the Taganrog seaport marks the unlikely spot where a shipment of oranges and a chance conversation helped set a young sailor on the road to unifying Italy.

**Location:** 47.2081, 38.9494

**Categories:** Monument, History, Italy, Taganrog

## Introduction

In April 1833 a schooner called the Clorinda tied up at Taganrog with a hold full of oranges, and among her crew was a restless young sailor named Giuseppe Garibaldi. He would spend only ten days in this Russian port on the Sea of Azov. But in a quayside tavern he met a fellow Italian who spoke of revolution, and something took hold. Near the seaport where the Clorinda once lay, a five-meter obelisk shaped like a wind-caught banner now marks the encounter that helped turn a merchant sailor into the man history calls the Hero of Two Worlds.

## Ten Days and a Conversation

Garibaldi was twenty-five, sailing the grain and citrus routes of the Black Sea, when his ship put in at Taganrog. There he fell in with Giovanni Battista Cuneo, a political exile from the Ligurian town of Oneglia and a member of Young Italy, the secret movement founded by Giuseppe Mazzini. Mazzini dreamed of an Italy unified not under a king but as a liberal republic, remade by political and social reform. Cuneo carried that dream into the taverns of a distant Russian port, and Garibaldi listened. Whatever passed between the two men, the sailor left Taganrog changed. Within a year he had sworn himself to the cause of Italian liberation, and the decades that followed would carry his name from South America to Sicily to Rome.

## A Banner in Stone

Taganrog did not forget the visitor. In 1961, for the centenary of Italy's unification, the city named a downtown street Ulitsa Garibaldi and raised an obelisk near the harbor. Designed by the local artist Yakovenko, the stella rises like a flag frozen mid-flutter, and its bronze bas-relief bore Garibaldi's profile beside a palm branch. The back still carries an inscription recalling the oath he took to free his homeland. The front once quoted Friedrich Engels, who called Garibaldi a hero of the antique kind, capable of miracles. That line was later removed. Twice the monument has been renewed, its bas-relief replaced in 1986 and the whole memorial restored in 2007.

## A Family Returns

In September 2007, on the two-hundredth anniversary of Garibaldi's birth and during Taganrog's City Day celebrations, a renovated monument was unveiled before an unusual guest: Annita Garibaldi-Jallet, the general's great-granddaughter, who traveled from Italy with representatives of the Italian embassy in Moscow. That such a descendant would cross a continent to stand at a Russian port speaks to how far the ripples of those ten days traveled. The monument is, remarkably, the only one dedicated to Garibaldi anywhere in the former Soviet Union, and a photograph of it once hung in a New York museum exhibition tracing Garibaldi memorials around the globe. A single tavern conversation on the Azov shore had earned its place on the world map.

## Flight Context

The obelisk stands at 47.2081°N, 38.9494°E near the Taganrog seaport, on the Sea of Azov. From the air the harbor and the low headland jutting into the water make the clearest landmark. Nearest airports are Taganrog Yuzhny (URRT) and Platov International at Rostov-on-Don (URRP), about 90 km to the east.
