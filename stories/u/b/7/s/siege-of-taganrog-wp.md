# Siege of Taganrog

In the summer of 1855 an Anglo-French fleet sailed to the far corner of the Sea of Azov to burn Taganrog's grain stores, and a merchant town found itself on the front line of the Crimean War.

**Location:** 47.2042, 38.9422

**Categories:** History, Military, Crimean War, Taganrog

## Introduction

On the morning of 3 June 1855, the people of Taganrog watched a line of paddle-steamers and armed launches gather offshore under a flag of truce. The envoys' demand was blunt: surrender the town, evacuate the civilians, hand over everything the Russian army had stored here. The governor, an aging general named Yegor Tolstoy, took an hour to consider. Then his answer came back: no. The truce flag came down, the guns opened up, and one of the strangest campaigns of the Crimean War fell on a quiet grain port at the eastern edge of the Sea of Azov.

## The Logic of the Azov

By its third year the Crimean War had settled into the grinding siege of Sevastopol, and the allies looked for a way to starve it. The answer lay in the Sea of Azov, the shallow inland sea whose ports funneled grain, timber, and tar to the Russian army. Cut those supply lines, the reasoning went, and Crimea itself could be strangled. Taganrog sat at the far eastern end, on a low spit of land jutting into the water, and to British and French planners it looked like a stepping stone toward Rostov-on-Don and the rear of the Russian forces. A fleet of some forty small warships and thousands of troops was assembled for what they called the Azov Campaign. Rostov itself lay safely out of reach, guarded by shoals no warship could cross.

## Fire on the Waterfront

When Tolstoy refused the ultimatum, the bombardment began. A four-oared gig ran ashore carrying Lieutenant Cecil Buckley and Boatswain Henry Cooper, who set the military depots ablaze before rowing back through the fire. Both men earned the Victoria Cross, and by the accident of alphabetical seniority Buckley's was the first ever formally awarded. The grain and timber burned, the customs house burned, government buildings burned, and a gunboat under construction was destroyed in the harbor. Curiously, the attackers suffered almost nothing: a single marine gunner was struck in the face by a spent musket ball. The Russian defenders, a scattering of Don Cossack sotnias and volunteers, could do little against ships that could stand off and shell the shore at will. Among the buildings caught in the crossfire was one mansion so riddled with shot that locals afterward called it the House with Bullets.

## A Summer of Skirmishes

The June raid was not the end but the beginning of a long, restless summer. The Russians rushed more Cossacks to the coast and threw up an earthwork battery, and the allied gunboats returned again and again to knock it down. One British ship, HMS Jasper, ran aground on a spit west of town after local fishermen, the story goes, quietly moved the buoys that marked safe water. Her stranded crew was rescued, the hull scuttled, and Cossacks later stripped her guns and blew apart what remained, the only allied warship lost in the entire Azov campaign. Patrols and small landings continued through October, until on 20 November the last reconnaissance found the harbor empty of vessels, and as sea ice began to form the fleet withdrew to warmer waters.

## The Reckoning

Russian forces at Taganrog stood down in June 1856, and ordinary life crept back. The cost to the town was heavy. Twenty mansions were destroyed outright and seventy-four damaged; nearly two hundred more buildings, most of them granaries and storehouses, were burned or wrecked. The war had cost Taganrog more than a million rubles, and in recognition of what its people had endured, Tsar Alexander II exempted the town's citizens from taxes for the year 1857. One hundred and sixty-three residents received medals for their service in the defense. To this day a cannonball remains lodged in the wall of a Taganrog church, and a monument honors the defenders of 1855, small memorials to the summer the wider world's war washed up on the Azov shore.
