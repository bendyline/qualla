# Sundial, Taganrog

The oldest surviving sundial in Russia has kept an approximate watch over a Taganrog stairway since 1833, its shadow tuned precisely to the latitude of the town.

**Location:** 47.2127, 38.9375

**Categories:** History, Landmark, Science, Taganrog

## Introduction

The oldest working clock in Taganrog has no gears, no spring, and no face to wind. It is a sundial, set into the ground beside the Depaldo Stairs since 1833, and it tells time the way it always has, by the slow slide of a shadow across an engraved dial. Its metal pointer, the gnomon, was cut at a very particular angle: one of its sharp corners equals the exact latitude of Taganrog, forty-seven degrees and twelve minutes north, so that its edge aims true at the pole of the Earth. It is the oldest surviving sundial in all of Russia.

## Time by the Sea

The sundial was installed under Baron Otto Germanovich Frank, the sixth governor of Taganrog, in a port that lived and traded by the daylight. Its dial is more clever than most. Beyond simply marking the hours, it carries a set of engraved corrections, one for each month of the year, calculated by formula so that a careful reader could reconcile the sun's time with a mechanical clock. In its prime the dial showed true local solar time, which ran about twenty-five minutes behind Moscow, and a knowledgeable observer could nudge that reading into official time using the monthly tables. The device was, in effect, an open-air instrument of astronomy, standing where anyone climbing up from the harbor could consult it.

## The Drift of Centuries

Nearly two centuries of weather and repair have taken their toll. The sundial was redecorated in 1972, again in 1990, and once more in 2006 and 2007, and along the way its gnomon was replaced more than once. Each restoration, however well meant, nudged the geometry a little off true, and the old dial now keeps time only approximately, its careful corrections no longer quite matching the sky. There is a gentle irony in that: an instrument built to measure time with precision has itself been altered by the passage of time, its accuracy worn away by the very decades it was made to count.

## A Second Sun

In 2000, Taganrog gave its ancient timekeeper a modern descendant. Before the central entrance to Gorky Park, a new sundial was installed on a fundamentally different plan. Rather than standing upright, it casts its shadow onto a plate tilted at forty-seven degrees and twelve minutes to the horizon, matching the latitude of the town, which evens out the hour markings and even allows for a second scale to read both winter and summer time. Its error is less than a single minute. The two dials make a quiet pair across the city: the venerable original by the stairs, beloved and imperfect, and its exacting successor by the park, a reminder that Taganrog has been watching the sun cross its sky for a very long time.

## Flight Context

The old sundial sits at 47.2127°N, 38.9375°E on Grecheskaya Street, beside the Depaldo Stairs descending toward the Sea of Azov. From above, the stairway and the old-town grid meeting the harbor are the key landmarks. Nearest airports are Taganrog Yuzhny (URRT) and Platov International at Rostov-on-Don (URRP), roughly 90 km east.
